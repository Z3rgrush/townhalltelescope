2027 Budget Message 
Pursuant to Section 29-1-103(e), C.R.S., as amended, the following budget message has been 
prepared and included as part of the 2027 Budget for the Town of Lyons, Colorado. 
Services Provided 
The Town of Lyons provides a full range of municipal services to residents and businesses. These 
include water, sanitation, stormwater, and electric utilities; street maintenance; law enforcement; 
parks and recreation; cultural programming; public improvements; building inspections; business 
and contractor licensing; land use planning; municipal court services; and general administrative 
support. 
Long-Range Planning 
The 2027 Budget reflects the Board of Trustees’ continued commitment to maintaining essential 
services while advancing the community’s long-term goals. These goals are identified in Lyons 
Thrive! Comprehensive Plan, as well as several other long-term planning documents that have 
been adopted by the Board of Trustees. 
In 2025, the Town initiated the development of Long-Range Financial Plans for major funds to 
improve forecasting, guide capital investment decisions, and strengthen the Town’s overall 
financial position. These plans are in early draft form and will continue to evolve as new 
information and priorities emerge. Integrating them into the annual budget process is an important 
step toward aligning day-to-day financial management with long-term strategic objectives. 
Budgetary Basis and Approach 
Town accounts are organized by fund, each treated as a separate accounting entity with self-
balancing accounts for assets, liabilities, fund equity, revenues, and expenditures. Each budget 
is appropriated at the fund level. 
Governmental and enterprise fund types are budgeted on a modified accrual basis, which may 
differ from the financial reporting basis. This approach includes budgeting for capital outlay and 
current debt service. 
Revenue and expenditure projections are based on historical trends, inflationary expectations, 
anticipated rate adjustments, and known operational changes.  
Fiscal Overview 
The Town enters 2027 with adequate financial reserves, with each operating fund projected to 
maintain reserves equal to three months of operating expenditures. While the Town is positioned 
2
to meet its financial obligations in 2027, modest revenue growth and rising operational costs 
present challenges to long-term financial sustainability. 
The 2027 Budget reflects continued delivery of essential services, infrastructure investment, and 
a focus on expenditure management. However, operating expenditures are growing faster than 
recurring revenues in certain funds, particularly the General Fund. If this trend continues, future 
budgets may require adjustments to revenues, expenditures, or service levels to maintain long-
term financial sustainability. 
 
Budget Highlights 
Revenue 
 Charges for Services:  Approximately 51% of total revenue ($4.6 million) is generated 
from charges for services, primarily utility us er fees for electric, water, and wastewater 
operations. These revenues are restricted to their enterprise funds and may not be used 
for general governmental purposes. 
 Taxes: Taxes account for roughly 39% ($3.5 million) of total revenue. The Town continues 
to see minor increases in sales tax revenue. The Town is conservatively projecting a 
modest 1.2% ($28,700) increase in sales tax collections over 2026 year-end projections. 
This is a 5.3% ($117,500) increase over 2026 original budget projections. 
 Property Tax: The 2027 mill levy is estimated to be 17.553 mills, which includes a required 
temporary credit of 1.969 mills. Property tax revenue is projected at approximately 
$1,043,500. 
Expenditures and Staffing 
Operating expenditures are projected to increase by approximately 3.7% in 2027, driven primarily 
by a 16.5% ($125,000) increase in the Boulder County Sheriff’s and a 16% increase in electric 
transmission costs ($182,800).  
The personnel budget, which includes salaries, taxes, insurance, and retirement benefits, is 
increasing by 0.2% ($4,300) compared to 2026. The budget provides for a 2.4% market 
adjustment and a 1.6% merit pool for staff. Consistent with regional and national trends, insurance 
benefits costs are rising by 13.0%. 
To manage rising personnel costs while maintaining a balanced budget, the Town has made 
staffing adjustments. Building on similar measures implemented in 2026, the 2027 Budget 
reduces staffing by 0.4 full-time equivalent (FTE) positions, with responsibilities redistributed 
among existing employees. Budgeted seasonal staffing hours within the Parks and Public Works 
departments have also been reduced. These adjustments reflect the Town's continued efforts to 
manage personnel expenditures while maintaining essential service levels.

Capital Improvements 
The 2027 Budget includes continued investment in infrastructure and equipment replacement. 
Major projects include additional funding for t he Safe Routes to School sidewalk improvements 
along 3rd Avenue, from Main Street to Seward and Stickney, electric undergrounding, sewer line 
replacement along North 5th Avenue from Seward to Steamboat Valley Road, and essential 
equipment replacements. Projects carried forward from 2026 include the 3rd Avenue sidewalk 
improvements and stormwater flagstone culvert repairs. 
Future improvements to the Wastewater Treatment Plant may be incorporated through a 
subsequent budget amendment once the project scope, funding requirements, and availability of 
outside financing have been determined.

the 2026 budget, primarily due to minor increases in property and sales tax revenue.
Operating expenditures are expected to rise about 3.1% ($109,000), driven largely by a
$125,000 increase in the Boulder County Sheriff’s contract.

Conclusion 
The Town of Lyons enters 2027 with adequate financial reserves and a continued commitment to 
responsible fiscal management. Although the Town is positioned to meet its financial obligations 
in the coming year, the growing gap between operating revenue and expenditure growth presents 
a challenge to long-term financial sustainability. 
The continued development and implementation of long-range financial plans will play an 
important role in identifying future funding needs, evaluating expenditure priorities, and guiding 
decisions necessary to maintain the Town's financial health. 
The contributions of Town staff, the Board of Trustees, and the community throughout the budget 
development process are sincerely appreciated. The 2027 Budget reflects a shared commitment 
to responsible stewardship of public resources and to maintaining Lyons as a resilient, vibrant, 
and sustainable community.
