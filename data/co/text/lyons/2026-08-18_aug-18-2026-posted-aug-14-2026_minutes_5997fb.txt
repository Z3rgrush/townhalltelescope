Hydrogel Pilot Proposal
● Peter presented hydrogels as a potential water-retention tool for Lyons residents 
and town plantings
○ Can reduce irrigation volume by up to 50%; absorb up to 400x their dry 
weight
○ Absorb and concentrate fertilizers (phosphate, potassium, ammonium) 
near roots, reducing fertilizer/pesticide use
○ Application rate: ~5g per kg soil (~20 lbs per acre); cost now very low 
(~$6/lb on Amazon)
○ Break down into cellulose/polysaccharide and polyvinyl alcohol 
components after ~5 years; EPA-certified
● Not widely adopted in the US; more common in China and globally
● Peter’s current experiments: plantings in the Bear Garden and near town hall 
statue
○ Over-irrigated sites not suitable; technology works best in drier, targeted 
applications
● Proposed pilot: test plot at community garden, pollinator garden, or food forest 
orchard
○ Food forest has empty raised beds; Adrian manages the nonprofit; Peter 
knows the secretary
○ Setup idea: 75-gallon toad tank, drip timer, baseline measurements for 
comparison
● Board receptive &agreed to take logistics offline and report back
● Note: West Wall garden club controls irrigation schedule and resists changes; 
overwatering of pollinator planters near La Merm’s also flagged (likely rotor head 
overspray)
