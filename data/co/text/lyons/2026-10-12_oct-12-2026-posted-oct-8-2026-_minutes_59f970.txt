A. Meeting notes and recorder process.  
• The Recorder led a discussion on formalizing the meeting-notes workflow. The group reviewed the 
history of note-taking approaches, which included typed notes, Zoom AI notes managed by staff, and 
then the Chair preparing notes directly.  Previous delays and inconsistencies, including confusion over 
where finalized notes were posted and previous notes that were not properly posted, were also 
discussed. The PRC noted that the Notes should include a roll call, as the town code requires a formal 
roll call at each meeting, with excused and unexcused absences recorded, since repeated unexcused 
absences carry consequences for commission members. The Commission had not been consistently 
doing this and agreed to correct the practice starting with this meeting (see Item 1). 
• The PRC discussed and agreed to the following process for the steps involved for the Meeting notes as 
this will be the formal Record of the meetings:   
1. The Chair will continue to use the AI method for a transcript/first draft, but will send the draft 
transcript to the Recorder after the meeting for edits and comparison to their notes to make the 
notes more streamlined.  
2. The Recorder will do a first pass for names/accuracy, compare the AI notes to their notes, and 
make edits and adjustments to the AI notes so they better reflect the meeting conversations and 
topics. This includes accurate names, titles, and conversation summaries and next steps/tasks.  
3. The Recorder will circulate the draft to PRC commissioners for comments, with a date that 
comments are due within the required week after the meeting. All PRC members, staff, and 
Liaison will send comments/edits to the Recorder within that time and by the identified deadline.  
4. Within 24 hours of the date identified in the circulated notes, the Recorder will incorporate 
these comments and will send the Updated Draft incorporating the comments to all the PRC as 
well as Town Records.   
5. Per standard procedure, at the following PRC monthly meeting, the PRC will discuss the 
previous month’s notes. If no edits are required and the notes are approved, the Recorder will 
email the approved meeting notes to Town Records that same day, and cc all of the PRC.  
Note: If a discussion (or concerns) are raised during draft meeting note approval and the notes 
need to be revised, the recorder will adjust notes based on this discussion. The recorder would 
then circulate the revised notes to the PRC for comments, and the PRC would approve the notes at 
the following meeting. Upon approval, Meeting notes are emailed to Town Records and the PRC.

5. North St. Vrain (Eagle Canyon) Annexation Referral — Planet Bluegrass Presentation 
• Zach Tucker (Planet Bluegrass) presented, remotely and with some audio difficulty, a proposal to annex 
and rezone two parcels near Eagle Canyon: the existing 19661 parcel and adjacent, currently unannexed 
county parcel 19617. The intent is to create a new lodging concept using modular, shipping-container-
based accommodation units (facades to be designed to fit the area's aesthetic; up to roughly 36 units at full 
build-out, phased in over time), along with renovation of the existing MoCon building into a hotel office, 
gym (potentially open to residents as well as guests), a sit-down breakfast restaurant, and additional 
office/gathering space. An existing residence on the unannexed parcel would likely become staff housing or 
additional short-term lodging. 
• Dave Cosgrove clarified the Commission's role: annexation referrals are routed to all boards and 
commissions for comment; the PRC does not make the underlying land-use decision (that authority rests 
with the Planning and Development Commission, an Ecology Advisory Board, and ultimately the Board of 
Trustees), but is asked to weigh in on park- and recreation-related impacts. Detailed site design — 
including exact unit stacking/height and lighting fixtures — will be determined in a subsequent 
development-plan phase, after annexation and rezoning are approved. 
• Several Eagle Canyon-area residents (Doug Steele, Diana Hicks, and Randy Hicks) asked questions and 
raised concerns, including: 
• Requests to see a map of the affected parcels. 
• How and when decisions about unit stacking/height (visual impact on Steamboat Mountain) would be 
made, and whether that falls within PRC's purview (staff clarified it is primarily a 
Planning/development-code matter, with PRC weighing in on park-related impact). 
• Existing and future park capacity, given that town parks are already busy on weekends, and how added 
visitors from the development would be accommodated. 
• Safe pedestrian, bike, and golf-cart connectivity between the site and town parks/trails, given current 
road conditions; interest in CDOT and developer partnership on a sidewalk or trail connection (with a 
precedent cited of a developer funding sidewalk improvements elsewhere in town). 
• Lighting and dark-sky impacts, given the Commission's separate Dark Sky/Urban Night Sky Places 
initiative for Lyons Valley River Park and Bone Park; Zach indicated the developer shares an interest 
in minimizing light pollution, with fixture details to be worked out at the development-plan stage. 
• Whether a conservation easement could be placed on the open space north of the site (town-owned, 
zoned park land, and understood by the Commission to fall within its purview) and on the upslope area 
toward Steamboat Mountain. 
• Drainage and wildlife impacts, with a request that any analysis be performed by an independent, town-
sponsored third party rather than relying solely on the developer's engineers. 
Next Steps- The Commission discussed adding written comments to a briefing document previously 
prepared  and circulated by the Chair, to be submitted as the PRC's referral response. Agreed discussion 
points to include: a request that Planet Bluegrass/the developer consider a conservation easement on the 
upslope open space; support for exploring a trail/sidewalk connectivity partnership with the developer and

6. PRC Recommendation: Special Event Fees and Waivers Policy (Revised Draft) 
• The Chair presented a substantially revised version draft of the special event fees and waivers policy, 
developed with further input from Kim Mitchell (special events staff) and Dave Cosgrove. The draft 
distinguishes two types of charges under the town's special event permit: the application fee, and 
additional/incidental charges (e.g., restroom servicing, cleaning) itemized on Page 8 of the Special Event 
Permit. The revised draft organizes events into three categories: 
Category A — town-sponsored events or events run in partnership with the town: no application 
fee and no additional charges. 
Category B — local nonprofits and community-focused events: application fee applies, but some 
or all additional charges may be waived at the town's discretion. 
Category C — commercial or outside promoters/organizers: full application fee and additional 
charges found on Page 8 of the Special Event Permit apply. 
• The draft explicitly separates fee policy from two related but distinct issues: sponsorship requests (treated 
as a separate town process, not decided under this policy) and economic-impact claims (which can be 
raised with the Economic Development Committee if consistent with the policy, but are not a basis for a fee 
waiver under this framework). The group also discussed how the policy would apply in practice — for 
example, that a non-profit organizer running multiple small events  could submit one combined annual 
application but would still owe the application fee.  
• Commissioners recommended the Board presentation open with a clear problem statement (i.e., that events 
are not currently well differentiated, which has caused inconsistent treatment) before presenting the 
recommended categories. The Chair will add an introductory “problem statement” framing (why a policy is 
needed) to the special event fees presentation. 
• Discussion also returned to a broader, unresolved question on Roles and Responsibilities raised earlier in 
the meeting (see Item 4-B). In order for the PRC to be of best service to the Community, Town Staff and 
BOT, the alignment and process of these requests and the Priorities for the Town needs to be discussed and 
clarified. The PRC discussed how requests like this one are to reach the PRC.  Commissioners agreed this 
needs clearer definition and a clearly identified channel (unlike this request). In the meantime, the 
Commission agreed that they should continue finishing work already underway than leave it unresolved.

8. Town/Staff Update (Dave Cosgrove) 
• Pond water quality: Blue-green algae has been identified in the pond; the town is awaiting concentration 
test results to determine toxicity. A proprietary nano-bubbler part needed for the aeration system has been 
ordered and is expected by Wednesday; staff have been repairing other components in-house to avoid 
vendor costs. The town is looking for a new water-quality service provider after a poor experience with the 
current vendor. 
• Charcoal-to-propane conversion: Staff are evaluating the charcoal-to-propane request raised in July, but 
flagged operational/maintenance and liability concerns with commercial gas grills. Staff have researched 
how other regional park systems (e.g., Boulder County) have addressed similar fire-restriction issues, 
including removing or limiting grills. A possible phased approach was discussed: reducing charcoal grills at 
campsites/tent sites over time, piloting gas grills at shelters only, and considering camp stoves (already 
used at RV sites) as an alternative. No decision has been made; changes would likely be phased in at the 
riskiest locations over the next couple of years. 
• Other items: Installation of the beaver flow monitoring device (originally planned for August 5) was 
postponed due to the algae response and can be rescheduled at the vendor's availability. Festival season 
(including Planet Bluegrass events) went well with minimal impacts and continues to generate parking and 
camping revenue for parks. Staff are planning fire mitigation project work and are waiting for cooler, 
wetter conditions before proceeding, to avoid fire risk during the mitigation work itself. 
Next Steps- Staff will continue pursuing pond water-quality remediation, reschedule installation of the beaver 
flow monitoring device, and continue evaluating a charcoal-to-propane grill pilot.
