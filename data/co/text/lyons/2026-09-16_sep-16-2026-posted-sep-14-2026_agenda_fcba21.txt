Town’s  Water Rights Dedication Policy 
Except for Affordable Housing, Attainable Housing, Mixed Level Housing 
Projects, Commercial Development, or Economic Development 
Opportunities, for each Service Connection one (1) additional unit of 
Lyons C-BT Water shall be required for the Water Rights Dedication. For 
each Service Connection added in accordance with an Affordable 
Housing, Attainable Housing, Mixed Level Housing Projects, Commercial 
Development, or Economic Development Opportunities either one (1) 
additional unit of Lyons C-BT Water, or one and four tenths (1.4) shares 
of capital stock in the Lake McIntosh Reservoir Company, shall be 
required for the Fixed Dedication. 
 
Town’s Water Rights Portfolio 
Lyons currently holds 731 units of C-BT Water. In the current WY 2025 
letter, the Town currently holds 68 shares in Lake McIntosh Reservoir 
Company and is dedicating an additional seven (7) shares for five (5) 
commercial Service Connections and 52.3 shares for 37.3 affordable 
housing Service Connections. Based on the summary of Service 
Connections above and the terms of the 2003 Agreement as amended 
in the 2010 Agreement and the 2019 Agreement, Lyons needs to 
dedicate a total of 708.8 units of Lyons C-BT Water along with 59.3 
shares of Lake McIntosh Reservoir Company water to meet the 
requirements of the Agreements. As such, there are 22.2 units of C-BT 
water owned by Lyons but not yet required for dedication as well as 8.7 
shares of Lake McIntosh Reservoir Company stock.

Minutes from 10/16/2024 UEB “Cash in Lieu for Water Shares Discussion”  
 
BUSINESS 
Cash-in-Lieu for Water Shares  
• Keith Chatﬁeld is a developer who co-owns the remaining 8 unbuilt lots 
in Lyons Valley Park (LVP). He still needs to dedicate CBT water shares 
on 4 of his lots. He secured 4 CBT shares from a private seller for $50K 
each. According to Keith:  
o Neighboring towns average $23K (Longmont, Ft Collins, Mead, 
Loveland, Berthoud) for water share dedication.  
o CBT auction proceeds have averaged $49K.  
o It was noted by the UEB that Lyons is required to dedicate one CBT 
share per ¾" tap, regardless of water use or lot size. This is required by 
Longmont Water. Other towns have their own arrangements.  
• According to Aaron recent auctions place the average CBT share price 
around. There was confusion about who paid the 6% commission to the 
auction house but was settled that taken out of the auction price.  
• Aaron also researched declaring undedicated shares to be surplus. This 
would be useful in establishing full market value of water shares. 
 o There are many obstacles since Northern Water has strict rules 
against using water share sales as a money-maker.  
o It is strongly discouraged for towns to relinquish any water shares. 
o Northern Water does not know of any towns that have done this.  
o Northern Water stated that Lyons would need to work with a lawyer if 
we wanted to continue exploring selling any surplus shares.  
o They also stated that if a farmer, for instance, sold water shares, they 
would not permit him to buy more in the future.  
o Aaron also pointed out that the agreement with Longmont Water does 
not permit us to sell any of our shares. If we were to proceed, they may 
end the agreement and require us to treat our own water.  
o Lee expressed concern for the future if Lyons had relinquished shares 
only to need them again. 
o Trustee Mark Browning expressed willingness to still explore this 
because Lyons is unique in that it has reached build-out (unless it 
annexes).  
• There was confusion over how Longmont views our Lake Macintosh (LM) 
shares. We have repeatedly operated with the understanding that one 
LM share equals 0.7 CBT shares and that we can use LM shares for 
water dedication for “economic development” or “affordable housing. ”  
o Apparently, it’s more complicated than that and Longmont really only 
cares about how many CBT shares we have dedicated.  
o If Summit Housing, Spirit Hound, 317 Evans, and Riverbend recent 
use of LM shares doesn’t offset the need to dedicate equal CBT shares, 
then would we have enough? It’s not a concern with our normal 70% 
water allocation, but it could be a concern in drought years if our 
allocation dropped to 50%.      
• Returning to the priority topic of what share price to recommend to the 
BOT for cash-in-lieu purposes.  
o Cope recommended that we accept the offer from Chatﬁeld of $50K 
for his remaining 4 lots. This is the same as he got from a private sale. 
The justiﬁcation was that it was offered and is a needed $200K that we 
shouldn’t pass up. While it is below the average auction sales price, 
Cope noted that there really isn’t a market that we can participate in 
since Northern Water places so many obstacles in the way of 
municipalities selling shares.  
o Trustee Browning believes that we have a ﬁduciary duty to maximize 
the sales price and that the BOT is compelled to accept no less than 
$60K per share.  
o Meline, Jim Kerr and Hall believe that we should ask for $60K and if 
Chatﬁeld can get them for less elsewhere, he should.  
o Jimm Vosburgh suggested $55K by way of compromise.  
o The ﬁve UEB members in attendance settled on recommending that 
the town accept $56K for each of the four remaining lots in LVP which is 
a little less than the $60K minus 6% commission. It was also noted that 
this sets NO precedent for the future – it only applies to this buyer for 
these lots at this time. The vote was unanimous at 5-0.
