Sec. 13-2-60. - Electric service rates and charges. 
(a) 
Total Monthly Rate. The total monthly rate for service shall be the total of the base rate per 
month, and the additional use rate. 
(b) 
Setting Rates and Fees. Electric rates and fees shall be set by resolution with a public 
hearing upon at least thirty (30) days' notice in the Town's newspaper of record, but no 
more than sixty (60) days from the date of the public hearing. The proposed rates shall be 
open to public inspection at least thirty (30) days prior to the public hearing. Notice of any 
changes to rates or fees shall be sent to any ratepayer not residing in the Town by mail. 
Final rates and fees shall be included in the Town's Rates and Fees Schedule. The Utility 
and Engineering Board will review the rates annually and provide any recommended 
changes to the Board of Trustees by the second board meeting in September. 
(c) 
The form 2S Meter is a meter used in a 240V , single-phase, three wire service. 
(d) 
One (1) meter for each unit unless otherwise approved by the Electric Department, each 
unit provided with electric services shall install and maintain a separate electric meter. 
(e) 
Where older multiple-unit buildings are served from a single meter, the base rate shall be 
multiplied by the number of dwelling units served by the meter, and only one (1) consumer 
shall be responsible for payment of all rates, fees, and charges. 
(f) 
Required exit lighting. Where a separate circuit connected to service lines prior to the 
meter or main metering installation for exit lights is required by ordinance or regulation, the 
electricity delivered and metered for such exit lights shall, for purposes of billing, be added 
to the electricity use measured on the main meter, with the total connected load on the exit 
light circuit added to the measured demand or the main circuit. 
Sec. 13-3-70. - Water service rates and charges. 
(a) 
Rates. Use of water through a Town-approved water meter shall be charged, on a monthly 
basis, a monthly base charge shown below plus a quantity use charge. 
(b) 
Setting Rates and Fees. Water service rates and fees shall be set by resolution with a public 
hearing and included in the Town's Rates and Fees Schedule. The Utility and Engineering 
Board will review the rates annually and provide any recommended changes to the Board of 
Trustees by the second board meeting in September. 
(c) 
Metered rates based on connection size. The rates provided by Subsection (a) above are 
applicable to water service provided by connections or taps one (1) inch or smaller in size. 
Taps or connections larger than one (1) inch in size shall be charged at the same rate 
provided in Subsection (a) above unless otherwise provided by a written agreement 
between the Town and the consumer. 
(d) 
All new water services and connections to be metered. All new applicants for water service 
either inside or outside Town limits shall be required to install meters and shall pay the 
applicable metered water rates. In the event that a water service line must be repaired or 
replaced to any premises which does not obtain water service through a Town-approved 
water meter, a Town-approved water meter shall be installed at the cost of the owner of 
such property within ninety (90) days of such repair or replacement. 
(e) 
No rate reduction for nonresident consumers. Rate reductions for qualiﬁed consumers 
provided by Article 5 of this Chapter shall not be available for consumers of services 
residing outside the Town. 
(f) 
Periodic CPI rate adjustment. On every January 1 of each year, any ﬂat rate or standards 
monthly service charge per unit provided in this Section shall be adjusted in accordance 
with the estimated Consumer Price Index (CPI) as established by the U.S. Bureau of Labor 
Statistics for the Denver/Boulder metropolitan area. Rates shall be rounded to the nearest 
ﬁve-cent increment. 
Sec. 13-4-70. - Wastewater service rates and charges. 
(a) 
Rates and charges for wastewater services. Rates and charges shall be imposed for the use 
of the Town wastewater system and sewage treatment plant. All monthly charges stated in 
this Section are for a "unit" as this term is deﬁned in this Article. 
(b) 
Setting rates and fees. Wastewater service rates and fees shall be set by resolution with a 
public hearing and included in the Town's Rates and Fees Schedule. The Utility and 
Engineering Board will review the rates annually and provide any recommended changes to 
the Board of Trustees by the second board meeting in September. 
(c) 
Surcharge added. A surcharge may be added to the rates and charges speciﬁed in this 
Section in accordance with other provisions of this Article. 
(d) 
Meter adjustments. In the event that (1) a new customer becomes responsible for service 
any time following January 1 of any year, or (2) an existing customer demonstrates, to the 
Town Administrator's satisfaction, that a water leak or malfunctioning meter has 
improperly affected actual use during the most recent period of December 1 to March 1, 
then the Town Administrator may estimate and adjust the customer's meter usage for 
purposes of this Section by using the average usage for all residential users within the Town 
for the entire calendar year. Adjustments for an existing customer shall extend only to bills 
for that calendar year and only for bills subsequent to the time when the error is brought to 
the attention of the Town Administrator. In the event that the customer disagrees with the 
Town Administrator's estimation, the customer may elect to continue payment of monthly 
wastewater charges pursuant to the applicable provision of Paragraph (a)(1) or (a)(2) above 
until such time that actual water consumption and billing amounts are obtained for service 
during the period of December 1 to March 1. 
(e) 
Monthly minimum user fees. In the event that any owner of a wastewater connection or tap 
purchased or otherwise granted or transferred to the owner after March 7, 1998, fails to 
connect or otherwise install such connection or tap and commence wastewater service to 
the owner's property within eighteen (18) months from the date of purchase or grant 
transfer of the wastewater tap, the owner shall pay each month, commencing on the ﬁrst 
monthly billing period following such owner's failure, the minimum monthly rate or charge 
as set forth in this Section. 
(f) 
Periodic CPI rate adjustment. On January 1 of each year, any ﬂat rate or standard monthly 
service charge per unit provided in this Section shall be adjusted in accordance with the 
estimated Consumer Price Index (CPI) as established by the Colorado Office of State 
Planning and Budgeting. Rates shall be rounded to the nearest ﬁve-cent increment. This 
Article shall be amended to reﬂect each periodic CPI rate adjustment, provided that failure 
to amend the rate contained in this Chapter shall not limit, preclude, or prevent the 
operation and application of such periodic adjustment commencing each January 1. 
Sec. 13-7-60. - Stormwater service rates and charges. 
(a) 
There is hereby imposed on each customer a stormwater utility fee. The stormwater utility

Setting rates and fees. Stormwater rates and fees shall be set by resolution with a public 
hearing and including in the Town's Rates and Fees Schedule. The Utility Engineering Board 
will review the rates annually and provide any recommended changes to the Board of 
Trustees by the second board meeting in September. 
(c) 
Exemptions. The following are exempt from the stormwater utility fee imposed by this 
Article: 
(1) 
All public highways, streets, roadways, sidewalks, bike paths and other public rights- of-
way, all Town-owned property, and lakes used as stormwater facilities. 
(2) 
All railroad rights-of-way. 
(d) 
Periodic CPI rate adjustment. On every January 1 of each year, any ﬂat rate or standards 
monthly service charge per unit provided in this Section shall be adjusted in accordance 
with the estimated Consumer Price Index (CPI) as established by the U.S. Bureau of Labor 
Statistics for the Denver/Boulder metropolitan area. Rates shall be rounded to the nearest 
ﬁve-cent increment. 
 
 
 
By Evan Kirk, Project Director, SOG EFC
Stormwater, or simply runoff, is precipitation that does not soak into the ground.
Stormwater management involves controlling the quantity and quality of stormwater as it
eventually enters waterways via ditches, culverts, and stormwater drains. Successful
stormwater management ensures public health and safety. Stormwater management
programs may also be responsible for regulatory compliance, public education, and other
requirements. Thus, it will come as no surprise that managing stormwater is expensive.  
In North Carolina, stormwater is defined by statute as a public enterprise just like water,
sewer, and solid waste. As a public enterprise, local governments can charge a
stormwater utility fee to raise revenues to fund a stormwater management program.
Statewide total revenues from stormwater fees exceeded those from solid waste fees. But
are local governments generating stormwater revenues equitably? What is meant by
“equitable?” Is one stormwater fee structure more equitable than the rest? First, some
definitions and examples of stormwater fee structures. 
Flat Fee
A flat fee means all customers in the same customer class pay the same fee, regardless
of the amount of impervious surface on individual parcels. The flat fee amount may vary
by customer class, which can include those in the example below, as well as multi-family,
agricultural, institutional, etc.
 
Uniform Fee
A uniform fee is also known as a per ERU fee. ERU is an acronym for equivalent
residential unit, which is the average impervious surface area on a residential parcel in a
community. Customers are charged based on the total impervious surface area on their
property. 
In the example below, one ERU is 2,500 square feet of impervious surface area. If a
parcel has 5,000 square feet of impervious surface, they have 2 ERUs and thus would be
charged $6 per month. Like this example, many per ERU fee structures charge 1 ERU for
all residential properties.
Why is there a stormwater fee? The City created a stormwater utility fee to fund the
Federal and State mandated Phase II Stormwater Program. The City’s Stormwater
Management Program is funded completely through this stormwater fee. This is a
separate utility fee, not tax dollars. The residential fee is $3 per month. The
nonresidential fee is based on the amount of impervious surfaces on each property
at $3 per 2,500 square feet of impervious surfaces per month.
Now, what is meant by ‘equitable?’ Equitable stormwater management means that the
cost to serve a property is equal to the stormwater fee charged to that property.  
The cost to provide stormwater services to a property is proportionate to the amount of
runoff coming from that property. The best way to estimate runoff is impervious surface
area. The more impervious surface area on a property, the greater the cost to service that
property. Thus, a fee structure that is based on impervious surface area will be most
equitable, at least in theory, but is this the case in practice? 
In “The Financial Impact of Different Stormwater Fee Types: A Case Study of Two
Municipalities in Virginia,” Amanda Fedorchak calculates multiple fee structures for
Roanoke and Blacksburg, Virginia. Each fee structure provides the same total revenue for
each city. In the case of Roanoke, residential parcels account for 28.6 percent of
impervious surface area. Therefore, a perfectly ‘equitable’ stormwater fee structure would
mean that residential customers pay 28.6 percent of revenues. The same logic would
hold true for all other customer classes. 
In the flat fee by customer class example, residential customers are responsible for 2.4
times their equitable revenue share. In the per ERU example, residential customers are
responsible for 1.1 times their equitable revenue share. The table below shows the
results for residential customers and an aggregate of all other customer classes, which is
labeled as non-residential. Note that the results are similar in Blacksburg.
 
 
Is it really that simple?
The example of Roanoke Virginia shows that charging a flat fee by customer class places
a much higher burden on residential customers, charging them more than twice the
amount that it costs to serve them. So, it would seem obvious that a per ERU structure
would be preferable, yet imperfect, if equity is the goal. However, calculating the exact
amount of impervious surface on every parcel is administratively burdensome and can be
a cost barrier for establishing utilities in small jurisdictions. As a result, many utilities opt to
charge a flat fee for residential customers of one ERU. How does this structure affect
equity?
The table below shows that it barely changes the outcome. This result is likely because
there is not much variation in the impervious area on a residential parcel while the
variation in impervious area across non-residential parcels can be quite substantial.
 
 
Therefore, if equitable revenue responsibility across customer class is important to a local
government and its stakeholders, moving away from a stormwater fee structure that
charges flat fees to all customer classes may be advisable. However, per ERU is not the
only option. Tiered fee structures are also a possibility.
 
Sources
[1] Fedorchak, Amanda. JAWRA. 2017. “The Financial Impact of Different Stormwater
Fee Types: A Case Study of Two Municipalities in Virginia.”

Recommended Lyons Electric Rate Increase 
UEB Meeting September 2, 2026 
 
Summary:  
 
Due to a dramatic increase Lyons electric transmission wholesale costs an increase in Lyons electric rates is 
recommended.  Lyons increased transmission costs are a result of Lyons entering the Southwest Power Pool 
(SPP) regional transmission organization (RTO) effective April 1, 2026.  A 9 percent increase in electric rates is 
recommended for both the base and electric rates to cover a projected annual increase of $140,000 in 
transmission costs. In addition Lyons should consider requiring solar production meters on legacy solar 
installations prior to the current January 2030 requirement. 
 
Background: 
 
On April 1, 2026, the Southwest Power Pool (SPP) expanded its service territory into the Western Interconnection 
of the nation’s power grid. MEAN voted to join SPP at its January 2026 board meeting or which Lyons is a voting 
member. MEAN has member utilities on both sides the interconnect.  This makes SPP the first RTO with services 
spanning two interconnections, positioning its member organizations to benefit from an operationally and 
geographically diverse integrated system.  SPP is a nonprofit corporation mandated by the Federal Energy 
Regulatory Commission to ensure reliable supplies of power, adequate transmission infrastructure and competitive 
wholesale electricity prices on behalf of its members. 
 
Whether the goals of lower energy rates and higher grid reliability are achieved and offset the much higher 
transmission costs still needs to be seen. 
 
Both MEAN and Lyons were surprised by the dramatic increase in transmission cost due to SPP's RTO expansion. 
Last October MEAN projected a 15% increase for WAPA LAP transmission but cautioned that the changes 
resulting from SPP's RTO expansion were unknown.  These are pass through transmission costs on the MEAN bill 
that MEAN does not control. 
 
The MEAN board, including Lyons, voted in January to support MEAN joining SPP's RTO expansion starting April 
1st.  Based on three months of billing, the new SPP transmission rates are projected at 132% higher than the 
previously projected WAPA LAP transmission rates. 
 
Lyons received the first shock with the June MEAN bill. Transmission costs reflect the previous month.  So the 
April changes would first be reflected in the May bill.  Apparently, the changeover did not happen smoothly and the 
May bill reflected the old WAPA LAP rates.   The shocking June bill included the new SPP transmission rates along 
with a large adjustment for the May bill.  Now after recently receiving the July bill and discussing it with MEAN it is 
understood the increase is real and needs to be budgeted with a Lyons electric rate increase. 
 
Table 1 - SPP Transmission Analysis below shows the cost analysis.  An increase in transmission costs of 6.3 
percent was already anticipated in the Lyons 2026 budget process.  The new analysis shows a 132 percent 
increase is now expected above the originally budgeted transmission amounts. Lyons now has a shortfall of 
$250,944 - $108,036 = $142,909.  Lyons 2025 audit lists a revenue amount of $1,638,221.  Increasing this amount 
by the shortfall requires a revenue increase of roughly 9 percent.

The primary drivers of the increased SPP transmission involves the Network Integration Transmission Service 
(NITS) costs. Lyons is in the Loveland Area Projects (LAP) zone. Under SPP the customers in the LAP zone have 
decreased while the revenue requirements have increased by 28 percent. 
 
NITS costs are based on the average annual monthly co-incident peak hour for each customer in the zone.    
Under WAPA, the LAP load amount that Lyons was part of equaled 1,667,357 KWs. Under SPP, effective April 1, 
2026, that zonal load amount has decreased to 1,269,662 KWs, a reduction of approximately 24%. Lyons current 
zonal load amount is 2,329 KWs which is less than 0.183 percent of the zone.  A smaller zonal load means each 
customer bears a larger proportionate share of the revenue requirement, which negatively impacts the cost for 
Lyons and all customers in the zone. This results in Lyons being responsible for a larger percentage of an 
increased bill.  While the magnitude varied, all MEAN communities that are now in SPP-West experienced an 
increase in Transmission costs.  
 
In addition WAPA had an exception for behind the meter generation (BTMG) of less than 150 KWs.  All of Lyons 
solar installations are less than 20 KWs. SPP does not have an exception, so Lyons solar production is now being 
included in co-incident peak hour load calculations.  Whereas MEAN exempts Lyons legacy solar installations for 
the fixed cost of recovery charge (FCRC), SPP will not. MEAN did not report the legacy solar installations for the 
2025 previous year peak load calculation, but is including legacy solar for the 2026 peak load calculation.  This is 
of concern as none of Lyons' legacy solar installations have production meters and without production metering 
the name plate capacity is used in peak load.  Most of Lyons peaks are during periods of none to low solar 
production.  Lyons should therefore consider requiring solar production meters on legacy solar installations prior to 
the current January 2030 requirement.
