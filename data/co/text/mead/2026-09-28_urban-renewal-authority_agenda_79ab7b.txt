Agenda Item Summary  
  
Agenda Date: 9/28/2026  
Subject: MURA Increment Incentive Policy  
Presented by: Robert Rogers, MURA Contract Counsel 
  
Summary: 
The Mead Urban Renewal Authority (“MURA” or the “Authority”) regularly receives requests 
from property owners and developers for financial assistance, most commonly a pledge or 
reimbursement of tax increment revenue (“TIF”) to defray the cost of public improvements 
associated with a development or redevelopment project. To date the Authority has evaluated 
those requests case by case under three informal criteria: demonstrated need, the tax revenue 
the project is projected to generate, and a showing that the project bears an extraordinary cost 
and/or the delivery of extraordinary benefit. The proposed Policy memorializes that practice and 
gives it structure, so that similar requests are evaluated similarly and the record more fully 
supports each decision the Board makes. 
 
The Policy would be adopted for the Authority’s own governance. It would create no right, 
entitlement or cause of action in favor of any applicant, would not expand the Authority’s 
powers under the Colorado Urban Renewal Law, and would not amend or subordinate any 
existing cooperation, revenue sharing or remittance obligation, including the Authority’s 
agreements with the Town, Weld County, the St. Vrain Valley School District, the Mountain 
View Fire Protection District and the Mead Place Metropolitan Districts. Available increment for 
any new request would be measured net of those obligations. The Board would retain full 
discretion to approve, deny, condition or modify any request, and may depart from the Policy in 
a particular matter by stating its reasons in the record. 
 
Key components of the proposed Policy: 
•    Threshold eligibility (Section 5) — location within a plan area, consistency with the urban 
renewal plan and the Town’s regulations, eligible costs only, good standing, site control and 
demonstrated capacity. Construction of the improvements may not have commenced when the 
application is submitted, so that the “but for” showing is not made after the fact. 
•    Three evaluation criteria (Section 6) — demonstrated financial need, supported by a 
sources and uses statement, a pro forma modeled with and without assistance and a return 
analysis; governmental return on the investment, supported by year-by-year revenue 
projections for each overlapping taxing entity together with payback and coverage metrics; and 
extraordinary cost burden or extraordinary public benefit. Criteria One and Two must each be 
satisfied; Criterion Three may be satisfied under either of its two prongs. 
•    Sizing (Section 4.4) — assistance is limited to the least of (1) the financing gap, (2) the 
eligible costs actually incurred and certified, and (3) project increment capacity over the 
approved term. 
•    Form, amount and term (Section 7) — pay-as-you-go reimbursement from increment, on a 
subordinate, non-recourse basis, is the preferred form; guideline ceilings apply to the share of 
eligible costs reimbursed, the share of annual increment pledged and the length of the 
reimbursement term; and an abbreviated Small Project Track scales the submittal to a more 
modest request. 
•    Coordination with metropolitan districts (Section 8) — no improvement may be reimbursed 
Page 3 of 32
twice, the Board is presented with the aggregate public support for a project (Authority 
assistance, district financing, fee credits and grants), and district financing is generally 
expected to be the primary source where district capacity exists. 
•    Process and cost recovery (Sections 9 and 10) — pre-application conference, completeness 
review, staff evaluation, Board study session, consultation with affected taxing entities, 
negotiation and action by resolution, with the Authority’s third-party review costs borne by the 
applicant under a funding and reimbursement agreement. 
•    Required terms of any agreement (Section 11) — a special, limited obligation payable solely 
from pledged increment that is not a multiple fiscal year obligation under TABOR; certification 
of costs; completion, dedication and acceptance; construction milestones and an outside date; 
recapture rights; and allocation to the applicant of the risk that increment falls short. 
•    Exhibits — a two-part application checklist (Exhibit A) scaled to the size of the request, and 
a Board Evaluation Summary (Exhibit B) that reduces the analysis to the figures on which the 
Board acts. 
 
POLICY DETERMINATIONS RESERVED TO THE BOARD 
The draft reserves the following determinations to the Board and shows them in brackets: 
• the maximum percentage of eligible costs that may be reimbursed (Section 7.2(i)); 
• the maximum percentage of annual project increment that may be pledged, with the 
balance retained by the Authority for administration and plan area-wide purposes 
(Section 7.2(ii)); 
• the maximum reimbursement term, capped in any event by the remaining term of the 
applicable plan (Section 7.2(iii)); 
• whether interest should accrue on unreimbursed eligible costs — the draft provides that 
it does not (Section 7.2(iv)); 
• the dollar threshold for the Small Project Track (Section 7.4); 
• the time allowed for completeness review (Section 9.3); and 
• the amount of the application fee and of the initial deposit (Sections 10.1 and 10.2). 
Staff requests the Board’s direction on these items at the town hall discussion so that they may 
be resolved in the draft presented for formal consideration.  
Financial Considerations: 
Adoption of the Policy commits no Authority funds and pledges no increment. Its financial effect 
is prospective and protective. The Authority’s third-party review costs — legal, financial 
advisory, market and engineering peer review — are shifted to the applicant through a non-
refundable application fee, a replenishing deposit and a funding and reimbursement 
agreement. Assistance is capped by the lesser-of test and by the guideline limits in Section 7.2, 
and, as drafted, interest does not accrue on unreimbursed costs. Sections 4.5 and 8 prevent 
the same improvement from being funded both by the Authority and by an overlapping 
metropolitan district, a risk of practical significance given the overlap with the Mead Place 
Metropolitan Districts. 
 
Sections 2.4 and 11(h) address the uncertainty of the increment itself. The amount actually 
received depends on the methodology by which base and increment values are proportionately 
adjusted, on statutory and constitutional changes to assessment rates and valuation, and on 
the pace of development. The Policy discloses that uncertainty, treats all projections as 
estimates, and places the risk of a shortfall on the applicant rather than on the Authority.

SECTION 1.  PURPOSE AND INTENT 
1.1 Creation and mission.  The Mead Urban Renewal Authority (the “Authority” or “MURA”) was 
created by the Board of Trustees of the Town of Mead, Colorado (the “Town”) in 2016 pursuant to the 
Colorado Urban Renewal Law, C.R.S. § 31-25-101, et seq. (the “Urban Renewal Law”). The 
Authority’s purpose is to eliminate and prevent the spread of blighted conditions within the Town, to 
assist private development and redevelopment that would not otherwise occur, and to plan and 
construct public improvements necessary to achieve those objectives. 
1.2 The need for a written policy.  The Authority regularly receives requests from property owners, 
developers and prospective developers (each, an “Applicant”) for financial assistance, most commonly 
in the form of a pledge or reimbursement of tax increment revenue, to defray the cost of public 
improvements associated with a development or redevelopment project. To date the Authority has 
evaluated those requests case by case under informal criteria. This Policy memorializes and refines 
that practice. 
1.3 Objectives.  This Policy is intended to: 
(a) give Applicants predictable, transparent guidance regarding the information the Authority 
requires and the standards it applies; 
(b) give the Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) a consistent analytical framework, so 
that similar requests are evaluated similarly and the record supports each decision; 
(c) ensure that assistance is limited to what a project genuinely requires and is supported by 
a documented public return; and 
(d) demonstrate to the Town, Weld County, the St. Vrain Valley School District, the 
Mountain View Fire Protection District and other overlapping taxing entities that the 
Authority’s use of tax increment financing is disciplined and accountable. 
1.4 Guideline; not an entitlement.  This Policy is a statement of the Authority’s general approach 
and is adopted for the Authority’s own governance. It does not create, and shall not be construed to 
create, any right, entitlement, expectation, property interest, contract, or cause of action in favor of any 
Applicant or any third party, and no Applicant may rely on this Policy as a representation that an 
increment award will be provided. The Board retains full discretion to approve, deny, condition, or 
modify any request, and may decline to provide assistance for any lawful reason, including the 
unavailability of revenue, competing priorities within a plan area, or the cumulative effect of prior 
commitments. Satisfaction of every criterion set forth in this Policy does not obligate the Authority to 
provide assistance. 
SECTION 2.  LEGAL FRAMEWORK 
2.1 Statutory limits.  All assistance provided by the Authority must be for an undertaking or activity 
authorized by the Urban Renewal Law, must be located within (or, in the case of certain improvements, 
must serve) a duly adopted urban renewal plan area, and must be funded from sources lawfully

available to the Authority via it revenue sharing agreements with overlapping taxing jurisdictions and 
otherwise. Nothing in this Policy expands the Authority’s powers under the Urban Renewal Law. 
2.2 Applicable plans.  This Policy applies to requests within the 2016 Mead Urban Renewal Area 
and within any urban renewal plan area hereafter adopted or modified by the Town Board. Where a 
specific urban renewal plan establishes objectives, priorities or limitations, those plan provisions 
control over this Policy. 
2.3 Existing agreements preserved.  This Policy is subject to, and does not amend, supersede or 
modify, any existing cooperation agreement, tax sharing agreement, TIF remittance agreement, 
intergovernmental agreement or reimbursement obligation to which the Authority is a party, including 
without limitation its agreements with the Town, Weld County, the St. Vrain Valley School District, 
the Mountain View Fire Protection District, the Mead Place Metropolitan Districts, and any future 
cooperation agreements with metropolitan districts with the Town. Available increment for any new 
request will be evaluated net of those obligations. 
2.4 Uncertainty of increment.  The amount of tax increment revenue actually received by the 
Authority depends on matters outside the Authority’s control, including the methodology by which the 
County Assessor and the State Property Tax Administrator proportionately adjust base and increment 
values, statutory and constitutional changes to assessment rates and valuation, obligations to remit 
additional revenue arising from voter-approved mill levy increases and future legislation. All 
projections prepared under this Policy are estimates only. The Authority does not guarantee the receipt 
of increment and will not assume the risk that increment falls short of projections. 
SECTION 3.  DEFINITIONS 
As used in this Policy: 
“Assistance” means any financial support provided by the Authority to or for the benefit of an 
Applicant or a Project, in any of the forms described in Section 7. 
“District” means a Title 32 metropolitan district or other special district organized under 
Colorado law whose boundaries include all or part of a Project. 
“Eligible Costs” means the costs of public improvements and other undertakings and activities 
that the Authority is authorized to finance or reimburse under the Urban Renewal Law and the 
applicable urban renewal plan, including reasonable design, engineering, permitting, 
construction management and financing costs directly allocable to those improvements. 
Eligible Costs do not include the cost of vertical or private improvements, tenant finish, 
furniture, fixtures and equipment, working capital, marketing, developer fee, developer 
overhead or profit, or carrying costs on private improvements. 
“Extraordinary Cost Burden” has the meaning given in Section 6.3(a). 
“Extraordinary Public Benefit” has the meaning given in Section 6.3(b).

“Financing Gap” means the amount by which the Total Project Cost exceeds the sum of (i) 
the debt the Project can reasonably support on then-prevailing market terms and (ii) the equity 
a reasonable investor would commit to the Project at a market rate of return, as demonstrated 
under Section 6.1. 
“Project” means the development or redevelopment for which Assistance is requested,

“Project Increment” means the tax increment revenue actually received by the Authority and

the Authority to another taxing entity or District with respect to that revenue. 
“Total Project Cost” means all hard and soft costs of the Project, including land, from all 
sources, whether or not eligible for Assistance. 
SECTION 4.  GENERAL PRINCIPLES 
4.1 Gap financing of last resort.  Assistance is intended to bridge a demonstrated financial gap that 
prevents an otherwise desirable Project from proceeding. It is not intended to enhance an already-
feasible Project’s return, to substitute for private capital that is reasonably available, or to replace other 
public funding for which the Project qualifies. 
4.2 Public improvements only.  Assistance is provided for Eligible Costs. The Authority does not 
fund the Applicant’s private improvements or profit, although the Authority recognizes that 
reimbursement of Eligible Costs necessarily improves the Project’s overall economics. 
4.3 Pay-as-you-go preferred.  The Authority’s preferred structure is reimbursement of certified 
Eligible Costs from Project Increment as and when collected, on a subordinate, non-recourse basis. 
The Authority will consider the issuance of bonds or other borrowing only where the Board finds the 
transaction to be in the Authority’s interest, the obligation is payable solely from pledged increment, 
and the Authority is not exposed to repayment risk. 
4.4 Sizing — the lesser-of test.  The maximum Assistance the Authority will consider is the least of: 
(i) the Financing Gap; (ii) the Eligible Costs actually incurred, paid and certified; and (iii) the Project 
Increment capacity over the approved term, applying the caps in Section 7.2. 
4.5 No double recovery.  An Eligible Cost that is funded, reimbursed, credited or financed from 
another public source will not also be reimbursed by the Authority, to the extent so funded. See Section 
8. 
4.6 Risk allocation.  The Applicant bears the entrepreneurial risk of the Project and the risk that 
increment is generated more slowly, or in a lesser amount, than projected. 
4.7 Proportionate review.  The depth of review should be proportionate to the size and complexity 
of the request. Section 7.4 provides an abbreviated track for smaller requests so that a modest, well-
documented request is not burdened with institutional underwriting requirements.

4.8 Net public benefit.  Taken as a whole, a Project receiving Assistance should leave the Town, the 
Authority and the overlapping taxing entities measurably better off than they would be without it. 
SECTION 5.  THRESHOLD ELIGIBILITY REQUIREMENTS 
A request will not be advanced for substantive evaluation under Section 6 unless each of the following 
is satisfied. The Board may waive any threshold requirement for good cause stated in the record. 
(a) Location. The Project is located within an existing urban renewal plan area, or the 
Applicant has requested and the Town Board has initiated the adoption or modification of 
a plan area that would include the Project. 
(b) Consistency. The Project is consistent with the applicable urban renewal plan, the Town’s 
comprehensive plan, and the Town’s zoning and development regulations, or the Applicant 
is actively pursuing the entitlements necessary to make it so. 
(c) Eligible Costs. Assistance is sought only for Eligible Costs. 
(d) Timing. Construction of the improvements for which Assistance is sought has not 
commenced as of the date the application is submitted. A “but for” showing is generally 
not credible after the fact, and the Authority will not reimburse costs incurred before the 
Board has had an opportunity to act. 
(e) Good standing. The Applicant and its affiliates are current on all property taxes, 
assessments and fees on the Project site and are not in default under any agreement with 
the Town, the Authority or any District. 
(f) Site control. The Applicant owns the Project site or holds an enforceable contractual right 
to acquire or develop it. 
(g) Capacity. The Applicant demonstrates the development experience and financial 
capability to complete the Project. 
(h) Not a pre-existing obligation. The Assistance is not sought for improvements that the 
Applicant is already obligated to construct at its sole cost under the Town’s development 
regulations, a development agreement, an annexation agreement or a similar instrument — 
unless the Applicant demonstrates that the obligation itself constitutes an Extraordinary 
Cost Burden under Section 6.3(a), and the Town concurs in writing that reimbursement is 
consistent with that obligation. 
SECTION 6.  EVALUATION CRITERIA 
Every request will be evaluated against the three criteria in this Section. Criteria One and Two must 
each be satisfied. Criterion Three may be satisfied through either of its two alternative prongs. 
6.1  Criterion One — Demonstrated Financial Need (the “But For” Showing) 
The Applicant must demonstrate that the Project cannot reasonably proceed, in the form and at the 
quality proposed, without Assistance. The Applicant shall submit: 
Page 10 of 32
MURA INCREMENT INCENTIVE POLICY |  Page 6 of 18 
(a) Eligible Cost schedule. An itemized schedule of Eligible Costs by improvement, 
supported by an engineer’s opinion of probable cost or competitive bids, identifying for 
each item (i) whether it is required by the Town as a condition of approval, (ii) whether it 
is oversized or extended beyond the Project’s own needs, and (iii) what property outside 
the Project it serves. 
(b) Cost comparison. A statement of Eligible Costs as a percentage of Total Project Cost, 
together with the Applicant’s explanation of how that burden compares to a comparable 
project on an unconstrained site in the region. This comparison is the beginning of the 
analysis, not the end of it; a high ratio invites the question but does not answer it. 
(c) Sources and uses; pro forma. A complete sources-and-uses statement and a development 
and operating pro forma, modeled both with and without the requested Assistance, 
disclosing the key market assumptions on which it depends (absorption, rents or sale prices, 
escalation, vacancy, capitalization rate, interest rate, loan-to-cost, and construction period). 
(d) Return measures. Calculation of the Applicant’s return with and without Assistance, 
using measures appropriate to the product type — for income property, unleveraged yield 
on cost, stabilized return and internal rate of return; for for-sale product, gross margin and 
return on cost — together with evidence of the return a reasonable investor or lender would 
require for a project of comparable type and risk. 
(e) Statement of the Financing Gap. A quantified statement of the Financing Gap and of the 
amount of Assistance requested, reconciled to the lesser-of test in Section 4.4. 
(f) Independent review. The Authority may retain a financial advisor, appraiser or other 
consultant to review the submission, at the Applicant’s expense under Section 10. The 
Applicant shall cooperate with that review and provide reasonable supporting 
documentation. 
(g) Duty to update. If assumptions material to the Financing Gap change before the Authority 
and the Applicant execute a definitive agreement, the Applicant shall promptly notify the 
Authority and submit a revised analysis. 
6.2  Criterion Two — Governmental Return on the Investment 
The Applicant must demonstrate the revenue the Project is projected to generate for the Authority, the 
Town and each overlapping taxing entity, so that the Board can weigh the public return against the 
public investment. The Applicant shall submit: 
(a) Revenue projection by entity. A year-by-year projection, over the remaining term of the 
applicable plan, of incremental property tax revenue attributable to the Project, allocated 
separately to the Authority, the Town, Weld County, the St. Vrain Valley School District, 
the Mountain View Fire Protection District, any other overlapping taxing entity, and any 
overlapping District.

MURA INCREMENT INCENTIVE POLICY |  Page 7 of 18 
(b) Other municipal revenue. A projection of incremental Town sales and use tax (including 
construction use tax), building permit and plan review fees, tap and system development 
fees, and any other material Town revenue attributable to the Project. 
(c) Three views of the increment. The property tax projection shall be presented (i) gross, (ii) 
net of the requested Assistance, and (iii) net of the Authority’s remittance and sharing 
obligations under existing agreements, so that the Board can see what each entity actually 
receives during and after the reimbursement period. 
(d) Service impacts. A good-faith identification of the incremental demand the Project will 
place on Town and overlapping entity services, including public safety, fire, streets, water 
and wastewater, drainage, parks and schools, and of any mitigation the Project provides. 
(e) Summary metrics. The submission shall reduce the foregoing to a small number of figures 
the Board can act on: total projected public revenue over the term; the ratio of total 
projected public revenue to the Assistance requested; the projected payback period; and 
projected annual coverage of Project Increment over the annual reimbursement obligation. 
The Authority does not adopt a fixed minimum return ratio, because the appropriate ratio varies with 
product type, risk and the strength of the Criterion Three showing. The Board expects, however, to be 
able to state on the record the approximate public return generated for each dollar of increment pledged. 
6.3  Criterion Three — Extraordinary Cost Burden or Extraordinary Public Benefit 
Assistance is reserved for Projects that are distinguishable from ordinary development. This criterion 
is satisfied by a showing under either subsection (a) or subsection (b). 
(a)  Extraordinary Cost Burden.  The Project bears public improvement costs materially in excess of 
those borne by comparable development, by reason of site conditions, location, or public requirements 
that exceed the Project’s own needs. Factors the Board may consider include: 
• off-site or oversized infrastructure that serves property beyond the Project; 
• regional or shared detention, drainage or stormwater facilities; 
• arterial or collector roadway, intersection, signal, or Colorado Department of 
Transportation-required improvements; 
• oversized water, sewer or reclaimed water mains, lift stations, or storage; 
• utility relocation or undergrounding, railroad or highway crossings, and irrigation ditch 
crossings, relocations or agreements; 
• environmental remediation, demolition, or removal of derelict structures or debris; 
• adverse soils, groundwater, geotechnical or floodplain conditions requiring 
extraordinary mitigation; 
• parcel assembly, irregular configuration, or absence of adjacent infrastructure; and 
• improvements required by an adopted Town plan that exceed what the Project itself 
generates demand for.

MURA INCREMENT INCENTIVE POLICY |  Page 8 of 18 
(b)  Extraordinary Public Benefit.  The Project confers a benefit on residents, businesses and taxpayers 
within the plan area that is materially greater than the benefit ordinary development confers. Factors 
the Board may consider include: 
• public improvements usable by or serving the broader public rather than only the 
Project; 
• infrastructure capacity that unlocks or accelerates development of adjacent property; 
• dedication or construction of a public facility, park, trail, plaza, or civic amenity; 
• attainable or income-restricted housing, with an enforceable affordability covenant; 
• long term primary employment, or a use that fills a documented gap in goods or 
services available in the Town; 
• preservation or rehabilitation of a historic or landmark structure; 
• elimination of blighting conditions disproportionate to the Project’s scale; and 
• a demonstrable catalytic effect on the balance of the plan area. 
(c)  Documentation.  A showing under this criterion must be supported by more than narrative. The 
Applicant shall quantify the extraordinary burden or benefit — for example, by isolating the cost of 
the oversizing, by identifying the acreage or number of units the improvement serves beyond the 
Project, or by describing the enforceable commitment that secures the benefit. 
(d)  What is not extraordinary.  The Authority does not regard as extraordinary the ordinary costs of 
development, including internal streets and drives, on-site utilities and grading, standard landscaping 
and signage, and ordinary permit and impact fees. 
6.4  Additional Considerations 
In addition to the three criteria, the Board may consider any of the following: 
• the quality of the Project’s design, materials and site planning; 
• the Applicant’s readiness to proceed and the certainty of the development schedule; 
• the Applicant’s track record on comparable projects and with public partners; 
• the effect of the Project on existing Mead businesses, including the extent to which 
projected sales are new to the Town rather than transferred within it; 
• the Authority’s prior commitments and the cumulative amount of increment already 
pledged in the plan area; 
• whether the Applicant has pursued other reasonably available funding, including 
District financing, Town fee credits, and state, federal and regional grant programs; 
and 
• the position of the Town and overlapping taxing entities.

SECTION 7.  FORM, AMOUNT AND TERM OF ASSISTANCE 
7.1 Available forms.  The Authority may provide Assistance in any of the following forms, or a 
combination of them: 
(a) reimbursement of certified Eligible Costs from Project Increment on a pay-as-you-go 
basis (the Authority’s preferred form); 
(b) a direct payment or grant, where the request is modest and the Board finds an immediate 
payment more efficient than a long-term reimbursement obligation; 
(c) payment on the Applicant’s behalf of a Town fee or cash-in-lieu obligation, where that 
obligation funds an eligible public improvement; 
(d) design, construction or acquisition of public improvements by the Authority directly; and 
(e) issuance of bonds or other obligations payable solely from pledged increment, subject to 
Section 4.3. 
7.2 Guideline limits.  Absent a finding of exceptional circumstances stated in the record, Assistance 
will be structured so that it does not exceed: 
(i) [____]% of Eligible Costs; 
(ii) [____]% of the Project Increment received in any year, with the balance retained by the 
Authority for administration and for plan area-wide purposes; 
(iii) a term equal to the lesser of [____] years from the first reimbursement payment or the 
remaining term of the applicable urban renewal plan; and 
(iv) interest shall not accrue on unreimbursed Eligible Costs. 
7.3 Downward flexibility.  These are ceilings, not entitlements. The Board may approve Assistance 
at any level at or below these limits, or none at all. 
7.4 Small Project Track.  A request for total Assistance of $[__________] or less may be reviewed 
under an abbreviated process. The Applicant shall complete Part 1 of Exhibit A only, and the Executive 
Director may waive the independent review under Section 6.1(f) and reduce or waive the deposit under 
Section 10. The three criteria still apply, but the depth of documentation should be scaled to the request. 
SECTION 8.  COORDINATION WITH OVERLAPPING METROPOLITAN 
DISTRICTS 
8.1 The overlap.  Many Projects within the Authority’s plan areas lie within, or are expected to lie 
within, a District that also finances public improvements and may reimburse the Applicant for the same 
improvements from its own mill levy. The Board evaluates the total public support for a Project, not 
the Authority’s component in isolation. 
8.2 No double reimbursement.  An Eligible Cost is eligible for Authority Assistance only to the 
extent it is not reimbursed, financed, credited or funded by a District, by a Town fee credit or waiver, 
Page 14 of 32
MURA INCREMENT INCENTIVE POLICY |  Page 10 of 18 
by another urban renewal authority or public entity, or by a state, federal or regional grant. Where a 
cost is partially funded from another public source, only the unfunded balance is eligible. 
8.3 Certification.  Each request for reimbursement must be accompanied by a certificate of the 
Applicant, and where applicable of the District, identifying all public sources applied or expected to 
be applied to the certified costs and confirming that no portion has been or will be reimbursed twice. 
The Authority shall have audit rights sufficient to verify the certificate. 
8.4 Aggregate support.  The Board will be presented with the aggregate public support for the Project 
— Authority Assistance, District reimbursement or financing, fee credits and grants — expressed as a 
percentage of Eligible Costs and of Total Project Cost. 
8.5 Order of resort.  Where a District exists or is proposed with the mill levy authorization, debt 
authorization and revenue capacity to finance the improvements, the Authority will generally expect 
District financing to be the primary source and Authority Assistance to be supplemental. The Board 
recognizes that this expectation must yield where the improvements benefit property outside the 
District, where District capacity is genuinely unavailable within the Project’s timeframe, or where the 
improvements are of a type more appropriately financed by the Authority. 
8.6 Coordination with formation.  Where a Project contemplates formation or inclusion of a District, 
the Authority will coordinate with the Town so that the service plan, any cooperation agreement, and 
any Authority agreement are consistent with one another and with this Policy. 
SECTION 9.  APPLICATION AND REVIEW PROCESS 
9.1 Step 1 — Pre-application conference.  Before submitting an application, a prospective Applicant 
should meet with the Executive Director and staff to review the Project, the applicable plan area, this 
Policy, and the likely scope of submittal and review costs. The conference is informal and does not 
commit the Authority. 
9.2 Step 2 — Application.  The Applicant submits a complete application in the form of Exhibit A, 
together with the application fee and deposit required by Section 10 and an executed funding and 
reimbursement agreement. 
9.3 Step 3 — Completeness review.  Staff reviews the application for completeness within [____] 
business days and notifies the Applicant of any deficiency. Substantive review does not begin until the 
application is complete and the deposit is funded. 
9.4 Step 4 — Substantive review.  Staff, with the assistance of the Authority’s legal counsel, financial 
advisor and other consultants, as appropriate, evaluates the request against Sections 5 and 6 and 
prepares an evaluation summary in substantially the form of Exhibit B. 
9.5 Step 5 — Board study session.  The Applicant presents the Project to the Board at a study session 
or regular meeting. The Board may provide non-binding direction regarding whether and on what 
general terms it would consider Assistance. No indication given at this stage binds the Authority.

MURA INCREMENT INCENTIVE POLICY |  Page 11 of 18 
9.6 Step 6 — Taxing entity consultation.  Where the request would materially affect revenue 
otherwise flowing to an overlapping taxing entity, or where a new plan area or plan modification is 
required, the Authority will consult with the affected entities and, where required by the Urban 
Renewal Law, undertake the applicable notice, impact report and negotiation process. 
9.7 Step 7 — Negotiation.  If the Board directs, staff and counsel negotiate a redevelopment, 
reimbursement or other definitive agreement containing the terms required by Section 11. 
9.8 Step 8 — Board action.  The Board considers the proposed agreement at a public meeting and 
acts by resolution. The resolution should recite the Board’s findings under each of the three criteria 
and the basis for the amount and term approved. 
SECTION 10.  FEES, DEPOSITS AND AUTHORITY COSTS 
10.1 Application fee.  Each application shall be accompanied by a non-refundable application fee of 
$[________], which may be reduced or waived by the Executive Director for a request on the Small 
Project Track. 
10.2 Deposit and reimbursement agreement.  Before substantive review begins, the Applicant shall 
deposit $[________] with the Authority and execute an agreement obligating the Applicant to 
reimburse the Authority for all third-party costs it incurs in evaluating and documenting the request, 
including legal, financial advisory, market and feasibility study, engineering peer review, and, where 
required, conditions survey, impact report and plan preparation costs. The Applicant shall replenish 
the deposit on demand. Unexpended funds are returned upon completion or withdrawal. 
10.3 Treatment of Authority costs.  The Board may, in its discretion, permit some or all of the 
Authority’s reimbursed costs to be treated as Eligible Costs recoverable by the Applicant if Assistance 
is ultimately approved. 
10.4 Administrative fee.  Any agreement may provide for an ongoing administrative fee or retainage 
payable to the Authority from Project Increment, in an amount reflecting the Authority’s cost of 
administering, accounting for and reporting on the Assistance. 
SECTION 11.  REQUIRED TERMS OF ANY AGREEMENT 
Any agreement providing Assistance shall include, at a minimum, provisions addressing the following. 
Authority counsel shall confirm that each item is addressed or expressly waived by the Board. 
(a) Limited obligation. The Authority’s obligation is special and limited, payable solely from 
the pledged Project Increment when and if received; it is not a debt or general obligation 
of the Authority, the Town, or any taxing entity, is not payable from any other fund, and 
does not constitute a multiple fiscal year direct or indirect financial obligation within the 
meaning of Article X, Section 20 of the Colorado Constitution.

MURA INCREMENT INCENTIVE POLICY |  Page 12 of 18 
(b) Certification of costs. Reimbursement only of Eligible Costs actually incurred and paid, 
certified by the Applicant and verified by the Authority, supported by invoices, evidence 
of payment, lien waivers and, where applicable, engineer certification. 
(c) Completion and acceptance. Construction of the improvements to Town standards, and 
conveyance, dedication and acceptance of the improvements by the Town or applicable 
entity, as a condition of reimbursement. 
(d) Milestones. Deadlines for commencement and completion of construction, with 
automatic termination or reduction of the Authority’s obligation if the deadlines are not 
met and no extension has been granted. 
(e) Outside date. A fixed date after which the Authority’s reimbursement obligation 
terminates, whether or not the full amount has been paid, with any unpaid balance 
extinguished. 
(f) Recapture. The Authority’s right to withhold, offset or recover payments in the event of 
material misrepresentation, failure to complete the Project substantially as approved, or 
failure to satisfy a public benefit commitment on which the approval was based. 
(g) Valuation protection. Where the Board deems it appropriate, a covenant establishing a 
minimum assessed value or restricting the Applicant’s ability to protest valuation below an 
agreed floor, and a prohibition on seeking an abatement, refund or tax exemption that 
would impair the increment. 
(h) Revenue risk. Express acknowledgment that the Applicant bears all risk of shortfall in 
increment, including risk arising from assessment rate changes, valuation methodology, 
legislation, and the pace of development. 
(i) No double recovery. The representations, certifications and audit rights described in 
Section 8. 
(j) Reporting cooperation. The Applicant’s obligation to provide the information the 
Authority needs to satisfy its annual reporting and audit obligations under the Urban 
Renewal Law. 
(k) Insurance and indemnity. Customary insurance requirements and an indemnity running 
to the Authority, the Town and their officers, employees and agents, together with a 
statement that no third-party beneficiaries are intended. 
(l) Assignment. Restrictions on assignment, with reasonable accommodation for collateral 
assignment to a construction or permanent lender and for assignment to an affiliate or a 
District, subject to Authority consent. 
(m) Default and remedies. Cure periods, and limitation of the Authority’s exposure to 
withholding of payments and termination rather than damages. 
(n) Governmental immunity. Preservation of the Authority’s rights, immunities and 
protections under the Colorado Governmental Immunity Act.

SECTION 12.  CONFIDENTIALITY AND PUBLIC RECORDS 
12.1 The Authority is subject to public records law.  The Authority is subject to the Colorado Open 
Records Act, C.R.S. § 24-72-201, et seq. Material submitted to the Authority is presumptively a public 
record. Applicants should submit financial information with that understanding. 
12.2 Marking and assertion of exemptions.  An Applicant should clearly and specifically mark 
material it contends is confidential commercial or financial information, trade secret, or otherwise 
exempt, and should state the basis for the claim. The Authority will assert applicable exemptions where 
it reasonably may do so and will notify the Applicant of any request seeking material so marked, but 
the Authority cannot guarantee that any material will be withheld and will comply with any order of a 
court of competent jurisdiction. 
12.3 Executive session.  The Board may consider matters concerning the negotiation of an agreement 
in executive session to the extent permitted. Any determination, direction or approval must be made in 
open session. 
SECTION 13.  CONFLICTS OF INTEREST 
13.1 Disclosure and recusal.  Commissioners shall comply with the conflict of interest provisions 
of the Urban Renewal Law and with Part 1 of Article 18 of Title 24, C.R.S., and with any applicable 
Town policy. A Commissioner with a personal or private interest in a matter under this Policy shall 
disclose that interest on the record and shall not vote on, or attempt to influence or participate in any 
Board discussions regarding the matter. 
13.2 Entity representatives.  A Commissioner who serves on the Board as the designated 
representative of an overlapping taxing entity is not, by reason of that representation alone, disqualified 
from participating in matters affecting increment allocable to that entity. 
SECTION 14.  GENERAL PROVISIONS 
14.1 Reservation of discretion.  Nothing in this Policy limits the Board’s discretion. The Board may 
waive, modify or depart from any provision of this Policy in a particular matter, and should state the 
reason for doing so in the record. 
14.2 No effect on prior commitments.  This Policy applies prospectively to requests submitted after 
its effective date and does not alter any agreement or commitment of the Authority in effect on that 
date. 
14.3 Amendment.  This Policy may be amended by resolution of the Board at any time. 
14.5 Severability.  If any provision of this Policy is held invalid, the remainder shall continue in 
effect.
