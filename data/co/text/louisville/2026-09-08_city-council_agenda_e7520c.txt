Subject:  Joint Meeting with Cultural Advisory Board 
Date: September 8, 2026 
Prepared By: Brandi Cummings, Director 
Presented By: Cultural Advisory Board 
 
Summary:  
This joint meeting between the City Council and the Cultural Advisory Board (CAB) is 
intended to create an open, collaborative discussion about the role of arts, culture, 
events, and creative expression in building a vibrant and connected Louisville 
community. 
The Cultural Advisory Board advises the Arts and Events Division of the Cultural 
Services Department and helps ensure that Louisville’s community is reflected in 
citywide events, celebrations, arts programming, and cultural initiatives. Through this 
work, the Board supports opportunities for residents to gather, experience different 
cultures and perspectives, and build connection through shared community 
experiences. 
The CAB also advances the City of Louisville’s commitment to strengthening the local 
arts ecosystem. The Board helps steward and grow Louisville’s public art collection and 
administers the City’s annual Art Grant program, which provides direct support to artists 
and arts organizations and helps remove barriers to creating and presenting art in 
Louisville. 
During the joint meeting, CAB members will share perspectives on the community’s 
evolving cultural needs, opportunities to strengthen Louisville’s arts ecosystem, and 
priorities for public art, community events, celebrations, and artist support. Council will 
have an opportunity to share its priorities and perspectives and discuss how the City 
can continue fostering an inclusive, welcoming, and vibrant cultural environment. 
The goal of the conversation is to exchange perspectives, identify areas of alignment, 
and strengthen collaboration between City Council and the Cultural Advisory Board 
around Louisville’s near- and long-term cultural priorities. 
 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
City Council and the Cultural Advisory Board meet jointly each year to discuss priorities 
for arts, culture, and community events and to maintain strong coordination between 
Council and the Board. 
Page 3 of 34
 
Throughout the year, the Cultural Advisory Board works alongside the Arts and Events 
Division to help shape community experiences that reflect the breadth of Louisville’s 
residents. This includes advising on citywide events and cultural celebrations such as 
Summer Concerts in the Park, the World Music Series, Transgender Day of 
Remembrance, and other community programs and gatherings. 
 
The Board also plays an important role in cultivating Louisville as a place where artists 
can create, contribute, and thrive. Through oversight of the City’s growing public art 
collection and administration of annual Art Grants, the CAB helps expand access to the 
arts, reduce barriers for local creatives, and build an arts ecosystem that contributes to 
Louisville’s identity and sense of place. 
 
This year’s joint meeting provides an opportunity for Council and the CAB to consider 
how these efforts can continue to evolve, where opportunities exist to deepen 
community participation and representation, and how arts and culture can contribute to 
Louisville’s broader goals for community connection, vibrancy, and quality of life.

Subject:  Training: Workplace Respect — Preventing and Ending Harassment 
— Presented by Chuck Passaglia 
Informational only. While Council may ask questions and have discussion, no action will 
be taken by Council. There will be no public comment section.  
Date: September 8, 2026 
Prepared By: Jeff Monzingo, Director 
Presented By: Chuck Passaglia 
Employment Law Attorney 
 
Summary:  
The City of Louisville is committed to maintaining an environment where employees, 
elected and appointed officials, volunteers, residents, and others who interact with the 
City are treated with professionalism, dignity, and respect. This commitment is reflected 
in City policies that emphasize a safe working environment, trust and respect, open 
communication, and high-quality public service.   
 
This training will be presented by Chuck Passaglia, a respected local Employment Law 
Attorney. Mr. Passaglia founded Employment Law Solutions, Inc., which specializes in 
providing advice and counsel in employment matters, conducting impartial and thorough 
workplace investigations, and training the entire workforce in critical employment law 
compliance topics, such as the laws of the workplace, ethics and standards of conduct, 
managing employees' absences, compensation and benefits, and harassment 
prevention. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
In 2023, Colorado significantly amended its workplace harassment laws through the 
Protecting Opportunities and Workers' Rights (POWR) Act. Most notably, harassment 
no longer needs to be "severe or pervasive" to potentially violate Colorado law. These 
changes make awareness, prevention, and early intervention increasingly important. 
 
The City's Harassment Policy expressly applies to members of City Council, and all 
other City-appointed commissions, boards, and committees. The policy prohibits 
unlawful harassment, and reinforces the importance of reporting these concerns and 
preventing retaliation.  
 
The City Council and Board and Commission Members hold positions that regularly 
interact with employees, residents, volunteers, contractors, and one another. A shared 
understanding of appropriate conduct helps support the safe, respectful, and 
professional environment established through City policy.

Analysis: 
The training will provide practical information, including: 
• Bias: Understanding conscious and unconscious bias and common mistakes 
individuals make in their interactions with others.  
• The Law: Learning United States and state civil rights laws, including Title VII, 
ADEA, and ADA, Colorado's Anti-Discrimination Act, and Colorado's new POWR 
Act. 
• Harassment: What is the difference between what is unfair and what is unlawful? 
What is the difference between discrimination and harassment? How do our 
negative stereotypes frame problems?  
o New types of harassment: Remote work, texting, and misuse of social 
media. 
o The five key principles of workplace harassment: Understanding 
"unwelcomeness", the "majority does it rule", "bystanders have rights too", 
"appearances...and context do matter", and "intentions are irrelevant." 
o Regaining respect: Reporting problems, avoiding retaliation, and 
mechanisms to curb or end harassment, including proper bystander 
intervention.  
• Supervisory Responsibilities: Six obligations Colorado's new POWR Act places 
on managers, employer liability, how to respond to complaints, a supervisor's 
duties to manage work before, during, or after a complaint is made, and some of 
the tools supervisors can use to address disrespect in the workplace.  
 
The goal of this training is not simply legal compliance. It is to ensure those serving the 
City understand current legal standards, City policy, appropriate boundaries, and their 
role in maintaining a respectful, professional, and ethical environment. 
 
Council Work Plan: 
Equity, diversity, and inclusion (EDI) is a core value of the City. Harassment training and 
EDI initiatives are interconnected, as each reinforces the other to build a safer and more 
equitable workplace culture.

Although this is considered a City responsibility, each of us must take an active

It is the City's firm conviction that equal opportunity will contribute to the success

the Human Resources Office immediately if they feel there is a violation of this 
policy. The matter will be fully investigated and, if appropriate, immediate action 
taken. Where practical, the employee's identity will be kept confidential. 
 
Harassment Policy  
 
     (updated February 2025) 
     Eligible employment status: 
• All Employees 
The City strives to maintain a work environment free of unlawful harassment. 
Unlawful harassment is unwelcome physical or verbal conduct or any written, 
pictorial, or visual communication directed at an individual (or group) because of 
that individual's (or group's) membership in, or perceived membership in, a 
protected class, that is subjectively offensive to the individual alleging 
harassment and is objectively offensive to a reasonable individual who is a 
member of the same protected class. Harassment does not need to be in-
person and can occur over electronic media such as Zoom or other electronic 
platforms. Prohibited behavior may include but is not limited to the following: 
 
• Verbal conduct, such as epithets, derogatory comments, slurs, or jokes. 
• Physical conduct such as assault or blocking an individual’s movements. 
 
This policy, including the sexual harassment policy, applies to all employees, 
including managers, supervisors, coworkers, and non-employees, such as 
customers, clients, vendors, consultants, etc. These harassment policies also 
apply to members of City Council, the Planning Commission, all other City-
appointed commissions, boards, and committees. 
Sexual Harassment 
 
Page 23 of 34
Because sexual harassment raises issues that are, to some extent, unique in 
comparison to other types of harassment, the City believes it warrants separate 
emphasis. 
 
The City does not tolerate sexual harassment and inappropriate sexual 
conduct. Sexual harassment is defined as unwelcome sexual advances, 
requests for sexual favors, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual 
nature when: 
 
• Submission to such conduct or communication is made explicitly or 
implicitly a term or condition of employment. 
• Submission to, objection to, or rejection of such conduct or 
communication is used as a basis for employment decisions affecting 
an individual. 
• Such conduct or communication has the purpose or effect of 
unreasonably interfering with an individual’s work performance or 
creating an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment. 
 
All employees are expected to always conduct themselves in a professional 
and business-like manner. Conduct that may violate this policy includes, but 
is not limited to, sexually implicit or explicit communications whether in: 
 
• Written form, such as cartoons, posters, calendars, notes, letters, and emails. 
• Verbal form, such as comments, jokes, foul or obscene language of a 
sexual nature, gossiping or questions about another’s sex life, or 
repeated unwanted requests for dates. 
• Physical gestures and other nonverbal behavior, such as unwelcome 
touching, grabbing, fondling, kissing, massaging, and brushing up 
against another’s body. 
Complaint Procedure 
 
If you believe there has been a violation of the EEO policy or harassment based 
on a protected class, including sexual harassment, please use the following 
complaint procedure. The City has established a program designed to prevent 
harassment, deter future harassers, and protect employees from harassment. 
The City takes prompt action to investigate and/or address complaints of 
harassment or alleged discriminatory or unfair employment practices. The City 
also takes prompt remedial actions, when warranted, in response to complaints 
of harassment or discriminatory or unfair employment practices. The City 
therefore encourages employees to make a timely complaint to enable the City 
to investigate and correct any behavior that may be in violation of this policy. 
 
Employees should report any alleged violation to a Manager, Department 
Director, or Human Resources Director. Managers and Department Heads 
shall promptly notify the Human Resources Director or City Manager, as 
Page 24 of 34
appropriate, after receiving a report of an alleged violation. 
 
The City will investigate the matter and, if appropriate, take corrective action. 
Your complaint will be kept as confidential as practicable. 
 
The City prohibits retaliation against an employee for filing a complaint under 
this policy or participating in a complaint investigation. If you perceive 
retaliation for making a complaint or participating in the investigation, please 
follow the complaint procedure outlined above. The situation will be 
investigated and, if appropriate, corrective action will be taken. 
 
Anti-Retaliation 
 
The City is committed to maintaining a work environment that is free of retaliation 
for engaging in protected activity such as reporting violations or participating in 
an investigation pertaining to alleged violations of laws, policies, or procedures. 
Situations or behaviors protected from retaliation include, but are not limited to, 
(in each case a “Protected Activity”): 
 
• Requesting a reasonable accommodation based on religion or disability. 
• Refusing unwanted sexual advances or protecting others from unwanted sexual 
advances. 
• Reporting fraud, waste of public funds, or unethical conduct. 
• Testifying, assisting, or participating in an investigation regarding 
alleged discrimination, harassment, or misconduct. 
• Raising reasonable concerns about violations of health or safety rules 
or about threats to health or safety in the workplace. 
• Disobeying an order believed to violate City policies and procedures 
or local, state, or federal laws and regulations. 
• Testifying, assisting, or participating in an employee disciplinary hearing. 
• Engaging in other activity protected by local, state, or federal laws and 
regulations. 
 
The City will not retaliate against any City employee who engages in a Protected 
Activity. Retaliation occurs when an employer takes an Adverse Action against 
an employee because the employee engaged in a Protected Activity. Adverse 
Action includes, but is not limited to: 
 
• Demotion. 
• Suspension. 
• Termination. 
• Failing to hire or consider for hire or promotion. 
• Failing to give equal consideration in making employment decisions or 
to make impartial employment recommendations. 
• Adversely impacting working conditions or otherwise denying any 
Page 25 of 34
employment benefit to an employee. 
• Creating a hostile or intimidating work environment. 
The City prohibits retaliation even if the concerns raised are not confirmed 
following an investigation. However, an employee may be subject to Adverse 
Action if the employee knowingly made a false allegation, provided false or 
misleading information in the course of an investigation, acted with reckless 
disregard as to the truth or falsity of information provided, or otherwise acted in 
bad faith. 
 
This anti-retaliation policy does not exempt employees from the consequences 
of their own misconduct or inadequate performance, and self-reporting such 
issues is not a Protected Activity. The policy also does not prevent the City from 
managing employee performance and addressing conduct issues after an 
employee has engaged in a Protected Activity, so long as the Protected Activity 
is not the reason for the performance management. 
 
Any City employee who retaliates against an employee engaged in a Protected 
Activity or who otherwise violates this policy is subject to disciplinary action, up 
to and including termination of employment.
