Subject:  Resolution No. 75, Series 2026 — A Resolution Approving a First 
Amendment to the Business Assistance Agreement with Louisville 
Cyclery, LLC 
Date: August 18, 2026 
Prepared By: Vanessa Zarate, Economic Vitality Manager 
Presented By: Vanessa Zarate, Economic Vitality Manager 
 
Summary:  
A Business Assistance Program incentive was approved for Louisville Cyclery in April 
2021 for a 50% sales tax rebate for five years with a maximum rebate of $58,000. At the 
time of the incentive approval, Louisville Cyclery was moving locations within Louisville 
and existing sales tax was used to set a baseline to determine the incremental increase 
in sales tax generation used to calculate qualifying rebates. It has since been 
discovered that some of the rebate calculations and payments did not account for the 
sales tax base and an overpayment of $16,854.27 was made. The attached amendment 
to the agreement allows the business to keep the funds, as their agreement terminates 
this year (with their payment for 2025) and they are still under the maximum rebate 
($58,000) identified in the agreement. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
The City of Louisville created the Business Assistance Program to support new, 
expanding, and relocating businesses in the City through fee and tax rebates. The 
incentives are paid out based on actual fees and taxes incurred and the BAP 
agreements are based on estimated costs and include a cap on total rebate eligibility. 
Additions or changes to the overall Business Assistance Program occur regularly as 
business needs are identified and evolve. 
 
Louisville Cyclery received a BAP in April 2021 to support their relocation and 
expansion plans at a new location at 889 South Boulder Road.  The BAP included a 
sales tax rebate, with a base sales tax identified as $23,063 and any sales tax rebates 
limited to additional sales tax generated above the base. 
 
Development Proposal: 
N/A 
 
Analysis: 
At the beginning of every year, Economic Vitality and Finance work together to issue 
rebates for all active BAPs. The sales tax base was not clearly defined in the original 
Page 3 of 242
BAP agreement, leading to some rebates being paid without consideration of the sales 
tax base ($23,053). As a result, the business received an overpayment of $16,854.27.  
 
The business did not receive a rebate in 2021 or 2024, but received a total rebate of 
$23,944.10 since 2021.The rebates made in 2023 and 2025 did not account for the 
sales tax base. Their stated incentive rebate cap was $58,000. With the overpayment, 
the business received less money than their stated incentive cap.  
 
The agreement terminates this year and the business is not eligible for additional 
rebates following the last rebate payment made for 2025. The total rebates paid are 
under the maximum rebate ($58,000) identified in the agreement.  Since the 
overpayment was made due to an administrative error by the City, and payments are 
under the identified maximum rebate allowed, staff recommends that the agreement be 
amended to allow the business to retain the overpayment. 
 
Council Work Plan: 
This item is not identified in the City Council Work Plan. 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
This action will have fiscal impact to the City of $16,854.27.  Because the overpayment 
is within the budgeted cap of $58,000, a budget amendment is not required. 
 
Alternatives: 
1. Approve the First Amendment to the BAP Agreement with Louisville Cyclery, 
allowing the business to retain an overpayment of $16,854.27.   
2. Not approve the First Amendment to the BAP Agreement with Louisville Cyclery. 
This would result in the City seeking reimbursement for the overpayment.    
 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends that the City Council approve the BAP Amendment for Louisville 
Cyclery, allowing the business to retain an overpayment of $16,854.27.

FIRST AMENDMENT TO BUSINESS ASSISTANCE AGREEMENT  
FOR LOUISVILLE CYCLERY, LLC IN THE CITY OF LOUISVILLE 
 
THIS FIRST AMENDMENT TO BUSINESS ASSISTANCE AGREEMENT 
(“Agreement”) is made and entered into as of the _______ day of 
______________________, 2026, between the CITY OF LOUISVILLE, a Colorado home 
rule municipal corporation (the "City"), and LOUISVILLE CYCLERY, LLC, a Colorado 
limited liability company (the “Company”).   
 
 WHEREAS, by Resolution No. 28, Series 2021, the City Council approved a 
Business Assistance Agreement (the “Agreement”) with the Company in connection with 
the Company’s expansion and relocation to 899 E. South Boulder Road; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the business assistance provided to the Company included a rebate 
of fifty percent (50%) of sales tax attributable to new retail sales generated at the new 
location, up to a maximum amount $58,000; and  
 
 WHEREAS, pursuant to Chapter 3.24 of the Louisville Municipal Code, sales tax 
rebates are limited to the amount of sales tax collected by the City over and above a base 
amount negotiated and agreed to by the Company and the City; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the Council Communication accompanying Resolution No. 28, Series 
2021, indicated that the sales tax base amount for the Agreement should be set at 
$23,063 based on the sale tax generated at the Company’s prior location within the City; 
and  
 
 WHEREAS, it was recently discovered that the rebates paid to the Company in 
2023 and 2025 failed to include a deduction for the established sales tax base and that 
no rebate payment was made to the Company in 2024, which resulted in an overpayment 
to the Company of $16,854.27 (the “Overpayment”); and  
 
 WHEREAS, the Agreement expired in April 2026, and the Overpayment exceeds 
the remaining rebate payments due to the Company; and  
 
 WHEREAS, despite the Overpayment, the total sales tax rebated to the Company 
is less than the $58,000 cap set forth in the Agreement; and   
 
 WHEREAS, requiring the Company to return the Overpayment to the City may 
negatively impact the Company’s finances and will not serve the purpose of the City’s 
BAP program, which is to encourage the retention and expansion of sales tax generating 
businesses and employers; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the City Council finds it is in the best interest of the City to allow the 
Company to retain the Overpayment.

SUMMARY: 
Staff requests City Council action on a proposed Business Assistance Package ( BAP) 
for Louisville Cyclery LLC, which proposes to relocate its existing business within the
City of Louisville.

Greg Jones is the owner of Louisville Cyclery LLC, which currently operates at 1032 E. 
South Boulder Road. The business started in 1980 and has always been locally owned
and operated. Due to a pending redevelopment project, the building which currently
houses Louisville Cyclery has been sold and is anticipated to be demolished. Mr. Jones
has been seeking new commercial space to keep the business close to Downtown and
the South Boulder Road Corridor for a number of months. To learn more about the
company, please visit their website: https:// www. louisvillecyclery. com/. 
Louisville Cyclery intends to lease space at 889 E. South Boulder Road. In the existing
location, the shop occupies 2,500 SF and would be increasing to occupy 3,086 square
feet. The owner intends to make minor renovations to the space in order to move the
business to start operating by September 2021, though some more renovations may be
undertaken over time. The anticipated renovations are estimated at $ 200, 000. Louisville
Cyclery currently has 12 employees and hopes that with expanded space for
operations, it will grow to 20 employees over the first five years in the new location. 
Average job salaries at the business are approximately $ 40, 000. 
Louisville Cyclery LLC has not yet executed a lease on the proposed location. If
assistance is approved, they would lease the space in May and start the process to
relocate shortly thereafter. The applicant estimates that in the new store location, the
business would generate $ 7, 631, 375 in gross retail sales over the next five years. 
Between January 2016 and February 2021, Louisville Cyclery remitted $ 146, 410 in
Retail Sales Tax to the City. Since the applicant is requesting Sales Tax rebates, staff
calculated a base increment year; the business is only eligible for " new" Sales Tax
generated due to its new location and expansion. 
To establish a base increment year for rebate purposes, staff generally uses the year
prior to BAP application. 2020, however, was an extremely unusual year for many small

Discussions occur with the prospective business concerning any specific
assistance needed in the early phases of the development. 
A Business Assistance Request Application is completed and submitted by the
company prior to a decision to locate in the City or to undertake substantial
expansion ( in the case of an existing business). 
The data from the business assistance form is analyzed by staff and a
preliminary recommendation is made to the City Manager requesting assistance
based upon income generation to the City, number of jobs, average salaries, job
diversity, what direct and indirect benefits this company may bring to the City of
Louisville, and consideration of the other criteria above. Staff will refer applicant
to the preservation planner to determine actions related to the Historic

construction use tax rebates, and sales tax rebates ( if applicable). All rebates will

Construction Use Tax, or Consumer Use Tax ( not including Open Space Tax of
0. 375%, Historic Preservation Tax of 0. 125%, or Recreation Center Tax of
0. 150%) unless the City considers a lesser amount to be appropriate.

one- step or two- step process. 
o One -Step: If the company agrees to use their name publicly, then staff
prepares a business assistance agreement for formal Council
consideration during a public meeting, or
o Two -Step: If the company needs to remain confidential, then staff
schedules a presentation before City Council to seek policy direction. 
If Council' s response is positive, staff prepares a letter to the
project stating that the City is willing to offer a business assistance
pending formal approval by City Council. 
The company may go public with their announcement to relocate to
the City or to substantially expand within the City. 
Timed with the company' s project nearing completion in the
planning and/ or building phase, staff recommends formal adoption
of the business assistance. The timing of the formal adoption is
used to ensure that the company has met the planning and
development guidelines as outlined by the City.

authorizes the City Manager to fund and implement the Business

Subject:  Resolution No. 76, Series 2026 — A Resolution Approving a First 
Extension and Amendment to Ice Rink Management and License

Date: August 18, 2026 
Prepared By: Rob Zuccaro, Director 
Presented By: Rob Zuccaro, Director 
 
Summary:  
Attached is a letter from the Main Street Louisville Business Improvement District (BID) 
requesting $35,000 in additional funding for the 2025–2026 Old Town Skate ice rink 
operations. The request cites a funding shortfall that resulted in a financial loss for the 
contracted operator and insufficient funds to pay for the chiller rental required for the ice 
rink. 
 
If Council approves the additional funding request, the attached resolution and 
amendment to the existing agreement with the BID would authorize the additional City 
funding. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
By Resolution No. 77, Series 2025, the City approved an agreement with the BID 
authorizing it to operate the Old Town Skate ice rink at Steinbaugh Pavilion for the 
2025–2026 season. The agreement included $30,000 the City deposited with the BID to 
be used for the BID's management services and expenses, plus the use of the City's ice 
rink equipment, which was purchased by the City's Urban Renewal Authority. In 
addition, the City spent $16,720.23 on items needed to support the ice rink, for a total of 
$46,720.23 paid by the City to support the BID for the 2025–2026 season.  The 
agreement required the BID to document its management fee and expenses in monthly 
reports to the City, and to return unused deposited funds to the City at the end of the 
term of the agreement. The BID entered into a separate contract with its selected 
operator, and the City was not a party to that agreement. 
 
The City assumed responsibility for the ice rink in 2023 after the previous operator 
announced it would no longer continue operations. The City contracted directly with an 
operator for the 2023–2024 and 2024–2025 seasons before the BID assumed 
responsibility for operations during the 2025–2026 season.

Analysis: 
The City is currently working with the BID on a new agreement for the upcoming 2026–
2027 season. Staff anticipates that the agreement will include a request for a City 
contribution of $90,000 to $100,000, and may also include a revenue-sharing 
component. If approved, the requested additional funding for the 2025–2026 season, 
combined with the $46,720.23 already paid to the BID, would bring the City's total 
contribution to $81,720.23, which remains below the anticipated City contribution for the 
2026–2027 season. However, this will be part of a larger budget discussion with Council 
to evaluate the City's contributions to various events.  
 
The request letter from the BID includes a summary of costs and revenues for the 
2025–2026 ice rink operations. The letter states that the additional funding would allow 
the BID to pay the outstanding $19,809 balance for the chiller rental and provide the 
operator with a net gain of $14,696 for managing the ice rink. The BID would also waive 
the $8,000 Zamboni rental fee owed by the operator. Under the proposed agreement 
amendment, the BID will still be required to provide accounting of all deposited funds, 
and return any remaining amount to the City. 
 
Council Work Plan: 
This item supports the Economic Vitality priority in the 2026 City Council Work Plan. 
 The ice rink helps activate and draw customers to the Downtown business district 
during the winter and holiday season. 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
The City has sufficient funds in the 2026 budget designated for ice rink operations to 
cover the request for additional funds for the 2025-2026 season and to cover the 
anticipated contract for the upcoming 2026-2027 season. 
 
Alternatives:

• Approve additional funding at an alternative amount determined by City Council.  
• Not approve the additional funding request. 
 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff does not have a recommendation on this item and is facilitating the BID's request 
to Council.

Dear City Council Members, 
We are writing this letter to request additional funding for the Old Town Skate Ice Rink for 
this past, 2025-2026 season. 
Background: 
The Main Street Louisville Downtown Business Improvement District (BID) entered into an 
agreement in September 2025 with the City of Louisville to operate the Old Town Skate Ice 
Rink during the 2025-2026 season. At that time, the BID had a hand-shake agreement with 
long-time ice rink operator, Boulder Creek Events (BCE), to operate the ice rink and the BID 
would oversee operations and liaise as necessary with the City to ensure smooth, 
consistent and reliable operations. 
Unfortunately, BCE backed out of operating and left the BID scrambling to ﬁnd a new 
operator and to execute all the pre-production and installation planning. BCE did introduce 
the BID to Blue Line Sports and Entertainment, who agreed to take on the operations of the 
rink for the season. However, because of the last-minute nature of the arrangements, the 
BID needed to shoulder much of the installation expenses and  enter into a rental 
agreement for the Chiller. The agreement between the BID and Blue Line stipulated that 
Blue Line would reimburse the BID for all expenses paid on behalf of the new operator, Blue

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND: 
Information from Gigi Yang, Louisville Historical Museum 
 
1013 La Farge shares an unusual history with several houses on the block that were 
Page 45 of 242
built by E.J. Di Francia as dowries for his daughters. E.J. Di Francia appears to have 
been living in Louisville by 1891 and opened his first saloon on Front Street at around 
that time. The home’s history also contributes to the story of property ownership by 
families of Italian heritage in the neighborhood and along La Farge. Also notable is 
ownership by only two families over its 120-year history including the Di Francia family 
and the extended Giorzelli, Enrietto, and DiSalle families.  
 
The 1916 City Directory lists Camillo and Margaret Di Francia Domenico living at 518 
La Farge which could be an early address for 1013 La Farge. Margaret Di Francia was 
Maria and Eusebio’s eldest daughter. She married Camillo Domenico in 1912 and 
appears to be the only Di Francia daughter to live in one of the “dowry” houses.1013 La 
Farge was sold by Maria Di Francia in 1928 to Joseph and Estella (Stella) Bottinelli 
Giorzelli (1906-2006). Both Joseph and Stella grew up in Louisville. A number of 
tenants are mentioned in the Louisville Times as living as this address throughout the 
1950s-1970s. While the 1013 La Farge remained primarily as a rental property, 
descendants of the Giorzelli family lived nearby at the original Giorzelli home at 1000 
La Farge and still live there today. In 2000, June Enrietto transferred ownership of 1013 
La Farge to her daughter, Barbara DiSalle. June passed away in 2023 and in 2025, 
Barbara DiSalle sold the property to Craig Thompson who is the current owner.  
 
Please see the attached Social History Report (Attachment No. 2) for a more detailed 
history.

ARCHITECTURAL INTEGRITY 
1013 La Farge is included in the PaleoWest "100 Architectural Inventories for the City 
of Louisville” (2023). Its description is as follows: “1013 La Farge is a wood frame home 
that has a hipped roof with a roughly 5 x 5 ft flat area at the peak. A small front-gabled 
section extends east from the south end of the east side. An entry door is just north of 
this extension. A small porch runs from the main entry door to the northeast corner of 
the house. The porch has ornate historic wooden supports and a hipped roof. A small 
hipped-roof addition extends west from the rear (west) side of the house and has a 
hipped roof. A small entryway with its own hipped roof exists on the northwest corner of 
the house. The house has horizontal siding that appears to be either vinyl or aluminum. 
The house sits on a stacked stone foundation. 1013 La Farge has both double hung 
and fixed picture windows. Windows on the front-gabled extension and south side may 
be original…The entry door and all windows on the east and north sides, with the 
Page 47 of 242
possible exception of two windows on the front-gabled extension, are post-1950 
replacements. Windows on the south side may be original. A brick chimney at the roof 
peak has been replaced with a modern chimney. The house had wood siding in 1950

1040 La Farge Avenue. PaleoWest recommends 1013 La Farge Avenue for recording 
and further investigation because it may have the potential to yield further information 
about residential development and patterning in Louisville.
