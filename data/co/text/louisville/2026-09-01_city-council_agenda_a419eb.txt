City Council Communication 
Agenda Item 5.B.  
 
Subject:  Resolution No. 78, Series 2026 — A Resolution Adopting an 
Updated Policy Regarding Payments for Cleanup and/or Restoration 
Expenses Resulting from Sewer Backups or Water Meter Failures 
Date: September 1, 2026 
Prepared By: Mindy Olkjer, Executive Assistant 
Samma Fox, Deputy City Manager 
Presented By: Samma Fox, Deputy City Manager 
Diana Langley, City Manager 
 
Summary:  
In an effort to maintain positive customer relations the City adopted a policy in 1997, 
then amended that policy in 2004 to provide for reimbursement of up to $3,500, then 
amended that policy in 2008 to increase the reimbursement of up to $7,500 for cleanup 
costs that a residential property owner incurs if the City determines that a sewer backup 
occurred as a result of a blockage in a City sewer main. 
 
The City’s current policy, Resolution No. 22 Series 2008, provides for reimbursements 
for residential customers only and does not include the requirement that customers 
submit an insurance claim as a precursor to eligibility for City reimbursements. Staff is 
seeking adoption of the attached resolution, which would expand the policy to apply to 
non-residential customers as well as require the property owner to submit an insurance 
claim before pursuing reimbursement from the City. Additionally, the updated policy 
would give the City Manager and Risk Management Specialist authority to determine 
the amount of funding to be paid to the impacted customer. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
Any blockages or problems in the property owner's sewer service line are the 
responsibility of the property owner. Periodically, and in spite of the City’s ongoing 
maintenance and repair activities, an obstruction or other blockage in a City-owned 
sewer main causes a backup of sewage in the City sewer main into service lines and 
into residences, businesses, and other properties. Further, City-owned water meters 
unexpectedly fail on occasion. Flooding can be a concern for potential property damage 
from a failed meter since many meters are located in areas of the home that are not 
regularly frequented. A leak could potentially go unnoticed for an extended period and 
result in significant water damage. The cases we have experienced, however, are 
reading and accuracy issues as well as flow restrictions. Flow restrictions may not 
cause direct water damage, but they can still have significant impacts, particularly for 
commercial customers that rely on adequate water flow for their operations or 
Page 19 of 321
processes. 
 
Although the City may have no legal liability for damages in such situations, the costs 
for cleanup and restoration can be substantial. The City’s current policy established by 
Resolution No. 22 Series 2008, provides for reimbursement of up to $7,500 for 
residential customers only and does not include the requirement that customers submit 
an insurance claim as a precursor to eligibility for City reimbursements. 
 
Development Proposal: 
NA 
 
Analysis: 
Staff is proposing the following changes to the policy: 
• Expand the policy to apply to non-residential customers; with close to 900 
businesses located in Louisville, there is potential for sewer backups to occur 
that would impact commercial customers.  
• Require the property owner to submit an insurance claim before pursuing 
reimbursement from the City.  
• Provide discretion to the City's Risk Manager/Specialist, subject to approval by 
the City Manager or their designee, to determine the appropriate amount of 
reimbursement recommended up to a maximum amount of $7,500. The current 
policy outlines that the amount of reimbursement made to a property owner shall 
be recommended by the City's insurance claims adjustor. In the majority of 
cases, however, submitting a claim to the City’s insurance carrier is not feasible.  
 
 
Council Work Plan: 
NA 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
Authorizing the Risk Manager/Specialist to recommend reimbursement amounts has the 
potential to lower the overall annual reimbursements paid out. Specifically, the 
requirement that property owners pursue a claim with their insurance company, in most 
cases, will generate insurance payouts to property owners, offsetting dollar-for-dollar 
the reimbursement amount the City would pay out per event. 
 
From 2007 through 2025, there were an average of three sewer backups and twenty-
three water meter failures per year in Louisville. The water and sewer systems are 
operated as enterprise funds, with all costs associated with the system recouped 
through fees and charges. Reimbursement costs will be included as an operational line 
item(s) within water and/or sewer accounts. These operating expenses will be included 
in the next annual rate adjustment scheduled for implementation May 1, 2027. In the 
last five years (2021-2025), the City has issued an average of $4,117.11 in 
reimbursements. Note that no funds have been issued for 2026 to date. 
 
Page 20 of 321
Consideration of impacts to both residential and commercial water and sewer rates are 
as follows based on an average of 3 full reimbursements per year: 
 
The charge for residential sewer service is currently $53.40 per month. If the City 
increased rates on residential users to cover the cost of one $7,500 reimbursement 
annually, it would add 9 to 10 cents to the average residential monthly fee. If we 
average 3 full reimbursements per year it would be necessary to increase rates by 27 to 
30 cents per month (or about 0.5% to 0.6%) per single family residential account. 
 
The charge for residential water service is currently $55.39 per month. If the City 
increased rates on residential users to cover the cost of one $7,500 reimbursement 
annually, it would add 9 to 10 cents to the average residential monthly fee. If we 
average 3 full reimbursements per year it would be necessary to increase rates by 27 to 
30 cents per month (or about 0.5% to 0.6%) per single family residential account. 
 
The charge for non-residential (representative commercial) sewer service is currently 
$139.51 per month. If the City increased rates on non-residential users to cover the cost 
of one $7,500 reimbursement annually, it would add $1.50 to $1.60 to the average non-
residential monthly fee. If we average 3 full reimbursements per year it would be 
necessary to increase rates by $4.50 to $4.80 cents per month (or about 3.2% to 3.4%) 
per non-residential account. 
 
The charge for non-residential (representative commercial) water service is currently 
$100.47 per month. If the City increased rates on non-residential users to cover the cost 
of one $7,500 reimbursement annually, it would add $1.50 to $1.60 to the average non-
residential monthly fee. If we average 3 full reimbursements per year it would be 
necessary to increase rates by $4.50 to $4.80 cents per month (or about 4.4% to 4.8%) 
per non-residential account. 
 
 
  
 
Alternatives: 
1. Amend the proposed updated policy with the following options: 
a. Modify the maximum reimbursement amount.  
b. Do not expand the policy to cover non-residential customers.  
c. Do not require that the property owner submit a claim to their insurance 
first.

POLICY REGARDING CITY PAYMENTS FOR CLEANUP AND/OR RESTORATION 
EXPENSES RESULTING  FROM SEWER BACKUPS OR WATER METER FAILURES 
 
I. Introduction.  Periodically, and in spite of the City’s ongoing maintenance 
and repair activities, an obstruction or other blockage in a City-owned sewer main causes 
a backup of sewage in the City sewer main into service lines and into residences, 
businesses, and other properties. Further, City-owned water meters unexpectedly fail on 
occasion.  Although the City may have no legal liability for damages in such situations, 
the costs for cleanup and restoration can be substantial. In an effort to maintain positive 
customer relations if a backup or water meter failure occurs, the City may pay for the 
cleanup and restoration required by a sewage backup that may have been caused by 
blockage of the sewer main or by a water meter failure. The total cost to the City for the 
cleanup services and restoration of the property to a condition comparable to what existed 
before the event may not exceed $7,500 per occurrence. 
 
II. Guidelines.  The following guidelines shall be used in determining the 
circumstances under which such payment may be made: 
 
  1. Payment under this Policy is intended for those situations where the 
City has no legal liability to pay for the damages caused by a backup in the main 
sewer line or the failure of a City-installed water meter. In those cases where the 
City’s claim adjustor finds that the backup or meter failure was caused as a result 
of City negligence, the claim will be adjusted appropriately without payment under 
this Policy. 
 
  2. Payments for costs of cleanup and/or restoration may be made to 
the property owners of residential and non-residential properties and only in those 
circumstances where a water meter fails unexpectedly or the backup occurs in the 
sewer main and not when a meter fails as a result of accident, misuse or neglect 
by the property owner or occupant, or when the backup is caused by an obstruction 
in the property owner’s service line.  No payments shall be made if the City 
determines the backup or meter failure was the result of negligence, accident or 
intentional conduct on the part of any contractor or the owner or occupant of the 
affected property. In situations where a tenant or occupant is legally responsible 
for cleanup and/or restoration costs, payments may be made to the tenant or 
occupant in lieu of the property owner.   
 
  3. The Public Works employee responding to the sewer backup or 
meter failure complaint will make the determination of whether water damage 
occurred as a result of a meter failure or some other cause or whether the backup 
occurred in the City sewer main or whether the backup occurred as a result of a 
blockage in the property owner’s service line.

  4. If the property owner or occupant has property insurance in place, 
they shall first submit a claim for the cleanup and restoration costs to their 
insurance provider. The City will pay for only those costs that are not covered by 
insurance and subject to the limits in this Policy. If the property owner or occupant 
does not have any property insurance, the City may pay for all cleanup and 
restoration costs, subject to the limits in this Policy.

Summary:

Background / Prior Discussions: 
The City utilizes Tyler EnerGov as its Enterprise Permitting and Licensing system. 
Crystal Reports are embedded throughout the application and provide 39 standardized, 
print-ready documents used by staff and customers throughout the permitting process. 
The Application had over 100 reports, but Planning narrowed the need down to 39. 
These reports generate many of the City's official documents, including: 
• Building permits 
• Licenses 
• Certificates of Occupancy 
• Certificates of Completion 
• Inspection reports 
• Public notices 
• Professional licenses 
• Invoices and receipts 
• Zoning permits 
• Other regulatory documents 
 
Crystal Reports has reached end-of-life and is no longer the supported reporting 
platform for Tyler EnerGov. Tyler Technologies has identified SSRS as the supported 
replacement platform. Existing reports should continue working, but after October 31st, 
there will not be an ability to edit or create new reports. Staff often needs to edit reports 
Page 36 of 321
due to regulatory changes. 
 
Development Proposal: 
NA 
 
Analysis: 
The Information Technology department performed a comprehensive evaluation of 
available conversion options to identify the solution that provided the best long-term 
value while minimizing implementation risk. 
Three options were evaluated: 
CrystalMigration.com 
Staff tested this automated conversion service by submitting several City reports for 
conversion. Although the initial conversion cost is only $65 per report, the vendor 
charges $150 per hour to manually correct errors created during the automated 
conversion process. Testing demonstrated numerous conversion issues requiring 
manual correction, making the total project cost difficult to predict and likely higher than 
initially anticipated. Staff determined this approach presented a level of cost uncertainty 
and quality risk.  
Park Consulting Group 
Park Consulting Group proposed converting the City's reports for $74,500. While 
technically capable, this proposal represented the highest overall project cost and would 
still require ongoing cost and coordination with Tyler Technologies for future support.  
Tyler Technologies (Recommended) 
Tyler Technologies, the City's existing Enterprise Permitting and Licensing vendor, 
proposed completing the conversion for $58,500, approximately $16,000 less than the 
Park Consulting proposal. Tyler is currently offering a 35% promotional discount for 
Crystal Report conversions through October 2026 as part of its transition away from 
Crystal Reports.   
Selecting Tyler also provides significant long-term advantages. As the software 
manufacturer and ongoing support provider, Tyler is uniquely positioned to ensure 
converted reports function correctly within EnerGov, remain compatible with future 
software upgrades, and receive continued vendor support throughout the life of the
