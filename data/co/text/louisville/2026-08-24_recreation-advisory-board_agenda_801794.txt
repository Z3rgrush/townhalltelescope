A. Golf Fund Update — James Franks, Deputy Director of Finance 
 
Golf RAB Presentation - July 27 
James asked the RAB to review golf round rate modeling results included in his 
presentation. Increasing green fees (as the largest source of revenue) can’t 
address funding issues alone, so City Council will need to consider cuts as well. 
He then led us through his presentation (see link above) that included various fee 
increase modeling scenarios. These included a 0% annual green fee increase 
(Model One), 5% (Model 2), and $10 per 18 hole round equivalent (Model 3). 
These models showed eventual deficits up to $1.1 million dollars by 2032. For a 
comparison over the same time frame: 1) a hypothetical 45% increase in green 
fees would be required to cover projected expenses and generate a surplus; and 
2) a hypothetical 21% increase would not be enough to cover projected expenses. 
Adam asked that the RAB consider additional modeling scenarios they would like 
to see beyond what James presented this evening. He also asked for RAB’s 
recommendations for how best to address projected funding deficits over time 
including an appropriate % increase in annual green fees. 
Some RAB members questioned City Council’s commitment to ensure the 
necessary long term adequate funding to sustain the viability of the CCGC as a 
competitive alternative to other local gold courses. 
Michele suggested that we consider only Models 2 and 3 and some other RAB 
members agreed. She expressed a preference for a % increase model (#2) than a 
flat fee model (#3). Otherwise, certain golfers (e.g., seniors) could be impacted 
disproportionately. Lisa suggested we recommend a 5% annual green fee rate. 
Dan suggested these increases be variable depending on the time of the week or 
other factors. Michele asked that we at least try to cover operating expenses with 
the % increase the RAB recommends. She also suggested we consider other 
revenue sources (e.g., lessons) even though these have generate less revenue 
than annual green fees. Dan suggested the RAB consider Model 3 to raise some

2027-2028 Coal Creek Golf Course Fees 
RAB Recommendations 
Residents: 
The RAB believes that it is important to recognize the value of Louisville residents to the 
long-term success of the CCGC. As a municipal golf course, residents are at the core of our 
mission.  While some, but not all, of our peers offer discounts on greens fees to residents, 
we feel that a resident discount program is imperative. Due to the ongoing financial 
challenges facing the golf course, a blanket discount may not be the best option at this 
time however. Although actual counts are inexact, it is estimated that approximately ___% 
of golfers are Louisville residents.  We would like this number to be higher. Therefore, we 
recommend implementing the following policies: 
(Note: this is just a list of ideas thrown out for purposes of discussion with the Board and 
staff, not a final recommendation) 
- Resident discounts on green fees on certain weekdays, maybe in alignment with the 
senior discounts  
- A discount on the Preferred Player pass – maybe $150 – to encourage residents to 
commit to the CCGC 
- Early booking capabilities, similar to those offered to Imperial and Regal 
passholders 
- “Residents Play Free” day   
- Resident Off-Peak Rate – provide deeper discounts to residents during the slower 
times.  
- Earlier booking time for residents 
- Resident Appreciation Days – schedule a few days throughout the year for possibly a 
happy hour or clothing/golf club event  
 
Annual Passes:  
- Regal – Increase by 5% from 2026 rate 
- Imperial - Increase by 5% from 2026 rate 
- Monarch – No increase for 2027 
- Preferred Player – No increase for 2027 
- Add extra value to our pass program
