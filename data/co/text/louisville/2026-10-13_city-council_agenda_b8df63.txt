     Members with Differing Needs
Not all members of a board have the same interactions during a meeting and it is 
incumbent on all members to make sure the meeting runs in a way that everyone is 
included. A few meeting ground rules will reap major benefits:
• Establish a turn‐taking and participation protocol, don’t talk over each other
• If an ASL interpreter is in the meeting, participants should pause between questions 
and/or comments to give the interpreter time to catch up.
• Maintain eye‐contact with the person communicating, not the interpreter.
• Don’t speak for anyone other than yourself.
• Eliminate side conversations so the interpreter can focus on the main conversation.
• Take breaks as needed to allow everyone to catch up.
• Follow up with written action items so everyone knows what was decided.

Ember Brignull, Open Space Superintendent 
From: Open Space Advisory Board 
Date: 9/29/2026  
Re: Proposed Development of 8600 Baseline Horse Property (Parcel OO)  
 
Background: 
 
8600 Baseline has long been one of the three highest-priority acquisition recommendations 
from the Louisville Open Space Advisory Board (designated OO on the OSAB maps).  The 34-
acre property includes a barn and farmhouse on land long used for agriculture. It is bisected 
by two irrigation ditches that provide essential riparian habitat for mammals, birds, and 
insects. It is bordered by Lafayette and Boulder County Open Spaces, which connect to 
Louisville’s North Open Space 
All the other OSAB acquisition priorities either already have conservation easements or are 
now in development zones under the new comprehensive plan. Thus, preserving this 
property offers the best use of dollars in the open space reserve and offers a unique 
opportunity to provide continuity with adjoining open-space properties and maintain the 
rural character of the area. 
This property is currently owned by Stephen J. Tzap, who has filed a preapplication review 
with the City of Lafayette to annex and rezone the property and provide services so that he 
can build a total of 185 homes (74 townhomes, 146 single-family detached). In 2018, a 
previous developer proposed an 87-home development, which was rejected by Lafayette’s 
planning commission because the annexation did not comply with the city’s Comprehensive 
Plan policies and it was unclear if the city would have the financial ability to provide city 
services such as water and wastewater.

Purpose: 
While the property in question is in Boulder County, its proximity to current Louisville, 
Lafayette, and Boulder County Open Space make it an ideal site for either a joint purchase or 
a jointly funded conservation easement.  Although this land is not in Louisville’s boundaries, a 
joint venture would follow the precedent set by several other joint land acquisitions near but 
not in the city, like the Mayhoffer Open Space. 
Next Steps: 
OSAB recommends that the city work with Lafayette and Boulder County to either purchase 
the property fee simple or negotiate a conservation easement, perhaps with the right to 
develop two custom home properties along the western edge.

Summary:  
The City of Louisville sponsors and partners on a wide range of community events, 
including City-produced celebrations such as the Fourth of July, Summer Concerts in 
the Park, and the Labor Day Parade, as well as partner events with the Chamber of 
Commerce and Downtown Business Association, such as Taste of Louisville, Pints in 
the Park, and Street Faire. These events require significant in-kind support from multiple 
City departments. 
 
In addition to City-sponsored events, the number and complexity of Permitted Special 
Events - such as 5K and bike races and other large gatherings - has increased, creating 
additional coordination needs and contributing to staff capacity constraints across 
departments. 
 
At the July 22, 2026 Budget Workshop, staff presented several service-level reduction 
options for Council consideration, including implementing limits on special events, 
adjusting rates to recover Police Department and Public Works costs, and evaluating 
the level of support provided for internal versus external events. Council requested that 
staff return with additional information for further discussion and direction. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
City-Sponsored Events and Contracts 
The City of Louisville manages, oversees, and sponsors many special events such as 
the Fourth of July celebration, Summer Concerts in the Park, and the Labor Day 
Parade. The City also partners with the Chamber of Commerce and Downtown 
Business Association on events like Taste of Louisville, Pints in the Park, and Street 
Faire. These events receive significant in-kind support from multiple City departments, 
including the City Clerk's Office, Public Works, Police Department, Cultural Services, 
and Parks, Recreation and Open Space.

Permitted Special Events 
Permitted Special Events are those larger events that require approval and coordination 
from several departments (i.e. Farmer's Market, 5K races, etc.). There has been an 
increase in the number and complexity of permitted events, which also has an impact 
on staff capacity.  
 
Budget Considerations 
As part of the July 22, 2026 Budget Workshop, staff presented several service-level 
reduction options to Council related to special events. Those included: 
• Implement a limitation on the number of special events 
• Consider billing rate adjustments to recoup Police Department and Public Works 
costs 
 
At that time, City Council requested that staff bring more information related to special 
events back to Council for discussion/direction.

N/A 
 
Analysis: 
Below are items for Council consideration and direction.  
 
Implement a limitation on the number of special events 
Attached is a list showing 2026 Special Events. During the months of May through 
October, there were multiple events throughout the month, with overlapping events on 
some days, and back-to-back events on multiple consecutive weekends. Recognizing 
the positive impacts of these events in terms of bringing people to Louisville and 
contributing to the City's economic vitality, there can be negative impacts on 
surrounding neighborhoods, and these events require staff time that is typically outside 
the normal work day, thereby leading to overtime. Staff would like to understand from 
Council if there is a desire to consider any of the following: 
• Per Day Limits - Limit the number of events that can occur per day (no more 
than X events / day). A variation of this could be to limit the number of events 
that can occur per day in a specific area, such as downtown, or the number of 
events that require City staff support.  
• Per Type Limits - Limit the number of specific types of events per year (for 
example, no more than X running races / year). 
• By Venue Limits - Limit the number of events that can occur at a specific 
venue per year (in 2026 the number of events in Community Park was limited 
due to drought conditions as a conservation measure). 
• By Route Limits - Require that events occur on City pre-designated routes 
(applicants must select from a set of pre-set routes for running / bike races). 
Pre-designated routes make traffic control more routine, thereby gaining 
Page 23 of 50
efficiencies for set-up and take down.  
 
Staff Billing Rate Adjustments 
Races, certain larger events, and those serving alcohol may require police officers to be 
in attendance. The City has an Extra Duty Officer Application and Contract (Contract) 
that applicants fill out and sign to acknowledge and agree to in order to have police 
officer coverage. The rates noted in the Contract are not the fully burdened rates for the 
officers, so there is still some subsidy from the City when police officers provide 
coverage for those events. Staff intends to correct this for 2027 and will revise the rates 
to reflect the full cost to the City.  
 
However, a similar process is not in place to recoup the costs for Public Works 
assistance, which is typically related to traffic control set-up and take down. As noted 
previously, many of the special events are held outside of the City's business hours, and 
Public Works staff receive overtime for assisting with the events. Staff would like to 
understand from Council if there is a desire to charge for Public Works staff time. If so, 
staff will develop a contract for Public Works services. 
 
Council Work Plan: 
Special events are not specifically listed in Council's Work Plan. These events 
contribute to the overall quality of life for Louisville, but can have budget and 
neighborhood impacts. 
 
Fiscal Impact:

various events. In addition to these costs, it is estimated that the City Clerk's Office 
provides approximately $10,000 / year and the Public Works Department provides 
approximately $11,000 / year in staff costs for both city and third-party events.

Promoting and advertising business workshops, events, programs, incentive policies and other
business -related information hosted by the City and Economic Vitality partners to include on
website, newsletter and social media.

with Cultural Services)

the Library, the History Museum and the Recreation/ Senior Center.

marketing. City logo will not appear on political statements or individual business
sales/ promotions.

such as Advocacy Meetings and Board of Director meetings.

Permitted Special Events
October 13, 2026
• Permitted Special Events are those larger events that require approval and 
coordination from several departments (Farmers Market, 5k races, etc.)
• These do not include neighborhood block parties or smaller park events with 
attendance lower than 250
• We have seen an increase in number of events, and complexity of events in the past 
few years. 
• On August 1, we had 3 events on a single day (Farmer's Market, 150/250 
Celebrations and the Lucky Pie Bike Races)
• Factors to keep in mind:
• Front and Center Project – this will impact several of the areas where events 
have historically taken place
• Ongoing drought conditions – we capped the number of events in Community 
Park this year to preserve the grass for resident users

Permitted Special Events
October 13, 2026
• Options to control permitted special events:
• Per Day Limits - Limit the number of events that can occur per day (no more than X events / day). A 
variation of this could be to limit the number of events that can occur per day in a specific area, such as 
downtown, or the number of events that require City staff support. 
• Per Type Limits - Limit the number of specific types of events per year (for example, no more than X 
running races / year).
• By Venue Limits -  Limit the number of events that can occur at a specific venue per year (in 2026 
the number of events in Community Park was limited due to drought conditions as a conservation measure).
• By Route Limits - Require that events occur on City pre-designated routes (applicants must select 
from a set of pre-set routes for running / bike races). Pre-designated routes make traffic control more 
routine, thereby gaining efficiencies for set-up and take down.

Staff Billing Rates
October 13, 2026
• Police Department - Races, certain larger events, and those serving alcohol 
may require police officers to be in attendance. City has an Extra Duty Officer 
Application and Contract to reimburse the City for police officer time. Rates 
noted are not the fully burdened rates so there is still some subsidy from the 
City when police officers provide coverage.
• Staff intends to correct this for 2027 and revise the rates to reflect the full 
cost to the City.
• Public Works – Public Works provides traffic control for many events, often 
outside of the City’s normal business hours. The City does not currently 
charge for Public Works time.
• Is Council interested in charging for Public Works services?
