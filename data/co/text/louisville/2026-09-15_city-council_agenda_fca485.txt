Subject:  Resolution No. 83, Series 2026 — A Resolution Approving a Quote 
from Insight Public Sector, Inc., to Provide Microsoft 365 and 
Support Services in an Amount Not to Exceed $160,763 
Date: September 15, 2026 
Prepared By: Paulina Bennett, IT Director 
Presented By: Paulina Bennett, IT Director 
 
Summary:  
Presented for Council approval is a request for authorization to renew the City of Louisville annual 
agreement with Insight Public Sector, Inc. for Microsoft 365 licensing and support services. The 
agreement will maintain the City's core licensing for approximately 360 users for a total amount not 
to exceed $160,762.73. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
Microsoft 365 is one of the City's most heavily used technology platforms. It provides the 
applications and services employees use every day to create and store documents, communicate by 
email, collaborate through Microsoft Teams, access shared information through SharePoint and the 
City's intranet, analyze information through Power BI, and securely sign in to City systems. 
 
The City currently maintains Microsoft licensing for approximately 360 users. The proposed 
agreement continues the City's core licensing while providing greater flexibility than the City's prior 
licensing arrangement. 
 
Under Insight's Microsoft Cloud Solution Provider model, the City may add licenses outside the core 
agreement and pay only for the applicable portion of the year. This flexibility is important as the City 
evaluates expanding access to more part-time, seasonal employees, interns, and volunteers through 
the 2027 budget process. Any expansion beyond the core licensing included in this agreement will 
be subject to separate budget authorization. 
 
The proposed licenses and services will be purchased under Insight's Microsoft Cloud Solution 
Provider terms.  
 
Development Proposal: 
N/A 
 
Analysis: 
Information Technology evaluated proposals from Insight, CDW-G, and the Statewide Internet Portal 
Authority (SIPA). Insight provided the lowest annual cost and included support resources that provide 
additional operational value to the City.

Insight's annual proposal is $5,531.11 less than SIPA and $6,300.17 less than CDW-G. 
In addition to providing the lowest cost, Insight includes the following support enhancements not 
included with SIPA or CDW-G: 
• Cloud Success Manager 
• Technical consulting resources 
• Adoption resources 
• Workshops at no additional cost 
• Visioning sessions 
• License Management Portal 
 
The support component is a significant benefit because the City has not previously had this level of 
direct Microsoft support access. This will allow IT staff to submit technical tickets, receive specialized 
assistance, and resolve complex Microsoft issues more efficiently. 
 
The annual renewal provides continuity for the City's core licensing while retaining flexibility for 
workforce changes. The proposal maintains the City's current mix of Microsoft 365 Government 
Community Cloud licenses and specialized products such as Power BI, Planner and Project, Visio, 
Power Apps, Power Automate, and Microsoft 365 Copilot. This Agreement includes no new services 
and is renewing our existing Microsoft license counts.  
 
Council Work Plan: 
This item falls under Council's Core Services priority. 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
The total cost of the annual agreement is $160,762.73. This is budgeted in the 2026 budget for 
Software Subscription and Maintenance Fees 101172-521110. Previously, this item was a multi-year 
subscription-based agreement. This will no longer be a multi-year agreement due to Microsoft 
offering a different style of agreement. This amount covers the City's current core Microsoft licensing 
for approximately 360 users. 
 
Alternatives: 
Do not approve the request. Doing so would prevent the City from renewing one of its most critical 
and widely used technology platforms. Without active licensing, employees could lose access to 
email, Microsoft Teams, Office applications, SharePoint, the City intranet, document storage, and 
other essential services. Failure to renew would significantly disrupt City operations and increase 
cybersecurity, compliance, and business continuity risks. 
 
The city could evaluate Google for business as an alternate to Microsoft. This would require a

significant project and at this time, this is not budgeted nor are resources available to complete this 
effort. Additionally, the City would still need certain Microsoft solutions and would need to add 
additional security applications. 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends approval and authorization to execute the annual renewal with Insight Public 
Sector, Inc. for Microsoft 365 licensing and support services in a total amount not to exceed 
$160,762.73.

Subject:  Resolution No. 84, Series 2026 — A Resolution Approving an Agreement with 
Jason Mumm Consulting Services for Utility Finance Consulting Services 
Date: September 15, 2026 
Prepared By: Cory Peterson, Deputy Director of Utilities 
Presented By: Kurt Kowar, Director 
 
Summary:  
Staff recommends approval of a resolution to approve a sole source agreement with Jason Mumm 
Consulting Services (JMCS) to independently evaluate water, wastewater, and stormwater finances 
for Utility Rate, Cost-of-Service, and Fee Studies. The proposed authorization is a not-to-exceed 
contract of $136,500 and a 20% contingency of $27,500 for a total of $164,000.  
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
The City’s last major utility rate and tap fee evaluation was conducted in 2013 and has served as the 
baseline for the rate-setting process since then. The proposed JMCS scope provides a periodic, 
independent review to ensure the City’s methods and assumptions remain current and consistent 
with industry standards. The review will evaluate water, wastewater, and stormwater finances and 
recommend updates that reflect current service costs, infrastructure needs, and Council priorities. 
Recommendations will address rate stability, equitable cost allocation among customer classes, and 
affordability while maintaining the financial sustainability and reliability of the City’s utility systems. 
 
Staff recommends sole source selection of JMCS based on Jason Mumm’s extensive 
experience and specialized expertise in utility rates, cost-of-service studies, and tap 
fees. His qualifications include 30 years of municipal utility consulting, extensive 
Colorado experience, service as chair of the American Water Works Association’s 
Rates and Charges Committee, and work as a primary editor of AWWA Manual M1. 
This expertise, combined with his familiarity with Louisville’s current analysis and policy 
questions, will allow him to provide an informed, independent review and practical 
recommendations tailored to the City’s needs. 
 
This work builds on recent discussions with the City Council and Finance Committee regarding the 
financial evaluation that may be applied to future rates. A future financial evaluation was briefly 
presented to the City Council at its April 28, 2026, meeting, and the Finance Committee continued 
the discussion on July 16, 2026. Staff presented options for improving revenue stability, aligning 
charges with the cost of service, and planning for capital investment and future growth. The JMCS 
review will provide an independent evaluation of the assumptions and methods underlying these 
discussions and help guide future rate decisions.

Analysis: 
The proposed scope of work is broken down as follows: 
• Water, irrigation, and sewer tap fee study - Deliverables include draft and final tap 
fee memoranda, a recommended fee schedule with transition scenarios, an Excel 
fee model,  a permitted-capacity and rental-charge framework if pursued by the City, 
proposed supporting policies or ordinance amendments, and Finance Committee 
and Council presentation materials.  
• Cost-of-service studies - Deliverables include Excel cost-of-service models and a 
technical memorandum with unit costs by customer class and component for each 
authorized utility.  
• Rate structure design and options - Deliverables include adoption-ready rate 
schedules for the selected option or options, a customer bill impact analysis, and a 
rate-design memorandum.  
• Price elasticity and measurement plan - Deliverables include an elasticity 
memorandum documenting source studies, pooled ranges, and their application, and 
a written plan for future local measurement.  
• Independent analysis and reconciliation - Deliverables include an independent 
analysis and variance-reconciliation memorandum, a professional opinion letter on 
Consultant's results and their defensibility under industry practices and Colorado law 
in coordination with City counsel, and method notes for future staff updates.  
• Financial plan and capital funding review - Deliverables include a financial plan and 
capital funding memorandum with validated trigger years, funding amounts, and 
recommendations for pay-as-you-go versus bond financing.  
• Public engagement and Council support - Deliverables include meeting attendance, 
presentation support, and technical review of public material.s  
• Fee and ordinance crosswalk - Deliverables include a crosswalk covering all three 
utilities, a findings memorandum, and recommended code amendments.  
 
The review is targeted for completion by the end of the second quarter of 2027, with the proposal 
anticipating completion of active consulting work by mid-March. Staff intends to incorporate 
recommendations into the 2027 rate-setting process where timing allows. Follow-up analysis and 
evaluation of recommendations may continue through 2027 as needed. Proposed changes to rates, 
fees, or ordinances may require longer implementation periods depending on their complexity, the 
desired level of customer engagement, and the time needed for Finance Committee review. Any 
proposed changes will return for City Council consideration and approval.  
 
Council Work Plan: 
Consultant scope will provide direct support to the Council goal of ensuring competitive prices for all 
utility services. 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
The proposed evaluation was not included in the current operating budget and will be added through 
a future budget amendment coordinated with the Finance Department. Utility fund reserves are 
sufficient to cover the cost. Costs will be allocated to professional services budget line items as 
follows: 50% to the Water Fund, 30% to the Wastewater Fund, and 20% to the Stormwater Fund.

calculate unit costs by class and component. Water allocations will address base, maximum-
day, maximum-hour, customer, fire protection, and meter costs. Consultant will test staff’s peak-
related cost assumptions and document estimates based on system design criteria and peer 
demand curves where local maximum-hour data are unavailable. Wastewater allocations will 
address flow, biochemical oxygen demand, suspended solids, and customer costs, including the 
cost basis for environmental compliance and industrial surcharges. Stormwater analysis will 
evaluate the demand basis, including impervious area, and potential detention and green 
infrastructure credits and their financial impacts. Task 2 will establish the current baseline for the 
projections in Task 6. 
Deliverables. Excel cost-of-service models and a technical memorandum with unit costs by 
customer class and component for each authorized utility. 
Task 3 Rate structure design and options: Consultant will develop rate options based on City 
direction and the cost-of-service results. Water options will address fixed charges, tier 
breakpoints and rates, seasonal signals, charging from the first gallon, and any water-budget or 
super-saver alternatives retained by the City. Consultant will model monthly bill impacts across 
the residential distribution and nonresidential classes. Each option will address revenue stability, 
conservation signals, essential indoor use, and a multiyear phase-in that limits annual bill 
changes. Consultant will apply Task 4 elasticity assumptions and evaluate revenue variability 
under wet- and dry-year conditions, coordinated with the reserve and rate stabilization 
measures in Task 6. Consultant will provide interim tier-block adjustment options early in this 
task for consideration during the City’s routine 2027 rate adjustment. For water-budget options, 
Consultant will identify the required irrigable-area data and assess which designs the available 
data support. Field or GIS validation of irrigable area is a City responsibility and is excluded 
from Consultant’s scope. Wastewater options will address fixed and volumetric revenue, winter-
quarter averaging, strength-based environmental compliance and industrial surcharges, and 
reuse pricing. Stormwater options will compare flat account charges, tiered impervious-area 
charges, and measured impervious-area charges for billing feasibility and cost support. 
Consultant will evaluate a heavy-irrigation charge, including its cost or policy basis and its 
relationship to tiered rates, landscape-differentiated tap fees, and the capacity rental framework 
in Task 1.
