October 20th Agenda
 
Question – Item 5D – Business Assistance Agreement with Infleqtion Quantum, LLC - Section 4 provides
for consumer-use-tax rebates (with a cap of $189,900 in Section 5(b)), but the § 11 termination provision
does not appear to include the consumer-use-tax category in the event the Company ceases business
operations. Is that the intent?
Answer (Vanessa Zarate): That was not the intent. The edit has been made in the attached revised
agreement. Also, from a practice standpoint, the City does not clawback if the business totally
ceases operations – just if they move to a different jurisdiction.

the property was landmarked in 2015.

agreement with the owner to include the following edits:
 
 
 
Questions – Item 8A – City Manager’s Final 2027-2028 Budget Presentation
Golf Fund – The presentation shows approximately $3.1 million and $3.3 million in revenue for
2027 and 2028, while the fund summary shows approximately $2.896 million and $2.937 million.
Which figures reflect the proposed fee changes?

been updated, and will reflect the correct amounts for the November 2nd
presentation/adoption.

rate conversations, it has been decided to pull the northeast water loop out of the budget.
 
November 2nd Meeting
 
Question – 2027 City Manager Fees – Recreation Fees – How did staff and RAB develop the proposed
increases for 2027?
Answer (Diana Langley): At the September 21st RAB meeting, staff presented rate increase options
and benchmark information for surrounding municipalities (report attached). Yesterday, Ryder
also reached out to Kathy Martin requesting a brief summary of why the Daily Admission Non
Resident Premiums (%s) vary between Daily, Punch, Monthly, and Annual. Ryder noted that at a
high level, the Non Resident premiums (to Residents) are approximately the following:
Punch Passes - ~100% premium
Daily - ~70% premium
Monthly - ~67% premium
Annually - ~75% premium
 
More information will be provided in the 2027 City Manager Fee Council Communication
report. The intent is to keep this item on the Consent agenda.
 
Questions – 2027-2032 Capital Improvement Program
Golf Course Work Carts – The purchase of two work carts are programmed in 2028 and 2029 for
$34,000 each. The project descriptions states, “If we add gas powered carts we would add them in
2028 and electric carts are more expensive so we would add them in 2029 if directed to do so.”
What is the cost of electric carts as compared to gas carts?
Answer (Diana Langley): Staff will get that information and provide to Council prior to the
November 2nd City Council meeting.
Police and Municipal Parking Lot Expansion – The project proposes to install a 5,400 sf secure
parking area for staff parking to the south of the existing parking lot. $20,000 is programmed in
2027 for design and $100,000 is programmed in 2028 for construction. Is it possible to provide a
secure parking area for staff without expanding the parking lot?

particularly when the municipal court is in session, and determining if a portion of the
existing parking lot can be reconfigured to create a secure location for staff parking.

Dear Mayor Leh and Members of City Council:
 
With the deadline for comment approaching, Louisville Open Space Advocates (LOSA) is still
not sure whether City Council is considering consolidating OSAB into the recreation focused
boards or not.  Rather than lose the opportunity to make our views known, LOSA is submitting
this comment opposing the consolidation of OSAB into any other board or commission and
takes no position on the merger of the various recreational bodies.
 
The reasons for our opposition include:
 
1.  The fundamental goal of open space is to protect habitat, viewsheds,and wildlife. In
contrast, the fundamental goal of parks and the golf course is to develop for human
recreation.
2.  Different specialized knowledge and expertise is needed for open space than for
recreational programs.  Only the best qualified people should make decisions on open
space (and recreational programs).
3.  One of the stated rationales of the consolidation under consideration is to make the
recommendations consistent across this group of boards and commissions. OSAB,
however, is bound by the City Charter and specified ordinances, they cannot apply other
processes and standards to open space. In addition, unelected individuals should not be
delegated the authority to resolve competing programmatic needs and values. This is the
work of City Council which can be held accountable for their decisions at elections.
5.  Given the heavy workload that OSAB already handles and that their meetings already
routinely last 2-3 hours, it is unreasonable to ask volunteers to commit even more time. 
It would also be unreasonable for OSAB to shorten the amount of time they spend
discussing complex and important issues in order to handle issues currently handled by
other boards and commissions.
6.  A consolidation would create a board with so much breadth of scope that it would not
be able to serve the needs of City Council, residents, and the City.  It is hard to imagine
OSAB could meet their duties under the city charter if the time devoted to their issues
was shortened.  
7.  Consolidation of OSAB with other boards and commissions is inconsistent with the
spirit of the City Charter.  See section 10-1.
 
Thank you for reading this comment letter.
Best,
 
/s Cathern Smith for Louisville Open Space Advocates (LOSA)

Good afternoon,
 
Below are additional questions and responses for items on tonight’s agenda.
 
Question – Item Gi – Proposition 123 Commitment – Expedited Review Policies for Affordable Housing
Projects – The staff report on expedited review of affordable housing developments notes that
subdivision reviews do not qualify for such review. Why is this the case? Are there places in the City
where an affordable housing development would require a subdivision review?
Answer (Jeff Hirt): The state’s Fast Track requirements do not include subdivision approvals, so our
resolution follows that approach. Yes, affordable housing projects in Louisville could require
subdivision approval when creating new lots or reconfiguring existing ones. If a project requires
both subdivision approval and an otherwise qualifying application, such as a PUD, we would
evaluate Fast Track applicability on a case-by-case basis.
 
Questions – Item 8A – City Manager’s Final 2027-2028 Budget Presentation
Sid Copeland Administration Building - What has changed in the reduced scope compared to the
prior scope? The project sheet continues to include $400k for design in 2028, while stating that
final design is anticipated through the remainder of 2026 (p. 666). Is this correct? Is there
additional design work as part of the re-scope?
Answer (Diana Langley): Staff will update the project sheet to reflect the scope change.
Essentially, the reduced scope is based on expanding the main floor of the administration
building rather than adding a second floor. The timing of design will also be addressed in the
