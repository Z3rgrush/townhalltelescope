Subject:  Approving Consent and Acknowledgment for a Conditional 
Assignment of the LRC Grant Awards for the Grain Elevator 
Date: October 21, 2026 
Prepared By: Vanessa Zarate, Economic Vitality Manager 
Presented By: Vanessa Zarate, Economic Vitality Manager 
 
Summary:  
At the March 11, 2026 LRC meeting, the Louisville Revitalization Commission approved 
a grant award for the Grain Elevator property located at 540 County Road. This award 
included a 90% rebate of newly generated TIF, estimated at $216,000, a Façade 
Improvement Program grant of $72,500 and a Property Improvement Program award of 
$91,500.  
 
At the April 8, 2026 meeting, the LRC amended the Façade Improvement Program 
award by $36,000 to a total award of $108,500. Between all three grant awards, the 
total LRC commitment of $416,000. 
 
The property owner, Louisville Mill Site LLC, requests approval of a Consent and 
Acknowledgment of a conditional assignment of the grants to DR Lending LLC, who is 
providing a construction loan for the project. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
The Grain Elevator represents a unique part of Louisville’s agricultural history and is 
among the last remaining historic grain elevators in the State of Colorado. It has been 
considered a high value property for local historic preservation. The grain elevator was 
established as a national landmark in 1986 and a local landmark in 2015.  
  
In March and April 2026, the LRC approved a TIF (Tax Increment Financing) 
Agreement, Façade Improvement Program Agreement and Property Improvement 
Program agreement for the Grain Elevator project.  
 
In April 21, 2026, City Council approved a Preservation and Restoration Grant, a 
Planned Unit Development (PUD) and Special Review Use (SRU) for the Grain Elevator 
project. The PUD and SRU included approvals to redevelop and restore the property for 
use as an indoor and outdoor eating and drinking establishment and food truck court. 
The Preservation and Restoration Grant provides up to $1,535,789 from the Historic 
Preservation Fund to complete eligible historic preservation and restoration work on the 
Grain Elevator building and construction of an addition on the east side of the structure.  
Page 8 of 30
  
In 2012, the City purchased the Louisville Grain Elevator property for $950,000 using 
Historic Preservation Funds (HPF) in order to avoid the demolition of the historic 
building. The City then sold the property to Louisville Mill Site LLC for $200,000 and 
provided an HPF grant totaling $500,000 to complete stabilization work on the Grain 
Elevator. The City also provided fee waivers totaling $56,975 as part of this initial 
stabilization work and for development plan approvals that have since expired.  
  
The LRC has previously awarded $176,515 for past improvements to the Louisville Mill 
Site LLC. 
 
Development Proposal: 
 
 
Analysis: 
The approved and signed agreements all allow for assignment of the grant awards, with 
LRC approval. Please see below specific information for each grant.  
 
Façade Improvement Program  
Section G.5 of the approved Façade Improvement Program agreement allows for 
assignment of the agreement with approval of the LRC.
 
 
Property Improvement Program  
Section G.5 of the approved Property Improvement Program agreement allows for 
assignment of the agreement with approval of the LRC.  
 
TIF Agreement 
Section 9 f the TIF agreement speaks to assignments and transfers.

was approved to include revenues and expenditures for the urban renewal area.

received for the area and needed carry-forwards for awards that had been awarded but 
not yet completed and paid out to date.  
 
At the September 9, 2026 meeting, the LRC directed staff to move forward with a 
budget amendment for 2026 to combine project line items and assign unused funds into

Prepared By: Vanessa Zarate, Economic Vitality Manager

Summary:  
The Louisville Revitalization Commission (LRC) must approve a budget each year for 
the Urban Revitalization District (URD). The annual budget proposed by the LRC is then 
submitted to the Louisville City Council for review and approval prior to final LRC 
adoption before the end of the year.  
 
Staff is looking for feedback on the proposed 2027 budget with a recommendation for 
the LRC to send the budget to City Council for approval in November. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
The LRC must approve a budget every year for the following year. Per the 
Intergovernmental Agreement with the City of Louisville, the Louisville City Council must 
also approve the LRC budget.  
 
Historically, the LRC initially approved a budget at a meeting and voted to send it to the 
Louisville City Council for approval. After the City Council approval, the budget comes 
back to the LRC for final approval and adoption. Staff is intending for the Louisville City 
Council to approve the budget in November, with final LRC approval at the December 
2026 meeting. 
 
Development Proposal: 
 
 
Analysis: 
City Staff has prepared a proposed 2027 budget for the Highway 42 Urban Renewal 
Area. This budget includes projections for revenues and expenditures.  
The 2027 budget reflects the 2026 budget in many ways, with two notable differences.  
• Improvement Programming- this line item combines the Façade Improvement 
Program and Property Improvement Program line items into one combined line 
item. The funding remains at $650,000 for 2027 (the same level of program 
support in 2026) but combines the programs into one line to be able to provide 
more flexibility to the LRC to award grant funds and meet moving demands with 
Page 23 of 30
our commercial real estate and business communities.  
• Public Art- the LRC has a history of working with the City and our Cultural 
Services team to support public art throughout the Highway 42 Urban Renewal 
Area. Staff is proposing a budget of $75,000 to support continued public art 
throughout the urban renewal area. Some ideas that have arisen include a mural 
festival or some other larger scale art event.

realized at the time the budget is formally approved.

budget.
