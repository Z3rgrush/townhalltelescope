AGENDA ITEM  
Board of Zoning Adjustment (BZA) Consolidation with the Planning Commission – 
Ordinance Review and Recommendation 
BACKGROUND  
This topic was initially discussed by the Board of Trustees during its liaison appointments 
and was subsequently brought forward for further discussion regarding whether the 
functions of the Board of Zoning Adjustment should remain with a separate appointed 
board or be assigned to the Planning Commission. 
Colorado law permits a statutory town to assign the functions of the BZA to the Planning 
Commission. Consolidating these responsibilities is an approach utilized by other 
Colorado municipalities, particularly in communities that receive relatively few variance 
applications. This consolidation could help streamline administrative processes, reduce 
the need for separate board appointments, and improve efficiency in reviewing variance 
requests. 
At its September 1, 2026 meeting, the Board of Trustees discussed the matter and directed 
Staff to work with the Town Attorney to draft an ordinance that would assign the duties and 
responsibilities of the BZA to the Planning Commission. 
At the September 23rd, 2026 meeting the PC directed staff to moved forward with making 
this an action item and did not have any concerns with the draft. 
RECOMMENDED MOTIONS 
Motion to Recommend Approval: 
I move to recommend approval of Ordinance 896 amending Article IX of Chapter 16 of the 
Nederland Municipal Code to provide that the Planning Commission shall serve as the 
Board of Zoning Adjustment.

ORDINANCE NO. 896 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF NEDERLAND, 
COLORADO, AMENDING ARTICLE IX OF CHAPTER 16 OF THE NEDERLAND 
MUNICIPAL CODE TO PROVIDE THAT THE PLANNING COMMISSION SHALL 
SERVE AS THE BOARD OF ZONING ADJUSTMENT 
WHEREAS, the Town of Nederland (“Town”) is a statutory municipality organized and 
existing pursuant to the laws of the State of Colorado; and 
WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 31-23-307, the Board of Trustees is authorized to 
provide by ordinance for the composition and operation of a board of adjustment to hear and 
decide appeals, variances, and other matters arising under the Town’s zoning regulations; and 
WHEREAS, the Board of Trustees finds that assigning the functions of the Board of 
Zoning Adjustment to the Planning Commission will promote administrative efficiency, 
consistent application of the Town’s land use regulations, and informed decision-making; and 
WHEREAS, the Board of Trustees therefore desires to amend Article IX of Chapter 16 of 
the Nederland Municipal Code so that the Planning Commission serves as the Board of Zoning 
Adjustment and exercises the powers and duties assigned to that body. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF 
THE TOWN OF NEDERLAND, COLORADO: 
Section 1. Findings. The foregoing recitals are hereby incorporated as findings of the 
Board of Trustees. 
Section 2. Amendment of Nederland Municipal Code Section 16-231. Section 16-
231 of the Nederland Municipal Code is hereby repealed and replaced to read as follows: 
Sec. 16-231. Planning Commission to serve as Board of Zoning Adjustment. 
(a) The Town of Nederland Planning Commission is hereby designated to serve as the 
Board of Zoning Adjustment of the Town of Nederland, Colorado. When exercising the powers 
and duties assigned by this Article, the Planning Commission shall act as and be referred to as 
the “Board of Zoning Adjustment” or the “Board.” 
(b) The membership, appointment, terms, vacancies, removal, organization, officers, and 
rules of the Planning Commission, as established elsewhere in this Code, shall govern the Board 
of Zoning Adjustment.  
(c) The concurring vote of a majority of the entire membership of the Board shall be 
necessary to reverse any order, requirement, decision, or determination of an administrative 
official; to decide in favor of an applicant any matter upon which the Board is required to pass 
under this Chapter; or to grant any variance under this Chapter. 
Page 8 of 43
(d) Every final decision of the Board is subject to review pursuant to Rule 106(a)(4) of 
the Colorado Rules of Civil Procedure. Any action seeking such review must be filed within the 
time required by applicable law. 
Section 3.  Repeal of Nederland Municipal Code Section 16-234. Section 16-234 of 
the Nederland Municipal Code, concerning alternate members of the former separately appointed 
Board of Zoning Adjustment, is hereby repealed and reserved. 
Section 4. Amendment of Nederland Municipal Code Section 16-235. Section 16-
235(b)(4) and (5) are hereby repealed and replaced to read as follows: 
(4) Administration and enforcement. The Zoning Administrator or designee shall 
administer and enforce this Section. The Zoning Administrator may determine that a 
variance request should be processed as a standard variance before the Board of Zoning 
Adjustment pursuant to Sections 16-232 and 16-233. 
(5) If the Zoning Administrator denies an administrative variance request under this 
Section, the property owner may seek a variance from the Board of Zoning Adjustment 
pursuant to Sections 16-232 and 16-233. 
Section 5. Conforming References. References in the Nederland Municipal Code to 
the “Board of Zoning Adjustment,” “Board of Zoning Review,” or “Board,” when used in 
connection with the powers and duties described in Article IX of Chapter 16, shall mean the 
Planning Commission acting as the Board of Zoning Adjustment. 
Section 6. Transition. Upon the effective date of this Ordinance, the terms of the 
members and alternates appointed solely to the former Board of Zoning Adjustment shall 
terminate. Any matter pending before the former Board of Zoning Adjustment shall be 
transferred to the Planning Commission acting as the Board of Zoning Adjustment, which may 
continue the matter from its existing record or take such additional action as due process and 
applicable law require. 
Section 7. Severability. If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase of this 
Ordinance is held invalid or unconstitutional by a court of competent jurisdiction, the decision 
shall not affect the validity of the remaining portions of this Ordinance. 
Section 8. Repealer. All ordinances or portions thereof inconsistent or conflicting 
with this Ordinance are hereby repealed to the extent of such inconsistency or conflict.

Chipeta Park Playground – A Growing Capital Improvement Priority 
 
The Parks Department continues to face challenges with 
the aging playground equipment at Chipeta Park. Most 
recently, a slide has broken and will require replacement at 
an estimated cost of $5,000–$10,000 for this single 
component. 
This is precisely why the replacement of Chipeta Park's 
playground needs to become a priority for the T own. As the 
equipment continues to age, repairs are becoming 
increasingly expensive, and the cost of replacing individual 
components raises serious questions about the long-term 
financial practicality of continuing to maintain the existing 
playground. 
Chipeta Park is one of Nederland's most heavily used and beloved community spaces. Families 
rely on this playground year-round, and we have a responsibility to ensure that the equipment is 
safe, functional, and capable of serving our community for years to come. 
Rather than continuing to invest significant funds into aging equipment, I believe it is time for 
the T own to begin planning for a comprehensive playground replacement. I will be requesting 
that the Chipeta Park Playground Replacement Project be included as a Capital Improvement 
Project in the 2027 budget. 
Page 42 of 120
Page 24 of 43
This investment is not simply about replacing playground equipment. It is about protecting one 
of our most valuable recreational assets, improving accessibility, and providing a safe and 
welcoming environment for the next generation of Nederland's children. Delaying this project 
will not make the playground any younger or the repairs any less expensive. 
SMS Project – Sidewalk and Crosswalk Improvements 
Construction on the SMS Project is officially getting underway, bringing much-needed 
improvements to sidewalks and crosswalks throughout T own. These improvements will 
enhance pedestrian safety, accessibility, and connectivity for residents and visitors. 
The project represents another important step toward creating a safer, more walkable 
Nederland, particularly in areas where pedestrians and vehicles frequently interact. 
Construction is beginning, with completion currently projected for the end of November. The 
Parks Department looks forward to seeing these improvements take shape and the long-term 
benefits they will provide to the community. 
Wetland Restoration Project – Completed 
The installation of the wetland interpretive sign at 
the Fisherman's Parking Lot marks the final step in 
the completion of the wetland restoration project. 
This project was undertaken as a land reclamation 
effort associated with the Eldora Ignite building 
expansion. The restoration work helped reclaim and 
rehabilitate an important natural area, while the 
newly installed signage provides an opportunity to 
educate visitors about the importance of wetlands 
and their role in our local ecosystem. 
It is rewarding to see this project reach completion. The finished restoration area represents an 
investment in environmental stewardship and the preservation of Nederland's natural 
resources. 
New Zamboni Purchased for Nederland's Ice Rink 
The T own has purchased a replacement Zamboni for the Nederland Ice Rink, retiring the 1984 
machine that has served the facility for many years. 
Page 43 of 120
Page 25 of 43
The replacement was purchased from Blue 
Sport Stable in Louisville, presenting an 
exceptionally rare opportunity to acquire 
a used Zamboni so close to home. Used 
ice resurfacing machines are not 
commonly available, and finding one locally 
is almost unheard of. This opportunity 
allowed the T own to avoid the 
considerable transportation costs and 
logistical challenges typically associated 
with purchasing this type of specialized 
equipment. 
Blue Sport Stable also provided the T own with a very generous discount, making the purchase 
an even greater value. 
Replacing the aging 1984 Zamboni is an important investment in the continued operation of the 
Nederland Ice Rink. Reliable ice resurfacing equipment is essential to maintaining quality ice 
conditions, reducing mechanical downtime, and supporting the rink's long-term success. 
We are grateful to Blue Sport Stable for working with the T own to make this purchase 
possible. 
Fall Winterization of Parks and Town Facilities 
As temperatures begin to drop, the Parks Department has been busy preparing T own 
properties and facilities for the upcoming winter season. 
Winterization efforts include shutting off and securing outdoor water systems at Chipeta Park, 
the Gillaspie House, the Visitor Center, and Town Hall. 
These tasks are an essential part of protecting T own infrastructure from freezing temperatures 
and preventing costly damage to plumbing, irrigation systems, and outdoor fixtures. 
Although winterization may not be one of the department's most visible responsibilities, it 
requires considerable staff time, planning, and coordination. Properly preparing these facilities 
each fall helps reduce unexpected repairs and ensures that T own properties are ready for 
winter.

New Vista School Volunteers – An Outstanding Community 
Partnership 
The Parks Department would like to extend a huge thank you to New Vista School for its 
incredible volunteer efforts supporting wildfire mitigation projects at the Nederland Ice Rink 
and Public Works shop. 
The school sent approximately 20 students who dedicated an 
entire day to helping our department with mitigation work. 
These students were exceptionally hardworking, enthusiastic, 
and willing to tackle physically demanding tasks. 
Their efforts made a meaningful difference in the amount of 
work we were able to accomplish, and their positive attitudes 
and work ethic did not go unnoticed by our staff. 
Partnerships like this are invaluable to a small department with 
a tremendous number of responsibilities. Not only does 
volunteer assistance help us complete important projects, but 
it also provides young people with an opportunity to 
participate directly in caring for their community and public 
spaces. 
We sincerely appreciate New Vista School, the students, and 
the staff who helped organize this effort. Their willingness to give back to the community is 
something worth recognizing and celebrating. 
Department Outlook 
As we transition from the busy summer season into fall and winter operations, the Parks 
Department continues to balance infrastructure improvements, facility maintenance, 
recreational services, and long-term capital planning. While projects such as sidewalk 
improvements, wetland restoration, and equipment replacement represent meaningful progress, 
the growing maintenance needs of aging infrastructure remain a significant concern. 
The department will continue advocating for responsible investments that protect the T own's 
assets, reduce long-term maintenance costs, and ensure Nederland's parks and recreational 
facilities remain safe, functional, and enjoyable for years to come.

Big item of discussion was data centers. Every town is scrambling to figure out what to do 
about it.  
Most municipalities are not accounting for different data center size in legislation, which 
would be wise to do. There are four general types: 
Edge data centers: Smaller ones for gaming, local data processing, etc. 
 Enterprise data centers: Newer facilities are larger, but typically operated by a 
single organization like a hospital, university, etc. 
 Colocation data centers: Multi-tenant facilities, third party servers where 
businesses rent capacity. 
 Hyperscale servers: none in Colorado yet. 10s of thousands of homes equivalent.

Implications of data center development on Colorado climate goals. 
Alex DeGolia: 
 
How data centers could help support emissions reduction goals. 
 The massive increase in demand has cut into the progress made in clean energy 
industry. 
 
Propose that before connecting to data centers, utilities must follow CEP requirements. 
There is a push to exempt data centers from these requirements so a need to refresh and 
explicitly include data center load increases.  
 
Need to include accredited capacity so the clean energy isn’t only available during 
daytime. For example batteries could allow for accredited capacity. 
 
Emission intensity standard should match the best utility in the state. For example, if Meta 
builds its own power plant, it can’t exceed Excel’s emission intensity.

-Capacity that can serve as grid resource.
