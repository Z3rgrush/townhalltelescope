5.1 Temporary Use Permits Draft Ordinance 893 
Britt DeMinck, Community Planner introduced the AIM for Temporary Use 
Permits Draft Ordinance 893. 
 
The Planning Commission reviewed the proposed temporary -use permit 
regulations and generally agreed that the draft was well written, provided clear 
enforcement authority, and should move forward. Commissioners supported 
keeping the regulations flexible enough to accommodate different temporary 
uses while allowing the Town Administrator and staff to determine whether a 
proposed use is appropriate for a particular neighborhood.

Key points discussed included: 
• Temporary-use permits would be reviewed administratively by staff, 
with the Town Administrator making the final decision.  
• Permits would generally be limited to 90 days.  
• Staff could suspend or revoke a permit if the use creates health, safety, 
animal-welfare, or neighborhood impacts.  
• Continuing an activity after permit revocation would constitute a civil 
code violation and could result in municipal court penalties.  
• The general penalty provisions already in the Municipal Code are 
sufficient and do not need to be repeated in this section.  
• A permit -processing fee should be established by resolution and 
added to the Town’s fee schedule.  
• Neighbor notification would not be automatically required for every 
temporary use. However, staff may be authorized to require notification 
as a condition of approval when a use could significantly affect 
surrounding properties.  
• Review criteria already address unreasonable noise, odor, dust, 
lighting, traffic, and compatibility with neighborhood character.  
• The regulations would apply broadly rather than limiting temporary 
uses to particular zoning districts.  
• The administrative process should reduce staff workload and allow 
applications to be processed without waiting for Planning Commission 
or Board of Trustees meetings.  
 
The Commission directed staff to work with the Town Attorney and bring this 
as an action item for the next meeting. 
 
5.2 Flex Zoning 
Britt DeMinck, Community Planner introduced the AIM for Flex Zoning. 
 
The Planning Commission held an initial discussion about “flex zoning, ” a term 
referenced in the Comprehensive Plan and Downtown Subarea Plan but not 
currently defined in the Municipal Code or adopted planning documents. The 
concept is intended to provide greater flexibility for housing, redevelopment, 
commercial underused or vacant of the productive and activity, use 
properties.

Key points included: 
• Possible approaches include expanding permitted housing types, 
allowing a broader mix of residential and commercial uses, creating 
affordable-housing incentives, establishing an overlay or new zoning 
district, and using performance-based standards.  
• The industrial district, particularly the Wolf Tongue area, was identified 
as overly restrictive and potentially outdated. Options could include 
adding more permitted uses or creating a light-industrial category.  
• General Commercial properties near East Street Garage and Mountain 
Auto were also discussed because limited infrastructure and existing 
zoning may make development difficult.  
• The Commission emphasized balancing housing needs with 
commercial development and sales-tax revenue.  
• Rather than creating a broadly defined “flex zone, ” it may be more 
practical to review individual zoning districts and expand their 
permitted uses.  
• Property owners may continue using existing rezoning or application 
processes while the broader discussion continues.  
 
The Commission agreed that this issue depends heavily on the Board of 
Trustees’ upcoming strategic plan and should not advance immediately. Staff 
will summarize the discussion in an upcoming Board staff report and seek 
preliminary direction. If the Board i dentifies zoning flexibility as a priority, the 
Planning Commission may hold a work session around September to examine 
specific districts, vacant properties, and potential code amendments. Staff 
will also provide additional clarification about performance -based zoning 
standards.

AGENDA ITEM  
Amendment to Temporary Use Provisions – Administrative Approval of Similar Temporary 
Uses 
PURPOSE 
Ordinance No. 893 establishes a clearer and more flexible process for reviewing temporary 
uses. The proposed amendments would: 
• Establish a Temporary Use Permit process; 
• Define application requirements, review criteria, and conditions of approval; 
• Add standards for temporary grazing and other short-term land-management 
activities; 
• Authorize the Zoning Administrator to approve temporary uses that are substantially 
similar in character, duration, intensity, and impact to uses specifically listed in the 
code; and 
• Protect surrounding properties, public health and safety, natural resources, and 
environmentally sensitive areas. 
BACKGROUND 
At a previous meeting, the Planning Commission discussed the challenges associated with 
reviewing temporary activities that are not specifically listed in Section 16-77. 
The Commission expressed interest in a more flexible administrative process that would 
allow the Zoning Administrator to evaluate similar temporary uses, provided they satisfy 
established criteria and do not adversely affect neighboring properties or the community. 
The existing code offers limited guidance for activities that do not fit within the listed 
temporary-use categories. As new and evolving activities arise, this limitation has created 
uncertainty for applicants and staff. Ordinance No. 893 responds by replacing the existing

The structure below separates: 
• Goals: Broad community outcomes  
• Objectives: What the Town intends to accomplish  
• Key results: How the Board will know the objective was achieved  
• Initiatives: Specific projects, studies, staffing decisions, grants, and 
implementation activities  
Placeholder conventions 
• [DATE]: A deadline selected by the Board  
• [PLAN PERIOD]: The adopted strategic planning period  
• [TARGET]: A measurable performance target  
• [NUMBER]: A quantity selected after reviewing capacity  
• [BASELINE]: The starting measurement  
• [TIMEFRAME]: A period tied to another event, such as acquisition closing  
• [FUNDING CYCLE]: The next applicable grant or funding deadline

AGENDA ITEM  
Outdoor Commercial Activities – Use Group Table Discussion 
 
PURPOSE  
The Planning Commission will discuss whether staff should move forward with drafting 
amendments to the Use Group Table for “Outdoor sales, eating and drinking, or other outdoor 
commercial activities or establishments, ” which are currently restricted in the GC, CBD, and NC 
zoning districts, and whether these uses should be permitted by right in all three districts. 
 
BACKGROUND  
The Use Group Table currently restricts “Outdoor sales, eating and drinking, or other outdoor 
commercial activities or establishments” within the General Commercial (GC), Central Business 
District (CBD), and Neighborhood Commercial (NC) zoning districts. 
The Board of Trustees requested that the Planning Commission review the existing restrictions and 
consider whether amendments to the Use Group Table are appropriate. 
Specifically, the Planning Commission is being asked to discuss whether these outdoor 
commercial activities should be permitted as a use by right and whether that designation is 
appropriate in each of the GC, CBD, and NC zoning districts. 
 
PC DISCUSSIO N  
The Planning Commission should consider: 
• Whether staff should move forward with drafting amendments to the Use Group Table; 
• Whether outdoor sales, eating and drinking, and other outdoor commercial activities or 
establishments should be permitted as a use by right; 
• Whether use by right is appropriate in GC, CBD, and NC, or whether different treatment is 
appropriate in certain zoning districts; and 
• Whether additional standards, limitations, or definitions should be considered as part of a 
future amendment. 
 
REQUESTED DIRECTION  
Staff is seeking direction from the Planning Commission on whether to proceed with drafting 
amendments to the Use Group Table and, if so, in which zoning districts “Outdoor sales, eating and 
drinking, or other outdoor commercial activities or establishments” should be permitted as a use 
by right. 
 
Based on the Planning Commission’s direction, staff would prepare draft amendments for 
subsequent review and consideration.

Trails and Mountain Biking 
The Parks Department is excited to report that the 
Rink Link Trail is nearing completion. This project has 
been a large undertaking for our department over the 
last couple of years and represents countless hours of 
planning, public outreach, design coordination, and 
construction oversight. 
From the earliest community conversations to the build 
phase taking place today, every member of the 
department has contributed to bringing this vision to 
reality. The result will be a fully ADA-accessible trail 
connecting the rink area while improving recreational 
opportunities for residents and visitors of all ages and 
abilities. 
While completing the primary accessible trail remains our highest priority, we are already 
looking ahead to future enhancements. One concept under consideration is a one- to two-
foot-wide natural side trail that would incorporate technical mountain bike features for riders 
seeking a more challenging experience. This feature would allow experienced cyclists to enjoy 
technical riding opportunities while preserving the accessibility and safety of the main trail.  
Another exciting opportunity is the creation of a beginner "Learn to Bike" course in the rink 
parking lot. This space would provide young children with a safe environment to learn balance, 
pedaling, and basic bicycle handling skills away from traffic. Developing confident young riders in

Page 42 of 56
a controlled setting supports healthy recreation while helping 
families avoid learning on public roadways. 
The Rink Link Trail demonstrates the T own's continued 
investment in accessible recreation while creating opportunities 
for future improvements that serve users of all skill levels. 
 
Chipeta Park Rock Wall Improvements 
The reconstruction of the rock retaining wall at the entrance to Chipeta Park has been 
successfully completed. The new wall creates a more welcoming gateway into one of 
Nederland's most cherished parks while improving both the aesthetics and long-term stability 
of the entrance. 
This project is another example of the Parks Department's commitment to preserving and 
enhancing the T own's infrastructure. While visitors may simply notice an attractive new 
entrance, the completed wall represents skilled craftsmanship, and an investment in the 
longevity of Nederland's most heavily used community space. The improved entrance 
complements the park's natural beauty and reflects the pride our staff takes in maintaining 
Chipeta Park as a centerpiece of the community.

The Challenges Behind the Scenes 
While large capital projects often receive the most attention, much of the department's time 
continues to be devoted to unexpected maintenance issues that require immediate response. 
Equipment breakdowns, clogged restroom fixtures, fallen trees, irrigation repairs, and countless 
other unforeseen issues regularly interrupt scheduled work and require staff to quickly adjust 
priorities. 
In addition to routine maintenance, vandalism continues to place 
unnecessary demands on department resources. Recent graffiti 
at the skate park required staff time for cleanup and 
restoration—time that would otherwise have been spent on 
planned maintenance and improvement projects throughout the 
park system. 
Despite these challenges, Parks staff continue to respond with 
professionalism and dedication, balancing emergency repairs, 
routine maintenance, special events, and major construction 
projects while maintaining the high level of service our 
community expects. 
The department remains committed to improving Nederland's parks, trails, and public spaces 
while adapting to the daily demands that come with managing an active and growing park 
system.

Road Maintenance 
Throughout August, Streets staff continued routine road maintenance and grading 
operations across town. Work was completed on the following roadways: 
• Alpine Road 
• Lower Navajo Trail 
• Breed Street 
• Sundance Circle 
• South Jackson Street 
• West Spring Street 
• West Pine Street 
Maintenance focused on improving roadway conditions, addressing drainage 
concerns, restoring proper road crowns where necessary, and maintaining safe and 
reliable travel surfaces. As we move toward the end of the summer maintenance 
season, staff continues to prioritize areas requiring attention while taking 
advantage of the remaining favorable weather. 
Culvert Installation, Replacement, and Drainage Improvements 
A significant portion of Streets Department work during August was dedicated to 
culvert installation, replacement, cleaning, and drainage improvements. 
At the intersection of Alpine Road and Wildwood Road, as well as the 
intersection of South Hendricks Street and Spruce Street, staff repaired the 
damaged ends of primary drainage culverts and installed new inlet pans. This 
work involved excavating the existing culvert ends, removing damaged sections, 
installing extensions, and constructing proper inlet pans to improve water 
collection and flow. 
Staff then used the Vac truck to clear accumulated rocks, sediment, and road base 
that had become tightly packed inside the culverts. The repaired areas were 
finished with high-density erosion-control fabric and riprap to stabilize the

Additional extensive drainage work was completed at Breed Street and South

Perma-Zyme Road Stabilization Pilot 
The Perma-Zyme Road Stabilization Pilot Project is scheduled for the first 
week of September. 
A company engineer will be on site during the project to assist Streets staff and 
ensure that the product is mixed, applied, graded, and compacted according to the 
manufacturer's specifications. Having technical support present during the initial 
application will allow staff to gain firsthand experience with the process while 
giving the pilot the best opportunity to accurately demonstrate the product's 
effectiveness. 
The Streets Department will also be renting a larger water truck for the week. 
The additional water capacity will help streamline the application process, reduce 
downtime associated with refilling, and allow staff to maintain the proper 
moisture levels required during road preparation and product application.

Training and Community Support 
The Streets Department continues looking for opportunities to expand the skills of 
our staff and increase the ways we can assist the Nederland community and our 
partner agencies. 
As part of that effort, Streets Manager Joshua Davis will attend Basic 
Wildland Firefighter training from August 27 through August 31. This 
training builds upon the Sawyer A certification completed earlier this summer and 
will further develop the department's ability to safely assist with wildfire 
mitigation, fuels reduction, and other appropriate community needs. 
Additional professional development is scheduled for Streets staff this fall. 
Upcoming training includes a course in Surveying, Grade Checking, and 
Staking for Excavation. These skills have direct applications to road 
construction, drainage improvements, culvert installation, excavation, and 
infrastructure projects and will help staff perform more work efficiently and 
accurately in-house. 
Continuing education remains an important priority for the department. 
Developing a versatile and well-trained crew not only improves the quality and 
safety of our daily operations but also allows the Streets Department to be as 
useful as possible to the Town and the community we serve. 
Looking Ahead 
As August comes to a close, staff will begin transitioning from the height of the 
summer road and drainage maintenance season toward fall projects and 
preparations for winter operations. September priorities will include completion 
of the Perma-Zyme pilot project, continued drainage and roadway improvements, 
equipment maintenance, and preparation of snow-removal equipment for the 
upcoming winter season. 
The Streets Department will continue making the most of the remaining 
construction season while maintaining our focus on improving Nederland's 
roadway and drainage infrastructure and providing dependable service to the 
community.
