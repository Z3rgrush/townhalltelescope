VI. General Business 
 A. Planning Commission Follow-Up Discussion from the Joint Study Session with Town 
Council 
 
Chair Sawusch opened discussion to follow up on the Joint Study Session with Town 
Council, which included discussion on Proposition 123, Comprehensive Plan 
Page 4 of 225
Amendments, the Planning & Development 2027 Work Plan, and the Planning 
Commission's Roles & Responsibilities. 
 
 
Proposition 123: 
Commissioners explored next steps, including requirements from DOLA, and staff 
provided updates on ongoing conversations.  
 
Eliot Schaefer, Deputy Town Attorney, explained that three resolutions related to Prop 123 
were forthcoming before Town Council.  
 
Kelly Driscoll, Deputy Director of Planning and Development, confirmed that Erie is 
requesting a reduction in its Prop 123 unit requirement to 30 units, a request strengthened 
by the Town’s strong 2024 building permit numbers.  
 
Commissioners asked questions about DOLA discussions, waiver requests from other 
communities, and how other municipalities are navigating similar requirements.  
 
There was also substantial discussion about how Prop 123 might influence future updates 
to the UDC, particularly concerning affordable housing.  
 
Staff emphasized that affordable housing would be one of several focus areas within the 
broader UDC update. 
 
 
Comprehensive Plan Amendments: 
Chair Sawusch noted that since the Comprehensive Plan was recently approved, no 
additional discussion about modifying it was needed at this time. 
 
Kelly Driscoll explained that Windsor and several other peer communities allow their 
Planning Commissions to make recommendations on Comprehensive Plan amendments, 
and commissioners engaged in discussion about whether Erie should adopt a similar 
approach.  
 
Ultimately, the Planning Commission agreed to request the ability to make 
recommendations to Town Council on future Comprehensive Plan amendments, even 
though no immediate changes to the plan were under consideration. 
 
 
Planning & Development 2027 Work Plan: 
Chair Sawusch asked staff whether any updates or changes had been made to the 
Planning & Development 2027 Work Plan.  
 
Kelly Driscoll confirmed that there were no updates and that staff was not aware of any 
forthcoming changes. 
 
 
Planning Commission Roles & Resonsibilities: 
At Chair Sawusch’s request, Kelly Driscoll walked commissioners through the memo 
included in their packet, which detailed how various municipalities handle Planning 
Commission authority. Communities considered in the report included Windsor, identified 
as the closest comparable to Erie due to its similar size and growth rate. Kelly explained 
Page 5 of 225
the table of responsibilities and highlighted that Erie’s Planning Commission has a 
utilization rate of 67 percent, the second-highest among the communities studied.  
 
Commissioners asked clarifying questions about the Commission’s involvement in capital 
planning — Kelly confirmed Planning Commissions typically do not participate — and 
about staff recommendations for findings of fact, which staff deferred to Town Council and 
Administration. 
 
Commissioner Shatz elaborated on his memo, expressing a desire for the Commission to 
review final plats and initial zoning and voicing concerns about the lack of appeal 
pathways for administrative decisions.  
 
Kelly clarified that the Board of Adjustment & Appeals handles all appeals, including those 
stemming from administrative actions.  
 
Commissioners discussed ADU processes, the implications of Erie not being an ADU 
conforming community, and the broader subdivision process, noting that sketch plans are 
generally not used by applicants.  
 
The Planning Commission also discussed SRUs (Special Review Use), location and 
extent review (with Eliot Schaefer confirming this as a gap in the UDC), and whether staff 
would prepare a memo summarizing the meeting for Town Council, which staff affirmed. 
 
The Planning Commission will formally request four changes: an annual Joint Study 
Session with Town Council, the ability to make recommendations for UDC amendments, 
the ability to make recommendations for Comprehensive Plan amendments, and the 
ability to review and make recommendations on initial zoning.  
 B. Planning Commission By-Laws 
 
Chair Sawusch opened discussion on the Planning Commission By-Laws. 
 
The Deputy Director of Planning & Development introduced the draft by-laws to the 
Commission, prompting a series of questions and concerns from commissioners as they 
reviewed each section.  
 
Commissioners raised concerns about Section 11 regarding absences, particularly how 
the provision would be applied and interpreted.  
 
There were also questions about Section 6, especially related to who has the authority to 
cancel meetings and whether executive sessions are necessary under certain 
circumstances.  
 
Commissioners asked about Section 10 and whether yearly appointments should continue 
as they traditionally have, along with concerns about the pooling of time allowed within 
that section.  
 
Additional attention was given to Section 7, where commissioners questioned whether 
disclosures alone are a sufficient remedy for ex parte contact. 
 
Chair Sawusch expressed concern about the Commission’s ability to call special meetings 
and study sessions, which led to broader discussion about meeting schedules and the 
Page 6 of 225
Commission’s flexibility within the by-laws.  
 
Commissioners also discussed evidentiary rules in Section 14 and the implications for 
how hearings and decision-making processes are conducted. Some members raised 
concern about the use of the word “generally”; noting it may cause ambiguity. 
 
There was further discussion about the presence of legal counsel at meetings and 
whether by-laws should specify expectations around attorney attendance. 
 
Commissioners closed the conversation with questions about staggered terms, seeking 
clarity on how commissioner appointments and term timing should be structured under the 
draft by-laws.

the motion. Chair Sawusch adjourned the meeting at 8:32 P.M.

Site History: 
The original Town of Erie plat in 1928 laid out this property. Coal Miner Park occupies 
the southern half of this property, while the north side is the former Lion’s Club building. 
The current owner, Mona Chughtai, purchased the property in 2023. Until recently, the 
entire property has been under private ownership and the Town had a long-term lease 
for Coal Miner Park. Town Council recently approved a Minor Plat Amendment to split 
the property into two separate lots for the purpose of having the Town purchase the 
southern half of the property where Coal Miner Park sits. This Plat Amendment is in the 
process of being recorded with Weld County. The private property owner will retain 
ownership of the northern half of the property, which is the subject of this application. 
 
The applicant, Montessori Children’s World, proposes a large Childcare Center (16+ 
students). While a small Childcare Center (less than 16 students) is an allowed use by 
right in this zoning district, a larger operation requires a SRU approval from Planning 
Commission and Town Council. 
 
Future Required Applications: 
If Town Council approves the SRU, the applicant may proceed to permitting and 
construction.  
 
Proposal Overview: 
Montessori Children’s World proposes no significant site changes and thus does not 
trigger a full Site Plan review. It does, however, require a Special Review Use as a 
large Childcare Center. 
 
The site improvements will include new fencing to enclose a dedicated outdoor play 
space, new ADA infrastructure, and numerous internal building code updates. 
 
The operation will take advantage of existing parking lot and sidewalk infrastructure for 
access. While the location is adjacent to the existing Coal Miner Park, Montessori 
Children’s World will establish its own outdoor play area enclosed with fencing.

Access and Parking: 
The property has an existing parking lot that accesses High Street and the alleyway to 
the east. There are twelve parking spaces, including two ADA spaces, which are 
included in the operational plan for Montessori Children’s World. No off-street parking 
spaces are required for this project because the UDC exempts commercial uses in Old 
Town from off-street parking requirement. 
However, if off-street parking were to be required for this project, the UDC calculates 
parking for this type of use at one space per six children of licensed capacity. The 
twelve existing spaces would allow for up to 72 students, which is a much larger 
number than Montessori Children’s World expects to serve at this location. The final 
licensed capacity number will depend on State of Colorado approval. 
 
Additional Use and Operational Information: 
The operating hours will be Monday through Friday from 7:30am to 5:00pm, with a 
current staff of four teachers. The expected enrollment is more than 16 students but 
based on eventual licensing by the State of Colorado and is not known for certain at 
this time.

STAFF REVIEW AND ANALYSIS 
Staff finds the application is consistent with the Special Review Use approval criteria in the 
UDC, Section 10.7.11.C.2, as outlined below. 
a. The proposed use is generally consistent with the town's comprehensive plan and all 
applicable provisions of this UDC and applicable state and federal regulations; 
Staff: This large Childcare Center use is consistent with the secondary uses that are 
listed in the Comprehensive Plan for the Residential – Low category. The proposed use 
also seeks to revitalize a currently vacant building, which supports Comprehensive Plan 
goals related to infill and adaptive reuse.  
b. The proposed use is generally consistent with the purpose and intent of the zoning district 
in which it is located; 
Staff: The use is consistent with the NMU zone district. A small Childcare Center use is 
an allowed use by right, as it is consistent with the NMU district’s intent to include both 
residential and non-residential uses.  
c. The proposed use is generally consistent with any applicable use-specific standards set 
forth in section 10-3-2; 
Staff: The applicant is aware of and complying with state law, including without limitation 
C.R.S. § 26-6-101, et seq. and the Regulations of the Colorado Department of Human 
Services (12 CCR 2509-9). 
d. The proposed use is compatible with adjacent uses in terms of scale, site design, and 
operating characteristics (hours of operation, traffic generation, lighting, noise, odor, dust, 
and other external impacts); 
Staff: The proposed use makes modest building and site improvements, without 
constructing new buildings or making drastic changes to the scale of the development. 
The single-story building fits in well with the surrounding neighborhood, especially as an 
existing building that has been part of the Old Town neighborhood for many years. Staff 
expect the existing parking lot to be more than sufficient for the function of dropoff and 
parking for staff and visitors, and do not expect significant offsite traffic impacts. Hours of 
operation are typical business hours. 
e. Any significant adverse impacts anticipated to result from the use will be mitigated or 
offset to the maximum extent reasonably practicable; 
Staff: Staff do not anticipate significant adverse impacts from this use. 
f. Facilities and services (including sewage and waste disposal, water, gas, election, police 
and fire protection, and streets and transportation, as applicable) will be available to serve 
the subject property while maintaining adequate levels of service for existing 
development; 
Staff: As a site within an existing development, infrastructure and utilities exist and are 
available to serve this site/use. 
g. Adequate assurances of continuing maintenance have been provided; and 
Staff: Staff have no concerns about continuing maintenance and believe allowing this 
use to occupy this previously vacant building will promote long-term maintenance and 
viability of the site.

Narrative
My name is Mona Chughtai, I am the Director of Children’s World Montessori. For the 
past nine years, our early childhood program has been operating out of a church 
basement in Old Town Erie, providing excellent academic, leadership and social-
emotional learning. As the highly recommended program in the area we stay at capacity 
with a months-long waiting list as we cannot exceed to serve 15 children. 
To meet the high demand for quality childcare in our community, we are preparing to 
relocate to and renovate the property at 480 Cheesman Street. The property being 
significantly larger than the current location will accommodate more children and reduce 
waiting time for the families to get into the program. 
The current zoning allows for a small childcare center of 15 children. We are requesting 
Large Child Care Center,(16+ children). The final number of children will be decided by 
the Colorado state Licensing according to the square footage of the building. 
Our project aims to renovate and transform this blighted site into a functional, modern 
facility that will allow us to expand our enrollment and add valuable commercial space to 
this prominent Old Town location. 
To ensure the privacy and safety of the children we are requesting a 6 foot privacy fence 
to enclose the playground (the back side of the property facing the alley). 
We expect to begin renovations within the next three months, with a projected 
completion timeline of six months.
