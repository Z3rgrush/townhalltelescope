Executive Summary 
The purpose of this joint workshop is to provide City Council and the Planning Commission 
an opportunity to discuss three topics related to the Land Use Code update: Old Town Mixed-
Use (“OTX”) zoning districts, potential amendments to sustainable design requirements, and 
roof design for solar access.  
This memo primarily focuses on the OTX district discussion and includes requested spatial 
analysis of Old Town (e.g., lot sizes, building scales, alley networks, etc.). This analysis has 
been supplemented with additional context on how the new Code form-based regulations 
can be tailored to address the site-specific circumstances unique to these areas of Old 
Town. The memo also includes potential policy options for addressing concerns about the 
draft OTX-3 district located south of Emma Street.  
The Land Use Code Working group met on Sept. 2, 2026. Feedback from that meeting will 
be provided during the joint workshop presentation.  
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Sense of Place 

2 
 
Background Information 
At its July 8, 2026, meeting, the Planning Commission voted to recommend approval of the 
draft Land Use Code, with 17 recommended conditions of approval. One of the seventeen 
conditions was to hold a joint Planning Commission/City Council meeting to discuss OTX 
zoning, with specific concerns regarding the OTX-3 zoning. Planning Commission also 
requested additional graphic analysis on Public Road to better understand the existing 
conditions, including lot sizes, alleys, curb cuts, building heights, building uses, and parking 
areas.  
At its Aug. 18, 2026, meeting, City Council agreed with Planning Commission’s 
recommendation for a joint meeting to discuss OTX zoning and concerns with OTX-3, which 
is proposed to govern Public Road, south of Emma Street. 
The City received feedback from the community regarding the maximum building footprint 
permissible in OTX-3 zoning, which is drafted to limit buildings to 25,000 sq. ft. OTX-1 and 
OTX-2 also have building size maximums of 10,000 sq. ft. and 15,000 sq. ft., respectively. 
Currently there are no commercial building size maximums in the General Business (“B1”) 
zoning district that governs Old Town’s commercial areas.  
In addition to the existing conditions maps and analysis Planning Commission requested, 
staff has included additional context as to why multiple OTX zoning districts are proposed 
and the various guiding documents and objectives that were considered. Decisions on the 
Land Use Code update also take into account both existing allowances and existing uses. 
Although Legacy Lafayette prescribed a unique zoning district for Old Town, it did so to 
maintain the area’s existing character rather than change it. This approach differs from 
other zoning updates supported in Legacy Lafayette, where zoning updates are intended to 
affect change over time.  
Old Town Mixed-Use Zoning (“OTX”) 
The Legacy Lafayette Comprehensive Plan envisions the Old Town Mixed-Use land use as 
maintaining the area's eclectic, small-town character while integrating new development into 
the existing urban fabric. The plan promotes maintaining the historic orthogonal grid, block 
sizes, and lot width patterns that define Old Town's character.  
Legacy Lafayette promotes mixed-use zoning in both commercial and residential areas. As 
part of the Land Use Code Update, opportunities for residential uses in traditionally 
commercial districts have expanded. Opportunities for commercial uses within new 
residential developments have also increased and will be required for developments that 
exceed 40 acres. Because OTX zones are classified as commercial zoning districts, new 
residential uses will also be permitted only when residential uses are included as part of a 
mixed-use development that also includes commercial. 
3 
 
Key design and land use principles established by the comprehensive plan include: 
• Historic Pattern & Scale Preservation: Development should fit into the surrounding 
area, prioritize the adaptive reuse of existing structures, and enforce building step-
downs or transitions in height to protect landmarked properties and adjacent 
residential neighborhoods.  
• Active Main Street Corridors: S. Public Road, E. Simpson Street, and Baseline Road 
are described as active, walkable corridors. The plan calls for high ground-floor 
transparency, minimal front setbacks, active ground-floor commercial or civic uses, 
and upper-story residential or office space.  
• Corridor Revitalization & Main Street Character: Revitalization along S. Public Road 
toward S. Boulder Road focuses on extending a cohesive Main Street. 
• Site Design & Parking Placement: Primary access and vehicle parking are directed to 
alleys or side streets to keep parking behind buildings, ensuring continuous, 
pedestrian-oriented street frontages and corner building orientations throughout the 
district.

Under the existing land use regulations, the General Business (“B1”) district governs Old 
Town as well as other commercial areas across the city, providing no tailored standards 
specific to Old Town's distinct historic character. To address this gap, the initial OTX zoning 
draft (shared in Module 3) introduced a two-district approach with OTX-1 applied to lots 
fronting Public Road, E. Simpson Street, and Baseline Road, and OTX-2 applied to properties 
not facing those streets. This draft also included several form-based code elements, 
including building orientation requirements, lot combination limits, and maximum building 

5 
 
footprint caps of 5,000 sq. ft. and 16,000 sq. ft., respectively, in addition to adaptive reuse 
incentives.

Since restarting this project, the Land Use Code update has set and maintained an 
aggressive schedule throughout the past 18 months. To maintain the schedule, staff has 
shared code drafts with the public and the working group at nearly the same time as staff 
receives draft documents for review. Staff’s review of Module 3 found that the two-district 
approach failed to capture the nuances of different Old Town sub-areas. Staff also directed 
the City’s code consultants to adjust the maximum building footprints up from 5,000 sq. ft. 
to avoid creating non-conforming buildings in the OTX1 district.  
Site constraints, existing buildings and uses, and lot and block sizes were among the initial 
existing conditions staff considered when evaluating the proposed subdistricts. Old Town 
Tomorrow, which had recently looked at this same area, was another source that influenced 
the suggested boundaries for OTX-1, OTX-2, and OTX-3 subdistricts detailed below. 
• Old Town Main Street (“OTX-1”): Public Road north of Emma Street and E. Simpson 
Street are the two most traditional main streets in the Old Town Mixed-Use 
Comprehensive Plan category. Development abutting these roads should be oriented 
to the street, lined with smaller-scaled buildings, focused on ground floor commercial 
uses, and reflect the mix of structure sizes and types that comprise the diverse 
design of Lafayette’s original neighborhood, described in more detail in the 
Comprehensive Plan. 
• Old Town Baseline Corridor (“OTX-2”): Development on the south side of Baseline 
Road should allow for infill and adaptive reuse along with the extension of a compact, 

6 
 
walkable mix of structures and uses that help create a northern gateway into Old 
Town as the heart of the City. 
• Old Town Transition (“OTX-3”): The Old Town Transition district is that area along 
Public Road south of Emma Street where the scale and character of the area 
transitions from the core, walkable retail and commercial area to a mix of larger 
building forms with a mix of pedestrian and auto-oriented commercial uses that may 
include residential and mixed-use infill over time. 
In addition to explaining how these three districts are viewed, it is also helpful to clarify how 
they differ. Public Road north of Emma and E. Simpson Street function as walkable main 
streets for Old Town Lafayette, with short consistent block lengths. Lot sizes on OTX-1 are 
also smaller (averaging 8,300 sq. ft.) and these properties are typically served by alleys. 
Form-based zoning regulations can be drafted to both require parking to be derived from 
alleys and for parking to be limited to what can be afforded directly from alleys.  
Commercial properties along the south side of Baseline Road in OTX-2 operate differently. 
Vehicle speeds on Baseline Road differ (35 MPH vs 25 MPH), and although many of these 
lots have alley access, uninterrupted blocks limit their utility and often require access to 
come from Baseline. OTX-2 was created to address the unique needs of the commercial 
properties along the south side of Baseline with the knowledge that access to the site, 
parking, and form-based development controls would also need to be adjusted to respond to 
these factors.  
Although Public Road still has the same block form south and vehicle speeds south of Emma 
Street, the average lot size in OTX-3 exceed 23,000 sq. ft. and presence and utility of alleys 
become significantly less consistent, making Emma Street a natural transition point along S. 
Public Road between the historic core to the north and larger commercial lots to the south. 
This break is also consistent with the one included in the Old Town Tomorrow Action Plan.   
To accommodate these distinct physical layouts, alley networks, and existing development 
patterns, the project team refined the original framework into a three-district approach that 
largely also resolved building non-conformities.

Staff finds the additional maps and data continue to support the benefit of having three Old 
Town zoning districts. The data, however, is not the singular factor that has informed the 
recommendation for these three districts. The Land Use Code update also includes goals of 
providing additional predictability for development. The multiple district approach will allow 
for more nuanced form-based zoning tools and more specificity in use allowances. Alley 
access and parking controls have been noted for OTX-1 above. OTX-3 form regulations can 
be written to address development on larger lots. For example, commercial buildings can be 
required along South Public and transitional uses (such as residential) can be required or 
encouraged on the back of properties that abut adjacent residential uses. OTX-3 form 
8 
 
regulations will also need to address curb-cuts/vehicle access from South Public, encourage 
shared access, and address/limit parking visibility from public rights-of-way.  
General building uses south of Emma Street in OTX3 differ from OTX-1. While OTX-1 centers 
on smaller-scale main street retail, neighborhood dining, and local services, OTX-3 
accommodates broader commercial and larger format uses, like auto services, a post office, 
an HVAC contractor, and greenhouse/florist building. Establishing a district south of Emma 
Street provides City with a regulatory framework to accommodate larger buildings, regulate 
for specialized commercial operations and unique land uses within a defined segment of S. 
Public Road rather than allowing them to expand into the historic core (or banning them 
outright in Old Town).  
Public comments, Planning Commission, and City Council feedback appear to focus on the 
scale of development that could occur in OTX-3, with the 25,000 sq. ft. building footprint 
emerging as a central concern. The 10,000 sq. ft. (“OTX-1”), 15,000 sq. ft. (“OTX-2”), and 
25,000 sq. ft. (“OTX- 3”) limits have been presented based on the following context: 
• That no building size limit currently exists; 
• That the largest buildings in Old Town approach or slightly exceed 10,000 sq. ft.; and 
• That larger lots in Old Town (within OTX3) could currently build structures with 
footprints that range from 20,000 to 52,000 sq. ft. 
The City has also heard comments that the Land Use Code Update is not doing enough to 
remedy the development review process. One key tool that is intended to help with 
development review is predictability and the draft form-based regulations (which continue to 
be refined) for OTX zoning will establish clearer development expectations for each of these 
areas. Having form-based regulations that can be tailored to the unique circumstances that 
exist in different areas of Old Town is critical to having more predictable development 
expectations for OTX areas. 
Staff has also heard the concerns raised about building massing and is supportive of 
reducing the size of the building footprint proposed for OTX-3 if that is the direction Planning 
Commission and City Council provides.  
Summary of Sept. 2 Land Use Code Working Group Outcomes  
The Land Use Code Working Group met on Sept. 2 to discuss several topics, including Old 
Town zoning, the housing mix point system, and lot and building type dimensional 
standards. The Working Group also elected to add Building Height in Old Town to their list of 
discussion topics. After a detailed conversation, the group reached consensus on the 
following recommended changes relating to the Old Town Mixed-Use (“OTX”) district: 
9 
 
1. Public Road Character: Update the draft Land Use Code to ensure that development 
along Public Road (specifically in OTX-3 south of Emma Street) reflects the same 
general character as the OTX-1 portion of Public Road. (Ordinance #2) 
2. OTX-3 Lot Coverage: Reduce maximum lot coverage in OTX-3 from 90% to 80%. 
(Ordinance #1) 
3. OTX-3 Building Footprint: Reduce the maximum building footprint in OTX-3 from 
25,000 sq. ft. to 10,000 sq. ft. (Ordinance #1) 
4. Rezoning 700 Block: Rezone the 700 block of Public Road to OTX-1. (Ordinance #1) 
5. Future Alley System: Explore ways to require alley access in OTX-3 to support creation 
of a future alley network. (Ordinance #2) 
6. Building Height in OTX-3: Limit buildings to two stories (Ordinance #1) and adjust the 
maximum height (in feet) accordingly (Ordinance #2). The Working Group requested 
that staff prepare materials on this topic for a future working group meeting.  
7. Additional Meeting: Request that another Working Group meeting be held before 
adoption of Ordinance #2. (Staff noted and the Working Group acknowledged that 
additional changes and meeting will likely require a longer deferred effective date – 
120 days vs. 90).

neighborhoods) and the dimensional standards for housing types. The group recommended 
the following:

Features 
At its Aug. 18 meeting, City Council also directed staff to add Sustainable Design Features to 
the workshop agenda to discuss the number of requirements was adequate. The draft Land 
Use Code includes sustainable design standards for multi-unit and non-residential projects. 
These standards require new development to incorporate at least two sustainability 
features, while redevelopment must include at least one.  
Available sustainability features include: 
1. Bird-safe glazing that makes glass visible to birds. 
2. On-site renewable energy systems beyond what building or energy codes require. 
3. Additional EV charging stations or EV-ready spaces beyond Lafayette Building Code 
minimums. 
11 
 
4. Enhanced bicycle amenities, like repair stations, covered parking, e-bike charging, or 
similar features. 
5. Preservation and reuse of an existing building or structure. 
6. Green infrastructure such as bioswales, rain gardens, or permeable pavement that 
meets Land Use Code and PWDSS requirements. 
7. Additional tree planting or landscaping beyond minimum standards. 
8. Community garden, food-production areas, or similar shared outdoor amenities. 
9. Cool roofs, green roofs, or similar roof treatments. 
10. Other sustainable site or building features that provide equal or greater 
environmental benefit, subject to Planning Director approval. 
The options above vary in cost, from the relatively inexpensive bike repair station ($1,500) 
to higher-cost on-site renewable energy stations. The project team has not modeled the 
projected costs of each option and would therefore recommend further evaluation when 
considering an expansion of this requirement.  
Roof Pitch/Solar Panels  
During its Aug. 18 meeting, City Council directed staff to add roof pitch requirements for 
solar panels to this workshop agenda. 
The draft Land Use Code does not require projects to orient buildings or design roof pitches 
to maximize solar access. However, the draft code does exempt solar panels from maximum 
height limits (allowing up to 10 feet of additional height) and explicitly allows roof- or ground-
mounted solar energy systems in every zoning district except Open Space. 
If council wishes to consider a policy requiring solar-oriented building design, staff 
recommends adding this item to the Sustainability Menu summarized above (e.g., if 60% of 
buildings in a project are oriented and constructed for future roof-mounted solar). A blanket 
requirement for all development may prove challenging due to site constraints, topography, 
and existing orientation standards, like requirements for buildings to face Public Road in Old 
Town.

Next Steps 
Council is scheduled to consider second reading on the Land Use Code and Zoning Map at 
its Sept. 15, 2026, meeting. If approved as drafted, the code would become effective on 
Dec. 15, 2026. The deferral gives staff time to notify property owners affected by the zoning 
and map changes. It also gives Planning Commission and City Council time to incorporate 
additional refinements, including any changes from this workshop, through a second 
ordinance timed to take effect on the same date. As noted above, if Planning Commission 
and City Council agree to exploring OTX-3 standards further in Ordinance #2 and direct staff 
to coordinate another working group meeting, additional time (30-60 days) will need to be 
factored into the Dec. 15th date reflected above.

Working Group Recommendations (OTX)
1. Public Road Character: Update the draft Land Use Code to 
ensure that development along Public Road (specifically in OTX - 3 
south of Emma Street) reflects the same general character as the 
OTX - 1 portion of Public Road. (Ordinance #2) 
2. OTX-3 Lot Coverage: Reduce maximum lot coverage in OTX  -  3 from 
90% to 80%. (Ordinance #1) 
3. OTX-3 Building Footprint: Reduce the maximum building footprint 
in OTX-3 from 25,000 sq. ft. to 10,000 sq. ft. (Ordinance #1)

Working Group Recommendations (OTX)
4. Rezoning 700 Block: Rezone the West side of 700 block 
of Public Road to OTX - 1. (Ordinance #1) 
5. Future Alley System: Explore ways to require alley access 
in OTX - 3 to support creation of a future alley network. 
(Ordinance #2) 
6. Building Height in OTX-3: Limit buildings to two stories 
(Ordinance #1) and adjust the maximum height (in feet) 
accordingly (Ordinance #2). The Working Group 
requested that staff prepare materials on this topic for a 
future working group meeting.  
7. Additional Meeting: Request that another Working Group 
meeting be held before adoption of Ordinance #2. Staff 
noted and the Working Group acknowledged that 
additional changes and meeting will likely require a 
longer deferred effective date – 120 days vs. 90.

Working Group Recommendations (Housing)
8. Small Detached Home Size: Increase the maximum size of a Small
