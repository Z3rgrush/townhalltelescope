Options for Participating in the Meeting 
Attend the meeting in person at City Hall Council Chambers, 1290 S. Public Road. 
Submit written comments for Public Input to City Council at 
LafayetteCo.gov/PublicInputForm. If your written comments are received by 1pm on the day 
of the meeting they will be added to the meeting packet for that night. Written comments 
submitted for Public Input will not be read aloud during the meeting. 
Participate by telephone. Call 1-877-853-5247 (toll free). Once connected, you will be asked 
for the meeting number. The meeting number is 869 1163 2580. Press # after entering the 
number. To request to speak, press *9 during Public Input to raise your hand. When it is 
your turn, we will unmute your microphone. 
To request interpreter services, please submit a request at least two (2) days prior the 
meeting at LafayetteCo.gov/InterpreterRequestForm .

Public Input - Submission #62708
Date Submitted: 10/6/2026
Dear Mayor and Members of the Lafayette City Council, I am writing to formally submit my public comment for
the October 6, 2026, meeting to voice my strong opposition to the allocation of public funds for Flock Safety
automatic license plate readers (ALPRs) in the 2027 city budget (Item I). While I fully support robust public
safety initiatives, the mass surveillance capabilities inherent to the Flock network present severe and
unaddressed risks to the privacy and civil liberties of Lafayette residents. These automated camera systems
continuously track, log, and store the movement of ordinary citizens who are not suspected of any crime. A
network of this scale effectively turns our public roads into passive checkpoints, creating a permanent
database of our daily routines. Furthermore, I have deep concerns regarding the lack of transparent, localized
policies governing who has access to this collected data, how securely it is stored, and which external law
enforcement agencies it will be shared with. Committing taxpayer dollars to a proprietary surveillance network
without strict, community-vetted privacy safeguards is an overreach that does not align with the values of
accountability and transparency we prize in Lafayette. I urge the City Council to remove the funding for Flock
safety cameras from the 2027 budget until a comprehensive, public evaluation of its privacy impacts, data
retention limits, and civil liberty safeguards can be independently conducted. Thank you for your time,
consideration, and dedication to keeping our community both safe and free. Sincerely, Stacey Au Lafayette,
CO

K. First and Final Reading / Emergency Ordinance No. 22, Series 2026 / An Emergency Ordinance

absent for the vote.

Moratorium on the Acceptance, Processing, and Approval of Any Land Use

Executive Summary 
Although the City does not currently have an application for development of a data center, 
concerns exist regarding the potential impacts a large data center may have on community 
resources and utility capacity and whether the current Development and Zoning Code 
(“DZC”) and proposed amendments to the Land Use Code (“LUC”) would adequately protect 
resources and the community from such impacts. The moratorium is intended to provide the 
City with the time necessary to study the land use, utility, environmental, and other 
community impacts associated with data center development, and to develop, consider, and 
adopt appropriate standards, or other regulatory measures governing such uses, in order to 
mitigate risks and protect community resources.  
City Council passed on first reading Ordinance No. 21, Series 2026 unanimously on October 
6, 2026. 

2 
 
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Environmental Stewardship 
Background Information 
As the demand for computing infrastructure continues to grow, the development of data 
centers also continues to grow nationally and in Colorado. Data centers are specialized 
facilities for housing network servers, computers, and networking infrastructure necessary 
for digital and computing services. Data centers have existed for decades and can vary 
considerably in terms of purpose (telecommunications, cloud storage and computing, AI 
model training, crypto-mining, etc.), customer structure (onsite, offsite, and co-located), and 
scale (small, medium, large, and “hyperscale”). A data center facility encompasses not only 
digital infrastructure, but also supporting physical structures, including cooling structures, 
power and backup power generation facilities, energy transmission structures, stormwater 
facilities, offices, and parking. Data centers can be built in phases, meaning the full build-
out occurs across multiple phases over time. Potential impacts can vary greatly depending 
on the size, design, and technology employed by the facility.  
The current Code of Ordinances does not have specific regulations, standards, or definitions 
to address the unique characteristics and impacts of data center facilities. The proposed 
Land Use Code (“LUC”), recently adopted on second reading on September 15, 2026 (Ord. 
No. 15, Series 2026, with a Feb. 1, 2027, effective date), defines and regulates “data 
centers” uniformly as a singular use type, treating all such facilities equally regardless of 
their size, peak electrical demand, or water consumption profiles.  
Imposing a moratorium on the acceptance, processing, and approval of applications for the 
development or construction of a data center allows the City the time needed to: 
• Research potential impacts of a data center in Lafayette; 
• Draft appropriate regulations; and  
• Monitor anticipated state legislation regulating data centers in 2027. 
Ordinance No. 21, Series, 2026, if approved by City Council on second reading, would 
establish a moratorium on the acceptance and consideration of land use applications for 
new or expanded data center facilities. The moratorium would remain in effect for 18 
months, or until appropriate regulations are adopted into the Lafayette Code of Ordinances, 
whichever occurs first. For purposes of the proposed moratorium, a data center facility is 
defined as “a facility for which the primary purpose is the storage, processing, or 
transmission of digital data, including high-density computing, server farms, and artificial 
intelligence training or inference facilities.” The moratorium would not apply to routine 
3 
 
maintenance activities on existing facilities, onsite enterprise server rooms that are 
accessory to a primary business that is not a data center, government-owned emergency 
infrastructure, or applications deemed technically complete prior to the effective date of this 
ordinance. 
During a moratorium, staff anticipates involvement from the Sustainability and Resilience 
Advisory Board (“SRAB”) and the Planning Commission before proposed regulations are 
ultimately brought to City Council. Public engagement and the advisory board process will 
take several months to complete. 
During the moratorium period, the City will not accept, process, or review:  
• Planning applications for new and expanded data center facilities;  
• Building and engineering permits for new and expanded data center facilities; and 
• Any other development applications related to new and expanded data center 
facilities. 
The moratorium is specific to new or expanded data center facilities meeting the definition 
above. Applications submitted for existing facilities, for minor changes to the site or changes 
that do not expand or intensify the data center operations, would not be included in the 
proposed moratorium. The moratorium also would not apply to routine maintenance 
activities on existing facilities, onsite enterprise server rooms that are accessory to a primary 
business that is not a data center, government-owned emergency infrastructure, or 
applications deemed technically complete prior to the effective date of the ordinance. The 
moratorium does not prevent potential future applicants from engaging with City staff and 
participating in a pre-application meeting.  
Next Steps 
If the moratorium is enacted, it will become effective 10 days after passage. During the 
moratorium, City staff will evaluate the data center provisions in the recently approved LUC 
to determine whether those regulations are adequate to address impacts from data centers. 
Staff will also continue to study the data center industry, energy and resource consumption 
trends and concerns, and community and environmental impacts—both negative and 
positive—of data center development in other communities. In addition, staff will also 
monitor anticipated 2027 state legislation concerning the regulation of data centers. At key 
decision points, staff will seek City Council direction regarding input from City advisory 
boards and the public on proposed regulations addressing the size and zoning (location) of 
4 
 
data centers, compatibility with surrounding uses, and the protection of community and 
utility resources.  
Recommendation 
Staff recommends that Council adopt Ordinance No. 21, Series 2026, imposing an 18-
month moratorium on applications for the development or construction of data center 
facilities as defined in this memo and the ordinance.  
Proposed Motion Language 
Council motion to approve on second reading Ordinance No. 21, Series 2026, imposing an 
18-month moratorium on the acceptance, processing, and approval of any land use 
application or building permit for the development or construction of data centers and 
related land uses.

Resolution No. 2026-28 appoints the law firm of Collins, Cole, Winn, & Ulmer, PLLC, as 
special legal counsel to provide legal representation and advice to the City regarding 
metropolitan district formation, operations, and related matters. Attorney Kathryn Winn, a

The City requires specialized legal expertise to assist City Council, the City Attorney’s Office, 
and City staff with metropolitan district matters, including review of service plans, financing 
structures, district governance, and development-related issues. Immediate legal support is 
needed in connection with metropolitan districts proposed by Kensington Development 
Group, LLC, for The Range at Lafayette development and annexation proposal located at the 
southwest corner of Highway 287 and Arapahoe Road, as well as for the development of a 
model metropolitan district service plan for use by the City.

City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Economic Prosperity 
Background Information 
Sections 4.9 and 4.10 of the City Charter authorize the City Council or City Manager, upon 
recommendation of the City Attorney, to engage special legal counsel when specialized 
expertise is necessary, and require City Council to establish the rates of compensation for 
such services.  
Metropolitan districts can be a significant component of new development financing and 
governance in Colorado. Due to the complexities of district formation, service plans, 
financing structures, and governance issues, the City Attorney has identified a need for 
specialized legal counsel to advise the City on metropolitan district matters and support the 
City's review of proposed service plans to ensure they align with City interests and 
community goals. 
The immediate need for these services arises from proposed metropolitan districts 
associated with The Range at Lafayette development and annexation project. In addition, 
the City seeks assistance in developing a model service plan framework to guide future 
metropolitan district proposals within the City.  
The law firm of Collins, Cole, Winn, & Ulmer, PLLC, and specifically attorney Kathryn Winn, 
possess substantial experience advising municipalities regarding metropolitan district 
formation and governance. The City Attorney and City Manager have determined that the 
firm's specialized expertise will provide valuable assistance in protecting the City's interests 
and ensuring the City Council’s informed decision-making on metropolitan district matters 
with respect to The Range at Lafayette development and annexation project, and, more 
generally, with respect to the creation of a model service plan for the City.  
Resolution No. 2026-28 (Attachment A) establishes hourly compensation rates for attorneys 
and support staff of the firm, including an hourly rate of $455 for lead attorney Kathryn 
Winn. (See Exhibit A attached to the resolution.) Any future rate increases will require City 
Council approval pursuant to Section 4.10 of the Charter. The City will also reimburse the 
firm reasonable expenses in accordance with an engagement letter approved by the City 
Attorney. Funding for these legal services is available through existing appropriations, with 
future expenditures remaining subject to annual appropriation requirements under Colorado 
law. 
Lastly, Resolution No. 2026-28 authorizes the City Attorney to execute an engagement letter 
from Collins, Cole, Winn, & Ulmer, PLLC, which sets forth the terms of the firm’s 
representation of the City. The engagement letter is consistent with the material terms of the 
resolution and has been reviewed and approved as to form by the City Attorney.
