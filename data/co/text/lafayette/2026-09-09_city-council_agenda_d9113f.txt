SUMMARY OF PROCEEDINGS 
CITY OF LAFAYETTE, COLORADO 
City Council / Planning Commission Joint Workshop 
September 9, 2026 
Mayor Tapia Vega opened the September 9, 2026, joint workshop of the Lafayette City 
Council and the Lafayette Planning Commission at 6pm in the Council Chambers at 
Lafayette City Hall, 1290 S. Public Road, Lafayette, Colorado.  
Those attending in Council Chambers included Mayor Saul Tapia Vega, Mayor Pro Tem Tim 
Barnes, Councilors Crystal Gallegos, Adam Gianola, and Annmarie Jensen, Planning 
Commission Chair Darcia Thomas, Vice Chair Dahvi Wilson, and Commissioners Brianna 
Lehman, Zachary Mettler, Frank Phillips, Joe Schoenbeck, and Bill Watson. Councilors Kyle 
Beaulieu and David Fridland attended remotely.  
City staff included City Manager Kady Doelling, Deputy City Manager Megan Davis, City 
Attorney Mary Lynn Macsalka, Assistant to the City Manager Alex Nelson, City Clerk Lynnette 
Beck, Long Range Planning Manager Phil Kleisler, Planning Manager Jacob Nitchals, and 
Planning and Building Director Steven Williams. 
Elizabeth Garvin and Gabby Hart from Clarion Associates were also present. 
I. Land Use Code Update Joint Workshop  
Phil Kleisler presented the Land Use Code project goals and steps, the Working 
Group recommendations related to Old Town Mixed-Use (“OTX”) zoning districts, 
sustainable design requirements, roof design for solar access, and reviewed a 
potential adoption path and next steps. 
Councilors and Commissioners discussed the following Working Group 
recommendations related to the Old Town Mixed-Use (OTX) district (Ordinance #1 
refers to Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026 that passed first reading on August 18, 
2026; Ordinance #2 refers to a second set of code amendments to be considered 
after passage of Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026): 
1. Public Road Character: Update the draft Land Use Code to ensure that 
development along Public Road (specifically in OTX‑3 south of Emma Street) 
reflects the same general character as the OTX‑1 portion of Public Road 
(Ordinance #2). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this in a second ordinance. 
2. OTX‑3 Lot Coverage: Reduce maximum lot coverage in OTX‑3 from 90% to 80% 
(Ordinance #1). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this on second reading of 
Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026.

3. OTX‑3 Building Footprint: Reduce the maximum building footprint in OTX‑3 from 
25,000 sq. ft. to 10,000 sq. ft. (Ordinance #1). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this on second reading of 
Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026. 
4. Rezoning 700 Block: Rezone the 700 block of Public Road to OTX‑1 (Ordinance 
#1). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this on second reading of 
Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026. 
5. Future Alley System: Explore ways to require alley access in OTX‑3 to support 
creation of a future alley network (Ordinance #2). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this in a second ordinance. 
6. Building Height in OTX‑3: Limit buildings to two stories (Ordinance #1) and adjust 
the maximum height (in feet) accordingly (Ordinance #2). The Working Group 
requested that staff prepare materials on this topic for a future working group 
meeting. 
There was general agreement to not limit buildings to two stories in second 
reading of Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026. Height and stories will be addressed 
at a later date. 
7. Additional Meeting: Request that another Working Group meeting be held before 
adoption of Ordinance #2. 
There was general agreement for this proposal. The anticipated effective date of 
Ordinance No. 15, series 2026, was amended on second reading from December 
15, 2026, to February 1, 2027, to accommodate another Working Group 
meeting. 
8. Small Detached Home Size: Increase the maximum size of a Small Detached 
House from 1,200 sq. ft. to 1,500 sq. ft. (Ordinance #1). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this in second reading of 
Ordinance. No. 15, Series 2026. 
9. Window Well Encroachments: Explore allowing window wells to encroach into side 
setbacks, with consideration for public safety (e.g., fire access) (Ordinance #2). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this in a second ordinance. 
10. Rear Setbacks for Alley-Loaded Lots: Consider creating separate rear setback 
standards for properties with alley-loaded garages (Ordinance #1). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this in second reading of 
Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026.

11. Building Height Definition: Amend the definition of “Building Height” to account 
for building code requirements that ensure adequate site drainage (Ordinance 
#2). 
There was general agreement to move forward with this in a second ordinance. 
Council and the Commission considered the following sustainable design features: 
(1) bird-safe glazing that makes glass visible to birds; (2) on-site renewable energy 
systems beyond what building or energy codes require; (3) additional EV charging 
stations or EV‑ready spaces beyond Lafayette Building Code minimums; (4) 
enhanced bicycle amenities, like repair stations, covered parking, e‑bike charging, or 
similar features; (5) preservation and reuse of an existing building or structure; (6) 
green infrastructure such as bioswales, rain gardens, or permeable pavement that 
meets Land Use Code and Public Works Standards and Specifications requirements; 
(7) additional tree planting or landscaping beyond minimum standards; (8) 
community garden, food‑production areas, or similar shared outdoor amenities; (9) 
cool roofs, green roofs, or similar roof treatments; and (10) other sustainable site or 
building features that provide equal or greater environmental benefit, subject to 
Planning Director approval. 
There was discussion around creating a point or tiered system associated with the 
choices, as some options were more intensive and expensive than others, providing 
City funds to help offset costs, and other options for promoting these features. 
There was general agreement to add this topic to the 2027 work plan. 
Council and the Commission discussed establishing building orientation standards 
for solar access, incorporating the standards as part of the staff review process, and 
adding this to the list of sustainable design features.  
There was general agreement that the topic should be presented to the Sustainability 
and Resilience Advisory Board for a recommendation.  
The workshop was adjourned at 8:51pm.
