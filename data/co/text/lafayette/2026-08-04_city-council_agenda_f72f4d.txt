Subject: Request for Council Direction on Flex Industrial Uses in the AMX-2 District Dear Mayor and Council
Members, As you prepare for tonight's work session on the Land Development Code update, I respectfully
ask that you discuss the proposed land use framework for the AMX-2 District and provide direction to staff to
evaluate adding Flex Industrial as a Permitted Use before the draft code returns for public hearings. Over the
past several weeks, I have met with City staff, several members of Council, and business and property
owners within the Aspen Ridge Industrial Area. Those conversations have reinforced a common goal:
supporting the City's vision for a modern employment district while ensuring the code continues to
accommodate the types of businesses that have made Aspen Ridge successful and will remain important to
Lafayette's economic future. To accomplish that objective, I respectfully recommend the following definition:
Proposed Definition – Industrial, Flex Industrial, Flex: A multi-tenant or single-tenant employment use
designed to accommodate a variety of office, research, light industrial, contractor, artisan, production,
assembly, warehouse, showroom, and commercial support activities within enclosed buildings. Flex Industrial
uses typically combine office space with warehousing, storage, assembly, fabrication, research, product
development, light food processing, packaging, wholesale and retail distribution, makerspaces, light craft
production, or similar low-impact employment activities. Limited accessory retail sales associated with
products manufactured, assembled, packaged, or distributed on-site are permitted. This definition supports a
broad range of modern employment uses, including: • Medical device manufacturing and research •
Engineering, technology, and product development • Specialty manufacturing and fabrication • Food
production and packaging • Makerspaces and artisan workshops • Contractor offices and building suppliers •
Warehousing, assembly, and fulfillment operations • Showrooms with limited accessory retail sales These are
clean, low-impact employment uses that occur primarily within enclosed buildings and are entirely compatible
with the City's vision for a high-quality employment district. Equally important, permitting these uses by right
creates a more efficient and predictable regulatory framework. If the City determines these businesses are
appropriate within the AMX-2 District, they should not be required to obtain Special Use Review each time a
business seeks to occupy a building, expand its operations, or adapt to changing market conditions. Requiring
discretionary public hearings for otherwise compatible employment uses creates unnecessary uncertainty for
businesses while consuming valuable City resources. Each Special Use Review requires staff analysis, public
notice, preparation of reports, Planning Commission hearings, and potentially additional City Council
consideration. That level of review is appropriate for uses with unique or potentially significant impacts. It is
not an efficient process for businesses operating in an established employment district that was specifically
intended to accommodate light industrial and commercial activity. A clear list of permitted Flex Industrial uses
would: • Provide certainty for existing businesses and future investment. • Encourage reinvestment and
adaptive reuse of existing buildings. • Reduce unnecessary costs and delays for applicants. • Allow City staff,
the Planning Commission, and City Council to focus their time on projects that genuinely require discretionary
review. • Continue protecting surrounding properties through objective development and performance
standards rather than case-by-case public hearings. This approach supports the City's long-term vision while
recognizing that successful employment districts must remain flexible enough to accommodate evolving
industries and business models. I respectfully encourage Council to raise this issue during tonight's
discussion and provide direction to staff to evaluate incorporating Flex Industrial as a Permitted Use within the
AMX-2 District before the proposed code returns for public hearings. Thank you for your thoughtful
consideration and for your continued work on this important update to Lafayette's Land Development Code.
Respectfully, Kristin Dean, AICP Founder & Principal Touchstone Planning & Development

Executive Summary 
City Council is asked to approve the Second Amendment to the Real Estate Purchase 
Agreement with D&G, LLC for the sale of a portion of City-owned property at Lot 18, Vista 
Business Park. This amendment extends the closing date and confirms the updated legal 
description of the property as Outlot E, Vista Business Park Replat C Minor Subdivision. The 
amendment supports the City’s economic prosperity goals and ensures the transaction 
proceeds with accurate property boundaries.  
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Economic Prosperity 
Background Information 
The City owns Lot 18, located at 1395 Horizon Avenue, Lafayette, CO 80026, which houses 
the “Red Zone” potable water tank. D&G, LLC owns the adjacent Tract A in Trailhead 
Business Park and approached the City in 2025 to purchase a small triangular parcel (about 

2 
 
420 square feet) in the westernmost corner of Lot 18. This parcel, referred to herein as the 
“Subject Parcel,” will allow D&G, LLC to construct a driveway over existing utilities and a 
high-pressure gas line, providing required access to Horizon Ave. for Tract A, as mandated by 
the City’s Development and Zoning Code (Chapter 26 of the Code of Ordinances). (See 
Figure 1, below.) 
Figure 1. Vicinity Map 
 
In September 2025, City Council approved Ordinance No. 17, Series 2025, which authorized 
the sale of the Subject Parcel and approved the Real Estate Purchase Agreement, which 
required Lot 18 to be subdivided, before closing, to divide the Subject Parcel, as a new 
outlot, from the remainder of Lot 18. On July 29, 2026, the City approved the required 
subdivision plat, titled “Vista Business Park Replat C,” which divides Lot 18 into “Lot 18 C” 
and “Outlot E” (the Subject Parcel to be conveyed to D&G, LLC). (See Figure 2, below.) 
 
 

3 
 
Figure 2. Excerpt from Vista Business Park Replat C, showing subdivided Lot 18 and Outlot E 
 
There is a minor difference between the original legal description in the Real Estate 
Purchase Agreement (412 sq. ft.) and the platted dimensions of Outlot E (420 sq. ft.), 
resulting in a difference of 8 square feet. Staff finds this difference immaterial and confirms 
that Outlot E, as shown in the plat, is the property intended for sale. This is about a 1.9% 
difference in area between the original metes and bounds legal description in the 
Agreement and the platted Outlot E.  
Resolution No. 2026-20 and the Second Amendment to the Agreement are needed to (i) 
extend the closing date now that the required subdivision plat is completed, (ii) to confirm 
that the City Council finds the differences between Outlot E and the original legal description 
to be immaterial, and (iii) that the land in Outlot E as shown in Vista Business Park Replat C 
constitutes the Property that the City Council intends to sell and convey under the 
Agreement and per Ordinance No. 17, Series 2025.  
To those ends, Resolution No. 2026-20 and the Second Amendment will: 
• Extend the closing deadline to August 12, 2026 (with authority for the City Manager 
to extend the closing deadline up to an 60 additional days if needed); 
• Confirm Outlot E as the property to be conveyed; and  

4 
 
• Authorize the City Manager to execute the Second Amendment and to take all 
necessary actions and execute all necessary documents to complete the transaction. 
Next Steps 
If approved, the City Manager will execute the Second Amendment and all documents 
needed to close the sale by August 12, 2026, or within an additional 60 days if an extension 
is required. Upon closing, the Vista Business Park Replat C subdivision plat will be recorded, 
followed by a special warranty deed conveying Outlot E to D&G, LLC. 
Recommendation 
Staff recommends approval of this resolution.

Executive Summary 
In April of 2026, the City selected D2C Architects to develop a modernization plan for Fire 
Station #1. The original contract was $99,992 and executed by the City Manager. Since that 
time the City has received favorable feedback on a Congressionally Directed Spending 
application to support a fire department training facility adjacent to Fire Station #1. If this 
grant is successful, construction would be completed in 2027. Therefore, some aspects of 
the original architectural contract will be insufficient to complete the necessary pre-
construction work for this facility, and additional services would be needed to further refine 
this design and implement grant funds (if awarded).  
This Council action allows the City Manager to approve subsequent change orders not to 
exceed a total contract amount of $170,000. Funding is available in the 2026 budget for 
this change order.  

2 
 
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Excellent City Services and 
Infrastructure 
Background Information 
Fire Station #1 was built in 1999. At that time, the organization was largely volunteer, and 
station #1 was the only fire station in Lafayette. In 2010 an addition was added to support 
full-time staffing which was further expanded with the construction of Fire Station #2 in 
2013. At present the fire station is staffed by five to seven staff per day and an additional 
five administrative personnel during the week. The station has outgrown its original use and 
will benefit from a modernization in the future.  
Early in 2026 the City issued a Request for Proposals that complied with Section 44 of the 
Municipal Code and reviewed several submittals from architecture firms. The scope of work 
was to develop a phaseable approach to modernizing the facility while maintaining 24-hour 
operations. D2C Architects was selected after interviewing the top three firms from an initial 
review of the proposals. 
Nearly simultaneously the City had an opportunity to pursue Congressionally Directed 
Spending through the Office of Senator Hickenlooper. The development of a firefighter 
training ground and building was submitted for this process utilizing City-owned land that is 
immediately adjacent to Fire Station #1. At present this grant has passed the first round and 
is moving through the federal process. The City will be notified of award in the fourth quarter 
of 2026. 
This funding opportunity has provided the Fire Department an opportunity to alter the scope 
of the contract with D2C Architects to include design work that better prepares the City to 
move forward with construction in a more efficient manner, should the grant be awarded.  
Next Steps 
None.

Executive Summary 
The purpose of this agenda item is to present an update of the additional due diligence 
requested by City Council on February 17, 2026, regarding the long-term preservation of the 
historic Coca-Cola mural sign. Since that meeting, staff has completed further outreach with 
preservation partners, evaluated potential options, confirmed landmark eligibility, updated 
restoration cost estimates, and explored funding opportunities. 
Based on this work, staff has identified feasible preservation options and is seeking City 
Council direction regarding the preferred long-term preservation strategy for the mural sign. 
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Sense of Place 
Background Information 
History 
The historic Coca-Cola mural sign originated as a painted wall advertisement on the south 
wall of Pat and Gar's Hi-Way Bar at 201 N Public Road in the late 1940s or early 1950s. The 
mural promoted both Coca-Cola and the local business while serving travelers along the 
Lincoln Highway corridor. 

2 
 
Prior to demolition of the building in 2015, community members organized a fundraising 
campaign to preserve the mural. The south wall containing the sign was carefully removed 
and relocated to Lafayette Fire Station No. 1, where professional conservators completed 
stabilization and preservation work. The total cost to move the sign, complete professional 
restoration, and install associated flatwork and lighting was approximately $136,000, 
$126,000 of which was raised by the community. 
The sign was then relocated and installed on the south-facing wall of 103 N Public Road in 
2016, where it remains today. Following these efforts, ownership of the sign was transferred 
to the City. The Lafayette Urban Renewal Authority (“LURA”) has an easement on the wall 
with the property owner, RJ Capital Investments, LLC. The easement also stipulates that 
LURA retains the sole right and obligation to maintain the mural. 
Condition Assessment 
Following installation in 2016, the mural experienced significant deterioration from 
prolonged outdoor exposure. 
In 2024, LURA and the City retained John Canning & Co., a nationally recognized 
architectural conservation firm, to evaluate its condition. Their evaluation found extensive 
paint loss, cracking, substrate deterioration, and unstable paint layers caused by prolonged 
exposure to sunlight, wind, ultraviolet radiation, and moisture fluctuations. The report noted 
that portions of the paint are so fragile that even light contact results in paint loss. 
John Canning & Co. concluded that continued outdoor exposure will accelerate deterioration 
and identified relocation to a climate-controlled indoor environment as the most sustainable 
long-term preservation option, with replication or enhanced protection as alternative 
approaches. More information on the alternative approaches can be found in Attachment A. 
February 17 Council Meeting 
At the February 17 Council meeting, staff provided an overview of the history of the sign, 
preservation and partnership exploration in recent years, and potential options for Council 
discussion. Council had a robust discussion and ultimately requested the following due 
diligent items be conducted prior to making a decision:

NTRODUCTION  
J
ohn Canning Co. was contracted by the city of Lafayette to visually assess and determine possible 
conservation treatment options for the Coca Cola mural located in downtown Lafayette, CO. Fine art 
conservator Rachel R. Gilberti assessed the mural on May 7th, 2024. 
M
ultiple murals were originally painted on the side of a building located at 201 N. Public road 
as advertisements in the late 1930s or early 1940s. There is still evidence that previous signs were painted 
underneath, a Coors and a Pabst Blue Ribbon Beer advertisement. In 1949 or early 1950s, sign painters 
created the current advertisement promoting "Pat and Gar's Hi-way Bar" with a large Coca Cola mural 
and slogan. The promotional sign was relatively short lived and covered over with siding. The covering 
coincidentally protected the mural from deterioration due to weather. It was uncovered once again on June 
2015 and moved to the local fire station prior to the demolition of the building. In 2016, the mural 
underwent a major restoration treatment and was reinstalled outdoors in its current location as an 
historical landmark. 
FI
NDINGS 
To 
assess a new project, you have to start by understanding what the structure is and what are the 
elements that affect it. Due to the murals’ position, it receives sun and wind throughout most of the day 
without much protection. There is an awning to protect it from rain but it’s too small to provide the mural 
with full protection. These observations of weather are important to note because of their correlation as to 
why the mural has deteriorated so quickly after just 8 years of being restored. One should also consider 
the likelihood that the historic paint layers underneath are continuing to fail, rendering the prior 
restoration mute. It is also important to consider what the structure is that makes up the mural. Questions 
like what is the mural made out of, what is the mural painted on, and how does that wall stand, must be 
known in order to make appropriate choices going forward. We found the mural to be oil based paint on 
top of wood and also there is rusted hardware in the wood.  
W
ith all of the external elements noted, an assessment has to be made on the mural itself. Color, 
proportions, layers, shading, and use of space are all to be considered. It’s not as simple as noting that a 
color needs to be fixed here or there. Noting how the mural was painted in the first place will dictate what 
needs to be fixed and how that should be best accomplished. The paint is peeling everywhere throughout 
the space and the slightest touch will cause it to break off. The wood itself is cracking and splitting, 
causing exposed wood and gaps. Cleaning tests were attempted to better understand the state of the paint, 
however this was proven to be difficult due to the fragility of the paint.  
The 
mural paint is oil based on exterior wooden siding. The size is approximately 14' x 26' feet with 
authorship unknown. It is currently exhibiting extensive paint loss due to exposure to outdoor elements, 
more specifically sun and wind exposure.  The mural faces south west and therefore is exposed to the sun 
or UV rays for the majority of the day. This not only causes fading of the colors in the paint layers, but

also weakens their binding capabilities which leads to flaking paint and paint loss. There is also extensive 
unstable craquelure (meaning cracks) throughout the entirety of the mural which will lead to continued 
paint loss if not stabilized. Since the mural is painted on wood, it is subject to the natural expansion and 
contraction of the wood from the fluctuations in temperature. The rate of absorption of moisture by the 
substrate can affect the stability of the paint layer, and extreme weather fluctuations can severely 
accelerate this process. 
W
hen considering these conditions, one aspect that is often understated is the importance of the item 
itself. Why was this built? Who built it? What was happing at the time? How has it lasted this long? Why 
was it chosen to be saved? Who are the people that care about it now? Who are we saving this for? If you 
want to restore an item of great worth you have to understand the desire behind it. For us, reading the 
plaque next to the mural offered some great insight, and allows us to offer the following 
recommendations. 
RECO
MMENDATIONS 
T
here can be several possible approaches to conserving this mural.  The first being to conserve it where it 
stands. By consolidating the substrate and paint layer, clean the surface and fill in paint where there is 
missing paint to mimic 2016 conservation treatment, then apply a UV protective varnish layer.  
Th
e second approach is to do the first approach but add additional protection by use of a bigger awning 
and plexiplass covering. The extended the awning would protect the paint layer from the sun and rain. 
While the transparent protective plexiglass covering with a UV protective coating would further inhibit 
exposure to the elements. 
Th
e third approach would be a more long term solution. With the mural undergoing the first approach and 
in addition, move it to a climate controlled environment. 
A
 fourth option to consider would be to replicate the mural and move the original to an indoor location. 
The replication would be done with One Shot Sign Painters’ paint, which is an oil based paint often used 
for this purpose (as the name would indicate). This replica could then be covered with plexiglass with an 
extended awning as outlined in option two. The original would be moved somewhere inside for display to 
protect it from the outdoor elements.  
COS
T CONSIDERATIONS 
In 
following through on any of these recommendations, or a combination thereof, cost considerations are 
obviously a factor. While there are a variety of paths that could be taken regarding plexi-glass and awning 
extensions, Canning is able to offer more concrete budgetary figures on work we would perform 
ourselves, and do rather frequently.  
To c
onserve the mural as it stands by consolidation of existing paint layers and inpainting where 
necessary, a budget of $90K-$100K would be reasonable. This would not include any amendments to an 
awning or installation of plexi-glass. 
To 
replicate the mural in its entirety, Canning would propose a budget of $50K-$60K. This budget would 
only apply to painting and would not include purchasing the substrate, which should be a wood less 
susceptible to rot and insects.
