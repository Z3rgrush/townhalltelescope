valid arguments against the concept being put forward by the developers of the Boulder Ranch. Well over
2000 individuals have already signed petitions opposing this development and there are sure to be more.

out annexation and development of the 8600 Baseline property. Terrance and Noel Hefty

Public Input - Submission #61776
Date Submitted: 8/27/2026
Submit written public input | Enviar comentario público por escrito
Dear Mayor and Lafayette Planning Commissioners: I am writing to strongly oppose the proposed annexation
and development of 8600 Baseline Road. The applicant is asking Lafayette to annex a rural 35-acre parcel
and permit 185 housing units, an activity center, professional offices, and treatment facilities. The applicant's
own materials leave major traffic, infrastructure, environmental, and financial questions unanswered. The
Planning Commission should recommend denial at least until all outstanding issues have been thoroughly
studied and negotiated with the developer. Otherwise, the developer will be negotiating on his terms, not the
best interest of the City and the Community. 1. The City and the Community have already rejected annexation
of this property at a much lower density. In 2018, the Planning Commission unanimously rejected a prior
annexation proposal, and City staff recommended denial. That proposal involved approximately 66 to 86
homes. The current proposal seeks 185 units. The road constraints, surrounding rural land uses,
infrastructure limitations, and compatibility concerns have not meaningfully changed. The proposed density
has more than doubled, so the basis for denial is stronger, not weaker. To put the community’s position into
perspective, approximately 184 people attended the initial neighborhood meeting in 2018. During a show-of-
hands vote, 180 attendees opposed the development, four expressed no opinion, and none supported it. 2.
The applicant has not demonstrated that Baseline Road can safely absorb the development. Baseline is
already a constrained two-lane corridor with recurring backups. Backups associated with the railroad crossing
are already significant, and a development of this magnitude would add traffic whenever a train blocks or
slows the corridor. Every additional residence, employee, patient, visitor, and service vehicle would use the
same limited roadway. The preapplication does not quantify daily trips, peak-hour delay, railroad-related
queuing, emergency-response impacts, or the effect on Nyland Way and nearby intersections. A complete
independent traffic study should be required before annexation is even considered. 3. Baseline Road is
already a documented safety concern. Lafayette has identified portions of Baseline Road as part of its High
Injury Network. Two pedestrians were seriously injured in separate incidents on Baseline Road in February
2025, leading to significant traffic-management changes and community awareness. Adding a development of
this scale before the applicant demonstrates that the corridor can operate safely would introduce significantly
more vehicles, turning movements, cyclists, and pedestrians into an already strained area—with no practical
ability or stated intention to widen the road or add lanes. 4. There is no realistic, identified plan to expand
Baseline Road at this location. The site is constrained by Lafayette's municipal water treatment and storage
facilities, rural parcels, open space, agricultural land, drainage areas, and the Goodhue and Davidson
irrigation ditches. The applicant has not identified a feasible, funded roadway expansion plan or explained
who would pay for Baseline safety upgrades and supporting infrastructure. 5. The proposal is not rural in
scale, density, or design. The property is zoned Rural Residential in Boulder County and is surrounded by
rural residential property, agricultural land, open space, the Nyland community, Snow Peak Lane, and City
utility facilities. The proposal includes townhomes, duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, cottages, zero-foot interior
setbacks, and structures up to four stories and 50 feet. Its density must be evaluated against the entire
surrounding rural community, not only the 35-acre parcel. Calling this development rural would be misleading;
it is urban-scale development that would materially change the established character and density of the area.
ADDITIONALLY, THE APPLICATION DOES NOT EXPLAIN HOW THE THREE EXISTING PROPERTIES
ALONG SNOW PEAK LANE WOULD BE AFFECTED BY OR INCORPORATED INTO THE ANNEXATION.
WOULD THESE PROPERTIES ALSO BE ANNEXED, OR WOULD THEY BE LEFT AS AN ISOLATED
ISLAND POCKET OF UNINCORPORATED BOULDER COUNTY? 6. The proposal relies on exceptions from
normal City engineering standards. The applicant states that street and parking dimensions would vary from
standard City engineering requirements through PUD flexibility and proposes streets and driveways at
minimum emergency-access widths. A project of this size must meet the City's normal safety and
infrastructure standards without reduced dimensions designed to increase residential yield and developer
profit. 7. Critical infrastructure and financial obligations remain undefined. The raw-water dedication method
has not been finalized, written approvals from the ditch companies have not been obtained, and fiscal
responsibility for streets, drainage, trails, utilities, and other public improvements is described only as the City
'and/or County.' Before the City even considers proceeding with annexation, the funding impact to the citizens
of Lafayette must be clearly defined. The City should require a clear capital improvement plan, binding
developer contributions for water, wastewater, power, roadway and Baseline safety improvements,
appropriate bonds or financial guarantees, and a full analysis of the long-term cost to Lafayette taxpayers and
utility customers. Will input fees contributed by the developer cover such costs? The reality is that most
residences in this area shop in either Louisville or Boulder. Making this a community that cannot subsidize its
long-term existence. 8. The environmental impacts have not been adequately addressed. The applicant has
not provided complete analysis of energy demand, water supply, wastewater and sewer capacity, drainage
and stormwater, wildlife habitat, or light pollution from 185 units and new street lighting. The application also
does not explain how the Goodhue and Davidson ditches would be protected from construction impacts,
runoff, spills, or other contamination. Very importantly, how can Snow Peak Lane and neighboring properties
who own ditch rights be compensated. Reports that Preble's meadow jumping mouse, a federally protected
species, may have been present on or near the property warrant an independent, in-depth endangered-
species and habitat study before the City advances the application. 9. The claimed public benefits are not
defined or enforceable. Only approximately 8% of the units are identified as affordable or middle-tier
workforce housing, and the application does not state how many units would actually be reserved for
residents with intellectual and developmental disabilities or aging-in-place residents. The application also
does not define the future public benefit or long-term impact, the realistic net tax revenue after the cost of
required City services and infrastructure, or protections against the property being sold or substantially
redesigned after annexation. 10. Other essential questions remain unanswered. The record does not
establish who owns the mineral rights or how those rights could affect the property; whether the developer
has any experience with comparable rural developments; how 185 additional residential units and the
proposed commercial activity would affect the already limited and distressed RTD bus service; how much of
the property would be permanently dedicated to Lafayette, Boulder County or another public entity as
protected open space; or how the development would be screened from adjacent neighborhoods, Snow Peak
Lane, and existing open space. These are not minor design details; they go directly to whether annexation is
appropriate. 11. The annexation record is not complete enough to support approval. This development
presents more unanswered questions and a substantially greater impact than the proposal the City previously
rejected. Annexation should not be used to approve a level of density that the existing road network,
surrounding land uses, environment and rural infrastructure cannot support. Until the applicant resolves these
issues with independent studies and binding commitments, there is no factual basis for the City to reverse its
prior conclusion. I respectfully urge the Planning Commission to wholeheartedly and unanimously recommend
denial of the annexation of 8600 Baseline Road. Sincerely, Michael Meints 679 Snow Peak Lane Lafayette,
CO 80026

Written Public Comment: Agenda Item V.G, Public Hearing / First Reading / Ordinance No. 15, Series 2026
(Land Use Code and Zoning Map) Submitted via LafayetteCo.gov/PublicInputForm Mayor and Members of
City Council: My name is Brad Edwards. I am the lead pastor of The Table Presbyterian Church, which leases
the northwest church building at 700 W. South Boulder Road, within Site-Specific Adjustment #3 of the draft
Zoning Map. I support adoption of the Land Use Code, and I am grateful for two provisions in particular: the
right-sized parking standard for assembly uses (2 spaces per 1,000 SF GFA, Table 26-5-1), and Section 5.b
of the Ordinance, which gives owners in transition situations a path to request alternative zoning. This
comment is offered in support of the discussion Council directed at its July 28 workshop concerning rezonings
that create special use and nonconforming use scenarios. Because our lots are the clearest case of that
scenario in the most recent draft of the city’s rezoning plan, our church requests one change that would
achieve the same results in the least restrictive way possible, and with far more consistency with the
comprehensive plan. 1. The request: designate the two lots in Site-Specific Adjustment #3 as NC
(Neighborhood Mixed-Use Center). This is our primary request, and it is a simple one that would resolve
every issue raised below. Retaining AMX-2 would require at least four separate code amendments and
recurring discretionary adjustments to approximate the same outcome. Three grounds: (a) Consistency. To my
knowledge, no other house of worship in the draft Map is designated AMX2, and several (the vast majority)
are designated NC, including: First United Methodist, Lafayette UU, the Church of Scientology Boulder
Mission, Eternal Savior Lutheran Church, New Church of Boulder Valley, Flatirons Community Church, and
Immaculate Conception Catholic Church. The latter is similarly situated on the same arterial corridor, yet
mapped NC. In fact, the only religious assembly properties mapped AMX-2 sit on this one stretch, which
contains only churches. (b) History and present function. These lots have been in continuous religious
assembly use since at least 1976, on land the Waneka family gave for that purpose. Today they are the
weekly home of four congregations serving roughly 400 people, including two of Lafayette's three
congregations of color: the Boulder Chinese Evangelical Free Church and Tabernáculo Bautista. AMX-2 is, by
the staff report's own description, a district that guides the development and redevelopment of existing
commercial sites. These lots are neither commercial, never have been, nor are they awaiting redevelopment.
The designation is inconsistent with the entire history of the property and with its present civic function. (c)
Comprehensive Plan conformance. NC is the code's implementation of the Plan's neighborhood mixed-use
typology, and this node at South Boulder Road and 287 is precisely where the Plan envisions that pattern. NC
satisfies the same conformance criterion (Sec. 26-16-8(c)) on which the adopted Map relies, and preserves
the property’s ability to host community and cultural functions in the future. 2. Piecemeal amendments within
AMX-2 would not solve the inconsistency, and I want to be clear that we are not requesting them as a
substitute. In AMX-2 as drafted, assembly of 7,500 square feet or more requires Special Use Review while
secular uses of comparable or greater intensity (Museum, Government facility, Public recreational club or
facility, Hospital) are permitted by right. That disparity needs correction however these lots are rezoned; a
sanctuary generates the same trips and gathering pattern as a museum of the same size. But even if Council
amended that one cell from S to P, AMX-2 would still: (i) convert a simple lot split into a major site plan for the
complete development parcel under Sec. 26-2-10(a)(2), imposing parcel-wide discretionary review on land
whose use is not changing;  (ii) prohibit much-needed day care services, which are permitted by right in NC
and are a customary ministry of congregations like ours; and  (iii) apply build-to and frontage-occupancy
standards written for new corridor redevelopment to an existing campus, requiring adjustment requests in
perpetuity. Each of those would need its own amendment or recurring relief. One district designation, NC,
resolves all of the above at once. A stack of accommodations inside a district designed for a different future
unnecessarily risks creating a substantial burden when another district (NC) accomplishes the same
governmental interest and better fits the property’s past, present, and future use. 3. Independent of our lots:
the base “Assembly, Religious or Secular” row in Table 26-3-1 is blank in all 21 districts and needs an entry
before second reading. Only the two sub-rows (“Located on a Main Street” and “Bldg. footprint ≥ 7,500 SF”)
carry entries, and the table's key provides that a blank cell means prohibited. Read literally, an assembly
space under 7,500 square feet not located on a Main Street is prohibited everywhere in Lafayette: every
church, storefront congregation, small lodge, or civic hall. I do not believe this is intended, particularly given
the code's careful religious-accommodation provisions elsewhere (e.g., Sec. 26-7-4(a)(2)(F)). I would note
that Section 6 of the Ordinance does not permit staff to correct this administratively, because supplying a use-
table entry changes a permitted, conditional, or prohibited use. It therefore requires a Council amendment at
first or second reading, or through the anticipated second ordinance. 4. Section 5.b. I understand the property
owner intends to submit a request under Section 5.b seeking the NC designation described above. I
respectfully ask Council to direct staff to process that request promptly, so that any approval can take effect
with the Ordinance's December 15 effective date. Thank you for your work on this code and for the
opportunity to comment. I am happy to provide any further information and/or meet with any parties relevant
to the above. Steve Owings, Founding Pastor, Living Water North Gate DBA Catch The Fire Boulder 700 W
South Boulder Rd steve.owings@catchthefire.com 303-668-1329

Counsel to Provide Legal Representation and Advice to the City with Respect to 
Public Finance Matters and Setting the Rate of Compensation Therefor 
September 1, 2026 
Prepared by: 
• Kady Doelling, City Manager 
• Mary Lynn Macsalka, City Attorney 
 
Executive Summary 
The City of Lafayette is in need of specialized legal services to represent and advise the City 
and its staff regarding TABOR election compliance and procedures for the LaMont Does tax 
extension ballot issue, as well as wind-down and closure of public improvement districts 
upon payoff of bonded debt used to finance the districts’ public improvements. These issues 
require the assistance and advice of attorneys with specialized experience in public finance, 
bonding, TABOR, and special districts. In addition, the scope of representation would include 
the potential for other work within Butler Snow’s public finance expertise, subject to specific 
engagements approved by the City Manager, subject to rates approved by City Council and 
availability of appropriated funds. The attorneys at the law firm of Butler Snow LLP have the 
necessary legal experience and qualifications to represent and advise the City on these 
matters.  
 
 
 

2 
 
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Economic Prosperity 
Background Information 
Public finance work, including matters involving TABOR; local sales, use, and property taxes; 
the establishment, implementation, and closure of public improvement districts such as 
government improvement districts (“GID”), special improvement districts (“SID”), and 
reimbursement assessment districts (“RAD”); public-private partnerships; certificates of 
participation; and related election and campaign-finance work, all require specialized legal 
counsel with experience and expertise in these areas. Lafayette historically had public 
finance/bond counsel on retainer to advise the City and the City Attorney’s Office whenever 
legal issues or assistance was needed for the foregoing types of public finance work. 
However, that attorney retired approximately 5 years ago. 
Last year, when the City needed bond counsel for the capital bond election and bond 
issuance work, a competitive request for proposals was done, and the City Council selected 
the law firm of Butler Snow, LLP, including attorneys Kim Crawford and Dalton Kelley and 
their team. Since Council appointed Butler Snow as special legal counsel for bond work, the 
City has had a productive and positive working relationship with Ms. Crawford, Mr. Kelley, 
and other attorneys at the firm.  
Butler Snow also recently assisted staff with the drafting of the LaMont Does ballot issue 
and is providing TABOR-related election advice as that measure advances to the ballot this 
November. However, this work is not covered by the scope of the bond counsel appointment 
approved by City Council in Resolution No. 2025-29. Butler Snow has provided a very 
reasonable fee estimate for this work of $5,000.  
The City has also requested advice from Butler Snow related to the wind-down of the 
Exempla GID and SID, which no longer have bonded debt, and other GIDs in the City that are 
nearing bond payoff in the coming years, and the attorneys at Butler Snow have stated that 
they can assist with this work for a very reasonable estimate of $5,000 - $7,000 in fees.  
The City Attorney’s Office recommends the City Council appoint the law firm of Butler Snow 
LLP, as special legal counsel to advise and represent the City on TABOR, public finance, tax, 
public improvement districts and other special districts, public-private partnerships, and 
related election and campaign-finance advice. The City Manager would be authorized to 
approve specific engagements of Butler Snow for matters within the scope of this 
appointment, in accordance with Section 4.9 of the Charter. Work performed by Butler Snow 
would be subject to the rates of compensation approved by City Council in the attached 
resolution, pursuant to Section 4.10 of the City’s Charter.  
3 
 
Because Butler Snow has already performed work for the City on the LaMont Does tax 
extension ballot issue, the resolution is drafted to approve the rates of compensation as of 
May 1, 2026, so the City may compensate Butler Snow for work performed since that date.  
Next Steps 
If Council approves this resolution, the City Manager will execute engagement letters with 
the law firm of Butler Snow, LLP, on behalf of the City, for the LaMont Does 2026 TABOR 
election and related advice, and for advice on GID wind-down and closures, subject to 
approval of the engagement letters as to scope and form by the City Attorney. The attorneys 
at Butler Snow, LLP, will continue legal work for the City on the above-referenced matters 
and as may be requested by the City from time to time, at the hourly rates approved by 
Council in the attached resolution. 
Recommendation 
The City Attorney recommends approval of this resolution.

Executive Summary 
The Intergovernmental Agreement (“IGA”) between the Colorado Department of 
Transportation (“CDOT”) and the City is presented for City Council approval. The IGA 
establishes the operation and maintenance responsibilities of the City for a portion of the 
“SH7 Corridor Improvements – 95th Intersection in Boulder, County” project. CDOT 
anticipates starting construction this fall with construction extending through early 2028. 
The City’s operation and maintenance responsibilities will begin after the project 
construction is complete. 
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Mobile and Connected 
Background Information 
CDOT is preparing to begin intersection improvements at Colorado State Highway 7 (“CO7”) 
and 95th Street. The planned intersection improvements for CO7 (Arapahoe Road) include 

2 
 
two through-lanes in each direction, a dedicated eastbound bus lane, a multi-use path, 
improved transit facilities, and pedestrian crossing improvements. CDOT began the design 
phase of the project in 2021 and anticipates that construction will begin fall 2026 and 
continue through early 2028.  
The IGA between CDOT and the City outlines the City’s operation and maintenance (“O&M”) 
responsibilities for portions of the intersection improvements. Exhibit A of the agreement 
specifies the O&M activities the City must perform. Generally, these responsibilities apply to 
the multimodal improvements located between the curb and gutter and the CDOT right-of-
way limits. The City will be responsible for routine maintenance, including snow removal for 
the multi-use path and the concrete bus amenity pads. The IGA also clarifies that any 
improvements constructed by CDOT in the City-owned right-of-way will remain the City’s 
responsibility to maintain.  
CDOT anticipates completing construction in early 2028. Once the project construction is 
finished, the City’s O&M responsibilities will begin. Existing O&M programs will be expanded 
to include the additional responsibilities associated with this CDOT project. Staff will request 
the necessary funding to support these expanded O&M programs during the 2028 budget 
process.

described in the IGA.

IV. Term 
This Agreement shall begin the date approved by all Parties and shall extend for 
the useful life of the improvements, unless earlier modified or terminated by written 
agreement of the Parties. 
 
V.       CDOT Commitments 
A. CDOT shall accept inspection, operation, maintenance, and repair 
responsibilities for the portions of the Work identified as CDOT’s area of 
responsibility shown in Exhibit A. 
 
B. CDOT shall, at its own cost and expense, inspect, operate, maintain,  repair, 
and make ample provision each year for said activities for those portions of the 
Work identified as CDOT’s area of responsibility in Exhibit A.   
 
C. CDOT grants the Local Agency access to enter CDOT Right of Way (“ROW”) 
to perform inspection and maintenance duties. Though a separate access 
permit will not be required, notification to CDOT of a Local Agency approved 
and CDOT accepted Method of Handling Traffic shall be required for any work 
impacting traffic. 
D. CDOT (and FHWA, if applicable) may make periodic inspections of the Project 
and/or Facilities to verify that they are being adequately operated, maintained, 
and repaired. If CDOT inspections indicate the Project are not functioning as 
designed, CDOT may issue a written notice to the Local Agency to cure 
deficiencies. In the event the deficiencies are not remedied within the Project 
timeline after written notice from CDOT to the Local Agency, CDOT may take 
whatever steps CDOT deems necessary to maintain the Project. The Local 
Agency shall reimburse CDOT its actual and documented costs for such 
maintenance and repair work including labor, equipment, supplies, and 
materials. If CDOT repairs any deficiencies, it is under no obligation to maintain 
or repair in the future.
