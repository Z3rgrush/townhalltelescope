Executive Summary 
Per the City Charter, the City Manager is charged with the responsibility of recommending an 
annual spending plan to City Council to fund municipal services for Lafayette residents. In 
the 2027 Recommended Budget, revenue is projected at $128 million with anticipated 
expenses at $130 million across all funds. The difference between revenue and expense is 
the planned use of fund balance, largely in the Capital Project Fund and utility funds. 
However, 2027 General Fund expenses are slightly less than anticipated revenues. As a 
result, this budget does not include the use of fund balance in the General Fund.  
During this workshop, staff will provide an overview of the 2027 Recommended Budget, 
including revenue and expenditure projections, fee updates, and a review of the Utility 
Enterprise Funds. In addition to the content provided in this memo, each department will 
present an overview of their primary functions, total full-time equivalent staff members, 
expenses/revenues (by type and fund), and key highlights for 2027 and beyond.

City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Fiscally Responsible 
Background Information 
The 2027 Recommended Budget represents a balanced approach to investing in City 
services and capital infrastructure and is the result of collaborative efforts across the entire 
City to orient spending in the coming fiscal year toward maintaining high-quality service 
levels and advancing City Council’s priorities. The 2027 Recommended Budget was 
prepared in the midst of a resilient yet uncertain economy and revenue growth environment. 
Throughout the 2027 budget process, the City relied on stability, adaptability, and 
prioritization while adhering to best practices, and staff focused on optimizing existing 
resources and realigning current funding to support the City’s highest priorities and core 
services. 
Revenues 
The City relies on a diverse mix of revenues to provide stable funding for municipal 
operations and services, as outlined by fund in Figure 1. The largest sources include sales 
and use taxes, property taxes, service fees, and utility charges. Other revenue categories 
include cost allocation, transfers, and intergovernmental. Cost allocation is a mechanism in 
which other funds help support internal services that are provided by the General Fund such 
as human resources, technology, and finance. Transfers represent an internal flow of 
resources between funds for specific purposes such as replacement of vehicles (Fleet 
Fund), insurance costs (Insurance Fund), and employee benefits costs (Employee Benefits 
Fund), among others. Intergovernmental represents receipts of revenues from external 
governmental sources such as the county, state, or federal government. 
For over a year, multiple economic indicators have shown that Colorado’s growth has 
moderated after a prolonged period of strong expansion. Indicators reflect slower increases 
in real estate prices, consumer spending, job growth, and construction activity. Lafayette has 
felt the impact of this moderation through slower growth in City revenue collections. 
In addition to state-level trends, the U.S. economy continues to experience heightened 
uncertainty, with mixed economic signals and shifting policy dynamics. The City continues to 
monitor potential impacts from federal policy changes, including effects on the supply chain, 
capital infrastructure costs, and consumer spending patterns.

Sales and Use Tax 
Sales and use tax flows directly into the General Fund; Legacy Open Space Fund; Parks, 
Open Space and Trails Fund; Mental Health and Human Service Fund; and Public Safety 
Fund. Sales and use taxes in 2026 are currently up 6.3% over 2025 actuals and are 
projected to end the year 6% over the 2026 original budget forecast. This increase is mostly 
as a result of the funding received from Erie related to the Nine Mile revenue sharing 
agreement as 2026 is the first full year that this revenue will have been received. 
Additionally, sales and use audits conducted in 2025 brought several businesses into 
compliance with tax reporting and remittance, thus leading to increased collections in 2026.  
Due to the uncertainty in the economy and its direct impact on the consumer, the City is 
budgeting an increase of 3% in sales tax for the 2027 budget, or a total of $26.2 million, 
which includes expected distributions from the Nine Mile revenue sharing agreement.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
While the City primarily relies on sales tax, use tax is also a key revenue source. Use tax 
includes motor vehicle use tax, business use tax, and construction use tax, all of which are 
volatile based on a number of economic and community conditions, including new 
commercial development and vehicle purchases. Motor vehicle use tax is down 15% year-to-
date (“YTD”) in 2026 compared to 2025 actuals, largely driven by a one-time uptick last year 
due to robust electric vehicle incentives. Construction use tax is projected to decline in 2026 
from 2025 and is currently down 23% when compared to 2025 actuals. However, business

Property Tax 
Property taxes flow primarily into the General Fund and Debt Service Fund, the latter of 
which is used to fund debt service on a 2016 bond that financed construction of the Great 
Outdoors Water Park. This debt and associated mill levy will be paid off and expire in 2026.  
Property tax tends to act as a revenue stabilizer given its slower velocity of assessment and 
collection in combination with the general strength of the local real estate markets over the 
last decade. The Boulder County Assessor’s Office reassesses property values every two 
years and, as such, property taxes typically see a large increase in assessment years and a 
small change in the non-assessed years. Over the last five biennial assessment years, the 
total assessed values of real estate in Lafayette have increased by an average of 16.7%, 
while the average increase for the last five non-assessment years has been 1.3%. 
Total assessed value is projected to decline in 2027 and thus the resulting property tax 
collected due to the implementation of a new State tax law. This law provides up to a 
$70,000 reduction in taxable value for homeowners and lowers the assessment rate for 
commercial properties. 2026 General Fund property tax is projected to increase 2.3% over 
2025 actuals and forecast to decrease slightly in 2027 (down 0.1%) as compared to 2026.  
General Fund property tax forecast in the 2027 budget is $12.6 million. 
Fees for Service 
Fees are charges for specific services provided to the community such as recreation, 
development review, and building permits.  
The City Code allows City Council to establish certain charges and fees for municipal 
programs, goods, and services to defray the City’s costs in providing such services (Chapter 
5, Article 1, Sec. 5-4). The costs of the fee must be reasonably related to the City’s costs of 
providing the programs, goods, or services to residents and other users.  
To manage these fees, the City has maintained a fee schedule wherein specific fees are 
updated during each annual budget process through resolutions. In 2025, the City updated 
the fee schedules and clarified how fees are updated, where the fee is established (in the 
code, through a resolution, or through a separate fee schedule), and who (City Council or the 
City Manager) approves any updates. Specifically, City Council approves and updates fees 
related to the licenses, permits, or other charges that are generally related to public peace, 
public health, and safety, including but not limited to liquor and marijuana licensing; 
planning, building, and development; emergency medical services; utilities; and solid waste 
5 
 
collection. The City Manager updates fees related to operations, including but not limited to 
facility rentals and use of park, recreation, and golf amenities and services.  
Following a study of parks, recreation, and golf fees in 2025, a cost recovery model 
was adopted in January of 2026. Implementation of this model will be phased with fee 
adjustments that include the addition of nonresident rates being assessed for admission to 
the Great Outdoors Water Park (effective summer 2026) and the Bob L. Burger Recreation 
Center (effective fall 2026). Staff is also working to transition the current reduced rate 
recreation program to a new financial assistance program for income qualified residents. 
The new program which will be rolled out in September to allow for flexible user access to 
recreation programs and services.   
Anticipated Fee Changes for 2027  
Staff has identified a few areas for fee changes and updates that will be incorporated into 
the 2027 budget, including:  
• Planning/Building Fees – In conjunction with the recent permit software changes in 
Planning and Building, staff is proposing fee updates for 2027. Planning fees 
increased in 2026 for the first time since 2019 and in order to account for increased 
costs and changes in best practice that have not consistently been addressed since 
2019 (prior the changes in 2026), these fees need to continue to be updated. 
Planning fees are still well under market compared to Planning fees of comparable 
municipalities across the state. With this information, Planning fees are proposed to 
increase 9% in 2027. 
• Fire Code Fees – The Fire Department participates in the plan review process and 
conducts routine inspections at commercial and industrial properties. At present 
there are only a few fees established or charged for these services. Beginning in 
2027, the City is proposing a fee schedule to be implemented for cost recovery for 
the most time intensive services. This fee schedule is based on the hourly workload 
of Fire Department staff to review and complete inspections and reviews. As these 
inspections are generally related to new construction. 
The City’s water, water reclamation, and stormwater utilities operate as enterprises under 
the Colorado Taxpayer Bill of Rights. Each utility enterprise functions as a separate 
“business” with rates and fees set by Council and assessed to utility customers with the 
intent of generating sufficient revenue to support projected expenses. The primary sources 
of revenue are monthly rates and fees assessed to existing utility customers; “tap fees” 
assessed to new customers to offset impacts on existing and future infrastructure; and 
dedication of water rights (or cash-in-lieu of water rights) by new customers to increase the 
City’s water rights portfolio to meet associated increased demands. The utilities are not 
6 
 
funded by tax dollars and typically have unique revenue and expenditure trends that do not 
correlate consistently with overall economic trends.  
The City commissioned Raftelis Financial Consultants in 2025 to update the financial plans 
for each of the three utility enterprises, including analysis of both projected revenues and 
expenditures. A multi-year rate/fee recommendation was developed with the first phase 
implemented Jan. 1, 2026. While some projected costs have escalated more rapidly than 
projected, and 2026 water revenues remain uncertain due to drought restrictions, proposed 
2027 rates remain based on the 2025 analysis. This includes Jan. 1, 2027, adjustments of 
16% in the water utility, 9% in the water reclamation utility, and 8% in the stormwater utility.  
Tap fees for new customers connecting to the City system are proposed to increase by 2.9% 
based on the regional Harvey-Witman Index, which tracks water utility-related capital costs.  
A new analysis will be commissioned in early 2027 to evaluate 2026 revenues and overall 
revenue trends, review operating costs, and update projected capital costs and project 
timing. That analysis will inform the proposed 2028 budget and rates.    
Revenues associated with fee changes will be incorporated into the 2027 budget for 
approval. 
Expenditures 
With limited capacity to add new ongoing programs based on slowing revenue growth, the 
City focused on sustaining core services and maintaining previous operational investments, 
all while strategically planning to address the City’s capital needs.  
The 2026 budget gave heightened attention to capital needs, ensuring that critical 
infrastructure and facility investments were prioritized to support long-term community 
resilience and service delivery and this attention continues in the 2027 Recommended 
Budget. The anticipated 2027 expenditures are broken down by fund in Figure 2 and

Salaries and Benefits 
Salaries and benefits remain one of the largest categories of City expenditures and reflect 
the City’s ongoing commitment to the compensation philosophy established in 2024. That 
philosophy has been applied successfully, helping the City remain competitive in the market 
and recruit and retain top talent. The 2027 Recommended Budget includes funding to 
maintain alignment with this philosophy, and the restructuring of the City’s health benefits 
for 2027 supports a more financially sustainable and balanced total compensation 
structure. For 2027, the City is restructuring its employee health benefits to provide more 
choice, improve long-term financial sustainability, and better align costs with actual plan 
use. The updated package includes a modernized Health Maintenance Organization 
(“HMO”) plan and a new High-Deductible Health Plan with a City-funded Health Savings 
Account. These changes support informed healthcare decision-making, ensure the City 
remains competitive, and allow for a more balanced total compensation structure.   
For salaries, the 2027 budget includes a projected 4% average increase for general City 
employees. The City also has two employee associations with collective bargaining 
agreements, the International Firefighter’s Association (“IAFF”) and the Fraternal Order of 
Police (“FOP”). Each agreement includes a 4% salary increase for members in 2027.

Operating 
In the environment of economic uncertainty and flattening sales and use tax revenue, staff 
made a concerted effort to assess base operating budgets to ensure that budgeted funds 
were necessary and, if not, where funding could be repurposed to reduce net budget 
increases. This added budget scrutiny, refinement, and reclassification helped to offset the 
net budget increases.  
Transfers 
This category consists primarily of transfers from a variety of funds to the General Fund for 
cost allocation, from the General Fund to the Capital Projects Fund to support new capital 
projects, and internal contributions to fund such things as City-wide insurance premiums 
and bulk vehicle purchasing. Contributions to the Employee Benefit Fund are not included in 
this transfer line item to allow for more accurate presentation of salaries and benefits 
expenses. 
Debt Service 
Debt service will increase in the 2027 Recommended Budget compared to the 2026 budget 
to include debt service payments for the capital bond package related to design and 
construction of the three new or improved facilities at the Bob L. Burger Recreation Center, 
Service Center, and Civic Center, approved by voters at the Nov. 4, 2025, coordinated 
election.   
Debt Services includes a 2002 Lafayette Corporate Campus General Improvement District 
(“GID”) General Obligation Bonds; 2006 Lafayette Tech Center GID General Obligation 
Bonds; 2012 Water Revenue Refunding Bonds; and 2021 Water Reclamation Revenue 
Refunding Bonds to finance improvements to the Water Reclamation Facility that are 
required based on regulations issued by the State of Colorado.  
Capital 
As in recent budget years, capital spending continues to be a high priority for the City and is 
reflected as such in the 2027 Recommended Budget. The City is continuing to more actively 
plan for capital repair and replacements to begin addressing compounding capital facility 
and project needs. Included in the 2027 Recommended Budget are several capital 
improvement projects intended to more proactively plan for capital replacements and 
improvements prior to their failure.

Budget Responsibilities

• City Manager, with assistance of Department Heads, prepares and 
submits a balanced annual budget and proposed rates and fees to

Lafayette’s Compensation Philosophy
Adopted in 2024, the City’s compensation 
philosophy focuses on:
• Financial Sustainability – Supported by 
ongoing revenues
• Market Competitiveness – Competitive total 
compensation aligned with our labor market
• Internal Equity – Consistent and equitable pay 
for similar work
22

Total Compensation Overview
23
• Overall, total compensation remains competitive and aligned with 
our regional market
• 2027 Opportunity: Rebalance total compensation to strengthen 
salaries while preserving high-quality benefits
Why restructure our health benefits?
• Employee Priorities – Greater investment in salary is a consistent 
employee priority
• Rising Healthcare Costs – Recent significant increases with continued 
increases expected
• More Choice – Employees have requested additional healthcare options
• Benefits vs. Utilization – Current coverage exceeds both the market and 
what most employees utilize
Employee Partnership: An Employee Benefit Group was formed to identify 
employee priorities, helping the City shape a plan that maintains excellent 
benefits and supports long-term financial sustainability.
24
2027 Health Plan Restructure
• Maintain an HMO Option
• Continue offering a high-quality Kaiser HMO
• Introduce modest employee cost-sharing
• Plan will remain market-leading compared with peer agencies
• Add a New HDHP + HSA Option
• Introduce a second healthcare option with no employee premium share
• City-funded HSA contribution of $1,750 Employee Only / $3,500 other tiers
• Provides employees greater choice and flexibility

Rebalancing Total Compensation in 2027
• 2027 Proposed Salary Increase for General Employees: 4% 
• Aligns with the 4% increases included in the Police and Fire collective 
bargaining agreements
• Health plan savings help support increased investment in salaries
• Maintains market-leading benefits while strengthening base pay
• The 2027 budget successfully rebalances total compensation while 
maintaining market competitiveness and long-term financial 
sustainability.
