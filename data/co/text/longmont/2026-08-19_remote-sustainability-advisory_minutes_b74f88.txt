A. Platte River Power Authority Update – Noncarbon Energy & Integrated Resource Plan  
Staff Member Darrell Hahn, the City of Longmont’s Director of Electric Services (Longmont Power & 
Communications or LPC), reminded the Board about the City’s relationship with Platte River Power 
Authority (Platte River) as one of its four community owners.  He then introduced Platte River staff 
members who presented on the current progress toward its 100% noncarbon energy by 2030 goal, 
as well as some contributing factors impacting it.  Platte River Staff Member Melie Vincent noted 
that several assumptions from the 2018 Resource Diversification Policy had not materialized, 
making it impossible to be at 100% noncarbon by 2030; however, Platte River was still striving to 
achieve a 100% noncarbon energy future.  She emphasized that ensuring reliability and 
affordability was equally as important as environmental responsibility in the organization’s 
planning considerations.  Platte River provided power to approximately 350,000 residents across 
the four communities, and in July 2026, reached a new peak demand of about 740 megawatts 
(MW) of energy. 
Vincent highlighted several steps of progress since 2018 to add wind and solar energy, as well as 
some energy storage.  In 2023, Platte River entered the Southwest Power Pool’s energy imbalance 
market.  Its 2024 Integrated Resource Plan (IRP) was approved in 2024, and Platte River went live 
with Phase 1 of the Black Hollow Sun project in 2025.  Because of a Department of Energy 
Emergency Order, Platte River was unable to retire Craig Unit 1 at the end of 2025.  Vincent 
explained that selling some renewable energy credits (RECs) decreased Platte River’s overall 
percentage of noncarbon energy that could be reported by owner communities in 2025 because

those credits were accounted for through the entities who purchased them.  Platte River intended 
to continue selling RECs through 2029 and investing the revenue received to offset the cost of 
investing in new noncarbon and associated energy projects, and to soften rate pressure on 
customers. 
Vincent outlined work underway to develop the 2028 IRP Update, beginning with conversations 
with external consultants to advise on emerging issues in technology.  She shared that in early 
2027, Platte River would begin collecting and modeling data, and stakeholder engagement 
opportunities would be available throughout the process.  Platte River expected to finalize the Plan 
by January 2028, with presentation to their Board later that spring and subsequent filing with the 
Western Area Power Administration (WAPA) in July 2028.  The SAB expressed interest in Platte 
River staff returning during the 2028 IRP update process, but a specific date was not set. 
City of Longmont Staff Member KirkJaffe then explained how Platte River’s sale of RECs impacted 
Longmont’s modeled greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from 2025 through 2029, even though 
Platte River continued to add more noncarbon energy resources.  Longmont completes a GHG 
inventory every three years, and she noted that staff was expecting to see an increase in emissions 
in the 2027 GHG Inventory, but then a significant decrease in the 2030 GHG Inventory, provided 
that Platte River discontinued selling RECs.  KirkJaffe shared modeling projections with the Board 
based on the new information from Platte River.  This model included Platte River’s renewable 
energy projection and assumed the City of Longmont would meet all other targets identified in the 
Sustainability Plan and Climate Action Recommendations Report that support GHG emissions 
reductions.  The City would update GHG modeling in the year ahead to accommodate separate 
scenarios to see progress toward its targets and expectations for where it would be in 2030. 
The Board asked several questions for further details about planning for additional emerging 
technologies, affordability, and reliability concerns.  Vincent noted that forging the path forward in 
renewable energy technologies provided constant opportunity to learn about the relationships 
between all the factors in creating a noncarbon energy future, which would also require patience 
and flexibility in discovery.  As those findings unfolded, forecasting models for how the technology 
relationships worked together would become more accurate over time.  Platte River Staff Member 
Paul Davis shared about incentives and programs offered with the owner communities to educate 
residents and business owners about energy conservation, encouraging them to adopt new 
technologies, and helping them make their homes and businesses more energy efficient.  He 
mentioned a couple upcoming programs that residents could participate in that would help build a 
virtual power plant network, including smart thermostats and electric vehicle charge management. 
Melie Vincent left the meeting at 5:30 pm. 
Additional conversation between the Board and City of Longmont staff followed about which 
metrics would trigger more focused responses to its climate goals if staff was predicting that those 
goals would not be met.  Staff noted that there were no specific metrics that would trigger this 
response; instead, staff provided updates, as available, to City Council on goal progress to inform 
decision making at that level.  Staff advised the members that their feedback on the annual report 
could help inform City Council of any reprioritization the Board thought the City should address to 
achieve those goals.
