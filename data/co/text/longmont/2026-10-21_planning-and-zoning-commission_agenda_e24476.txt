Project Summary
This project originated from a citizen-initiated request to amend the Land Development Code 
(LDC) to allow Contractor’s Shops within the Mixed Use-Employment (MU-E) and 
Nonresidential-Primary Employment (N-PE) zoning districts. Per LDC procedures, the request 
was referred to the City Council for consideration. During the Regular Session on February 
24th, 2026, Council directed staff to begin analyzing the proposed changes.  In response to 
Council directions, staff initiated a review of relevant code sections, potential land use impacts, 
and compatibility concerns associated with expanding where Contractor’s Shops may operate. 
This analysis is ongoing and is intended to help determine whether the requested use 
changes could be supported and what additional standards may be necessary to mitigate 
impacts. 
This item is being presented to the Planning and Zoning Commission for review, comment and 
recommendation. Staff’s analysis and the Commission’s recommendation will be forwarded to 
the City Council for a zero reading (introduction, no decision being made) on December 1, 
2026.

II. Project Description
The applicant has requested amendments to Section 15.04.020, Table of Allowed Uses, to 
broaden where Contractor’s Shops may operate within the city. Specifically, the request 
seeks to allow Contractor’s Shops as a Permitted Use in the Mixed Use-Employment (MU-E) 
zoning district and as a Conditional Secondary Use in the Nonresidential- Primary 
Employment (N-PE) zoning district.  Under current regulations, Contractor’s Shops are 
categorized as an Industrial Use within the Storage and Warehousing classification and are 
permitted only as a Conditional Use in the MU-E district, subject to Conditional Use Review 
by the Planning and Zoning Commission in a public hearing.
The Land Development Code defines a Contractor’s Shop as an establishment that provides 
a trade service, including, but not limited to, plumbing, carpentry, glass/glazing, welding,

The LDC includes purpose statements for all zoning districts, including the Mixed Use-
Employment (MU-E) and Nonresidential-Primary Employment (N-PE) districts. These purpose 
statements provide the framework for understanding the intended land use patterns, 
development form and expectations within each district, and are helpful when evaluating how 
Contractor’s Shops fit within those contexts.
Mixed Use-Employment (MU-E) District
The purpose of the MU-E district is to provide areas appropriate for diverse employment by 
encouraging a variety of employment spaces in close proximity to attached residential and 
supporting services. The district offers flexibility in building types and uses, supports adaptive 
reuse of existing buildings and promotes employment and live/work opportunities as well as 
multifamily residential uses. MU-E is intended to accommodate a wide range of business 
opportunities within a pedestrian-friendly environment and provide easy access to parks, 
open space and public transit.
Contractor’s Shops are already allowed in the MU-E district today as a Conditional (C) use. 
This conditional status requires a public hearing process where the Planning and Zoning 
Commission can evaluate site-specific characteristics, require mitigation, and ensure 
compatibility with adjacent activities and uses.  Staff continue to have concerns about 
expanding the use to a Permitted (P) use in the MU-E, as requested by the applicant, 
because Contractor’s Shops commonly involve fleet mobilization, early-morning and late-
evening operations, loading and unloading of materials and equipment, noise and substantial 
outdoor storage, all impacts that may conflict with the MU-E’s mixed-use and amenity focused 
intent.
If the Planning and Zoning Commission recommends approval to change Contractor’s Shops 
from Conditional (C) to Permitted (P) in the MU-E, staff would recommend adopting new use-

Nonresidential-Primary Employment (N-PE) District
The N-PE district is intended to establish areas for a wide range of employment uses, 
including manufacturing, industrial processing, research and development, warehousing and 
wholesaling, office-flex space and large employment facilities such as office campuses and 
corporate headquarters.  The district also accommodates limited supporting commercial uses 
intended to meet the daily needs of employees within the district. 
The purpose statement of the N-PE district better aligns with the operational characteristic of 
Contractor’s Shops when comparing to the MU-E. The district is intended for higher-intensity 
employment uses that could involve fleet vehicles, outdoor storage of materials and 
equipment, loading and unloading of materials, and other trade-based service activities, 
impacts like those generated by Contractor’s Shops. The absence of residential uses within 
the N-PE means the district is generally less sensitive to these impacts, which suggests that 
the N-PE may be more capable of accommodating Contractor’s Shops than the MU-E.
This does not mean Contractor’s Shops are a perfect fit for the N-PE district. Their relative 
compatibility stems more from the fact that no residential uses are permitted within the district 
rather than from consistency with the full range of permitted uses in the N-PE. Furthermore, 
parcels within the N-PE can still be located adjacent to residential uses or residential lots in 
residential zoning districts. In these situations, Contractor’s Shops could still generate 
operational, audio and/or visual impacts that may affect nearby homes, even if the business is 
located with the N-PE zone district.
Given these factors, staff supports allowing Contractor’s Shops in the N-PE district, as it 
appears to be a better fit compared to the MU-E. At the same time, staff question the 
applicant’s request to allow the use as a Conditional (C) use in the N-PE while proposing it as 
a Permitted (P) use in the MU-E, especially since the N-PE district appears to offer a more

Summary of Zoning Context
This was a citizen-initiated request and staff recognize that applicants may approach requests 
to consider changes to the Table of Allowed Use based on their individual experience rather 
than a full understanding of how different zoning districts function or relate to one another. For 
that reason, it is understandable that the applicant’s proposal may not fully align with the 
broader land use considerations evaluated by staff. Through this analysis, staff may 
recommend an approach that differs from what was originally requested, including identifying 
the N-PE district as a potentially more suitable location for Contractor’s Shops compared to 
the MU-E. Even though this alternative was not part of the applicant’s initial proposal, staff’s 
role is to consider the request comprehensively and offer recommendations that expand 
opportunities for Contractor’s Shops while also protecting existing and future disparate uses. 
Following City Council’s direction to process an amendment to the Land Development Code 
regarding Contractor’s Shops, staff initiated the required steps to carry out that directive. As 
part of processing the request, staff conducted a thorough review and analysis of the 
proposed changes, including evaluating existing code language, considering potential 
operational and compatibility impacts and identifying areas where additional standards may 
be necessary to support any future amendments.

and Zoning Commission on how they should be refined. Following P&Z input, staff will refine

1. Standards Applicable to All Contractor's Shops
a. Contractor's shops shall conduct fabrication, assembly, repair, maintenance, 
painting, and other trade activities associated with the contractor's business within 
an enclosed building. Outdoor areas shall be used only for activities expressly 
permitted in this section.
b. Outdoor operation or testing of equipment shall be limited to activities necessary for 
loading, unloading, inspection, or preparation for transport.
c. Employee vehicles and commercial vehicles associated with a contractor's shop 
shall be parked on-site within designated parking areas.
d. Loading, unloading, and temporary staging of materials and equipment associated 
with active projects is permitted, provided such activities comply with applicable 
standards of this code.
e. Contractor's shops shall comply with all other applicable standards of this 
development code and other applicable regulations.
These standards establish baseline operating expectations for all Contractor’s Shops, 
regardless of location, zoning district or proximity to residential uses. Requiring fabrication, 
repair and assembly activities to occur indoors helps contain noise, dust and visual impacts. 
Limiting outdoor operations to essential tasks such as loading equipment and materials helps 
prevent unnecessary disruption. The requirement for onsite parking is intended to ensure 
employees and commercial vehicles do not spill into surrounding neighborhoods, particularly 
older or mixed-use areas where street parking is frequently used by nearby residences. 
Finally, by requiring orderly loading, staging and general compliance with the Land 
Development Code, shops should operate in a predictable, well-managed way, reducing the 
likelihood of off-site impacts on residential uses.
2. Residential Proximity Standards
a. A contractor’s shop in the Mixed Use-Employment zoning district, without outdoor 
storage does not require conditional use review, 
b. Any outdoor storage proposed as part of a contractor's shop located within 250 feet

conditional use approval pursuant to section 15.02.060.C.
c. Contractor's shops located within 250 feet of residential use or a residential lot in a 
residential zoning district shall limit outdoor activities, including loading, unloading, 
staging, movement of equipment, and access to outdoor storage areas, as follows:
1. No outdoor activities shall occur between 7:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m., Monday 
through Saturday. 
2. No outdoor activities shall occur before 9:00 a.m. on Sundays and Holidays.

3. Standards for Contractor's Shops Without Outdoor Storage
a. All vehicles, trailers, equipment, materials, and supplies associated with the 
contractor's shop shall be stored within an enclosed building.
b. Outdoor storage of vehicles, trailers, equipment, materials, supplies, or other items 
associated with the contractor's shop is prohibited. 
c. Contractor shops approved without outdoor storage are prohibited from having any 
Outdoor sales, display of merchandise, storage or other activity which would

These standards seek to provide a clear and predictable option for applicants who choose to 
operate a Contractor’s Shop without outdoor storage.  Some applicants may decide that 
keeping all vehicles, equipment, materials and supplies indoors is advantageous because it 
reduces external impacts and could allow the use to be permitted by right in some locations. 
By prohibiting outdoor storage and outdoor activities, these standards safeguard that 
Contractor’s Shops choosing this approach maintain a use fully enclosed within a building. 
When all equipment, vehicles and materials are stored and activities are conducted indoors, 
the business can operate in a manner similar to other permitted indoor uses such as artisanal 
manufacturing and light/medium industrial uses, which generate minimal exterior impacts and 
function entirely within an enclosed building. This gives applicants flexibility on how they 
structure their operation and allows Contactor’s Shops without outdoor activities to fit more 
easily into areas of the city where outdoor yards would otherwise create noise or visual 
impacts.

VI. Neighborhood Notification and Posting and Stakeholder Input
Per Section 15.02.040.J.1 of the Land Development Code, no public notification is required for 
Land Development Code text amendments. Because this item involves proposed revisions to 
the Table of Allowed Uses and the creation of new use-specific standards, it is processed as 
an LDC text amendment and therefore required no mailed, posted or published notice.
Prior to the public hearing before the Planning and Zoning Commission, staff conducted target 
outreach. The draft use-specific standards and proposed changes to the Table of Allowed 
Uses were emailed to the City of Longmont Impact Committee, which includes representatives 
from the Longmont Downtown Development Authority (LDDA), the Longmont Area Chamber 
of Commerce and Longmont Economic Development Partners (LEDP), with a request to 
review and comment. At the time of writing this communication, no comments had been 
received. Upon follow-up, staff was informed that the committee did not anticipate submitting 
comments, as the proposed changes appeared minor to the organizations represented on the 
Impact Committee.

Section 15.02.060.D.3. – Land Development Code (text) Amendments
The sole review criterion for proposed text amendment to the development code is that 
the proposed amendment is consistent with the purpose and intent of the land 
development code set out in Section 15.01.030.
Per the Land Development Code, text amendments are evaluated under a single review 
criterion: that the proposed amendment is consistent with the purpose and intent of the Land 
Development Code, as set forth in Section 15.01.030. This section outlines the Code’s 
overarching goals, including protecting public health and welfare, maintaining livable 
neighborhoods, promoting orderly development, conserving community character, and 
minimizing adverse impacts, particularly adjacent to residential uses.

The standards are designed to mitigate external impacts commonly associated with

compatibility, staff are still uncertain whether all impacts can be fully mitigated in the MU-E and 
N-PE districts. The amendment’s consistency with Section 15.01.030 therefore depends 
heavily on careful siting decisions and the adoption of strong, enforceable use-specific 
standards.

adoption of use-specific standards, but the compatibility concerns described earlier in this

II. Project Description
On January 27, 2026, Councilmember Marsing directed the City Manager and City Attorney, “on 
a timeline consistent with staff’s current workplan, to prepare and introduce on zero reading for 
Council’s consideration a draft ordinance amending Section 15.04.020 of the Longmont 
Municipal Code to allow single-family attached dwellings in the R-SF zoning district, and to 
return to Council with any recommended or questions or revisions prior to first reading.” This 
communication seeks to inform the Planning and Zoning Commission on the proposed 
amendment that the Commission has already expressed interest in and allow the Commission 
to provide feedback to staff that can be provided to the City Council at prior to zero reading of 
the ordinance. Staff analysis and any official feedback the Commission would like to provide will 
be provided to the City Council at zero reading for the ordinance which is scheduled for 
December 1, 2026. During the zero reading the Council will receive a presentation from the Staff 
and then have an opportunity to provide direction on the amendment prior to a first reading.

The purpose of the residential single-family (R-SF) zone district as outlined in Section 
15.03.020.C.1 of the Longmont Land Development Code (LDC) is “…to establish and preserve 
residential neighborhoods with primarily single-family detached dwellings on medium to larger 
lots. The district is intended to be located in proximity to activity centers and other public 
amenities or complementary uses and activities such as schools, parks, and open spaces”. 
Single-family detached dwellings are defined in the LDC as, “…detached dwelling designed 
exclusively for, and occupied by, one family.” (Section 15.10.010.A). 
Single-family attached dwellings are defined in the LDC as, “… a building containing between 
two and four single-family dwelling units, each with its own entrance, which are joined together 
by a vertical common party wall.” (Section 15.10.010.A). 
In the City of Longmont there are 19,797 lots that are zoned R-SF, encompassing approximately 
4,239 acres of land. Under the current LDC and State law, accessory dwelling units (ADUs) are 
allowed by right as an accessory use to a single-family detached dwelling regardless of lot size 
and density requirements in a given zoning district. 
The minimum lot size in the R-SF zone district is 5,000 square feet (Table 15.03.020(2)) and the 
density requirement for the R-SF zone district is between one and eight dwelling units per acre 
(Section 15.03.080.B.3.a.ii). Amending Table 15.04.020(2) by permitting attached single-family 
dwellings would allow duplexes, triplexes, and quadplexes by right in the R-SF zone district

This update would not change the density range requirement, nor would it change the minimum 
lot size requirement. Staff has created a series of maps to help visualize what this code update, 
adding “dwelling, single-family attached” as a permitted use in the R-SF zone district in Table 
15.04.020(2), would mean in terms of lots eligible for potential development of attached single-
family dwellings (Attachment B). If the allowed density for the R-SF zone district is between 1 
and 8 dwelling units per acre, the minimum lot size for a single dwelling unit is 5,445 square 
feet. This was calculated by dividing the number of square feet in an acre (43,560) by the 
maximum allowed number of units (8). Although the code does allow 5,000 square feet as the 
minimum lot size for R-SF lots (and 4,000 square feet for R-SF lots with alley loaded garages) 
this larger and more restrictive number was used by staff in this analysis to align with the intent 
of Envision Longmont for the R-SF zone district to prioritize lower density housing. Using this 
number (5,445sqft), staff have created maps to show which R-SF properties in the City of 
Longmont would be able to accommodate attached single-family home developments assuming 
no subdivision of the lots (See attachment B). 
There are currently 2,633 R-SF zoned lots in the city that are at least 10,890 square feet (the 
minimum lot size required to accommodate two dwelling units based on density requirements 
for the R-SF zone district) and have one existing dwelling unit. This means that there are 2,633 
R-SF lots that would be eligible for attached single-family development if the proposed code 
update is approved; this is 13.3% of the total lots in the R-SF zone district. Extrapolating further 
to calculate how many lots could accommodate three and four attached units while complying 
with the maximum density requirement means that there are 530 lots that are at least 16,335 
square feet (can accommodate up to 3 dwelling units/ triplex) and 161 lots that are at least 
21,780 square feet (can accommodate up to 4 dwelling units/quadplex). Accessory dwelling 
units (ADUs) are permitted by right on all R-SF lots as an accessory use to detached single-

As proposed, density requirements and minimum lot standards would still be applicable, but 
duplexes, triplexes, and quadplexes would be permitted by right. These developments would 
require site plan review and approval by the Development Review Committee under current 
review procedures required in Section 15.02. An existing R-SF lot that meets the density and lot 
size requirements to allow an attached single-family dwelling, but already has an ADU, would 
not be eligible for attached single-family development because Section 15.02.080.E.3.a.ii 
stipulates that an ADU may only be allowed as an accessory use to a detached single-family 
dwelling.
The proposed code update does not modify or update the density requirement for the R-SF 
zone district, nor does it update or modify lot standards found in Section 15.07.030. Section 
15.07.030.E.2 states that “lots intended for attached residential dwellings, such as townhome 
lots, shall have a minimum street frontage of 20 feet.”.
III. Alignment with Envision Longmont
The Envision Longmont Comprehensive Plan outlines the following characteristics for “Single-
Family Neighborhood”
1. Includes neighborhoods (of all ages) that are comprised predominantly of single-family 
detached homes.
2. Includes protections for existing structures designated as historic landmarks by the 
City of Longmont, and provides guidelines for new development or redevelopment of 
non-designated structures in historic districts in order to protect the character and 
integrity of historic neighborhoods.
3. Secondary uses, such as accessory dwelling units, are designed in a manner to fit the 
scale and intensity of surrounding residential uses, or are integrated into the overall 
design of new Single-Family Neighborhood developments.
4. A well-defined pattern of blocks and direct pedestrian and bicycle connections provides 
residents with direct access to nearby centers and corridors, as well as to services and 
amenities such as schools, parks, open space, or recreation centers.
Envision Longmont specifies the land uses for Single-Family Neighborhood in the following 
way:
Primary: Single-family detached homes
Secondary: Accessory dwelling units, parks, greenways, recreation, community gardens, 
schools, places of worship, and other complementary uses.

Staff has considered the proposed text amendment and would offer the following analysis to 
help the Commission and the City Council in determining the next steps for this proposal.
1. The text amendment as proposed will not require new units to be for sale units 
(townhomes). In fact, it will make rental units easier to be constructed in the R-SF zone 
district.
2. The comprehensive plan, Envision Longmont, will need to be amended so that this 
code amendment is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan and the purpose of the R-
SF land use.
3. By amending Section 15.04.020 to allow attached single-family dwellings in the R-SF 
zone district, any proposed development will still be subject to density standards in 
Section 15.03.080.B.3.a.ii.
4. By amending Section 15.04.020 to allow attached single-family dwellings in the R-SF 
zone district, any proposed development will still be subject to lot dimensional 
standards in Section 15.03.020.C.2.
