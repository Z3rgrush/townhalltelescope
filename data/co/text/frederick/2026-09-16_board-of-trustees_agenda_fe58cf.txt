Safe & Resilient Community 
 
• The Police Department recently launched the Blue Heart Initiative, a community -
based safety and awareness program designed to strengthen relationships 
between first responders and individuals with visible as well as invisible disabilities, 
including those who are neurodivergent or living with mental or physical health 
conditions. The initiative focuses on building trust before a crisis occurs through 
responder training and community engagement. Participating residents display a 
bold, blue heart sticker on their home, vehicle, wheelchair, or backpack to alert first 
responders. Additionally, they complete confidential disability profiles providing 
responders information on how to approach, communicate with, and best them in 
times of need. The program promotes increased safety, dignity, and improved 
communication between officers and the community which leads to more positive 
outcomes. 
• The Police Department’s Axon Assistant software is now live on all officers' body-
worn cameras. This software gives them access to three valuable tools in the field 
without compromising safety. Real-Time Translation provides two-way translation 
for 50+ languages for traffic stops or other incidents when a human interpreter is 
not immediately available. Agency Inf o, also called Policy Chat, is loaded with 
department policy, procedures, municipal code, court dates, field sobriety test 
instructions, and business emergency contacts so officers can get immediate, on-
scene answers. The General Info tool answers everyday  questions from open 
sources such as street names for common drugs, HazMat class meanings, simple 
calculations, and similar quick facts so officers can keep their attention on the 
person rather than a phone. All tools are officer -activated via their body c amera 
and are intended as decision support. Critical actions continue to follow written 
policy and, for translation, a certified interpreter is utilized when formal statements 
or court use is required.

Summary Statement: 
 
Reallocate the Municipal Services Building bond interest expense among the funds that 
use and benefit from the facility. The allocation is based on FTEs housed in the 
building, with the General Fund retaining the Fleet and Facilities share. This 
Page 26 of 39
amendment does not create new debt or increase Town spending; it more accurately 
assigns an existing obligation to the benefiting funds. 
 
Detail of Issue/Request: 
 
The 2026 adopted budget currently reflects $2,281,700 in Municipal Services Building 
bond interest expense within the General Fund. However, the Municipal Services 
Building supports multiple Town operations and houses employees, equipment, 
supplies, and operational functions associated with the Street and Alley Fund, Water 
Fund, Stormwater Fund, Parks and Open Space Fund, Fleet, and Facilities. 
To more accurately align the cost of the facility with the funds receiving the benefit, staff 
is recommending that the bond interest expense be reallocated based on the number of 
full-time equivalent employees associated with each operating area housed within the 
facility. Using FTEs provides a consistent and supportable methodology for distributing 
the cost based on relative use of the building. 
Under the proposed allocation, the Street and Alley Fund would be allocated 
approximately $499,781, the Water Fund $541,956, the Stormwater Fund $501,890, 
and the Parks and Open Space Fund $485,019. The remaining approximately 
$253,054 associated with Fleet and Facilities would remain in the General Fund 
because those functions are accounted for within the General Fund. 
The requested action is approval of Ordinance No. 1424 to amend the 2026 Fiscal Year 
Budget and reflect this revised allocation. The amendment does not increase the 
Town’s total debt obligation or overall expenditures; it only changes the fund-level 
allocation of an existing bond interest expense.
