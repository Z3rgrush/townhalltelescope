RE:   Proposition 123 Affordable Housing Fast Track Expedited Review Policy 
 
SUMMARY 
The purpose of this memorandum is to provide the Planning & Zoning Commission with an 
overview of the proposed Affordable Housing Expedited Review Policy and associated 
Resolution related to Colorado Proposition 123 requirements for participating local 
governments. 
In 2022, Colorado voters approved Proposition 123, creating a statewide affordable 
housing funding program administered through the Colorado Department of Local Affairs 
(DOLA). One requirement for continued eligibility for Proposition 123 funding is the 
establishment of an expedited review process for qualifying affordable housing 
developments pursuant to C.R.S. § 29-32-105(2). 
The proposed policy establishes a formal City procedure to prioritize and expedite review of 
qualifying affordable housing applications in which at least ﬁfty percent (50%) of the 
residential units meet the affordability thresholds established in State statute. The policy 
formalizes review timelines, identiﬁes applicable development review processes, and 
establishes procedures intended to ensure ﬁnal decisions are rendered within the required 
ninety (90) calendar day review period following determination of a complete application. 
Importantly, DOLA is currently offering early adoption incentive funding to communities 
that adopt compliant expedited review policies and resolutions by June 30, 2026. The City 
of Edgewater has been identiﬁed as eligible for approximately $45,000 in incentive grant 
funding associated with early adoption. 
Staff intends to utilize these funds to support the development of more robust affordable 
housing implementation strategies and regulatory tools as part of the ongoing 2026 Zoning 
Code Update project. Potential future work products may include affordable housing 
incentives, missing middle housing strategies, administrative process improvements, form 
and scale standards, and other housing-related code modernization efforts consistent with 
the Edgewater 2040 Comprehensive Plan and recent State legislation.

The proposed policy is also intended to formalize and document review practices that, in 
many cases, are already generally consistent with the City’s existing development review 
timelines and customer service objectives. 
Staff will provide an overview presentation of the proposed Resolution and Policy at the 
upcoming meeting and will be available for questions and discussion.

WHEREAS, the City of Edgewater, Colorado ( “City”) is a home rule municipality 
having all powers conferred by Article XX of the Colorado Constitution, including the 
power to regulate the zoning and use of land within its jurisdiction; and 
WHEREAS, in 2022, Colorado voters approved Proposition 123 and the creation 
of a State Affordable Housing Fund which makes funds available to Colorado local 
governments on certain conditions in furtherance of State affordable housing goals; and 
WHEREAS, to be eligible for State Affordable Housing funding, a local 
jurisdiction must have committed to increasing its affordable housing stock by an 
average of three percent (3%) per year from 2023 through 2026, a commitment 
previously made by the Edgewater City Council (“Council”) through the adoption of 
Resolution No. 23-11; and 
WHEREAS, an additional condition of funding eligibility is to have a plan in place 
to expedite the local review process for development projects in which at least fifty 
percent (50%) of the residential units are affordable, as defined by C.R.S. §§ 29-32-101 
and 29-32-105(2); and 
WHEREAS, the expedited review process must allow eligible projects to be 
reviewed and decided within ninety (90) days of receipt of a complete application, a 
timeline that the Edgewater Municipal Code (“Code”) currently allows; and 
WHEREAS, nonetheless, the Council wishes to formally and expressly direct 
Staff to expedite the review and processing of qualifying projects pursuant to a formally-
adopted policy governing the same, to reflect the Council’s continued and deliberate 
support of the creation of affordable and attainable housing in the City.

Colorado Proposition 123 Compliance Policy 
 
Section 1 — Purpose and Intent 
The purpose of this policy is to establish an Expedited Review Program for Affordable Housing 
Projects in compliance with Colorado Proposition 123 (C.R.S. 29-32-105(2)) and the Colorado 
Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) Fast Track Guidance. 
This policy is intended to: 
• Maintain eligibility for Proposition 123 funding  
• Support housing affordability goals in the Edgewater 2040 Comprehensive Plan  
• Provide predictable and efficient development review timelines  
• Maintain high-quality design and compatibility standards  
• Ensure compliance with Edgewater Municipal Code Chapter 16 Zoning  
This policy applies to qualifying Affordable Housing development applications submitted to the 
City of Edgewater. 
 
Section 2 — Definitions 
2.1 Affordable Housing 
For purposes of this policy, Affordable Housing shall be defined consistent with Proposition 
123: 
Affordable Housing means:  A housing development in which 50 percent or more of the 
dwelling units are: 
Rental Housing 
• Affordable to households earning 60% Area Median Income (AMI) or less  
Page 6 of 16
• Housing costs not exceeding 30% of monthly household income  
For-Sale Housing 
• Affordable to households earning 100% AMI or less  
• Mortgage costs not exceeding 30% of monthly household income  
*these quantified qualifiers and definitions may change from time to time per C.R.S. § 29-32-
104 
 
2.2 Area Median Income (AMI) 
Area Median Income (AMI) shall be defined using HUD income limits for the Denver-Aurora-
Lakewood Metropolitan Area and updated annually. 
 
2.3 Expedited Review 
Expedited Review means the City will issue a Final Decision within 90 calendar days following 
determination of a Complete Application. 
 
2.4 Complete Application 
A Complete Application shall include: 
• Required application materials as outlined Chapter 16 of the City’s code or other 
established city policy  
• Required studies by city designees or referral agencies 
• Required plans by city designees or referral agencies 
• Required fees – established by city resolution  
• Required documentation by city designees or referral agencies 
The 90-day timeline begins only after the application is deemed complete by the City.

RE:   Technical Focus Group Update – May 7, 2026 Meeting Summary 
The purpose of this memorandum is to provide the Planning and Zoning Commission with a 
summary of the first meeting of the Zoning Code Update Technical Focus Group (TFG), 
held on May 7, 2026. The meeting focused on discussion of key themes emerging from the 
Diagnostic Report, Intensification Guide, and draft zoning framework concepts. As the 
Commission has previously reviewed the Diagnostic Report and related materials, this 
memorandum primarily summarizes the feedback, concerns, and questions raised by 
participants during the discussion.  
The meeting included invited representatives from the Planning and Zoning Commission, 
City Council, Sustainability Board (absent), Historic, Arts, Recreation and Parks Board, 
Edgewater Renewal Authority, and two members of the development community. Staff and 
consultant representatives facilitated discussion regarding neighborhood character, 
housing diversity, intensification strategies, code usability, and implementation 
challenges associated with state legislation and the Edgewater 2040 Comprehensive Plan. 
Key Themes and Feedback 
A substantial portion of the discussion focused on balancing neighborhood character 
preservation with the need to accommodate additional housing and comply with state 
legislation. Participants generally acknowledged that Edgewater’s historic development 
pattern—characterized by smaller homes on relatively large lots—forms a significant 
component of the community’s identity and “village in the city” character. There was broad 
recognition that recent redevelopment trends toward substantially larger homes have 
altered neighborhood scale without meaningfully increasing housing diversity or 
affordability. 
Several participants expressed support for the emerging approach of encouraging 
smaller-scale housing forms, including ADUs, cottage clusters, and other “missing 
middle” housing prototypes, as a method of accommodating additional housing while 
maintaining neighborhood scale. Participants noted that promoting smaller units may align 
better with current market realities and affordability needs than continuing patterns of 
large single-house redevelopment. 
Discussion also emphasized the importance of maintaining ownership opportunities 
within future housing models. Participants highlighted that long-term community stability 
and wealth-building opportunities are tied not only to rental housing availability, but also to 
attainable ownership products. The group discussed how cottage cluster and small-lot 
Page 14 of 16
ownership models may provide alternatives to traditional single-family housing while 
preserving community character. 
The group also discussed the challenges associated with preservation incentives and 
redevelopment pressures. While participants generally supported incentivizing 
preservation of older structures where feasible, there was acknowledgment that many 
existing homes were not originally constructed for long-term durability and that 
replacement of some structures will inevitably occur over time. The discussion 
emphasized the need for zoning regulations that encourage context-sensitive 
redevelopment rather than attempting to fully prohibit change. 
Questions and Discussion Topics Raised 
Participants raised several technical and policy questions throughout the meeting, 
including: 
• How revised zoning regulations would address existing lot size irregularities and 
common 25-foot and 50-foot lot configurations; 
• The feasibility and ownership structure of cottage cluster developments and 
whether units could be individually owned; 
• Infrastructure considerations associated with increasing ADU intensity, including 
sewer capacity and service requirements; 
• Concerns regarding luxury mixed-use development and whether future zoning 
incentives would effectively support attainable housing and small businesses; 
• Potential height limitations within future mixed-use zoning districts; and 
• Whether increased reliance on administrative approvals for certain project types 
may face resistance from the community. 
Participants also discussed how parking requirements, financing structures, and 
construction defect laws continue to create barriers for smaller-scale and ownership-
oriented housing products. Several attendees noted that reducing parking requirements 
and creating more predictable administrative review pathways may improve feasibility for 
smaller projects and local developers. 
Process and Code Administration 
The group generally agreed that the City’s existing development review process 
functions relatively efficiently compared to neighboring communities, but participants 
supported continued efforts to streamline regulations and improve predictability. 
Particular emphasis was placed on improving code usability by consolidating regulations, 
incorporating more graphics and visual examples, and reducing situations where 
applicants must navigate multiple code sections to understand basic development 
standards. 
Page 15 of 16
There was also discussion regarding the importance of community trust in 
administrative approvals and objective standards. Participants noted that clearer and 
more predictable regulations may help reduce unnecessary public hearing burdens while 
still maintaining alignment with community goals and adopted policy direction. 
Next Steps 
The next Technical Focus Group meeting is scheduled for June 8, 2026. That meeting is 
anticipated to focus on review and discussion of draft low density residential zoning 
districts and their associated development standards, including proposed dimensional 
standards, housing configurations, lot coverage, height, setbacks, and ADU provisions. 
Staff will continue to provide summary updates to the Planning and Zoning Commission 
following each Technical Focus Group meeting as the code update process continues to 
be reviewed.
