I
INTRODUCTION
The purpose of this report is to summarize key findings of the 
diagnostic phase of Edgewater’s Zoning and Land Use Code Update. 
This work was led by the consultant team in coordination with City staff 
and is intended to identify strengths, gaps, and areas of misalignment 
within the current zoning framework and newly adopted city policy.
The diagnostic aims to ground the code update in local conditions 
to guide future development that complements Edgewater’s unique 
character and charm, supports a high quality of life for residents, and 
aligns with long-term growth goals. These include expanded housing 
options, revitalized mixed-use corridors, and development patterns 
that reinforce the community’s vision as articulated in the Edgewater 
2024 Comprehensive Plan.
The findings presented here will inform the structure, content, and 
priorities of the upcoming code updates.

I. INTRODUCTION
The Zoning and Land Use Code Update was initiated in spring 2025 by the City of Edgewater, in 
coordination with a consultant team led by Studio Onaran of Pel-Ona Architects and Urbanists, 
with support from Peter J. Park Planning and Design and Metta Urban Design. The project launched 
shortly after the adoption of several state land use and zoning laws, including HB24-1152 and HB24-
1313, which prompted the City to implement short-term code amendments focused on compliance 
with new state law. These initial changes were adopted by City Council in June 2025.
WHY NOW?
Although Edgewater has historically seen less development pressure than many neighboring Front 
Range communities, the City has seen steady growth in recent decades. Recent changes in state 
law now require zoning to allow higher residential densities along transit corridors and accessory 
dwelling units (ADUs) in single-unit residential zones. Combined with Edgewater’s proximity to 
regional destinations, these changes are expected to increase development interest in the near 
future.
To proactively guide anticipated growth, the City adopted the Edgewater 2040 Comprehensive 
Plan in September 2024, establishing a clear vision for how and where growth should occur to 
maximize community benefits while minimizing adverse impacts. However, Chapter 16 (Zoning) 
of the Municipal Code has not been updated to align with the new Comprehensive Plan. Although 
incremental amendments have occurred, a comprehensive update is now necessary to fully 
support the City’s adopted growth strategy and meet recent state mandates. Therefore, the “Zoning 
and Land Use Codes Update” is both timely and essential.

Almost everyone we talked to expressed that the review processes 
have been running smoothly and relatively fast, especially 
compared to some of the neighboring municipalities. However, 
this doesn’t mean that some improvements cannot be made. 
Currently any housing with three or more units goes through 
Planning and Zoning Commission and Council. Changing this 
may speed the process and reduce the load of the Planning and 
Zoning Commission and Council. Maybe the medium density 
developments should be reviewed administratively. Speed is 
affordability. Also, code users and staff agree that administrative 
adjustment needs to be more streamlined. 
Both the applicants and staff, who use the code frequently, express 
that it is well-organized and easy to navigate. However, when we 
have asked if visuals could help, the common answer was yes, that 
this code currently is not an inspiring document and that visuals 
can help to set clear expectations.  We also heard that some 
definitions are placed in locations hard to find and adding a “Rules

I. INTRODUCTION
The Edgewater 2040 Comprehensive Plan (Edgewater 2040) outlines a 
strategic vision for the city’s future focusing on maintaining Edgewater’s 
vibrant community. The plan emphasizes enhancing and supporting diverse, 
equitable, and authentic residential neighborhoods. This includes providing 
a spectrum of housing opportunities to meet identified gaps, incentivizing 
accessible design methodologies, updating zoning for Accessory Dwelling 
Units (ADUs) and small-scale multi-family housing, and creating programs 
to mitigate eviction and support affordable housing.
The Code Update and Diagnostic Report is informed by Edgewater 
2040 Comprehensive Plan. The plan describes  ‘Places’ as 
Neighborhoods & Housing, Mixed Use & Commercial Corridors, and 
Community Character and Design. The findings of this report are 
organize in these categories for continuity with the plan.  One finding 
to note is throughout the planning process, community members 
expressed a need for additional and diverse housing, increased 
density opportunities, and intentional commercial development 
that is reflective of the character of Edgewater. The 2040 Edgewater 
Framework map shows the community’s intensions for growth 
throughout the City and informs the report’s findings, recognizing the 
Commercial, Main Street, and Residential corridors. 
Additionally, this report references the plan’s Goals and Strategies 
to bolster the analysis and to identify areas where the code requires 
updating to align with the plan recommendations.

Observations & Findings
One of the identifying characteristics observed throughout the 
Edgewater community character was small, cottage-scaled, 
traditional historic architecture. These characteristics embody 
a “village in the city” feel, mixed with some larger contemporary 
buildings. Even though Edgewater’s character is eclectic with larger, new 
modern houses, they are limited in number. The small historic cottages 
are the ones that create the cozy scale on most block faces. 
The eclectic character is appealing for the time being.  If development 
continues to be in the form of larger buildings, the character of a 
neighborhood will change. As part of the code update, specific attention 
will be given to preserving and encouraging the cottage scale “village in 
the city” feel.
This, however, doesn’t mean that new development should be limited. 
It means that the new development should include the small and 
“cottage-like” building types as well.

In Articles 5 and 6 Chapter 16: Zoning of the Municipal Code sets the 
minimum front yard setback as 25 feet for structures within R-1 and R-2 
zone districts. In the same chapters the code also sets the minimum lot 
width as 60 feet for detached houses, 75 feet for street corner lots (again 
for lots within R-1 and R-2 zone districts).  
The maximum height permitted within these districts is 25 feet for 
principal building, with up to 28 feet encroachment permitted for 
pitched roofs (for roofs 2/12 or steeper). The maximum height permitted 
for accessory buildings is 15 feet. 
The definition of the term “lot coverage” in Section 16-1-50 reads:
Lot coverage is determined by dividing that area of a lot which is occupied 
or covered by the total horizontal projected surface of all buildings, 
including covered porches and accessory buildings, all covered decks, 
patios, parking areas and drives, or covered portions thereof, by the gross 
area of that lot.
But then, in Articles 5 and 6, where maximum lot coverages for R-1 and 
R-2 are set as 35%, it states:
Notwithstanding the definition of lot coverage under Section 16-1-50, the 
following areas are excluded from the calculation of lot coverage in this 
district: (1) that part of one-story unenclosed porches that extends into 
the required front yard, as authorized by Section 16-3-90; and (2) all other 
portions of one-story unenclosed porches and accessory structures that do 
not exceed, in the aggregate, one hundred and twenty (120) square feet.

and Zoning Commission and Council.

adjustment needs to be more streamlined.

III
CONCLUSION
In light of what we have heard, observed, and read, we conclude the 
diagnostic phase with the following list of task recommendations:  
1. Craft new mixed-use zones with diverse form types to address the four 
corridor contexts identified by Edgewater 2020; all zones permitting 
residential uses. 
2. These regulations need to be crafted to encourage a diversity of 
businesses including the small ones. Parking requirements should 
support inclusion of small businesses.
3. In order to preserve community character and housing diversity the 
regulations should enable and encourage the small dwelling units and 
limit the larges ones.
4. Craft innovative regulations enabling inclusion of ADUs addressing the 
three lot / building types identified in Section 2 of this report.
5. Encourage preservation of current structures, especially historic cottages, 
by means of dimensional and floor area bonuses. 
6. Craft regulations enabling “missing middle” prototypes such as cottage 
clusters, common courts, and mixed building type courts. 
7. Reduce non-conformities and encourage developments that preserve 
the existing community character. Revisit lot size, setback, height, and lot 
coverage restrictions especially on lots currently zoned as R-1 and R-2. 
8. Produce standards for medium density development to provide more 
certainty reduce the load for Planning and Zoning Commission and City 
Council reviews. 
9. Introduce visual material and explanation of the intent, where needed, 
especially to clarify rules of measurement. 
10.  Revisit and reorganize some of the content in the Articles 3, 16, and 22 to 
create a more user-friendly code.
