ITEM 4.4 — PRESENTATION: TECHNOLOGY INFRASTRUCTURE (Discussion Begins 
1:44:00) 
Commissioner Ramirez recognized Kevin Bibbs, Executive Director of Technology Services, for 
presentation and remarks. 
Key points: 
• Department Scope: The Technology Department covers all aspects of information 
technology including security, infrastructure (switches, firewalls), enterprise software 
(Microsoft applications), telephony and phone systems covering all UG government 
buildings, backup services, and meeting support. 
• Current Scale of Operations: The department supports 1,600 devices, 1,400 users, over 100 
applications, 40 departments, and all UG facilities including community centers and 
underlying infrastructure. The department operates with a total of 22 staff members, four (4) 
of whom are responsible for enterprise desktop support. 
• Staffing Concerns: Bibbs noted a significant staffing ratio disparity. The average IT support 
ratio industry standard is between 1:80 and 1:125 individuals. The UG Technology 
Department is currently operating at a ratio of approximately 1:350, placing significant strain 
on staff. Hours of support range from 6:30 AM to sometimes midnight. Bibbs highlighted 
that team members frequently work outside their assigned roles to ensure operations are 
maintained and that the team functions as a collaborative unit. 
• Accomplishments: Bibbs presented a comprehensive listing of completed service requests 
and projects over the last 12 months across all supported departments. He emphasized the 
importance of the ticketing system and asked all UG staff with device issues to submit tickets 
to ensure work is addressed in the order received. 
• Upcoming Projects: 
A. Purchase and installation of an Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) for the data 
center to ensure network switches remain operational — one UPS has already been 
purchased. 
Page | 4 
B. Replacement of core switches — already purchased through departmental cost 
savings; scheduling is pending and has been deliberately timed to avoid the World 
Cup period to minimize downtime. The department is coordinating with 24/7 teams to 
schedule installation. 
C. Expansion of wireless access points throughout all UG buildings to increase capacity 
for growing device needs. 
D. Advancement toward a zero-trust network security model. 
• Budget Overview: Bibbs noted that the Technology Department budget can be complex. He 
explained that early in his tenure, he established a clear separation between the cost of 
running the Technology Department itself versus enterprise system expenditures (such as 
Microsoft agreements, Workday, and Esri platforms). 
• Areas of Opportunity (Challenges): 
A. Staffing shortages at the 1:350 ratio cause strain and slower response times. 
B. Legacy systems and outdated hardware remain in use because they hold critical data. 
C. Staffing constraints limit the pace of cloud adoption and modernization efforts. 
• Vision for Technology: 
A. Achieve full staffing capacity. 
B. Transition to 100% cloud-based application architecture. 
C. Ensure government can operate 24/7. 
D. Collaborate with procurement to incorporate technology requirements earlier in the 
RFP process — Bibbs acknowledged and thanked the procurement group for this 
collaboration. 
E. Sunset legacy applications in coordination with the Clerk's Office — Bibbs 
specifically thanked UG Clerk Monica L. Sparks and her staff for their assistance. 
F. Improve overall security posture and ensure correct software license allocation. 
G. Provide comprehensive staff training resources across Microsoft applications, 
cybersecurity, and enterprise systems.

Commissioner Ramirez recognized Crystal Sprague, Director of the Performance and Innovation 
Division (PNI), for presentation and remarks. 
Key points included: 
• Department Structure: PNI sits within the reporting structure of the Chief Information 
Officer (CIO Kevin Bibbs) and is composed of three divisions: 311 (previously presented),

• Project Portfolio: The project management team manages a vast portfolio including 
payment processing, fiber and camera networks, and enterprise platforms. Staff includes 
specialists from multiple disciplines, three certified Project Management Professionals 
(PMPs), a certified Program Management Professional (a credential rare in the United 
States), and staff certified in Scrum, Six Sigma, and Behavior Modification. 
• Role of Project Management: Sprague emphasized that PNI goes beyond traditional project 
management, managing programs and projects through a full life cycle — from exploration 
and requirements gathering through the RFP and purchasing process, all the way through to 
post-deployment enterprise system management. Sprague noted that involving project 
managers early — even at requirements gathering and vendor selection — significantly 
improves project outcomes.

• Governance Structure: PNI manages several governance and change management boards. 
Seven change control/change management boards approve, reject, or defer proposed changes 
to systems and processes. 
• Workday Case Study: Sprague used the Workday deployment as an example of successful 
enterprise system implementation. Nineteen (19) modules were deployed between fall 2022 
and winter 2023, fully digitizing the employee lifecycle and fiscal activities into one system 
of record. One specific example provided was the change-of-address process, which prior to 
Workday required supervisor and HR involvement, affidavits, manual handoffs, and manual 
updates to nine external systems of record plus a paper file. Post-Workday, the process takes 
three steps, is completed in minutes, and was processed 531 times in 2025 with no manager 
involvement required. Workday has 87 unique integrations that have fired over the last 18 
months. 
• Integration Expectations: Sprague emphasized that nearly every modern system requires 
integration with other systems. Integrations require careful management not just at 
deployment but in the years after. She noted that integration expectations must be defined 
before a platform is purchased, including who will own the integration post-deployment. 
• Standardization Example: Three departments were found to be using three different credit 
card machines, resulting in three different customer experiences and three separate 
reconciliations. Upon full review, five separate payment systems were discovered. 
Standardizing these systems reduces errors, simplifies training, and standardizes the customer 
experience. 
• Platform Investment Strategy: Sprague emphasized the importance of "buying today with 
tomorrow in mind," meaning that procurement of platforms must account not just for the 
purchase price but for post-deployment support, integration management, licensing, staff 
training, and long-term optimization.

• Outdated software and hardware operating beyond its life cycle

paywalls or terms-of-service changes

• No ability to troubleshoot effectively due to outdated system architecture 
• Upcoming ADA compliance requirements as of April 2027: Failure to comply could result in 
fines of up to $75,000 for the first violation and $150,000 for subsequent violations

ITEM 4.1 — RESOLUTION: HOUSING AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT (HUD) LEAD 
HAZARD CONTROL AND HEALTHY HOMES (LHC-2600-DC-0013) GRANT (Discussion 
Begins 02:37:15) 
Commissioner Ramirez recognized Terrie Garrison, Interim Director of Public Health, Jennifer Stewart, 
Environmental Health Manager, and Anthony Lee Jr., Lead Hazard Control Program Supervisor, No 
Lead KCK Program, for presentation and remarks. 
Terrie Garrison introduced the item and explained that the Wyandotte County Public Health Department 
was requesting approval to apply for and accept a continuation of the No Lead KCK grant through HUD 
(Housing and Urban Development). A site visit from HUD was being conducted on the day of and the 
day following the meeting. 
 
Jennifer Stewart, Manager, Division of Air Quality & Environmental Services presented the 
public health context: 
• Lead exposure causes serious health problems, particularly for developing fetuses and children 
under the age of six. 
• There is no safe level of lead exposure. 
Page | 10 
• HUD requires that eligible homes have children under the age of six in the home or a pregnant 
woman, in addition to meeting low-income criteria. 
• The grant focus is on homes built before 1978, when lead paint was still heavily used. 
• Lead is commonly found in paint on windows and friction surfaces (doors), and in exterior soil. 
• Effects of lead exposure include impaired growth, hearing loss, behavioral problems, reduced IQ, 
and in severe cases seizures, coma, or death. 
 
Anthony Lee Jr., Lead Hazard Control Program Supervisor, presented data specific to Wyandotte 
County: 
• Homes built before 1950 in Wyandotte County represent 22% of the housing stock — one of the 
highest rates in the Kansas City metro area. 
• Homes built between 1950 and 1978 represent 27.3% of the housing stock — also nearly the 
highest in the metro. 
• Wyandotte County has the highest rate of homes with peeling paint on interior or exterior 
surfaces in the Kansas City metro area, indicating a significant need for this program. 
 
Terrie Garrison, Interim Health Director, provided the grant details: 
• The current HUD lead abatement grant was received following a December 2022 application. 
• The current grant ends December 1, 2026. 
• HUD issued a Notice of Funding Award on July 20, 2026, with an application deadline of 
August 31, 2026. 
• The department is requesting approval to apply for a four-year continuation grant for $5,000,000, 
with a 10% grant match. 
• The match will be funded through existing personnel salaries (Jennifer Stewart, Anthony Lee Jr.) 
from the Health Department's Health Levy budget, and through collaboration with the UG 
Community Development CDBG grant, including a staff member's salary for environmental 
reviews and roofing program costs at eligible homes. 
 
Program Details provided by Anthony Lee Jr., Lead Hazard Control Program Supervisor: 
• The program is available to both homeowners and landlords in Kansas City, Kansas. 
• Homeowners must have a child under age six in the home (or a grandchild visiting six or more 
hours per week) and meet income guidelines. 
Page | 11 
• Landlords do not need children present but must give priority to families with children under age 
six for at least three years post-work. 
• The application process involves income verification, documentation of children in the home, 
and blood lead screening. 
• Processing takes one to two days. A risk assessment (surface-by-surface inspection, dust wipe 
samples, and soil sampling) is then conducted. 
• The program provides full lead abatement at no cost to homeowners or landlords, including 
interim controls (mulch barriers) or full abatement (soil tilling and sod replacement) for exterior 
soil contamination. 
• The No Lead KCK program is the only lead abatement program of its kind in the state of Kansas. 
• Applications are available online at ykck.org/lead, by phone, by mail, and in person. Program 
staff also conduct outreach at community events. 
 
Current Grant Results: 
• 53 properties assessed for lead 
• 45 properties found to have lead 
• 30 properties completed 
• 26 additional properties currently in the queue

ITEM 4.3 ORDINANCE/RESOLUTION: SALES TAX INITIATIVE (Discussion Begins 
03:13:45) 
Commissioner Ramirez recognized Alan Howze, Interim County Administrator, and Wendy Green, 
Deputy Chief Counsel, for presentation and remarks. 
The gap between identified community needs and available resources, consistent with concerns 
identified during the budget process. He presented potential sales tax options as tools that other 
communities have used to target specific needs with dedicated funding mechanisms. 
 
Kansas Sales Tax Primer presented by Alan Howze and Wendy Green: 
• The State of Kansas caps total sales tax (state and local, excluding CIDs and TDDs) at 11.5%. 
• The State charges a base sales tax rate of 6.5%. 
Page | 14 
• The current general county sales tax is 1%, of which 25% is retained by the county and 75% is 
distributed to cities. 
• Under Kansas State law, counties may levy special purpose sales taxes for two purposes only: (1) 
health and health activities, or (2) jail or law enforcement center facility construction or 
remodeling. County special purpose sales taxes must be in increments of 0.25%, up to 1%. 
• The current city general sales tax is 1%. 
• The current city special purpose sales tax is 0.625%, comprised of: 
• 0.025% EMS special purpose sales tax (enacted 2004, no sunset provision) 
• 0.375% public safety and neighborhood infrastructure sales tax (renewable every 10 
years; not up for renewal until June 2030) 
• The remaining city special purpose sales tax capacity is 0.375%, available in increments of 
0.05% up to 1%. 
• Special purpose city sales taxes do not go toward Star Bond repayments; only general sales taxes 
are included in Star Bond increment calculations. 
Revenue Estimates: 
• County side: Each 0.25% generates approximately $12 million annually. 
• City side: Each one-eighth cent (0.125%) generates approximately $5.3 million annually. 
• The total available 0.375% on the city side generates approximately $16 million annually. 
Resident Survey Results:

Commissioner Kump provided background, noting that on July 13, 2026, the Administration and Human

back by the Board of Commissioners on August 6, 2026, for further review. 
 
Page | 4 
Presentation by Dr. Shelley Kneuvean, CFO: 
Dr. Shelley Kneuvean explained the history and context of the proposed policy amendment, noting that 
additional materials had been distributed to committee members late the prior week due to timing 
constraints. 
Key points: 
Background and Process: 
• The UG has a current grant policy in place, approved by the Board of Commissioners. 
• The Mayor suggested changes to the existing policy, which were reviewed by the AHS 
Committee and then sent to the full Commission for consideration. 
• The full Commission discussed the policy further and sent it back to the AHS Committee for 
additional review. 
• Staff conducted additional research and compiled a summary table outlining the grant approval 
practices of other Kansas communities. 
Research Findings: 
• The research found that Board of Commissioner approval is required in nearly every case among 
surveyed Kansas communities. 
• Threshold dollar amounts are the primary differentiators between jurisdictions. 
• Most surveyed communities require board approval before applying and accepting grants, which 
is consistent with the UG's current practice. 
Proposed Policy Changes (Modeled After Johnson County, Kansas): 
Dr. Kneuvean outlined the staff's recommended revisions, which were modeled after Johnson County, 
Kansas's, grant policy: 
1. Administrative Approval to Apply and Accept (Up to $150,000):  The proposal allows 
administrative approval for grants up to $150,000, provided: 
• There is no match requirement, or the match is less than 10% (i.e., less than $15,000); 
and 
• The grant does not require a budget amendment (meaning the funds are within the 
existing department's budget). 
• The Mayor's original proposal had recommended a $100,000 threshold; staff 
recommended $150,000 to align with Johnson County's policy. 
2. Commission Approval Required:  Commission approval would be required to apply and accept 
if: 
• The grant is over $150,000; 
• The match is over $15,000; or 
• The grant includes an addition of personnel. 
Page | 5 
3. Fast Track Provision:  Language was included to allow grants to be fast-tracked when a grant 
opportunity is identified but cannot be placed on the agenda in time through the normal process.  
This would not be the preferred option but was necessary to avoid losing grant opportunities due 
to scheduling constraints. 
4. Retroactive Approval:  Language was included to allow the County Administrator to approve 
grant applications on an emergency basis (e.g., grants with a two-week response time), with the 
requirement to bring the application before the committee and Commission at the next available 
meetings for retroactive approval.  If the Commission were to decline to approve the grant at that 
point, the application could be withdrawn, and no commitment would be made. 
5. Community Partner Selection (Procurement Process):  New language was added regarding 
the selection of community partners in grant applications.  Going forward, a notice of interest 
would be issued through the procurement process so that all nonprofit partners have an 
opportunity to submit interest in partnering with the UG.  Sole source justifications would be 
allowed — consistent with the existing purchasing policy — but would require approval by the 
Purchasing Director and the County Administrator. 
6. Monthly Grant Report:  The policy codifies the existing practice of providing a monthly report 
to the committee detailing what grants have been applied for, the status of applications, and 
awards received.  This report would also be posted publicly on the UG's website under 
Finance/Grants as a transparency measure.

Summary of the draft proposal: 
The Commission has decided to place a countywide health sales tax on the November ballot.  In 
response to community questions about how those funds would be used and how citizens could have a 
say, Commissioner Davis developed this draft proposal.  Commissioner Davis noted that the UG's legal 
department had not yet had sufficient time to fully review the document and that changes and edits 
would be forthcoming.  Commissioner Davis encouraged committee members and the public to provide 
feedback to Wendy Green in the Legal Department to ensure legal compliance.  Commissioner Davis 
also invited Commissioner Ramirez and all committee members to share additional recommendations at 
the next meeting on October 12. 
• Whereas Clauses (Preamble):  Restated the existence of the health tax initiative and the intent to 
establish a citizen oversight board to advise on how resulting funds are spent.  Referenced 
Wyandotte County's health rankings as measured by the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, 
conducted through the University of Kansas, which consistently places the county toward the 
bottom in health and social determinants.  Referenced oversight board models from Alameda 
County and Santa Clara County in California as the basis for this proposal. 
• Section 1 — Establishment of the Board: Establishes the Citizen Oversight Board, which is 
contingent upon the health sales tax passing.  The board would be similar in structure to the 
Community Benefits Advisory Board.  Each commissioner would make one appointment, and 
the Mayor would appoint the Chair of the Board, who would be responsible for presiding over 
board meetings.  All appointed members must be residents and must maintain residency within 
their respective commissioner's district. 
• Sections 2–3 — Board Composition:  Covered additional details related to board membership 
consistent with section one's description. 
• Sections 4–5 — Terms of Office and Meetings: 
A. Appointments to the board would be coterminous with the appointing elected official's 
term of office. 
B. The board would be required to meet no fewer than three times per calendar year. 
• Section 6 — Eligible Uses of Funds:  Outlined the types of programs and services eligible for 
funding.  Commissioner Davis noted that Legal would need to review this section to align it with 
the language of the health tax.  Eligible uses contemplated in the draft include: tiny home 
villages, mobile health clinics, shelter facilities, nursing homes, and other health and housing-
related services.  Health disparities in the county intersect with issues such as housing instability, 
and the intent of the fund is to address these overlapping needs. 
• Section 7 — Duties of the Board:  Requires the board to meet regularly, review expenditures of 
the funds, gather public feedback, and submit an annual report to the Board of Commissioners 
detailing how funds have been spent. 
Page | 7 
• Section 8 — Reporting Metrics:  Includes specific metrics for tracking the impact of the fund, 
including: 
A. Wyandotte County Jail population — to assess whether health interventions reduce 
incarceration rates given the prevalence of health conditions among incarcerated 
individuals, and the associated cost of approximately $100 per day per incarcerated 
person. 
B. EMS calls — to track whether improved access to healthcare reduces reliance on 911 
emergency services, which is a significant expense. 
C. Income and job placement metrics, among others, reflecting the understanding that health 
encompasses the overall quality of life. 
• Sections 9–10 — Jail Population Data and Annual Report:  Section 9 addresses coordination 
with the Sheriff's Department to obtain jail population data, noting the commitment cannot be 
guaranteed but that the request would be made.  Section 10 establishes that an annual report 
would be submitted to the Board of Commissioners by March 31 of each year, though that 
timeline may need to be adjusted. 
• Section 11 — Healthcare Sales Tax Fund:  States that funds collected from the health sales tax 
would be maintained in a separate fund and would not be commingled with other funds, 
consistent with state law. 
• Section 12 — Bylaws:  Requires the Board to adopt its own bylaws, in compliance with the 
Kansas Open Meetings Act and the Kansas Open Records Act (KORA). 
• Section 13 — Board of Commissioners' Responsibilities:  Addresses the formal steps the Board 
of Commissioners would need to take to codify the sales tax upon its passage. 
• Sections 14–15 — Additional Provisions:  Commissioner Davis noted that a severability clause 
may not be necessary and could potentially be removed.  Section 15 addresses the need for staff 
support to be available for the board to operate effectively. 
 
Commissioner Davis emphasized that this proposal, if the tax passes, would give the community the 
ability to have a say in how approximately $10 million in health tax revenues are spent and to track the 
effectiveness of those expenditures.  All provisions are contingent on the health tax passing in 
November and that the document remains a draft subject to further legal review and committee input.

ORDINANCE NO.      
 
AN ORDINANCE ADDING ARTICLE XV, SOLICITORS, CANVASSERS, AND 
PEDDLERS TO CHAPTER 19, LICENSES, PERMITS, AND MISCELLANEOUS 
BUSINESS REGULATION TO THE CODE OF ORDINANCES FOR THE UNIFIED 
GOVERNMENT OF WYANDOTTE COUNTY/KANSAS CITY, KANSAS. 
WHEREAS, the Unified Government of Wyandotte County/Kansas City, Kansas is 
authorized pursuant to Article 12, Section 5 of the Kansas Constitution and applicable Kansas 
law to exercise home rule authority and adopt ordinances relating to matters of local concern; 
 
WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners finds that reasonable regulation of 
solicitors, peddlers, and canvassers is necessary to protect the safety, privacy, and security of 
residents and property owners;  
 
WHEREAS, severe weather events and other emergencies may result in increased door-
to-door solicitation for services relating to storm damage, debris removal, tree removal, roofing, 
construction, and other property repairs;  
 
WHEREAS, the Unified Government desires to establish clear and reasonable 
requirements for people engaging in such solicitation to protect residents from potentially 
confusing, intrusive, or unwanted solicitation during periods when residents may be particularly 
vulnerable following severe weather events; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Unified Government desires to establish a clear and consistent process 
for the registration, regulation, and enforcement of people engaged in solicitation within the 
Unified Government. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE BOARD OF 
COMMISSIONERS OF THE UNIFIED GOVERNMENT OF WYANDOTTE 
COUNTY/KANSAS CITY, KANSAS:  
 
Section 1. That Article XV, Solicitors, Canvassers, and Peddlers, is added to Chapter 19, 
Licenses, Permits, and Miscellaneous Business Regulation, of the Code of Ordinances for the 
Unified Government of Wyandotte County/Kansas City, Kansas to read as follows: 
19-440. Definitions. 
For purposes of this Article, the following words shall be considered to have the 
following meanings: 
(a) Soliciting shall mean engaging in any of the following activities for the purpose of 
obtaining a sale, order, subscription, appointment, or other commercial transaction: 
(1) Seeking to obtain orders for the purchase of goods, wares, merchandise, 
foodstuffs, or services of any kind or character for consideration; 
(2) Seeking to obtain prospective customers for the purchase of goods or services; 
or 
(3) Seeking subscriptions for books, magazines, periodicals, newspapers, or other 
publications when such activity is conducted for a commercial purpose. 
(b) Residence shall mean every separate living unit occupied for residential purposes by 
one or more persons, contained within any type of building or structure. 
(c) Solicitor or canvasser shall mean any person who travels from place to place, from 
house to house, or from street to street for the purpose of soliciting, as defined herein, whether 
such person is acting for himself or herself or as an employee, agent, or representative of another 
person or business. 
(d) Peddler shall mean any person who travels from place to place, from house to house, 
or from street to street carrying, conveying, or transporting goods, wares, merchandise, food, or 
other tangible property and offering the same for sale or making sales and deliveries to 
purchasers. 
(e)  License shall mean a license issued by the Unified Government authorizing a 
solicitor, canvasser, or peddler to engage in the activities regulated by this Article. 
(f) Person shall mean any individual, firm, partnership, corporation, association, 
organization, or other legal entity. 
Sec. 19-441 License Required. 
(a) It shall be unlawful for any person to engage in the business of a solicitor, canvasser, 
or peddler within the corporate limits of the Unified Government without first obtaining and 
possessing a valid, unexpired license issued pursuant to this Article. 
(b) A person engaged in soliciting, canvassing, or peddling shall have the license readily 
available and shall exhibit the license upon request of any person to whom the person is 
soliciting, canvassing, or offering goods or services. 
(c) A license issued under this Article shall be valid only for the person to whom it is 
issued and shall not be transferable. 
Sec. 19-442. Application for License. 
An applicant for a license under this Article shall submit an application to the Unified 
Government on a form prescribed by the Unified Government. 
(a) The application shall include, at a minimum: 
(1) The applicant’s full name and permanent address; 
(2) The applicant’s local or temporary address, if applicable; 
(3) A valid form of government-issued identification; 
(4) The name, address, and telephone number of the applicant’s employer or 
business, if applicable; 
(5) A brief description of the nature of the business, goods, or services to be 
solicited, canvassed, or sold; 
(6) The proposed dates during which the applicant will engage in soliciting, 
canvassing, or peddling within the Unified Government; 
(7) A description of any vehicle to be used in connection with the activity, 
including the make, model, and license plate number; and 
(8) The location where services are to be performed or where the goods or 
property proposed to be sold or orders taken for the sale thereof are 
manufactured or produced, where such goods or products are located at the 
time the application is filed, and the proposed method of delivery. 
(b) The applicant shall certify that the information provided on the application is true and 
correct. 
Sec. 19-443. Issuance of License. 
(a) Upon receipt of a completed application and any applicable fee, the Unified 
Government shall review the application for compliance with this Article and shall issue a 
license to the applicant if the application satisfies the requirements of this Article of Code of 
Ordinances for the Unified Government. 
Sec. 19-444. Hours of Solicitation. 
(a) No person shall engage in commercial soliciting, canvassing, or peddling at a 
residence within the Unified Government except between the hours of 9:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. 
unless the person has been expressly invited by the occupant of the residence to solicit, canvass, 
or conduct business 
Sec. 19-445. No Soliciting Signs and Requests to Leave. 
(a) No solicitor, canvasser, or peddler shall enter or remain upon private property for the 
purpose of soliciting, canvassing, or peddling when the property displays a clearly visible sign or 
notice stating “No Soliciting,” “No Peddlers,” “No Canvassing,” or words of similar meaning. 
(b) A solicitor, canvasser, or peddler who is personally informed by the owner, occupant, 
or person in control of private property that soliciting, canvassing, or peddling is not permitted 
on the property shall immediately leave the property and shall not return for the purpose of 
soliciting, canvassing, or peddling without the express invitation of the owner or occupant. 
(c) A person who enters upon private property in violation of subsection (a) or who 
refuses to leave after being directed to do so in accordance with subsection (b) shall be in 
violation of this Article. 
Sec. 19-446. Prohibited Conduct. 
It shall be unlawful for any solicitor, canvasser, or peddler: 
(a) To make any false, fraudulent, or materially misleading statement concerning the 
nature, quality, quantity, or price of any goods or services for the purpose of inducing a sale; 
(b) To represent that the person is an employee, officer, agent, or representative of the 
Unified Government when the person is not; 
(c) To represent that the person is authorized or licensed by the Unified Government 
when the person does not possess a valid license issued pursuant to this Article; 
(d) To fail to display or produce a valid license upon request; 
(e) To enter or remain upon private property in violation of a clearly posted “No 
Soliciting” or similar notice; 
(f) To refuse to leave private property after being requested to do so by the owner, 
occupant, or person in control of the property; or 
(g) To engage in soliciting, canvassing, or peddling at a residence outside the hours 
established by this Article, except upon express invitation. 
Sec. 19-447. Suspension or Revocation of License. 
(a) The license administrator may suspend or revoke a license issued pursuant to this 
Article for: 
(1) Fraud, misrepresentation, or a materially false statement in the license 
application; 
(2) Violation of any provision of this Article; 
(3) Use of the license by a person other than the licensee; 
(4) Failure to comply with a lawful request to leave private property; or 
(5) Conducting the licensed activity in a manner that constitutes a violation of law 
or creates a threat to the public health, safety, or welfare. 
(b) Any person aggrieved by the suspension of his or her license by the license 
administrator may appeal to the Governing Body within five (5) days after notice of such 
suspension or revocation. 
(c) Suspension or revocation of a license shall not prevent the Unified Government from 
pursuing any other remedy or penalty authorized by law. 
Sec. 19-448. Exemptions. 
The requirements of this Article shall not apply to: 
(a) Persons engaged solely in political campaigning or the distribution of political 
literature, religious, charitable, civic, educational, or other constitutionally protected advocacy or 
expression, to the extent such activity is protected from municipal licensing or permitting 
requirements by federal or state law; 
(b) Persons making sales pursuant to a prior invitation or appointment with the resident or 
occupant; 
(c) Persons making deliveries of goods or services previously ordered; 
(d) Persons conducting transactions at a permanent place of business; 
(e) Such other persons or activities as may be exempted by ordinance or applicable law. 
Sec. 19-449. Penalty. 
(a) Any person who violates any provision of this Article shall, upon conviction, be 
subject to a fine of not less than $500.00 and not more than $1,000.00 for each violation. 
(b) Each separate act or occurrence in violation of this Article may constitute a separate 
offense. 
(c) In addition to any fine imposed pursuant to this section, the Unified Government may 
suspend or revoke any license issued under this Article when authorized by this Article. 
(d) The remedies and penalties provided by this Article shall be cumulative and shall be 
in addition to any other remedy or penalty available under applicable law. 
Section 2. This ordinance shall take effect and be in full force from and after the 
passage, approval, and publication in the official Unified Government newspaper.

Purpose of proposed regulation 
• Storms cause influx of solicitors 
• Establish clear expectations
• Provide residents with a mechanism to discourage unwanted 
solicitation
• Establish consistent hours for solicitation
• Provide an enforcement mechanism for violations
Other Cities 
• Bonner Springs requires a solicitor license, has an application/investigation 
process and included fees, and solicitor license is separate from its general 
business license.
• Kansas City, Missouri requires a business license for 
peddlers/hawkers/hucksters and has a “No Solicitation” property restriction. 
Enforcement handled by KCPD. 
• Lenexa does not require a solicitor permit, but has extensive commercial-
solicitation restrictions, including 10 a.m.–8 p.m. hours, main-entrance 
solicitation, identification requirements, no-soliciting signs, and a prohibition on 
certain convictions. Complaints go through the Police Department. 
• Olathe does not require a permit but rather regulates hours and complicate with 
posted “No Soliciting” notices through its municipal code. Enforced by Olathe 
PD.
Other Cities
• Overland Park regulates commercial solicitation through its municipal 
code, including restrictions on solicitation at  properties displaying “No 
Soliciting” or similar signs and other conduct restrictions. OPPD handles 
complaints
• Shawnee eliminated its individual solicitor permit requirement but requires 
solicitors to have a valid business license and regulates hours, no-
soliciting properties, identification, repeat visits, etc. 
• Topeka requires peddler/solicitor license with application and criminal 
background check; $250 license fee plus $25 background check fee. 
• Wichita regulates door to door sales through street vending regulations, 
license required

Aging and Human Services: 
Bridging the Gap to Services in 
Our Community
Presented by Phyllis Wallace 9/14/2026
September 14, 2026  Administration and Human Services Standing Committee
Opportunity for 
Alignment
On June 1, 2026, Area Agency on Aging and Human Services 
Department officially merged to create a stronger, coordinated 
effort to support individuals and families of our most vulnerable 
populations in our community.  This new unified structure will 
bring together two departments who do similar work with different 
populations. Both departments administer services and 
programming under contractual oversite under the Kansas 
Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS).
Overview
The newly combined Aging and Human Services Department 
provides coordinated access, education, advocacy and supports 
for older adults, individuals with disabilities, caregivers, and their 
families throughout the community. Serving as a “no wrong door” 
for assistance, the department connects residents with in-home 
supports, fair housing, dispute/mediation, and community-based 
resources while working with local partners to promote 
independence, inclusion, and self- sufficiency.
