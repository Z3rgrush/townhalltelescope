Proposed Budget Framework (Discussion Begins 2:01:49) 
Budget Director Lindsey outlined the proposed budget framework, which included the following 
key elements: 
• Align commission priorities; 
• Structurally balanced budget: no use of fund balance; 
• Four percent (4%) growth targets for departments; 
• Inclusion of a one (1) mill increase for both the City and the County in the proposed budget; 
• Fiscal discipline, infrastructure and neighborhood improvements, organizational 
performance, and long-term sustainability; 
• Departments funded at 100% of their position inventory; 
• Shift of some personnel costs out of the Special Street and Highway Fund into the City 
General Fund; 
• Shift of some public safety positions from the Public Safety and Infrastructure Special 
Sales Tax Fund into the City General Fund; 
• Plan to rebuild both the County and City fund balances to achieve a 25% fund balance 
target; 
• Repurposing of funded vacant positions; and 
• Consolidation of positions where possible.

Commissioner Discussion — FY2026 Amended Budget: Ongoing Identified Costs  
Commissioner Stites raised a question regarding the "Ongoing Identified Costs" line item of 
approximately $3 million, describing it as vague and requesting clarification. 
Budget Director Lindsey explained that the figure included interfund corrections, such as a Turner 
Fire Station reimbursement and Transit match grant funds that needed to be paid back. 
Interim County Administrator Howze clarified that these were accounting corrections to ensure 
funds were appropriately placed into the correct accounts, characterizing the adjustments as 
cleanup items. 
Commissioner Stites questioned why prior year budget items were being addressed through the 
FY2026 amended budget, expressing that the explanation was "clear as mud." 
CFO Dr. Shelley Kneuvean provided further clarification, explaining that in prior years, expenses 
were approved and budgeted (using the Turner Fire Station as an example), but the corresponding 
transfer to the project fund was not made due to human error. When the audit identified the error, 
the dollars that had fallen to fund balance had to be corrected and removed from the fund balance, 
as those dollars should never have been placed there. She confirmed there were several such 
instances that needed to be corrected. 
Commissioner Stites asked how the total reached $3 million. 
CFO Dr. Kneuvean clarified that the $3 million in "Ongoing Identified Costs" was composed of: 
• Personnel Reforecast 
• Unexpected Operational Costs 
• Service Level Adjustments

Commissioner Discussion — Budget Practices and Commission Authority 
Commissioner Ramirez expressed concern about what he characterized as a long-term practice 
in which, when revenue comes in higher than projected, staff adds it to departmental line item 
budgets without coming to the commission at that time and instead waiting until the next budget 
cycle. Commissioner Ramirez stated that this is his personal opinion and that he does not believe 
this should be a practice, emphasizing that the commission holds the power of the purse, similar 
to a legislature, and that additional revenue should not automatically result in additional spending 
authority. He stated this was not the fault of the current budget team or administrator but was a 
long-term practice that needed to be broken. He called on the commission to examine budget policy 
regarding how to handle revenues that exceed projections in the adopted budget, what process 
should be followed for staff to utilize those funds, and that waiting until the next budget cycle was 
not appropriate. 
 
Mayor asked whether the money in question had already been spent. 
Budget Director Lindsey confirmed that personnel reforecast costs, unexpected operational costs 
(jail billing, fuel, fire apparatus maintenance, and senior citizen rebate) had already been spent. 
 
Commissioner Kump echoed Commissioner Ramirez's concerns, stating a preference that hirings 
outside the budget and expenditures of this magnitude be brought before the commission for 
approval before spending. Commissioner Kump stated that elected officials are the holders of the 
coin purse and that expenditures of $4 million to $10 million outside the budget should require 
commission approval prior to spending.  
Interim County Administrator Howze confirmed this position, stating that the power of the purse 
is a fundamental authority belonging to the commission. He noted that items in the proposed 
budget not yet committed or expended were being brought to the commission for decision. He 
acknowledged that some items, such as interfund corrections, were obligatory, but that ongoing 
personnel costs creating significant long-term commitments should require prior commission 
approval.

Commissioner Davis inquired about the cadence by which staff is notified of over-budget 
situations and whether there is a threshold below which the county administrator has discretion to

Budget Director Lindsey confirmed the threshold is $50,000, and if an expenditure exceeds

engagement with the commission regarding emerging budget changes is needed.

Commissioner Discussion — SAFER Grant, Fire Department Staffing, and Sheriff's 
Department 
Commissioner Pacheco raised concerns regarding the SAFER grant-funded fire department 
positions, specifically inquiring about how the math would work when the grant funding expires, 
noting that 17 positions would be expiring. 
Budget Director Lindsey explained: 
• The proposed budget funds 12 of the 17 expiring grant-funded positions. 
• Overtime would be used to address the remaining gap. 
• Additional efficiencies within the system would also be explored. 
• If the additional mill is not approved, the recommendation would be to reduce the funded 
positions from 12 to 9. 
Commissioner Pacheco stated plainly that he could not support a budget that cuts police or fire 
in any way, emphasizing that these are fundamental services his constituents depend on daily and 
that net-negative staffing in a few years is not something he could support.  
Commissioner Pacheco also asked specifically about the Sheriff's Department increase of 
approximately 7.4%. 
Budget Director Lindsey explained: 
• Six (6) currently frozen positions are proposed to be unfrozen at a cost of $660,000; 
• An additional $900,000 is needed to reforecast the sheriff's budget to fully fund existing 
positions (due to the prior practice of "trending" personnel budgets at less than full cost); 
and 
• Contractual in-career payouts also required additional funding. 
 
Sheriff Soptic — Testimony on Department Funding and Jail Operations (Discussion Begins 
3:17:13) 
At Commissioner Pacheco's request, Sheriff Soptic was invited to the podium to address the 
commission directly. 
On Unfreezing Positions: Sheriff Soptic explained that he has 32 unfilled Position Identification 
Numbers (PINs) in the Sheriff's Office. He emphasized that filling all 32 would not give the 
department extra personnel — historically, PINs have been given up through prior administrations, 
reducing the total authorized count. He stated that even if all 32 were filled, the department would 
only reach a break-even point in staffing, not have surplus personnel. Sheriff Soptic clarified that 
the $660,000 in the proposed budget is not for new pins — it is to have the funding available to 
fill existing, already-authorized positions should qualified applicants be found. 
The maximum class size the department can train at one time is approximately 19 deputies. 
On Jail Billing: Sheriff Soptic addressed the jail billing issue directly: 
21 | P a g e  
 
• The Wyandotte County Jail is a county jail, not a city jail. Cities within Wyandotte County 
(KCK, Bonner Springs, Edwardsville) may house arrestees in the county jail and are billed 
a daily rate per statute. 
• The daily rate had not been increased since 2011. Sheriff Soptic calculated that CPI growth 
from 2012 to 2024 represented approximately a 33% increase with no corresponding rate 
adjustment. 
• He raised the rate from $86.50–$86.75 per day to $100 per day — a gradual increase, 
noting that at least one other Kansas county charges $240–$260 per day. 
• He stated that the primary driver of increased jail billing costs to the cities is not the rate 
increase but rather the increased length of stay of municipal inmates. 
• When a municipal charge is disposed of and a county charge remains, the county absorbs 
the cost and billing to the city ceases. 
• He reported the current jail population fluctuates between 480 and 520 inmates. The jail 
was originally built for 310 inmates before double-bunking. 
 
Commissioner Burns asked about historical context, recalling that the county was sued by the 
ACLU in the early 1980s to build a new jail. Commissioner Burns expressed deep appreciation for 
the work of the Sheriff's Office. 
 
Commissioner Davis requested that Interim County Administrator Howze obtain a memo from 
Municipal Court regarding the longer sentence trends, and that the legal office (which supervises 
the prosecutors) also provide context. 
Deputy Chief Counsel Casey Meyers confirmed she supervises the prosecutor's office and had 
previously reached out to Municipal Court Judge Schultz. Judge Schultz's response was that longer 
jail stays are often the result of probation violations leading to revocation and imposition of 
underlying jail sentences, as well as prosecutors in some cases requesting longer sentences, 
particularly in domestic violence cases. Ms. Meyers agreed to draft a comprehensive memo for the 
commission.

impacted his department.

Commissioner Lopez inquired about charging inmates a daily room-and-board rate. 
Deputy Chief Counsel Casey Meyers confirmed that state statute does allow for such a charge but 
that it would require commission approval to adopt a policy. She noted she does not know how 
many Kansas jurisdictions currently do this.  
Sheriff Soptic acknowledged the idea but noted that recovering costs from inmates across the state 
has proven difficult and that there are associated collection costs.

Commissioner Hill asked what percentage of costs relate to homeless individuals or those with 
mental health conditions.  
Sheriff Soptic stated this is difficult to quantify because the cost to house an individual is 
essentially the same regardless of their background — the cost differential comes from medical 
costs for those with serious medical conditions and from extended length of stay tied to 
competency evaluations. 
 
Commissioner Stites asked what rate would cause sticker shock. 
Sheriff Soptic indicated some counties charge $50–$260 per day, and Johnson County recently 
raised its rate to $100 per day. He confirmed the daily rate is a flat fee applied uniformly to all 
cities per statute and cannot be tiered by usage volume. 
Commissioner Stites noted that the primary user of the county jail is Kansas City, Kansas (both 
for city charges and overall population). He raised the question of whether it would be possible to 
structure a different formula by city usage.  
Sheriff Soptic confirmed the statute does not allow for differentiated rates — it must be a flat fee 
applied equally to all municipalities. 
 
Commissioner Pacheco made an extended statement to the commission and the public, 
summarizing his observations from the budget presentation and the sheriff's testimony.  
Key points included: 
1. There is approximately a $100 million funding gap between what departments have 
identified as their true needs and what is in the proposed budget across 32 funds. 
2. Many items on the proposed cut list (Land Bank mowing, building maintenance mowing, 
new software initiatives) are technically non-mandated (discretionary) but are services the 
community and department heads have identified as genuine needs — not pet projects. 
3. Union COLAs are contractual and cannot be cut. Non-union COLAs are part of 
compensation management. 
4. 58% of the county budget is state mandated, leaving very limited flexibility. 
5. The budget is not an overspending problem — it is actually an underspending problem 
when viewed against what is required to properly serve the public. 
6. Support for the additional mill on both the city and county sides. 
7. He stated he would not vote for any budget that results in net-negative positions in public 
safety departments. 
8. He noted that the county's debt burden is declining — from approximately 44% of property 
tax revenue going to debt service to approximately 39%, a 5% improvement — and that 
the faster debt is paid down, the more room the commission will have to reduce property 
taxes or improve services.

9. He stated the budget should be viewed through the lens of community needs and 
department needs, not just a desire to cut spending. 
 
Commissioner Ramirez asked legal staff to clarify the legal distinction between "may" and 
"shall" as used in state statutes governing county functions. Chief Legal Lawson responded: 
• "Shall" means the county is directed to fund/do something. 
• "May" means the county has the option. 
Legal staff confirmed that mandated departments and programs are governed by statutes using the 
word "shall," meaning the county is legally required to fund them. 
 
Commissioner Ramirez then asked about the delinquency rate used in the FY2027 proposed 
budget. 
Budget Director Lindsey stated the rate is 6% (applied to real estate taxes only).  
Commissioner Ramirez asked for the actual delinquency rate from the last tax year. CFO Dr. 
Kneuvean stated the team is conducting a deep dive into this data, and the team is actively working 
on it. 
Commissioner Ramirez stated his personal positions: 
• City side: He supports taking the full valuation increase without an additional mill (revenue 
neutral) and believes the commission can find $2 million in efficiency. 
• County side: He supports the one (1) mill increase, given that 58% of the budget is state-
mandated and cuts are very difficult without affecting mandatory services. 
Commissioner Ramirez also asked about the legal ramifications of not fully funding the Sheriff's 
Department. Legal staff confirmed that the Sheriff has the right to file a lawsuit in district court if 
the county fails to fund his constitutionally required office, which would result in additional costs 
to the county and its taxpayers. 
 
The Mayor offered the following comments: 
• Expressed personal opposition to adding the additional mill due to community concern 
about property tax increases. 
• Raised the concept of a furlough as a potential alternative cost reduction — specifically, 
one day per year for all employees, including elected officials. 
• Budget Director Lindsey confirmed furloughs have been used in the past — approximately 
six (6) days annually during the Great Recession and approximately two (2) weeks during 
COVID-19. 
A. CFO Dr. Kneuvean noted that furloughs cannot apply uniformly — public safety 
and other minimum-staffed departments (jails, fire, police) cannot be furloughed 
because of minimum staffing requirements. Union contracts also complicate 
furlough implementation.

B. Interim County Administrator Howze noted that furloughs on the union side are

did not agree to furloughs during past fiscal crises, resulting instead in layoffs. 
• The Mayor expressed a preference for zero-based budgeting discipline — where revenue

Commissioner Davis asked staff what direction was needed from the commission so that the 
September 3, 2026 meeting could result in a clear budget direction. He noted that without specific

Commissioner Davis indicated his likely positions: 
• City side: No additional mill; use fund balance to cover the gap if necessary, rather than

• County side: Support the mill increase; cutting from the county side is very difficult given

Commissioner Davis noted that Land Bank mowing, which disproportionately impacts the

Commissioner Bynum suggested that rather than eliminating entire program categories, the 
commission consider a 1% across-the-board reduction in the City General Fund budget, which 
would get close to the $2 million needed. 
CFO Dr. Kneuvean confirmed the math: 1% of $191 million equals approximately $1.9 million. 
Commissioner Bynum noted that some line items are fixed (fire truck costs, lease obligations) but 
suggested that a combination of small reductions across the proposed new expenditures, combined 
with some use of fund balance, might provide a middle path. 
 
Commissioner Pacheco responded that while the concept is understandable, in practice, cost is 
cost — if a department's budget reflects what a fire truck costs or what a contractual service costs, 
reducing the budget by 1% means something specific within that department will not be funded. 
He reiterated his concern that departments are already operating at minimum levels. 
 
Commissioner Bynum additionally suggested a combination of fund balance use and small 
reductions across multiple programs for the City side, rather than eliminating any single large 
program.
