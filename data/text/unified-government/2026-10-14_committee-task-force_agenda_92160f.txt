Sales Tax Initiative Update - Angel Ferrara wanted to thank the Park Board and Mario 
Escobar for their support. Advised it can be a burden when it comes to conversations about 
taxes. Mario Escobar made it a point to advocate for Parks and Recreation; we really appreciate 
the efforts. Where we are right now, the internal team is working on preparing information, fact

initiatives.  First, there’s a county initiative for the Health Department, which involves a 1.8-

different initiatives; we are working with a lot of internal UG Departments to give educational 
information to the public, she reminded the Park Board. Election day is November 3rd, and 
early voting; if passed, a 38-cent sales tax could generate roughly $16 million annually. This 
would be for City Parks only. When split 50/50, Parks and Recreation would get roughly $8 
million, which is currently four times more revenue than the capital we get on an annual basis 
 
                                                  
                                                 IV- NEW BUSINESS   
 
Public Works Garland Park Project – Angel Ferrara introduced Dave Reno from Unified 
Government, Public Works Communication Engagement Officer, sharing a presentation on 
Garland Park Project Landfill Gas Management System Improvements; also wanted to thank 
him and his team for sharing with the Park Board to keep them informed, as the community 
will have questions 
• Dave Reno – Introduced Diana Miles, Solid Waste Manager for the Unified Government, as 
well as Jeff Miles. Dave Reno shared a presentation to give a brief site history of the park. 
From 1972-1974, this was a landfill; the landfill was capped, and a planned park began. 
We're here tonight to give the Park Board an update on the construction project at John 
Garland Park, specifically on the landfill that's underneath the park.  In 1976, the RCRA law 
increased regulatory standards for solid and hazardous waste management, impacting the 
John Garland Park landfill, which closed in 1974. A major regulatory change came in 1980 
with CERCLA, or Superfund Law, allowing the EPA to monitor landfills for groundwater 
seepage and methane emissions. Following monitoring, methane from decomposition was 
detected, prompting the city to take action. After investigations by the EPA, the Kansas 
Department of Health, and the city, a plan was implemented. The park was reopened, and a 
gas management monitoring and extraction system is being repaired, improved, and 
expanded as part of the ongoing construction project. Dave Reno continued with a 
presentation on how they will extract the methane from the landfill and vent it safely into the 
atmosphere. He continued with more detailed information and advised that the project will 
begin in October 2026; the duration for the project is estimated to be about 120 days.  
o Mario Escobar – Asked if Garland Park was open and if it's safe 
• Diana Miles – Answered, yes, the park is open and it's safe; we monitor every month, and 
then we report it to KDHE, and they check our monitoring 
o Dr. Jane Winkler Philbrook – Asked if a building could be installed, since the land was a 
landfill 
• Laura Drescher – Engineer, Manager of Civil Environmental group answered that it is 
correct; it is not recommended to develop significant infrastructure; however, it is very 
common to use closed landfills for parks and golf courses, parking lots 
o Sarah Lynch – Asked how long John Garland Park will be closed and also asked if they 
have considered agricultural plants. She stated the community has reached out to her 
about having this done 
• Dave Reno – Answered that he wasn’t sure of the specifics  
• Angel Ferrara – Responded that they would have to get back to her 
o Antwaun Jones – Asked, based on that map, from what I could tell, so those… the new 
pipes, they're inside the old pipes, based on the boundary, or are they outside the pipe, 
the original pipes 
• Laura Drescher – Responded: The collector pipes will remain in the same location as before. 
One pipe will go to the North and the other to the South of the blower. This involves making 
repairs to the existing collector pipe at that exact location. Additionally, new wells will be 
installed on UG-owned property, which will connect to this system, thereby expanding it 
with the additional drilled well 
o Ariel Burns – Asked if they happen to run into damaged pipe they weren’t aware of; will 
they replace it while they are working on it or report it 
• Dave Reno – Answered, yes, if they find other areas that need to be repaired or replaced, 
they will fix them 
o Mario Escobar - Asked if other cities with methane emissions issues have managed to 
resolve them; did those problems recur? 
• Laura Drescher – Responded: landfill gas continues to develop as the waste degrades over 
time. Typically, that big decrease in its generation over time. There are a lot of studies that 
put that time period around 30 years, which is why some of the newer solid waste 
regulations require monitoring of closed landfills for at least 30 years. That's called a post-
closure period. 
o Jim Schneweis – Asked if there is any chance of a fire starting 
• Laura Drescher – Responded that she wasn’t aware of any history of this happening; if it 
were to happen, the cause could be oxygen that gets down into a landfill. I would not 
anticipate a high risk of that 
o Andrea Generaux – Asked if this presentation that was shared tonight would be available
