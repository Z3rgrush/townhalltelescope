Item 4.3 – UPDATE:  MIDTOWN STATION PRESENTATION (Discussion Begins 02:26) 
Interim County Administrator Alan Howze presented an update on the Midtown Station project. 
He reported that at the August 3, 2026, Economic Development & Finance meeting, directions 
were provided to work with Midtown and the developer, Eastside Innovation Kansas (EIK), on 
the Midtown Station project to pursue a feasibility and economic impact study. The Unified 
Government (UG) had been in consistent communication with the developer since that meeting. 
Interim County Administrator Howze reported that as of Friday, August 28, 2026, the UG and 
the developer team reached agreement on the scope of the feasibility study. The study will 
provide the elected body with an analysis of economics and development financing and provide 
the developer with information on available incentives and financial support tools. 
Key details: 
• Firm conducting the study: Peckham Guyton Albers & Viets (PGAV), under 
contract with the UG. 
• Funding: The developer has agreed to fund the study at a cost of $40,000. 
Timeline: 
• Final draft study projected to be delivered by September 30, 2026. 
• Final study to be delivered by October 14, 2026. 
• The study and PGAV's presentation of findings will be placed on the  
November 2, 2026, EDF meeting agenda. 
 
Three Focus Areas of the Study: 
1. STAR Bond Analysis – Review of tourism and visitation estimates; determination of 
whether the project may be potentially eligible for STAR bonds as requested by the 
developer. 
2. Development Finance Review – Review of information provided by the developer 
regarding the impact of incentives on the proposal, total development costs, market 
feasibility for the number of units proposed, the developer's pro forma, and extraordinary 
costs associated with the project. 
Page | 6 
3. Revenue Analysis – Examination of what public revenue could be generated from the 
project as scoped, including taxes generated and the revenue stream both with and 
without incentives, and the ability to service infrastructure required for the project. 
 
The report is not intended to make recommendations on types or levels of incentives and will not 
make a recommendation as to whether the project should proceed.  
The study does not bind the UG or the Commission to any course of action, and that those 
decisions remain fully within the purview of the elected officials. Also confirming that extensive 
discussions with the Midtown team, including Jonathan Arnold and Eric Murray, had established 
a common understanding of the purpose and scope of the study.

Item 4.4 – PRESENTATION: OVERVIEW OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT 
INCENTIVES (Discussion Begins 36:55) 
Chief Financial Officer, Dr. Shelly Kneuvean and Deputy Chief Financial Officer, Debbie 
Jonscher, presented the Unified Government's economic development incentive programs.  
The presentation covered economic development incentives:   
STAR Bonds Program 
Authorization: Kansas Statute KSA 1217; initiated in 1993. Wyandotte County has the most 
STAR Bonds districts throughout the state of Kansas. 
Purpose and Eligibility: 
• Financing tool for commercial, entertainment, and tourism projects designed to draw 
visitors from outside the community, the state, and the Midwest. 
• Must be approved by the state of Kansas prior to proceeding; the state determines 
eligibility. 
• Projects must meet significant scale thresholds, including minimum development value 
and projected visitation numbers. 
 
How STAR Bonds Work: 
• Bonds function like a public loan to fund improvements and infrastructure for eligible 
project costs. 
• Eligible project costs are independently certified by a third-party consultant working for 
the UG. 
• Bonds fund a portion of development costs; private financing covers the balance. 
• Bonds are repaid by the new sales tax increment generated from the attraction and 
surrounding development, not from general revenues or property taxes. 
• The pledged sales taxes include: 
Page | 8 
A. State sales tax: 6.5% (corrected from 6.25% as erroneously shown on slide) 
B. Kansas City general sales tax: 1% 
C. County sales tax: approximately 93% of the total allocated to Kansas City, Kansas 
• Sales taxes not captured for STAR bond repayment: Transient Guest Tax (10% as of 
April 1), EMS Tax (0.25%), and the Dedicated Sales Tax (3/8 of a cent). 
• The UG and taxpayers are not obligated to repay the bonds; risk is borne by bondholders. 
One exception: the original Kansas Speedway (Prairie Delaware) bonds included a 
general appropriation clause, which has not been triggered as revenues have been 
sufficient. 
• No property tax abatement is included unless specifically authorized by special enabling 
legislation (NASCAR/Speedway was an exception; all other STAR bond properties pay 
property taxes). 
Active STAR Bond Districts: Three active districts, all located in the Legends/Legends West 
area: 
1. Kansas Speedway (Prairie Delaware), One active bond (refinanced in 2014); terminates 
2027. 
2. Northwest Speedway (American Royal), One bond approved in 2025 for $123 million 
(authorized up to $155 million; reduced based on independent revenue assessment); 
terminates 2046. 
3. Vacation Village — Four bonds covering multiple project sub-areas: 
• Schlitterbahn/Home Field area 
• U.S. Soccer / Compass Minerals 
• Home Field bonds (2022 and 2025) 
• Bond related to auto mall; bonds terminating between 2034 and 2035 
Schlitterbahn bonds did not default following the tragedy at that facility; repayment has 
continued primarily through revenues generated by the auto dealerships in the project area.

II. INDUSTRIAL REVENUE BONDS (IRBs) 
Authorization: State law; for industrial, manufacturing, commercial, office, nonprofit/institutional, 
and certain multifamily housing uses. 
 
Incentive Types: 
• Property tax abatement up to 10 years and/or sales tax exemption on construction 
materials. 
• Negotiated Payment in Lieu of Taxes (PILOT) from companies receiving abatement, 
distributed proportionately to all taxing districts. 
• The UG has no financial liability on IRBs; the company purchases and repays the bonds. 
Page | 11 
Active IRBs in Wyandotte County: 
• 49 active IRBs total 
• 2 with sales tax exemptions only 
• 47 with tax abatement (all paying PILOTs) 
• Total certified investment from all 47 abatement IRBs: approximately $2 billion 
• Total negotiated PILOT payments: $54.6 million 
Annual PILOT Revenue: 
• 2025: $4.6 million retiring 
• 2026: $5.2 million retiring 
Examples of IRB Projects: 
• Ecovyst — Series of IRBs supporting ongoing expansion of a local manufacturing 
company; nearly all abatements have now expired. 
• Turner Logistics Park — Located near the community college along Turner Diagonal. 
• Marvin Windows 
• Urban Outfitters 
• These four companies alone generated approximately 2,900 jobs. 
UG currently does not require companies receiving IRBs to report job creation metrics. This will 
be addressed in upcoming policy revisions to require reporting of job numbers, job quality, 
compensation, benefits, and ideally zip code data to determine access for Wyandotte County 
residents. 
 
III. TAX INCREMENT FINANCING (TIF) 
Authorization: KSA 1217-70; primarily for blight removal, prevention of blight, and commercial 
or mixed-use development. 
 
How TIFs Work: 
• Only the sales or property tax increment above the pre-development base is captured for 
eligible project costs and infrastructure. 
• Base revenues continue flowing to all taxing districts. 
• May be structured as bonds or pay-as-you-go. 
• The UG has no financial responsibility for TIF bonds; no UG backing. 
Page | 12 
Active and Inactive TIFs: 
• 8 active TIFs 
• 4 inactive TIFs 
• Geographically, TIFs have been used primarily on the east side of the community. 
• 3 of the active TIFs have outstanding bonds (all performing as expected). 
Examples of TIF Projects: 
• Rock Island Bridge (Flying Truss), CID overlay, discussed earlier in the agenda. 
• Apartments in the West Bottoms area. 
• Downtown grocery store — part of the financing mechanism now serving as United 
Grocers (or similar). 
 
IV. TRANSPORTATION DEVELOPMENT DISTRICTS (TDD) 
Authorization: KSA 1217; allows an additional sales tax (up to 2%) levied on businesses within 
the designated area. 
Key Features: 
• Does not affect general, EMS, or dedicated sales taxes. 
• 100% of property owners within the district must agree to self-impose the additional tax. 
• Proceeds can only be used for transportation-related improvements as approved in a 
development agreement by the mayor and commission. 
• Located primarily in the Legends area and Fairfax area. 
• 6 active TDDs, of which 3 have bonds with multiple series; some have been fully repaid. 
• Generally, the UG does not back these bonds (one early exception exists but is no longer 
standard practice). 
 
V. COMMUNITY IMPROVEMENT DISTRICTS (CIDs) 
Authorization: KSA 12-6826; up to 22 years; up to 2% additional sales tax. 
Key Features: 
• 55% approval of property owners required to establish. 
• Used throughout the entire community — west side, midtown, Fairfax, downtown, KU 
Med area, and other commercial districts. 
• 17 active CIDs in Kansas City, Kansas. 
Page | 13 
• 3 CIDs in Edwardsville; 1 CID in Bonner Springs. 
• 3 have outstanding bonds; no UG financial backing on those bonds. 
• Pay-as-you-go structure is generally preferred. 
VI. NEIGHBORHOOD REVITALIZATION ACT (NRA) REBATE PROGRAM 
Kansas Neighborhood Reinvestment Act; the Commission recently updated the policy regarding 
prioritized areas. 
Encourages residential and commercial property owners to invest in and improve their properties 
by providing a rebate of a portion of the incremental increase in property tax value resulting from 
improvements. 
Primarily benefits east side of the community, Midtown, and parts of Bonner Springs — areas 
where commercial or residential reinvestment is most needed. 
Program Metrics: 
• 325 active parcels enrolled in the program. 
• 2025 total rebated: $4.6 million. 
• Range of tax rebates: $303 (minimum) to $293,000 (maximum, commercial) with most 
residential rebates averaging approximately $4,000. 
• 2025 increment revenue no longer rebated (now going to taxing districts): approximately 
$607,000, of which $267,000 went to the UG and the remainder to other taxing districts. 
• This data is tracked and included in revenue projections as rebates expire. 
• All projects that proceed to the commission level go through a third-party cost-benefit 
analysis. 
• Regarding the Kansas Department of Commerce transparency database (required by state 
law passed two sessions prior), Greg Kindle (WYEDC), cautioned that the database is not 
fully accurate, it may report an IRB as a $100 million incentive when it is actually a sales 
tax exemption only and recommended the state invest in a better-quality database tool.
