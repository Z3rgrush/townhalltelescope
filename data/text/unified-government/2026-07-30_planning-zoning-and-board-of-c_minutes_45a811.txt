Public Announcements:  (Discussion Begins 03:24)  
Mayor Watson made several community announcements: 
1.  Interim County Administrator Recognition 
Mayor Watson acknowledged Interim County Administrator Alan Howze, noting that the meeting 
was his second meeting in that role.  Mayor Watson stated that she had requested Mr. Howze 
provide the Commission board with updates, and that he had been doing so faithfully since he 
started.  Mayor Watson asked commissioners if they were happy with the reports and received 
unanimous agreement. 
 
2.  2026 Night Out Against Crime 
The 2026 Night Out Against Crime is scheduled for Tuesday, August 4th, from 5:00 PM to 8:00 
PM at the National Guard Armory, 100 South 20th Street, Kansas City, Kansas.  Mayor Watson 
described the event as enhancing the relationship between neighbors and law enforcement and 
bringing back a sense of community.  The free community event would connect public safety 
professionals with community members to share resources.  Highlights included free hot dogs, 
chips, and drinks; free T-shirts for the first 200 people; and door prizes including a 55-inch TV 
raffle. 
  
3 | P a g e  
 
3.  Christal Clear Conversation 
The next Christal Clear Conversation is scheduled for Tuesday, August 4, at 6:00 PM at 
the Eisenhower Recreation Center, 2901 North 72nd Street, Kansas City, Kansas.  The public was 
invited to connect with their elected officials. 
 
4.  Joe Delaney Learn to Swim Program 
Mayor Watson extended congratulations to the UG Parks and Recreation Department and their 
partners — the YMCA, GEHA, and the Hunt Family Foundation — on another successful summer 
completion of the Joe Delaney Learn to Swim Program.  
 
5.  Camp Fury KC — KCK Fire Department 
The Kansas City, Kansas, Fire Department welcomed over 28 female youth campers for Camp 
Fury.  Camp Fury KC is a week-long camp introducing girls in grades/ages nine through twelve 
to careers in public safety, in partnership with local police and fire departments.  The camp is led 
by female KCK Fire Department police officers and firefighters.  Mayor Watson commended the 
KCK Fire Department and Police for their work encouraging youth and demonstrating the benefits 
of public service. 
 
6.  Public Works Department News 
The Public Works Department hosted a friendly softball competition and community support event 
on Friday, July 17th, at 6:00 PM at City Parks Field, 2601 Park Drive, with teams led by captains 
Tony Fisher and Corey Kraft.  A 50/50 raffle raised $350 donated to the Police Athletic 
League.  Mayor Watson noted that only one injury occurred. 
Mayor Watson further reported that Public Works staff volunteered at the Wyandotte County Fair 
by helping park vehicles and assisting with operations throughout the event.  Their efforts raised 
money for Joy in the Box, a local toy drive founded by Public Works employees Dwayne Smith 
and Russ Owens in 2019.  Mayor Watson asked the founders to stand and acknowledged their 
work.  The Wyandotte County Fair volunteering event raised $1,500 in support of the toy drive. 
 
7.  Public Works Department — APWA National Accreditation 
Mayor Watson announced that the American Public Works Association awarded national 
accreditation to the Public Works Department.  A Public Works Expo production team visited 
Public Works on Wednesday, July 22nd, to film a feature highlighting the department's people, 
services, and national accreditation.  The completed video will be presented during the National 
American Public Works Association Expo in Texas and shared online.  Mayor Watson described 
it as a six-minute documentary-style video focusing on leadership at all levels, organizational 
culture, strategic planning, and teamwork.

8.1. SPECIAL USE PERMIT APPLICATION (Discussion Begins 31:37) 
8.1.A: SP2026-035 - JOHN BADGLEY 
Mayor Watson noted this item was previously heard by the Board of Commissioners on June 24, 
2026, at which time no action was voted upon. 
Legal Counsel Angela Lawson presented the options available to the Commission: 
1. Approve the special use permit (adopting the Planning and Zoning Commission's 
recommendation) — requires six votes. 
2. Deny the special use permit (overriding the Planning and Zoning Commission's decision) 
— requires eight votes. 
3. Return the matter to Planning and Zoning for further analysis — requires six votes. 
 
Staff and the Applicant presented opening comments. 
 
Commissioner Pacheco made a motion to approve the item.  The motion died due to lack of a 
second. 
 
Commissioner Davis made a motion to approve the SUP with the condition that all conditions be 
fulfilled within seven months of approval.  The motion was seconded by Commissioner Pacheco.  
Commissioner Davis withdrew his motion. 
 
Commissioner Kump stated for the record “I just wanted to make a larger point that I think, if any 
of our short-term rentals anywhere in the city are getting complaints or police reports, I think, with 
the planning director being notified, the Commission should also be notified, just as a as a rule 
going forward, regardless of what we do tonight.  Just so we're aware of how these may or may 
not be impacting the neighborhoods there, and I just wanted to make that point and get it on the 
record.”

8.2. MISCELLANEOUS – LANDMARKS COMMISSION  
8.2.A: CA2026-004 - 625 MINNESOTA AVENUE (Discussion Begins 1:25:12) 
Legal Counsel Angela Lawson explained that there were two votes to be taken.  She noted the 
Landmarks Commission's finding that it was not appropriate to demolish the building, and that for 
the Board of Commissioners to make a different determination, the Commission would need to 
find:   
(1) the project included all possible planning to minimize harm to the project; and  
(2) there is no feasible and prudent alternative to demolishing the library building.  She 
requested the applicant present their plans so the Commission could review whether these 
standards were met. 
 
Staff and the Applicant presented opening comments. 
 
Commissioner Davis stated for the record, “One, I want to address the letter from Chief Billy 
Friend, right, neighbor of ours, whose letter I think there's concern regarding the demolition and it 
having a negative impact on here in Park and on the cemetery. So, can you all just speak to kind 
of will there be or what's the analysis of the demolition and its impact, if any, on the cemetery? 
 
Applicant representative Brad McKenzie, SAP Design Architects, responded “So Chief Friend 
actually came to the landmarks meeting, and we had an opportunity to talk to him before and after 
the landmarks meeting, and once he learned what the plan was, he said he was actually in full 
support of the library. And I believe he sent an email to the mayor and commissioners expressing 
that support. And like I said, we actually had he was he was recently working on a project where 
he had a bunch of information that he was going to share with me that we could use in the library 
design, and then we were supposed to meet with him this week. Unfortunately, his father passed 
away, so that got postponed. But we're definitely engaging with him, and he is now in support. I 
think he said what his concern was is he felt like the building was going to be demolished and 
something else go in its place besides a library, and when he learned that the plan was to rebuild a 
library, then he was in full support of.” 
 
Commissioner Davis replied for his record statement “Gotcha, that's what I have heard, and so I 
just wanted to get somebody on record saying that, but I didn't want folks to watch this meeting 
and just think that we did not address that because it is a very important concern. The other thing 
I would just kind of like to point out, and I think there's a lot of exciting momentum happening in 
downtown, and when this was kind of bubbling up. It's been kind of, you know, there's been kind 
of secrets and rumors, and now it's like, oh, this is real. They're actually coming before us. They're 
spending some money, all that stuff. I think for us to consider, you know, what Build Wyco was 
doing, what you know, us getting the grocery store, and now library. I mean, and then of course
