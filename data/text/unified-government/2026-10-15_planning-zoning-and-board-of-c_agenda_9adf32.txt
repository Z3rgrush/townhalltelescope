RESOLUTION:  DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE GRANT (2026 - 2029) TO SUPPORT SUBSTANCE USE 
PEER SUPPORT RECOVERY PROGRAM  
BACKGROUND 
Requesting Fast Track to Gain Commission Approval to Submit before the October 1 submission deadline. 
 
• The Public Health Department was awarded the Department of Justice (DOJ) Comprehensive Opioid, 
Stimulant, and Substance Use Program (COSSUP) grant in September 2023.  It was a 3-year grant for $1.3 
million.  The grant term ends on 9/30/2026.  This application is a submission for the next round of funding 
that currently supports our Peer Program. 
• The grant funds the Health Department’s Peer Support Recovery Program.  That includes 3 FTE:  2 peers who 
are in long-term recovery from substance abuse, and a Program Coordinator. 
• The grant also funds an .8 FTE licensed substance-use counselor who resides within UG Community 
Corrections.  These services are performed by a behavioral health partner, who is sub-warded funds through 
the grant.  In the 2023-2026 grant cycle, Wyandot Behavioral Health Network is the partner. 
•  The grant funds the following activities: 
1. A partnership with the UG Drug Court to provide recovery support services.  This includes peer-to-
peer counseling, navigating healthcare and social services, crisis counseling, and other services.  
2. A partnership with the UG Drug Court and UG Community Corrections to assess those who have 
committed eligible offenses for substance use disorder or a mental health disorder so they can 
join the Drug Court and Behavioral Health Court. 
▪ This grant has shortened the assessment period from an average of 3 weeks to several 
days. 
3. Distributing naloxone (Narcan) to community agencies and training staff and residents on using it. 
4. Performing street outreach to homeless residents with substance-use disorders to get them into 
recovery and move them into housing. 
▪ Partnership with the Kansas City, Kansas, Police Department CIT team. 
5. Teaching classes at the Drug Court for those in the program. 
6. Supports for Drug Court participants to complete the 12-month recovery, including transportation 
assistance and short-term hotel vouchers.  

• The deadline for submitting the application is October 1, 2026. 
  
• For this application, we are adding the UG Behavioral and UG Veterans Courts to serve all the Specialty 
Courts. 
  
Funding amount is $1.3 million over 3 years, 10/01/2026 – 9/30/29

RESOLUTION:  FISCAL YEAR 2026 EDWARD BYRNE MEMORIAL JUSTICE ASSISTANCE GRANT 
(JAG) PROGRAM - LOCAL FORMULA  
BACKGROUND 
• The FY26 Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) program provides funding to state and local 
government agencies. Allocations are based on a formula that accounts for crime statistics and population data. 
• The PD has received a similar grant award for the past several years, dating back to at least FY2015. 
• The KCKPD has been allocated $108,838 in federal grant funding through this grant program for FY2026. The 
project period for this award spans from 10/1/25-9/30/29. 
• The PD plans to spend this funding on the following items: 
o Virtual Reality (VR) Taser/De-escalation Training 
o Server Infrastructure 
  
RECOMMENDATION 
Approve 
Fast Track 
 
It is requested that this item be fast tracked to the October 15, 2026 Board of Commissioners meeting.  
BUDGET IMPACTS / FINANCIAL CONSIDERATIONS 
• Both of the aforementioned projects will be 100% grant funded with no match requirement from the UG. 
o The VR program is subscription-based; future subscriptions will be funded with future JAG award 
dollars. 
o Since our server infrastructure CIP was cut for the 2027 budget year, we will use grant funding to 
supplement in order to complete this project as planned. 
  
LEGAL/ POLICY CONSIDERATIONS 
• As part of the grant application process, we will need the following forms reviewed by Legal and signed by the 
CAO: 
o Standard Form 424 (SF-424) 
o Disclosure of Lobbying (SF-LLL)

Presentation by Alan Howze, Interim County Administrator, and Staff:  
General Sales Tax Framework in Kansas: 
• Any sales tax requires voter approval — commission action places the item on the ballot. 
• The state sales tax cap is 11.5% (excluding CIDs and TDDs). 
• The state sales tax rate is 6.5%. 
6 | P a g e  
 
• At the county level, there is a general sales tax of 1% — distributed 25% to the county 
general fund and 75% to cities by formula under state law. 
• Under Kansas law, there are two allowable special purpose county sales taxes: one for 
health and health services, and one for a jail or law enforcement center facility. 
• Special purpose county sales taxes are allowed in increments of 0.25%. 
• On the city side, there is a current 1% general sales tax and two existing special purpose 
sales taxes: the EMS tax (0.25%) and the Public Safety and Neighborhood Infrastructure 
Sales Tax (0.375%). 
• City special purpose taxes are allowed in increments of 0.5% up to 1%. 
• Remaining available capacity on the city side for a special purpose sales tax: 0.375% 
(three-eighths of one percent). 
Estimated Annual Revenue: 
• County side: Each 0.25 cent raises approximately $12 million annually. 
• City side: Each one-eighth cent (0.125%) raises approximately $5.3 million annually. 
• A three-eighths cent (0.375%) city tax would raise approximately $16 million 
annually and approximately $160 million over 10 years. 
Resident Survey Results: A resident survey conducted earlier in 2026 indicated interest in a 
targeted special purpose sales tax. Highest-ranked categories for potential use included: 
1. Unhoused and affordable housing 
2. Parks and Recreation 
3. Public Safety 
4. Street repaving 
 
Wendy Green, Deputy Chief Counsel, provided detailed legal analysis: 
County Special Purpose Health Sales Tax: 
• Authority: KSA 12-187(b)(5) 
• Up to 1% of a countywide retailer sales tax may be used for healthcare services, in 
increments of 0.25%. 
• Eligible uses include: local health departments, preventive healthcare services, mental 
health services, indigent health care, and other health-related activities. 
7 | P a g e  
 
• Critically: Unlike other countywide sales tax provisions, revenue raised under this 
provision stays solely with the county — it is not distributed to other taxing entities within 
the county. 
• Per Attorney General opinions, this provision is only for the county. 
City Special Purpose Sales Tax — Existing Taxes: 
• EMS Tax (0.25%): Established in 2004; does not have a sunset provision as it was 
established before the law changed; remains until repealed by the governing body. 
• Public Safety and Neighborhood Infrastructure Tax (0.375%): Divided one-third/one-
third/one-third among police, sheriff, and public works (with a small parks provision). Has 
a 10-year renewal sunset; currently expires at the end of June 2030; renewal process would 
begin approximately 2028. 
• Remaining available capacity: 0.375% (three-eighths of one percent) for a new city special 
purpose sales tax. 
 
Proposed Options Presented: 
County Side: 
• Resolution: One-quarter percent (0.25%) Special Purpose Countywide Sales Tax for the 
purpose of financing the cost of providing healthcare services as allowed under Kansas 
law. 
City Side: Three options were presented for the three-eighths (0.375%) Special Purpose City Sales 
Tax: 
• Option A: Three-eighths of one percent (0.375%) for the purpose of financing park capital 
improvement projects (including parks, community centers, and trails) and infrastructure 
projects such as roads, bridges, and sidewalks. 
• Option B: Three-eighths of one percent (0.375%) solely for the purpose of financing park 
capital improvement projects (including parks, community centers, and trails). 
• Option C: Three-eighths of one percent (0.375%) solely for the purpose of 
financing infrastructure projects such as roads, bridges, and sidewalks.

Item No. 1 - PUBLIC HEARING: SELF-SUPPORTING MUNICIPAL IMPROVEMENT 
DISTRICT (SSMID) (Discussion Begins 15:05) 
Jeff Conway, Legal Counsel, introduced the item. 
 
Presentation by Jeff Conway, UG Legal Counsel 
Jeff Conway, Legal Counsel for the Unified Government, provided a brief overview of the statutes 
governing the SSMID and the renewal process. 
6 | P a g e  
 
Mr. Conway explained that the SSMID was originally established as an eight-year plan, then 
renewed as a ten-year plan (the current iteration), which will expire December 31, 2026. The 
process now in motion contemplates another possible ten-year renewal. 
The statutorily required steps in the process: 
• June 4: The SSMID Advisory Board recommended renewal for ten years. 
• That recommendation set the hearing dates in motion, which went through the Economic 
Development and Finance (ED&F) Committee and then to the full commission. 
• The commission adopted a resolution setting tonight (September 3) as the public hearing 
date. 
• That resolution was published three times as required by statute. 
• The City Planning Commission heard the item and deemed it consistent with the master 
plan. 
• The resolution was sent to all property owners within the district, though only commercial 
property owners will ultimately be taxed. 
• Following tonight's public hearing, state law requires a 30-day layover before action can 
be taken. 
• Adoption of the ordinance is scheduled for October 15. 
 
Mr. Conway further discussed the cluster of Kansas statutes governing the SSMID process, with 
the primary statute being KSA 12-1796. Mr. Conway read the statutory definition of "services" as 
it applies to the SSMID, which includes: 
1. Provision of special or additional services such as sanitation, security of persons and 
property, and care and maintenance of public facilities including sidewalks and other public 
areas; 
2. Financial support of public transportation service and publicly owned parking facilities, 
including operation and maintenance; 
3. Development plans and programs for future development of the district; 
4. Development, promotion, and support of community events and activities open to the 
general public; and 
5. Any other service the city is authorized to perform and does not perform to the same extent 
citywide. 
 
Each time any new improvement or service is offered, the public hearing and adoption process 
must be followed. The opposition mechanism: if 40% or more of property owners within the 
proposed district sign a petition in opposition, the matter can be taken off the agenda. Conversely, 
the commission itself could propose dissolution, but a petition by 51% of owners could stay that 
matter. 
7 | P a g e  
 
The advisory board structure: the SSMID Advisory Board is the group that meets periodically, 
decides priorities for the district, and advises on the best use of funds. The governing board of the 
SSMID is the Board of Commissioners, which holds final authority over the district. 
The district is proposed to remain the same size and same boundaries as the past ten years. SSMID 
is the taxing entity (created by statute, mandatory for covered commercial property owners); the 
Downtown Shareholders is a voluntary group focused on the vision and future of downtown, 
similar to a Chamber of Commerce. The SSMID issued an RFP for an agent to conduct accounting 
and administrative functions, and the Downtown Shareholders was selected.  
 
Presentation by Edwin Birch, Chair of the SSMID Board 
Edwin Birch, Chair of the SSMID Board, acknowledged that downtown KCK still faces significant 
redevelopment challenges, including vacant buildings and properties needing investment.  
Signs of renewed investment and interest, including: 
• Redevelopment of the former abandoned UMB Bank building; 
• Purchase of the Brotherhood Building by a new owner; and 
• Future development of a new library. 
 
A strong, vibrant downtown is essential to sustaining momentum and attracting further investment, 
and that the SSMID supports the foundation of a healthy downtown by creating a cleaner, safer, 
and more welcoming environment. 
SSMID is not responsible for: downtown vacancies, delinquent taxes, property investment 
decisions, code issues, or property ownership decisions — noting those are larger challenges 
requiring collaboration among many stakeholders. SSMID help create the conditions for progress. 
 
Presentation by Dawn Ratan, Executive Director, Downtown Shareholders of Kansas City, 
Dawn Ratan, Executive Director of the Downtown Shareholders of Kansas City, Kansas (DTS), 
provided a comprehensive presentation on the work of both DTS and the SSMID. 
 
Key points: 
SSMID Overview: 
• Active since 2008; renews every ten years; DTS serves as its manager. 
• Primary focus is cleaning, security, and marketing. 
• Funded by property tax (ad valorem) assessments on commercial properties within the 
district, plus annual voluntary contributions in lieu of taxes from the Unified Government 
(UG), Board of Public Utilities (BPU), and KU Health Systems. Non-profits and 
governmental entities are not subject to the SSMID tax, hence the voluntary contributions. 
• An advisory board exists on which each commissioner has appointed a member; the board
