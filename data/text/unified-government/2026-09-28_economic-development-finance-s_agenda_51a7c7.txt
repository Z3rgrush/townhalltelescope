Overview Presented by Lead Analyst Sam Her: 
• Total Budget: Approximately $2.4 million. 
• Full-Time Equivalents (FTEs): Eight (8). 
• Primary Divisions: Economic Development Administration and the Land Bank. 
• Personnel: 42% of total budget. 
• Contractual Services: 53% of total budget, of which 63% is allocated to mowing services. 
• Notable 2027 Budget Increases: 
A. Mowing services: $100,000 increase. 
B. Clearance and demolition (tree removal): $50,000 increase (bringing total to 
approximately $75,000). 
C. Property appraisals: $25,000 increase. 
Mr. Peterson added that two (2) additional positions were built into the Economic Development 
budget, though they were not reflected on the slide. He further clarified the distinct funding 
structure of the department: 
 
 
Page | 4 
• Economic Development functions are primarily a city function and are funded from the 
city side of the budget. 
• Land Bank functions are historically a county function and are funded from the county 
portion of the budget. 
• The Wyandotte Economic Development Organization (WEDO) receives contributions 
from both city and county funds to serve both the city and county. 
 
Budget Stage Summary (Presented by Michael Peterson): 
 Budget Stage  Amount 
Base Budget (per 2026 approved budget) ~$1.9 million 
Requested Budget ~$2.39 million 
Funded Budget ~$2.39 million (full request funded) 
Economic Development was treated as a higher-priority department and its full identified needs 
were built into the budget due to transitions occurring within the department over the past year. 
 
Presentation by Interim Economic Development Director Jud Knapp: 
Interim Director Jud Knapp walked through the department's specific budget requests: 
• Mowing Increase ($100,000): 
A. The department increased mowing frequency from once per month to twice per 
month. 
B. The department removed 665 lots from the mowing rotation (through sales, 
options such as CJR's option program, and other dispositions), reducing the total 
number of lots being maintained. 
C. The department is currently maintaining approximately 2,200–2,300 lots. 
D. The additional frequency of mowing has significantly reduced community 
complaints. Knapp noted that neighbor calls to the office had decreased 
dramatically since the switch to twice-monthly mowing. 
E. The $100,000 request is to sustain this frequency through the end of the year. 
 
Page | 5 
• Clearance and Demolition / Tree Removal ($50,000 increase, to $75,000 total): 
A. Current budget for this line is $25,000. 
B. Each tree removal costs approximately $2,000. 
C. Trees targeted are dead trees hanging over houses and neighboring land bank lots 
that pose a public safety risk. 
D. The increased funding would allow approximately 38 tree removals. 
• Property Appraisals ($25,000): 
A. Conducted at the direction of the County Administrator to obtain independent, 
third-party appraisals on specific properties (e.g., Midtown, Indian Springs, 99th 
and Lamborghini Road, land bank grain silos) for the purpose of selling those 
properties at fair market value. 
 
The Chair opened the public comment.  No comments were received.  The Chairman closed the

COMMISSIONER QUESTIONS AND DISCUSSION 
Commissioner Davis asked about the purpose of the appraisal line item.  
Interim Economic Development Director, Jud Knapp responded that the County Administrator 
directed the department to obtain independent appraisals for specific properties including 
Midtown, Indian Springs, 99th and Lamborghini Road, and the land bank grain silos to facilitate 
sale at fair market value. 
 
Commissioner Pacheco, participating remotely, asked for clarification on whether Economic 
Development activities funded by Kansas City, Kansas extend to other parts of the county, such 
as Bonner Springs and Edwardsville.  
Chief Legal Counsel, Angela Lawson, responded that those cities handle their own economic 
development functions, and the Kansas City, Kansas Economic Development Department does 
not conduct activities within those cities. She clarified that the Land Bank handles county 
properties outside of Kansas City, Kansas.  
Mr. Peterson further clarified that the Wyandotte Economic Development Organization receives 
contributions from both city and county funds and serves both city and county. 
 
Page | 6 
Commissioner Stites asked why the Land Bank, with approximately 95% or more of its 
properties located within Kansas City, Kansas, is funded from the county side of the budget 
rather than the city side.  
Deputy Chief Counsel, Wendy Green, responded that the Wyandotte County Land Bank was 
established as a county land bank by statute and ordinance, was the first land bank in the state of 
Kansas, and is embedded in state statutes as a county function; which is why it is funded from 
the county budget. 
 
Commissioner Howard asked the following questions regarding mowing: What problem does 
the increased mowing frequency solve, and how will its effectiveness be measured. 
Mr. Knapp responded that the once-monthly mowing generated constant complaints about 
overgrown lots. Since switching to twice-monthly mowing, calls have decreased significantly 
and neighborhoods look better. 
Commissioner Howard asked regarding the $75,000 allocated for tree removal, how is the UG 
prioritizing the 38 trees for removal, and is there a documented risk assessment so that the most 
dangerous trees are addressed first. 
Mr. Knapp explained that the abatement team maintains a prioritized list. The department holds 
funds in reserve for storm emergencies and typically spends remaining funds on prioritized tree 
removals toward November, once severe weather season has passed. 
 
Commissioner Bynum noted the update that 665 lots had been removed from the mowing 
rotation and asked whether that represented lots sold or removed through other mechanisms.  
Mr. Knapp clarified that the 665 lots were removed from the mowing rotation through various 
means including options (such as CJR's option program), not necessarily outright sales. Also 
confirming that the department is currently maintaining approximately 2,300 lots. 
Commissioner Bynum noted the desire to receive a future update on how many options are 
outstanding and how many tax-producing houses have been built on former land bank lots. The 
Chair suggested this update could be provided at one of Commissioner Davis's Neighborhood 
and Community Development Committee meetings. 
 
Commissioner Howard also raised concerns regarding contractor performance with respect to 
trash removal prior to mowing. He cited specific locations, 13th and Parallel, and Quindaro 
where contractors appeared to be mowing over trash, including campaign signs, rather than 
removing it first.  
Page | 7 
Mr. Knapp confirmed that the contract requires contractors to pick up trash before mowing, and 
that the abatement team is called in if there is more than one trash bag's worth of material on a 
lot. He acknowledged receiving few complaints about this issue but committed to addressing it if 
informed of specific incidents. 
Interim County Administrator, Alan Howze, recommended that community members report such 
incidents through 311, noting that a photo accompanying the complaint would be helpful and 
that staff could trace the complaint back to the specific contractor responsible for that lot. 
 
Commissioner Howard further noted a concern about contractors cutting weeds only to the 
extent of simply mowing, without attending to weeds at stop signs and intersections, and asked 
whether contracts include standards for comprehensive landscaping.  
Knapp confirmed that trash pickup is included in contractor contracts.  
Commissioner Howard encouraged enforcement of the contract terms and expressed interest in 
offering contracts to community members who are willing to do the work thoroughly and with 
pride. 
 
Commissioner Bynum suggested the committee revisit the contract terms regarding breach 
provisions and the process for terminating contracts if services are not being rendered, as 
specified. 
 
This item was for information only, and no action was required.

 Budget Stage   Amount

Mr. Peterson noted that the Appraiser's budget actually decreased from the base budget due to a 
reorganization undertaken by County Appraiser Matt Willard outside of the formal budget 
process. 
 
Presentation by County Appraiser Matt Willard: 
County Appraiser Matt Willard provided remarks on the current status of his office: 
• Reorganization: The reorganization involved eliminating certain positions and 
restructuring the office to improve operations and recruitment. Mr. Willard expressed 
pride in this effort, noting that it had positioned the office to recruit more competitively. 
Also noting that, just that week, the office had received an application from an appraiser 
currently employed at another county, something that had not occurred in years. 
• State Compliance: The county commission was to receive a letter from the Kansas State 
Property Valuation Division (PVD) that week confirming the Appraiser's Office was 
in substantial compliance for the 2026 valuation year, both statistically and procedurally.  
• Valuation Notices: Assessment notices went out this year without requiring an extension, 
which Mr. Willard noted had not been routine historically. 
 
Remarks by Interim County Administrator Alan Howze: 
Interim County Administrator Howze requested that Mr. Willard explain the role of the Kansas 
Department of Revenue (KDOR) and the State Property Valuation Division (PVD) in measuring 
the office's performance for the general public's benefit. 
Page | 9 
• The state measures appraiser performance by comparing sales prices to assessed values. 
For residential properties, this is based on 2025 calendar year sales compared to 2025 
values (the most recently available). 
• State standards require residential values to fall between 90% and 110% of the median 
sales price ratio. 
• The Wyandotte County residential ratio was approximately 94%, slightly under 100% of 
actual market value, within compliance. 
• The commercial ratio was approximately 95%, a significant improvement from prior 
years, when commercial properties historically ran lower ratios than residential 
properties. Mr. Willard noted his office had made deliberate efforts to recalibrate 
commercial values to bring commercial and residential ratios into closer alignment. 
• Procedural compliance refers to adherence to rules, regulations, and statutes governing 
how appraisal work must be conducted, as overseen by the PVD. 
The Appraiser's Office is a strong advocate for the Unified Government's proposed new tax 
management system, which is being built into the 2027 budget. The Appraiser’s office has 
communicated how this new system would improve efficiency and workflow across the Unified 
Government. 
 
The Chair opened the public comment.  No comments were received.  The Chairman closed the

COMMISSIONER QUESTIONS AND DISCUSSION  
Commissioner Burns commended Mr. Willard and his staff for the quality of their public 
outreach and education efforts, noting that when citizens understand the state-driven nature of 
the appraisal process, it helps reduce tensions around property valuation complaints. He 
encouraged Mr. Willard to continue these efforts. Burns also noted that Mr. Willard 
appropriately informs residents that, if they wish to change the statewide system, they should 
contact their state-level elected officials. 
Mr. Willard acknowledged Commissioner Burns's comments, noting that public education had 
been a deliberate priority starting approximately four years ago and has been expanded since. He 
agreed that sharing factual information about how the property tax system works and directing 
residents to state legislators when systemic changes are desired and an important function of the 
office. 
 
 
Page | 10 
Commissioner Pacheco echoed Commissioner Burns's remarks, commending the Appraiser's 
Office for its fiscal responsibility in holding its budget flat. Also referencing prior conversations 
with Mr. Willard regarding circuit breaker programs available in other states and stated his 
commitment to pursuing such property tax relief mechanisms. Commissioner Pacheo also 
inquired about what Mr. Willard attributed the recent out-of-county applicant interest to. 
The reorganization enabled Mr. Willard to increase the starting salary for appraiser positions, 
which is a primary factor in recruitment. The office's statewide reputation for excellence and 
strong internal culture as contributing factors. 
 
Commissioner Bynum asked about the 2026 round of property valuations, specifically 
regarding residential appeals.  
Mr. Willard confirmed the 2026 valuations are for taxes assessed in December (based on January 
2026 values) and mailed in March 2026. He estimated fewer than 3,000 total appeals (residential, 
commercial, and vacant lots combined), noting that commercial owners of multiple properties 
often file appeals on all properties at once, and that condominium appeals, though numerous in 
count, often represent a single consolidated action.

Presentation by CFO Dr. Shelley Kneuvean: 
Chief Financial Officer, Dr. Shelley Kneuvean provided a comprehensive overview of Finance 
Department operations by division: 
Motor Vehicle Division: 
• Performs over 120,000 transactions per year. 
• For every dollar in fees collected, the Unified Government retains 12 cents locally, 
with 88 cents remitted to the state. 
• The proposed $8 fee increase would essentially achieve near-full cost recovery for the 
Motor Vehicle Division's operations, with approximately $100,000 still coming from the

• The Bonner Springs Motor Vehicle Annex, open two days per week for renewals, was

• A new QList line management system (estimated cost: approximately $15,000) is being

transaction type, allowing for more efficient window scheduling. The system was 
reviewed at Johnson County and is already in use there. 
Page | 13 
• An express checkout concept is under development, which would allow customers to 
upload documents in advance for pre-review before arriving in person, improving 
efficiency and reducing waiting times. 
• CFO Kneuvean noted that frontline Motor Vehicle positions are hard to fill and retain due

requirement changes would help attract local candidates. 
• Approximately 60% of eligible renewals are now completed online, though this rate has 
plateaued. Many transactions must still be completed in person or by mail due to state 
requirements (e.g., insurance verification through the state upload system). 
Accounting Division: 
• Approximately 26,000 accounts payable processed per year. 
• Approximately 400,000 revenue source transactions managed per year. 
• Manages capital assets, audits, and 1099 filings for independent contractors. 
• Received the Triple Crown Award for excellence in financial reporting documents. 
• Workday has implemented to transition paper-based processes to electronic, improving 
efficiency significantly. 
Treasury Division: 
• Collects over $325 million in property taxes annually as custodian for all taxing districts 
in Wyandotte County. 
• Distributes tax collections to appropriate taxing districts. 
• Delinquent Tax Sale: Scheduled for October 14, 2026. The process began with 203 
properties under review and has been reduced to 158 properties as residents entered 
payment plans or resolved delinquencies. Kneuvean noted that court oversight provides 
important protection for property owners' rights. Properties must be in the third year of 
full tax delinquency before proceeding to tax sale. 
• CFO Kneuvean noted that Deputy Chief Attorney Wendy Green assists with the tax sale 
process. 
• No uptick in foreclosures was observed; a downward trend in delinquencies.  
Finance Administration:  
• Administers over 430 separate debt-related actions per year. 
• Manages grants compliance for over $28 million in federal grants annually, plus state and 
local grants. 
• Oversees more than 120 total grants. 
Page | 14 
Payroll Division: 
• Processes over 56,000 payroll transactions (checks/electronic payments) per year. 
• Processes approximately 100,000 benefit-related transactions per year. 
• Manages W-2 filings and assists employees with retirement processing (KPERS and 
KPNF). 
• Runs payroll every week, alternating between public safety and non-public safety cycles. 
• Efficiency significantly improved through Workday implementation. 
Purchasing Division: 
• Processes over 2,000 purchase orders per year. 
• Issues approximately 50 independent bids per year.

• Purchasing Card Administration: Following criticism from the Legislative Auditor and

Quali Incorporated: An online platform to assist small businesses in navigating government 
resources and procurement opportunities. It had been paused due to budget constraints; the 2027 
budget restores funding ($15,000) to bring the program back.  
Sharon Reed was identified as the staff contact for questions regarding this program. 
Push Button: A system improvement that will automate population of data tables in the 
Comprehensive Annual Financial Report, pulling data directly from existing financial systems 
rather than requiring manual entry. This is a one-time cost of $80,000 with substantially reduced 
ongoing costs.

Employee Health Benefits 
HR Director Ramirez provided a detailed history and current status of the Unified Government's 
Employee Health Fund. 
 
Key Definitions 
• Self-Insured Plan: An arrangement where the employer and employees bear the cost of 
medical claims directly, rather than paying a fixed premium to an insurance carrier. 
• Stop Loss Coverage: Insurance purchased by the self-insured employer to protect against 
catastrophic individual claims. The UG's current stop loss threshold is $400,000 per 
individual claim, above which the stop loss carrier reimburses claims. 
Historical Overview: 
• The current self-funded plan structure originated in 1990 with the consolidation of seven 
separate insurance plans that previously existed across the city and six participating 
unions (some of which were fully insured, some self-insured). 
• In the early years, due to lack of comprehensive claims history, the plan was underfunded 
by approximately $200,000 for approximately 18 months. 
• The Employee Benefits Committee (EBC) was established to provide joint employer-
employee governance over the health plan. The committee includes one voting 
representative from each participating union (except skilled trades, who remain on their 
union's own health plan), as well as a UG Commission representative.  
• Commissioner Pacheco currently serves as the Commission representative. The 
Committee functions as a recommending body; final authority rests with the County 
Administrator. 
• Employee Benefits Committee established a target of maintaining a 30% fund reserve, 
sufficient to cover approximately 2.5 to 3 months of incurred but not reported (IBNR) 
claims and to cover unexpected large claims below the $400,000 stop loss threshold. 
• Many union contracts include language limiting annual premium increases to no more 
than 14%. 
Current Fund Status: 
• Current health reserve balance: approximately $18 million (~42% reserve ratio — above 
the 30% target). 
• 2027 proposed employer and employee premium increase: 3% (below the actuarially 
projected 4.1% needed to maintain reserves).

• Stop loss premiums are expected to increase by approximately 25% at next renewal.

Revenue Neutral Rate Discussion 
Commissioner Bynum raised an issue for broader discussion, noting that upon reviewing 
the Notice of Estimated Tax recently mailed to property owners (required by Kansas state law), 
she observed that her property tax owed to the community college had increased despite the 
college adopting a revenue neutral mill levy rate. 
Commissioner Bynum explained the mathematical dynamic at issue: when a property's assessed 
valuation increases significantly, the impact of that increased valuation may more than offset any 
reduction in the mill levy rate that accompanies a revenue neutral budget decision, resulting in a 
higher individual tax bill even though the taxing entity chose revenue neutral. 
UG Clerk, Monica L. Sparks, confirmed the scenario as described. Kneuvean noted that the 
illustrative example presented (property value doubling from $1,000 to $2,000) represents a 
100% valuation increase and is not realistic as a typical case and recommended that actual or 
near-realistic value change figures be used in any public education effort. 
 
Commissioner Howard asked whether the UG website has a digital property tax calculator 
available to residents to help them understand their individual tax bills.  
UG Clerk Sparks confirmed that no such calculator currently exists.  
 
Commissioner Bynum and Clerk, Monica L. Sparks, noted that the mathematical formula is 
available, but no interactive tool exists. 
Deputy Chief Financial Officer, Debbie Jonscher suggested that a simple formula explaining 
how to calculate individual tax changes could be prepared and included in public education 
materials, with the caveat that overall countywide valuation changes do not uniformly apply to 
individual properties which may rise, fall, or remain flat at rates different from the county 
average. 
 
Commissioner Davis suggested that the August 24, 2026, public hearing, would be an 
appropriate venue to include slides explaining the revenue neutral concept with real examples, 
noting that residents could bring their own mailed tax estimates to the meeting for reference.

Commissioner Pacheco offered an extended comment on the structural challenge of property 
taxation, noting: 
• The major driver of increased tax bills is valuation growth, not mil levy decisions. 
• Revenue neutral decisions do not protect individual property owners whose assessed 
values rise above the county average growth rate. 
• Wages in Wyandotte County are already below the state average, exacerbating the impact 
of valuation growth on affordability. 
• Circuit breaker programs, available in many other states, could effectively blunt the 
impact of valuation spikes on property owners and represent an important legislative 
priority. 
• The community should understand that valuation growth; while a positive indicator of 
property wealth translates directly into higher tax bills unless circuit breaker mechanisms 
are in place. 
 
Commissioner Bynum expressed agreement and asked that a clear, public-facing explanation of

STAR Bonds Program 
Authorization: Kansas Statute KSA 1217; initiated in 1993. Wyandotte County has the most 
STAR Bonds districts throughout the state of Kansas. 
Purpose and Eligibility: 
• Financing tool for commercial, entertainment, and tourism projects designed to draw 
visitors from outside the community, the state, and the Midwest. 
• Must be approved by the state of Kansas prior to proceeding; the state determines 
eligibility. 
• Projects must meet significant scale thresholds, including minimum development value 
and projected visitation numbers. 
 
How STAR Bonds Work: 
• Bonds function like a public loan to fund improvements and infrastructure for eligible 
project costs. 
• Eligible project costs are independently certified by a third-party consultant working for 
the UG. 
• Bonds fund a portion of development costs; private financing covers the balance. 
• Bonds are repaid by the new sales tax increment generated from the attraction and 
surrounding development, not from general revenues or property taxes. 
• The pledged sales taxes include:

A. State sales tax: 6.5% (corrected from 6.25% as erroneously shown on slide) 
B. Kansas City general sales tax: 1% 
C. County sales tax: approximately 93% of the total allocated to Kansas City, Kansas 
• Sales taxes not captured for STAR bond repayment: Transient Guest Tax (10% as of 
April 1), EMS Tax (0.25%), and the Dedicated Sales Tax (3/8 of a cent). 
• The UG and taxpayers are not obligated to repay the bonds; risk is borne by bondholders. 
One exception: the original Kansas Speedway (Prairie Delaware) bonds included a 
general appropriation clause, which has not been triggered as revenues have been 
sufficient. 
• No property tax abatement is included unless specifically authorized by special enabling 
legislation (NASCAR/Speedway was an exception; all other STAR bond properties pay 
property taxes).

Existing Unified Government Rebate Programs 
 
The Unified Government provides a sales tax rebate program up to $200 for qualified 
seniors and disabled persons with an income equal to the threshold established under the 
State Homestead Property Tax Refund Program.   
 
Rebates are also offered to eligible citizens who are BPU ratepayers in Wyandotte County 
for a portion of their BPU, gas, or telecommunications utility bills. If residents qualify for 
utility rebates, they also qualify for BPU PILOT relief.  If residents qualify for the utility PILOT 
waiver, they will also qualify for the sales tax rebate. 
 
The UG also assists individuals with applying for the State Homestead program outlined 
above.  There is currently no local rebate program related to property taxes. 
 
Circuit Breaker Assistance for Resident Equity (CARE) Program Proposal 
 
Commissioner Pacheco proposed the Circuit Breaker Assistance for Resident Equity 
(CARE) Program to address significant increases in assessed value that surpass the 
increase in household income (see Attachment 2). 
As proposed, CARE is designed as a Unified Government rebate program paid from the 
County General Fund as rebates to eligible homeowners after property taxes are billed and 
paid.  The program: 
 
• Does not change appraised or assessed values 
• Does not alter assessment ratios or appraisal methods set by state law 
• Complies with Kansas' uniform-and-equal requirements by operating as General 
Fund spending rather than valuation changes or tax exemptions 
 
As proposed, there are three “triggers” to determine eligibility for a portion of the County 
property tax to be rebated: 
 
1. Tax amount compared to household income rebate: County taxes are more than 5% 
of the household income up to $1,000 
2. Increase in assessed value rebate: Greater than 10% increase in assessed value 
driving the tax increase – rebate for amount over the 10% increase of the County 
property taxes up to $1,000 
3. Long term resident rebate:  20% rebate if resident has lived / owned home more 
than 10 years – rebate for year-to-year County tax increase up to $1,000

Policy & Administrative Considerations 
A few key considerations: 
• Legal Requirements - Any rebate program must be uniformly administered based 
on clearly identified criteria. 
 
• Taxes Eligible to be Rebated – Rebated property taxes can only be done for the 
Wyandotte County or the City of Kansas City, Kansas portion; the Unified 
Government cannot rebate taxes due to other taxing districts; (Commission 
Pacheco’s proposal focused on the County portion).  
 
• Eligibility Considerations - All cities in Kansas focus their property tax rebate 
programs to assist lower income and seniors, except for Edgerton.  Should a 
program administered by the UG focus on these populations or be broader as 
proposed by Commissioner Pacheco?  Should there be a needs test for a rebate 
program? 
 
Would single family owner-occupied units be eligible or also single-family owner-
occupied attached units (duplexes, townhomes)? 
 
It is assumed that if the home has an active Neighborhood Reinvestment Act (NRA) 
refund approval, it would not be eligible for this program. 
 
• Financial Considerations – There are a total of 47,980 single family detached 
homes in Wyandotte County.  Assuming every household qualified for a rebate with 
a maximum of $1,000, the total rebate budget would be $47.98 million. Depending 
on the criteria for eligibility, it is not anticipated that all 47,980 homes would qualify.  
This figure would go up if condos, duplexes, and townhomes were included. 
 
Regardless, rebate programs should be funded with a finite amount in the budget as 
they are for all other cities offering tax rebate programs in Kansas.  An application 
process is used to determine eligibility, and the budget is distributed based on the 
criteria and the available funding for the rebate program (all cities currently do this 
except the UG).  To ensure the program remains in budget, all cities except for the 
Unified Government have established a fixed budget which is then distributed to the 
qualified applicants based on the amount available.  The UG should evaluate ways 
to cost contain any rebate programs such as considering a fixed budget,

MEMORANDUM 
TO: All County Appraisers, County Clerks and County Treasurers 
FROM: Bob Kent, Director 
 Property Valuation Division 
DATE: August 26, 2026 
SUBJECT: HB 2644 and Application of K.S.A. 79-1460 
 
The Kansas Legislature passed HB 2644 during the 2026 legislative session that imposes restrictions on 
certain property appraisals. Prior to the passage of HB 2644, K.S.A. 79-1460 imposed limited restrictions 
on increases in appraised value following a successful valuation appeal. Specifically, when commercial 
real property received a reduction in value through the appeals process, the county appraiser was required 
to monitor that property for the next two taxable years. If the appraised value increased by more than 5% 
from one year to the next, excluding new construction, change in use, or change in classification, the 
appraiser was required to either adjust the value based on information presented in the prior appeal or 
obtain an independent fee simple appraisal. This statutory requirement applied only to commercial real 
property and did not apply to residential real property or mobile homes. 
 
HB 2644 expands the scope and duration of post-appeal review requirements under K.S.A. 79-1460. 
Beginning with final determinations made on or after January 1, 2026, the statute now applies to 
commercial real property, residential real property, and personal property used as residential mobile 
homes. In addition, the monitoring period has been extended from two years to five years. During this 
five-year period, if the appraised value of a property increases by more than 5% from the prior year, 
excluding new construction, change in use, or classification, the county appraiser must either adjust the 
valuation based on information from the prior appeal or obtain an independent fee simple appraisal 
performed by a Kansas certified real property appraiser. 
 
The controlling factor of whether existing law (two years for commercial property) or the new legislation 
(five years for residential, residential personal property mobile homes, or commercial property) is applied 
is now determined by the date of the final determination of the appeal, and not the tax year under appeal. 
Any required adjustment to the value will be applied to the appealed valuation for the aforementioned 
subclasses for that tax year, and subsequent 5% limits will be applied beginning the year following the 
appealed tax year but, also taking specified criteria into consideration. The following outlines the general 
application of HB 2644 requirements. 

 
Residential or Personal Property Manufactured Home Example – Final appeal determination prior to-
January 1, 2026 
▪ Residential property value was appealed in 2025, and the final determination of value was 
reduced and rendered prior to January 1, 2026. 
▪ The changes to K.S.A. 79-1460 in HB 2644 do not apply because the property class was 
residential and the appeal decision was rendered prior to January 1, 2026, when the statute did not 
apply to residential property. 
▪ The appraiser may rely on market trends and CAMA generated values without additional 
obligations. 
 
Commercial Property Example – Final appeal determination prior to January 1, 2026 
▪ Commercial property value was appealed in any year, and the final determination of value was 
reduced and rendered prior to January 1, 2026. 
▪ There was no new construction, change in use, or change in classification to the property. 
▪ Because the final determination of value was rendered prior to January 1, 2026, the value is 
subject to a two-year monitoring period beyond the base valuation year. The appraiser must 
review the prior year’s appeal evidence and limit any value increase in those two years to 5% or 
obtain an independent single property appraisal. 
▪ This requirement only applies to commercial property for a 2-year monitoring period. 
 
Commercial, Residential or Personal Property Manufactured Home Example – Final appeal 
determination after January 1, 2026, but prior to October 31 of the current year 
▪ A residential, commercial or personal property manufactured home property value was appealed 
and reduced, and a final determination of value was rendered after January 1, 2026, but prior to 
October 31 of the same year. 
▪ There was no new construction, change in use, or change in classification to the property. 
▪ The value is subject to a five-year monitoring period beyond the base valuation year. The 
appraiser must review the prior year’s appeal evidence and limit any value increase in the five 
years to 5% or obtain an independent single property appraisal. 
▪ This requirement only applies to residential, commercial or personal property manufactured home 
properties for 5 years. 
 
For all property appeals in the subclasses described in K.S.A. 79-1460(b), when the valuation for real 
property has been reduced due to a final determination made pursuant to the valuation appeals process for 
the prior year, and the county appraiser has already certified the appraisal rolls for the current year to the 
county clerk pursuant to K.S.A. 79-1466, and amendments thereto, the county appraiser may amend the 
appraisal rolls and certify the changes to the county clerk to implement the provisions of this subsection 
and reduce the valuation of the real property to the prior year's final determination, except that such 
changes shall not be made after October 31 of the current year.
