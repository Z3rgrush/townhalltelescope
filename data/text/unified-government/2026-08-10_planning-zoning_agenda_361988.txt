PROPOSAL 
Detailed Outline of Requested Action: The applicant, Miguel Madrigal, is requesting the 
following Variance at 1404 North 59th Street: 
 
This appeal has been filed to subdivide a parcel into three (3) residential lots. Section 27-
280(b) states that the depth of a lot shall not exceed three (3) times the width. 
Lot 1 has a width of 96.00 feet and a depth of 448.75 feet. The appellant is requesting a 
depth-to-width ratio of 1:4.67, a violation of 160.75 feet. 
 
Lot 2 has a width of 96.00 feet and a depth of 447.75 feet. The appellant is requesting a 
depth-to-width ratio of 1:4.66, a violation of 159.75 feet. 
 
Lot 3 has a width of 138.30 feet and a depth of 635 feet. The appellant is requesting a 
depth-to-width ratio of 1:4.59, a violation of 220.10 feet. 
 
City Ordinance Requirements: Article XXI Sections 27-502 – 27-709, Article VII Sections 
27-245-339, Article XI Section 27-215, and all other applicable standards within Chapter 
27. 
 
KANSAS STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS 
1. The variance requested arises from such condition which is unique to the 
property in question and which is not ordinarily found in the same zone or 
district; and which is not created by an action or actions of the property 
owner or the applicant.

The property does not have sanitary sewer within the vicinity and due to the 
small size of the parcel as it currently exists, there is not enough land area to 
warrant the extension of sanitary sewer to this parcel as the cost of doing so 
would far outweigh the benefit of running the sanitary line. Further, in order to 
extend sanitary sewers, easements would be needed from several land owners. 
Therefore, due to the small size of the existing parcel and the lack of sanitary 
sewer, it is difficult to divide this property in such a way that the remaining lot can 
meet the width-to-depth ratio, especially given the limited road frontage. 
 
The proposed lot layout could be modified so that Lots 1 and 2 comply with the 
ordinance, leaving only Lot 3 in need of a variance. The applicant's decision not 
to redesign these lots to meet the requirements of Section 27-280(b), which 
states that a lot's depth shall not exceed three (3) times its width, does not 
constitute a unique condition of the property. Rather, the need for the variance 
results from the proposed design, not from any physical characteristic or unique 
condition of the property. 
 
2. The granting of the permit for the variance will not adversely affect the 
rights of adjacent property owners or residents. 
 
Granting this variance will not adversely impact the rights of adjacent property 
owners as each lot will remain at least one (1) acre in size with one (1) single-
family home located upon the each lot. 
 
3. The strict application of the provisions of the zoning ordinance of which 
variance is requested will constitute unnecessary hardship upon the 
property owner represented in the application. 
 
Due to the parcel's limited road frontage and existing depth, it is not feasible to 
divide the property into three lots while maintaining the required width-to-depth 
ratio for all three parcels. However, the lot configuration could be redesigned so 
that Lots 1 and 2 comply with the ordinance, leaving only Lot 3 in need of a 
variance. While denying the variance would prevent the property from being 
divided in the manner proposed by the applicant, it would not preclude a revised 
design that reduces the number of variances required. 
 
4. The variance desired will not adversely affect the public health, safety, 
morals, order, convenience, prosperity, or general welfare. 
 
The variance will not adversely affect the public health, safety, morals, order etc. 
of the surrounding property owners. 
 
5. The granting of the variance desired will not be opposed to the general 
spirit and intent of the zoning ordinance. 
 
Granting this variance is opposed to the zoning ordinance of Section 27-280(b) 
states that the depth of a lot shall not exceed three (3) times the width.

KANSAS STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS

BOZA2026-013 August 10, 2026 4 
 
This appeal has been filed to grant a variance from the maximum number of wall 
signs per business. Section 27-728(b)(8)b, Table VIII-11-7.4 states that for a 
multi-tenant building, one (1) wall sign is allowed for each business or institution 
that has its own ground floor entryway or storefront. It is being proposed to have  
three (3) wall signs on the three (3) exterior walls of the corner commercial 
tenant space leased by Daves Hot Chicken, one (1) wall sign on each exterior 
wall, except the east façade, where there will be no signs. The building as a 
whole is a multi-tenant commercial building and Daves hot Chicken has a 
separate entrance for its tenant space; therefore, only one (1) wall sign is 
allowed for the tenant. While the second tenant, Panera, has also applied and 
been approved for a Variance in regards of signage quantity in July of 2025. 
 
2. The granting of the permit for the variance will not adversely affect the 
rights of adjacent property owners or residents. 
 
The two additional wall signs are far more noticeable in their number than in 
their proportional increase in size. The applicant is also aligning their request 
with the precedent set by the neighboring tenant to the east, Panera, which 
received a similar Variance approval in July 2025.  
 
3. The strict application of the provisions of the zoning ordinance of which 
variance is requested will constitute unnecessary hardship upon the 
property owner represented in the application. 
 
The strict application of the provisions of the zoning ordinance would be a 
hardship on the applicant due to the number signs on the subject property. 
There are other businesses in the Village West area, albeit prior to 2016 sign 
code, which allowed multi-tenant buildings to have more than one (1) wall sign 
per tenant. A second and third wall sign, located on the rear façade that has no 
pedestrian access nor windows, is not necessary and therefore the denial of 
which does not constitute unnecessary hardship upon the property owner. 
 
4. The variance desired will not adversely affect the public health, safety, 
morals, order, convenience, prosperity, or general welfare. 
 
There are two (2) tenant spaces within the subject commercial building, each 
with their own separate entrances. Per the Sign Code, each tenant space is 
allowed a wall sign. Therefore, there are two (2) wall signs allowed on the 
subject building. If the requested variance is granted, then there will be a total of 
three (3) wall signs for the subject property. The variance request would place 
wall signs on the west and south facades rather than adding more wall signs to 
the north façade; while there is still a cluttering effect taking place with the 
number of signs on the building, Staff does not believe the request have an 
adverse effect on the public morals, order, prosperity, and general welfare. 
 
5. The granting of the variance desired will not be opposed to the general 
spirit and intent of the zoning ordinance. 
BOZA2026-013 August 10, 2026 5 
 
The spirit and intent of Section 27-728(b)(8)b, Table VIII-11-7.4 is to maintain the 
aesthetics of buildings within the Unified Government by limiting the number 
signs that can cover those buildings. As previously addressed in the analysis of 
Statutory Requirement #3, it is reasonable to expect that a corner tenant space 
with an entrance and/or storefront windows on two (2) facades would have an 
equivalent wall sign on each façade.  While allowing two (2) additional wall signs 
beyond the existing sign on the north façade at the main entrance, it does not 
conflict with the spirit and intent of the zoning ordinance.

KANSAS STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS 
 
1. The variance requested arises from such condition which is unique to the 
property in question and which is not ordinarily found in the same zone or 
district; and which is not created by an action or actions of the property 
owner or the applicant. 
 
Variances 1 – 14: These appeals have been filed to grant variances from 
variances maximums outlined in the sign ordinances, including height of banner 
signs, accessory signs, number of wall signs, square footage of wall signs, 
number of projecting signs, cumulative square footage of incidental signs, 
quantity and size of accessory signs, and number of monument signs. While all 
commercial properties within Kansas City, Kansas are subject to the sign 
ordinance, the development of the subject property is of a larger scale than most 
agricultural properties, as they are cumulative 400,000 square foot barns and 
arena, rather than a standard commercial business, which necessitates the 
need for additional signage, be it to help guide guests to and through the site, or 
to advertise the additional uses occurring on the site. Additionally, the subject 
property is located in close proximity to the Village West Entertainment District, 
where several other similar uses, such as The Legends (Tanger Outlets), 
Sporting Park, Bass Pro Shops, Nebraska Furniture Mart, Hollywood Casino, 
and the Kansas Speedway have been granted either sign variances, or Special 
Use Permits for their signage per Section 27-739. As a result of the scale of the 
business, and the allowance for non-compliant signage in the surrounding 
region, the variance stems from a unique condition. 
 
2. The granting of the permit for the variance will not adversely affect the 
rights of adjacent property owners or residents. 
 
Variances 1 – 14: The granting of variances for non-conforming signage does 
not have the potential to impact nearby residences, as they are further north 
along North 118th Street, closer to Parallel Parkway than American Royal Way or 
State Avenue. The impacts of this signage, however, would be mitigated by 
other additional regulations, such as limits on internal illumination, cut-off fixtures 
for lighting, and the location of non-compliant signage. 
 
3. The strict application of the provisions of the zoning ordinance of which 
variance is requested will constitute unnecessary hardship upon the 
property owner represented in the application. 
 
Variances 1 – 14: Strict application of the zoning ordinance would require the 
proposed use to meet the criteria of the sign ordinance in its entirety, including 
limits on the number of wall signs to one (1) per building, limits of the cumulative 
size of the wall signs to 50 square feet, a limit of one (1) monument sign, limit to 
BOZA2026-017 August 10, 2026 7 
one (1) projecting sign, limits to incidental signs to 36 square feet, limit of one 
(1) banner sign, and limits to the size and height of accessory signs to 9 square 
feet and two (2) feet in height. While the requirement to follow the sign 
ordinance is not an unnecessary hardship and is a known requirement for any 
development, a hardship is created when comparing the subject development to 
surrounding developments (i.e. the Hollywood Casino, Margaritaville, and Buc-
ee’s Travel Cetner), which have been afforded generous sign rights, either 
through variances or Special Use Permits for larger signage. Requiring this 
particular large venue to meet the sign code requirements, while other similarly 
scaled projects have allowances for non-conforming signs, would constitute an 
unnecessary hardship. 
 
4. The variance desired will not adversely affect the public health, safety, 
morals, order, convenience, prosperity, or general welfare. 
 
Variances 1 – 14: The granting of variances for non-conforming signage is not 
expected to adversely affect the public health, safety, morals, order, 
convenience, prosperity or general welfare. Any potential impacts would be 
mitigated by other additional regulations, such as limits on internal illumination, 
cut-off fixtures for lighting, and the location of non-compliant signage. 
 
5. The granting of the variance desired will not be opposed to the general 
spirit and intent of the zoning ordinance. 
 
Variances 1 – 14: The spirit and intent of the limits to the number of signs 
allowed is to prevent the over-abundance and overcrowding of signage, as well 
as the prevent signage from dominating the facades, site, or frontages of a 
commercial business. While the proposed sign package exceeds these 
allowances in various aspects, when accounting for the size and scale of the 
proposed use, an indoor/outdoor multi-use, agricultural-related complex, with 
numerous barns and arena, the proposed sign package is shown to be designed 
in a manner that still meets the spirit and intent of the ordinance, while 
accommodating for the various uses on-site.
