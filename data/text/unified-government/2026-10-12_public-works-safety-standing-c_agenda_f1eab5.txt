Item 4.4:  UPDATE:  POLICE DEPARTMENT – REAL-TIME INTELLIGENCE CRIME 
CENTER (RTICC), LICENSE PLATE READERS (LPR) & DRONE (Discussion Begins 
14:30) 
Chief Karl Oakman provided a comprehensive overview of the Kansas City, Kansas, Police 
Department's (KCKPD) Real-Time Crime Center (RTCC) and associated technologies.  KCKPD 
was one of the first agencies in the metropolitan area — behind Kansas City, Missouri, — to 
establish an RTCC, beginning operations in 2022 and becoming fully established in 2024.  This 
initiative was part of his strategic plan upon becoming Chief in June 2021, and that the 
Page | 5 
department has advanced from lagging behind most metro departments in technology to being 
one of the most advanced, largely through the use of federal grants. 
The following components were presented: 
• License Plate Readers (LPRs / ALPRs):  Chief Oakman explained that the department 
has used ALPRs since 2020 and has since expanded its program.  Three vendors are 
currently in use:  Genetec, Axon, and Flock.  LPRs are deployed in three configurations:  
vehicle-mounted, fixed cameras, and mobile trailers.  Chief Oakman clarified that LPRs 
are cameras that take still photographs, not video, and are distinct from the department's 
video camera network. 
• The Unified Government (UG) operates approximately 550 total cameras, fed through the 
RTCC, covering UG buildings, parks, intersections, traffic cameras, and select public 
areas such as Azura and Kansas Speedway.  KCKPD owns 215 video cameras covering 
KCK buildings and street-level locations. 
• Furis Core Box:  A device provided to businesses (e.g., convenience stores, apartment 
complexes, schools) that enables existing cameras of any brand to become "smart 
cameras" integrated into the RTCC via a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU).  
Currently, 193 cameras are integrated through the Fusis system. 
• Connect KCK (Camera Registry Program):  A voluntary program allowing residents to 
register their personal cameras (e.g., Ring cameras) with the police department.  The 
department does not have direct access to view registered cameras but uses the registry to 
identify nearby residents who have agreed to share footage voluntarily in the event of an 
incident.  As of the meeting date, 300 residents have registered for the program. 
• Access Controls and Policy Safeguards: 
A. Only sworn officers and civilians specifically assigned to the RTCC have access 
to the LPR system. 
B. All searches are logged and require a stated purpose and an active case number. 
C. Partner agencies must also supply a case number to access shared data. 
D. Flock has implemented updated controls allowing agencies to specifically 
determine which other agencies they share ALPR data with. 
E. Officers undergo annual technology awareness training covering guidelines 
equivalent to those of the Kansas Criminal Justice Information Systems (KCJIS). 
F. ALPR data is currently retained for 30 days via Flock (minimum now 
configurable to 7 days; Genetec's maximum retention is 2 years, currently under 
review). 
G. Violations of ALPR policy may result in discipline, termination, or criminal 
charges. 
Page | 6 
H. Two independent audits are conducted:  one through the IT Unit and one through 
a full-time body-worn camera reviewer who monitors officer use of LPRs as part 
of daily review. 
• Recent Case Example:  Chief Oakman highlighted a recent double homicide at Seventh 
and Nebraska.  Within 10 to 30 minutes of the incident, RTCC cameras identified the 
suspect, and ALPRs tracked the suspect's vehicle to an address in Kansas City, Missouri.  
Using data-sharing agreements with Kansas City, Missouri, law enforcement was able to 
make an arrest within six hours of the incident — a process that previously would have 
taken three days to a week. 
• Drone Program:  KCKPD launched its drone program approximately two years ago and 
now operates 11 drones total, including Drones as First Responders (DFR), which are 
dispatched from dedicated drone boxes at fixed locations, as well as mobile drones 
stationed at each patrol station.  All DFR operations are managed from the RTCC.  The 
department maintains approximately 15 trained drone pilots. 
• Coverage maps displayed showed overlapping coverage with Kansas City, Missouri's 
drone program, illustrating mutual aid capability.  Drones are used for officer safety, 
EMS support, situational awareness, disturbances, suspicious vehicles and persons, and 
area checks. 
2026 Drone Usage Statistics: 
A. 1,000 total deployments 
B. 257 total flight hours 
C. Average of 12 flights per day 
• DJI Drone Waiver:  The department has received a state waiver to continue using DJI 
drones, which are currently the most advanced in camera capability.  The department 
uses American software — not Chinese software — with the DJI hardware, as no 
comparable American drone has yet matched DJI's camera imagery and capacity. 
• Gunshot Detection:  The department uses Flock's gunshot detection system — a separate 
product from Flock's ALPR offerings.  The system relies on 227 audio detection devices 
across three coverage areas:   (1) downtown to Central Avenue corridor, (2) the northeast 
corridor, and (3) an area around 78th Street and State Avenue in midtown.  Exact device 
locations are not disclosed for officer safety reasons. 
A. The system is not an active listening device — it does not record live audio and 
does not capture human voices.  It is triggered only by specific sounds:  gunshots, 
crashes, fireworks, and loud sideshow-type activity.  The system was funded 
through the Crime Gun Intelligence Center (CGIC) grant. 
• Cell Phone Forensics:  The department uses Cellebrite and GrayKey to preserve and 
analyze evidence on phones recovered during active criminal investigations.  All content 
extraction is conducted pursuant to a lawful warrant in accordance with rules of evidence, 
Page | 7 
including probable cause requirements.  Data types extracted include text messages, 
phone calls, photos, app data, and location information — including data that may have 
been deleted by the suspect. 
• Cell Phone Forensics Statistics: 
A. 253 phones received 
B. 173 phones with completed download analysis 
• Gun Crime Intelligence Center (GCIC):  In 2022, KCKPD was awarded a federal grant of 
$700,000 to establish one of 54 Gun Crime Intelligence Centers in the country.  KCKPD 
is one of only two in the metropolitan area (the other being Kansas City, Missouri) and 
one of only two in the state of Kansas (the other being Wichita). 
A. The center coordinates intelligence hubs for local, state, and federal responses to 
mass shootings and other firearm-related crimes, including firearms analytics in 
partnership with the National Public Safety Institute and the Bureau of Alcohol, 
Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF).  The department uses the National 
Integrated Ballistic Information Network (NIBIN) to match shell casings from 
crime scenes to other incidents.  Chief Oakman noted that Kansas City, Kansas, 
had one of the highest shell casing match rates in the four-state area in the prior 
year, enabling the department to solve numerous crimes and link incidents 
together. 
• Counter-Drone Detection:  KCKPD is in partnership with Kansas City, Missouri, and has 
one of the most advanced drone detection systems in the metro area, including both 
mobile and fixed detection sites throughout the city.  The system is designed to detect, 
track, and mitigate unauthorized drone threats. 
A. FBI clearance is required to legally activate countermeasures.  This capability was 
funded through a $2.5 million federal grant.  The system has the technical 
capacity to counter potential drone threats from hostile actors. 
 
• Federal Grant Funding Summary (RTCC-Related): 
Grant Amount

Crime Gun Intelligence Center (CGIC) Federal Grant ~$700,000 
Counter-Drone Detection Federal Grant $2,500,000

(JAG) PROGRAM - LOCAL FORMULA  
BACKGROUND 
• The FY26 Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) program provides funding to state and local 
government agencies. Allocations are based on a formula that accounts for crime statistics and population data. 
• The PD has received a similar grant award for the past several years, dating back to at least FY2015. 
• The KCKPD has been allocated $108,838 in federal grant funding through this grant program for FY2026. The 
project period for this award spans from 10/1/25-9/30/29. 
• The PD plans to spend this funding on the following items: 
o Virtual Reality (VR) Taser/De-escalation Training 
o Server Infrastructure 
  
RECOMMENDATION 
Approve 
Fast Track 
 
It is requested that this item be fast tracked to the October 15, 2026 Board of Commissioners meeting.  
BUDGET IMPACTS / FINANCIAL CONSIDERATIONS 
• Both of the aforementioned projects will be 100% grant funded with no match requirement from the UG. 
o The VR program is subscription-based; future subscriptions will be funded with future JAG award 
dollars. 
o Since our server infrastructure CIP was cut for the 2027 budget year, we will use grant funding to 
supplement in order to complete this project as planned. 
  
LEGAL/ POLICY CONSIDERATIONS 
• As part of the grant application process, we will need the following forms reviewed by Legal and signed by the 
CAO: 
o Standard Form 424 (SF-424) 
o Disclosure of Lobbying (SF-LLL) 

o DOJ Certified Standard Assurances 
o DOJ Certifications Regarding Lobbying; Debarment, Suspension and other Responsibility Matters;
