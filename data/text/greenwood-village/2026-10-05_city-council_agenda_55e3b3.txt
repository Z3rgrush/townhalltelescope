Executive Summary: 
  
On August 3rd, 2026, City Council approved two emergency ordinances (08-26, 09-26) expanding the existing 
permit parking district in the Cherry Creek Village North (CCVN) neighborhood and establishing a new permit 
parking district in the Village on the Lake (VOL) neighborhood.  This was in anticipation of a significant parking 
shortage at Cherry Creek High School (CCHS) resulting from the Cherry Creek Campus Rebuild Project prior 
to the first day of school.  To date, a total of 88 warnings and 18 citations have been issued to non-permitted 
vehicles and permits have been issued to approximately 125 households across all of the parking districts. 
  
Recommendation: 
  
Staff is providing an update and seeking input from Council moving forward on this issue.

Discussion 
  
Following Council's approval of the two emergency ordinances regarding permit parking districts, staff drafted 
a message to the affected residents.  Staff also created highly visible temporary permits and mailed them, 
along with the drafted letter, to 208 households within the new district areas. The temporary permits were 
intended to allow ample time for residents to come to City Hall and obtain their permanent permits while still 
being able to park in the street.  Additionally, staff worked with HOA leadership within the CCVN and VOL 
neighborhoods, who each agreed to send the same message to residents via email or by other internal 
messaging means. Due to its proximity just east of the CCHS campus as well as being a brand-new district, 
staff deployed two message boards stationed at both entrances to VOL advising that student parking was 
prohibited. Staff also updated the public website and ensured messaging was sent out via the newsletter.  
 
When school began on August 17th, 2026, Code Enforcement Officers from the Police Department and from 
Neighborhood Services began a two-week educational campaign issuing 87 written warnings to vehicles 
parked in the districts without a permit.  At the end of the educational period, the message boards were pulled 
from the VOL entrances. On August 31st, 2026, staff began a strict enforcement campaign issuing citations to 
vehicles parked in the districts without a permit.  
 
In order to increase convenience for residents, police department staff modified their office hours on multiple 
occasions to remain open late so that residents could come to city hall and obtain their permits after normal 
business hours. Additionally, staff hosted a night at Village Greens Park, offering residents an additional option 
to obtain permits closer to their homes without needing to travel to city hall.  That evening, staff issued 12 
parking permits and 18 visitor passes to members of 9 different households.  In total, staff has issued permits 
to approximately 125 households across each of the parking districts since the August 3rd meeting.

Recently, staff developed and implemented a convenient, online application process for residents to apply for 
parking permits.  Residents can submit required proof of residency via the online form and, if approved, the 
permits are mailed directly to their homes. 
 
School Resource Officer (SRO) staff have remained engaged with Cherry Creek School District (CCSD) staff 
throughout the school year and have been able to manage normal operations without significant disruption. 
SRO and Code Enforcement officers continue to conduct daily proactive patrols of the districts and have 
issued approximately 18 citations since August 31st. Approximately 7 weeks into the school year, staff has 
successfully managed the impacts of student parking within the neighborhood parking districts. 
 
Future Considerations: 
Since the beginning of the school year, CCSD has made a number of changes internally when it comes to 
handling student parking. They have contracted with local churches, are utilizing a number of parking spaces 
in Village Greens Park, hired crossing guards and security patrols to monitor parking lots and high-volume 
student crossing points and have recently begun enforcing student parking within Village Greens Park by 
booting vehicles without proper student parking permits.   
 
The potential impact on neighborhood parking over time is unknown and difficult to predict.  It's plausible to 
see changes throughout the school year and into the future impacted by winter weather, variations in student 
parking permit issuances, changes in CCSD policy, and changes in the ongoing construction project and 
losses or additions to off-campus parking spots.  The permit parking districts enable the police department to 
manage residential parking impacts by students which otherwise would not be possible.  Thus far, staff has 
taken a proactive approach to education and enforcement, however, over time, it is expected that staff could 
take on a "complaint-based" approach to enforcement. The existing parking districts in Cherry Creek Village 
South (CCVS) and the southern portion of CCVN have been managed this way for years.  As residents and 
students become familiar with the parking districts over time, this approach has worked well in balancing the 
needs of residents while holding students accountable for parking on neighborhood streets.

A Bill for an Ordinance 
Ordinance No. 08                                     Series of 2026 
Introduced by: Councilmember Stahl 
An Emergency Ordinance Establishing a Parking Permit District for the Village on 
the Lake Neighborhood 
Whereas, the Cherry Creek High School Campus Rebuild Project (“Project”) will result in 
a significant parking shortage beginning August 17, 2026, the first day of school, with parking 
demand expected to exceed supply on that campus by hundreds of spaces for the duration of the 
Project; and 
Whereas, pursuant to Chapter 8, Article 3 of the Greenwood Village Code, the City has 
previously established parking permit districts north and south of that campus to address 
impacts, and the Village on the Lake neighborhood and other public roads in the area are now at 
risk of hazardous traffic conditions and adverse impacts due to their proximity to the Project 
and lack of existing protections; and 
Whereas, the City Council finds that: 
a. The Village on the Lake neighborhood is likely to be substantially impacted by parking 
related to Cherry Creek High School, especially during the course of the Project; 
b. The anticipated overflow parking will result in hazardous traffic conditions, including 
congestion, reduced sight distance, and emergency access limitations; 
c. Increased vehicle volumes and parking will result in excessive noise, air pollution, and 
litter, adversely affecting neighborhood quality of life; 
d. Residents and their visitors will have difficulty parking near their homes during school 
hours; and 
e. Establishing a permit parking district will mitigate these impacts, preserve public 
safety, and promote the welfare of residents. 
Whereas, Section 6.05 of the City’s Home Rule Charter authorizes emergency ordinances 
when necessary for the immediate preservation of public property, health, peace, or safety, and 
the facts showing such urgency are stated herein; and 
Whereas, City Council further finds that a regular ordinance process pursuant to Chapter 
8, Article 3 would not take effect until after the first day of school, leaving City residents exposed 
to the very risks the permit district is intended to mitigate, and immediate action is necessary to 
preserve the health, safety, and welfare. 
Now, therefore, the City Council of the City of Greenwood Village, 
Colorado, Ordains: 
Section 1. Parking Permit District for Village on the Lake Neighborhood Established on 
an Emergency Basis.  
Page 6 
A parking permit district is hereby established for the Village on the Lake neighborhood, as 
depicted on the  map in Exhibit A, attached hereto and incorporated herein (the “District”), 
effective immediately upon adoption of this ordinance. 
Section 2. District Limitations. 
Motor vehicle parking on public roadways within the district is prohibited at all times except 
when the motor vehicle has affixed to the windshield a resident permit issues pursuant to 
Section 3 below or has hung on the rear-view mirror a visitor permit or temporary visitor permit 
issued pursuant to Section 4 below. 
Section 3. Resident Permits. 
The owner, owners, lessee or lessees of each residential unit within the district may be issued a 
resident permit at no cost by the Police Department for each motor vehicle registered to the 
address of the residential unit. The permit shall consist of a numbered decal which shall be 
permanently affixed to the lower left-hand portion of the rear windshield of the vehicle. 
Section 4. Visitor Permits. 
The owner of each residential dwelling unit within the District shall also be issued at no cost 
three (3) reusable visitor permits coded by address allowing the motor vehicle in which it is 
displayed to be parked on public roadways within the district. The permit shall be hung on the 
rear-view mirror of the motor vehicle to which it is assigned. Additional visitor permits may be 
issued by the Police Department upon a satisfactory showing of need and may be issued on a 
temporary basis only. 
Section 5. Effective Date. 
This ordinance is enacted as an emergency ordinance in accordance with Section 6.05 of the 
City’s Home Rule Charter, and is necessary for the immediate preservation of the public health, 
safety, and welfare of the citizens and residents of the City. This ordinance shall take effect 
immediately upon adoption and shall be repealed on the earlier to occur of: (1) 180 days from 
the date of adoption; or (2) the adoption of an ordinance in the regular course of the City’s 
business which contains provisions establishing or amending the permit district herein. 
Introduced and approved on the first and final reading on the 3rd day of 
August, 2026, and ordered published.
