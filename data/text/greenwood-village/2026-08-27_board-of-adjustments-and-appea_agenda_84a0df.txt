Analysis 
Staﬀ has reviewed the request to modify the principal structure exceeding the side principal structure setback 
and ﬁnds that the site's shape and orientation create an undue hardship that does not meet the Approval Criteria 
for relief from the side yard setback regulations established in the Monaco Height PUD.   
While the triangular shape of the parcel is unusual for the zoning district, the orientation of the existing principal 
structure, Monaco Height PUD setbacks  limits, plat and adjacent parcels, and the buildable area of the parc el 
does not prevent the Applicant from building a zoning-compliant garage addition.  
Staﬀ ﬁnds that the variance request is inconsistent with the intent and purpose of the R-1.0 zoning district. The 
intent of the R-1.0 zoning district is to emphasize open space (Exhibit 4), and while the shape and orientation of 
the parcel limit the Applicant from expanding the structure to the north , the Applicant has space to reduce the 
size of the proposed structure or move the structure into the rear yard. The proposed encroachment would create 
visual or privacy impacts beyond what is typical for structures within this zoning district and the Monaco Height 
PUD. While the proposed structure is one-story in height and is architecturally integrated into the structure, the 
proposed structure’s distance to the north property is not in accordance with the expected open space for the 
vicinity or zoning.  
In order to obtain a permit for the proposed garage addition, the Applicant must follow all other requirements 
of the City,  including any other regulations subject to the parcel outlaid by  the Land Development Code, 
Greenwood Village Drainage Criteria, and the currently adopted International Residential Code. These 
regulations will  ensure any impacts regarding drainage, public health, safety, and welfare are appropriately 
addressed at the permit review level. Staﬀ notes that the proposal would not result in signiﬁcant, unique, and/or 
chronic adverse impacts on the use of adjacent properties.

Prior to approval of a variance, the BOAA shall make ﬁndings concerning the criteria listed below. For a variance 
to be granted, the request must meet at least one Criterion from Section A and all of the Criteria from Section B, 
in the determination of the BOAA. Staﬀ’s ﬁndings are presented in the tables below:

garage addition; however, the parcel's shape 
constrains the buildable area and would require

accommodate a compliant location.

PLANNING AREA DESCRIPTION 
Planning Area Boundaries 
The Greenwood Hills planning area is generally bounded by Belleview Avenue on the 
north, Orchard Road on the south, Holly Street on the west, and Quebec Street on the 
east.  It also includes an existing shopping center south of Orchard Road at Holly 
Street. 
Planning Area History 
This planning area became part of the Village in 1975.  Smaller annexations completing 
the current makeup of the planning area occurred in 1966, 1978, 1979, and 1989.

GREENWOOD HILLS CHARACTER 
 
Natural Environment 
The extensive natural environment found in this planning area 
includes native plantings wetlands, and open meadows. 
 
Built Environment 
Greenwood Hills’ residents value the low density zoning, natural 
and maintained open spaces, wildlife, and semi-rural character of 
the streets.  Extensive wetlands and natural drainage corridors, 
with healthy riparian vegetation exists throughout the community. 
 Land Uses:  The planning area is comprised of single family 
residential dwellings.  At the southwest corner of this 
planning area is a commercial shopping district located at 
the southwest corner of Holly and Orchard.  In the northeast 
corner of the planning area is a series of low-rise commercial 
office buildings.  Two churches operate in the planning area 
on residential zoned land with special use permits. 
 Residential Density:  This low density, single family 
neighborhood consists of a range of densities, generally 
consisting of lots just below one acre up to 2.5 acres. 
 Street Characteristics:  Paved streets meander throughout 
the planning area.  Valley pans and grass drainage ditches 
are most common, with curb and gutter on Monaco Street 
and the perimeter streets. 
 Landscape:  A combination of natural and manicured 
vegetation exists throughout the planning area.

THE GREENWOOD HILLS PLAN 
Planning Area Vision 
The planning area will retain its low density residential and 
attractive large-lot and rural character.  Mature vegetation 
located in older neighborhoods will be preserved, while 
additional trees and other landscape enhancements will be 
located in developing areas.  Significant views of the Rocky 
Mountains will remain an important feature of this planning 
area. 
Planning for the community should entail all possible 
elements to protect tranquility, security, and quality of life 
from the impacts of transient automobiles, pedestrians and 
bicycle traffic, and from pollution, stormwater run-off, 
flooding, noise, and lights.  The safety of children traveling to 
and from school shall be a paramount concern in trail 
planning, maintenance and improvements.  No further 
nonresidential development (except open space) shall occur 
in the planning area. 
 
Planning Area Goals, Objectives, & Policies 
The goals/objectives have been associated with four primary 
categories for a residential planning area, including: land 
use, transportation, community services and amenities, and 
natural environment.  Policy direction is provided, where 
appropriate, to further define the needs and interests of the 
planning area.

Analysis 
Staﬀ have reviewed the request to modify the principal structure exceeding the side principal structure setback 
and ﬁnd that the site's shape and orientation create an undue hardship that meets the Approval Criteria for relief 
from the side yard setback regulations established in section 16-8-40 of the LDC.  
With respect to existing site conditions, Staﬀ acknowledges that the home was built before annexation into the 
City of Greenwood Village, being platted and built within the previous Arapahoe County development standards 
at the time (1959).  
The irregular shape of the plat, location of the existing principal structure, and the R-1.0 Zoning District setbacks 
limit the buildable area of the parcel to 17% of the total property area. While the zoning district is meant to 
emphasize open space, the shape and area of the speciﬁc parcel limit the ability of the homeowner to modify a 
substantial portion of the home without reducing the size of the home . Staﬀ ﬁnds that the variance request is 
consistent with the intent and purpose of the R-1.0 zoning district. 
While the Applicant requests to increase the nonconforming portion of the principal structure in regard to area, 
the request results in a reduction in the total linear encroachment of the principal structure by 3 feet. Much of 
the area proposed to be encroaching in the side yard setback is for the construction of a covered patio, which has 
less visual impact on adjacent neighbors than the existing structure currently within the side yard.  The proposed 
encroachment would not create visual or privacy impacts beyond what is typical for structures within this zoning 
district. 
In order to obtain a permit for the proposed structure, the Applicant must follow all other requirements of the 
City, including any other regulations subject to the parcel outlaid by the Land Development Code, Greenwood 
Village Drainage Criteria, and the currently adopted International Residential Code. These regulations will 
ensure any impacts regarding drainage, public health, safety, and welfare are appropriately addressed at the 
permit review level. Staﬀ notes that the proposal would not result in signiﬁcant, unique, and/or chronic adverse 
impacts on the use of adjacent properties.
