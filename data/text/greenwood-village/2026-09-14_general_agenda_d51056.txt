Background      
 
• The zoning of the property is Town Center (T.C.), and the Subject Property is located in the Interstate 
Corridor Area of the City’s Comprehensive Plan (Attachment 4). Adjacent properties to the west and 
north are also zoned T.C., while the property to the south is zoned Business Professional (B-1). Adjacent 
uses include Interstate 25, oﬃce, restaurants, and residential.  
• The lots comprising the Subject Property were annexed in 1975. The 1975 Annexation was a large 
annexation that included properties from Holly Street on the west to Dayton Street on the east.  
• The Landmark Master Development Plan (MDP) was approved by the City Council in 2007. The MDP’s 
design standards are intended to supplement the City of Greenwood Village’s codes, ordinances, and 
other development standards. Its development standards serve to enhance the quality of life, minimize 
adverse impacts from development, protect economic vitality, encourage high-quality development, and 
discourage less attractive and less enduring development alternatives.  
• Where the MDP’s development standards conﬂict with the Land Development Code (LDC), the MDP’s 
standards shall supersede the LDC.  
• The Landmark SDP was amended once in March of 2014 to consolidate SDP retail parcels 3, 4, 5, and 6, 
and it was also amended in July of that same year to update the coversheet’s land use table.  
• The subject property is parcel 5, lot 7. It has been anticipated as retail. 
• Per Section 16-23-40 (b), the Neighborhood Input Meeting (NIM) requirement was waived by the 
Community Development Director as the Landmark MDP anticipated the proposed use when it was 
approved back in 2007.  
• On July 21, 2026 the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval for the 
SDP. The Planning and Zoning Commission is not a recommending body for Development 
Improvement Agreements (DIA). 
Applicant Request     
Site Development Plan & Development Improvement Agreement  
Construction of one approximately 9,800-square-foot commercial building, surface parking, landscape 
improvements, lighting, loading/valet area, rooftop screening, and a solar canopy. Public Improvements 
include alterations to the curb and gutter. The existing curb and gutter line will be demolished and replaced 
along Greenwood Plaza Boulevard to accommodate (3) parking spaces and a valet zone. Interspersed 
between the parking areas will be lawn and planting areas. Two new curb cuts will be constructed to allow 
access to the parking areas on the site. One at the southwest corner of the property along Greenwood Plaza 
Boulevard, and one at the northeast corner of the property. Along Greenwood Plaza Boulevard, the sidewalk 
will be reconstructed, with new ADA compliant ramps at the curb cut. 
Exceptions Requested: 
1. The proposed development will not deviate from the Land Development Code/Site Development 
Plan, except for one exception. The lighting fixtures presented by the Landmark Site Development 
Plan are over a decade old and the fixtures provided are no longer being produced. In-lieu of the SDP 
specified fixtures, currently available light fixtures that match the optics and visual appearance of the 
SDP fixtures as closely as possible are proposed. 
Analysis    
Comprehensive Plan 
The Corridor vision identiﬁes the area as the Village’s primary employment district and calls for a mix of low- 
to mid-density oﬃce, institutional, and civic uses supported by retail and restaurant amenities, with high 
standards for site planning, architecture, landscaping, lighting, and signage. Conformance with the 
Comprehensive Plan is part of the review criteria for Site Development Plans. Staﬀ ﬁnds that the proposed 
development conforms with the relevant Comprehensive Plan goals and policies as outlined for the Interstate 
Corridor Planning Area (Attachment 4). The plan supports complementary retail and restaurant amenities.

Compatibility of Surrounding Land Uses  
The proposed development will be consistent with the surrounding commercial uses found adjacent to the 
Subject Property. Restaurants, retail stores, residential, and oﬃce uses are located in the general vicinity of the 
Landmark. Residential uses are adjacent to the Subject Property to the southwest across Greenwood Plaza 
Blvd, approximately 300 feet from the development site. Due to design standards speciﬁcally intended to 
mitigate adverse impacts, Staﬀ does not expect these residential properties to be impacted disproportionately 
by an additional restaurant locating in an existing retail center. The proposed building is proportional to 
existing buildings within the Landmark and the Interstate Corridor concerning height, Net Floor Area (NFA), 
number of stories, and ﬁts within the built environment of the Interstate Corridor Planning Area. 
Building Architecture  
The proposed building architecture is comprised of high-quality modern design elements that meet the intent 
of the LDC’s design standards. The elevations and material board identify three primary building materials are 
utilized: limestone veneer with reclaimed brick, transparent storefront glazing (glass), and Corten steel (both 
corrugated and ﬂat). The MDP encourages building massing to be broken into smaller components through 
vertical and horizontal articulation. The submitted elevations and 3D views show changes in plane, varied 
materials, parapet and roof-screen elements, glazing, and articulated façade bays. The proposed building 
consists of prominently street-facing entries. The mechanical equipment located on the roof would be screened 
by corrugated Corten steel screen panels that complement the rest of the building and are of suﬃcient height. 
Venting and rooftop elements are grouped within an organized roof composition. The trash enclosure would be 
housed in a conditioned interior trash room that is fully enclosed and concealed from exterior views. The 
proposal exceeds the baseline requirement for concealment because refuse storage is internal to the building. 
Any exterior service doors are architecturally integrated. The solar collection equipment would be installed 
with an antireﬂection coating and is architecturally coordinated with the main building. 
Open Space and Landscaping 
The proposed development meets the required amount of open space of the T.C. zone district and the Master 
Development Plan providing 19,213 square feet of open space for a coverage amount of 39% where 25% of the 
master planned area is required and 30% is required by the T.C. zone district. The proposed landscaping 
includes perimeter and frontage landscaping and the Landmark MDP emphasizes climate-adapted planting, 
tree planting along street frontage, tree lawns/tree grates, and a comfortable pedestrian streetscape. The 
proposed landscaping includes native/low-water seed mixes, ornamental grasses, shrubs, deciduous and 
evergreen trees, irrigation, seating, and pottery planters. It is also worth noting that 14 trees are provided along 
approximately 320 linear feet of frontage where 8 trees are required.  
Lighting 
The MDP calls for site lighting that supports pedestrian activity while limiting nuisance and spillover. The 
application includes a photometric plan and lighting details, with lighting distributed along walkways, parking, 
and entries. The SDP’s photometric plan includes ﬁve (5) surface parking area light poles, all of which are new. 
The LDC limits parking lot light pole height for commercial properties adjacent to residential properties to 
eighteen (18) feet. A mounting height of eighteen (18) feet is being proposed. The proposed light pole ﬁxture 
luminaire adheres to the requirements outlined in the LDC’s site and design standards with regard to color 
temperature (4000k) and ﬁxture type (sharp cut-oﬀ type, with a ﬂat lens).  
There are three (3) surface-mounted downlights proposed in the solar collection canopy. The LDC requires that 
light levels in parking lots, parking structures, and pedestrian walkways shall not exceed a maximum foot-
candle ratio diﬀerence of four to one (4:1) from the brightest areas to the darkest areas, which is being met. 
The photometric plan complies with the recommended minimum and maximum foot candles for building 
entrances, parking areas, and loading areas and all lighting is proposed at a 3,000 Kelvin color temperature, 
which is below the 4000 Kelvin maximum for retail adjacent to residential. The Applicant has eliminated any 
light spill greater than 0.2-foot candles at 5 feet across the property line.

Access and Parking 
The MDP seeks to minimize curb cuts and expressly envisions shared/cross parking allocations among parcels. 
The site plan provides one organized access pattern from Greenwood Plaza Boulevard, 39 on -site spaces, and a 
note allocating 49 oﬀ-site spaces in the Landmark parking structure. The Subject Property can be accessed from 
the northeast and southwest corners. No new access points into the Landmark development are being proposed.  
The MDP discourages parking from overwhelming the street environment and requires pedestrian access from 
parking areas to buildings and sidewalks. Parking is placed primarily to the side/northeast of the building rather 
than between the building and the street, and the plan provides sidewalks, curb ramps, and an accessible route 
to the main entry. Staﬀ ﬁnds that the intent of the MDP is being met as the parking layout is subordinate to the 
street frontage and supports pedestrian movement.  
Traﬃc Impacts 
Per the Greenwood Village Transportation Impact Study Standards, the A pplicant submitted a Traﬃc Impact 
Study Waiver Request (Attachment 6) for the proposed 9,800 NFA restaurant. The proposed development was 
included in the original traﬃc analysis for the Landmark development, with traﬃc associated with the site 
incorporated into the overall trip generation assumptions for the development. The proposed use does not alter 
the approved access conﬁguration, roadway or intersection geometry, or circulation system serving the site, and 
the anticipated increase in traﬃc relative to the previously analyzed development is not expected to materially 
impact the local or regional transportation system. Additionally, the restaurant will not operate during the AM 
peak period, resulting in minimal AM peak-hour impacts, particularly compared with traﬃc previously analyzed 
for the Landmark development.  Public Works staﬀ reviewed and concurred with the ﬁndings and 
recommendations of the waiver request and supported the Community Development Director’s approval of the 
waiver.  
Drainage 
The Applicant prepared a ﬁnal drainage report for the proposed development (Attachment 7). The Community 
Development Engineer has reviewed the drainage report  for the development and ﬁnds that the proposed 
drainage design meets the requirements of the Drainage Criteria Manual. 
The development is classiﬁed as a Tier 3 development with land disturbance over 1 acre. Drainage from this site 
was considered as part of the Final Drainage Report for the Landmark , originally prepared in 2007 by Western 
Engineering Consultants. In the Landmark Final Drainage Report, the Brinkerhoﬀ Restaurant site falls within 
basin IJ, which was designed to have a developed imperviousness of up to 82.3% for the site. The proposed 
development complies with the assumptions stated in the Final Drainage Report, with an overall site 
imperviousness of 78.7% and generally follows historic patterns. Flows from the site will be collected in the 
proposed on-site storm sewer where they will ultimately be conveyed to the existing detention and water quality 
pond on the southern side of the lot. Tier 3 development requires water quality and ﬂood attenuation to be 
provided. Given the application proposes a site imperviousness less than what was originally anticipated for the 
basin, the overall Landmark drainage design and existing sub -regional detention pond can suﬃciently 
accommodate the development and satisfy the requirements of the Greenwood Village Drainage Criteria Manual.

THE CORRIDOR PLANNING AREA VISION 
The Corridor, as a whole, serves as the primary employment district 
within the Village.  This area provides a diverse economic and 
employment base.  The concept of the Corridor planning area is to 
contain a mix of low to mid-density office, institutional and civic uses 
supported by retail and restaurant amenities.  The existing residential is a 
supporting use to the commercial/employment base. `The Corridor has a 
background dominated by open areas, quality landscaping, scenic views 
and plazas.  The vision preserves Colorado view corridors that attract and 
serve people who desire to work in an area that maximizes the unique 
experience of Colorado culture and natural beauty.  The Corridor sets 
high standards in site planning, architecture, landscaping, lighting, and 
signage amidst an open space, park-like setting with tree-lined streets.

• Encourage development that supports a diverse economic and 
employment base.

commercial and employment aspects of the Corridor Planning Area. 
Policies

the business office community and the Village residents.

POTENTIAL IMPACTS on the LOCAL and REGIONAL TRANSPORTATION 
SYSTEM 
For trip distribution, at least 80% of site traffic is expected to enter and exit through the 
intersection of Greenwood Plaza Blvd. and Berry Avenue. Approximately 40% is 
expected to travel to and from the west via Berry Avenue and Quebec Street, and another 
40% to and from the south via Greenwood Plaza Blvd. and Arapahoe Road. This 
distribution would add 22 vehicles per hour during the PM peak hour to both the 
eastbound left-turn and northbound right-turn movements, and 14 vehicles per hour to 
both the southbound right-turn and southbound left-turn movements. Synchro v12 
analysis indicates that the critical southbound left-turn movement currently operates at 
LOS C, with a control delay of 22.7 seconds per vehicle. In the future, the intersection 
will operate with all-way stop control (AWSC). With site-generated traffic and AWSC, 
the total delay in seconds per vehicle reports 13.4 seconds and LOS B. This change of 
traffic control indicates a negligible impact on the intersection. When site-generated 
traffic is distributed to the signalized intersections at Quebec St./Berry Ave. and 
Arapahoe Road/Greenwood Plaza Blvd., the impact is virtually nonexistent. Synchro 
reports are attached.
