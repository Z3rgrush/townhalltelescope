  B. PZ-26-00033 Variance At 11 Sunset Ln (2026-202)  
 
Staff Presentation: 
Jonathan Mehki, Planner I, presented the case. He stated that notice of the date, time, and place of the 
hearing was published on the City website at least 15 days prior to the hearing, as required by the 
Greenwood Village Municipal Code. Notice the hearing was also posted on the property for at least 15 
days prior to the hearing, as required by the Greenwood Village Municipal Code. Notice of the date, time, 
and place of the hearing was also sent to property owners as required by the Greenwood Village 
Municipal Code for this type of hearing at least 15 days prior to the hearing. 
Planner Mehki stated that the parcel is zoned R-1.0, is currently used as a single-family residence, and is 
located in the West End Planning Area of the Greenwood Village Comprehensive Plan. He stated the 
home was originally built in 1959 and was later incorporated into the City. He stated the home is legally 
nonconforming with regard to setbacks, with the existing principal structure encroaching into both the 
front and side setbacks. He stated that from all available data, the current footprint of the home has 
remained since at least 1980. The current owners purchased the property in March of 2026. 
Planner Mehki presented site photos of the front yard, the side yard from the driveway, the rear facade, 
the existing sun room, and addition located in the north side yard, which is the area under discussion. 
Planner Mehki stated that the applicant is requesting approval of a variance to the principal structure 
setbacks as established in Section 16-8-40 of the Land Development Code (LDC). He stated the total 
side yard setback area encroached would be 17% of the northern side yard, 17 feet at the closest point 
to the side yard property line, and 1,303 square feet of total encroachment. He continued by stating that 
no changes are requested to the rear yard or the southern portion of the yard. He stated that the 
applicant is looking to reduce the total distance of the encroachment while increasing the net area of the 
encroachment. 
Planner Mehki stated that in order for the Board to grant a variance to a development standard outlined 
in the R-1.0 zoning district, at least one of the Section A criteria must be found in the affirmative and all 
Section B criteria must be found in the affirmative. 
Planner Mehki stated that staff found criteria A1 in the affirmative, as special conditions exist on the 
property due to the unusual orientation of the plot and structure, along with the size of the parcel. He 
stated that Staff found criteria A3 in the affirmative, as the difficulty results from the property's pre-
existing legal nonconformity as the structure was built in 1959 under prior Arapahoe County standards on 
an irregularly shaped lot. 
Planner Mehki stated that staff found all three Section B criteria in the affirmative. Staff found the 
harmony and beauty of the neighborhood will not be impacted, as many nearby properties have 
nonconforming principal structures and the variance reduces the linear encroachment on the existing 
home. Staff found the use of adjacent properties was not injured, as the proposed patio has less visual 
impact than the existing encroaching structure and the overall linear encroachment of the principal 
structure is reduced by three feet. Staff found the variance would not harm the public health, safety, or 
welfare. 
Planner Mehki stated that staff is recommending approval of the variance request. 
Applicant Presentation: 
Mackenzie Woodley, 41 Preserve Parkway North, Greenwood Village, CO 80121, explained that the 
applicant work with an architectural group and have completed many homes in the Greenwood Village

  C. PZ-26-00043 Variance At 9424 E Orchard Dr (2026-196)  
 
Staff Presentation: 
Planner Nehl presented the case. He stated that notice of the date, time, and place of the hearing was 
published on the City website at least 15 days prior to the hearing, as required by the Greenwood Village 
Municipal Code. Notice the hearing was also posted on the property for at least 15 days prior to the 
hearing, as required by the Greenwood Village Municipal Code. Notice of the date, time, and place of the 
hearing was also sent by mail to property owners as required by the Greenwood Village Municipal Code 
for this type of hearing at least 15 days prior to the hearing. 
Planner Nehl stated that the applicant, Taylor Carlile, was present and would present after the staff 
presentation. 
Planner Nehl stated that the property is zoned R-1.0 in the Orchard Hills PUD and is located in the 
Orchard Hills planning area of the Comprehensive Plan. He noted that the Orchard Hills PUD does not 
outline accessory structure setback requirements, and therefore planning staff resorted to the accessory 
structure setbacks outlined in the Land Development Code. The existing house was built in 1973 and the 
current homeowners purchased the property in 2023. 
Planner Nehl stated that the applicant is requesting a two-part variance to Section 16-22-10 of the Land 
Development Code to build a detached accessory dwelling unit with side and rear accessory structure 
setback encroachments on a property zoned R-1.0, which requires a minimum 20-foot side setback and 
a 25-foot rear setback. The proposed detached ADU would be set at 17 feet on the east side yard and 20 
feet on the north rear yard from the property lines, for a total of 177 square feet of the structure 
encroaching. 
Page 6 
City Council Agenda - Final August 27, 2026 
 
 
Planner Nehl stated that, as attached in the staff report, the homeowners provided a signed statement of 
use indicating compliance with Section 16-22-40, that no accessory building shall be used as a dwelling 
unit other than for guests staying fewer than 15 days in a 30-day period. 
Planner Nehl stated that the proposed detached structure will be 8.5 feet in height and 626 square feet. 
He noted that the allowed maximum height for accessory structures is 12 feet and that the proposal is 
therefore in compliance with the height standards outlined in the Land Development Code. 
Planner Nehl stated that the floor plan included in the proposed plans and provided in the staff report 
identifies the structure as an ADU. He explained that an ADU use is not outlined in the code, but that a 
typical ADU use is defined by having space for living, sleeping, cooking, and bathrooms independent of 
the main residence, and deferred to the applicant to further explain the purpose and need. 
Planner Nehl presented site photos of the existing conditions, including an overview showing the 
property at the end of a cul-de-sac with the proposed area in the northeast corner and existing open 
space and trails at the rear; the driveway, from which the proposed area would be hardly visible due to 
the angle and length of the driveway; a photo facing north showing the existing vegetation surrounding 
the proposed ADU and the slightly elevated walking trail; a photo facing east toward the adjacent 
neighbor's property line, which would be most impacted by the proposal; a photo facing southwest 
toward the primary residence showing the many mature trees on the property; and a photo of the side 
yard facing west from the primary residence, which staff believes could be a potential area that could 
comply with zoning requirements and may require the removal of one or two trees that could be 
replanted elsewhere on the property. 
Planner Nehl stated that staff found none of the Section A criteria in the affirmative. Staff found that no 
special condition or circumstance exists, as the subject property contains many existing mature trees that 
the property owners could remove and/or relocate to accommodate the proposed ADU in a more zoning 
compliant location. He stated the applicant currently has reasonable use of the property for the lot's 
primary purpose, which is an existing home; Staff further found that the variance will not ameliorate a 
difficulty which did not result from the acts or omissions of the applicant. 
Planner Nehl stated that staff found Section B1 in the negative, as the proposed ADU encroaching into 
the rear and side yard setbacks puts the structure closer to the neighbor's property line to the east, which 
may affect the harmony of the neighborhood. Staff found Section B2 and B3 in the affirmative, as the 
variance will not substantially or permanently injure the appropriate use of adjacent properties in the 
same zone district, there is nothing unusual or out of character for the proposed ADU, and the variance 
will not have an adverse impact on public health, safety, or welfare. 
Planner Nehl stated that, based on the review criteria and findings, staff recommends that the Board of 
Adjustments and Appeals deny the requested variance. 
Questions to Staff: 
Chairman Foottit asked whether Section A3 could be answered in the affirmative, as there appears to be 
a difficulty in that locating the ADU in a space that did not encroach into the setback would require the 
applicant to remove a fairly mature tree, which granting the variance would avoid. 
Planner Nehl responded that this was possible. 
Chairman Foottit stated that he struggles with Section A1 because of the use of the word "or," and that 
this lot and those nearby all share the same burden of being strange shapes. Chairman Foottit asked 
whether staff had surveyed all of the lots in the R-1.0 PUD district. 
Page 7 
City Council Agenda - Final August 27, 2026 
 
 
Planning Manager Stertz responded that staff had not, that there is a wide variety of zoning within the 
City, and that the context of the analysis in this case was the adjacent locations within the neighborhood. 
Boardmember Moxcey asked staff to define an ADU and whether a garage, a shed, and an ADU are the 
same or different. 
Planning Manager Stertz explained that an ADU is not defined specifically in the code and that the code 
does not treat the use separately; it is an accessory structure. He added that accessory structures run 
the gamut in form and can include a garage, a shed, or a pergola and that the code acknowledges this 
as an accessory structure that happens to have a kitchen, a bed, and an office. 
Boardmember Moxcey asked where the 12-foot height limit comes from, given the proposed 8.5 feet. 
Planning Manager Stertz stated that Section 16-22-10 provides that, in the R-1.0 zone district, for 
accessory structures greater than 8.5 feet in height, for each additional foot of height over 8.5 feet an 
additional four feet of setback on all sides shall be required, up to the point where principal building 
setbacks are met, after which no further setback is required. Planning Manager Stertz further stated that 
the height section of Article 8 for the R-1.0 zone district provides that accessory structures shall not 
exceed 12 feet in height and shall comply with the minimum setbacks provided in Article 22. 
Boardmember Aaron asked staff to return to the Section B criteria and wanted to clarify that the finding 
on Section B1 is primarily dictated by the fact that the structure is closer to the neighbor to the east, 
rather than the green space and trail at the rear. 
Planner Nehl confirmed yes and stated that he mentioned it may potentially impact the neighbor and that 
he would not know unless the neighbor specifically said that a structure being closer to the property line 
would be a nuisance, so that was something he wanted to take in as a factor. 
Chairman Foottit asked whether the natural old thicket of brush and trees between the proposed site for 
the ADU and the trail that runs into the park straddles the property line. 
Planner Nehl stated that was safe to say, with some vegetation on the lot and some on the other side of 
the property line. 
Planning Manager Stertz stated that the order of business is to have the applicant present, and that 
some of the Board's questions may be answered through that testimony. Planning Manager Stertz 
suggested it may be beneficial to have the applicant do their presentation and then follow up with 
questions to both staff and the applicant. 
Chairman Foottit agreed.

sport courts where current residents do not use the court after a certain time but future owners come in. 
Planning Manager Stertz stated that the guidance he is giving the Board is that the Boardmembers are

GREENWOOD HILLS CHARACTER 
 
Natural Environment 
The extensive natural environment found in this planning area 
includes native plantings wetlands, and open meadows. 
 
Built Environment 
Greenwood Hills’ residents value the low density zoning, natural 
and maintained open spaces, wildlife, and semi-rural character of 
the streets.  Extensive wetlands and natural drainage corridors, 
with healthy riparian vegetation exists throughout the community. 
 Land Uses:  The planning area is comprised of single family 
residential dwellings.  At the southwest corner of this 
planning area is a commercial shopping district located at 
the southwest corner of Holly and Orchard.  In the northeast 
corner of the planning area is a series of low-rise commercial 
office buildings.  Two churches operate in the planning area 
on residential zoned land with special use permits. 
 Residential Density:  This low density, single family 
neighborhood consists of a range of densities, generally 
consisting of lots just below one acre up to 2.5 acres.

Planning Area Vision 
The planning area will retain its low density residential and 
attractive large-lot and rural character.  Mature vegetation 
located in older neighborhoods will be preserved, while 
additional trees and other landscape enhancements will be 
located in developing areas.  Significant views of the Rocky 
Mountains will remain an important feature of this planning

elements to protect tranquility, security, and quality of life 
from the impacts of transient automobiles, pedestrians and 
bicycle traffic, and from pollution, stormwater run-off, 
flooding, noise, and lights.  The safety of children traveling to 
and from school shall be a paramount concern in trail 
planning, maintenance and improvements.  No further 
nonresidential development (except open space) shall occur 
in the planning area.

one (1) front yard on a lot.
