 A. Grace Chapel, 8500 E Orchard Road To Repurpose Existing Building And Parking Lot For A 
Place Of Worship And Event Center (2026-181)
A quasi-judicial public hearing was held at 6:07 PM. regarding Case No. PZ-26-00017, a Special 
Use Permit and Case No. PZ-26-00026, a Site Development Plan for Grace Chapel at 8500 E 
Orchard Rd. Mayor Lantz presided with eight Councilmembers present.
Interim City Attorney Kathie Guckenberger stated a notice of the date, time, place, and purpose of 
the hearing was published on the City's website, for which she offered into evidence Exhibit A, an 
affidavit of publication from the City Clerk. Notice was also posted on the property for which she 
offered into evidence Exhibit B, a certificate of posting. Notice of the date, time, place, and purpose 
of the hearing was also sent by mail to property owners as outlined in the Municipal Code per this 
type of application at least 15 days prior to this hearing. She offered into evidence Exhibit C, which 
was an affidavit of mailing. She noted these items complied with the Greenwood Village Municipal 
Code, and it was her opinion the Council had jurisdiction to proceed with the public hearing. 
 
Mayor Lantz accepted Exhibits A, B, and C into evidence and made them part of the hearing record.
 
 i. Case No. PZ-26-00017; Special Use Permit; Grace Chapel (Barnacle) (2026-182)
Grady Timmerman, Planner II, reviewed the subject property and request. He noted the applicant 
was requesting approval of a Special Use Permit (SUP) and Site Development Plan (SDP) to allow 
Grace Chapel to establish a place of worship use with a 1000-seat auditorium in the existing building 
at 8500 E Orchard Rd. He noted the property was zoned Mixed Commercial (MC) and was located 
in the Interstate Corridor Planning Area of the Comprehensive Plan. He noted adjacent parcels were 
zoned MC and included office and research and development uses.
Planner Timmerman reviewed photographs of the existing property and proposed elevations. He 
noted the building would be repaired and repainted, and new rooftop screening would be installed. 
He reviewed the proposed landscaping and noted it included 21 new trees and 300 new shrubs in 
addition to the existing 44 trees. He stated open space would increase from 10.9% to 20%. He noted 
the proposed lighting met the requirements of the Master Development Plan (MDP) and Land 
Development Code (LDC). He stated the subject property was accessed through S Wabash Way 
and that the proposed 250 parking spaces met the LDC requirement of one space per four seats in 
the sanctuary. He noted staff anticipated no significant traffic impacts and that the existing traffic 
network could accommodate the proposed development. 
Planner Timmerman reviewed the SUP and SDP approval criteria and noted all have been met. He 
noted the development was compatible with surrounding land uses. Staff found the application 
mitigates adverse impacts as there would be no change in mass to the building. 
He stated Council shall determine whether the SUP was transferable to other owners and shall 
make such determination as a condition of approval per section 16-270(f) of the LDC. He noted the

Greenwood Village Planning and Zoning Commission recommended approval of the SUP and SDP 
on August 4, 2026. 
Applicant Presentation:
Pastor Josh Weidmann, Lone Tree, CO, noted he grew up in south Denver and that Grace Chapel 
was founded in south Denver in 1979. He noted the existing location was on I-25 near Park 
Meadows mall. He noted the church was nondenominational and had been in the same location for 
30 years. He noted the existing location provided space for police and fire departments to meet and 
run drills and that they founded a free counseling center. He stated they hold community events and 
festivals. He noted he would like to take an old landmark and bring it new life with a public coffee 
shop and co-working space. He noted Grace Chapel was debt-free. 
Mr. Doug Spuler, 1001 17th St, Ste PL100, Denver, CO, 80202, on behalf of Beck Architecture, 
noted public safety would be improved as there would be more eyes on the street. He noted the 
project would enhance the building’s aesthetic quality and provide learning opportunities and 
programs. He stated the project would improve site and building accessibility and promote 
responsible stewardship.
Mr. Coy Williams, 6200 S Syracuse Way, Ste 300, Greenwood Village, 80111, on behalf of Kimley 
Horn, noted the church would be an owner/operator and take pride in the landscaping and upkeep of 
the building. He noted the church would promote leisure and learning opportunities via the coffee 
shop. He stated the development would be compatible with surrounding office and high-density 
residential land uses. He noted the traffic study did not trigger any improvements and stated the 
access point on the northwest corner of the property was deemed unsafe and would be removed. He 
noted the proposal was granted a landscape variance from the Denver Tech Center Architectural 
Control Committee (DTC ACC) for 20% instead of the required 30%. He noted the landscape 
percentage on the property would be doubled and that the disturbed area would be less than one 
acre. He noted pedestrian and ADA access would be improved on the site. 
Mr. Williams noted places of worship were allowed as a special use in the MC zone district. He 
noted the landscaping would be enhanced and existing tree canopy maintained where possible. He 
noted the building design would be significantly enhanced. 
Mr. Spuler reviewed the existing site conditions and noted the property was 4.8 acres and 
approximately 210,000 square feet. He stated the general site circulation was conducive to the 
proposed use. He noted the existing building was 59,000 square feet with 303 parking stalls. He 
reviewed the landscape plan and noted the increased landscaping areas and open space. He noted 
the parking count would be reduced to 250 spaces and would meet the code. He noted a green 
space and playground would be added. 
Mr. Spuler reviewed the open space plan and noted the proposed landscaping additions would 
exceed the open space requirements. He noted the parking coverage would be 22% and would be 
under the 40% maximum allowed by the code.  
Mr. Spuler reviewed floor plan diagrams and noted the existing dock would be removed and 
replaced with a staff entrance. He noted the first floor would include the sanctuary, classrooms, 
administrative and counseling space, and the coffee shop. He noted the roof would have new and 
replaced mechanical screens for existing equipment. He stated new windows for the north and 
glazing for the western facades would be installed. He indicated the existing herringbone wood

signage would conform to code and DTC ACC requirements. He stated no exceptions were

Council Questions/Responses:
Councilmember Wiesner asked if the SUP should include details regarding hours of operation, rental 
practices, and lighting as a previously approved SUP in a residential area had those restrictions. 
Planning Manager Derek Stertz stated Council had the authority to condition approval. 
Councilmember Wiesner asked if staff was concerned about lighting, noise, and other potential 
issues. Planning Manager Stertz noted staff was not concerned. Community Development Director 
Derek Holcomb noted that placing conditions on the approval was unnecessary and would be 
onerous to enforce. He noted the code had standards for noise, lighting, access, and traffic and that 
they had been reviewed for the proposal. 
Councilmember Wiesner asked if the coffee shop and classes would be allowed under the SUP. 
Planning Manager Stertz noted the coffee shop and other activities would be allowed per the zoning. 
Councilmember Wiesner noted the Shepler’s sign should be removed. Planning Manager Stertz 
noted that could be a condition of approval. 
Councilmember Nelson asked if the playground was for public use. Pastor Weidmann noted it was 
not intended for public use for security reasons.
Councilmember Nelson asked for clarification regarding the counseling. Pastor Weidmann noted all 
the counselors would be certified biblical counselors. 
Councilmember Johnston asked for clarification regarding the parking. Planner Timmerman noted 
the Greenwood Village code requires one parking space per four seats for religious uses. 
Councilmember Johnston asked for clarification regarding holiday parking and overflow. Pastor 
Weidmann noted extra services are run for Easter and Christmas to ensure everyone has a seat and 
a parking spot.
Councilmember Doyle asked for the plan regarding the sign. Pastor Wideman noted they would like 
to keep the existing sign. He noted the sign had been in place since 1977 and that it was a landmark 
in Greenwood Village. 
Councilmember Doyle asked if the sign was allowed per the Code. Planner Timmerman noted the 
sign was legally nonconforming and that, if it had not been in active use for over six months, it could 
be considered abandoned. Planning Manager Stertz noted the sign was not per code. Director 
Holcomb noted Council could condition the approval to keep and maintain the sign. 
Councilmember Doyle asked if there would be signage on the building. Mr. Spuler noted they would 
work with staff and the DTC ACC regarding signage. Planning Manager Stertz noted signage would 
be requested through the Planning and Zoning Commission. 
Councilmember Doyle asked for clarification regarding the open space requirement. Planner 
Timmerman noted the open space requirement was 20%. He noted that if the applicant requested a
sign package that met the code, a sign variance would not be necessary.
