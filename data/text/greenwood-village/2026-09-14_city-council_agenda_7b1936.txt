Update of Carson Park Art Installation (Suzanne Moore - Parks, Trails & Recreation Director 
and Lisa Neeper - Cultural Arts Manager) 
   
To:   Honorable Mayor and City Council 
Through:   John Jackson, City Manager 
Shawn Cordsen, Deputy City Manager 
From:   Suzanne Moore, Parks, Trails, and Recreation Director 
Lisa Neeper, Cultural Arts Manager  
  
Executive Summary: 
  
Upon completing the requirements of the Public Art Policy, including a public meeting, Greenwood Village Arts 
and Humanities Council (GVAHC) would like to obtain City Council's approval to proceed with fabrication and 
installation of the proposed sculptures by Kevin Shaffer for the Carson Park public art project. 
  
Recommendation: 
  
Staff and the GVAHC recommend that City Council approve moving forward with the fabrication and 
installation of the three sculptural pieces by Kevin Shaffer in Carson Park. 
  
Attachments: 
  
1. Carson Park Public Art - Public Meeting Boards 
2. Exhibit A - Proposed Art Installation Locations 
  
Discussion 
  
At the August 4, 2025 study session, the City Council expressed its support for $80,000 from the GVAHC 
account balance to be included in the approved 2026 Cultural Arts budget to support the integration of a 
sculpture project into the Carson Park improvement project and in recognition of the City’s 75th anniversary.  
The City's Cultural Arts staff publicly solicited the call for artists in December 2025, with a submittal deadline of 
January 30, 2026. There were 32 proposals received. The GVAHC members judged the proposals in March 
and hosted in-person presentations from the three finalists at their April monthly meeting. The three Colorado 
finalists were James Lynxweler, Ian Houghton, and Kevin Shaffer. At the June 1, 2026 study session, GVAHC 
presented Kevin Shaffer’s work to the City Council and recommended contracting him for the Carson Park 
public art project. The recommendation was well received, and City Council gave approval to proceed forward 
with Kevin Shaffer as the artist for the project. 
Per the City’s Public Art Policy, a public input meeting was held on August 26, 2026 in Carson Park, to solicit 
public comment on the proposed pieces and their location in the park. The meeting was advertised by a sign 
posted in the park, in the Greenwood Village newsletter, and via Notify Me alerts. The boards presented to the 
public at the meeting are attached. The public meeting had five people in attendance, all in favor of the 
proposal.  
The GVAHC is now bringing the proposal back to City Council for approval to move forward with fabrication 
and installation. The timing of the installation is anticipated to be during the final phases of the Carson Park 
pavilion construction so that there can be some efficiencies realized in concrete, landscaping, and restoration. 
This is expected to occur in the summer and fall of 2027. The exact locations proposed for installing the three

Discussion Regarding Artificial Intelligence Use (Garrett Graybeal - Senior Management 
Analyst and Kathie Guckenberger - Interim City Attorney) 
   
To:   Honorable Mayor and City Council 
Through:   John Jackson, City Manager 
Shawn Cordsen, Deputy City Manager 
From:   Garrett Graybeal, Senior Management Analyst  
  
Executive Summary: 
  
The City is carefully evaluating how artificial intelligence may support municipal operations by developing a 
draft Acceptable Use Policy, creating staff training, and conducting a small, controlled pilot program of 
Microsoft Copilot. This methodical approach ensures the City understands both the benefits and risks of AI 
before considering broader adoption. The focus remains on protecting sensitive information, maintaining 
human oversight, and upholding public trust as the City explores future use. 
  
Recommendation: 
  
This item is presented for informational purposes only; no action or direction from City Council is requested at 
this time. 
  
Attachments: 
  
None 
  
Discussion 
  
The City of Greenwood Village has formed an internal Artificial Intelligence (AI) Working Group to explore how 
emerging AI tools may responsibly support city operations. This group was formed to ensure that the City 
approaches AI thoughtfully, cautiously, and with appropriate safeguards in place. At this stage, the City is not 
broadly deploying AI technology; however, many cloud-based systems that the City uses are beginning to 
have AI built into their services. Staff is focused on understanding the capabilities of these tools, assessing 
risks, and developing the policies and training necessary to guide future use. 
 
A key part of the group’s work is the development of a draft AI Acceptable Use Policy. This draft policy is still 
in progress and has not been finalized or adopted. Its purpose is to establish clear expectations for safe and 
responsible AI use, with particular emphasis on the protection of sensitive, confidential, and regulated 
information. The draft policy also underscores the importance of maintaining human oversight of any 
AI-assisted work and ensuring that staff remains accountable for the accuracy and appropriateness of any 
AI-generated content. The Working Group continues to refine this draft as different departments raise 
questions about records retention, data classification, and compliance requirements. 
 
Additionally, the Working Group is preparing training and guidance. The goal of this training is to clarify when 
AI may be helpful, what risks to consider, and how to handle City information safely when using AI tools. This 
includes guidance on recognizing the limitations of AI systems and the importance of verifying any 
AI-generated output before using it in City work or functions. 
 
To better inform both the policy and the training plan, the City has begun a small, controlled pilot program of 
Microsoft Copilot involving members of the Working Group. This limited program allows staff to test enterprise-
level AI tools in a supervised environment, giving the group practical insight into how AI behaves in real
