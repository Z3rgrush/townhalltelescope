Water Rate Discussion & Modeling
Lead Presenter: Trustee Sara Taylor (Water Rate Committee Chair)
Sara presented the Water Rate Committee’s full report, summarizing work completed since April.
1. Purpose of the Committee
To identify a sustainable water rate structure that funds:
Operations & maintenance
Infrastructure and capital replacement
Required reserves (debt, emergency, O&M)
Longterm system stability
2. Summary of RCAC Findings
Sara reviewed the earlier RCAC analysis:
Current revenue covers less than 75% of annual expenses.
Current revenue covers 0% of required reserves, despite the $3 reserve fee.
RCAC recommended $128/month to fully fund the system.
A reduced estimate of $68/month would cover expenses only.
Current rates ($35–$41) are significantly below sustainable levels.
3. Committee Financial Analysis
Operating Budget
Fully funded annual operating need: $238,800 (projected to 2027).
Includes 3 years of 4% inflation, updated payroll allocations, and overtime.

4. Proposed Rate Structure
Committee established foundational policies:
One tap = one account = one usage allowance.
Same usage allowance for all users (residential & commercial).
Base rates determined by category (intown/outoftown, residential/commercial, mixeduse).
Overage rates identical for all users.

6. Overage Tier Structure
Committee consolidated the system into five tiers.Example (using 6,000gallon allowance):
Tier 1: 0–6,000 gallons (included in base rate)
Tier 2: 6,001–10,000 gallons – ~$3/1,000 gal
Tier 3: 10,001–20,000 – ~$6/1,000 gal
Tier 4: 20,001–50,000 – ~$16/1,000 gal
Tier 5: 50,000+ – ~$32/1,000 gal
7. Usage Allowance Analysis
Committee evaluated 4,000 / 5,000 / 6,000 gallon allowances.Revenue differences were minimal; committee recommends 6,000 gallons as the standard allowance.
8. Bulk Water Rates
Current rate: $1.50 per 100 gallons.Committee recommends $2–$3 per 100 gallons, noting bulk users do not pay tap fees or base rates.
9. Tap Fees
Current tap fee: $5,000.Committee recommends increasing to cover one annual debt payment (~$11,230).A grace period may be considered.
10. Conservation Rates
Committee recommends:
Reducing included gallons during conservation periods.
Doubling overage rates during conservation periods.
11. Council Discussion
Topics included:
Fairness for multiunit properties on a single tap.
Airbnb classification (treated as mixeduse).
Outoftown tap moratorium.
Need for improved billing software and smart meter integration.
Importance of council presenting rate changes clearly to the public.
Direction to Staff
Staff will:
Prepare materials for council review at the next regular meeting.
Assist in developing a single rate proposal for public hearing.
Prepare required notices.
