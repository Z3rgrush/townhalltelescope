Commissioner Dahlkemper:
Commissioner Dahlkemper shared that with the wildfires not only in our state, across the United States, and in Europe, it really underscores how devasting the wildfires can be.  The Colorado Fire Commission is working on issues related to wildfires resiliency across the State of Colorado, from mitigation, forest improvement, and the affordability of homeowners insurance. Commissioner Dahlkemper serves on a sub-committee with the Colorado Fire Commission on insurance. She is proud of the first of its kind project that will happen in South Jefferson County to build a wildfire ready development, with high standard with the IBHS (Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety). The new development will include specific mitigation strategies and construction materials that will help lower the risk of wildfire. Commissioner Dahlkemper stated that there was an update on the Colorado Fair Plan which was related to insurance coverage, being a part of the sub-committee there are great leaders involved who provide their best advice. Commissioner Dahlkemper, Commissioner Zenzinger and Commissioner Kerr and many commissioners from rural, mountain, and urban communities gathered this week in Routte County for Counties & Commissioners Acting Together (CCAT) annual retreat to identify the 2027 legislative priorities on affordability, revenue diversification and wildfire resiliency. It is very helpful to come together to better understand the large issues that we are facing and recognize that the way we go about it may be different from community to community.  Commissioner Dahlkemper thanked Attorney General Phil Weiser for speaking about communities and counties shaping and informing state policy. Commissioner Dahlkemper also thanked State Representatives Meghan Lukens, and Andy Boe’senecker for the review of the upcoming legislative session as well as listening to the concerns that came up from the county commissioners, it was a very informative and in-depth session. Commissioner Dahlkemper is looking forward to the Colorado Counties Inc. Front Range District meeting this Friday in Lafayette. It’s an opportunity to meet with other commissioners along the front range to dig deep into these issues and more. Lastly Commissioner Dahlkemper was a part of the 4-H fair and was very impressed with the talent of the students that were involved.
Commissioner Zenzinger:
Commissioner Zenzinger was excited to celebrate Colorado Day on Saturday, with the unique nature of Colorado’s 150
th
anniversary birthday coinciding with the American 250 celebrations. In honor of that Commissioner Zenzinger along with Jeffco Parks and Open Space and 50 volunteers worked in South Valley Park on a project pulling invasive weeds. These non-native species of weeds tend to suck up all the water and destroy the natural landscape. Commissioner Zenzinger enjoyed the opportunity to participate in the Counties & Commissioners Acting Together (CCAT) summer retreat. It was an opportunity for commissioners to collectively think about ways to impact county governments through state legislature. Regarding policy changes they discussed bill ideas and landed on three key topic areas affordable housing, wildfire hardening opportunities and revenues of diversification. Commissioner Zenzinger was excited to have those conversations from Jefferson County’s perspective and the future impact the initiatives could have on the Jefferson County community, and other counties. Commissioner Zenzinger was able to attend the groundbreaking ceremony of The Ives II, the second initiative of an affordable housing project located in northern Wheat Ridge. The Ives II is an exciting project because it focuses on creating affordable units that are up to 3 bedrooms, which can be utilized by  families. There is also a portion of this project that will be dedicated to individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities, which is a great opportunity and partnership with DDRC (Developmental Disabilities Resource Center). Commissioner Zenzinger thanked and congratulated Foothills Housing Authority for their work and effort. Commissioner Zenzinger attended several meetings for the Jefferson Center for Mental Health and Wheat Ridge Urban Renewal Meetings with nothing to report.
Commissioner Kerr
: Commissioner Kerr stated that this weekend is the Wheatridge Carnation Festival, He encouraged the public to attend. Commissioner Kerr stated that there are 6 boards that still have openings and they extended the application process until today. Commissioner Kerr reminded people that with school starting in Jefferson County traffic does increase during certain times of the day, and wants to remind everyone to be safe, slow down and look twice to make sure everyone arrives alive. Commissioner Kerr noted that with national and state headlines involving the current administration taking another look at Head Start, temperatures being the hottest in the region and recorded in 148 years, and an ICE (Immigration and Customs Enforcement) detainee passing away in custody. These headlines do impact us in Jefferson County. Jefferson County does have a Head Start program which serves families and children in Jefferson County. Several years ago, with the high temperatures Jefferson County put a climate action plan in place, tracking the numbers on a regular basis, and have saved a lot of money under the climate action plan. In regard to the ICE (Immigration and Customs Enforcement) detainee passing away, the Commissioners work closely with the Jefferson County Sheriff’s office to make sure that state laws are followed and that everyone in our community is as safe as possible. With headlines that occur national in scope they do impact us closely here in Jefferson County, Commissioner Kerr clarified that Jefferson County is tracking all of these headlines.
