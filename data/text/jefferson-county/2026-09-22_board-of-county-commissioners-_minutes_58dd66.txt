Reports
Commissioner Dahlkemper:
Commissioner Dahlkemper attended a burn demonstration that was conducted by the Institute for Business and Home Safety Insurance Institute as well as West Metro Fire and it really underscored how critical mitigation efforts are in helping with wildfire resiliency. The event was attended by about one hundred state and local leaders, media, first responders, insurance specialists, as well as other partners working on these issues and working to build greater wildfire resiliency across the state. This was a really great opportunity to see why that Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) building codes that the Board approved prior to July are so important. Commissioner Dahlkemper thanked IHS and West Metro and other partners who were involved in that effort, including Brian Keating and his team for their great work on the Wildland Fire Management Initiative. Commissioner Dahlkemper thanked the Conifer Area Council for the opportunity to share an update on the county budget, wildfire resiliency, and the new County Manager. Commissioner Dahlkemper gave a huge shout out to the Conifer Library, which was featured in American Library Magazine's 2026 library design showcase.  She encouraged the public to visit the cozy library.  Lastly, Commissioner Dahlkemper shared she was excited about the recent HeadStart Expansion in Lakewood.
Commissioner Zenzinger:
Commissioner Zenzinger attended the 10 County Budget Conference recently. This conference is for the ten largest counties in Colorado: Adams, Arapahoe, Boulder, Douglas, El Paso, Jefferson, Larimer, La Mesa, Pueblo, and Weld. Commissioner Zenzinger shared it was a great opportunity to meet with peer counties to talk about budget constraints, revenue issues, spending trends and economic factors that impact overall budgets. She noted that the most fascinating session was
From Budget Line to Bot how AI is Changing the Work of Local Government
. It was presented by Kate Marshall, who in addition to the session, gave everybody a copy of her book
AI at Work
. Commissioner Zenzinger noted that she learned a lot about how much we have already come to rely on AI and that there is quite a bit that is happening in the background of our everyday lives. She thanked Douglas County for hosting the event. Commissioner Zenzinger also attended the Metro Area County Commissioners Meeting, where Commissioner Dahlkemper presented on Jefferson County’s wildfire response. During this meeting Commissioner Zenzinger learned that the chairmanship of this council will be passed over to her next month. Commissioner Zenzinger attended a Peace Pole Dedication Event which was sponsored by the Wheat Ridge Rotary Club and was attended by the City of Wheat Ridge. The event was a reminder of how we as people and a community strive for peace in our everyday lives, and she was glad the county participated. Commissioner Zenzinger congratulated the Jefferson County Public Library for winning 5 awards at the Colorado Association of Library Conference (CALCON). The awards were for a former JCPL trustee Kim Johnson who was honored with the Outstanding Library Trustee Award, the next for the retired JCPL Executive Director Donna Walker who received the Distinction in Library Services Award. The Gene Mayo award for Excellence in Adult Literacy was presented to JCPL‘s Adult Services Team for their artificial intelligence technology classes. Also, the Conifer Library received the Innovative Spaces Award, and the Equity Diversity and Inclusion Award was presented to JCPL‘s Kids and Families Teen Services and DEI teams for their 2025 summer camps. Commissioner Zenzinger expressed how extraordinarily proud the Board is of the Jefferson County Public Library and noted these awards really are a testament to the incredible impact that JCPL makes to the communities they serve.
Commissioner Kerr:
Commissioner Kerr thanked JCPL and their retired Executive Director Donna Walker for their amazing work. Commissioner Kerr mentioned the expansion of the Lakewood HeadStart program and the Peace Pole Dedication by the Wheat Ridge Rotary Club. Commissioner Kerr noted some recent fundraising events such as the 5th Annual Be The Light Gala by PorchLight, and the 25th Run/Walk by Second Wind Foundation. He also noted that September is Suicide Prevention Month. Commissioner Kerr mentioned an upcoming Run/Walk event will be taking place at Dinosaur Ridge on September 27th. He noted that the public can either register at the event or register ahead of time using the “sleep in” option. Commissioner Kerr shared that the Mountain Resource Center is having their annual fundraising gala on October 1st and that Colorado Opioid Abatement Council is having their annual conference in Breckenridge next week under the leadership of Attorney General Phil Weiser. Commissioner Kerr mentioned that this Saturday is National Public Lands Day. To celebrate this event Open Space Staff and Volunteers will be doing projects all over the county and then getting together in the afternoon at Bear Creek Lake Park.
County Manager:
County Manager Aldrete was interviewed by Denver7 news about Jefferson County where she reiterated the County’s focus on affordability, economic development, fiduciary responsibility with the taxpayer dollars and challenges that the county is facing with federal and state cuts to the Human Service Programs.
County Attorney:
County Attorney Sorrells shared she will be moving to South Carolina in December and her last day with the County will be December 4th. She thanked everyone for a great seven and a half years with the County.
13.
