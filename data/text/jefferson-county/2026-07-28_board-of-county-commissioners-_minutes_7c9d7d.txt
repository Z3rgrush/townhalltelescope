13.
Reports
Commissioner Dahlkemper:
Commissioner Dahlkemper began by highlighting hers and Commissioner Zenzinger’s participation in the Jeffco Housing Advocacy Steering Committee. The group includes county commissioners, a state legislator, area mayors, housing experts, and developers that specialize in affordable housing. The committee has worked to identify specific policy levers to advance affordable housing for not only Jefferson County, but across Colorado. Commissioner Dahlkemper shared appreciation for the county Planning and Zoning team, County Attorney’s office, housing coordinator and others for their efforts. She spoke about attending the annual Jeffco 4-H fair and helping kick-off the event on behalf of the Board of County Commissioners. She explained that the fair is not only important for showcasing the talent of children and teens in the program, but to help the youth build confidence, discover new things, learn to work through setbacks, and support one another. Commissioner Dahlkemper noted that she was in 4-H herself, highlighting her own sewing skills. She gave a shoutout to the fair participants for their tremendous and impressive work. Commissioner Dahlkemper spoke about Jefferson County hosting the Chatfield Watershed Authority, for which she is the Chair. The Board's top priorities include protecting water quality for drinking, outdoor recreation, aquatic life, and fire mitigation. The Commissioner mentioned that the Board of County Commissioners joined over thirty volunteers this past Saturday morning at Conifer Rotary's Roadside Mitigation Project and thanked the County's Road and Bridge division and volunteers for directing traffic, cleaning debris, sawing fallen trees and so much more. Commissioner Dahlkemper acknowledged the importance of this volunteer work as it creates a much safer environment for foothills communities and more efficient evacuation processes should a fire occur. She gave a shoutout to Conifer Rotary’s project organizer Ann Imse, volunteers, and partners for their incredible work throughout the day. Commissioner Dahlkemper spoke about the groundbreaking for an affordable housing development that all the County Commissioners attended. The housing development in Wheat Ridge offers more affordable options for working families, aging adults, veterans and other community members. She noted that the development, called The Ives, focuses on one-bedroom units. The Ives II offers units up to three bedrooms and aims to serve more families and children. She highlighted that the county provided $6.6 million in private activity bonds which allowed for tax exemption for the project. The commissioner listed and congratulated project partners such as Foothills Regional Housing, the City of Wheat Ridge, banking and finance experts, DOLA, and other state leaders. Commissioner Dahlkemper gave a shout to former schoolboard member, Danielle Barta, along with Westernaires and thanked them for the invitation to participate.
Commissioner Zenzinger:
Excused.
Commissioner Kerr:
Commissioner Kerr spoke about the Westernaires group and their Western Heritage event. He noted that the event had many attendees which included hundreds of children from the metro area and other elected county officials. The Commissioner highlighted that the Westernaires have their own museum. He thanked them for including the Jefferson County Board of County Commissioners in the event and stated that he looks forward to future events. Commissioner Kerr mentioned the Jeffco Employee Appreciation Night at the Denver Zoo. He expressed appreciation for the Denver Zoo employees that reached out to help organize the event for Jeffco employees and expressed how glad he was that so many employees took the opportunity to attend. Commissioner Kerr acknowledged the record-breaking temperatures in Colorado and how this will draw the public to areas such as Bear Creek Lake Park. The park has experienced parking issues, specifically the Red Rocks Ranch area and an area off C-470. He stated that the City of Lakewood and the county have placed "No Parking" signs which will be enforced by Lakewood police and county deputies. The commissioner urged the public to arrive earlier to secure parking in proper areas to enjoy the park's amenities. Commissioner Kerr explained that today, the 28th, is the deadline for applying for Boards and Commissions. The deadline has been extended for six Boards and Commissions which includes the Board of Review, County Cultural Council, Equine and Agricultural Heritage Commission, IDEA Advisory Commission, the Jefferson Center, and the Revenue Retention Citizen's Review Committee. He noted that the extended application deadline will be next Tuesday, August 4th. Commissioner Kerr shared that there will be more information about the new County Manager coming soon.
