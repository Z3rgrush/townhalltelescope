GENERAL PUBLIC COMMENT 08-26-26
John Ims, 66049, commented that the County’s reserves far exceed its own fund
‑
balance policy, noting recent reporting that the County ended 2025 with uncommitted cash equal to 52% of expenditures and has not followed its 25% cap guideline for years. He urged Commissioners to return a portion of the excess reserves to taxpayers, suggesting a refund modeled on Rand Paul’s proposal of six cents per dollar.
Sophia explained that rising property taxes, insurance, and related costs will significantly increase both her rental properties and her own mortgage payment, creating financial strain for her and her husband as seniors. She argued that County budgeting decisions, not landlords, are driving housing unaffordability and urged Commissioners to listen to citizens and rein in spending.
Dustin Stumblingbear shared that scammers targeted his family after a relative’s property-tax case, but County staff across multiple offices treated his mother with respect and helped her navigate the situation. He thanked all County employees for their professionalism and said their kindness made his mother feel excited about moving to Douglas County.
Reid closed public comment.

REGULAR AGENDA 08-26-26
4.1.
ADMINISTRATION/HEARING TO EXCEED REVENUE NEUTRAL RATE 08-26-26
Brooke Sauer, Finance Manager, presented the first procedural step in certifying the County’s budget to the State of Kansas, outlining the combined Revenue Neutral Rate and budget hearings for Douglas County, the County cemetery districts, and Consolidated Fire District No. 1. She reviewed the 2027 budget development process, including more than 20 hours of Commissioner deliberations, $15.4 million in funding requests, one
‑
time allocations for several community projects, ongoing program funding, mill
‑
levy reductions, and projected fund balances across major operating funds.
Reid opened a Public Hearing to accept public comment on exceeding the Revenue Neutral Rate (RNR) for Douglas County and Cemetery Special Districts within the 2027 County Budget.
Melanie Coffman stated concerns that Douglas County is holding $133.9 million in reserves, associating it to a “thrifty grandma” hiding cash while still asking taxpayers for an additional $4 million. She urged Commissioners to stay revenue neutral and vote no on exceeding the RNR.
Brent B. said that the County’s expense budget is inflated, relies on conservative revenue estimates, and continues patterns of padded departmental costs and duplicated delinquent
‑
tax assumptions. He said these practices result in record
‑
high property taxes, poor stewardship, and financial strain for residents. He urged Commissioners to vote no on exceeding the RNR.
Reid closed public comment.
Sarah Plinsky conducted a roll call vote to exceed the Revenue Neutral Rate:
Reid, Chair, District 2-
Yes
Willey, Vice Chair, District 3 - Yes
Kelly, Member, District 1 - Yes
Dorsey, Member, District 4 - No
Anderson, Member, District 5 – No
The vote passed 3-2.
Reid closed the public hearing.
4.2.
ADMINISTRATION/2027 DOUGLAS COUNTY BUDGET 08-26-26
The Board considered adopting the 2027 Operating and CIP Budget. Brooke Sauer, Finance Manager, was available for questions.
Reid opened public comment on the 2027 Operating Budget.
Major Karen Holness, Salvation Army, emphasized the Salvation Army’s long history of providing hope and nondiscriminatory support in Douglas County, serving thousands through homelessness assistance, food programs, and emergency aid. She noted the organization has never been included in the County Budget and respectfully requested future funding consideration to continue strengthening community services.
Steve Nowak, 66044,thanked the County Commission for its consistent support of heritage preservation, noting that such backing allows the Historical Society and Watkins Museum to tell inclusive stories without political influence. He emphasized that this freedom enables historians to share meaningful narratives with the public and ensures equal access for all county residents.
Sophia, 66044, asked Commissioners to clarify how much of the budget increase is due solely to rising real
‑
estate valuations and to provide the amount of the budget dedicated to administration and personnel. She noted that many important community groups need support but emphasized the size of the overall budget and requested clearer information.
John Ims, 66049, said that rising County and City service costs are unsustainable for many residents, especially seniors like himself who rely on limited incomes. He warned that continued increases will push people out of their homes, leading to displacement, homelessness, or forcing them to leave the community.
Bev Turner, Children’s Advocacy Center, thanked the Commissioners and community for supporting the Children’s Advocacy Center, which provides free forensic interviews, advocacy, medical referrals, trauma therapy, and coordinated multidisciplinary case reviews for children affected by abuse. She emphasized that County financial support ensures these critical services remain accessible for all child victims and their caregivers.
Stella Trombley, 2521 Wanda Way, asked whether the County budget provides itemized information on employee and police salaries, as well as revenue from traffic violations and parking fees. She also questioned an online claim about Kansas funding the state of Israel if such information would appear in the County budget, if it exists.
Reid closed public comment and the public hearing.
Reid moved to adopt the 2027 Douglas County Operating and Capital Improvement Plan Budget as presented. Motion was seconded by Kelly.
The Commission moved to discussion:

Kelly expressed disappointment that there was a desire by a minority of the Commission to not increase revenue by more than one penny. He noted that this politically popular stance doesn’t match the two weeks of budget work the Commission completed. He stressed that July’s process is meant to identify County needs and set a tax rate that can meet them. Kelly warned that staying revenue neutral would force ongoing cuts because inflation continually raises the cost of County services. While acknowledging tax increases can burden residents, especially those on fixed incomes, he maintained the County must still address rising costs. He added that, based on the earlier vote, he expects two commissioners to oppose the budget and wants to know which services they would cut to reach revenue neutrality.

Anderson stated that after reviewing road and bridge fund balances, she acknowledged she had misunderstood some numbers but clarified that while fund balances will decrease in future years, road and bridge operational expenses are actually rising. Anderson argued that the County has sufficient balances and flexibility in other line items to avoid exceeding revenue neutral. Some funding such as mental health and certain health related programs could be shifted into the mental funding bucket. She emphasized the importance of periodically pausing and reassessing what the County funds. Ultimately, she will vote for the budget because she supports many parts of it, but he does not support exceeding revenue neutral due to the impact on residents across the county.

Willey responded by noting that legal and statutory limits restrict what can be shifted from mill
‑
levy support to the Behavioral Health Sales Tax Fund, and some items like economic development cannot legally be moved. The Mobile Response Team, spoken in the ballot language, was moved under the Behavioral Health Fund. She encouraged Anderson to consult legal counsel on understanding the ballot language for the Behavioral Health Sales Tax.

Reid said the scope is narrow on what can be used under the Behavioral Health Sales Tax but there needs to be ongoing discussion.

Dorsey asked when was the last time the County had to dip into the reserves.
Sarah Plinsky, Count Administrator, explained that the County is already drawing from reserves to cover the Employee Benefits Fund and has reduced spending there by using fund balance to offset increases. She noted the County routinely uses one
‑
time dollars for one
‑
time projects in both the General Fund and Behavioral Health Fund, which is a responsible budgeting approach. She added that many funds (listed in the Journal World as unplanned expenses or emergencies) are already committed to capital projects and equipment replacements so the County can pay cash and avoid taking on additional debt.

Dorsey said he believes the County could fund the proposed budget and still remain revenue neutral. He argued that large budgets often contain areas that can be trimmed and pointed to road and bridge projects as an example. Last year, several major projects came in about 40% under conservative estimates, suggesting there is room to reduce spending, potentially by about $300,000, while still completing planned work. He suggested hiring county planners instead of subsidizing the city’s planning department, delaying a $300,000 code update project, replacing outside legal counsel with in
‑
house attorneys, temporarily reducing funding for the Economic Development Corporation during its transition, lowering the general reserve and further reduce costs by using a lower delinquency rate. Dorsey also recommended shifting more of the salary increase toward merit rather than across
‑
the
‑
board raises and advocated freezing Commissioner salaries. Although he disagrees with exceeding revenue neutral, he will still vote for the budget because he supports most of its contents.

Staff confirmed that in order to get revenue neutral on this budget the Board would need to cut $3.9 million.

Willey pointed to the Commission’s eight detailed public budget discussions Each Commissioner did this work separately and then came together to discuss each line item. She gave an example of road and bridge where last year a proposal for a project on Farmer’s Turnpike and N1800 Road came in too high and the Board chose not to do it and pivoted to another project. How we stay under budget is not just a guess. There is a lot of engineering that goes into how these estimates are made. If the County cannot find bids that are reasonable, because we are cash funded CIP, we can move from one project to another. There is a lot more to it. Willey said she plans to support this budget. Nobody got what they wanted. That’s how it works.
Willey also encouraged residents to participate earlier in the process, especially during July budget meetings, so they can offer specific input while the budget is being crafted.
After discussion, the motion carried 5-0.
4.3.
ADMINISTRATION/ PUBLIC HEARING TO EXCEED THE REVENUE NEUTRAL RATE FOR CONSOLIDATED FIRE DISTRICT NO. 1 2027 BUDGET 08-26-26
Brooke Sauer, Finance Manager, provided a brief presentation for the Revenue Neutral Rate (RNR) hearing and the 2027 Operating Budget for Consolidated Fire District No. 1 (CFD#1). She noted that Commissioners previously approved a 3.83 mill increase to support the District’s workforce stabilization and service reliability initiative.The approved funding adds 10 full
‑
time positions (six firefighters, three captains, one battalion chief) to strengthen staffing and ensure continuous fire response coverage. The initiative establishes two
‑
person staffing at Stations 111 and 151, improving emergency response capability, reducing reliance on volunteers, and enhancing firefighter safety.
Dorsey asked whether the District could hire all planned staff within one year. Fire Chief John Mathis responded he believes they will be able to hire all staff in one year. That is the goal.
Reid opened the public hearing to exceed the Revenue Neutral Rate (RVR) for CDF#1. No comment was received. Reid closed the public hearing.
Willey noted that the mill levy increase was a difficult decision but emphasized that staffing improvements were essential for firefighter and community safety.
Dorsey said he has a hard time with a mill levy increase but supports fire and medical safety.
Anderson said she supports a mill levy increase when it comes to fire medical and safety.
Reid moved to approve Resolution No. 26-16 expressing the intent to levy a property tax rate exceeding the Revenue Neutral Rate for Consolidated Fire District No. 1. Motion was seconded by Dorsey.
Reid conducted a roll call vote to exceed the Revenue Neutral Rate.
Reid, Chair, District 1 – Yes
Willey, Vice Chair, District 3 – Yes
Dorsey, District 4 – Yes
Anderson, District 5 – Yes
Kelly, District 1 – Yes
Passed 5-0.
