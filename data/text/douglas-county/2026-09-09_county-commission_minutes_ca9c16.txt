WORK SESSION 09-09-26
The Board received an update from Heartland Community Health Center regarding psychiatry and behavioral health services. Representatives from Heartland, included CEO Julie Brandstrom; COO Rachel Hartford; Director of Quality Amy Sielaff; and
Director of Psychiatry Dr. Nickolas Evangelidis, along with community partners and staff.
Bob Tryanski, Director of Behavioral Health Projects, provided background on the psychiatric infrastructure work group that formed prior to the Mental Health Sales Tax and creation of the Treatment and Recovery Center.
Heartland leaders described significant growth in outpatient psychiatric access since 2018, highlighting increased provider staffing, strong demand for pediatric and adolescent services, and the integration of psychiatry with primary care, therapy, Medication-Assisted Treatment for Substance Use Disorder (MAT/SUD) services, pharmacy, and care coordination.
Heartland reported steady increases in both unique patients and encounters, with demand remaining high and no-show rates around 25% but below industry averages. The organization emphasized challenges related to provider recruitment and retention, noting that six full-time psychiatric providers are needed to meet current demand and that two new hires will soon restore that level.
Commissioners asked about coordination with schools, wait times, payer mix changes, and upcoming Medicaid/Prospective Payment Systems (PPS) shifts expected in 2027. Heartland explained that confidentiality limits direct communication with schools but community health workers frequently help families navigate education and social-service systems. The County was also informed that wait times for urgent pediatric referrals remain about two weeks but are expected to shorten as new providers begin practice. Heartland and County staff discussed the importance of continued collaboration through the Psychiatric Infrastructure Work Group, including planning outside the annual budget cycle to act quickly on provider recruitment opportunities.

3.1.
ZONING & CODES/TEMPORARY BUSINESS USE PERMIT 09-09-26
The Board received a request from Joshua Bell of Bell’s Turf Land Management to allow a landscaping business and related activity to continue at 1307 E 1200 Road, also known as “Kasold Curve” through December 31, 2027. Karl Bauer, Douglas County planner, presented the item.
Bauer explained that the property is owned by Tenants to Homeowners and is planned for future annexation into the City of Lawrence for an affordable housing development. Bell’s Turf currently uses the site for equipment storage; a use not permitted in the A
‑
2 district without a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) or rezoning.
However, given the intent to annex and redevelop the property, staff determined a Temporary Business Use (TBU) Permit to be more appropriate. The TBU request would allow the landscaping business to continue operations on the site until December 31, 2027, or until annexation occurs, whichever comes first. Bauer reviewed surrounding land uses, access via Kasold Drive and a gravel driveway that crosses the Lawrence Loop Trail, sanitation via portable toilets, buffering from nearby residences, and the absence of noise complaints. Sensitive lands including prime soils, wetlands, and floodplain exist on the site but are outside the areas used for storage. He reported that 83 notification letters were sent to nearby property owners and no responses were received.
Staff recommended approval of the Temporary Business Use permit ZTBU-2026-0003 to allow a landscaping business and related activity to continue at 1307 E. 1200 Rd. through December 31, 2027, with the following conditions:
1. This permit shall expire upon annexation into the corporate limits of a city if such annexation occurs prior to the expiration date;
2. Approval of this request is to allow the continued use of the property as equipment storage for the applicant’s landscaping business, as shown on the submitted plans, only;
3. Any lighting shall be directed downward and away from adjacent properties;
4. The applicant shall secure general liability insurance in accordance with Section 12-306-41.03(f) of the County’s zoning code and submit proof of insurance to Douglas County Zoning and Codes, and;
5. All other applicable rules and regulations shall be continually followed.
The applicant, represented by Rebecca Buford of Tenants to Homeowners and Josh Bell of Bell’s Turf, addressed the Commission. Buford noted that previous seasonal fireworks sales on the property were a fundraiser but would not continue due to upcoming annexation work. She explained that Bell’s Turf provides landscaping services for Tenants to Homeowners and has used the property temporarily while searching for a commercially zoned location, and the arrangement has been mutually beneficial. Both applicants stated they intend to relocate once development of the affordable housing project advances.
Commissioners asked questions regarding prior fireworks sales, the lack of an existing permit, and the annexation timeline. Buford shared that annexation efforts are expected to begin late 2026 or early 2027 and emphasized that affordable housing development depends on securing funding. Staff confirmed an extension of the TBU beyond 2027 would require reapplication but would be a straightforward process.
Reid opened the item for public comment. No comment was received.
There were no ex parte Communications from the Commission.
Several commissioners noted appreciation for the applicants’ efforts to come into compliance, and affirmed that the temporary nature of the request, combined with the planned annexation, made the permit appropriate.
Willey moved approval of a Temporary Business Use permit to allow a landscaping business and related activity to continue at 1307 E. 1200 Rd. through December 31, 2027, with the conditions as listed in the staff memo. Motion was seconded by Anderson and carried 4-0.

ADMINISTRATION/PUBLIC HEARING FOR SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT PLAN 09-09-26
The Board received a presentation from Kim Criner Ritchie, Sustainability Manager; and Julie Davis, Project Manager with Burns & McConnell on the proposed Douglas and Jefferson County, Kansas 2026 Solid Waste Management Plan.
Criner-Ritchie explained that Kansas Statute requires each county to adopt and submit a Solid Waste Management Plan every five years, and that Douglas and Jefferson counties have a longstanding interlocal agreement to jointly plan for solid waste management through a regional committee. After decades of incremental updates, the counties undertook a full-scale revision, which, if adopted, will replace the 1996 Regional Plan and serve as the statutory five-year update. She noted that community engagement was conducted separately from the City of Lawrence’s solid waste master planning process, with results cross-referenced where appropriate.
Davis of Burns & McDonnell, project manager for the plan, provided an extensive overview of the updated regional plan, including waste generation data, landfill capacity projections, community engagement results, and an assessment of existing solid waste, recycling, household hazardous waste (HHW), and diversion programs across both counties. She highlighted that Douglas County generates approximately 112,000 tons of waste annually, with significant diversion opportunities remaining, particularly in organics and food waste. Survey feedback identified substantial dissatisfaction with open-market residential hauling in unincorporated areas, mostly related to cost, inconsistent service, and lack of curbside recycling. The analysis also found high usage of county and city recycling drop-off sites but noted capacity, contamination, and illegal dumping challenges. The HHW program participation has declined, and awareness gaps exist for both hazardous waste and electronic waste disposal. Davis summarized the plan’s recommended strategies, which included supporting municipal curbside programs, monitoring open-market hauling conditions in rural areas, updating recycling drop-off contracts, exploring expanded yard and food waste diversion partnerships, considering equitable HHW cost-sharing models, expanding e
‑
waste collection, and prioritizing public education and outreach to improve awareness of existing programs.
Commissioners asked questions about the inclusion of Jefferson County data, committee membership and statutory representation requirements, and next steps for implementing plan recommendations. Criner-Ritchie explained that Jefferson County approved the plan the previous evening and that the Regional Committee comprised of representatives from both counties, multiple municipalities, KU environmental health and safety, public health, private haulers, and other stakeholders would be well
‑
positioned to guide prioritization and further action.
Commissioners discussed food waste diversion emphasizing a strong community interest and the potential for future collaboration with municipal partners and
private entities.
Reid opened a Public Hearing to receive comment on the Douglas and Jefferson County, Kansas 2026 Solid Waste Management Plan.
Robert Dickerson emphasized that organics make up roughly 40% of Lawrence’s landfill waste and argued that food and yard waste diversion should be elevated from merely monitoring to an active, goal
‑
driven priority, including developing a distributed farm
‑
based composting network. He also urged making public education central to the plan, noting that lack of awareness is the biggest barrier and contributes to costly contamination in recycling streams.
Reid closed the Public Hearing.
Dorsey suggested adding a representative from Haskell University to the Regional Committee.
Willey moved to approve Resolution No. 26-17 adopting the Douglas and Jefferson County, Kansas 2026 Solid Waste Management Plan as the Regional Solid Waste Management Plan for Douglas County and authorizing its submission to the Kansas Department of Health and Environment as the required five-year update. Motion was seconded by Anderson and carried 4-0.
