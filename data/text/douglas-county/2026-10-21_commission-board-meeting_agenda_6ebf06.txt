Subject: Employee Benefits Broker and Consulting Services 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: 
Douglas County currently utilizes Willis Towers Watson as its employee benefits broker and 
consultant. The County has not conducted a competitive RFP for benefits broker services in at least 
15 years. Given the size and complexity of the County's self-funded healthcare plan, the financial 
impact of healthcare costs, and the important role a benefits broker plays in managing the plan, staff 
determined it was an appropriate time to conduct a competitive review of available firms, services, 
expertise, and compensation arrangements. 
  
The County issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) for employee benefits broker and consulting 
services and received eight proposals, including one from the incumbent, Willis Towers Watson. 
Following an initial evaluation of the proposals, three firms were selected for interviews: Gallagher, 
Lockton Companies, and Willis Towers Watson. 
  
The interview panel consisted of County Administration staff, two members of the Healthcare 
Committee, and the Procurement Coordinator. Following the interviews and evaluation of the firms, 
the panel unanimously selected Lockton Companies as the firm best suited to meet the County's 
needs. 
The County's healthcare plan is self-funded, meaning the County assumes the financial responsibility 
for paying employee and dependent medical claims rather than purchasing a fully insured health plan. 
The County contracts with various vendors to administer the plan, provide networks of medical, dental 
and prescription providers, manage prescription benefits, and provide other services. An experienced 
benefits broker serves as an advisor and advocate for the County in managing these relationships 
and overseeing the overall performance and financial health of the benefits program. 
  
A benefits broker provides expertise and resources that would be difficult and costly for the County to 
maintain internally. Key benefits of having a broker include: 
  
 Providing financial oversight and cost management by analyzing claims experience, identifying 
cost trends and emerging healthcare expenses, evaluating plan performance, and 
recommending strategies to manage costs while maintaining competitive benefits. 
 Assisting with vendor advocacy and accountability by representing the County's interests when 
working with insurance carriers, pharmacy benefit managers, and other vendors.  
 Assisting with large claim management and marketing the County's stop-loss coverage 
annually. Negotiating competitive pricing and contract terms, evaluating carrier financial 
Page 6 of 82
strength, and assist with large claims and reimbursement issues. Stop-loss insurance protects 
the County against unusually high individual claims and overall plan claims exceeding 
established coverage limits. 
 Providing financial projections, renewal estimates, funding recommendations, and multiple 
budget scenarios to help the County make informed decisions and plan for future healthcare 
costs. This is particularly important given the County's annual budget development timeline. 
 Providing guidance on changing federal and state regulations, benefits plan requirements, and 
compliance responsibilities.  
  
 Providing access to industry data, public-sector comparisons, and knowledge of emerging 
healthcare trends, products, and cost-containment strategies. 
Having a broker who takes an active role in these areas helps the County make informed decisions, 
manage financial risk, hold vendors accountable, and provide a competitive benefits program to 
employees while being responsible stewards of taxpayer dollars. 
  
The RFP requested a comprehensive range of strategic, financial, operational, and compliance 
services, including: 
  
1. Strategic Benefits Consulting 
o Develop short- and long-term benefits strategies. 
o Recommend plan design enhancements and cost-containment initiatives. 
o Assist with vendor evaluations and procurement activities. 
o Benchmark plan performance against public-sector and regional peers. 
2. Self-Funded Plan Consulting 
o Provide ongoing analysis of claims experience and utilization trends. 
o Conduct financial forecasting and reserve analysis. 
o Provide funding recommendations. 
o Evaluate emerging healthcare cost drivers and identify opportunities to improve plan 
performance. 
3. Stop-Loss Procurement and Management 
o Market the County's stop-loss coverage annually and negotiate favorable terms and 
pricing. 
o Evaluate carrier financial strength and contract provisions. 
o Provide detailed stop-loss renewal analysis. 
o Assist with large claim reviews and stop-loss reimbursement issues. 
4. Reporting and Analytics 
o Provide monthly claims reporting, year-to-date claims dashboards, large claimant 
reporting, utilization reporting, trend analysis, and financial forecasting. 
o Provide an interactive dashboard and/or reporting platform that allows County staff to 
review key plan metrics monthly, including claims by service category, cost trends, stop-
loss performance, and budget-to-actual comparisons. 
5. Budget Planning Support 
o Provide preliminary renewal projections in advance of County budget deadlines. 
o Develop budget forecasts and multiple funding scenarios. 
o Present healthcare cost projections to County leadership. 
o Assist with long-range financial planning and understand the County's public-sector 
budget development process, which begins in February/March each year. 
6. Compliance Support 
o Provide guidance on ACA, HIPAA, and COBRA requirements, as applicable. 
o Provide federal and state regulatory updates and legislative monitoring. 
7. Vendor Management

The interview panel identified several strengths that distinguished Lockton's proposal and 
presentation: 
 A large team with diverse professional backgrounds and specialized expertise available to 
support the County's benefits program. 
 Lockton currently serves 241 public-sector clients and demonstrated a strong understanding of 
the importance of being responsible stewards of taxpayer dollars. Their experience with public 
entities provides familiarity with the unique budgeting, reporting, and accountability 
requirements of local government. 
 Lockton emphasized building a genuine partnership with the County by taking the time to 
understand its needs, challenges, and goals rather than offering a one-size-fits-all approach. 
 Lockton took the initiative to review the County's current employee benefits guide, which is 
publicly available on the County's website, before the interview. They had already identified 
potential opportunities to improve benefit offerings and employee education. 
 Provided a clear plan of action by coming prepared with a detailed plan for the first project they 
would assist the County with, including dedicated team support throughout the process. This 
demonstrated a willingness to take initiative and begin identifying opportunities before being 
selected. 
 Lockton has an in-house attorney available to assist with regulatory compliance and related 
matters. 
 Demonstrated extensive stop-loss resources and capabilities, including an understanding of 
increased underwriting scrutiny and the availability of nurses on staff to assist with large claims 
and related matters. 
 Offered multiple compensation arrangements, including direct payment by the County or a 
combination of direct payment and vendor commissions. This provides the County with 
flexibility in structuring compensation and greater transparency into the cost of services. 
 Lockton's privately owned structure allows the firm to emphasize client relationships and 
service without some of the competing priorities associated with publicly traded firms. 
Overall, what stood out most to the interview panel was that Lockton did more than describe the 
services they could provide. They demonstrated their approach through their preparation, knowledge 
of the County's existing benefits program, identification of potential opportunities, and willingness to 
develop a plan before being selected. This gave the panel confidence that Lockton would be a 
proactive, engaged partner invested in the County's success. 
  
Based on the proposals and interviews, the panel unanimously recommended Lockton Companies. 
 
FISCAL IMPACT: 
The compensation arrangements proposed by Lockton differ from the County's current arrangement 
with Willis Towers Watson. 
  
Under the current arrangement, Willis Towers Watson receives a 4.5% direct commission on the 
County's stop-loss coverage. This is the only line of business for which Willis receives a direct 
commission. The commission is built into the stop-loss premiums the County pays to the stop-loss 
carrier each month. Based on the current contract and stop-loss premiums, Willis Towers Watson 
earns approximately $73,170 annually through this commission. 
  
Page 3 of 10
Page 8 of 82
In addition to the direct stop-loss commission, Willis Towers Watson receives override commissions 
from insurance carriers. These commissions are based on the broker's overall book of business with 
a particular insurance carrier. The amount of override commissions attributable to the County's 
business is not currently identified as a separate amount in the County's direct compensation 
arrangement. 
  
Lockton's proposal includes a total annual fee of $115,000 for the scope of services requested in the 
RFP. Lockton offers flexibility in how the fee is paid, including: 
 Direct payment by the County. 
 A combination of direct payment by the County and commissions received from vendors or 
carriers. 
The final compensation structure has not been determined and will be part of the contract 
negotiations. 
  
For comparison purposes, Lockton's proposed $115,000 annual fee is $41,830 more than the 
approximately $73,170 in annual direct stop-loss commission currently earned by Willis Towers 
Watson. However, this is not a direct comparison of the total cost of the two arrangements because it 
does not account for the additional override commissions received by Willis or any commissions that 
may be included in Lockton's final compensation arrangement. 
  
The County will seek a transparent compensation structure that clearly identifies the fees and 
commissions associated with the services provided and allows the County to understand the total 
cost of the broker relationship. 
  
The final fiscal impact will depend on the negotiated contract terms and compensation arrangement. 
No additional County expenditure is being requested for approval at this time.  
 
NEXT STEPS OR FUTURE ACTIONS: 
If authorized by the BOCC, staff will begin contract negotiations with Lockton Companies. 
Negotiations will include the final scope of services, annual compensation, fee and commission 
structure, contract term, performance expectations, and other contractual provisions. 
  
  
The County's goal is to complete negotiations and establish a November 1, 2026 start date for the 
new contract. The anticipated start date is intended to provide time for an orderly transition from Willis 
Towers Watson and allow Lockton to become familiar with the County's existing benefits program, 
vendors, plan financials, and upcoming renewal and budget planning activities. 
 
RECOMMENDED MOTION: 
Authorize the County Administrator to enter into contract negotiations with Lockton Companies for 
employee benefits broker and consulting services, with the goal of establishing a contract effective 
November 1, 2026.

Subject: Employee Benefits Broker and Consulting Services
BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Douglas County currently utilizes Willis Towers Watson as its employee benefits broker and 
consultant. The County has not conducted a competitive RFP for benefits broker services in at least 
15 years. Given the size and complexity of the County's self-funded healthcare plan, the financial 
impact of healthcare costs, and the important role a benefits broker plays in managing the plan, staff 
determined it was an appropriate time to conduct a competitive review of available firms, services, 
expertise, and compensation arrangements.
 
The County issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) for employee benefits broker and consulting 
services and received eight proposals, including one from the incumbent, Willis Towers Watson. 
Following an initial evaluation of the proposals, three firms were selected for interviews: Gallagher, 
Lockton Companies, and Willis Towers Watson.
 
The interview panel consisted of County Administration staff, two members of the Healthcare 
Committee, and the Procurement Coordinator. Following the interviews and evaluation of the firms, 
the panel unanimously selected Lockton Companies as the firm best suited to meet the County's 
needs.
The County's healthcare plan is self-funded, meaning the County assumes the financial responsibility 
for paying employee and dependent medical claims rather than purchasing a fully insured health plan. 
The County contracts with various vendors to administer the plan, provide networks of medical, dental 
and prescription providers, manage prescription benefits, and provide other services. An experienced 
benefits broker serves as an advisor and advocate for the County in managing these relationships 
and overseeing the overall performance and financial health of the benefits program.
 
A benefits broker provides expertise and resources that would be difficult and costly for the County to 
maintain internally. Key benefits of having a broker include:
 
Providing financial oversight and cost management by analyzing claims experience, identifying 
cost trends and emerging healthcare expenses, evaluating plan performance, and 
recommending strategies to manage costs while maintaining competitive benefits.
Assisting with vendor advocacy and accountability by representing the County's interests when 
working with insurance carriers, pharmacy benefit managers, and other vendors. 
Assisting with large claim management and marketing the County's stop-loss coverage 
annually. Negotiating competitive pricing and contract terms, evaluating carrier financial 
Page 16 of 82
strength, and assist with large claims and reimbursement issues. Stop-loss insurance protects 
the County against unusually high individual claims and overall plan claims exceeding 
established coverage limits.
Providing financial projections, renewal estimates, funding recommendations, and multiple 
budget scenarios to help the County make informed decisions and plan for future healthcare 
costs. This is particularly important given the County's annual budget development timeline.
Providing guidance on changing federal and state regulations, benefits plan requirements, and 
compliance responsibilities. 
 
Providing access to industry data, public-sector comparisons, and knowledge of emerging 
healthcare trends, products, and cost-containment strategies.
Having a broker who takes an active role in these areas helps the County make informed decisions, 
manage financial risk, hold vendors accountable, and provide a competitive benefits program to 
employees while being responsible stewards of taxpayer dollars.
 
The RFP requested a comprehensive range of strategic, financial, operational, and compliance 
services, including:
 
1. Strategic Benefits Consulting
oDevelop short- and long-term benefits strategies.
oRecommend plan design enhancements and cost-containment initiatives.
oAssist with vendor evaluations and procurement activities.
oBenchmark plan performance against public-sector and regional peers.
2. Self-Funded Plan Consulting
oProvide ongoing analysis of claims experience and utilization trends.
oConduct financial forecasting and reserve analysis.
oProvide funding recommendations.
oEvaluate emerging healthcare cost drivers and identify opportunities to improve plan 
performance.
3. Stop-Loss Procurement and Management
oMarket the County's stop-loss coverage annually and negotiate favorable terms and 
pricing.
oEvaluate carrier financial strength and contract provisions.
oProvide detailed stop-loss renewal analysis.
oAssist with large claim reviews and stop-loss reimbursement issues.
4. Reporting and Analytics
oProvide monthly claims reporting, year-to-date claims dashboards, large claimant 
reporting, utilization reporting, trend analysis, and financial forecasting.
oProvide an interactive dashboard and/or reporting platform that allows County staff to 
review key plan metrics monthly, including claims by service category, cost trends, stop-
loss performance, and budget-to-actual comparisons.
5. Budget Planning Support
oProvide preliminary renewal projections in advance of County budget deadlines.
oDevelop budget forecasts and multiple funding scenarios.
oPresent healthcare cost projections to County leadership.
oAssist with long-range financial planning and understand the County's public-sector 
budget development process, which begins in February/March each year.
6. Compliance Support
oProvide guidance on ACA, HIPAA, and COBRA requirements, as applicable.
oProvide federal and state regulatory updates and legislative monitoring.
7. Vendor Management
Page 17 of 82
oAct as an advocate for the County with its benefits vendors.
oFacilitate issue resolution and monitor vendor performance.
The selected firm is also expected to participate in BOCC, Healthcare Committee, and other County 
meetings as requested.
 
The interview panel identified several strengths that distinguished Lockton's proposal and 
presentation:
A large team with diverse professional backgrounds and specialized expertise available to 
support the County's benefits program.
Lockton currently serves 241 public-sector clients and demonstrated a strong understanding of 
the importance of being responsible stewards of taxpayer dollars. Their experience with public 
entities provides familiarity with the unique budgeting, reporting, and accountability 
requirements of local government.
Lockton emphasized building a genuine partnership with the County by taking the time to 
understand its needs, challenges, and goals rather than offering a one-size-fits-all approach.
Lockton took the initiative to review the County's current employee benefits guide, which is 
publicly available on the County's website, before the interview. They had already identified 
potential opportunities to improve benefit offerings and employee education.
Provided a clear plan of action by coming prepared with a detailed plan for the first project they 
would assist the County with, including dedicated team support throughout the process. This 
demonstrated a willingness to take initiative and begin identifying opportunities before being 
selected.
Lockton has an in-house attorney available to assist with regulatory compliance and related 
matters.
Demonstrated extensive stop-loss resources and capabilities, including an understanding of 
increased underwriting scrutiny and the availability of nurses on staff to assist with large claims 
and related matters.
Offered multiple compensation arrangements, including direct payment by the County or a 
combination of direct payment and vendor commissions. This provides the County with 
flexibility in structuring compensation and greater transparency into the cost of services.
Lockton's privately owned structure allows the firm to emphasize client relationships and 
service without some of the competing priorities associated with publicly traded firms.
Overall, what stood out most to the interview panel was that Lockton did more than describe the 
services they could provide. They demonstrated their approach through their preparation, knowledge 
of the County's existing benefits program, identification of potential opportunities, and willingness to 
develop a plan before being selected. This gave the panel confidence that Lockton would be a 
proactive, engaged partner invested in the County's success.
 
Based on the proposals and interviews, the panel unanimously recommended Lockton Companies.
FISCAL IMPACT:
The compensation arrangements proposed by Lockton differ from the County's current arrangement 
with Willis Towers Watson.
 
Under the current arrangement, Willis Towers Watson receives a 4.5% direct commission on the 
County's stop-loss coverage. This is the only line of business for which Willis receives a direct 
commission. The commission is built into the stop-loss premiums the County pays to the stop-loss 
carrier each month. Based on the current contract and stop-loss premiums, Willis Towers Watson 
earns approximately $73,170 annually through this commission.
 
Page 18 of 82
In addition to the direct stop-loss commission, Willis Towers Watson receives override commissions 
from insurance carriers. These commissions are based on the broker's overall book of business with 
a particular insurance carrier. The amount of override commissions attributable to the County's 
business is not currently identified as a separate amount in the County's direct compensation 
arrangement.
 
Lockton's proposal includes a total annual fee of $115,000 for the scope of services requested in the 
RFP. Lockton offers flexibility in how the fee is paid, including:
Direct payment by the County.
A combination of direct payment by the County and commissions received from vendors or 
carriers.
The final compensation structure has not been determined and will be part of the contract 
negotiations.
 
For comparison purposes, Lockton's proposed $115,000 annual fee is $41,830 more than the 
approximately $73,170 in annual direct stop-loss commission currently earned by Willis Towers 
Watson. However, this is not a direct comparison of the total cost of the two arrangements because it 
does not account for the additional override commissions received by Willis or any commissions that 
may be included in Lockton's final compensation arrangement.
 
The County will seek a transparent compensation structure that clearly identifies the fees and 
commissions associated with the services provided and allows the County to understand the total 
cost of the broker relationship.
 
The final fiscal impact will depend on the negotiated contract terms and compensation arrangement. 
No additional County expenditure is being requested for approval at this time.

Subject: Informational Comparison of Neighboring Kansas County Firework Regulations 
 
Purpose 
The Commission has requested information on extended fireworks sales dates in Kansas which 
took effect following the adoption of SB 199. This memorandum is intended to provide 
background information on local and State fireworks regulations, to identify similarities and 
differences between Douglas County’s fireworks code and those of neighboring communities, 
and to give an overview of how neighboring counties have responded to the adoption of SB 199. 
Additionally, staff gathered feedback from relevant agencies on a potential change. 
Background & Legal Framework 
K.S.A 31-501 through 31-512 is known as the “Kansas Fireworks Act”. In April of 2025, S.B. 
199 became effective. Among other things, S.B. 199 amended the definition of a “seasonal 
retailer” of fireworks from June 27th - July 5th to June 20th - July 7th. 
If a county does not have locally adopted fireworks regulations, regulation of the sale and 
discharge of fireworks defers to Kansas Fire Marshal, who is charged with administering the 
rules and regulations of the Kansas Fireworks Act. 
Douglas County has used Home Rule authority to restrict the dates in which fireworks could be 
sold and discharged since 1972, when the Board of County Commissioners adopted Res. 72-1. 
From 1972 to 2007, temporary retail sales were restricted to July 2nd-July 4th. This timeframe 
was updated to July 1st-July 4th in 2007, when the Commission adopted HR 7-6-4. The fireworks 
regulations have been amended multiple times since 2007; however the permitted sales dates 
have since remained unchanged. 
Douglas County currently regulates the sale and discharge of fireworks under HR 21-9-2. 
Among other things, this adopted code has restricted the sale of fireworks to July 1st-4th. Douglas 
County fireworks regulations are within Chapter 6 of the County Code (Fire Safety).
