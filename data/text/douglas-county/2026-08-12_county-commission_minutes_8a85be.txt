 3.1. ADMINISTRATION/TAX INCREMENT FINANCING DISTRICT FOR CITY OF 
EUDORA 08-12-26 
The Board received a presentation from Zack Daniel, Eudora City Manager, 
regarding a Tax increment Financing (TIF) District proposed on 10 Highway 
and E 2300 Road.  
  
The City received an economic development incentive application from Main 
Page 2 of 7
Street Development Group for a mixed use, commercial/industrial development 
project for approximately 133 acres in northeast Eudora in a recently annexed 
area commonly referred to as the Eisele property. The City of Eudora held a 
public hearing on July 27, 2026 and adopted Ordinance No.1189 making 
findings and establishing a redevelopment district pursuant to K.S.A. 12-1770. 
The hearing triggered the statutory notice period for Eudora USD and Douglas 
County to review the proposed district and determine whether the project may 
cause an adverse effect. The project is envisioned as being in two phases with 
total infrastructure improvement costs estimated at $31,363,050. 
  
Commissioners asked questions regarding annexation status which was 
already annexed and zoned, the purpose of industrial outdoor storage, and the 
rationale for the 100% TIF request. Daniel emphasized that negotiations with 
the developer and final determinations are ongoing, and the County will be 
notified of future hearings and decisions. Daniel confirmed that the County has 
30 days from July 27 to identify an adverse effect through resolution.If a 
revised TIF percentage is negotiated, the County will receive notice but will not 
vote again. 
  
Matt Gough, representing Main Street Development Group LLC, described the 
project’s goals: 1) The TIF district would enable the developer to construct 
necessary public infrastructure (roads, sewer, water extensions) to create 
developable commercial and industrial lots; 2) All upfront costs are borne by 
the developer and tax increment reimbursements occur only after infrastructure 
investment; 3) Neither the City nor County would be obligated to pay costs not 
covered by future increment; 4) The County’s existing tax base would remain 
intact; and 5) Without formation of the TIF district, the developer may elect not 
to purchase the property, leaving the land undeveloped until a future buyer 
emerges. 
  
Reid opened the item for public comment. 
  
Eric Hyde, 66046, expressed concern that a single developer would receive 
public benefits through the TIF while others would not, and he questioned the 
fairness and pace of ongoing development. He added that the project appears 
to fail the “but-for” test suggesting it would not happen without a TIF and 
shared his personal discomfort with how rapidly the area is changing. 
  
Reid closed public comment.  
  
Discussion by the Commission included:  
 If approved, the County would continue receiving current base tax 
revenues regardless of TIF percentage.  
 There was concern about the impacts on roads, emergency services 
and the Sheriff. Traffic and road impacts appear minimal because 
primary access is within the City of Eudora. Fire service impacts fall 
under the city’s jurisdiction.  
 Commissioners expressed surprise at the 20-year, 100% request, noting 
that recent TIF approvals in the region often fall below 100%.  
 Commissioners encouraged Eudora to consider community benefit 
when determining final TIF percentages.

 City representatives clarified that the school district’s 20-mill levy is 
protected by statute and not subject to the TIF. 
  
Kelly moved to authorize support for the Tax Increment Financing (TIF) District 
near K-10 Highway and E 2300 Road in Eudora, Kansas.  Motion was 
seconded by Reid and passed 3-1-1 with Anderson opposed and one recusal

 3.2. PLANNING/COMP PLAN AMENDMENT, CHAPTER 8 08-12-26 
The Board considered a Comprehensive Plan Amendment to Plan 2040 
Chapter 8C: Specific Plans, AMDT-26-0012, revising the West of K-10 Plan. 
Katherine Weik, Lawrence-Douglas County Metropolitan Planning Staff, 
presented the item.  
  
Weik presented a proposed amendment to the West of K-10 Area Plan, which 
is adopted by reference within Plan 2040, and therefore requires approval from 
the Planning Commission, Lawrence City Commission, and Douglas County 
Commission. The Planning Commission has recommended approval. Weik 
explained that the amendment is development-driven, requested by an 
applicant pursuing annexation, rezonings, and subdivision processes within the 
City of Lawrence. Because the broader West of K-10 Plan is currently 
undergoing a multi-year update process, staff is required to process 
amendments tied to active applications under existing adopted plans. 
  
Weik reviewed the criteria for Comprehensive Plan Amendment (CPA)s and 
stated staff found the proposed changes met all required considerations, 
including consistency with long-range goals, compatibility with adjacent land 
uses, and alignment with updated community priorities. The amendment 
adjusts classifications within Tier 2 growth areas, shifts some high-density 
residential designations, reduces industrial designations, and incorporates a 
51-acre conservation easement.  
  
Staff recommended approval of this Comprehensive Plan Amendment to Plan 
2040: Chapter 8C: Specific Plans, revising the West of K-10 Plan to include 
changes to the recommended Future Land Use designation, to revise the 
Future Land Use map and to incorporate general revisions and updates to the 
West of K-10 Plan.  
  
Corby Rust, Landplan Engineering, explained that the proposed CPA and 
annexation align closely with the existing West of K-10 plan. The request 
includes five zoning districts: low, medium, and high-density residential; a 
commercial center; and 51 acres of open space. A new 51-acre conservation 
easement replaces the previously planned industrial/warehouse zoning in that 
area. 
  
He noted that a detailed service plan accompanies the submittal, outlining 
future sanitary sewer, stormwater mitigation, stream preservation, water 
distribution, public transit, and street networks. Street planning, especially 
George Williams Way and N. 875 Road, guided updates to land use and 
zoning.

Rust emphasized that the proposal maintains overall consistency with the 
existing plan, identifies community benefits and emergency service 
considerations, and has both staff and Planning Commission support. He 
concluded that the annexation enables thoughtful, well-planned growth and 
requested approval. 
  
 Discussion included: 
 Willey asked whether high-quality prairies in the plan area would be 
affected by the Commission’s vote. Weik responded that while 
annexation could allow development on prime agricultural soils, other 
sensitive lands such as native prairies, streams, drainageways, slopes, 
floodways, and tree stands are protected under the land development 
code and would be reviewed and enforced during annexation, rezoning, 
subdivision, and site-planning processes. 
 Anderson questioned how often developers request amendments to the 
Comprehensive Plan to this degree. Staff responded that the plan is 
meant to be flexible and adaptable to changes. Weik explained that 
while Comprehensive Plan Amendments vary in scale, this request is 
unusual because it covers a large undeveloped area and coincides with 
an ongoing, but not yet completed, update to the West of K-10 Plan. 
She added that although draft language for the broader plan revision 
exists, it is still mid-process and has not gone through required public 
engagement, so the current amendment proceeds independently but 
remains generally aligned with the direction of the evolving update. 
 The amendment applies only to land in Tier 2. Tier 2 is all land focused 
and anticipated to be annexed or developed when drafted in 2009, in 
Plan 2020 and also anticipated in 2040. There is a small piece of Tier 3 
land that is not a part of this approval because the land use designation 
already anticipates the applicant’s proposed intensity and would not be 
a CPA.  
  
Reid opened the item for public comment.  
  
Dominque Sexton, 66049, urged the Commission to retain open-space 
designations in the future land-use map, and that visible stream corridors like 
Yankee Tank help the public understand the landscape’s history and ecological 
significance. She also emphasized that the area contains sensitive species and 
remnants of prairie and cautioned that updates to the map must reflect those 
realities so that important natural features are not overlooked. 
  
Reid closed public comment.  
  
Kelly said what he saw was not drastically different than what is in the K-10 
Plan. This is the approval of a future land use map but there is still another 
level of zoning that will need to happen and individual development plans on 
each parcel. It is a change and the need for industrial land is not as intense as 
once thought. The adjustment seems to make sense. He would also like to see 
the stream corridors on the map.

Willey saidthe stream corridor maps were helpful and that shifting land
