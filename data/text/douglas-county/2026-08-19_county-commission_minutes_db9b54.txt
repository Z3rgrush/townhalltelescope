3. REGULAR AGENDA 08-19-26 
 
 3.1. ADMINISTRATION/FY 2025 AUDIT 08-19-26 
John Nibarger, Senior Vice President of Assurance Services with AGH, LC., 
presented the Board with the annual Auditor’s Report and related findings.  
Page 1 of 3
  
Nibarger provided an overview of the auditor’s communication letter and 
highlighted key points from the audit which included an unmodified opinion. 
Additionally, the auditors identified no significant deficiencies or material 
weaknesses in internal controls. The 2025 Audit reflects a strong level of 
financial reporting and internal control compliance. 
  
Brooke Sauer, Finance Manager, presented a comprehensive overview of the 
County’s 2025 financial statements. She explained the year-end 
unencumbered cash balance of $133.9 million and detailed how those funds 
were categorized into restricted, committed, assigned, and unassigned 
classifications. She emphasized that the majority of the balance, 73%, is not 
available for general use because it is already dedicated to specific purposes 
or long-term plans.  
  
Sarah Plinsky, County Administrator, clarified misconceptions reported publicly 
that the funds were unplanned, noting that committed funds represent 
multi-year, budgeted investments in infrastructure and equipment. 
  
Sauer further reviewed the audit schedules, describing what each schedule 
covers budgeted funds, non-budgeted funds, related municipal entities, and 
agency funds used for tax distributions. She concluded by outlining ongoing 
work with the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) to review and 
strengthen          the County’s financial policies, which will be brought to the 
Commission in the coming months. 
  
Discussion included: 
 Dorsey asked why the mental health sales tax is called a capital fund. 
Staff explained it is because the County pays for debt service for the 
Treatment and Recover Center (TRC) and is viewed as the overriding 
classification for that fund. 
 Dorsey inquired about an adjustment to the unencumbered cash 
balance related to a prior-year payable. Nibarger explained that an 
accounts payable item had been recorded in last year’s financial 
statements as part of an audit adjustment but was not reflected in the 
County’s day-to-day records,  
 Dorsey asked about the reference to a comprehensive policy and 
procedure review. Nibarger noted this is standard information included 
for all clients, emphasizing general best practices around electronic 
transfers, cybersecurity, and periodic policy reviews, and confirmed 
there were no specific concerns for the County. 
 Anderson asked about internal controls and whether additional audits 
such as performance reviews might help identify improvement 
opportunities. Nibarger replied that while the financial audit did not 
identify any material weaknesses or significant deficiencies, other types 
of audits could be beneficial if the County desired deeper review in 
specific areas. 
 Willey inquired about the County’s ongoing financial policy overhaul with 
assistance from GFOA. Staff explained that a comprehensive list of 
policy recommendations will be brought to the Commission within 30–60 
days and discussed timing considerations related to the upcoming

procurement and 2027 implementation of the new financial and HR 
system. 
  
Reid opened the item for public comment. No comment was received.  
  
Reid moved to accept the 2025 financial statement. Motion was seconded by
