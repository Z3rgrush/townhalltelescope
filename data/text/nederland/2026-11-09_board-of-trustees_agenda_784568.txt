regardless of the heading under which such item appears. Discussion items 
may become action items if the Board determines that deferring final action 
on an item to a subsequent meeting is unnecessary or unwarranted and that 
taking  immediate  action does not compromise any third-party's rights.

Tuesday meetings and are available for inspection at Town Hall during normal 
business hours. The information is reviewed and studied by the Board of 
Trustee members, eliminating lengthy discussions to gain basic 
understanding. Short discussion on agenda items does not reflect lack of 
thought or analysis. The agendas are posted at Town Hall, the post office, 
and on the Town's website on the Friday prior to the meeting. Copies of the 
agenda and meeting packet are available at no cost via email from the Town 
Clerk or from the  Town website at www.nederlandco.org.

Section 1. Definition. "Acquisition costs" means fees and expenses for legal, financial, advisory, due-
diligence, transition, and other services related to the potential acquisition, financing, or operation of 
Eldora Mountain Resort that the Town would owe whether or not a closing occurs. It does not include 
amounts payable only from bond proceeds at a closing. 
Section 2. Spending halt. The Town shall incur no acquisition costs for work performed after the 
effective date of this resolution, except for work the Town Manager requests under Section 4, unless the 
Board authorizes them by a further resolution. Within three business days, the Town Manager shall 
direct each firm engaged for the acquisition, in writing, to stop billable work for the Town other than 
that requested work. This section does not affect amounts the Town already owes for work performed 
before the effective date. 
Section 3. Board approval of engagements. No new engagement, contract amendment, or expansion of 
scope that would incur acquisition costs may be made without prior approval of the Board by resolution. 
To the extent Resolutions 2025-03, 2025-11, 2025-16, 2025-19, and 2025-22 authorize the Town 
Manager to engage counsel, advisors, or "additional consultants, as necessary," that authority is limited 
by this section. 
Section 4. Final accounting. Within 14 days, the Town Manager shall ask each firm engaged for the 
acquisition for (a) fees paid, billed, and unbilled for work performed through the effective date of this 
resolution, and (b) any amount the Town would owe under the engagement's terms if the acquisition 
does not close. The Town Attorney shall identify which of the Town's obligations are owed now and 
which would be owed only at a closing. At the next regular meeting after that period, the Town Manager 
shall present a written report, included in the meeting packet, itemizing those amounts by firm and 
comparing the total with the Town's Sept. 30, 2026, estimate of about $2,500,000. 
Section 5. Milestones. The following milestones apply: 
1. By Jan. 31, 2027, a purchase agreement approved by the Board and signed by all parties, and

2. By April 30, 2027, a closing.

Dear BOT,
I have spent the last couple weeks coming up to speed on the Eldora purchase.  I’ve reviewed all the relevant documents - resolutions,
ordinances, agreements, budgets, audits, FAQs, AIMs, news articles, and BOT meeting recordings. I’ve reached several conclusions
which you can find below.
1. The already-incurred costs should not decide the purchase.  The relevant decision is whether the benefits justify the remaining costs
and risks to both the town and ski resort.
2. The draft indenture document is very complex.  The draft combines multiple payment priorities, capital-appreciation bonds, several
reserve accounts, detailed rules for moving money, and conditions for future borrowing.  The BOT needs to understand each provision
separately, as well as how they interact during a weak season and with a major equipment failure. My concern is whether the town has
the staff, independent expertise, and continuing resources to oversee the debt throughout the bond’s life.  Professional advisors help,
but the BOT still needs enough understanding to evaluate their recommendations.
Before proceeding with the bond issuance, I would like to see the BOT and public receive:
A. A plain-language explanation of every bond series and fund.
B. A complete annual repayment schedule, including accumulated interest.
C. Cash-flow projections for average seasons, consecutive weak seasons, and major equipment failures.
D. A clear account of what happens when reserves run out.
E. An explanation of who monitors compliance and what it costs.
F. An answer to the question “Can Eldora repay all principal and interest by final maturity, without assuming any future
refinance?”
G. An explanation of the conditions under which surplus funds are available for release to the town.
H. An explanation of how the $20 M in donations can replace the CapEx Requirement of $47,488,000.

A. accounting and cash management

D. lift licensing and inspections
E. permits and environmental obligations
F. maintenance and asset management
G. contracts and purchasing
H. pricing and business strategy
I. pass partnerships and revenue agreements
J. customer operations and technology
K. food, retail, rentals and ski school
L. public governance and reporting
M. personnel services
N. capital expenditure planning

budget for operation of the ski resort over the first few years of ownership?
