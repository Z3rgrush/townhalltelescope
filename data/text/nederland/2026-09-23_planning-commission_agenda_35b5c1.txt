AGENDA ITEM  
Rezone 242 E 5th St  (Legal Description: LOT 12 NEDERLAND HEIGHTS) & 252 E 5th St (Legal 
Description: LOT 13 NEDERLAND HEIGHTS), Nederland CO 80466 from General Commercial (GC) 
to Medium Density Residential (MDR). 
SUMMARY 
The applicant, LandShark Investments LLC, is seeking to rezone 242 E. 5th Street and 252 E. 
5th Street from General Commercial (GC) to Medium Density Residential (MDR). The stated 
purpose of the request is to allow construction of two single-family A-frame residences, one on 
each lot. The applicant proposes homes of approximately 2,100 square feet each, designed in a 
mountain-style A-frame architecture. 
The subject properties are currently vacant and undeveloped. The applicant states that site specific 
conditions, including limited highway access, limited commercial visibility and customer access, 
and significant slope, reduce the viability of commercial development and make residential use a 
more appropriate long-term land use.

CURRENT USE: 
The two subject parcels are vacant and undeveloped. They are currently zoned General 
Commercial (GC). 
GENERAL COMMERICAL ZONING: 
The applicant contends that continued General Commercial zoning is impractical because the 
properties do not have feasible direct commercial access from Boulder Canyon Drive (State 
Highway 119), are removed from the main flow of commercial traffic, and contain significant slope. 
The narrative states that commercial development would likely require parking, loading, circulation, 
accessibility improvements, grading, signage, lighting, and customer activity that would be difficult 
or visually intrusive on these lots. 
MEDIUM RESIDENTIAL ZONING APPLICANT’S PROPOSAL: 
The requested Medium Density Residential designation would allow the applicant to pursue one 
single-family homes on each lot. The applicant describes the homes as approximately 2,100 square 
feet each and designed in a mountain-style A-frame form intended to complement Nederland’s 
mountain town character. The applicant states that residential development can be integrated

commercial development. 
COMPREHENSIVE PLAN REVIEW: 
The applicant’s narrative references the Town of Nederland Comprehensive Plan adopted in March 
2025 and identifies several areas of consistency with the requested rezoning: 
Housing: The proposal would convert two vacant parcels into two residential housing opportunities 
within Town limits. The applicant states this supports the Comprehensive Plan’s goals related to 
sufficient housing and increased housing opportunities. 
Infill Development: The parcels are within existing Town boundaries, and the applicant states they 
are already served by municipal infrastructure. The proposal is characterized as infill development 
that uses existing streets, utilities, and municipal services. 
Community Character: The proposed A-frame residences are described as modestly scaled and 
reflective of traditional mountain architecture. The applicant contends that two homes would 
better preserve the quiet residential character of the surrounding area than a customer-oriented 
commercial development. 
Sustainable Land Use: The applicant states that the requested zoning better reflects the physical 
characteristics of the properties, including access limitations and topography, and would avoid 
extensive parking construction and unnecessary grading. 
ANALYSIS: 
Nederland Municipal Code (NMC) Section 16-255 provides the criteria and conditions for 
rezoning. Only one of the three criteria must be met for a rezoning. The section states that, for 
the purpose of establishing sound, stable and desirable development within the Town, rezoning of 
land is to be discouraged and should only be considered if one of the following applies: 
(1) The land to be rezoned was zoned in error and as presently zoned is inconsistent with the 
policies and goals of the Comprehensive Plan; 
Staff has not identified documentation demonstrating that the properties were zoned General 
Commercial (GC) in error. Therefore, Staff has not found evidence in the record to support the first 
component of this criterion. 
With respect to consistency with the Comprehensive Plan, the 2025 Comprehensive Plan identifies 
housing availability, infill development, efficient use of existing infrastructure, and preservation of 
Nederland’s mountain-town character as important land-use considerations. The subject 
properties are vacant parcels located within Town limits. Rezoning the properties to Medium 
Density Residential (MDR) would provide additional land for residential development within the 
existing developed area of Town and would be consistent with the Comprehensive Plan’s objectives 
related to increasing housing opportunities and accommodating growth through infill development.

While the proposed MDR zoning may further several Comprehensive Plan objectives, Staff has not 
identified evidence demonstrating that the properties were originally zoned in error and supporting 
the first component of Criterion (1). While the proposed MDR zoning may be generally consistent 
with several objectives of the Comprehensive Plan, including housing opportunities, the Planning

were originally zoned in error and Criterion (1) has been satisfied.  
(2) The area for which rezoning is requested has changed or is changing to such a degree that it 
is in the public interest to encourage a redevelopment of the area; 
The subject properties are currently vacant and zoned General Commercial. Access to the 
properties is provided from East 5th Street. Although the southern portion of the properties is 
adjacent to the State Highway 119 corridor, property controlled by the Colorado Department of 
Transportation lies between the subject parcels and the highway, limiting direct access from the 
highway. The properties also contain sloping terrain that may affect the type and intensity of 
development that can reasonably occur on the site. 
The surrounding area contains a mix of existing residential development and vacant or undeveloped 
properties. Properties immediately to the west of the subject parcels contain residential uses 
despite being zoned General Commercial (GC), and other nearby GC-zoned properties remain 
vacant. This development pattern suggests that the area does not function exclusively as a 
commercial district and includes established residential uses. Staff notes that  rezoning the subject 
parcels to Medium Density Residential (MDR) would be generally compatible with the existing 
residential character in this portion of East 5th Street and would allow currently vacant properties 
to be developed for residential use. 
The Comprehensive Plan supports infill development and additional housing within Town limits, 
and residential development of vacant property could advance those objectives. Based on Staff's 
review, the existing mix of residential development, vacant properties, and underutilized 
commercially zoned land may support a determination that the area has changed, or is changing, 
to such a degree that redevelopment would be in the public interest. The Planning Commission 
must determine whether the evidence in the record is sufficient to satisfy Criterion (2). 
(3) The proposed rezoning is necessary in order to provide land for a community-related use 
which was not anticipated at the time of the adoption of the Comprehensive Plan, and such 
rezoning will be consistent with the policies and goals of the Comprehensive Plan. 
 
The proposed rezoning would allow the properties to be developed for residential purposes. 
Additional residential development is not a use that was unanticipated when the 2025 
Comprehensive Plan was adopted. Housing is specifically identified as a community need, and the 
Comprehensive Plan supports increasing housing opportunities, infill development, efficient use of 
existing infrastructure, and preservation of Nederland’s mountain-town character.

AGENDA I TEM 
Outdoor Commercial Activities – Use Group Table Discussion 
 
PURPOSE  
The purpose of this agenda item is for the Planning to consider and take action on the ordinance 
presented tonight, which would amend the Use Group Table to allow “Outdoor sales, eating and 
drinking, or other outdoor commercial activities or establishments” as a use by right in the General 
Commercial (GC), Central Business District (CBD), and Neighborhood Commercial (NC) zoning 
districts. 
 
The ordinance also incorporates additional safety standards for outdoor commercial activities 
located near roadways, particularly in areas where there is limited or no physical separation 
between the outdoor use and vehicle traffic, consistent with the Planning Commission’s 
recommendation and feedback from Fire Marshal Andrew Joslin. 
 
BACKGROU ND 
The Use Group Table currently restricts “Outdoor sales, eating and drinking, or other outdoor 
commercial activities or establishments” within the General Commercial (GC), Central Business 
District (CBD), and Neighborhood Commercial (NC) zoning districts. 
 
The Board of Trustees requested that the Planning Commission review the existing restrictions and 
consider whether amendments to the Use Group Table are appropriate. 
Specifically, the Planning Commission was asked to discuss whether these outdoor commercial 
activities should be permitted as a use by right and whether that designation is appropriate in each 
of the GC, CBD, and NC zoning districts. 
 
The Planning Commission agreed that outdoor sales and outdoor eating and drinking should be 
permitted as a use by right in all three commercial zoning districts: General Commercial (GC), 
Central Business District (CBD), and Neighborhood Commercial (NC). 
 
At the last meeting the Planning Commission expressed concern with the broader language “or 
other outdoor commercial activities or establishments, ” finding that the language could be overly 
broad and potentially allow outdoor commercial uses that were not specifically contemplated as 
part of the amendment. The Commission recommended removing or further refining this language 
to more clearly identify the types of outdoor commercial activities intended to be permitted by 
right.

Sec. 16-101.  Outdoor sales, eating and drinking, and other outdoor commercial activities. 
(1) Applicability. Outdoor sales, outdoor eating and drinking, and other outdoor

(2) Functional activity area. The outdoor activity shall be confined to a clearly defined

maintained at all times. The route shall satisfy applicable accessibility requirements

required off-street parking space, accessible parking space, access aisle, fire lane, 
loading area, or vehicle circulation area unless the Zoning Administrator determines

AGENDA ITEM  
The purpose of this agenda item is for the Planning Commission to review and provide a 
recommendation to the Board of Trustees regarding a draft ordinance that would assign the 
duties and responsibilities of the Board of Zoning Adjustment (BZA) to the Planning 
Commission (PC). 
BACKGROUND  
This topic was initially discussed by the Board of Trustees during its liaison appointments 
and was subsequently brought forward for further discussion regarding whether the 
functions of the Board of Zoning Adjustment should remain with a separate appointed 
board or be assigned to the Planning Commission. 
Colorado law permits a statutory town to assign the functions of the BZA to the Planning 
Commission. Combining these functions is also an approach used by Colorado 
municipalities, particularly in communities that receive relatively few variance 
applications. 
At its September 1, 2026 meeting, the Board of Trustees discussed the matter and directed 
Staff to work with the Town Attorney to move forward with drafting an ordinance that would 
assign the duties and responsibilities of the BZA to the Planning Commission. 
In accordance with that direction, Staff and the Town Attorney have prepared a draft 
ordinance for Planning Commission review and recommendation before it is brought back 
to the Board of Trustees for consideration. 
QUESTIONS BEFORE THE PC 
Does the Planning Commission want Staff to bring the draft ordinance back as an action 
item at the October Planning Commission meeting?

Series at Chipeta Park. The event didn’t have much participation and DCC will 
consider ways to engage more Nederland residents if another Ride and Drive is 
planned in the future. 
DCFC EV Charger 
Staff have placed an “out of order” sign on the DCFC in the Visitor’s Center parking 
lot because we were unable to work with Efacec to get it operational. 
Staff worked with Boulder County and Winn-Marion to submit an application for 
Xcel’s EV charging rebates to offset costs of purchasing and installing a new DCFC.

CU Boulder MENV Capstone Project – Resilience Action Plan 
Staff are attending weekly meetings with the CU Boulder MENV Capstone Team that 
is assigned to the Town’s Resilience Action Plan. The students are finalizing the 
Gaps and Alignment Analysis that evaluates strategies from the Comprehensive 
Plan that relate to resilience and identifies their current status, relevant 
stakeholders, and outstanding needs. The team is working on the Strategies 
Implementation Matrix that will identify the priority resilience strategies for the Town 
and include additional information for implementation.

CEO Grant: 
Town of Nederland was awarded a Local IMPACT Accelerator grant from the 
Colorado Energy Office for $1,079,000. The BOT directed the Town Manager to sign

DRCOG BPC GRANT: 
Staff submitted the first reimbursement request for study materials related to 
professional certifications, including LEED certification and the Building Permit 
Technician certification. The reimbursement was approved, and staff will continue 
to utilize grant funding for professional development opportunities, including 
education, certifications, training, and conferences. 
DRCOG BPC GRANT – ROUND 2: 
Staff submitted an application for the second round of DRCOG BPC grant funding. 
The application seeks funding to support expanding the Sustainability Coordinator 
position to a full-time role, along with potential updates to the position’s title, 
responsibilities, and scope of work.
