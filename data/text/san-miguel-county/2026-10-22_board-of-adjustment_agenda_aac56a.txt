Proposal 
Setback Variance: Chris Hawkins, Alpine Planning LLC, on behalf of the Town of Mountain 
Village, owners of parcel #456536200023, a 56.34-acre parcel in the Community Housing (CH) 
Zone District, seeks a reduction of the setback from the County Road 63L Right-of-Way from 20 
feet to 7 feet, in order to allow for the construction of affordable housing while infringing on the 
wetland on the other side of the parcel as little as possible. The property is located in the Ilium 
Valley across from the Jail. A future subdivision creating 4 lots is envisioned for the parcel, this 
approval for a setback variance would be applicable to all lots resulting from a subdivision of the 
parcel #456536200023. The applicant has requested the variance as moving the footprint closer 
towards County Road 63L creates the most environmentally sensitive design. Locating the 
housing units as far away from the riparian corridor as possible and increasing the wetland 
setback mitigates impacts to the floodplain and wetlands.  
The Town of Mountain Village plans to create 4 lots from parcel #456536200023 with Lot 1 
slated for deed restricted affordable town homes and childcare, Lots 2 and 3 for constructing 
deed restricted affordable town homes and Lot 4 left for a potential land exchange with the 
United States Forest Service. Due to the complexity and scale of the development, planning staff 
recommends a five (5) year approval for this variance., with the option to request and extension.

snowmelt, snow plowing, snow storage, drainage features and stormwater conveyance.  Shaded

storm drain systems.  Snow storage in and along shaded roadways may not melt and can

1-1403 A. Variances 
 
 Appeals to the Board of Adjustment may be taken by any person aggrieved by 
inability to obtain a building permit or by the decision of any administrative 
officer or agency based upon or made in the course of the administration or 
enforcement of zoning.  Appeals shall be heard only where: 
            
I. The Board of Adjustment is authorized by a Board of Commissioners 
resolution to hear and decide, in accordance with the provision of the 
resolution, requests for special exceptions, interpretations of a zoning map 
or decisions of specific questions authorized by the resolution; or 
 
II. It is alleged by the appellant that there is error in any order, requirement, 
decision, or refusal made by an administrative official or agency based on 
or made in the enforcement of zoning where no other administrative 
remedy exists pursuant to Land Use Code Sections 1-19, 1-20 and/or the 
Board of Building Appeals rules of procedure; or 
 
III. By reason of exceptional narrowness, shallowness, or shape of a specific 
piece of property at the time of the enactment of any regulation or by 
reason of exceptional topographic conditions or other extraordinary and 
exceptional situation or condition of such piece of property, the strict 
application of any regulation would result in peculiar and exceptional 
practical difficulties to, or exceptional and undue hardship upon, the owner 
of such property, and where relief in the form of a variance from zoning 
may be granted without substantial detriment to the public good and 
substantial impairment of the intent and purpose of zoning, and where the 
hardship was not created or caused by the owner.  
 
In addition, demonstration by the applicant that access to sunlight for solar-driven 
devices would be unduly restricted without the variance requested may serve as 
cause for granting a variance. 
 
The intention of the 20-foot setback in the CH Zone was to provide greater separation from 
neighboring residential development- in this case, the property faces an industrial area. This 
property has an exceptional situation in that the topographical conditions on the parcel leave 
little room between the road and wetland area for a development and this variance would allow 
the property owners to proceed in the most environmentally sensitive manner.

Background
The Town of Mountain Village Housing Authority (“Town”) is the owner of property that is legally 
described as Parcel 1 According to the Alexander Ilium Property Subdivision Exemption Plat for Lot Line 
Adjustment as recorded at Reception Number 481406 (“Property”) as shown in Figure 1. The Property 
contains 56.34 acres and is in the Community Housing Zone District.
The Town purchased the Property in 2023 and since that time has completed the following actions in its 
goal of constructing affordable housing:
1. 2023 rezoning of the Property to the Community Housing (“CH”) Zone District.
2. 2023 subdivision of the Property through the subdivision exemption process.
3. 2024 Site Specific Market Study that indicates affordable housing is viable on the Property.
4. 2025 Housing Needs Assessment that identifies a demand for approximately 1,100 deed 
restricted affordable housing units.
5. 2025 Memorandum of Understanding with the Ilium Park Owners Association for water and 
sewer connections.
6. 2022-2025 conducted three (3) neighborhood meetings on affordable housing development 
plans for the Property.
7. Evaluation of development options on the Property with affordable housing plan options 
created by development, architectural, civil engineering and planning teams.
The Town’s subdivision plan for the Property is to create four (4) lots on the Property, with Lots 1-3 
intended for constructing deed restricted affordable housing and Lot 4 left for a potential United States 
Forest Service land exchange.
Development Plan Evolution
The housing development team’s initial conceptual plan for affordable housing on Lots 1-3 included 
placing the housing closer to the San Miguel River corridor and close to associated wetlands and 
floodplains with a driveway in between County Road (“CR”) 63L and the building on Lot 3 and large 
common shared parking lots and extensive paving and driveways as shown in Figure 2 and Figure 3. The 
Town then directed the preparation of plans that minimized the amount of paving and impervious site 
coverage; reduced environmental impacts by moving the proposed  buildings further away from the 
river corridor, wetlands and floodplain; and more appropriate building massing located away from the 
riparian corridor. The current conceptual plans for Lot 2 and Lot 3 show the buildings pushed towards 
CR 63L, the elimination of large parking areas, and the minimum sized driveway as shown in Figure 3. 
This plan provides for the best option for creating affordable housing that minimizes environmental 
impacts while also providing for critically needed deed restricted housing, with 7-foot setbacks 
proposed to the CR 63L Right-of-Way for the housing units on Lot 2 and Lot 3.
Community Housing Zone District Setback and Requested Variance
The CH Zone District establishes the following setbacks:
20’ - Right-of-Way and Any Abutting Parcel Not in the CH Zone District
10’ - Front Yard to Adjacent Buildings Only, 0’ Internal Setback to Allow Flexibility
10’ - Side Yard to Adjacent Buildings Only, 0’ Internal Setback to Allow Flexibility
The Town is seeking a variance from the Board of Adjustment (“BOA”) for a reduction in the 20-foot 
setback to the CR 63L Right-of-Way to a minimum setback of 7 feet which we believe provides for 
the desired reduction in riparian, wetland and floodplain impacts while also allowing for the efficient 
design and construction of affordable housing units.
Variance Criteria Analysis
The Town is seeking a variance to LUC Section 1-1403 A.III to allow for a setback reduction from m20 to 
7 feet, with our compliance comments shown in blue text:

The Property has an exceptional situation where (1) the Town Housing Authority intends to 
create approximately 49 deed restricted affordable housing units on proposed Lot 2 and Lot 3; 
(2) proposed Lot 2 and Lot 3 are located adjacent to the San Miguel riparian corridor and its 
associated wetlands and floodplain; and (3) the most environmentally sensitive design locates 
the housing units and associated building massing away from the riparian corridor to increase 
the wetland setback as much as possible to mitigate wetland impacts and also reduce impacts 
to the floodplain.
The Town Housing Authority has a great opportunity to create critically needed affordable 
housing on the Property, and the variance will allow it to create the most efficient and 
environmentally sensitive design with the building massing located adjacent to CR 63L further 
setback from the wetlands and riparian corridor.
The driveway width of 24’ is the minimum necessary to provide for access to the housing units 
with the proposed retaining walls to the south tucked as closely as possible to the driveway 
without any stepping to further minimize impacts to wetlands, floodplain and the riparian 
corridor. This townhome configuration also fits within the neighborhood aesthetic better than 
the previously proposed large apartment style buildings. The affordable housing units are 
built into the hillside to step up with the topography and placed as close as possible to the CR 
63L with a proposed 7-foot setback to the buildings.  This reduced setback allows the housing 
project development to be located 13 feet further away from wetlands, floodplains, the riparian 
corridor and creates an efficient housing project.
Required parking is proposed under the buildings instead of large surface parking lots that 
increase drainage and potential water quality impacts due to oils and fluids that can leak out of 
cars. Efficient parking under the buildings also allows for more open space, less visual impact, 
reduced impervious coverage and more efficient design. Guest parking is proposed along CR 63L 
away from the river corridor instead of larger parking lots that have more adverse impacts.
The proposed variance illustrated in Figure 4 and Figure 5 can be granted without substantial 
detriment to the public good because the proposed setback will provide adequate buffering to 
CR 63L. In addition, the proposed housing project is located south and west of a the intensively 
developed IPOA community in the Lawson Hill PUD, with some of the IPOA lots allowing a 0-foot 
setback to the CR 63L Right-of-Way. The variance will actually improve the public good because 
it will allow for the efficient provision of approximately 49 critically needed deed restricted 
housing units while protecting all areas outside of Lot 2 and Lot 3 in open space in perpetuity, 
and because it will allow for better mitigation to the wetland, floodplain and riparian impacts 
through increased setbacks to these natural features. The CH Zone District exists specifically to 
enable community housing as a public good, so the variance advances rather than impairs the 
purpose of the CH Zone District and the public good.

Motion by Talle Meehan to approve a variance reducing the rear yard setback from 
Page 34 of 36
twenty (20) feet to five (5) feet at 30 Stella Drive, Placerville for the purpose of 
expanding the existing Single-Family Residence and constructing a detached garage as 
shown on Exhibit B, Site Plan, and to adopt the Board of Adjustment resolution 
#2025-01, based on the finding the applicants have demonstrated an exceptional 
situation or condition and that the application meets the criteria of Land Use Code 
Section 1-1403 A. III., in particular, recognizing the exceptional topographical 
conditions of the parcel. The Board finds that the strict application of the regulation 
would result in an exceptional and undue hardship upon the owner of such property, 
and that relief in the form of a variance from zoning may be granted without 
substantial detriment to the public good and substantial impairment of the intent and 
purpose of zoning, and that the hardship was not created or caused by the owner. This 
approval is conditioned upon the following: 
1. The setback variance is limited to the expansion of the Single-Family 
Residence and the detached garage as shown in Exhibit B and does not 
authorize any further expansion or construction. 
2. All required development permits shall be obtained from San Miguel County 
by the owner(s) prior to commencing any construction on this parcel. 
3. During construction there shall be no staging or parking adjacent to or on 
Geyser Drive. 
4. This approval shall expire three (3) years from the recording date of this 
resolution. If a building permit is not obtained by that date and said permit does 
not remain active until issuance of a Certificate of Occupancy, this variance 
shall be void. 
5. All written representations of the applicant, in the original submittal and all 
supplements, letters and emails, are deemed to be conditions of approval, 
except to the extent modified by this Motion. 
 
Seconded by Sonny Lopez.
