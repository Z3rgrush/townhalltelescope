AGREEMENT FOR THE CONSTRUCTION OF 
THE LAWSON HILL CONNECTOR TRAIL IMPROVEMENTS 
This Agreement dated as of , 2026 (“Effective Date”) is made and entered into between the 
Lawson Hill Property Owners' Company, a Colorado nonprofit corporation (the "LHPOC") and San 
Miguel County, Colorado (the “County”), each of whom are referred to herein as a “Party” and/or 
collectively as the “Parties”. 
The purpose of this Agreement is to memorialize the Parties' intent in their collaborative effort to develop 
that portion of the multiple use trail connecting a certain intercept/commuter parking lot located within 
Lawson Hill (“Lawson Hill Commuter Parking Lot”) with the existing public paved hiker/biker trail 
(“Commuter Trail”) extending along the spur that connects into Lawson Hill over the Society Turn 
Parcel (the "Connector Trail ") to be located within the County. 
This Agreement provides the funding commitment of each Party to complete the Lawson Hill Connector 
Trail Project. 
BACKGROUND/RECITALS 
A. The LHPOC is the property-owners association that manages and administers the affairs
of the Lawson Hill PUD/Subdivision located in the unincorporated portion of San Miguel County, 
Colorado. 
B. The County is the owner and operator of the Lawson Hill Commuter Parking Lot within
the Lawson Hill Property Owners Company development which serves as a regional commuting hub 
providing parking and bus service for the east end of San Miguel County. 
C. The Lawson Hill Connector Trail Project ( “Project”) will be sited generally at the
location shown and described on attached Exhibit “A” which will occur on property owned by either the 
County or LHPOC and will create a multimodal connection between the Lawson Hill Commuter Parking 
Lot and other local commuting trails while also serving as an improved local multimodal pathway with 
improved safety within the commercial area of the LHPOC.    
D. The County and LHPOC desire to cooperate and assist each other with respect to the
funding, design, construction, operation and maintenance of the Connector Trail, which will be established 
and operated as a public, non-motorized pedestrian and biking trail. The Parties shall cooperate with each 
other as necessary and appropriate to establish public trail easements (“ Trail Easement ”) along the 
respective parcels upon completion of the work.   
E. The County and LHPOC have obtained grant funding to assist in the design and
construction of the Project. The funding from the Colorado Department of Transportation (“CDOT”) has 
been committed from the Transportation Alternatives Program  funding (“ CDOT Grant Funds ”) in 
amounts totaling $739,604.80 as indicated in the budget attached as Exhibit “B”. 
F. The County and LHPOC coordinated the design phase of the project and completed that
design in July of 2026. The total cost of design has been $92,060.00, of which the CDOT  Grant Funds 
contributed $58,400.00 and LHPOC provided $33,660.00 in funding to complete the design. 
G. Bids were solicited in August of 2026 for the project and the results of those bid proposals
were significantly higher  than the original estimate. The apparent low bid for the project totaled 
$1,966,198.09 which when including construction management services from the project engineer and 
quality control testing services left the budget at a $1,234,072.09 deficit.

H. After consideration of the project bids, alternatives, and other aspects of the project, the
County and LHPOC have determined that proceeding with the project as designed and with the grant 
resources is desirable. At the Board of Commissioners meeting on Septemb er 23, 2026 the County 
Commissioners moved to commit to match funding amounts pledged by LHPOC for the project in order 
to complete the construction. At the LHPOC Board meeting  on September 28, 2026, the LHPOC Board 
also moved to commit funds up to a total of $700,000 to complete the project , of which the $33,660.00 
contributed toward design fees is included in the $700,000 share of LHPOC. 
I. This agreement represents that commitment to cooperate together and complete the
construction phase of the Project in recognition of each Party committing to help meet the Project funding 
deficit. 
AGREEMENT 
1. The County agrees to be the fiscal and administrative agent for both the grant funding and
construction contracting. The County will pay contractors related to the construction of Project 
improvements in accordance with the Contract and CDOT standards. The  County will provide LHPOC 
with progress billing invoices for the LHPOC share of the project , together with supporting background 
documentation on a monthly basis follo wing the commencement of construction and LHPOC will make 
payment within thirty (30) days following receipt of a billing invoice.  The County shall obtain a  final lien 
waiver and release  from contractors and vendors providing labor, materials and supplie s for the Project 
when the work is completed and provide a copy to LHPOC.  
2. The term of this agreement will begin from the date of signature and continue through the
completion of the Project including the pre -construction period, construction period, subs tantial 
completion of the Project, and all grant reporting and settlement periods required to meet CDOT or other 
financial reporting requirements. 
3. This Construction Agreement pertains solely to the completion of the construction of the
Project and such other necessary and construction related activities. The Parties agree that the work shall 
proceed in accordance with the attached plans and generally in line with the attached cost estimate.  Any 
material changes to the plans and cost estimates shall be mutually reviewed and approved by the County 
and LHPOC.  It is anticipated that other costs and expenses associated with the subject of this Agreement 
will be negotiated between the County and LHPOC after installation and certification of completion of the 
trail Project  and provided for in the Trail Easement . Those will include responsibility for ongoing 
maintenance for the Trail and any landscape and irrigation maintenance adjacent or along the trail. Upon 
completion the parties will enter into Trail Easement to address these and any other necessary and 
appropriate items. 
4. If either Party to this Construction Agreement fails to perform in accordance with the
terms, covenants, and conditions of this Agreement or is otherwise in default of  any of the terms of this 
Agreement, after giving seven (7) days' notice to the other Party of the alleged default, and upon said Party 
in default having failed to cure said breach within thirty (30) days, the other Party shall have the option to 
terminate this Agreement and pursue any remedy available at law or in equity. 
5. In carrying out its obligations and activities under this Construction Agreement it is
understood and agreed that the Parties are each acting as an independent contractor with the other, and not 
as an agent, partner, joint-venturer, or employee of the other.

Background 
 
In 2023, the County began the process of updating the Natural Resources-related uses in the 
Land Use Code (LUC), in order to provide more current and comprehensive review of projects 
related to mining, oil and gas, logging, and renewable energy. The solar regulations were 
adopted in September, 2024.  Mining regulations were adopted in January, 2026.  The third piece 
of the Code update is Forestry Practices and that will be followed by oil and gas and geothermal 
resources. Each natural resources-related topic in Article 6 is a stand-alone section, with 
minimal cross-referencing to the rest of the Code. This limits the number of sections that an 
applicant, staff, and Commissioners must look at when reviewing a proposal. 
 
Article 6, Natural Resources, was created concurrent with the adoption of the solar regulations, 
which were adopted in September, 2024. Section 6-1 contains general provisions and 
definitions that apply to all natural resource projects.  As the different topics have been 
considered, related amendments have been made to Section 6-1 to provide more clarity. 
 
Joint Planning Commission and BOCC work sessions were held on March 26 and July 9 to 
discuss the draft Forestry Practices regulations.  A significant part of the discussions centered on 
how to streamline the review process for wildfire mitigation practices. 
 
Current Code 
 
Land Use Code (LUC) Article 5, Standards, contains the various zone district standards that 
identify where specific uses are permitted, as well as development review standards for uses 
subject to approval of a Special Use Permit (SUP). Logging is currently permitted in the 
Forestry, Agriculture and Open (F), Wright’s Mesa (WM), Wright’s Mesa Rural Agriculture 
(WMRA), High Country Area (HCA) and West End (WE) zone districts.  It is not listed as a 
permitted use in any other zone district, although forested areas exist (e.g. Rangeland Grazing 
(RG)).  LUC Section 5-17 contains the review standards for logging. The regulations focus on 
wetlands and Areas of Local and State Interest, including floodplain hazards, geologic hazards, 
historic and archaeological resources, wildfire hazards and wildlife habitat areas.  The 
regulations also address logging operations on federal and state lands. 
 
Within specific zone districts, logging is based on the amount of board feet hauled within a 12-
month period.  For example, in the F zone, hauling of 5,000 board feet in a year is a Use 
Allowed by Right, while hauling 5,000-300,000 board feet requires Administrative Review.  
Logging operations in which more than 300,000 board feet would be hauled in a 12-month 
period requires a Special Use Permit through a one-step BOCC review. 
 
Conditional Uses on Federal Lands, LUC Section 5-11 also applies to logging activities on 
public lands. Section 5-10 contains standards for review of SUPs.  Section 5-28, Floodplain

Recent Projects 
 
Logging activities in the County in recent years have primarily been related to wildfire mitigation 
and forest health.  The US Forest Service administers the Spruce Beetle and Aspen Decline 
Management Response (SBEADMR) program on federal lands, using an adaptive management 
framework to combat widespread beetle infestation.  Project-level NEPA review occurs, followed 
by issuance of USFS permits for logging of specific areas.  Additionally, West Region Wildfire 
Council has been working with property owners and subdivisions to develop and carry out 
wildfire mitigation projects.  Finally, individual property owners also conduct forest management 
and mitigation activities independent of the above-mentioned projects.  Many of those projects 
are developed through guidance from the Colorado State Forest Service. 
 
Regulatory Approach to Mitigating Impacts 
 
The intent of the Forestry Practices regulations is to mitigate the impacts of such development. 
The regulations are structured to achieve this goal. The regulations require an applicant for 
Forestry Practices to provide reports, plans, and studies that analyze impacts of the project and 
to explain in the application materials how the applicant will mitigate those impacts. Review 
standards align with application materials. The burden of proof is on the applicant to 
demonstrate through the application materials how the review standards are met. 
 
The organization of Section 6-4, Forestry Practices, is very similar to that of Section 6-2, Solar 
Energy Systems, and Section 6-3, Mining. Solar focuses significantly on siting and construction, 
while mining regulations emphasize impacts of the ongoing operation, and especially those that 
are unique to mining such as blasting, noise, and vibration.  Forestry Practices  focus on surface 
disturbance, management techniques, road impacts, and revegetation and restoration.

One of the most significant changes to the draft regulations is Section 6-401 I, General Permit 
Issued for Qualifying Wildfire Mitigation Practices.  Projects that meet the requirements of that 
section are automatically authorized to proceed. Specifically, projects between 1 ½ acre and 40 
acres of surface disturbance, conducted in association with West Region Wildfire Council 
(WRWC) or Colorado State Forest Service (CSFS) qualify for a General Permit.  Certain larger 
projects (20-40 acres) must submit a Notice of Coverage.  Non-qualifying practices less than 40 
acres are subject to administrative review. Projects with 40-80 acres of site disturbance are also 
Minor Forestry Practices, which is subject to Administrative Review.  Projects with more than 80 
acres of surface disturbance are Major Forestry Practices and subject to 2-step review.  The 
intent of the draft regulations is to allow quick response to agencies and property owners 
seeking to conduct smaller mitigation and forest health projects while maintaining appropriate 
review levels for larger projects that could have a more significant impact.   
 
Revisions: 
The minimum size for projects covered under General Permits has been increased to 1 
½ acres, while the maximum area of site disturbance for Minor Forestry Practices has 
been reduced to 80 acres. 
 
Definitions (Section 6-401 K): 
The definitions in Section 6-401 K apply specifically to Forestry Practices. Definitions include 
Forestry Practice, Major Forestry Practice, Minor Forestry Practice, Reforestation, and Small-
Scale Forestry Practice.  Excluded activities are listed in the Forestry Practice definition. 
 
In general, the type of Forestry Practice is dependent on the area of disturbance.  The definition 
of “Forestry Practice” lists a number of exclusions, including disturbance of less than 1 ½ acre, 
property maintenance, fuels reduction activities along roads and rights-of-way, activities 
secondary to other activities such as construction, and certain removal and harvesting of 
materials.  “Minor Forestry Practice” includes projects on sites less than 40 acres where less 
than 10% of the woody biomass is removed; Wildfire Mitigation Practice on less than 80 acres; 
Restoration Practices on less than 80 acres; or Practices to Remove Diseased Trees.   If a 
proposed project does not meet the definition of Minor Forestry Practice, it would be classified 
as a “Major Forestry Practice.” 
 
Revisions: 
The definitions have been revised to provide more clarity as to what sort of activities are 
not considered “Forestry Practice,” and would be considered “Property Maintenance.”  
The minimum size of a Forestry Practice has been increased to 1 ½ acres.  The 
threshold between Major and Minor Forestry Practices has been reduced from 100 acres 
to 80 acres.

 SECTION 6-4: FORESTRY PRACTICES 
(THIS SECTION IS ADDED NEW IN ITS ENTIRETY) 
6-401 General Provisions 
6-401 A. Purpose 
The purpose of this Section 6-4 is to facilitate Forestry Practices in a manner that protects 
public health, safety, welfare, the environment, and wildlife of San Miguel County; fosters 
the County’s sustainability -related goals and policies; and complements and does not 
conflict with or duplicate state and federal permitting processes.   
6-401 B. Applicability 
This Section applies to a Forestry Practice or the expansion of an existing Forestry 
Practice located partially or wholly in unincorporated San Miguel County on private or 
public lands.  
6-401 C. Permit Required 
A permit issued pursuant to this Section or an activity notice is required prior to the initiation 
or expansion of a Forestry Practice unless specifically exempted.  
6-401 D. Forestry Practices Permitted in Certain Zone Districts 
I. Major Forestry Practices on non- federal lands may be permitted pursuant to this 
Section 6-4 only in the following zone districts: 
a. Forestry, Agriculture, and Open 
b. Rangeland Grazing  
c. Wright’s Mesa  
d. Wright’s Mesa Rural Agriculture 
e. West End 
f. High Country Area 
II. Minor Forestry Practices may be permitted pursuant to this Section 6-4 in all zones. 
III. Federal Land. Forestry Practices on federal land may be permitted pursuant to this 
Section in all zone districts.  
6-401 E. Permit Level of Review 
 A permit for Forestry Practices is subject to the following levels of review: 
I. Major Forestry Practices are subject to Two- Step Review (Planning Commission 
and Board of County Commissioners review) as set forth in Section 3- 6, except 
that federal or state Major Forestry Practices require an Activity Notice as set forth 
Page 48 of 781
Exhibit B – Land Use Code Amendment – Forestry Practices 5 of 31 
 
in Section 6-401 J. 
II. Minor Forestry Practices are subject to Administrative Review procedures as set 
forth in Section 3- 4, except that federal or state Minor Forestry Practices require 
an Activity Notice as set forth in Section 6-401 J. 
III. Qualifying Wildfire Mitigation Practices shall comply with the County -issued 
General Permit in accordance with Section 6-401.I. 
6-401 F. Term of Permit and Commencement of Project 
I. The County may issue a permit for an indefinite term, or for a specified period, 
depending upon the size and complexity of the Forestry Practice.  
II. If commencement of a permitted Forestry Practice has not been initiated within 
three (3) years of permit issuance or if construction of a permitted Forestry Practice 
does not satisfy the time benchmarks identified in the permit, the permit shall be 
void and of no further force and effect.  
 a. Prior to expiration of the Major Forestry Practice permit, a Permittee may 
request an extension, stating the need for such extension. Following a duly 
noticed public meeting, the Board of County Commissioners may grant 
extension(s) of the approval for good cause shown. 
 b. Prior to the expiration of a Minor Forestry Practice permit, a permittee may 
request an extension, stating the need for such an extension. The Planning 
Director may grant the extension of the permit for good cause shown.  
6-401  G. Transfer of Permit 
I. Any permit for a Major Forestry Practice approved by the County may be 
transferred to another owner/operator only after a public meeting by the Decision-
Maker. The County must ensure, in approving any transfer, that the transferee 
satisfies the following criteria. 
II. Any permit for a Minor Forestry Practice approved by the Couty may be transferred 
to another owner/operator upon written approval from the Planning Director which 
certifies that the transferee satisfies the following criteria. 
III. Any transferee shall meet the following criteria: 
a. The proposed transferee shall comply with all the requirements, terms, and 
conditions contained in the permit and this Section;  
b. Such requirements, terms, and conditions remain sufficient to protect the 
health, safety, and welfare of the public, the environment, and wildlife; and 
c. The proposed transferee can provide an adequate guarantee of financial 
security. 
6.401 H. Annual Report  
A permittee for a Major or Minor Forestry Practice shall submit a report to the County 
Planning Director annually, or at different time intervals as determined by the County, 
detailing activities conducted by the permittee pursuant to the permit, the conti nued 
adequacy of required plans/studies/reports, the continued adequacy of the financial 
guarantee, if any, and a satisfactory showing that the work is being conducted in 
conformance with all conditions of the County permit and all applicable state and federal

6-401 K. Definitions  
CSFS Best Management Practices   
The most current CSFS guidelines, handbooks, and technical publications governing wildfire 
mitigation, forest health, forest road construction, and water quality protection including the 
Colorado Forest Road Field Handbook (2024 or as amended) and the Forestry Best Management 
Practices to Protect Water Quality in Colorado (2023 or as amended). 
Forestry Practice  
I.  Any activity conducted on or directly pertaining to forest land and related to 
growing, harvesting, processing, or removing woody biomass from any forest 
stands, including road and trail construction and maintenance; timber harvesting 
and mechanical thinni ng; slash disposal; transportation and removal of woody 
biomass; and revegetation. 
II. “Forestry Practice” shall not include: 
a. An activity that causes less than one and a half (1.5) acre of surface 
disturbance.  
b. Activities where the primary purpose is:  
i. Property Maintenance. 
ii. Fuels reduction and vegetation management projects along public 
or private roads, road rights-of-ways, or utility rights-of-ways. 
iii. The removal or harvest of incidental vegetation from forest lands, 
such as berries, ferns, greenery, mistletoe, herbs, mushrooms, and 
other products that cannot normally be expected to result in 
damage to forest soils, timber, or public resources. 
iv. Christmas tree farms or landscaping nurseries, which are 
separately regulated under the LUC.

v. Firewood collection or Christmas tree harvesting in accordance with 
a Federal land manager-issued permit. 
c. An activity that is a secondary or accessory use, where the primary use 
has been or will be permitted pursuant to another provision of the LUC 
(e.g., tree removal as part of grading and surface preparation for a 
residence, subdivision improvements, solar field, or industrial site).  
Forest stand  
A contiguous group of trees and associated understory vegetation that is sufficiently uniform in 
species composition, age, structure, and site conditions to be distinguishable as a unit for 
management purposes. 
Impact Area 
Those geographic areas in which adverse impacts are likely to be caused by the Forestry 
Practice.  
Major Forestry Practice 
Any Forestry Practice that the Planning Director determines does not qualify as a Minor Forestry 
Practice. 
Minor Forestry Practice 
Forestry Practices with the following primary purposes, regardless of whether the felled timber or 
woody biomass is commercially sold afterward, are Minor Forestry Practices. The County 
Planning Director determines whether a proposed Forestry Practice is a Minor Forestry Practice. 
The applicant has the burden of proof to demonstrate that the proposed Forestry Practice is a 
Minor Forestry Practice.  
I. Selective Removal: A Forestry Practice on a site that is less than eighty (80) acres 
where less than ten percent (10%) of the average volume of woody biomass per 
acre is harvested or removed.   
II. Wildfire Mitigation Practice. A Wildfire Mitigation Practice consisting of less than 
eighty (80) acres of surface disturbance. The County authorizes Qualifying Wildfire 
Mitigation Practices under a General Permit pursuant to Section 6-401 I. 
III. Restoration Practice. A Forestry Practice consisting of less than eighty (80) acres 
of surface disturbance, with the primary purpose of establishing or managing trees 
intended to mitigate the effects of historic land use practices, such as for 
windbreaks, riparian restoration, or shade strips. 
IV. Practices to Remove Diseased Trees. A Forestry Practice consisting of less than 
eighty (80) acres of surface disturbance with the primary purpose of cutting and 
removing trees and other woody biomass that are dead, dying, or diseased (e.g., 
spruce beetle epidemic, sudden aspen decline).  
V. One and one half (1.5) to five (5) acres: Any Forestry Practice consisting of one 
and one- half (1.5) to five (5) acres of surface disturbance for a purpose not 
otherwise listed as a Minor Forestry Practice, or not listed as excluded from 
Forestry Practices. Activities under one and one- half (1.5) acres are not Forestry 
Practices. 
Property Maintenance

Routine activities undertaken by a property owner or occupant to preserve the safety, 
appearance, and functionality of developed or actively used land, including the removal, trimming,

Revegetation

Wildfire Mitigation Practice

Section 6-404 Best Management Practices (BMPs) for General and Minor Forestry 
Practices   
Section 6-404 A Best Management Practices (BMPs) for General and Minor Forestry Practices 
The following best management practices (BMPs) shall apply to all Permittees subject to 
a General Permit issued for Qualifying Wildfire Mitigation Practices in Section 6-401.I. 
and Applicants for a permit for Minor Forestry Practices:  
I. Implement the CSFS Best Management Practices to the degree practicable. 
II. Notify the County Planning Department of any planned use of aerial resources 
associated with the Forestry Practice at least 24 hours prior to the activity. 
III. Planning BMPs  
a. Institute adequate drainage and erosion control measures, including 
applying slash material to disturbed areas and constructing water bars 
according to the CSFS Best Management Practices. 
b. Minimize soil compaction and erosion.  
c. Limit activities to when soil is dry or frozen. 
d. Complete work in shortest possible timeframe. 
e. Avoid cutting trees during active time periods for beetle species (generally 
late spring, summer, and early fall). Coordinate with CSFS for precise

f. Preserve as much understory vegetation as feasible.

riparian areas and waterbodies to the greatest degree practicable. If

riparian areas or waterbodies. 
f. Do not skid with the blade lowered. If a blade is necessary to stabilize

Section 6-405 Permit Application Materials for Major Forestry Practice 
The applicant for a Major Forestry Practice shall submit the following application materials 
to the Planning Director. The burden is on the applicant to demonstrate that the Major 
Forestry Practice will satisfy the corresponding Review Criteria. 
All maps shall be prepared at a scale that shows sufficient detail to evaluate the application 
against applicable Review Criteria.  
The Planning Director may waive application materials pursuant to 6-402 B. 
6-405 A. Information Describing the Applicant 
I. The contact information, including email address, organizational form, and 
business of the applicant and, if different, the owner of the Forestry Practice.  
II.  The names, addresses, and qualifications of the entity or individuals responsible 
for managing the Forestry Practice. 
III.  Written authorization of the application package by the owner of the property on 
which any feature, component, or surface disturbance of the Forestry Practice is 
located. 
 6-405 B. Information Describing the Major Forestry Practice  
Maps, plans, specifications, and description of the Major Forestry Practice in sufficient 
detail to evaluate the application against applicable Review Criteria in Section 6 -406, 
including: 
I. Vicinity Map. Map and description of the location of the Major Forestry Practice 
and topographic and cultural features, and federal and state- owned lands in the 
Impact Area, shown in a form acceptable to the Planning Director. Significant 
features including: 
a. Intermittent and ephemeral water features, wetlands, riparian areas, 
natural and artificial drainageways, ditches, wells, reservoirs, stock ponds, 
and the one hundred (100) year floodplain boundaries. 
b. Wildlife habitat areas. 
c. Soil types, geologic features, and hazards. 
II. Project Description. Description of the overall Project and the purpose(s) of the 
Major Forestry Practice.  
III. Timeline and Phasing. Description of proposed lifespan, timing, seasonal 
considerations, and phasing of the Forestry Practice.  
IV. Surface Disturbances. Description of the acreage of the site and the acreage of

a. The estimated acreage of expected surface disturbance expected for 
harvesting, including location(s) and acreages of disturbance on steep slopes
