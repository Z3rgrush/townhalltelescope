Re:  Land Use Code Amendment - Forestry Practices  
Date:  September 10, 2026 
 
Background 
 
In 2023, the County began the process of updating the Natural Resources-related uses in the 
Land Use Code (LUC), in order to provide more current and comprehensive review of projects 
related to mining, oil and gas, logging, and renewable energy. The solar regulations were 
adopted in September, 2024.  Mining regulations were adopted in January, 2026.  The third piece 
of the Code update is Forestry Practices and that will be followed by oil and gas and geothermal 
resources. Each natural resources-related topic in Article 6 is a stand-alone section, with 
minimal cross-referencing to the rest of the Code. This limits the number of sections that an 
applicant, staff, and Commissioners must look at when reviewing a proposal. 
 
Article 6, Natural Resources, was created concurrent with the adoption of the solar regulations, 
which were adopted in September, 2024. Section 6-1 contains general provisions and 
definitions that apply to all natural resource projects.  As the different topics have been 
considered, related amendments have been made to Section 6-1 to provide more clarity. 
 
Joint Planning Commission and BOCC work sessions were held on March 26 and July 9 to 
discuss the draft Forestry Practices regulations.  A significant part of the discussions centered on 
how to streamline the review process for wildfire mitigation practices. 
 
Current Code 
 
Land Use Code (LUC) Article 5, Standards, contains the various zone district standards that 
identify where specific uses are permitted, as well as development review standards for uses 
subject to approval of a Special Use Permit (SUP). Logging is currently permitted in the 
Forestry, Agriculture and Open (F), Wright’s Mesa (WM), Wright’s Mesa Rural Agriculture 
(WMRA), High Country Area (HCA) and West End (WE) zone districts.  It is not listed as a 
permitted use in any other zone district, although forested areas exist (e.g. Rangeland Grazing 
(RG)).  LUC Section 5-17 contains the review standards for logging. The regulations focus on 
wetlands and Areas of Local and State Interest, including floodplain hazards, geologic hazards, 
historic and archaeological resources, wildfire hazards and wildlife habitat areas.  The 
regulations also address logging operations on federal and state lands. 
 
Within specific zone districts, logging is based on the amount of board feet hauled within a 12-
month period.  For example, in the F zone, hauling of 5,000 board feet in a year is a Use 
Allowed by Right, while hauling 5,000-300,000 board feet requires Administrative Review.  
Logging operations in which more than 300,000 board feet would be hauled in a 12-month 
period requires a Special Use Permit through a one-step BOCC review. 
 
Conditional Uses on Federal Lands, LUC Section 5-11 also applies to logging activities on 
public lands. Section 5-10 contains standards for review of SUPs.  Section 5-28, Floodplain

Recent Projects 
 
Logging activities in the County in recent years have primarily been related to wildfire mitigation 
and forest health.  The US Forest Service administers the Spruce Beetle and Aspen Decline 
Management Response (SBEADMR) program on federal lands, using an adaptive management 
framework to combat widespread beetle infestation.  Project-level NEPA review occurs, followed 
by issuance of USFS permits for logging of specific areas.  Additionally, West Region Wildfire 
Council has been working with property owners and subdivisions to develop and carry out 
wildfire mitigation projects.  Finally, individual property owners also conduct forest management 
and mitigation activities independent of the above-mentioned projects.  Many of those projects 
are developed through guidance from the Colorado State Forest Service. 
 
Regulatory Approach to Mitigating Impacts 
 
The intent of the Forestry Practices regulations is to mitigate the impacts of such development. 
The regulations are structured to achieve this goal. The regulations require an applicant for 
Forestry Practices to provide reports, plans, and studies that analyze impacts of the project and 
to explain in the application materials how the applicant will mitigate those impacts. Review 
standards align with application materials. The burden of proof is on the applicant to 
demonstrate through the application materials how the review standards are met. 
 
The organization of Section 6-4, Forestry Practices, is very similar to that of Section 6-2, Solar 
Energy Systems, and Section 6-3, Mining. Solar focuses significantly on siting and construction, 
while mining regulations emphasize impacts of the ongoing operation, and especially those that 
are unique to mining such as blasting, noise, and vibration.  Forestry Practices  focus on surface 
disturbance, management techniques, road impacts, and revegetation and restoration.   
 
Proposed Code Amendment

The draft Forestry Practice regulations are substantially similar in format and content to the

current Code, Logging, will be stricken; that section will become “Reserved,” and can be used in 
the future if another topic is added to Article 5.  Zone districts will also be amended to remove 
references to logging.

As a stand-alone section, all zone districts where logging may be permitted are listed in Section 
6-401 D, rather than listing the use in each zone district section. It also contains all application 
requirements, and provides all review standards, except where referenced. This reduces the 
number of cross-references within the LUC and minimizes potential omissions and errors. The 
application requirements are specific and are directly connected to corresponding review 
standards.  The detailed application requirements make clear what must be submitted and 
addressed, in contrast to the very broad and general requirements of Article 4, and will help 
streamline pre-application meeting and letters.

General Provisions (Section 6-401): 
Section 6-401 enumerates the zone districts where forestry practices may occur, describes the 
levels of activities, and the permit review procedures for each. 
 
Revisions: 
Section 6-401A, Purpose, has been updated to reflect the County’s intent for regulation 
of Forestry Practices.  Section 6-401F has been revised to provide clearer direction 
regarding requests for extensions. 
 
Zone Districts (Section 6-401 D): 
 
As proposed, zone districts where Major Forestry Practices may be permitted on non-federal 
land are: 
 Forestry, Agriculture and Open (F) 
 Rangeland Grazing (RG) 
 Wright’s Mesa (WM) 
 Wright’s Mesa Rural Agriculture (WMRA)

Proposed Review Processes (Section 6-401 E): 
 Major Forestry Practices on private lands (not state or federal) are subject to Two-Step 
Review per Section 3-6. 
 Minor Forestry Practices on private lands are subject to Administrative Review per 
Section 3-4. 
 Qualifying Wildfire Mitigation Practices require a General Permit per Section 6-401 I. 
 Forestry Practices on federal and state lands require an Activity Notice per Section 6-
401 J.

Issued for Qualifying Wildfire Mitigation Practices.  Projects that meet the requirements of that 
section are automatically authorized to proceed. Specifically, projects between 1 ½ acre and 40 
acres of surface disturbance, conducted in association with West Region Wildfire Council 
(WRWC) or Colorado State Forest Service (CSFS) qualify for a General Permit.  Certain larger 
projects (20-40 acres) must submit a Notice of Coverage.  Non-qualifying practices less than 40 
acres are subject to administrative review. Projects with 40-80 acres of site disturbance are also 
Minor Forestry Practices, which is subject to Administrative Review.  Projects with more than 80 
acres of surface disturbance are Major Forestry Practices and subject to 2-step review.  The 
intent of the draft regulations is to allow quick response to agencies and property owners 
seeking to conduct smaller mitigation and forest health projects while maintaining appropriate 
review levels for larger projects that could have a more significant impact.   
 
Revisions: 
The minimum size for projects covered under General Permits has been increased to 1 
½ acres, while the maximum area of site disturbance for Minor Forestry Practices has 
been reduced to 80 acres. 
 
Definitions (Section 6-401 K): 
The definitions in Section 6-401 K apply specifically to Forestry Practices. Definitions include 
Forestry Practice, Major Forestry Practice, Minor Forestry Practice, Reforestation, and Small-
Scale Forestry Practice.  Excluded activities are listed in the Forestry Practice definition. 
 
In general, the type of Forestry Practice is dependent on the area of disturbance.  The definition 
of “Forestry Practice” lists a number of exclusions, including disturbance of less than 1 ½ acre, 
property maintenance, fuels reduction activities along roads and rights-of-way, activities 
secondary to other activities such as construction, and certain removal and harvesting of 
materials.  “Minor Forestry Practice” includes projects on sites less than 40 acres where less 
than 10% of the woody biomass is removed; Wildfire Mitigation Practice on less than 80 acres; 
Restoration Practices on less than 80 acres; or Practices to Remove Diseased Trees.   If a 
proposed project does not meet the definition of Minor Forestry Practice, it would be classified 
as a “Major Forestry Practice.” 
 
Revisions: 
The definitions have been revised to provide more clarity as to what sort of activities are 
not considered “Forestry Practice,” and would be considered “Property Maintenance.”  
The minimum size of a Forestry Practice has been increased to 1 ½ acres.  The 
threshold between Major and Minor Forestry Practices has been reduced from 100 acres 
to 80 acres. 
 
General Requirement for all Permit Applications (Section 6-402): 
The General Requirements include waiver of application materials where appropriate; 
reimbursement for consultants and referral agency costs; and acceptance of Federal and State 
Agency materials to satisfy application requirements. This section is substantially similar to the 
requirements contained in Mining. 
 
Minor Forestry Practice (Sections 6-403): 
The Permit Application Materials and Permit Review Criteria for Minor Forestry Practices is 
similar to those listed for Major Practices, but simplified to reflect the level of expected impacts.

Best Management Practices (BMPs) (Section 6-404) 
A number of Best Management Practices, based on the CSFS Best Management Practices, 
have been added. These include drainage and erosion control measures, minimizing impacts to 
soil, preservation of understory habitat, protecting wetlands, streams, and riparian habitat, and 
prevention of impacts to Gunnison Sage Grouse, raptors, and wildlife.  It also requires 
notification to the County if use of airborne resources is planned.  The development of new 
roads should be minimized.  Post-operational BMPs are included. 
 
Revisions: 
Section 6-404A.III, Planning BMPs, has been revised to state that cutting should be 
avoided during active time periods for beetles.  Specific times are not mentioned, as that 
can vary due to conditions from year to year.  The buffer area for wetlands, riparian 
areas, fens and floodplains has been decreased from 100 feet to 50 feet, in recognition 
that vegetation management may be necessary in those areas.  BMPs to limit impacts 
on Gunnison Sage Grouse are also added.  6-404A.IV, Operational BMPs, has been 
amended to include practices to be avoided when under a red flag warning or high wind 
event.  References to use of helicopters for extraction have been changes to “airborne 
resources” or “aerial extraction.”

Related Amendments 
Section 6-1, General Provisions, has continued to evolve.  Certain amendments are proposed to 
be made for items that have been found to be more universal as we have worked through the 
Natural Resources Code amendments.  Additionally, changes will be made in Article 3, 
Procedures, and Article 4, Submission Requirements, to change all references to Natural 
Resources activities to Article 6.  Similarly, the zone districts will be amended to remove 
“Logging” from the permitted and allowed uses, and Section 5-17, Logging, will be removed in 
its entirety.

Review Standards 
Review Standards for Land Use Code Amendments are contained in LUC Section 5-1802 and 
state, “Land Use Code Amendments may be initiated by the County or by persons who are 
residents of, or own property in, San Miguel County subject to compliance with the following 
standard.” This amendment was initiated at the direction of the BOCC. 
 
The only review standard, LUC Section 5-1802 A., states “Land Use Code Amendments shall 
be drafted in a form consistent with the organizational format and style of the code.” The 
proposed amendment has been drafted in the format and style of the code. In addition, Section 
1-4, Purposes of the Land Use Code, should be considered.  
 
1-402 Implement Policies 
To implement the policies of San Miguel County regarding land use and development, housing, 
growth and related issues, as adopted and amended from time to time. 
 
1-403 Create Common System of Administration and Regulation 
To combine the regulation of all aspects of land use and development and the use of land and 
natural resources into a common system of administration and regulation.  
 
1-404 Simplify the Land Use Regulatory Process 
To simplify the application and review process for such regulatory system. 
 
1-405 Protect Health, Safety and Welfare 
To protect the health, safety and public welfare of San Miguel County.

6-101 D. Applicability

6-401 General Provisions 
6-401 A. Purpose 
The purpose of this Section 6-4 is to facilitate Forestry Practices in a manner that protects 
public health, safety, welfare, the environment, and wildlife of San Miguel County; fosters 
the County’s sustainability -related goals and policies; and complements and does not 
conflict with or duplicate state and federal permitting processes.   
6-401 B. Applicability 
This Section applies to a Forestry Practice or the expansion of an existing Forestry Practice 
located partially or wholly in unincorporated San Miguel County on private or public lands.  
6-401 C. Permit Required 
A permit issued pursuant to this Section or an activity notice is required prior to the initiation 
or expansion of a Forestry Practice unless specifically exempted.  
6-401 D. Forestry Practices Permitted in Certain Zone Districts 
I. Major Forestry Practices on non -federal lands may be permitted pursuant to this 
Section 6-4 only in the following zone districts: 
a. Forestry, Agriculture, and Open 
b. Rangeland Grazing  
c. Wright’s Mesa  
d. Wright’s Mesa Rural Agriculture 
e. West End 
f. High Country Area 
II. Minor Forestry Practices may be permitted pursuant to this Section 6-4 in all zones. 
III. Federal Land. Forestry Practices on federal land may be permitted pursuant to this 
Section in all zone districts.

and Board of County Commissioners review) as set forth in Section 3-6, except that 
federal or state Major Forestry Practices require an Activity Notice as set forth in

The most current CSFS guidelines, handbooks, and technical publications governing wildfire

Forestry Practice  
I.  Any activity conducted on or directly pertaining to forest land and related to 
growing, harvesting, processing , or removing woody biomass  from any forest 
stands, including road and trail construction and maintenance; timber harvesting 
and mechanical thinning; slash disposal; transportation and removal of woody 
biomass; and revegetation. 
II. “Forestry Practice” shall not include: 
a. An activity that causes less than one and a half ( 1.5) acre of surface 
disturbance.  
b. Activities where the primary purpose is:  
i. Property Maintenance.

Major Forestry Practice 
Any Forestry Practice that the Planning Director determines does not qualify as a Minor Forestry

Minor Forestry Practice 
Forestry Practices with the following primary purposes, regardless of whether the felled timber or

than eighty (80) acres of surface disturbance . The County authorizes Qualifying

intended to mitigate the effects of historic land use practices, such as for

IV. Practices to Remove Diseased Trees. A Forestry Practice consisting of less than

Property Maintenance  
Routine activities undertaken by a property owner or occupant to preserve the safety, appearance, 
and functionality of developed or actively used land, including the removal, trimming, or clearing 
of trees, shrubs, brush, and other vegetation that 1) poses a hazard to structures, utilities, or persons; 
2) encroaches upon buildings, fences, driveways, sidewalks, or other improvements;  3) is dead, 
dying, diseased, or damaged, regardless of cause; 4) has fallen or been blown down and requires 
removal from the property; 5) is within a defensible space envelope of a structure; or 6) is 
necessary to maintain established landscaping, lawns, orchards, pastures, agricultural operations, 
or irrigation infrastructure. Property Maintenance does not include the clearing of native or 
naturally occurring vegetation for the purpose of land conservation, new development, grading, or 
significant expansion of the disturbed area beyond what is reasonably necessary to maintain an 
existing improvement on the property. 
Revegetation 
Activities, techniques, treatments, and methods used to reestablish the composition, structure, 
pattern, and ecological processes of disturbed area s in order to sustain terrestrial and aquatic 
ecosystems under current and future conditions.  
Wildfire Mitigation Practice  
Activities undertaken for the purpose of reducing wildfire hazard and risk, including fuel 
reduction, defensible space creation, selective tree thinning, prescribed understory clearing, 
removal of dead or dying vegetation, slash management, and forest heal th treatments, regardless 
of whether such activities also include the commercial sale of timber.

III. Planning BMPs  
a. Institute adequate drainage and erosion control measures, including

b. Minimize soil compaction and erosion.  
c. Limit activities to when soil is dry or frozen.

Section 6-407  Financial Security  
6-407 A. Financial Security Required 
The County may require the applicant for a permit for a Major or Minor Forestry Practice 
to file a guarantee of financial security (“guarantee”), in a form and amount acceptable to 
the County. 
6-407 B. Amount of Guarantee 
I. The amount of the guarantee must be based on the applicant’s submitted cost 
estimate and the County’s estimate of any additional costs to bring in personnel 
and equipment to accomplish any unperformed obligations under the guarantee.  
II. In determining the amount of the guarantee, the County will also consider: 
a. Estimated cost of restoring any impacted areas to their original condition or 
a condition acceptable to the County. 
b. Estimated cost of revegetation and restoration of the Forestry Practice. 
c. Estimated cost of performing all mitigation requirements and permit 
conditions.  
III. The guarantee will be set forth as a permit condition and shall specify as follows:  
a. The guarantee may be adjusted upon receipt of bids or other cost estimates 
to perform the requirements of the permit and these Regulations. 
b. The guarantee may be increased at any time that the County determines that 
the guarantee is insufficient to cover the purposes of the guarantee. 
IV. The County may review the guarantee for adequacy at any time. If the County 
determines that the guarantee is insufficient to perform its purpose, the County shall 
provide the permittee with written notice to increase the guarantee. 
 
V. The permittee shall post the additional guarantee within sixty (60) days from the 
date of the written notice. If the amount of the increased guarantee has not been 
provided within sixty (60) days from the date of the written notice, the County may

VI. If the permittee disagrees with the notice to increase the guarantee, the County shall

6-407 C. Release of Guarantee 
The County may cause the guarantee to be released upon the request of the permittee, based

I. The permittee has surrendered the permit to the County before the commencement

V. The County determines that applicable guaranteed conditions have been satisfied. 
6-407 D. Forfeiture of Guarantee 
I. If the County determines that a guarantee should be forfeited because of any

not made by the permittee, the County shall order the guarantee forfeited.

present statements, documents, and other information for the County’s 
consideration with resp ect to the alleged violation. At the conclusion of the 
hearing, the County shall either withdraw the notice of violation or enter an order 
forfeiting the guarantee. 
III. If the forfeiture results in inadequate revenue to cover the costs of accomplishing 
the purposes of the guarantee, the County’s Attorney shall take such steps as 
deemed proper to recover such costs , where recovery is deemed possible , 
including costs and attorney fees.

conflict with or duplicate state and federal permitting processes.

Property Maintenance  
Routine activities undertaken by a property owner or occupant to preserve the safety, appearance, 
and functionality of developed or actively used land, including the removal, trimming, or clearing 
of trees, shrubs, brush, and other vegetation that 1) poses a hazard to structures, utilities, or persons; 
2) encroaches upon buildings, fences, driveways, sidewalks, or other improvements; 3) is dead, 
dying, diseased, or damaged, regardless of cause; 4) has fallen or been blown down and requires 
removal from the property; 5) is within a defensible space envelope of a structure; or 6) is 
necessary to maintain established landscaping, lawns, orchards, pastures, agricultural operations , 
or irrigation infrastructure. Property Maintenance does not include the clearing of native or 
naturally occurring vegetation for the purpose of land conservation, new development, grading, or 
significant expansion of the disturbed area beyond what is reasonably necessary to maintain an 
existing improvement on the property. 
Revegetation 
Activities, techniques, treatments, and methods used to reestablish the composition, structure, 
pattern, and ecological processes of disturbed area s in order to sustain terrestrial and aquatic 
ecosystems under current and future conditions.  
Wildfire Mitigation Practice  
Activities undertaken for the purpose of reducing wildfire hazard and risk, including fuel 
reduction, defensible space creation, selective tree thinning, prescribed understory clearing, 
removal of dead or dying vegetation, slash management, and forest health treatments , regardless 
of whether such activities also include the commercial sale of timber.

6-402 C. Consultants and Referral Agency Costs 
The costs of consultant and referral agency reviews are the responsibility of the applicant. 
The Planning Director may authorize all or a portion of the review of any phase of the 
application to be performed by a consultant or consultants of the County’s c hoosing and 
sent to referral agencies that the County deems appropriate for the application. Copies of 
any such referral agency comments received must be forwarded to the applicant for its 
response. 
6-402 D. Expansion of Activities or Sequential Projects 
If an applicant receives a permit for a Forestry Practice project that is classified as a Minor 
Forestry Practice, and proposes an additional project within five (5) years of the approval 
date of the permit as an addition to or expansion of the approved Minor Forestry Practice 
such that the projects considered together would have been classified as a Major Forestry 
Practice, then the additional and any such subsequent proposals shall be reviewed as the 
next-higher level of size classification. The decisionmaker shall consider the cumulative 
impacts of the sequential projects or expansions, together and in combination with other 
past, present, and reasonably foreseeable future development , to determine complia nce 
with the applicable Review Criteria. 
6-402 E. Application Fee  
All applications for the development of Forestry Practices must include the application fee 
pursuant to the applicable Board Resolution for Fee Schedule. The Board shall establish 
fees pursuant to the Section 3-1410, and as set forth in the adopted fee schedule. 
6-402 F. Confidential Materials 
An applicant may work with the County Attorney to address how confidential information 
may be appropriately handled in the application materials.
