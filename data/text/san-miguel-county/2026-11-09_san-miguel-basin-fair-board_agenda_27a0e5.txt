San Miguel Basin Fair Board Meeting
October 26, 2026
The meeting was called to order at 7 p.m. on October 26, 2026. Fair Board members 
present were President Makayla Gordon, Vice President Regan Snyder, Tera 
Alexander, Hugh Hardman, Brent Alexander, and new member Westen Enstrom. Also 
present were J.D. Wise, San Miguel County Parks and Open Space Manager; Jacob 
Gordon, Extension Program Coordinator; and guest Liz Lukaszewski. San Miguel 
County Manager Mike Bordogna attended virtually.
Hardman moved to approve the agenda. B. Alexander seconded the motion. The 
motion passed unanimously.
B. Alexander moved to approve the August board meeting minutes. Hardman seconded 
the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
SMC County Manager Report:
Bordogna discussed the possibility of separating the Fair Board from San Miguel 
County and establishing an independent nonprofit organization. He noted that the 
County could provide priority use of the county fairgrounds and continued financial 
support. An independent structure could give the Board greater control over certain 
activities and expand grant opportunities. Bordogna suggested developing a 
memorandum of understanding to document the relationship between an independent 
501(c) nonprofit Fair Board and San Miguel County. The Board would also need to 
update its bylaws and develop a dissolution agreement entrusting the County with 
responsibility if the Board were to dissolve. Several members raised questions about 
the insurance an independent nonprofit would need to obtain to produce the county fair. 
Bordogna and Wise offered to research insurance requirements, and the Board 
identified a need to research other county fairs’ operations and organizational 
structures. Bordogna proposed conducting this research during the winter and reaching 
a decision by early spring, well before the next county fair.
Treasurers Report:
M. Gordon presented the treasurer’s report, noting a $13,000 unfavorable budget 
variance for 2026. Snyder explained that several invoices related to 2025 expenses had 
been recorded in 2026 because they were received late. M. Gordon reported that 
$7,100 in sponsorship funds remained outstanding. Bordogna explained that some 
sponsorship checks had been received but were returned by the County Clerk because 
they were not made payable to San Miguel County. M. Gordon also reported that Junior 
Livestock Sale commission revenue was below budget. The Board received a $5,000 
grant from the Lone Cone Legacy Trust, and M. Gordon was applying for a $10,000 
Westmeath grant. Snyder asked whether any deferred grant funds were available to 
help balance the budget.

4-H Coordinator Report:
J. Gordon presented the 4-H report. The 2026 4-H awards banquet has been 
rescheduled for 2 p.m. on November 8, 2026, at the Nucla Community Center.
Bordogna introduced J.D. Wise as the newly appointed Parks and Open Space 
Manager. Wise expressed enthusiasm for working with and supporting the Board and 
provided his contact information.
Guest Speaker:
Guest Liz Lukaszewski asked the Board to consider a market lamb class with a 
minimum final weight below 100 pounds. She explained that her family raises Churro 
sheep and would like to exhibit them in market classes, but that reaching 100 pounds 
before one year of age is difficult for the breed. Snyder expressed concern about 
creating a separate class for only one or two sheep and indicated that broader exhibitor 
participation would be needed for her to support a new class. T. Alexander expressed 
the same concern. M. Gordon noted that another family supported a lower minimum 
weight so they could exhibit Dorper lambs. Lukaszewski added that her family also 
raises Tunis sheep and hopes to exhibit Tunis lambs in 2027. The Board thanked 
Lukaszewski for raising the issue and agreed to continue discussing options. J. Gordon 
also noted a potential need to increase average daily gain requirements for all species.
Old Business:
The board discussed potential rule changes for 2027:
1. Revise the small animal and poultry rules based on the previous year’s judge’s 
recommendations, including simplifying the rules and addressing the dual-
purpose breeds category.  
2. Revise sale eligibility and limits, including stronger penalties for exhibitors who 
choose not to sell Grand or Reserve Champions and possible removal of a 
champion title if the animal is not sold.
3. Consider making the sale terminal.
4. Establish the top five overall placings for each species and include those 
placings in the sale order.
5. Consider increasing average daily gain requirements.
6. Consider adding a new market lamb class with different final weight 
requirements.
7. Consider reducing the number of market animals an exhibitor may sell from five 
to two.
8. Consider other ways to limit the number of animals sold through the Junior 
Livestock Sale, such as establishing caps for each species and allowing only the 
highest-placing animals to be sold.
Equipment purchases. M. Gordon presented quotes for panels and gates. The 
Board agreed to defer planned equipment purchases until a later date when the 
budget is balanced.

New Business
M. Gordon presented a letter from Ashley Tomlinson requesting appointment to the 
Fair Board. The Board unanimously agreed to recommend her appointment to the 
Board of County Commissioners.
Snyder moved to approve the following 2027 weigh-in dates. B. Alexander seconded 
the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
 Beef weigh-in: January 31, 2027, at 10 a.m.
 Lamb, goat, and hog weigh-in: May 2, 2027, at 10 a.m.
 Small animal weigh-in: June 27, 2027, at 6 p.m.
M. Gordon proposed moving the fair dance to the Saturday before the Junior 
Livestock Sale and noted that $3,500 had been budgeted for the event. The Board 
agreed that holding the dance on the Saturday before fair activities would be most 
advantageous. Members noted that the change would simplify event coordination, 
reduce stress on the Board and animals, and potentially increase participation 
because attendees would be less tired at the beginning of the fair.
The Board agreed to hold its next meeting at 7 p.m. on November 9, 2026, at the 
Sheriff’s Annex in Norwood.
B. Alexander moved to adjourn the meeting at 9:15 p.m. Hardman seconded the 
motion. The motion passed unanimously.
