Road Report September 2026 
 
East End Deep Creek District: 
Our East End crews kept busy this month performing a variety of projects including 
assisting the West End Crews in finishing up chip seal, replacing some large culverts, 
repairing a rock retaining wall, hauling material for projects, setting up signage, grading 
various roads and cleaning drains after rain storms.  Personnel also worked training new 
employees and helping them get orientated with the district and County.  Staff performed 
vehicle and equipment repair and maintenance as needed. Time was spent cleaning and 
organizing the shop and facility.  
 
Some of the larger projects and County Roads we worked on are as follows: 
 K69 - Black Bear Pass slide cleanup 
 S8 - Chip seal 
 U29 East - Chip seal 
 57J (Elk Creek) - Replaced culvert 
 67L (Mill Creek) - Replaced culvert 
 M44 - Built rock wall 
 M44 - Graded 
 56V - Graded, pulled ditches and drainages 
 58P - Graded, pulled ditches and drainages 
 60M - Graded, pulled ditches and drainages 
 
West End Districts – Norwood, Basin, and Egnar: 
Personnel from our West End districts worked together to complete a variety of projects in 
September.  Starting by finishing up with chip seal on roads in both the Basin and Egnar 
Districts. Hauling Gravel to projects in both the Basin and Egnar Districts.  Time was spent 
in all three Districts cleaning culverts, grading roads, repairing and cleaning cattleguards, 
trimming trees, clearing brush and mowing shoulders.  Crews performed vehicle and 
equipment maintenance as needed. 
The larger projects that were performed are as follows: 
 County Road F4 West - Gravel hauling from Mile Marker 1 to Mile Marker 2.

 County Road 31U - Gravel hauling from Mile Marker 6.5 to Cattle Guard. 
 W35: Culvert cleaning. 
 County Road S8, U29E, 56V, and X48 - Chip seal work. 
 Beaver Park Gravel Pit - Worked in the gravel pit, pushing material to the 
contractor. 
 Cattle Guard Repairs - County Roads F11, K8, 39N, 43Z, S44, and 31U. 
 Road Grading - 31U, 44ZS, W35, S44, U29E, and L40. 
 Tree and Brush Removal: 3HN and K1. 
 
Equipment Maintenance and repair that was performed by District staff: 
 Unit 341 - Chip Spreader: Replaced conveyor belt. 
 Unit 371 - Mac Water Truck: Replaced pump seal. 
 Unit 235 - Lowboy: Replaced pony motor. 
 Unit 321 - Broom: Replaced brushes. 
 
Personnel from Ouray County Road and Bridge assisted with chip seal this month.  We 
would like to extend a special thank you to Montezuma County Road and Bridge for their 
assistance after our chip spreader suffered a torn conveyor belt rendering it out of service.  
Montezuma County acted swiftly and responded to a call for help sending their chip 
spreader to help us complete work on County Roads S8 and U29E.  This breakdown 
occurred after all of the remaining asphalt to complete the project had been ordered and 
delivered.  Their assistance enabled us to only lose one half day of operation.  
 
 
 
Road and Bridge Administration: 
Staff worked with HR performing interviews and completing new hire forms for 
onboarding employees.  Road and Bridge with help from San Miguel County Human 
Resources was successful in filling or offering positions to fill nearly every opening in the 
Department this month.  
Time was spent reviewing and submitting comments for Oil, Gas and Geothermal and 
Forestry Practices Land Use Code amendments.    
Road and Bridge admin staff worked on finishing up the Norwood Fuel Island Canopy 
RFP process and moved on to the project award.  
Road and Bridge staff attended the first 2027 budget meetings to discuss our next fiscal 
year’s budget.   
Staff performed site inspections and reviewed Development and Road and Bridge permit 
applications.  Staff also performed inspections on completed projects to close out active 
permits. 
Time was spent putting together and forwarding documents for CORA requests received 
this month. 
Research and information were collected and reviewed on the potential to add new trucks 
to the fleet.  Proposals and lease options were considered and sent to San Miguel County 
Finance for review and consideration.    
Road and Bridge issued the notice to proceed on the retaining wall project on CR 58P.  
Contractors plan to start and complete work by the end of September.  This is the first 
phase of a multi-year project to improve the width and drainage on CR 58P.

manner provided for by law. 
 
1. The types of open meetings which may be held by the San Miguel County Board of 
Commissioners are: 
 
a. Regular Meetings 
Regular Meetings are generally held on the first and third Wednesday of each 
month. Matters considered by the BOCC during a Regular Meeting may include 
reports from department heads or elected officials, public hearings on specific 
issues, policy-making decisions, signing contracts or agreements, work sessions 
on specific issues, or approving expenditures or requests from county officials. A 
Regular Meeting shall be held after at least twenty-four (24) hours’ notice as 
posted at the designated posting place, recorded, and have minutes taken. 
 
b. Special Meetings 
Special Meetings may be called by the Chair when circumstances warrant or 
upon the request of a member of the BOCC or County Manager. A Special 
Meeting shall be held after at least twenty-four (24) hours’ notice as posted at the 
designated posting place, recorded, and have minutes taken. 
 
c. Emergency Meetings 
Emergency Meetings may be held without twenty-four (24) hours’ notice when 
necessary for the immediate preservation of public property, health, peace, and 
safety and shall be conducted pursuant to procedures as adopted by the BOCC 
by Resolution #2018-26. An Emergency Meeting shall be recorded and have 
minutes taken. Any formal decision made by the BOCC at an Emergency 
Meeting shall be ratified at the next scheduled Regular Meeting. 
 
d. Work Sessions 
Work Sessions are generally held on the second and fourth Wednesday of each 
month. A work session is an informal, deliberative session between staff and 
members of the BOCC. Matters considered during these meetings may include 
receiving reports from County staff, discussion of County business, or day-to-day 
oversight of property or employee supervision. No formal, policy-making 
decisions may be made at a work session. A work session shall be held after at 
least twenty-four (24) hours’ notice as posted at the designated posting place, 
recorded, and minutes will not be taken.

Background 
The County Planning Department is the custodian of many paper files documenting 
Land Use applications, surveys, maps, minutes, general correspondence and much 
more dating back to the 1970’s and beyond. These files take up significant space inside 
of the Planning Department and can be arduous to sort through in response to CORA 
(Colorado Open Record Act) requests and for research purposes.  
Proposed Solution 
After significant consideration the Planning Department has determined that scanning 
these files and hosting them within our existing Google Drive infrastructure is the best 
path forward to better utilize office space and to enable greater searchability within the 
files. The County has been in contact with RMMI Digital Documents Solutions and has 
decided to piggyback off of a Request for Proposals that was completed by Jefferson 
County in 2022. Colorado Revised Statues 24-110-201 allows municipalities to share 
contracts and avoid a separate RFP process.  
RM
MI would be responsible for collecting the files from the Planning Department, 
transporting them to their facility in Denver, and scanning all files into Google Drive. 
RMMI would retain the files while quality assurance is completed. All files will be 
scanned using Optical Character Recognition technology which can be used within 
Google Drive’s search function. This will be a huge asset for staff in completing CORA 
requests and research. The current cost estimated by RMMI for their services totals 
$32,898. This number is subject to change depending on the ultimate file count.  
Addi
tional departments who may want to digitize their files would be able to use RMMI 
and Google Drive to complete the same process.

I. INTRODUCTION 
Jefferson County is interested in contracting with firms for scanning, document enhancement, indexing 
for documents within the County.  
 
It is anticipated that most of the requested services will be for the Human Services Department.  
However, these servic es may also be requested by other Jefferson County divisions and departments, 
including, but not limited to, the Sheriff’s Office, Human Resources, Planning and Zoning, Safety & 
Compliance, Finance, and other offices within the County receiving documents f rom the public or 
transitioning past paper files or microfilm to an electronic format.   
 
II. BACKGROUND 
The types of documents for these requested services may include, but are not limited to the following: 
1. Applications from the public for receipt of services by Human Services 
2. Personnel Action Forms and I-9s 
3. Zoning maps, plans, CAD files, other land use matter files 
4. Worker’s Compensation claim documents and reports 
The quantities and needs  of each County division and department varies.  Below are estimates of 
documents needing these services.  As the need for scanning services is identified in the future, additional 
quantities may be added or removed. 
1. Human Services:  2500 banker boxes per month 
2. Human Resources: 1000 documents per month  
Jefferson County has the following goals: 
1. Services are provided by personnel who meet requirements such as, but not limited to Criminal 
Justice Information Services (CJIS) certifications, background checks, etc. 
2. Services are provided in accordance with all applicable laws ( e.g., Health Insurance Portability 
and Accountability Act of 1996) for redaction and documents are encrypted as needed and are 
stored in an appropriate manner.
