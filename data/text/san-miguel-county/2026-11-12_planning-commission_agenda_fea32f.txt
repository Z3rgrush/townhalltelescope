BACKGROUND 
 
The updated Employee Housing Impact Mitigation Fee was adopted on July 6, 2022, 
and went into effect on July 15, 2022. The Impact Fee applies to new construction of 
residences and additions to residences in the unincorporated County within the 
Telluride R-1 school district.  
 
It was amended in 2023 to increase the exempt residential floor area from 1,800 s.f. to 
2,000 s.f. and to revise the mitigation rate(s) so that gaps at transition points between 
mitigation rates were eliminated.   
 
Employee housing mitigation directly addresses the impact residential construction has 
on the related housing need generated by residential development. Payment of Impact 
Fees satisfies the requirement to provide housing for people associated with the 
construction of a residence and the employees needed for the ongoing maintenance of 
the property, e.g., housekeeping, landscaping, maintenance and snow removal.  The 
county utilizes the payments through purchasing housing units, collaboration on 
community housing projects, i.e., Sunnyside, and land purchases. 
 
As adopted in the Impact Fee, the number of employees generated (construction and 
ongoing maintenance) is calculated based upon the nexus studies from RRC 
Associates and the Housing Collaborative, LLC.  A graduated mitigation rate (18% to 
90%) is then applied, specifying the percentage of employee generation for which the 
project developer must mitigate.  The Land Use Code requires 400 s.f. per employee.  
That is then multiplied by the impact mitigation amount, or market gap amount, currently 
$1112 per square foot.  Refer to the San Miguel County Affordable Housing Fee 
Methodology Report for complete information on the Impact Fee.  Both the Town of 
Telluride and Town of Mountain Village are using similar methodology, with some 
differences in implementation. 
 
The Town of Telluride utilizes the same employee generation study as the county. It 
includes basements and heated garages in computing floor area, and utilizes a 90% 
mitigation rate across all square footages.  Its current Gap Fee, which was updated in 
2025, is $902 per square foot of mitigation required. The Town’s impact fee amount is 
roughly eighteen (18) percent less than the county’s for residences that are 5,000 
square feet. See table in Appendix E for Telluride’s EHIMF calculations at 2,001 s.f, 
4,000 s.f. and 5,000 s.f.

The Town of Mountain Village utilizes a recent employee generation study that appears 
to account for construction workers only. It utilizes a 60% mitigation rate across all 
square footages.  Its payment in-lieu fee amount established in 2024 is $606 per square 
foot.  The fee amount required is roughly seventy-five (75) percent less than the 
county’s for residences that are 5,000 square feet. See table in Appendix C for 
Mountain Village’s EHIMF calculations at 2,001 s.f, 4,000 s.f. and 5,000 s.f. 
 
With the County’s fee, a credit toward the Impact Mitigation Fee for the construction of a 
Caretaker unit or Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) is possible if the property owner 
chooses to deed-restrict that unit for occupancy by a qualified employee. 
 
Sales in the Telluride Region of free-market units in multifamily buildings are tracked 
annually, and the mean of the three (3) prior year’s average annual sales price per 
square foot is computed.  The market gap is based on the 3-year average. Three prior 
years of real estate sales data is utilized rather than just a single prior-year period to 
calculate a market price per square foot. This reduces the variability of the annual real 
estate sales and therefore minimizes annual fluctuations in the level of the proposed 
impact fees.  Sales values increased dramatically in 2021 through 2024, as shown in 
the following sales price graph, with a slight decrease in 2025.

30. The update can be done administratively.  However, due to litigation, staff was 
directed to hold off on the update in 2023 and 2024. In June 2025 the BOCC directed 
staff to update the Gap fee in a phased approach.

Exempt Floor Area 
There is an exemption for single-family dwellings and duplexes that have a floor area of 
2,000 s.f. or less.  Per the Land Use Code definition of Floor Area, garages and decks 
are not counted as floor area, and are exempt from the mitigation requirements.  
Basements with a floor-to-ceiling height greater than 5 feet are counted as floor area. 
Mechanical spaces are also counted.  
 
Impact Mitigation Rate 
The Mitigation Rate is graduated and reflects the 2,000 s.f. floor area exemption, as 
follows: 
 
▪       0 – 2000 s.f.:   0%    
▪ 2001 – 2500 s.f.:  18%  30% 
▪ 2501 – 3000 s.f.:  30%  42% 
▪ 3001 – 4000 s.f.:  42%  66% 
▪ 4001 – 5000 s.f.:  66%  90% 
▪ 5001 –11999 s.f.: 90%

DISCUSSION 
Proposed policy changes to the Employee Housing Impact Mitigation Fee 
 
The calculation of the Employee Housing Impact Fee has several variables. These 
variables are implemented either through a Board policy decision or through a 
nexus/linkage study. Variables, such as number of employees generated, can only be 
changed by a nexus linkage study performed by qualified staff or a consultant. The 
mitigation rate, exemptions, definitions, and floor area per employee are adopted by 
resolution as amendment to Land Use Code Section 5-1303.

Analysis of Incentives 
County staff has cursorily reviewed the incentives suggested for alignment with county 
priorities and programs.  Staff would appreciate direction from the county boards of 
which incentives to further investigate for potential use. 
 
Incentive 1:  Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) incentives 
 
Most ADUs and caretaker units in the county are used by family and guests. Even 
historically deed restricted units are not occupied by local workers. Most property 
owners do not wish to have local workers housed on their property. 
 
San Miguel County provides a 25% reduction on its building and plan review fees for 
residential structures containing 1,800 square feet or less of floor area.  It provides an 
additional 25% discount for affordable housing structures. 
 
The Accessory Dwelling Unit Grant Program (ADUG), enacted by Colorado’s ADU law 
(HB24-1152), is a potential funding stream and provides grants to certified ADU 
Supportive Jurisdictions for activities that promote and incentivize the construction of 
ADUs. The County could become certified as an ADU Supportive Jurisdiction. Available 
funding, however, is limited and would need to be matched by the County.  
 
The draft Housing Code Update includes incentives to create additional ADU and 
caretaker units, provided they are deed-restricted as workforce housing. 
 
Incentive 2: Conversion of structures into work force housing 
 
The use of existing mother-in-law suites, ADUs, or caretaker units for workforce housing 
could be an effective way of using housing units that are already constructed.  This 
could help take the county out of the role of real estate developer. A tricky question is 
what would property owners require in terms of an inducement to deed-restrict the 
rentable housing unit and rent out the property? Also, how would the county administer 
the payment of funds to private citizens. One potential incentive would be to allow the 
deed-restriction of existing units in lieu of fees for new dwellings being constructed 
elsewhere, as discussed in #4 below. 
 
Incentive 3:  Transfer fees 
 
In Summit County, property developers and owners have the option of avoiding the 
impact fee at the time of building permit issuance by voluntarily imposing a private 
transfer fee upon subsequent sale of the property. It is an option available to property 
owners developing residential structures.  If a property owner selects this option, the 
completed Housing Allowance Restrictive Covenant must be given to the Summit 
County Building Department prior to permit issuance and the Housing Allowance 
Restrictive Covenant is recorded restricting the property as described above in 
perpetuity.

Summit County’s voluntary real estate transfer tax of one-third of 1% of the sale price of 
a $750,000 residence in the Telluride R-1 School District would produce about $2,500. 
Exempting the first sale after the exemption would further reduce the effectiveness of 
the program.   
 
Incentive 4:  Off-site mitigation 
 
The county currently requires developers of new residential construction and additions 
to provide affordable housing for up to 90 percent of the employees generated by their 
projects, either by paying a cash-in-lieu fee, or providing it on site. 
 
The county has required that mitigation provisions occur on the property on which the 
impacts occur.  However, the county may be amenable subject to certain conditions to 
allow off-site housing mitigation contributions. This could be in the form of providing 
offsite land to the county for building affordable housing, or for providing employee 
housing units offsite.   
 
Under a new program in Aspen, a developer can explore the market for affordable 
housing credits to find out if they could negotiate a lower price than the cost of 
constructing new units or buying down existing free-market units. And developers who 
don’t want to pay the cash-in-lieu fee or build an accessory dwelling unit can pursue 
buying credits, especially if they are less expensive than the other options. 
 
The creation of a program for purchasing affordable housing credits would require an 
investment in the creation and upkeep of a new housing program.  It would be difficult to 
identify giving and receiving areas similar to a transfer of development rights program, 
which we recently opted not to pursue as part of the affordable housing LUC update. 
Most existing condominium Homeowners Associations (HOA) prohibit deed-restriction 
of units. 
 
A developer could conceivably build a multi-household building and sell credits to a new 
house builder in lieu of fees. (This was done in Telluride in the 1990s.) 
 
Incentive 5:  Development Flexibility 
 
Currently, the county is updating its Land Use Code in order to facilitate the provision of 
Affordable Housing in the unincorporated areas of the county. It is proposing to allow an 
additional Accessory Dwelling Unit, Caretaker Unit, or Secondary Residential Unit on 
properties if the property’s water well permit or utility allows for such. 
 
Just last month, the county approved a setback adjustment in support of the Town of 
Mountain Village’s affordable housing project in Ilium. The project proposes 49 units of 
workforce housing in a townhouse style development. To accommodate the proposed 
building layout, a seven (7’) foot front setback was approved by the Board of Adjustment 
where a 20’ setback was required.

The Society Turn project had a reduced parking requirement to reflect the differences in 
parking demand between morning and evening. This project will also produce a higher 
workforce housing bedroom count than was required by the applicant. 
 
Incentive 6:  Fee Payment Deferral 
 
San Miguel County does not offer payment schedules or deferrals for the payment of 
development fees.  They are due at the time of permit issuance.  It may be problematic 
to collect payment of fees after a permit is issued, and to institute a collection system. 
 
Incentive 7:  Voluntary Charitable Giving 
 
The county does not have staff dedicated to or programs related to the development of 
gifts or donations to the county for aims or projects like workforce housing. It has been 
the county’s goal to keep out of the way of non-profits who rely on grants and charitable 
donations to their organizations. We are wholly supportive of those non-profits that are 
in the business of providing or supporting affordable housing.

Employee Housing Impact Fee/Mitigation 
I. The employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation applies to all Development for which a 
Building Permit application is applied for and approved by the County, unless exempted 
from payment of the employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation pursuant to Section 5-1303 
G.XI of this Code, or that has otherwise fully mitigated its Employee Housing Impacts in 
accordance with the applicable LUC provisions. The employee housing Impact 
Fee/Mitigation applies to: 
a.   The new construction of a Building for which a Building Permit is required under the 
County's Building Codes; 
b.  The construction of an addition to an existing Building for which a Building Permit is 
required under the County's Building Code; or 
c.   A change in use where the new use generates more employees than the previous use 
generated. 
II.  The purpose of the employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation is to require the applicable 
development to pay to mitigate the impacts of development and land use to the employee 
housing stock managed or controlled by the County or its authorized designee, the San 
Miguel Regional Housing Authority. 
III.  The employee housing Impact Fee is based on the difference between the free market price 
of housing in the Telluride Region and the price that is affordable to households with 
incomes equivalent to the Area Median Income. The method used in calculating the 
employee housing Impact Fee has been adopted by the Board of County Commissioners 
based upon research conducted by Planning staff and is contained in Appendix E of this 
Code.

IV.   The employee housing Impact Fee constitutes a fee of general applicability within the 
County and as such shall be applicable to all property located within the Telluride R-1 School 
District in the unincorporated County. 
V.   Responsibility for Administration and Collection of Employee Housing Impact Fee 
 The employee housing Impact Fee will be determined by the County's Building Department 
or any other entity determined by the Board of County Commissioners to collect such 
employee housing Impact Fee. The Building Department will determine the Square Footage 
of Floor Area of a project for purposes of calculating the required employee housing Impact 
Fee. The Planning Office will make all other determinations and interpretations regarding 
employee housing Impact Fee administration required to properly implement these 
regulations, including, but not limited to, the calculation of any applicable credit as defined 
in this Code. The County intends to use the money solely for defraying the cost of capital 
facilities for deed-restricted, affordable housing pursuant to State of Colorado Statute 29-20-
104.5. 
VI.  When Employee Housing Impact Fee Is Calculated and Due 
The employee housing Impact Fee shall be a condition of a receipt of a Development Permit 
and shall be calculated and paid at time of issuance of a Building Permit. 
VII.  Calculation of Employee Housing Impact Fee 
The employee housing Impact Fee shall be calculated based upon the type of Development 
authorized to be constructed by the Building Permit, the number of employees generated 
by the development, and the total Square Footage of Floor Area proposed to be 
constructed as authorized by the Building Permit. 
VIII.  Determination of Square Footage of Floor Area Subject to Employee Housing Impact Fee 
The determination of the Square Footage of Floor Area of a Building that is subject to the 
employee housing Impact Fee will be calculated based on the San Miguel County Land Use 
Code, and the Department's policies, procedures and methods of calculating Square 
Footage of Floor Area under this Code. 
IX.   Mixed Use Buildings 
For Buildings that are mixed use (e.g., Commercial/Industrial/Residential Buildings) the 
employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation will be applied as follows: 
a.  Residential area (two (2) units or fewer) will be charged the employee housing Impact 
Fee for Residential Development.

XI.  Exemptions to the Employee Housing Impact Fee/Mitigation 
An employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation will not be required to be paid or constructed 
for the following categories of Development: 
a.  Any Single-family Residence, Duplex, Multi-family unit or Lot that is Deed-Restricted 
under the County R-1 Housing Deed Restriction, Covenant or other restriction approved 
by the Board of County Commissioners is exempt from paying the Impact Fee. 
b.  Change of Use 
 Change of use without adding additional Square Footage of Floor Area or without 
increasing employee generation as determined herein will not result in the assessment 
of an employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation. 
c.  Residential or Commercial Remodels 
 Commercial or Residential interior remodels that do not involve the construction of 
additional Square Footage or do not increase employee generation as determined herein 
will not result in the assessment of an employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation. 
d.  Affordable Housing in Subdivisions 
 Existing or proposed subdivisions that include deed-restricted affordable housing units 
properly restrained through deed-restrictions from open market sale or use to ensure 
that it is properly characterized as affordable housing, as such term is defined by this 
Code, may be given a level of credit to offset or mitigate the total employee housing 
Impact Fee assessed, unless there is an increase in the density or a change in use that 
increases employee generation. To evaluate and determine the level of credit assessed, 
the percent of deed-restricted, affordable housing units will be evaluated in relation to 
the impact mitigation required of that Development when approved.

e.  Single-family Dwelling Two Thousand Five Hundred Square Feet or Less of Floor Area 
 The Board of County Commissioners finds that residential improvements two thousand 
five hundred (2,000 2,500) sq. ft. or less are similar to the national average house size 
and are presumed to be occupied by local employees, and only residential 
improvements with a Floor Area of greater than two thousand five hundred (2,000 
2,500) sq. ft. should pay employee housing Impact Fees. This is based on the fact that 
residential improvements greater than two thousand five hundred (2,000 2,500) sq. ft. 
of Floor Area have a strong tendency for non-resident occupancy, and the employee 
generation for non-resident occupied residential structures (second homes) is 
significantly greater than that for resident-occupied improvements creating impacts 
beyond the County's ability to mitigate without a fee assessed for the development. 
f.  Other Exemptions 
 The Board of County Commissioners may grant additional exemptions and waivers of the 
employee housing Impact Fee/Mitigation when deemed to be in the public interest due 
to special circumstances or unique situations where the Development provides 
community benefit(s), or where the value of such benefit(s) meets or exceeds the 
mitigation that would otherwise be required.

M E M O R A N D U M 
 
TO:      San Miguel County Planning Commission 
FROM:   John Huebner, AICP, Senior Planner 
RE:      San Miguel County Request to Rezone portion of Placerville Park and Fire 
Station Parcel (Parcel #452334400915), from Forestry, Agriculture and 
Open (F) to Public (PUB) 
DATE:   November 12, 2026  
 
BACKGROUND 
San Miguel County is the holder of a Land Patent (No. 05-90-0028) granted by the 
United States of America for the tract of land, parcel #452334400915, that contains the 
Placerville Park and Telluride Fire Protection District Station #3, located near 
Placerville. The parcel contains approximately 8.3 acres, and the patent allows for a fire 
station and community park facilities only. The intent is to rezone the portion of the 
property surrounding the fire station to Public (PUB), for the purpose of making 
consistent zoning for the current and potential future activities on that section of 
property. The Telluride Fire Protection District is expected to submit an application(s) in 
the future for the development of a training tower and expansion of Fire Station #3. A re-
development plan of the fire station has been shared with the county. This application is 
not intended to serve that purpose. Should the property be rezoned, the fire protection 
district may then move forward with re-development plans, at which time specific details

PROPOSED REZONE 
This zoning amendment is being requested in light of the existing uses of the two county 
properties, which include a fire station, the Placerville community park, the historic 
Placerville schoolhouse community meeting space, and playground.  As the County has 
reviewed activities and entertained expanded uses of County owned parcels in the area, 
it recognized that some longstanding activities do not accurately reflect the purpose of 
the existing Forestry/Agricultural/Open zone district purposes (fire station), and instead 
fit much more closely with the Public (PUB) zoning which is appropriate as a reflection 
of the public ownership of the parcel by the County. Split zoning is proposed because 
PUB zoning does not allow parks, and F zoning does not permit fire stations. 
Furthermore, Parks (P) zoning does not allow fire stations and the Placerville 
Commercial (PC) zone does not allow further rezones, and its maximum height limit 
could restrict future fire station redevelopment. 
 
The subject of the proposed rezone application is located on the larger of the two 
County parcels that comprise the Placerville Park and the Telluride Fire Station #3 
property, containing about 8.3 acres. The property is currently zoned Forestry, 
Agriculture and Open (F).  Parcels to the south in the Placerville Townsite are zoned 
Placerville Commercial (PC) and Placerville Residential (PR), while the other adjoining 
parcels are zoned F.  The Geyser Lode parcels, further to the south located across the 
San Miguel River, are split zoned PR and F. 
 
It is proposed to only rezone a portion of the parcel, containing approximately 2.397 
acres, to Public (PUB).  The parcel is legally identified as Lot 34, Section 34, Township 
44 North, Range 11 West, NMPM, according to the Bureau of Land Management 
dependent resurvey and survey, accepted November 8, 1983, parcel #452334400915. 
The Applicant has provided a legal description of the portion of the parcel that is to be 
rezoned. Rezoning is identified in LUC Section 5-1803 and Section 3-601 E as a 
development application subject to Two-step Review, i.e., review and recommendation 
at a public meeting by the Planning Commission (CPC) and Public Hearing review and 
action at a public hearing by Board of County Commissioners (BOCC). This is the first 
step of a two-step county review process. If approved, the BOCC resolution will include 
the boundary description, describing the boundaries of the zone district, as shown on 
the following Boundary Description map (labeled Exhibit A of the application). This 
illustration, as noted, does not represent a monumented survey.  It is only intended to 
depict the provided description of the proposed rezone. 
 
The map illustrates the anticipated rezone area. There is legal access from Colorado 
State Highway 145 and Front Street. It is expected that re-development of the parcel 
would include an enlarged fire station and a new training tower. Water service would 
come from sharing and improving the existing well and systems of the adjacent 
Placerville Park operated by the County, or from stand-alone facilities. Sewage 
treatment is provided be by an onsite wastewater treatment system. 
 
The proposed development with an enlarged fire station with bunkrooms and training 
tower may incrementally impact the driveway and Front Street. A traffic study or letter,

The San Miguel County Comprehensive Development Plan is the vision document for

The subject property proposed for rezoning is within the San Miguel Canyon Area 
boundaries. The boundaries encompass “all developable land within one mile of the

continue with objective 5, allowing the rest area of the park and restroom facility near

supply, parks, drainage, school and emergency medical facilities;
