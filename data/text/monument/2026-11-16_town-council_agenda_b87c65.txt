TOWN OF MONUMENT 
COUNCIL MEMORANDUM 
TO: Mayor and Town Council 
FROM: Andrew S. Archuleta, Director of Public Works 
DATE: November 16, 2026 
SUBJECT: Resolution No. 81-2026 to Approving a Contract with Palomar to Purchase a 
Modular Office 
 
STRATEGIC PRIORITIES (2026–2028) 
Reinforce a Positive Work Culture - Promotes a positive and professional work culture that 
attracts and retains top talent and enhances high-quality service delivery to the 
community.  
 
BACKGROUND: The Public Works team has identified the need for additional office space 
to support the departmental needs. The proposed modular office will be located at the 
current Public Works shop at 506 Jefferson St. and will provide additional dedicated 
workspace for the staff. The current space available for the Public Works crew is limited 
and does not provide a clean, functional, and professional work environment that the staff 
need to effectively perform their duties. Public Works crew work hard every day to maintain 
the Town’s infrastructure, facilities, parks, and other essential services, and providing 
them with an appropriate space to complete work, hold meetings, take breaks, and 
prepare for daily operations is an important investment in the team. 
 
DISCUSSION/ANALYSIS: Public Works contacted Lewis-Palmer School District 38 to 
discuss their recent purchases of modular buildings. The district identified Palomar 
Modular Buildings as the company they had used through a cooperative purchasing 
program. Public Works then contacted Palomar and determined that their four-office 
modular building would meet the needs of the department and will fit within the Public 
Works Shop Facility. 

The proposed modular office will provide four offices, one restroom, a kitchenette, and a 
common space for the staff. The building will require water, sanitary sewer, and electrical 
service, which are being coordinated separately as part of the overall project. The office 
layout will provide our park and streets supervisors as well as both mechanics to have their 
own offices to conduct their daily work. The common space will provide the rest of the 
crew with a clean area to take breaks and lunch and stay out of the elements. This modular 
office is an overdue need for the Public Works team and something that they deserve.  
 
FINANCIAL IMPACT: The proposed contract with Palomar Modular Buildings is for an 
amount of $202,469.00. Funding for the purchase is available within the Facilities Capital 
Projects – Other fund. Palomar Modular Buildings is available through an existing 
competitively procured cooperative purchasing agreement previously utilized by Lewis-
Palmer School District 38. The Town’s Procurement Policy allows the Town to utilize 
cooperative purchasing agreements that have been competitively procured by another 
public agency or approved cooperative purchasing organization, allowing the Town to 
utilize the established contract rather than conduct a separate competitive solicitation. 
 
RECOMMENDED ACTION: We recommend the passage of Resolution No. 81-2026, a 
resolution approving a contract with Palomar to purchase a modular office. 
 
RESOLUTION NO. 81-2026 
 
A RESOLUTION APPROVING A CONTRACT WITH PALOMAR TO PURCHASE A 
MODULAR OFFICE 
 
 WHEREAS, the Town of Monument has prioritized reinforcing a positive work culture 
for 2026–2028, including promoting a professional, supportive, and values-driven organizational 
environment that attracts and retains a highly qualified workforce and enhances the delivery of 
high-quality services to the Monument community; 
 
 WHEREAS, the staff has identified the need for additional office space to support current 
and future departmental operations, and;  
 
WHEREAS, the existing space available to Public Works staff does not adequately meet 
the operational needs of the department, and the proposed modular office will provide employees 
with a clean, functional, and professional workspace to complete administrative duties, hold 
meeting, take breaks, and prepare for daily operations, and; 
 
WHEREAS, the proposed modular office will be located at the Public Works facility at 
506 Jefferson St and will provide dedicated office and shared workspace for Public Works 
employees, and; 
 
WHEREAS, Palomar Modular Building is available through an existing competitively 
procured cooperative purchasing agreement, and the Town’s Procurement Policy allows the Town 
to utilize cooperative purchasing agreements rather than conducting a separate competitive 
solicitation, and; 
 
WHEREAS, The staff have determined that the modular office proposed by Palomar 
Modular Buildings will meet the operational needs of the Public Works Department, and; 
 
WHEREAS, funding for the proposed modular office is available within the approved 
Town budget. 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE 
TOWN OF MONUMENT, COLORADO THAT: 
 
The Town Council hereby approves a contract with Palomar Modular Buildings to purchase a new 
modular office. The total amount of this contract is not to exceed $202,469.00 which will be 
funded by the Facilities Capital Projects fund. 
 
 PASSED AND RESOLVED by the Town Council of the Town of Monument, El Paso 
County, Colorado, this 16th day of November 2026 by a vote of ___ for and ____ against.

COUNCIL MEMORANDUM 
TO: Mayor and Town Council 
FROM: Andrew S. Archuleta, Director of Public Works 
DATE: November 16, 2026 
SUBJECT: Resolution No. 80-2026 to Approving a Contract with MVEA to Upgrade the 
Public Works Shop Transformer 
 
STRATEGIC PRIORITIES (2026–2028) 
Reinforce a Positive Work Culture - Promotes a positive and professional work culture that 
attracts and retains top talent and enhances high-quality service delivery to the 
community.  
 
BACKGROUND: The Public Works team is in the process of installing a new modular office 
building to support their operations. As part of the project, staff coordinated with Mountain 
View Electric Association (MVEA) to determine load ratings and to determine if the current 
transformer would be compatible with the new building.  
 
DISCUSSION/ANALYSIS: After MVEA received the electrical load requirements needed for 
the new modular office, they determined that the existing transformer would need to be 
upgraded to accommodate the additional electrical demand. The transformer will also 
need to be relocated from its current location as tree roots have lifted the current 
transformer.  
As MVEA is the sole electric provider serving the Public Works facility, they are responsible 
for the upgrade and relocation of the transformer and associated infrastructure. All 
associated work with the transformer will be coordinated with Green Electric who will be 
connecting power from the transformer to the new modular office. 

Approval of the proposed contract will allow MVEA to proceed with the transformer 
upgrade and relocation and allow the modular office project to keep moving forward.  
 
FINANCIAL IMPACT: The proposed contract with MVEA is for the not to exceed amount of 
$29,894.00. Funding for this work is available with the Facilities Capital Projects- Other 
funds. The Town’s Procurement Policy exempts sole-source purchases, including utility 
services that can only be provided by one supplier or source, from competitive bidding 
requirements.  
 
RECOMMENDED ACTION: We recommend the passage of Resolution No. 80-2026, a 
resolution approving a contract with MVEA to upgrade the Public Works transformer. 
 
RESOLUTION NO. 80-2026 
 
A RESOLUTION APPROVING A CONTRACT WITH MOUNTAIN VIEW ELECTRIC 
ASSOCIATION TO UPGRADE THE PUBLIC WORKS SHOP TRANSFORMER 
 
 WHEREAS, the Town of Monument has prioritized reinforcing a positive work culture 
for 2026–2028, including promoting a professional, supportive, and values-driven organizational 
environment that attracts and retains a highly qualified workforce and enhances the delivery of 
high-quality services to the Monument community; 
 
 WHEREAS, the Town of Monument Public Works Department is in the process of 
installing a new modular office building to provide additional space to support departmental needs, 
and;  
 
WHEREAS, additional electrical capacity is required to provide electrical service to the 
new modular office, and Mountain View Electric Association (MVEA) has determined that the 
existing transformer must be upgraded to accommodate the additional electrical load, and; 
 
WHEREAS, the existing transformer must also be relocated from its current location due 
to tree roots impacting the existing transformer, and; 
 
WHEREAS, MVEA is the electric utility provider serving the Public Works facility and 
is responsible for the upgrade and relocation of the transformer and associated utility, and; 
 
WHEREAS, the Town’s Procurement Policy exempts sole-source purchases, including 
utility services that can only be provided by one supplier or source, from the competitive bidding 
requirements, and; 
 
WHEREAS, funding for the proposed work is available within the approved Town budget. 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE 
TOWN OF MONUMENT, COLORADO THAT: 
 
The Town Council hereby approves a contract with MVEA to upgrade the Public Works shop 
transformer. The total not to exceed amount of this contract is $29,894.00 which will be funded 
by the Facilities Capital Projects fund. 
 
 PASSED AND RESOLVED by the Town Council of the Town of Monument, El Paso 
County, Colorado, this 16th day of November 2026 by a vote of ___ for and ____ against.

SUBJECT: Resolution No. 84.2026 - A Resolution Authorizing the Purchase and 
Implementation of the OpenGov Public Service Platform as a Coordinated 
Town-Wide Enterprise Software System Through an Omnia Cooperative 
Purchasing Agreement with an Initial Investment Not to Exceed $388,592.00. 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY. 
Staff is requesting Town Council authorization for the purchase and implementation of the 
OpenGov Public Service Platform through a Cooperative Purchasing Agreement. This 
investment would establish a coordinated, Town-wide enterprise software system to 
replace or consolidate disconnected departmental tools, reduce reliance on manual 
workarounds, and add additional functionality not part of existing software. The proposed 
scope encompasses financial management and budgeting; payroll and human resources; 
utility billing; grants; permitting and licensing, including code enforcement, business 
licensing, and public works permits; procurement and contract management; capital 
budgeting and project management; and continued enterprise asset management support. 
The pricing and scope documents establish a not-to-exceed authorization of $388,592.00 
for the initial investment. 
The recommendation is driven by three objectives: greater transparency, improved 
operational efficiency, and stronger cross-departmental coordination. The purpose is to 
strengthen the capacity of the Town’s existing workforce, not reduce staffing. By reducing 
time spent navigating system limitations and duplicating administrative work, the platform 
is intended to allow employees, many of whom serve in multiple roles, to devote more 
attention to technical work, customer service, financial oversight, and other core municipal 
responsibilities. Improved access to key operational metrics would also support informed, 
data-driven decisions by elected officials. 
DISCUSSION. 
Why is Staff Recommending This Investment. Why Do We Need New Software. 
The Town’s current software supports essential operations but consists largely of separate 
departmental systems. Staff rely on spreadsheets, email, duplicate data entry, manually

prepared reports, separate portals, and informal tracking methods to connect information 
across these systems. Many operational functions also remain manual. This fragmented 
environment makes it difficult to access a complete, consolidated history of projects, 
permits, licenses, contracts, payments, capital projects, customer accounts, and financial 
transactions. 
The broader goal is to strengthen organizational capacity. Better access to current 
information will help Town Council and management evaluate operations with greater 
confidence, allow departments to coordinate without recreating information, and give 
residents, businesses, applicants, and contractors clearer status updates and more 
consistent communication. 
Staff discussions confirmed that system limitations and software needs affect daily 
operations across all departments, with some of the following recurring challenges: 
• Financial Management and Budgeting: Recurring Caselle interruptions, crashes, and 
manual processes that affect payroll, accounts payable, reporting, and utility billing. 
• HRIS and Payroll: Payroll and timekeeping information is maintained across Caselle, 
Caselle Timekeeping, and BambooHR, requiring staff to manage and reconcile 
separate records. 
• Utility Billing: Caselle interruptions and manual billing processes create 
administrative work and complicate consistent access to customer-account 
information. 
• Grants Management: Current tracking and reporting rely on manual processes, 
creating a need for coordinated pre-award, post-award, compliance, and closeout 
records. 
• Permitting, Land Development, and Licensing: Staff must search separate records, 
manually verify submittals, and assemble reports from multiple sources. Routine 
configuration changes often require vendor assistance and are often found to be 
unsupported. 
• Business Licensing: The current payment processor, Express Bill Pay, will no longer 
be supported by Caselle. Current payments require manual updates because they 
are not reported in real time. 
• Code Enforcement: Staff needs coordinated tracking of complaints, inspections, 
photos, notices, deadlines, reinspections, and case closure, linked to related 
property and permit records typically found in the planning software. 
• Public Works and ROW Permitting: Staff need consistent tracking of right-of-way 
applications and inspections, with coordinated engineering review, inspection, and 
visibility into related development and utility work.

• Procurement and Contract Management: Processes are distributed across 
departments, with limited centralized tracking of solicitation documents, 
evaluations, contract terms, renewals, and expiration dates. 
• Capital Budget and Project Management: Staff need a more consistent connection

OpenGov is expected to reduce these administrative barriers through connected records,

The Town’s current software provides capabilities consistent with their cost and the

platform reflects evolving operational needs. Meeting those broader needs will require a 
corresponding investment in functionality, flexibility, and coordination across departments. 
Evaluation of Alternatives and Procurement Process. 
The recommendation before Council reflects a deliberate evaluation of the Town’s 
operational needs and available software alternatives. The project began as an effort to 
replace the Town’s permitting and land-development software. Staff documented 
requirements, developed an RFP framework and scoring criteria, and evaluated functional 
fit, configurability, data migration, integrations, reporting, implementation support, cost, 
and long-term scalability. 
As other departments participated, the evaluation identified needs extending beyond 
Planning. Finance, Utility Billing, Licensing, Grants, Public Works, Human Resources, 
Procurement, Contract Administration, and Capital Project Management depend on related 
information but currently operate across separate systems and processes. These 
discussions broadened the focus from a departmental replacement to a coordinated 
enterprise solution. 
Staff participated in nine vendor demonstrations and follow-up discussions including 
Accela, CitizenServe, Clarity, Tyler Technologies (EnerGov), Granicus (SmartGov), 
CivicReview, CloudPermit, GoGov, and OpenGov. Many alternatives offered capabilities 
comparable to the existing platform, lacked functionality needed to address current 
operational gaps, or did not provide the scalability sought to support Monument’s growth 
and goal of a unified platform. The most significant deficiencies found in other systems 
evaluated included: 
• Having no budgeting, accounting, utility billing or financial component

11/16/2026 
• Only having modules for Permitting, Licensing, and Code Enforcement 
• Lack of configurability   
• Significant ongoing professional support fees 
• Tiered pricing based on users, or applications submitted 
Through vendor demonstrations and follow-up discussions, staff identified OpenGov as the 
preferred alternative because it offered a stronger fit with the Town’s current operational 
requirements and long-term, cross-departmental needs. Its proposed scope extends well 
beyond permitting and licensing and its capabilities are expected to reduce duplicate data 
entry, manual tracking, and routine reliance on vendor assistance while improving 
coordination and access to information. The proposal’s inclusion of unlimited users, 
ongoing maintenance and support, and future software enhancements further supports the 
Town’s objective of adopting a scalable system that can accommodate growth and evolving 
service needs. 
After identifying OpenGov as the preferred platform, staff continued developing the RFP for 
a formal solicitation. During final preparation, OpenGov notified the Town of an available 
cooperative purchasing agreement under an existing awarded public contract. Staff 
evaluated this option as an alternative procurement method, separate from the assessment 
of the platform’s suitability for the Town’s needs. 
Before advancing the cooperative purchase, staff held an organization-wide software 
demonstration and countless subsequent departmental sessions to examine the platform 
against Monument’s specific workflows and requirements. These sessions reinforced broad 
departmental support and staff’s assessment that OpenGov could address the needs 
identified during the evaluation. The Town’s existing use of OpenGov Enterprise Asset 
Management in Public Works provided additional organizational familiarity and supported 
the opportunity for a more connected operating environment. 
Given the evaluation results and cross-departmental support, staff determined that 
pursuing the cooperative purchasing pathway was the most practical approach. Using an 
existing awarded contract avoids duplicating a solicitation and reduces the associated 
administrative workload, while retaining the Town’s responsibility to review the proposed 
purchase independently. 
The proposed purchase and contract have undergone review by the Town Attorney and IT 
Director, addressing scope, pricing, legal terms, cybersecurity, records, accessibility, 
implementation risk, and operational fit.  
The request before Council therefore follows needs assessment, evaluation of multiple 
alternatives, cross-departmental demonstrations, and review of the procurement 
approach. This significant Town-wide investment is recommended on the basis of a 
structured evaluation, rather than vendor preference or a single demonstration.

Why OpenGov. What Does OpenGov Provide. 
OpenGov offers a cloud-based integrated public service platform rather than a collection of 
unrelated departmental applications. The final scope is expected to include Financial 
Management and Budgeting; Human Capital Management, HRIS and Payroll; Utility Billing; 
Grants Management; Permitting and Licensing for Planning and Zoning, Code Enforcement, 
Business Licensing, and Public Works; Procurement and Contract Management; Capital 
Budget and Project Management; and Bluebeam integrations; and continued Enterprise 
Asset Management services. 
The platform’s primary advantage is the connection between these functions. A financial 
transaction can be related to a budget, contract, purchase, asset, grant, project, permit, fee, 
or utility account without requiring staff to rebuild the relationship in separate spreadsheets. 
Authorized staff can use shared dashboards and standardized reports, while role-based 
permissions preserve appropriate access and internal controls. Residents and applicants 
can use a more consistent public-facing environment for applicable submissions, 
payments, documents, notifications, and status information. 
The decision also reflects scalability and staff control as Monument grows. Configurable

The transition will be sequenced according to cross-departmental functional dependencies

existing software. 
• HRIS and Payroll: Automate payroll tax and financial processes and provide 
employee self-service access to pay and tax records. 
• Utility Billing: Coordinate meter and billing information while allowing customers to 
view usage and pay bills online. 
• Grants Management: Centralize pre-award, post-award, compliance, and closeout 
activities to strengthen grant tracking and reporting and eliminate current manual 
processes. 
• Land Development: Improve review routing, connected application records, and 
applicant status updates while reducing manual tracking.

This investment goes beyond replacing or upgrading existing systems by adding capabilities 
the Town does not currently have. Functions now managed manually, such as grants 
management and procurement, could be supported by centralized digital records and 
dashboards. A GIS-linked right-of-way permitting function would also improve visibility into 
the location of work and could be configured to support related public announcements for 
residents. 
Together, these added net new capabilities and connected records would create a more 
consistent, transparent operating environment across departments. Shared dashboards 
would improve workload visibility, reduce duplicate tracking, and provide management and 
Town Council with more reliable operational information. The investment is therefore 
intended to strengthen the Town’s capacity to deliver coordinated, accountable services as 
community needs evolve. 
Implementation. 
Implementation will be a coordinated transition rather than an immediate system 
replacement. OpenGov and departmental leads will sequence configuration and launches 
based on cross-departmental dependencies, staff capacity, and operational readiness 
while maintaining service continuity. 
Resolution No. 84-2026 – OpenGov Public Service Platform page 7 
11/16/2026 
OpenGov’s implementation methodology includes initiation, validation, configuration, 
training, testing, launch, and stabilization. The Town has designated an overall contract lead 
(Jenna Gorney, Senior Planner) and project leads that are subject-matter experts in their 
departments with authority to make timely decisions. Each workstream will confirm 
business rules, workflows, forms, fees, permissions, integrations, reports, data sources, 
and success measures before configuration. Staff will validate migrated records and 
complete user-acceptance testing and role-based training before go-live. 
The existing scope estimates roughly 12 months for full integration with the Finance systems 
and Permitting and Licensing being created first. 
Staff anticipates approximately two to three hours per week of implementation participation 
by each active departmental team or team lead, together with a weekly one-hour project 
status meeting. OpenGov is expected to perform roughly 80% of the system configuration, 
while Town staff validates requirements, records, workflows, and results. 
Legacy systems will remain available during migration, testing, reconciliation, and 
stabilization. Retirement of these legacy systems will occur on a rolling basis throughout 
2027 as modules go live and those systems will only be retired after the corresponding 
OpenGov module is operational and validated. 
While implementation temporarily requires staff to work in both systems, this controlled 
overlap provides uninterrupted service. 
Implementation will prioritize data integrity, service continuity, and each module’s 
operational readiness before launch. Services are expected to begin within two to four 
weeks after contract execution and the final sequence will be established during project 
planning.
