2026 Quarter 3 Financial Report:
The information in this report reflects the financial highlights for the Town of Monument along with a
prior two-year comparison for the similar period. This report will be posted on the Town of Monument
webpage at https://www.townofmonument.org/271/Finance-Department.
2026 Highlights by Fund:
General Fund
Total sales tax collections in 2026 increased by 10% over 2025 YTD. This increase is partially due
to the change in 2025 to self-collection of sales tax. As time continues through the years the Town
will see a more accurate reflection of year-over-year comparisons. The Town has seen a 2%
increase from 2024 Quarter 3 total sales tax actuals. It is important to note that the Town of
Monument’s sales tax is split with Triview Metropolitan District monthly based on the location of
purchase. Sales Tax in 2026 was distributed with 45% going to the Town’s General Fund, 14% to
Public Safety Police Fund, 22% to Water & Infrastructure, and the remaining 19% going to Triview
Metropolitan District. The Town continues to evaluate the tax revenue in 2026. It will be reflected
in the 2027 proposed budget presented on November 2nd and the final budget presented
December 7th of 2026, in addition to the economic and long-range forecasts used to present
budget information.   
Revenues for the Town of Monument General Fund for Quarter 3 have come in at 72% of budgeted
amounts. A significant contributor in the increased revenue for 2026 has been the increased
recovery of engineering review and legal fees related to planning projects.
With Monument working to reduce expenditures in the 2026 budget, the Town has seen higher
revenues over expenditures in Quarter 3 and is continuing to build the required reserves to
maintain Town operations. 
Water Enterprise Fund
The Water Enterprise Fund has increased water rates for all end users and implemented an
administrative billing fee. The water rate increase starts after the first 3,000 gallons used. The
Town has contracted with a third party to develop a Water Master Plan to identify improvements
and evaluate the future water system.
2F Police Safety Fund
Notable accounting changes in 2025 continued in 2026. In prior years, the 2F Public Safety Fund
and Law Enforcement were treated as one account; in the 2025 budget they are separated and the
money is accounted for correctly. This change caused the General Fund to encompass ~66% of all
Law Enforcement expenditures to comply with Ordinance and Resolution passed in 2022.
Additionally, the revenue that is generated by extra duty or school resource officers goes into the
General Fund as well.







Water impact and meter impact fees have been moved to a separate fund to account for impact fee
revenue and interest.

I. BACKGROUND 
On December 23, 2025, the Town ordered a solar-powered cellular telemetry station to provide 
continuous pressure monitoring within the Willow Springs subdivision. In early 2026, a second station 
was ordered to provide similar monitoring within the Wagons West subdivision. These areas are 
separated from the primary SCADA pressure-monitoring network by pressure-reducing valve (PRV) 
stations. Installation of these pressure transducers will provide the Water Department with 24/7 
pressure monitoring in both areas and expand the Department’s ability to monitor system conditions 
through the SCADA network. 
II. DISCUSSION 
Delivery of the telemetry stations occurred over an extended period, with the second station arriving 
in September 2026. The associated invoices, totaling $49,318, are now due for payment. Because the 
total exceeds the Town Manager’s approval authority, Council authorization is required before 
payment may be issued. 

Page 2 
 
 
 
Available records do not reflect completion of a competitive procurement process or a documented 
sole-source determination for these purchases. The purchases were initiated under a former 
department director who is no longer employed by the Town, and staff have been unable to locate 
documentation establishing the basis for using Alpine Civil Controls without competition. Accordingly, 
staff cannot independently verify the original procurement rationale and is presenting the matter to 
Council with that limitation disclosed. 
III. Invoices 
Staff has submitted Invoices for Council, authorizing payment of the invoices totaling $49,318. 
Approval will allow the Town to complete installation of the telemetry stations and incorporate these 
additional pressure-monitoring points into the existing SCADA network.
