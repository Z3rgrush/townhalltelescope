Staff Presentation 
Dan Ungerleider, Planning Director, introduced the draft Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan Update and 
accompanying Downtown Master Plan. He explained that the project represented nearly two years of public 
outreach, stakeholder engagement, technical analysis, policy development, and community visioning intended 
to guide the future of Monument over the coming decades. Hundreds of residents, business owners, 
stakeholders, and agency partners participated throughout the planning process. 
Representatives of the Lakota Group, led by Andy Cross, presented the final draft documents and summarized 
the planning process. The presentation included discussion of: 
• Existing conditions analysis and community visioning efforts. 
• Public participation activities, surveys, workshops, and focus groups. 
• Future Land Use planning recommendations. 
• Transportation and mobility strategies. 
• Infrastructure coordination and growth management. 
• Economic development initiatives. 
• Housing and neighborhood planning. 
• Parks, open space, and recreation objectives. 
• Downtown revitalization concepts. 
• Implementation strategies and performance measures.  
Consultants described six primary community pillars intended to guide future policy decisions: 
1. Positive Growth 
2. Connected Mobility 
3. A Booming Local Economy 
4. Resilient Neighborhoods 
5. Healthy Natural Environment 
6. Collaborative Stewardship 
The Downtown Master Plan portion of the presentation focused on downtown vibrancy, economic development 
opportunities, gateway improvements, redevelopment opportunity sites, enhanced connectivity, and future 
implementation priorities. Consultants emphasized that conceptual illustrations contained within the document 
were intended as visioning tools and not approved development proposals.

On September 9, 2026, the Planning Commission opened a public hearing in consideration 
of the Monument 2040 Plan, a proposed update to the Town’s Comprehensive Plan in 
accordance with Colorado State Planning Law, through Title 31 of the Colorado Revised 
Statutes (C.R.S.). The last update occurred in 2017. 
The process of drafting the updated Comprehensive Plan (also known as the Master Plan) 
began in early 2025 and was funded by a Department of Local AƯairs grant. Under the terms 
of the grant, adoption of the Plan must be completed by December 31, 2026. 
During the September 9, 2026, public hearing, Town staƯ and members of the Town’s 
planning consultant team presented a brief history of the document-development process, 
the ﬁndings documented in the Existing Conditions Report, and the components of the draft 
Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan (the “draft Plan”). Those components include the 
Downtown Master Plan, a Housing Action Plan, a Recreation and Tourism Plan, a Strategic 
Growth element, a reference to the Town’s annually adopted Three-Mile Plan, and a water-
supply element. The components shown in bold are required by state statute. Copies of the 
September 9, 2026, StaƯ Memo and Planning Commission meeting minutes are included as 
Attachments A and B to this StaƯ Memo. 
After receiving public testimony (see the draft Planning Commission meeting minutes dated 
September 9, 2026), the Planning Commission unanimously agreed to continue the public 
hearing until October 14, 2026, to provide the Commission and the general public with 
additional time to review the draft plans. Since that time, the Planning Department has 
received a range of thoughtful feedback from Planning Commission members, stakeholders, 
and community members. These comments reﬂected both support for the draft Plan and 
requests for reﬁnements prior to adoption. A copy of the written comments is attached as 
Exhibit C. 
The following is a summary of those written comments and StaƯ’s ﬁndings in relation to 
those comments organized into eight common themes.

1. Preservation of Small-Town Character and Community Identity. The most 
consistent theme throughout public engagement was protecting Monument's small-
town character, historic downtown, mountain views, rural roots, and community 
identity. The draft Plan explicitly identiﬁes these qualities as the community's most 
valued assets. 
The draft Plan states that residents want Monument to remain distinct from larger 
urban communities and calls for growth that respects the community's rural roots, 
mountain views, and peaceful setting. It speciﬁcally references lower-to-mid density 
development, preservation of community character, and limiting the scale of future 
growth. 
The Downtown Master Plan repeatedly identiﬁes downtown as Monument's historic 
and cultural heart and places a strong emphasis on preserving downtown's 
pedestrian scale, historic character, local businesses, and unique identity. 
Speciﬁc implementation strategies include: 
 Strengthening Downtown Design Standards. 
 Creating historic architectural design guides. 
 Preserving historic signage and character-deﬁning elements. 
 Expanding heritage-related programming and events. 
 Incorporating local history into placemaking and branding eƯorts. 
The draft Plan therefore acknowledges that growth should occur in a manner that 
reinforces Monument's identity rather than erodes it. 
2. Downtown Development and Future Housing. Feedback included di Ưering 
perspectives regarding future residential development, density, and mixed-use 
redevelopment opportunities in and around downtown. Some comments supported 
additional housing and redevelopment to strengthen downtown vitality, while others 
urged greater caution regarding building scale, multifamily development, and long-
term impacts on community character. 
The plans address this issue in several ways. 
First, the redevelopment concepts are presented as illustrative planning exercises 
rather than approved projects or entitlements. StaƯ notes that adoption of the plans 
does not approve any speciﬁc development proposal or rezone any property. 
Second, both plans repeatedly acknowledge that development intensity, density, 
architecture, and design remain subjects for future discussion and reﬁnement.

For example: 
 The Comprehensive Plan recommends establishing future density standards 
because none currently exist. 
 The Downtown Master Plan recommends visual design standards and 
architectural guidance before signiﬁcant redevelopment occurs. 
 Future code amendments would require separate public review processes. 
The draft Plan also attempts to direct growth into existing developed areas rather than 
encouraging outward sprawl by: 
 Promoting inﬁll development. 
 Concentrating redevelopment opportunities in downtown and corridor areas. 
 Supporting mixed-use development near services and amenities. 
 Encouraging redevelopment of underutilized properties. 
The draft Plan identiﬁes policies and implementation steps to concentrate future 
growth where infrastructure and services already exist. 
3. Supporting Local Businesses While Protecting Local Character.  Commenters 
supported eƯorts to strengthen downtown businesses, attract visitors, and expand 
local amenities, while emphasizing that future economic development should 
prioritize locally oriented businesses and avoid over-commercialization that could 
diminish community identity. 
The draft Plan recognizes concerns regarding over-commercialization, chain 
businesses, and big-box development. 
Public engagement ﬁndings speciﬁcally note that residents prefer local businesses, 
restaurants, co Ưee shops, boutiques, and community -serving businesses rather 
than chain retail and large-scale commercial development. 
The draft Plan responds by: 
 Prioritizing support for locally-owned businesses. 
 Recruiting desired business types such as co Ưee shops, bakeries, 
restaurants, and specialty retail. 
 Supporting façade improvement programs. 
 Creating business retention initiatives. 
 Expanding downtown marketing. 
 Increasing visitation to downtown businesses through events and 
programming.

The draft Downtown Master Plan, a component of the draft Plan, repeatedly identiﬁes 
local business development as a cornerstone of downtown's future success. 
4. Infrastructure, Water, Utilities, and Fiscal Sustainability. Several comments 
highlighted the importance of aligning future growth with infrastructure capacity, 
long-term ﬁscal sustainability, water resource planning, transportation needs, and 
utility coordination. Feedback suggested that community goals and desired 
outcomes should remain central to future growth discussions. 
The draft Plan addresses this concern through its Positive Growth pillar, which 
emphasizes coordination between land use decisions, infrastructure planning, and 
ﬁscal sustainability. 
Several goals speciﬁcally address: 
 Impact fees. 
 Infrastructure funding. 
 Utility provider coordination. 
 Water supply planning. 
 Regional utility partnerships. 
 Stormwater planning. 
 Capital improvement planning. 
Importantly, the draft Plan states that infrastructure planning should be aligned with

needed infrastructure investments. 
The draft Plan also acknowledges: 
 Long-term water supply concerns. 
 Dependence on Denver Basin groundwater. 
 Northern Delivery System eƯorts. 
 Loop Water Authority planning. 
 Regional water coordination. 
While some commenters suggested a greater emphasis on water supply than 
stormwater management, the plan does contain extensive sections dedicated to

 Expanding trail connectivity.

 Supporting recreation-based tourism.

6. Transparency, Accountability, and Implementation. Several comments

7. Public Engagement and Community Participation. Some feedback questioned 
whether additional community outreach or broader participation may be beneﬁcial 
for certain topics, particularly those involving housing, growth, and long-term

The process of developing the Monument 2040 Plan included 20 focus groups, two 
community-wide meetings, 270 survey responses, 10 pop-up events, and 
approximately 3,100 visits to the Monument 2040 project website. Engagement Week

owners, institutions, public safety, seniors, youth, elected o Ưicials, metro districts,

Monument Conversations event used facilitated discussions on Downtown, housing

Importantly, engagement did not simply collect comments and set them aside. The

improved walkability, and connectivity, and appropriately designed additional

The resulting draft Plan reﬂects a range of community priorities, including

The primary policy questions raised by the feedback received following the September 9, 
2026, initial Planning Commission public hearing, were not whether these themes are 
addressed in the draft Plan. Rather, it was whether the speciﬁc recommendations,

Plan strike the right balance between preserving existing character and accommodating 
future change. 
The draft Plan reﬂects the themes identiﬁed through public feedback and recommends 
additional public involvement before major implementation decisions are made, including 
decisions regarding: 
 Development code updates. 
 Public Works site redevelopment. 
 Housing Action Plan development. 
 Design standards updates. 
 Park planning eƯorts. 
 Infrastructure planning initiatives.

PURPOSE AND REQUESTED ACTION 
StaƯ requests that the Planning Commission conduct a public hearing, receive public 
testimony, review the draft Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan Update and the draft 
Downtown Master Plan, as a component to the Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan, and 
recommend approval of both documents to the Town Council. The Commission’s 
recommendation would advance the plans for ﬁnal consideration and approval by the Town 
Council, consistent with the Town’s local review process and Colorado’s statutory 
framework for municipal comprehensive planning. 
BACKGROUND 
The Town initiated the Monument 2040 planning process to update its long-range policy 
framework and establish a coordinated vision for growth, housing, economic development, 
mobility, infrastructure, community character, parks and recreation, public facilities, 
environmental stewardship, and implementation. The e Ưort also produced the Town’s ﬁrst 
Downtown Master Plan, which provides more focused direction for preserving, enhancing, 
and investing in Monument’s historic downtown. 
The planning process began in early 2025 and was completed with assistance from The 
Lakota Group and an interdisciplinary consultant team. The draft plans reﬂect multiple 
rounds of engagement with residents, business and property owners, regional partners, 
Town staƯ, appointed oƯicials, and elected oƯicials. The Town released draft versions dated 
August 26, 2026, for community review and hosted an open house on September 9, 2026, 
earlier today. 
PLAN OVERVIEW 
The draft Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan Update, including its associated Downtown 
Master Plan, establishes the Town’s long-range vision, goals, policies, and implementation 
strategies for the next 10 to 15 years. It is intended to guide future land use decisions, public 
investment, regulatory updates, intergovernmental coordination, and other actions 
necessary to implement the community’s desired future. 
DRAFT MONUMENT 2040 COMPREHENSIVE PLAN AND DOWNTOWN MASTER PLAN page 2 
September 9, 2026 
 
 Land Use and Community Character: Directing compatible growth while preserving 
the qualities that distinguish Monument. 
 Housing: Supporting a range of housing choices that respond to community needs. 
 Economic Development: Strengthening the local economy and expanding 
opportunities for businesses and residents. 
 Mobility and Connectivity: Improving transportation choices, safety, and connections

 Infrastructure and Resilience: Coordinating growth with water, stormwater, utilities,

 Parks, Recreation, and Natural Resources: Protecting valued resources and

 Implementation: Establishing priorities, responsible parties, partnerships, and

The draft Downtown Master Plan complements the Comprehensive Plan by providing a 
place-speciﬁc strategy for downtown. Its recommendations address land use, development 
character, the public realm, access and circulation, parking, placemaking, economic 
vitality, historic identity, and phased implementation. 
PLANNING COMMISSION REVIEW 
Under C.R.S. § 31-23-206, a municipal planning commission is charged with making and 
adopting a master plan for the physical development of the municipality. The plan serves as 
an advisory guide for land development decisions, and the commission must conduct a 
noticed public hearing and consider oral and written public comments before ﬁnal adoption. 
StaƯ recommends that the Commission evaluate whether the draft plans: 
 Accurately reﬂect the community’s vision and the direction developed through the 
public engagement process. 
 Provide a coherent and internally consistent framework for future land use, growth, 
mobility, infrastructure, housing, economic development, and public investment 
decisions. 
 Preserve Monument’s community character while preparing for anticipated growth 
and changing needs. 
 Provide clear, practical, and appropriately prioritized implementation actions.

STAFF ANALYSIS 
StaƯ ﬁnds that the draft Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan Update and Downtown 
Master Plan establish a coordinated, forward-looking policy framework that responds to the 
issues identiﬁed through the planning process. Together, the plans translate the 
community’s vision into goals, policies, strategies, and implementation actions that can 
guide future Town decisions. 
The drafts appropriately recognize that adoption is the beginning of implementation rather 
than the conclusion of the Town’s planning work. Future actions—including regulatory 
amendments, capital investments, partnerships, and program development—will require 
separate review, prioritization, funding, and public processes as applicable. Adoption of the 
plans does not rezone property or approve a speciﬁc development application; instead, it 
would establish the policy basis for future decisions in land use, capital improvement 
planning, and consideration of private investment within the Town of Monument and withing 
its 3-Mile Growth Area.

Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan Update and Downtown Master Plan 
Staff Presentation 
Dan Ungerleider, Planning Director, introduced the draft Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan Update and 
accompanying Downtown Master Plan. He explained that the project represented nearly two years of public 
outreach, stakeholder engagement, technical analysis, policy development, and community visioning intended 
to guide the future of Monument over the coming decades. Hundreds of residents, business owners, 
stakeholders, and agency partners participated throughout the planning process. 
 
Representatives of the Lakota Group, led by Andy Cross, presented the final draft documents and summarized 
the planning process. The presentation included discussion of: 
• Existing conditions analysis and community visioning efforts. 
• Public participation activities, surveys, workshops, and focus groups. 
• Future Land Use planning recommendations. 
• Transportation and mobility strategies. 
• Infrastructure coordination and growth management. 
• Economic development initiatives. 
• Housing and neighborhood planning. 
• Parks, open space, and recreation objectives. 
• Downtown revitalization concepts. 
• Implementation strategies and performance measures.  
 
Consultants described six primary community pillars intended to guide future policy decisions: 
1. Positive Growth 
2. Connected Mobility 
3. A Booming Local Economy 
4. Resilient Neighborhoods 
5. Healthy Natural Environment 
6. Collaborative Stewardship 
 
The Downtown Master Plan portion of the presentation focused on downtown vibrancy, economic 
development opportunities, gateway improvements, redevelopment opportunity sites, enhanced 
connectivity, and future implementation priorities. Consultants emphasized that conceptual illustrations 
contained within the document were intended as visioning tools and not approved development proposals. 
 
Public Comment 
Jodie Bliss 
Ms. Bliss expressed concerns regarding conceptual redevelopment illustrations contained within the 
Downtown Master Plan. She stated that several renderings appeared inconsistent with Monument's historic 
small-town character and could encourage redevelopment patterns that diminish downtown's existing 
identity. She advocated for stronger protections related to architectural design, building scale, massing, 
historic preservation, signage, lighting, independent businesses, and view corridors. She requested additional 
refinement before adoption of the document.

Community Vision
In 2040, Monument is a sought-after community on the front range of the 
Rocky Mountains known for its vibrant and historic Downtown, network of trails 
connecting neighborhoods, and thriving commercial corridors. The Downtown is 
busy with year-round activities leveraging Monument Lake, community parks, and 
a culture of local businesses and regional events. The Town is a recognized leader 
in progressive planning and an active participant in local, regional, and statewide 
initiatives. There is a strong sense of shared identity throughout Monument with 
residents on the east and west sides of the Interstate feeling part of the town’s 
special history and place in the region. 
• Effective in providing essential public service to residents with ongoing investment in 
staff, equipment, and infrastructure.
• Livable with a variety of housing options that meet the needs of the community’s 
population, ranging from young families to senior citizens.
• Thriving and resilient with a local economy driven by support for local businesses, the 
downtown, and bustling commercial corridors.
• Connected with commitments to bicycle transportation and pedestrian safety 
throughout the community.

In 2040, the Town of Monument is a community that has grown thoughtfully 
and sustainability to meet the needs of both current and future residents. The 
Town guides development with a clear vision and deliberate purpose, balancing 
new growth with the preservation of the landscapes, small-town character, and 
community values that make Monument unique. Through strategic investment in 
infrastructure, public services, and civic resources, Monument continues to build 
the capacity needed to support a high quality of life, a resilient local economy, 
and long-term community prosperity.

ACTIVE TRANSPORTATION

Monument. 
Goal 3: Provide a safe, connected bicycle network that accommodates riders of all ages and skill levels.
Goal 4: Work with PPACG to determine Complete Streets Standards and transitional goals for Monument. 
TRANSPORTATION SYSTEM
Goal 5: Leverage transit services to expand mobility opportunities for residents and employees.
Goal 6: Ensure roadway improvements complement surrounding land uses.
Goal 7: Prioritize transportation improvements based on safety, mobility needs, and travel patterns in Monument.
Goal 8: Support emerging transportation technologies that enhance system efficiency.
CORRIDOR PLACEMAKING & IDENTITY

Goal 11: Revitalize the auto-oriented commercial district on State Highway 105 between 2nd Street and Beacon Lite Road.

PILLAR STATEMENT
3
In 2040, Monument has a robust local economy bolstered by growth across multiple sectors: 
retail and services, light industry, office, and more. Residents support local businesses while 
benefiting from a strong base of national retailers. Residents enjoy myriad opportunities for 
dining and entertainment, both downtown and along commercial corridors.

ECONOMIC GROWTH
