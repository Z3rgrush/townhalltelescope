M E M O R A N D U M 
 
TO: The Honorable Mayor and Town Council 
FROM: Patrick Regan, Police Chief 
DATE: October 19, 2026 
SUBJECT: Ordinance No. 25-2026 – Regulation of Certain Prohibited Motorized Devices
 
 
 
BACKGROUND: The Monument Police Department has received recurring concerns regarding electric dirt 
bikes, e-motos, off-highway vehicles, and similar motorized devices operated on streets, sidewalks, trails, 
parks, open space, and other Town property. Some devices are marketed or described as e-bikes but do 
not meet Colorado’s definitions for Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3 electrical- assisted bicycles. The proposed 
ordinance is intended to address unsafe or unlawful operation while expressly preserving lawful electrical-
assisted bicycle use.  
 
SUMMARY: Ordinance No. 25 -2026 amends Section 10.04.015 of the Monument Municipal Code. The 
ordinance distinguishes lawful electrical-assisted bicycles from prohibited motorized devices and provides 
a local enforcement framework for Town public property. The ordinance: 
 
• Preserves lawful Class 1, Class 2, and Class 3 electrical-assisted bicycle use under Colorado law.  
• Prohibits operation of specified motorized devices on public property unless otherwise authorized 
by law.  
• Establishes civil parent or legal guardian accountability when a minor’s unlawful operation is 
knowingly caused, authorized, permitted, or allowed.  
• Authorizes peace officers to use education, warnings, citations, impoundment, and other lawful 
enforcement options based on the circumstances.  
• Authorizes the Police Chief to establish procedures for impoundment, storage, release, notice, 
and fee recovery.  
• Provides a general fine range of $50 to $1,000 and a parental- accountability civil fine of up to 
$250.  
 
IMPLEMENTATION: MPD intends to use an education-first implementation model while preserving officer 
discretion. Supporting materials include a community response plan, a proposed Municipal Court fine 
schedule, a regional comparison, a State/local enforcement-gap summary, a classification guide, and the 
September 8 Council follow-up checklist.  
 
FISCAL IMPACT: No direct fiscal appropriation is requested as part of the ordinance. Towing and storage 
costs may apply when a prohibited motorized device is impounded, and the ordinance authorizes the 
Police Chief to establish procedures concerning fee recovery.  
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends approval of Ordinance No. 25-2026.

EXHIBIT A – Amendment to  
Section 10.04.015 of the Town’s Municipal Code  
 
The adopted Model Traffic Code is amended and/or modified as follows: 
A. Purpose. The purpose of this Section is to preserve lawful electrical-assisted 
bicycle use, regulate the operation of prohibited devices on Town public property, establish 
parent and/or legal guardian accountability, and authorize impoundment when necessary 
to protect public health, safety, and welfare. 
 
B. Definitions. As used in this Section: 
 
1. “Electrical-assisted bicycle” means a Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3 
electrical-assisted bicycle as defined by Colorado law.  
2. “Electric dirt bike” means an electric-powered vehicle designed primarily 
for off-road recreational use that does not qualify as an electrical-assisted 
bicycle.  
3. “Electric motorcycle” or “e-moto” means an electric-powered 
motorcycle, moped, or similar motorized device that does not qualify as 
an electrical-assisted bicycle.  
4. “Off-highway vehicle” shall have the meaning established by Colorado 
law.  
5. “Parent or legal guardian” means a parent, legal guardian, custodian, or 
other person having lawful custody or control of a minor under eighteen 
years of age.  
6. “Prohibited motorized device” means:  
a. An off-highway vehicle;  
b. An electric dirt bike;  
c. An electric motorcycle, e-moto, or moped operated in violation of 
Colorado law; or  
d. Any similar motorized recreational vehicle that does not qualify as 
an electrical-assisted bicycle.  
7. “Public property” means real property owned, operated, maintained, or 
controlled by the Town, including, but not limited to, Town streets, 
sidewalks, trails, parks, open space, public parking areas, and public 
facilities.  
 
C. Preservation of Lawful E-bike Use. Nothing in this Ordinance shall be 
construed to prohibit or restrict the lawful operation of Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3 
electrical-assisted bicycles as authorized by Colorado law. A device shall not be considered 
an electrical-assisted bicycle solely because it is marketed, labeled, advertised, sold, or 
described as an “e-bike.” Actual classification shall be determined by Colorado law and 
the characteristics of the device. 
 
D. Prohibited Operation. 
 
1. No person shall operate a prohibited motorized device on public property 
unless otherwise authorized by law. 
2. This Section shall not prohibit: 
a. The lawful operation of properly registered and insured street-
legal vehicles operated in compliance with Colorado law. 
b. Government, emergency service, utility, or maintenance 
operations. 
c. Operation on private property with the permission of the property 
owner. 
 
E. Parent Accountability. No parent or legal guardian shall knowingly cause, 
authorize, permit, or allow a minor under eighteen years of age to operate or ride a 
prohibited motorized device in violation of this Chapter. Knowledge may be established 
by direct or circumstantial evidence. Nothing in this Chapter requires a peace officer to 
issue a warning, citation, or take any particular enforcement action. Officers may use 
education, warning, citation, impoundment, or other lawful enforcement action based on 
the totality of the circumstances.  
 
F. Enforcement and Impoundment. This Section may be enforced by any 
peace officer authorized by law. A peace officer may impound a prohibited motorized 
device when probable cause exists to believe that one or more of the following 
circumstances exist:  
 
1. The device was operated in violation of this Section.  
2. The operator fled, eluded, or failed to stop.  
3. The device was involved in a crash, injury, property damage, reckless 
operation, or repeat violation.  
4. The device lacks registration, insurance, license plate, decal, or 
documentation required by Colorado law.  
5. The device was operated by a minor after prior warning.  
6. Impoundment is reasonably necessary to protect public safety or preserve 
evidence.  
The Police Chief may establish procedures governing impoundment, storage, release, 
notice, and fee recovery consistent with the Town Code and Colorado law.  
G. Penalties. 
 
1. Unless otherwise specified, violations of this Section shall be punishable 
by a fine of not less than $50.00 and not more than $1,000.00. The 
Municipal Court may establish a schedule of payable fines and mandatory 
court appearances based on prior violations, aggravating circumstances, 
and public-safety risk. 
2. Violation of this Section based on parental accountability shall constitute 
a municipal civil offense punishable by a fine of up to $250.00.  
3. Each violation shall constitute a separate offense. 
 
H. Relation to State Law. This Chapter supplements Colorado law and shall 
not be interpreted to do any of the following: 
 
1. Reclassify lawful electrical-assisted bicycles.  
2. Require registration, licensing, or insurance for lawful electrical-assisted 
bicycles where not otherwise required by Colorado law.  
3. Limit enforcement of applicable Colorado law.  
4. Create restrictions on lawful Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3 electrical-
assisted bicycles beyond those established by Colorado law.

TO:  The Mayor and Town Council 
FROM:  Laurie Young, Finance Manager;    
DATE:  October 19, 2026     
RE: Ordinance 27 -2026 Amending Title 3 Section 3.09 of  the Monument Municipal Code 
Regarding Computer Software 
 
 
STRATEGIC PRIORITIES (2026–2028) 
 Ensure Long -Term Financial Health and Sustainability  - Supports long- range financial health, 
forecasting and sustainability to ensure efficient and resilient municipal services. 
 
 
BACKGROUND. 
The Town of Monument Title 3, Section 3.09.060 currently allows for the sales of computer software 
that is (1) prepackaged for repeated sales or license, (2) is governed by a tear-open nonnegotiable license 
agreement, and (3) is delivered to the customer in a tangible medium, to be exempt from sales tax; 
 
DISCUSSION. 
 In conformance with Colorado HB26 -1223, the exemptions under Section 39 -26-713(3), C.R.S., were 
narrowed so that only custom software developed for use by a particular user and software governed 
by a negotiable license agreement is exempt from taxation, and all other types of downloadable software 
are subject to sales tax beginning January 1, 2027. 
 
RECOMMENDED ACTION. 
Amotion to adopt Ordinance 27 -2026, an Ordinance Amending Title 3 Section 3.09 of the Monument 
Municipal Code regarding computer software.

Chapter 3.09 SALES TAX 
3.09.010 Imposition of sales tax. 
Sales Tax Rate. There is imposed a sales tax of three and fifty-hundredths percent on the sale of tangible 
personal property, products, and taxable services within boundaries of the Town, as provided herein at Section 
3.09.050 of this Code.  
(Ord. No. 22-2024, § 3, 10-7-2024) 
3.09.020 Sales tax as a separate and distinct item. 
Except as provided in Section 3.09.030, retailers shall add the sales tax, or the average equivalent thereof, to 
the purchase price or charge of the item or service, and provide the customer with a receipt that shows the sales 
tax charged as a separate and distinct charge; and, when added, such sales tax shall constitute a part of such price 
or charge and shall be a debt from the consumer or user to the retailer until paid and shall be recoverable at law in 
the same manner as other debts.  
(Ord. No. 22-2024, § 3, 10-7-2024) 
3.09.030 Sales tax included in the selling price. 
Only retailers selling malt, vinous or spirituous liquors by the drink, vending machine sales and special event 
vendor sales may include the sales tax in the sales price or charge. No retailer shall advertise or hold out to the 
public in any manner, directly or indirectly, that sales tax is not included as a part of the sales price to the 
consumer. No retailer shall gain any benefit from the collection or payment of the sales tax, nor shall the use of 
rates set forth in Sections 3.09.010 and 3.09.020 of this Chapter release such retailer from liability for payment of 
the full amount of the sales tax levied by this Chapter.  
(Ord. No. 22-2024, § 3, 10-7-2024) 
3.09.040 Sales tax on credit sales. 
Whenever tangible personal property is sold, including that sold in conjunction under a conditional sales 
contract whereby the seller retains title as security for all or part of the purchase price, the total sales tax based on 
the total purchase price, including finance and insurance charges which are not separately stated at the time of 
sale, shall become immediately due and payable. No refund or credit shall be allowed to either party to a 
transaction in case of repossession by the vendor of collateral securing the purchase price or any part of the 
purchase price.

SUBJECT: Resolution No. 69-2026 – A Resolution Approving an Intergovernmental

STRATEGIC PRIORITIES (2026–2028) 
Enable Responsible Community Development – Establishes clear, accountable plans, 
processes, and procedures for responsible and enduring community development and 
growth. 
 
BACKGROUND: The Town of Monument and El Paso County have maintained a long-
standing cooperative partnership for snow removal and ice control operations along 
roadway segments where service efficiencies can be achieved through adjacent 
maintenance routes. This Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) formalizes the continuation 
of this cooperative arrangement and establishes the responsibilities of each agency for 
designated roadway segments within the other’s jurisdiction. 
 
DISCUSSION/ANALYSIS: Under this agreement, each party will provide snow removal and 
ice control services on specific roadway segments identified in Addendum A. The roadway 
assignments are intended to improve operation efficiency, reduce travel time between 
service routes, and provide timely snow and ice control services to the traveling public. 
Services will be performed in accordance with each agency’s adopted snow and ice 
control policies and procedures.  
The agreement has a term extending through September 1, 2033, unless terminated earlier 
by either party with thirty (30) days written notice. The agreement does not transfer 

ownership or jurisdiction of any roadway segment, and each agency retains responsibility 
for roadways within its jurisdiction. The agreement further includes standard provisions 
related to governmental immunity, non-appropriation funds, and independent 
governmental authority.  
Approval of the IGA will continue a cost-effective and efficient regional partnership that 
benefits both agencies and enhances winter roadway maintenance operations for 
residents and visitors throughout the Monument area. 
 
FINANCIAL IMPACT: The agreement results in no direct fiscal impact on the Town. Snow 
removal and ice control operations performed under the agreement will be completed 
using existing Public Works personnel, equipment, and operating budgets. The operational 
efficiencies gained through coordinated snow removal routes are expected to offset the 
limited additional maintenance responsibilities assumed under the agreement. 
 
RECOMMENDED ACTION: Staff recommend approval of the Intergovernmental Agreement 
between the Town of Monument and El Paso County for snow removal and Ice control

ON CONSTRUCTION AND BUILDING MATERIALS AND CONSTRUCTION EQUIPMENT 
 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Monument (the “Town”) is a home rule municipality duly organized and existing 
under Article XX of the Colorado Constitution and the Town’s Home Rule Charter approved by the electors on 
November 8, 2022 (“Charter”); and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Monument is committed to maintaining and improving the public 
infrastructure necessary to serve its residents, businesses, and community; and 
 
WHEREAS, investment in streets, roads, bridges, sidewalks, and stormwater infrastructure is important 
to the Town’s ability to maintain reliable public infrastructure and address current and future capital needs; 
and 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to Ordinance No. 19-2026, the Town Council referred to the Town’s eligible 
electors a ballot issue proposing to increase the Town's Use Tax on construction and building materials and 
construction equipment from 2.0% to 3.5%, which would align that Use Tax rate with the Town's existing 3.5% 
sales tax rate; and  
 
WHEREAS, under the ballot measure, the increased Use Tax would be collected on construction and 
building materials and construction equipment when a building permit or right-of-way permit is issued if the 
Town's 3.5% sales tax was not previously collected on those materials or equipment, subject to the 
exemptions established in Ordinance No. 19-2026 and Chapter 3.10 of the Monument Municipal Code; and 
 
WHEREAS, the ballot issue identifies an estimated annual tax increase of $2,272,500 beginning January

WHEREAS, all revenue generated by the increased Use Tax would be dedicated to capital projects, 
including streets, roads, bridges, sidewalks, and stormwater improvements; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town Council recognizes the importance of identifying resources to invest in the 
infrastructure that supports the community and accommodates Monument’s continued development and 
growth; and 
 
WHEREAS, aligning the Use Tax rate applicable to construction and building materials and construction 
equipment with the Town's existing retail sales tax rate provides a consistent tax rate of 3.5% for the 
transactions addressed by the ballot measure; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town Council believes that investing additional resources in transportation and 
stormwater capital improvements will help the Town address important infrastructure needs and support the 
long-term well-being of the Monument community; and 
 
 
 
WHEREAS, the Town Council further believes that the voters of Monument should determine whether 
the proposed dedicated funding for these capital improvements should be approved. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE TOWN OF MONUMENT, COLORADO 
THAT: 
 
Section 1.  Support for Ballot Issue. 
 The Town Council hereby expresses its support for the ballot issue referred to the electors pursuant to 
 Ordinance No. 19-2026 and encourages Monument voters to review the ballot language and available 
 information regarding the measure before casting their ballots at the November 3, 2026 Regular 
 Municipal Election. 
 
Section 2.  Purpose of Additional Revenue. 
 The Town Council supports the measure because the additional revenue, if approved by the voters, will 
 provide a dedicated source of funding for capital projects including streets, roads, bridges, sidewalks, 
 and stormwater improvements, as provided in the ballot issue.  
 
Section 3.  Commitment to Responsible Stewardship. 
 If the ballot issue is approved, the Town Council affirms its commitment to the responsible and  
 transparent use of the additional revenues for the purposes authorized by the voters and consistent 
 with applicable law. 
 
Section 4.  Voter Determination. 
 The Town Council recognizes that the ultimate decision regarding the proposed Use Tax increase rests 
 with the eligible electors of the Town of Monument and encourages residents to review the ballot 
 language and available information before casting their votes. 
 
Section 5.  Effective Date. 
 This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon its adoption.

On August 26, 2026, the Planning Department released the draft Monument 2040
Comprehensive Plan and Downtown Master Plan for community review at
www. townofmonument. org/ m2040. The draft plans were presented during an open house on
September 9 and later that day were the subject of a public hearing before the Planning
Commission.
At its September meeting, the Planning Commission conducted a public hearing on the draft
Monument 2040 Comprehensive Plan Update and Downtown Master Plan. 
Commissioners, staff, consultants, and members of the public reviewed the documents,
which are intended to guide long- term growth, development, infrastructure investment, and
economic vitality. Following public comment and deliberation, the Planning Commission
unanimously continued consideration of both plans to October 14, 2026, to allow additional
time for review and comment.

timelines across project types, refining submittal requirements, and improving the code’ s
organization, usability, and navigation.
