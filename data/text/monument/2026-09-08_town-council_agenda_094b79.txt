BACKGROUND: The Town of Monument Planning Commission is to consist of seven (7) 
regular members and two (2) alternate members, the terms of which are staggered. 
Alternate members are utilized to ensure a quorum at each meeting and are appointed to 
serve as regular members in the absence of one or two regular Commissioners. All members 
are appointed for a two-year term.

regular members and no alternate members. On August 3, 2026, the Planning Department 
received an application from resident Daniel Tucker to serve as an alternate member of the 
Planning Commission. Mr. Tucker’s application is attached for consideration by Town 
Council.

impressed by his experience in civil and water engineering, and his desire to serve on the 
Planning Commission and his community. His background and qualifications make him a 
strong candidate for service on the Planning Commission.

2.40.020 - Membership—Terms—Term limit—Vacancies. 
The planning commission shall consist of seven regular members and two alternates. The 
term of each planning commission member and alternates shall be two years, ending 
December 31 of the year immediately following the year of his or her appointment, or until 
his or her successor is appointed. Terms shall be staggered with three members being 
appointed during the same year for terms of two years, and four members being appointed 
the following year for terms of two years. Members shall serve no more than four 
consecutive terms. Terms are considered consecutive unless they are at least a term apart. 
Current members who have served in excess of four consecutive terms shall serve until their 
current term expires. 
Vacancies occurring otherwise than through the expiration of term shall be filled for the 
remainder of the unexpired term by appointment by the Board of Trustees.

Proclamation Recognizing “Creek Week” 2026 
 
 
WHEREAS, The Town of Monument is fortunate to have extensive and diverse natural resources, such as forests, 
grasslands, riparian areas, lakes, creeks, and a wide variety of open spaces; and 
WHEREAS, the Fountain Creek watershed, including Fountain Creek, related wetlands, existing trails, and 
recreational facilities, is a unique watershed that is an important resource and asset to the 
residents and visitors of Fountain and the Pikes Peak Region; and 
WHEREAS, the Fountain Creek Watershed District is partnering with the City of Colorado Springs, Colorado Springs 
Utilities, El Paso County, Pueblo County, the Cities of Manitou Springs, Fountain, and towns of 
Monument and Green Mountain Falls, along with numerous community organizations to coordinate the 
13th Annual “Creek Week” cleanup, which will encourage the protection, restoration, and maintenance 
of the Fountain Creek watershed; and 
WHEREAS, this nine-day litter clean-up effort kicks off in Fountain Creek Regional Park on September 26th and 
runs through October 4th, 2026 throughout the watershed; and 
WHEREAS, “Creek Week” programs and activities are designed to raise awareness about the littering issue within 
our watershed, to encourage organizations and individuals to collect litter and debris to make Fountain 
Creek and the watershed cleaner and safer; and 
WHEREAS, businesses, churches, schools, non-profits, neighborhood associations, youth groups, service clubs and 
individuals are encouraged to form a "Creek Crew” to clean up at a “Creek Week” project site; and 
WHEREAS, “Creek Week” litter removal activities will reduce pollution in our creeks and clogs in our waterways that 
can lead to flooding, provide for a safer drinking water supply, and enhance wildlife habitat and 
property values. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, the Councilmembers of Town of Monument, Colorado hereby proclaim September 26 
through October 4, 2026, as “Creek Week” in the Town of Monument and encourage our citizens to help 
protect, restore, and maintain our waterways by participating in “Creek Week” activities. 
 
PROCLAIMED THIS 8th day of September 2026.

The issue is not lawful e-bikes
Monument supports lawful e-bike use

electric dirt bikes, or off-highway vehicles.

WHERE THE ORDINANCE APPLIES
Applies Only to Town Public Property
Town public property includes property owned, operated, maintained, or controlled by the Town of Monument, 
including:
• Town streets and sidewalks
• Town trails
• Town parks
• Town-owned or Town-controlled open space
• Town public parking areas
• Town public facilities
What is Restricted?
Prohibited motorized devices may not be operated on Town 
public property unless otherwise authorized by law.
Examples may include:
• Off-highway vehicles
• Electric dirt bikes
• Unlawfully operated electric motorcycles or e-motos
• Unlawfully operated mopeds
• Similar motorized recreational vehicles that do not qualify as 
lawful e-bikes
8
Exceptions
The ordinance does not prohibit:
• Lawful Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3 e-bikes
• Properly registered, insured, street-legal vehicles 
operated lawfully
• Government, emergency, utility, or maintenance 
operations
• Operation on private property with the owner’s 
permission
HOA, developer, school, county, state, and other private property are not automatically covered. Current Colorado law and other applicable authority remain available.

PROPOSED ORDINANCE MEASURES
Additional local tools, not a replacement for current Colorado law
Public-Property Prohibition
Prohibited motorized devices may not be operated on 
Town-owned, Town-operated, Town-maintained, or 
Town-controlled property unless otherwise authorized by 
law.
Parent Accountability
A proportional $250 municipal civil option when a 
parent or legal guardian knowingly causes, 
authorizes, permits, or allows unlawful operation by 
a minor.
Impoundment and Discretion
Discretionary impoundment when one or more listed 
factors exists, including unlawful operation, failure to 
stop, crash involvement, reckless or repeat conduct, 
prior warning, public-safety necessity, or evidence 
preservation.
Clear rules. Proportional accountability. Consistent enforcement.

WHY PARENT ACCOUNTABILITY?
The goal is involvement and prevention, not automatic liability
1
Access
Parents often purchase, transport, store, 
charge, maintain, or control the device.
2
Knowledge
A parent or legal guardian must knowingly 
cause, authorize, permit, or allow a minor 
to operate or ride a prohibited motorized 
device in violation of the ordinance.
3
Proportionality
A municipal civil option provides a more 
measured response than relying only on more 
serious criminal alternatives.
4
Discretion
A parent citation is not automatic. Officers 
retain discretion based on the totality of 
circumstances.
HB26-1125 proposed statewide parent penalties for certain minor electric-vehicle violations, but did not become law.
