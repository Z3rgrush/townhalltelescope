5. Economic Development Update – Dan Ungerlieder, Planning Director 
Dan Ungerlieder highlighted the revenue budget and listed some of the different economic 
tools available, such as State of Colorado Economic Development tools (Enterprise Zone 
Program, Rural Economic Development Initiative, Block grants, CDBG, etc), Pikes Peak 
Council of Governments, Town of Monument Economic Development Committee, towards 
the effort of business attraction and retention. 
Dan also spoke about the Town’s Comp Plan, which will be presented at the 11/6/2026 
Town Council meeting, which is a blending of opinions on the changes to Monument.  The 
Comp Plan includes 7 chapters that will become Goals for the Town, and include details on 
how to achieve each goal, with actions, time frames, who is responsible and 
measurements.  Dan also mentioned that in 2027 that Town will have a Downtown Master 
Plan. 
To John Lewis’s question about new businesses, Madeline and Dan Ungerlieder listed off 
ACCO (135 employee manufacturing facility), Target, HomeGoods and TJ Maxs, Chick-fil-a, 
Lost Island, and other retail. 
 
 
Dan DeBoo asked what roadblocks would stand in the way of the Comp Plan’s approval, to 
which Ungerleider answered; new participants who won’t know all the background and the 
need to re-educate, but Ungerleider is optimistic about the Plan’s approval.  Once the Plan 
is approved there will be a need to make sure the plan is put into use and not put on a shelf. 
Scott Gilson liked the idea of the Plan including a list of action items. 
Ungerleider added that having a plan in place, such as the Comp Plan, gives the Town a 
greater chance of being awarded grants. 
Desiree questioned if the Plan is prioritized and phased, to which Ungerleider answered 
Yes.  He went on to explain that the Plan includes a timeline, although it will ultimately be 
up to Town Council decisions and budget limitations. 
Ungerleider’s closing remarks about the Comp Plan is it presents the time and opportunity 
for people to get involved. 
 
6. Review 2027 Draft Proposed Budget – Jennifer Phillips, Finance Director 
Jennifer presented a high-level overview of the General Fund 2027 draft budget, which 
showed total proposed revenue of $16.31M and total proposed expenditures of $16.2M, 
plus total Town Wide revenue of $34.61M and total Town Wide expenditures of $44.19M 
and explained that reserve spending is allowable and appropriate.  Steve Murray also 
presented the Long Range Financial Forecast, which lead to conversation about revenue 
assumptions, sales growth, zoning and the Triview sales tax split. 
Scott asked if there is land in Monument left to develop, to which Dan Ungerleiger 
answered yes, and listed off the examples of 2nd Street, and Conexus, and that Hwy 105 is 
begging for improvement (which would require action by CDOT).   And when Scott asked 
about what the most important development is, roof tops vs Commercial, Dan answered 
that both are important, and that the Town needs places for employees to live and the 
attraction of new business.  Scott also asked if the Town is zoned correctly, to which Dan 
answered it’s ambiguous and challenging, and that the Comp Plan explains how to 
restructure zooming and there’s a need to work with property owners to have the right uses. 
Johan Moum asked some revenue questions: regarding General Fund “Other Revenue” the 
answer was the obligation of ARPA funds in 2025 and grant revenue in 2026, regarding 
General Fund interest changes, the answer was due to the appropriate allocation of 
interest to the different funds rather than all going to the General Fund, and regarding the 
uses for Use Tax the answer was Capital Projects.  
When Scott Gilson asked if any layoffs like in 2025 were being considered, the answer was 
no. 
Greg Bland complimented the Finance Team, with appreciation of quick action to budget 
changes and feels we are looking out for the Town’s best interests. 
Recommendations by Committee: 
Desiree Mings: feels that police don’t issue enough tickets at the lake and considered that 
to be lost revenue to the Town.  Madeline VanDenHoek’s response was to invite Desiree to 
talk directly with the Police Chief. 
John Lewis: regarding the new for additional Police Officers, recommends that in late 2027, 
when the Legacy Project is completed, the Town evaluate the staffing needs. 
Laura Kronick shared the Palmer Lake Kiosk Parking Report and asked about the budgeted 
amount for Monument Lake parking in 2027.  Jennifer answered that $40K is the current 
budget amount but could be restated to increase the amount in June. 
Johan suggested that rather than doing only the 2 north bound lanes of Jackson Creek 
Parkway, wait and do all 4 lanes at one time.  Madeline explained that the Town would lose 
 
 
grant funding if the Town waited, and that costs keep increasing over time, and additionally 
there are Storm Water needs that require attention now.
