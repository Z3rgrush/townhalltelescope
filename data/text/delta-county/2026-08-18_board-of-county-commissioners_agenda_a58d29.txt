RESOLUTION NO. 2026-R-_______ 
 
A RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF DELTA 
COUNTY, COLORADO, RELINQUISHING AND REVERTING ITS 2026 PRIVATE 
ACTIVITY BOND ALLOCATION TO THE STATEWIDE BALANCE OF THE 
COLORADO DEPARTMENT OF LOCAL AFFAIRS 
 
WHEREAS, Delta County, Colorado (the "County") is a legally established county of the State 
of Colorado; and 
 
WHEREAS, Delta County is authorized and empowered under the laws of the State of 
Colorado (the "State") to issue revenue bonds for the purpose of financing qualified residential 
rental projects for low- and moderate-income persons and families; and 
 
WHEREAS, Delta County is authorized and empowered under the laws of the State of 
Colorado (the "State") to issue revenue bonds for the purpose of providing single-family 
mortgage loans to low- and moderate-income persons and families; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (the "Code"), restricts the 
amount of tax-exempt bonds ("Private Activity Bonds") which may be issued in the State to 
provide such mortgage loans and for certain other purposes; and 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to the Code, the Colorado legislature adopted the Colorado Private 
Activity Bond Ceiling Allocation Act, Part 17 of Article 32 of Title 24, Colorado Revised 
Statutes (the "Allocation Act"), providing for the allocation of the State Ceiling among the 
governmental units in the State; and 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to an allocation under Section 24-32-1706 of the Allocation Act, 
Delta County has an allocation of the 2026 State Ceiling for the issuance of a specified principal 
amount of Private Activity Bonds prior to September 15, 2026 (the "2026 Allocation"); and 
 
WHEREAS, for the calendar year 2026, the County was notified by the State of Colorado that its 
direct PAB allocation is $2,200,348.00; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Act provides that a local government may allocate its private activity bond 
ceiling to projects within its jurisdiction, assign its allocation to another issuing authority, elect to 
carry forward the allocation for specified purposes, or relinquish its allocation to the Statewide 
Balance; and 
 
Page 9 of 57
Page 2 of 2 
 
WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners has determined that there are currently no 
eligible local projects ready to utilize the 2026 Allocation prior to the statutory deadline, and that 
electing a carryforward is not practical for the County at this time; and 
 
WHEREAS, to ensure that this tax-exempt bonding authority does not expire and is instead 
made available to benefit other affordable housing or qualifying economic development projects 
within the State of Colorado, the Board of County Commissioners desires to relinquish and 
revert its entire 2026 Allocation to the Statewide Balance maintained by the Department of Local 
Affairs ("DOLA"). 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF COUNTY 
COMMISSIONERS OF DELTA COUNTY, COLORADO, THAT: 
 
Section 1. The County hereby relinquishes and reverts its entire 2026 Private Activity Bond 
Allocation in the amount of $2,200,348.00 to the Statewide Balance of the Colorado Department 
of Local Affairs. 
 
Section 2. The County explicitly declines to issue bonds against, assign, or elect a carryforward 
for this specific allocation amount for the calendar year 2026. 
 
Section 3. The Chair of the Board of County Commissioners, the County Administrator, or their 
designated representative is hereby authorized and directed to execute any required state forms, 
notifications, or certifications to formally communicate this relinquishment to DOLA in 
compliance with the Act. 
 
Section 4. This Resolution shall take effect immediately upon its adoption. 
 
 
ADOPTED this 18th day of August, 2026.

SETTLEMENT AND RELEASE AGREEMENT 
 
This Settlement and Release Agreement ("Agreement") is made and entered into this ____ day of 
__________, 2026, by and between Delta County, Colorado, acting by and through its Board of 
County Commissioners ("County"), and Benson Brothers Truck & Equipment LLC* (see 
note in Paragraph 9) (referred to as "Benson"). The County and Benson may be referred to 
collectively herein as the "Parties." 
 
 RECITALS  
 
WHEREAS, a Treasurers Deed dated the 4th day of November, 1944, conveying the real estate 
involved in this Agreement, was delivered to the County, and recorded at Book 284, Page 352 of 
the records of the County Clerk and Recorder.   
WHEREAS, a Deed from County dated April 11, 1947, conveying the real estate involved in 
this Agreement, was made to S.A. Lewis with the County reserving onto the County all sand and 
gravel located on said property and the right to remove same from said lands, and recorded at 
Book 295, Page 137 of the records of the County Clerk and Recorder.   
WHEREAS, the County remains the owner of the sand and gravel located in the SW1/4 NW1/4 
of Section 3, Township 15 South, Range 95 West, 6th P.M.   
WHEREAS, Benson Brothers Truck & Equipment LLC is the current owner of the surface 
estate of the real estate located in SW1/4 NW1/4 of Section 3, Township 15 South, Range 95 
West, 6th P.M having a deed dated December 28, 2017, and recorded at Reception No. 700485 
of the records of the County Clerk and Recorder.   
WHEREAS, discussions have arisen between the Parties regarding operations, rights, and 
obligations associated with the Pig Mesa Gravel Pit, located in the SW1/4 NW1/4 of Section 3, 
Township 15 South, Range 95 West, 6th P.M.; and 
WHEREAS, the Parties previously engaged in a verbal agreement on October 26, 2005, which 
was subsequently memorialized in a letter dated October 31, 2005, regarding the operations, 
rights, and obligations associated with the Pig Mesa Gravel Pit; and 
WHEREAS, the Parties now mutually desire to fully and finally resolve, settle, and compromise 
any and all claims, disputes, and obligations arising out of or related to the Pig Mesa Gravel Pit 
and all prior agreements, including the aforementioned October 2005 agreements, without any 
admission of liability or wrongdoing by either Party.

TERMS AND CONDITIONS 
1. Transfer of Sand and Gravel Rights. 
Within fourteen (14) days of the mutual execution of this Agreement, the County agrees to 
convey, by quit claim deed, all of the County's right, title, and interest in and to the sand and 
gravel rights located on the real property described in Exhibit A (attached hereto and 
incorporated herein by reference) exclusively to Benson Brothers Truck & Equipment LLC.   
2. Comprehensive Waiver and Release by Benson. 
In consideration of the County’s conveyance of the sand and gravel rights as described in Section 
1, Benson, on behalf of itself, its owners, members, managers, agents, successors, and assigns, 
hereby absolutely, unconditionally, and irrevocably waives, releases, and forever discharges the 
County, its current and former commissioners, officers, employees, agents, and representatives 
from any and all claims, demands, rights, causes of action, liabilities, and damages of any kind or 
nature whatsoever, whether known or unknown, anticipated or unanticipated, arising out of or in 
any way related to the Pig Mesa Gravel Pit and the property located in SW1/4 NW1/4 of Section 
3, Township 15 South, Range 95 West, 6th P.M.   
3. Integration and Entire Agreement. 
This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties and encompasses and 
supersedes all prior and contemporaneous agreements, representations, and understandings 
between the Parties regarding the Pig Mesa Gravel Pit, specifically including, but not limited to, 
the verbal agreement of October 26, 2005, and the memorializing letter dated October 31, 2005. 
The written terms of this Agreement shall exclusively govern the Parties' rights and obligations. 
The Parties explicitly acknowledge and agree that they are not relying on any prior agreements, 
verbal statements, promises, or representations not specifically set forth in this writing. 
4. Colorado Governmental Immunity Act. 
No Waiver of Immunity. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to waive, limit, or 
otherwise modify, in whole or in part, any governmental immunity that may be available by law 
to Delta County, its officials, employees, contractors, or agents, under the Colorado

This Agreement shall be governed by, construed, and enforced in accordance with the laws of 
the State of Colorado. Venue for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall lie

6. Severability. 
If any provision of this Agreement is held to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable by a court of 
competent jurisdiction, such invalidity, illegality, or unenforceability shall not affect the 
remaining provisions of this Agreement, which shall remain in full force and effect. 
7. Binding Effect. 
This Agreement shall be binding upon and inure to the benefit of the Parties hereto and their 
respective successors, assigns, heirs, and legal representatives. 
8. Knowing and Voluntary Execution. 
The Parties acknowledge that they have read this Agreement in its entirety, fully understand its 
terms, and have had the opportunity to consult with independent legal counsel of their own 
choosing prior to executing this Agreement. The Parties execute this Agreement voluntarily and 
free from any duress or undue influence. 
9.  Benson Businesses. 
By signing this Agreement Benson agrees and affirms their authority to bind all other previous 
and current variants of the “Benson” businesses involved with the Pig Mesa Pit, including but 
not limited to, Benson Brothers, Benson Brothers LLC, Benson Brothers Truck and Equipment, 
and Benson Brothers Aggregate.   
10. Authority to Sign.   
Each individual signing this Agreement on behalf of a party warrants that they have the full 
authority to execute this Agreement and bind that party. 
11. Counterparts. 
This Agreement may be executed in one or more counterparts, each of which shall be deemed an 
original, but all of which together shall constitute one and the same instrument. Electronic or 
facsimile signatures shall be treated as original signatures.

What is a Core II Plan?   
 
A Core Services Plan II is an option for counties that demonstrate need in the prior fiscal year 
by being over-spent in their Core Allocation and wish to request funds in excess of the current 
allocation. If a county meets this criteria, they should complete a Core Services Plan II that 
outlines only the desired additional/expanded services planned if there are additional funds 
available. If the funding will be used for a County Designed service/program not detailed in 
the current Core Services Plan, additional information must be provided.

3.Complete the sections outlined in the document and email the completed Plan to Amy 
Sciangula (amy.sciangula@state.co.us) and Yerson Padilla (yerson.padilla@state.co.us)

or a portion of their Core Services Plan II will be approved.

4. 7.303.11, Type of Service - Family Engagement Meetings (FEMs) are periodic, solution-oriented 
discussions held with families and support members involved with Child Welfare (CW) with the 
intent to identify strengths and needs, in order to build upon an established foundation to 
maintain and increase safety for children in home and those with the goal of returning to home. 
 
  Components of the Service: FEMs are held with family members involved with CW through 
voluntary and involuntary cases, their identified supports, caseworker(s), and service providers 
as well as a supervisor either present or available as needed. This team convenes to have a 
solution-oriented conversation about what strengths the family has to build on, why the Child 
Protection Services (CPS) case is open, goals for safety and/or well-being, specific times in the 
case as required by Volume IV, and planning next steps. FEMs are also held on an as-needed 
basis for families who have been through the assessment process and will not have an ongoing 
case, but would benefit from a facilitated discussion about maintaining safety once their 
assessment is closed. 
 
 Program Goals: 1) Include families in the process of identifying risks and creating safety so that 
families work with Child Protective Services (CPS) as opposed to being worked on by CPS; 2) 
Hear and integrate children’s perspectives about what they need to be safe and well at home; 
3) Involve families natural supports in building safety to increase the likelihood that they will 
actively participate in sustaining changes the family has made beyond CPS’ involvement; 4) 
Incorporate families ideas about what they say will be most helpful in reaching their goals (e.g. 
if a parent has already tried a substance abuse treatment approach that did not work for them, 
what do they think would be more effective in getting them to sobriety now?); 5) Keep cases 
focused on why they opened and what goals they set out for closure, thereby shortening the 
average length of time that cases are open; 6) Find solutions that will safety keep children in 
their homes more often or reunify them with their parents more quickly; 7) If placements are 
necessary to keep children safe, increase the number of those placements with kin; 8) Identify 
any barriers in the case and engage families to continue making progress in their treatment 
plan goals; and 9) Compliance with Volume IV specific case occurrences that are required.
