1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PURPOSE
This  Engineering  Assessment  Report  has  been  prepared  by  Cooper  Engineering  Works,  LLC to  evaluate  the
operational status, treatment capacity, physical condition, and regulatory compliance pathway of the Golden Eagle Water
Treatment Plant (WTP) , formerly known as the Shea Water Company, LLC. The primary objective of this report is to
provide Delta County commissioners, planning staff, and regulatory authorities with a rigorous engineering opinion to
support a land re-zoning evaluation allowing 1-acre residential lot sizes for the proposed residential development.

lot sizes with complete technical confidence. The existing Golden Eagle Water Treatment Plant possesses a
nominal treatment capacity of 100 GPM (144,000 GPD), which is fully capable of supporting up to 360 residential
single-family  lots under  conservative  Colorado  State  standards  (300  GPD  per  lot).  The  mandatory  CDPHE
Community  Water  System  (CWS)  design  review  process  will  ensure  that  all  physical  redundancies,  storage
additions, and monitoring upgrades are fully implemented prior to expanding service beyond current thresholds.

2. PROJECT BACKGROUND & PROPERTY OVERVIEW
The Shea Water Company, LLC was originally established in the early 2000s by Jack Shea and the Shea family to
provide centralized potable water treatment. The original development proposed a 1,050-acre master-planned community
with commercial, residential, industrial, as well as 60 homes on an adjacent property north of Hwy 92. The water
treatment plant was fully constructed, commissioned, and placed into operation as a non-regulated private water system.
Currently, the facility actively services 7 existing residential taps.

5.1 CDPHE Storage Mandate & Expansion Threshold
For non-redundant package plants without duplicate treatment trains, CDPHE regulations typically enforce a minimum 3-
day finished water storage requirement based on average daily demand. 
Phase 1 Reserve Ratio (105 Lots): Total daily demand is 31,500 GPD. The 80,000-gallon storage provides 80,000 /
31,500 = 2.54 days of reserve.
CDPHE 3-Day Compliance Limit: At 300 GPD/tap, the 80,000-gallon tank satisfies the strict 3-day storage rule for up
to 71 residential taps (71 × 300 GPD × 3 days = 63,900 gallons).
Storage Expansion Trigger: When development exceeds 71 residential taps (and prior to reaching full Phase 1
buildout of 105 taps), additional finished water storage capacity must be constructed. Expansion can be executed in
modular concrete or bolted steel tanks.
Disinfection Contact Time (CT): Internal baffling or extended flow piping must be added during storage expansion to
ensure adequate chlorine contact time (T10) compliant with CDPHE surface water treatment rules.
5.2 Fire Flow Reserve
The  80,000-gallon  finished  storage  tank  provides  substantial  fire  protection  capacity.  Standard  residential  fire  flow
requires 500 GPM for a 2-hour duration, totaling 60,000 gallons. A fire event consumes 60,000 gallons, leaving 20,000
gallons of immediate potable reserve while raw water pumping and WTP production continuously replenish finished
volume.

The minimum lot size proposed is 1 acre.  The systems in the OWTS are designed such that the 
effluent delivered to the system is treated and released within each 1-acre lot.  Setbacks are already 
defined per lot such that this requirement is met.  Effluent leaving the OWTS system ultimately enters the 
groundwater system. 
 
The effluent from the leach fields is clarified wastewater that has undergone primary anaerobic 
treatment in a septic tank but still contains high levels of pathogens, nutrients, and dissolved organic 
matter. It is primarily water (over 99%), but the remaining fraction carries significant chemical and 
biological contaminants that require further filtration and purification by the surrounding soil.  The 
effluent from the septic tank flows into the leach field via closed pipe. 
 
The leach field itself does not merely dump this liquid; it acts as a natural bioreactor. As the 
effluent exits the perforated pipes within the leach field and enters the gravel, it passes through a 
biological layer called a biomat. This layer of microbes consumes the remaining organic matter. 
 
As the liquid trickles further down through unsaturated soil, oxygen converts ammonium into 
nitrates, and the soil particles bind to phosphorus and filter out pathogens before the water safely returns 
to the deep groundwater table. 
 
It is favorable to these systems that the Mancos Shale has signification natural fracturing.  It is 
generally a brittle gray-shale and the fracture permeability allows water to permeate into the cracks and 
fissures, eventually entering into the natural groundwater system at depth. 
 
By design, the OWTS systems are sized such that any water that leaves a property has undergone 
treatment and is clean and ready to enter the groundwater system and/or evaporate or be used by foliage 
and vegetation. 
 
The OWTS regulations provide methods for sizing each system in relation to the amount of water 
that will be used for that system.  Most systems are sized in relation to the number of bedrooms in the 
residence.  It is expected that the bulk of the systems will be in the range of 3 bedroom to 5 bedrooms.  
Per the OWTS regulations, the design flow rate from these households will range from 300 gallons per 
day (gpd) to 600 gpd, respectively.

Foundation Recommendation:  
 
The foundations will likely consist of continuous spread footing and/or monolithic concrete slab 
on grade.  This will be dependent on the subgrade and slope at each specific lot and builders’ preferences.  
 
For shallow foundations, some lots where the Mancos Shale is weathered will require extensive 
excavation (3 to 4 ft over-excavation) and replacement with some native soil and with at least 2 ft of 
imported structural fill. Shallow foundation systems, resting on properly constructed structural fill, may be 
designed based on an allowable bearing capacity starting at 1800 psf and a minimum dead load of 300 psf.  
 
The structural fill beneath the foundations may be composed of some processed on-site materials 
such as the surface soil (non-swelling alluvium) and/or imported structural fill or other materials as approved 
by the engineer. Some lots will require a geotechnical investigation prior to residential construction. 
 
 
Site Description: 
 
This parcel is a pre-developed subdivision east of Austin, CO. Specifically, it is south of Highway 
92 and north of the Gunnison River. Utilities and gravel roadways are already installed. This includes a 
pump station lifting water from the Gunnison River. All existing structures have been demolished in 
preparation for development.  The site is zoned for 35 acres but once re-zoned to 1 acre parcels the 
subdivision of the lots and design and construction of homes could commence. 
 
There is significant elevation change throughout the parcel. The pattern is generally lower 
elevation on the north end, with higher elevations to the south on the ridgeline that overlooks the 
Gunnison River. The lowest elevation is approximately 5200 ft, and the highest is 5440 ft. The elevation 
change generally corresponds with geologic changes across the parcel. The general pattern is Dakota
