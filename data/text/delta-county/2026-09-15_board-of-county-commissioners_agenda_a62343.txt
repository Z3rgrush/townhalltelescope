Nomi Gray, 29587 Redlands Mesa Road, Hotchkiss: 29 
Nomi read the following letter on Travis Jardon’s behalf: 30 
During previous meetings we’ve heard comparisons to neighboring counties that have smaller setbacks 31 
for animal confinement operations, but I think there is an important piece of context that deserves 32 
consideration.  Mesa and Montrose County’s have substantially less agricultural activity as a share of 33 
their local economies than Delta County does.  Agriculture represents roughly 1.7% of Mesa County’s 34 
economy and about 3.5% to 4% in Montrose.  In Delta County, agriculture represents approximately 6% 35 
to 8% of economic output and roughly 12.5% to 15% of county employment.  The suggestion of stronger 36 
setbacks necessarily discourage agriculture deserves a closer look.  In fact, some of Colorado’s strongest 37 
agricultural counties have adopted significant setbacks for intensive livestock operations.  Take Morgan 38 
County. Morgan is one of Colorado’s leading agricultural county and its regulations provide for 1,320-foot 39 
setbacks for livestock confinement operations.  That county has demonstrated that meaningful setbacks 40 
can coexist with a strong agricultural economy.  Also, consider Yuma County.  Yuma ranks second 41 
among Colorado counties in the market value of agriculture products sold and more than one billion in 42 
agricultural sales in 2022, yet Yuma County requires a one-mile setback for intensive agricultural 43 
operations from the nearest regularly occupied structure unless the required variance is obtained.  These 44 
examples demonstrate something important, protecting neighboring properties with reasonable setbacks 45 
does not come at the expense of a strong agricultural economy.  Delta County is an agricultural 46 
community and I believe we can protect the agricultural heritage while also protecting the rights and 47 
quality of life for neighboring property owners.  In closing the Planning Commission has carefully 48 
considered this issue and has made a recommendation.  I respectfully ask you to support your Planning 49 
Commissions recommendation and approve the amendment as presented in your packet. 50

TO:  US Forest Service  
 
RE:  2001 Roadless Rule Rescission Proposed Rule-FS-2025-001-224066 
 
 
Delta County Board of Commissioners (BoCC) offers the following letter specific to the 
Proposed Rule to Rescind the 2001 Roadless Rule.  Delta County is located in Western Colorado 
and is 35% US Forest Service (USFS) land.  We have consistently partnered with USFS for 
decades on numerous projects, Forest Planning, NEPA projects, roads and improving safety 
infrastructure.  Delta County BoCC were at the table for the entire CFLERP and SMEADMR 
projects and we were very consistent that treatments and mitigation should be based on science 
and that the scope and scale should be sufficient to address forest health.  All of Delta County 
residents benefit from healthy forests and protected water resources.   
 
Large fires have significantly altered the landscape, impacted watersheds and harmed our 
economic stability and this will continue as the age of trees and total moisture continues to 
decline.  Devastating post-fire flooding, erosion, degradation of water quality and damage to 
water infrastructure frequently follow such fires.  Additionally, the age of trees has led to 
significant downfall which has reduced wildlife habitat and viable grazing acres as entire areas 
are no longer accessible.   
 
Over the last 24 years, it is clear that a blanket policy purportedly intended to keep forested 
landscapes “in a natural state” by restricting road construction and maintenance soundly failed. 
Instead, we’ve seen large swaths of this nation’s forested lands at ever-increasing risk of disease 
and insect infestation, unacceptably high risk of catastrophic wildfire, and unable to perform 
desirable roles as wildlife habitat and watersheds.  
 
By rescinding the 2001 rule, USDA and the U.S. Forest Service (USFS)will be eliminating an 
unnecessary layer of regulatory burden that has prevented successful forest planning. Recission 
of the rule will not immediately reopen the 44.7 million acres where road construction and 
maintenance are currently restricted under the rule (excluding lands under state agreements), 
because the expectations of the rule have been included in every forest planning exercise for the  
 
Page 17 of 222
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
last 24 years. Instead, forest managers, stakeholders, and the public will be able to actively plan 
for management of these acres in their forest planning activities as they move through plan 
revisions or supplemental analyses. Adaptive management practices that meet the requirements 
of drought impacted forest were not considered a quarter of a century ago. Recission of the 2001 
Roadless Rule will allow proactive administration of Wildfire Ready Action Plans, National 
Cohesive Wildland Fire Management Strategy, Community Wildfire Protection Plans and other 
programs to achieve stated goals of climate resiliency and conservation.  
 
For more than two decades, forest health across the West has declined because managers were 
required to fragment their management approaches along artificial boundaries created by the 
rule. The Roadless Rule prohibited access to broad areas, impacting the ability of the agency and 
the community to respond to fire, search and rescue scenarios, and the need for habitat 
restoration among many other functions – all to the detriment of both the functionality of the area 
as a public land unit, but also to the health of the ecosystem itself. Delta BoCC urges USDA and 
USFS to rescind the Roadless Rule and immediately begin prioritizing forest plan amendments 
to address dire ecosystem needs.  
 
Delta BoCC looks forward to continued work with USFS to solve landscape issues and work 
collaboratively.  For additional questions, feel free to contact Delta County Administrator, 
Robbie LeValley, at rlevalley@deltacountyco.gov or 970-874-2102.

III. Legal Analysis and Professional Opinion 
Pursuant to section 37-92-301(4), C.R.S., a conditional water right can be made

• The Subject Water Rights can be made absolute at any point given that they are

• The decreed quantity of water and covered uses far exceed the projected water demand 
of the Clear Water Enterprises Rezone. 
• The approved augmentation plan is sufficient to augment any out-of-priority 
depletions that would occur in the event of a senior call using water stored in Blue 
Mesa Reservoir pursuant to a valid contract.

Rezone, and the plan for augmentation approved in Case No. 02CW39 and associated contract 
with the Bureau of Reclamation is adequate to cover all potential out-of-priority depletions 
that may occur in the event of a senior call.
