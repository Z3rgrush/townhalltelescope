COUNTY HEALTH POOL ( CHP) RATES – Diana Korbe met with the Board concerning the County Health 
Pool rates. There was a meeting last Thursday. Originally there was going to be a three percent increase in medical, 
dental there was no increase and vision there was a three percent increase and life insurance there was no increase.  
However, the county went down a band, so rates decreased. On PPO B 2000 it decreased $11 for the employee only 
plan. The family plan decreased by $26.00.  
 
Several plans are available, including Medical Plan A and Medical Plan B 500. Logan County has consistently 
offered Medical Plan B 2000, HDHP2000, and HDHP 2500. Ms. Korbe received  employee feedback requesting 
separate employee-only, employee-plus-one, and family rates, with a larger family discount. However, the 
alternative Tier 3 plans cost substantially more when offered at three coverage levels, so the County has traditionally

pays 100% of the premium for employees’ health, dental, and vision. Some employees said to decrease the family 
rate, they would be willing to pay a portion of the employee rate. Right now, the county pays 65% of the family

only premium, because right now it ’s zero, and give the family more of a discount, but when you compare 200 
employees to the three comments she received, that doesn’t really make sense.

NIYOL WIND ROAD USE AGREEMENT  – Chanu Nadupuru, Megan Higgi ns, and several Niyol 
representatives met with the Board via the Teams App to outline a project to replace blades on two towers  and 
proposed access routes. They requested permission to temporarily remove signs at County Roads 67 and 24. No 
road improvements are planned, although turn radii will be installed along the roadway on private property. The 
landowners have been notified by the project’s  land agents. Pilot cars traveling ahead of the trailers will remove  
stop signs and other road signs immediately before each turn and reinstall them after the truck passes. 
 
Six new turbine blades will be delivered to replace the six old blades being removed. At one intersection, crews 
will place and compact gravel containing aggregate no larger than one inch and a high proportion of fines, then 
reclaim the site after the work is complete. Because the affected  landowner was not part of the original project, a 
separate compensated easement agreement was obtained. Other participating landowners with turbines on their 
property have been notified of the blade replacements. 
 
Commissioner Brownell asked whether there was enough room to navigate the corner at County Roads 67 and 24 
because of trees on the north side. Takkion, the contractor transporting the blades, proposed making a three-point 
turn. Crews will mow an 80-foot section of the field and install mud mats; the corn has already been harvested. 
  
Alan Samber said he is working with an attorney on a road use agreement for NextEra. He recalled that the battery
