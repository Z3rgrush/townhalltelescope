COMMISSIONER YAHN UPDATES ON COLORADO WATER CONCRESS AND OTHER -  
Commissioner Yahn updated the Board on the Colorado Water Congress meeting he attended last week. There was 
a lot of discussion on the Colorado River. Becky Mitchell, who is Colorado’s  commissioner for the upper four 
upper states attended as well as Amy Ostick, who is the head attorney for the Interstate Compacts. The state engineer 
also attended. There was extensive discussion about what's going on the Colorado River. T he federal government 
released their final environmental impact statement on how they're going to share the river. It's more supply driven 
than before.  
 
Previously, if California and Arizona needed water, they called for water and took it, and then water was released 
out of Powell to get Mead back in balance, that's the way it's been run since 2007. This is a new way of doing things 
and is more governed by how much water is in the system. There's concern that the reservoirs are going to get down 
below power pool, and they won't be able to generate electricity. Yesterday, Nevada sued the federal government. 
California and Arizona may follow suit. Commissioner Yahn had read  that Arizona was agreeing to some cuts, 
about 1.25 million acre-feet of cuts between the three states, and maybe more next year, depending on how much 
supply there is. The federal government's idea was to revisit it every two years and maybe adjust, but they were 
still encouraging the states to try to come to some kind of an agreement. 
 
Commissioner Brownell asked if ther e had been a ny new developments on the Perkins County Canal. 
Commissioner Yahn said that there have not been any meetings recently. However, he has offered show the Army 
Corps of Engineers what a diversion structure looks like on the South Platte  River. He will meet with them on  
Friday at the North Sterling diversion structure . He will talk to them about how difficult it is to run water in the 
wintertime, because that's what they're going to face. The system that Nebraska is proposing is about the same size 
as North Sterling, the reservoir size, the ditch size, and the diversion.  
 
Alan Samber informed the Board that there is a case in the Court of Appeals that should come down on the decision

opinions to see if the case has come out.   
 
Commissioner Yahn also mentioned that on September 28th, Colorado must give a rebuttal to Nebraska’s complaint 
to the Supreme Court.  
 
Commissioner Brownell reported that he and Commissioner Santomaso toured a battery energy storage facility in 
Pueblo. He f elt good about Logan County’s regulations after the tour, that they are protecting Logan County 
residents. The facility they toured was 100 megawatts  and the one that is proposed for Logan County is 200 
megawatts. The site was powered by solar panels and the one in Logan County will be powered by wind  towers. 
The setbacks looked similar and he felt comfortable with the fire suppression.

He was concerned about noise; however, they were standing right next to and amongst the containers. They were 
discharging at the time they were there and that is when it is supposed to need the most heat reduction. The fans 
were all running and they point them towards each other in the container rows, so it helps contain the sound. They 
were about two to three tenths of a mile away from it. The guy behind them left his vehicle running and you couldn’t 
hear the site at all. He felt good that our noise regulations are adequate to protect anybody living close by. In that 
site, the closest people would be at least half a mile away.  
 
Commissioner Santomaso said the control room was impressive. Computer monitors track every solar panel and 
battery component, and staff knew immediately when one  panel appeared to be  turned upside down. The site is 
monitored 24 hours a day by both personnel and an automated system. If an issue arises, operators can shut down 
and isolate the affected area to keep it under control. The battery storage facility has operated for three years without 
any major issues.

FAIRGROUNDS MASTER PLAN – Mr. Cullip asked the Board whether  they had given further consideration 
to the plan developed last year. As he prepares next year’s  budget, he needs direction on the capital expenditure 
line. Although several topics  were raised during the Fair Board meeting, no decisions have been made, and no 
further discussions have taken place. 
 
Commissioner Brownell said he had not received any input from the Fair Board. Mr. Cullip said the recent Fair 
Board meeting ran long, so the poultry building was not discussed. The Fair Board will meet again in September 
to review the matter in more detail. Additional information was also presented regarding cattle pens and possible 
redesigns of some tie-outs, which may help to address some of the issues previously discussed. 
 
Commissioner Brownell said he would like to begin discussions and consider capital improvements, possibly for 
the following year. Commissioner Santomaso agreed, noting that the Master Plan is extensive. Mr. Cullip suggested 
completing the poultry building first and addressing another area in a future year. 
 
Commissioner Brownell said he would be more comfortable allowing additional time for discussion with the Fair 
Board and focusing  on a phased approach. Aside from  the poultry building, he would consider budgeting for 
improvements next year and reviewing other projects afterward. 
 
Commissioner Yahn said any cattle pen concerns discussed at the Fair Board meeting were already addressed in 
the Master Plan. He said the County should avoid spending significant funds on pens that may not be usable long 
term, and further discussion is needed to ensure any improvements align with the long- term plan. Commissioner 
Santomaso noted that the pens discussed were portable.
