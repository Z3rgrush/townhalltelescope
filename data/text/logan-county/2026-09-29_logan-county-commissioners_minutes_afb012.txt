PENNIES FOR POTHOLES – Anna Rivers met with the Board. Her organization, Pennies for Potholes is  a 
registered issues committee with the City of Sterling in support of ballot initiative 2A. The ballot issue is for a 1% 
sales tax increase. The funds will go entirely for road maintenance with in the City of Sterling. She is in charge of 
signage, postcards, mailers, and in support of the ballot issue . She is asking if the Logan County Commissioners 
would endorse Pennies for Potholes. The City of Sterling estimates $40 million dollars are necessary from a recent 
paving assessment that was done. A similar ballot initiative failed last year. Ms. Rivers discussed why the City of 
Sterling is doing this again when the citizens clearly said no last time.   
 
Ms. Rivers stated that Sterling City Manager John Sheldon learned that the sales tax needed to have a shorter time 
frame. So, this sales tax will be for only five years, and then the citizens will have the opportunity to reassess and 
see if the city did a good job with the funds and were good stewards with their money. T he other thing was to 
educate citizens in the writing of the ballot issue that this is not money that's going to go to the general fund that 
can be spent on whatever the city wants. It is a dedicated tax that will go to road maintenance  only. The City is 
optimistic that it could go better this year.  
 
Commissioner Yahn asked how much revenue  was projected to be generated. Ms. Rivers will have to ask Mr. 
Sheldon for the details of that. It is not enough to cover the $40 million necessary for the paving, it’s only enough 
to get started. He does have a detailed plan where the highest priority roads are. Also, they will use other funds to 
deal with the underlying septic and drainage issues and only after those have been addressed will they use money 
from this fund to repave.  Commissioner Yahn stated that t he City has had many unexpected issues with sewer 
lines crossing Highway 138, so they are having to pour a lot of funds into that right now. Ms. Rivers said that they 
are hoping to pull together some grants from the state to help pay for that situation.  
 
Commissioner Santomaso asked where the money was coming from  for the drainage study. Ms. Rivers said that 
there was a grant, possibly a DOLA grant, this is in progress or waiting for final approval based off the last City 
Council meeting. That would be a necessary first step before they start repaving roads, and then the actual work of 
doing the repairs, there are already funds in the general fund for at least some of that work. It will not rely on this 
sales tax increase.

LOGAN COUNTY AMBULANCE BUDGET – Sterling Fire Chief Cody Howell and Chief Knudsen met with

RURAL HEALTH TRANSFORMATION PROGRAM (RHTP ) GRANT - Chief Howell reported that Chief  
Lonnie Knudsen has been working very hard to work with the Rural Health Transformation Program  (RHTP) to  
advance EMS services. They want the interfacility transports taken care of if possible. They were approved for a 
Rural Health Transformation grant of $2,096,141 that will pay for salaries, equipment and services to expand 
services and develop the interfacility transport program. They will provide all the documentation to the Board so 
that they can see what the plan looks like. The positions will be added very soon to help manage and  develop it. 
One of the first positions to be added will be a Critical Care Medic paid for by the grant. They want to discuss 
starting that as well as some of the Part-time Single-Role EMT Personnel.   
 
Commissioner Yahn asked if the grant funding would start immediately. Mr. Knudsen said that before any expenses 
can be incurred, a signed grant contract must be in place. This will need to be done by October 15th. The state has 
sent out the approved budget. As soon as he receives it, he will forward it to the Board for review. That will include 
the statement of work. If we would like to request advanced payment, he will need to submit a letter by October 
15th through Google Forms and he can do that . T hat way, as equipment is ordered, it’s not hitting a budget 
somewhere else.    
 
The grant will add about $1 million in salaries to cover the Interfacility Transport (IFT) Division, and another $1 
million in equipment. The funds must be spent between now and the end of December, in order for the feds to add 
more money into it. This is a five-year grant of roughly $2 million per year. Mr. Knudson said that they are heavy 
in year one because they’re buying SUVs, ambulances, cardiac monitors, and other equipment. Once that is done 
in about year two or three, they will add another IFT ambulance and another full-time position. The idea behind the 
RHTB grant is to obtain sustainability with the assistance of grant funding.  
 
The IFT ambulance would probably not be delivered prior to January, because of build timelines, but it would be 
invoiced and paid to show that the funds have been spent. The rest of the equipment would be delivered by the end 
of 2027. Those funds along with the salaries of the Critical Care Medic would cover the first million dollars.   
 
The grant cannot replace funding for existing services, so the 911 and IFT services must remain separate. However,

the Critical Care Medic may assist with mass -casualty incidents or periods of high call volume, respond to high-
acuity 911 calls, and handle critical-care IFT transports. Separate financial records must be maintained for the two 
services throughout the five -year grant period. Commissioner Yahn asked whether IFT personnel could work 
overtime for the 911 service. Mr. Knudsen believed they could, provided separate pay codes were used. 
 
Mr. Knudsen said it is a huge win for the EMS system and for the community to be able to get this kind of support. 
It will give them five years to think about sustainability and what it looks like for EMS in Logan County and 
especially on the 911 side. 
 
Commissioner Yahn asked whether the ambulance service could bill for interfacility transports after the grant  
covers startup costs and whether that revenue must remain in the IFT division or could support the broader system. 
Mr. Knudsen said he had similar questions that may be addressed in the grant contract and deliverables. He expects 
the service will be billable,  and the revenue will likely remain in an IFT fund to sustain the division and replace 
equipment and ambulances. He added that considerable time has been devoted to generating revenue and sustaining 
the 911 system. 
 
Commissioner Yahn said that, based on discussions with other ambulance services, interfacility transport often 
helps fund 911 services. He suggested integrating the two as soon as possible. Chief Howell explained that although 
the grant cannot supplant exi sting revenue, it may cover some budgeted items, such as the single -role medics 
requested for interfacility transport. Moving those costs to the grant could reduce the budget, as could grant 
coverage for certain equipment and other listed items. Mr. Knudse n said he would remove those items from the 
budget to avoid duplication. He also noted that they had been considering a capital equipment fund and applying 
for additional grants. The grant -funded integration technology—including equipment, Wi-Fi routers, a nd 
hotspots—will support both the 911 and IFT systems, providing a significant benefit to 911 services. 
 
Mr. Knudsen spoke with Jason Thyne of Sterling Regional MedCenter yesterday and told him about the grant. Mr. 
Thyne could not attend the meeting today. The RHTP grant was not successful in getting the community paramedic 
funding.  
 
Commissioner Santomaso said that the grant award is great. However, at the end of five years, EMS is at a point 
now where we're not sustainable. At the end of five years, he does not want to set up the next set of commissioners 
with a $3 million negative balance. Chief Howell stated that the great thing is by about year two to three, we should 
see if the revenue is going to help sustain it. If it’s not, we can figure out how we’re going to have to fund it, and it 
gives us time.  
 
The deadline for the grant contract to be signed is October 15 th. County Attorney Alan Samber would like to see 
the grant contract before it is forwarded to the Board for signature. The next business meeting is October 6th.
