Attorney Collin Mielke discussed the moral obligation. The big concern from the county’s legal perspective is 
compliance with Tabor. The re’s also a statute that Alan had pointed out in Title 30 about loans. Mr. Mielke has 
looked at both of those. The moral obligation would be an annual appropriation. Tabor limits the county’s ability 
to enter into multiple fiscal year financial obligations unless there is a prior vote authorizing it. If it is subject to the 
county’s annual appropriation, and they look at the budget to see whether they have the ability and/or the desire to 
appropriate funds toward the obligation. They need to look at it and look at their budget, but they don’t have an 
obligation to actually expend any funds unless they feel like it fits within their budget for that upcoming fiscal year.  
 
It seems like a good opportunity for the SPMD, because the intention is that the county never has to spend a dime 
on this financing. Projections show that the county wouldn't need to. From the lender's standpoint, the district’s not 
as creditworthy as it would be if it had somebody with a little bit more financial resources backing it. From a Tabor 
standpoint, it looks like it would be a good option for the district. Alan would need to be sure that the county is 
comfortable with that.  
 
In addition to that, there's Title 30 statute that Alan had mentioned which has some limitations potentially from a 
2020 bill that passed regarding loans from the county. Mr. Mielke looked at it and can see  where Alan is coming 
from on whether that's a limitation that would apply to the county. He will discuss this further with Alan  and see 
whether the statue is really intended to be applied in this sort of situation where it says the county can issue loans 
to other governmental entities, but there are interest rate requirements and things of that nature. This is intended as 
a backstop or a letter of credit sort of situation where the county is pledging its credibility to assist in getting the 
district over this last hurdle for the loan. 
 
Mielke said that maybe the statute really wasn’t meant to apply to that situation. Possibly it’s meant to apply when 
the county is actually issuing a loan and funding things on a regular basis. He has not had a chance to discuss this  
with Alan whether he agrees with that interpretation.  
 
James Manning of Columbia Bank explained that the moral obligation is similar to cosigning a loan with your 
child. The bank’s expectation is if revenues are insufficient to meet debt service payments, that the county would 
step in. That is what the bank is leaning on, the county’s credit history to help the district get over the first couple 
years’ gap until they have been able to generate their own history. From the bank’s analysis and from what he has 
looked at, he does not anticipate there ever being a need for the county to step in at all. Ultimately, that’s what they 
are looking for. Additional county support that would ideally roll off within two to three years. O nce there are a 
couple years’ worth of audits, and once there is history for the district . It would be something that would roll off 
just to help them in the initial years. The bank has very good comfort with the 75-mill levy, but that in and of itself 
does not cover the proposed debt service on the debt . Bridging the gap between the debt mill levy and what debt 
service is, with the end goal of obviously allowing SP MD to decrease the rates that they're charging to their 
community.  
Columbia Bank is headquartered out of Tacoma, Washington, but ha s recently expanded into the Southwest. Mr. 
Manning operates out of Phoenix, but oversees Colorado, Arizona, and Nevada. He used to, at a prior bank, manage
