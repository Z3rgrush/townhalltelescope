VIAERO FIBER OPTIC – Braden and Lance McAlister of Viaero Fiber Optic met with the Board  to discuss 
bringing fiber internet service to the courts and Sheriff’s Office. Although both facilities currently use Magnifi, 
Viaero could provide backup service during a temporary Magnifi outage. Viaero would install fiber to the Justice 
Center at no cost.   
 
Viaero is working to install fiber internet along Colorado Highway 14 to their store in Sterling on Main Street . 
Viaero has installed fiber internet for the City of Sterling at the airport off Highway 14. They currently have fiber 
coming down Interstate 76 past the prison. They have had some interest from the City of Sterling in installing fiber 
internet at the museum. Viaero’s fiber internet comes out of the 16 th Street Mall area in Denver and goes all the 
way to Grand Island, Nebraska. They own the fiber internet; they don’t buy it from anyone.  
 
Alan Samber asked if Viaero had a landman doing easement research out by the Justice Center because he will 
need to do an improvement location survey due to the proposed coroner’s office building. Alan needs updated 
information if it is available. Brandon McAlister said that they have not done any easement research in the area and 
will not be doing any work until they have someone interested in signing up for Viaero fiber.  
 
The Board asked when the project would start. Lance McAlister said that they are installing fiber right now north 
of the interstate. They are going to talk to the state about servicing the prison with fiber internet. The CDOT building 
is located immediately north of the Justice Center. Viaero is in negotiations with the City of Sterling right now to 
provide service to all city  buildings. The City of Sterling has a water treatment facility and wastewater facility 
approximately a mile north of the Justice Center.  
 
Viaero would like to meet with the Board again with their president who was unable to attend the meeting today. 
The Board will start doing some research and let them know if the County will want to sign up with Viaero.  
 
ROAD AND BRIDGE DEPARTMENT  – Mike Burri, Road and Bridge Department Operations  Manager told 
the Board that he had talked to Wayne Howard about the bridge grant progra m.. Mr. Howard recommended that 
the department apply for the bridge planning and engineering grant for the bridge on CR 26 first and the following 
year to apply for a construction grant. He asked the Board if they want ed to hold that bridge open for a two- to 
three- year project. Alan Krier thinks they can fix the bridge, but Mr. Burri told him he needed plans. Krier would 
leave the deck where it is and take the piling out and put concrete walls on both ends. The deck can be done on 
another date, because the deck itself is good, it’s just the piling in the ground that needs to be repaired. Or they can 
put some new wood piling with some concrete footers on the bottoms and just drop them down and pour concrete 
and get it to stay that way, but that’s more of a temporary fix. Full concrete end walls would be better, and he can 
actually make them wider at that time.

The grant application window has just opened up. However, the Board agreed that it did not want to close the bridge 
for that long of a period of time. Alan Krier said he couldn’t do it this year, but he said he could do it first thing 
after the water is shut down again. Mr. Krier showed Mr. Burri a set of blueprints that the bridge could be built 
from. Mr. Burri asked if the Board would want to get another engineer to design another bridge and put it out for 
bid. Burri will let Mr. Howard know that the County will not be pursuing a grant for the bridge on CR 26. 
 
The bridge inspector said the bridge deck was in pretty good shape. It is the bridge piling that is in bad shape. If 
new pilings and end walls were put in, they might upgrade the bridge, but the bridge rating would never be upgraded 
to what is needed without building a new structure. The bridge is at a 10-ton rating and if they want to spend the 
money to do it they would want to get it to a 50-ton rating. 
 
Commissioner Yahn spoke with Matt Harris, a  local engineer in Fort Morgan who completed work on the South 
Platte and has experience with  diversion structures. He said Harris would likely cost less than most Front Range 
engineers and might be able to design a bridge suitable for this site and similar future projects. Soil samples would 
be needed to determine the required size of the substructure. Commissioner Yahn will ask whether Harris has time 
to complete the work for the County. Few complaints have been received about the bridge closure; only one nearby 
resident has commented because he uses the bridge to move equipment.  
 
The Board discussed another old bridge that is near the Proctor/Crook area on the back side of the railroad tracks.  
It is very narrow and you can’t get any truck or piece of equipment or anything across it. The road goes right past 
a person’s house and back to the highway. The bridge is on the bridge inspection report. A nd is tagged as being 
bad. The bridge could probably be removed and not be replaced because there is no traffic on it. It is not worth 
putting in a box culvert.   
 
The Board continued to discuss the bridge on CR 26 and decided to hold a public meeting about the possibility of 
permanently closing and removing the bridge. The public will be noti fied by posting a sign at the location of the 
bridge in both directions.     
 
Mr. Burri received a call that day about CR 71. The caller was concerned not about the bridge, but about water that 
had washed over the road while CR 79 was closed because of downed electrical poles. Burri inspected the road and 
found it somewhat rough but not severe; however, because the caller travels it daily, he requested that it be bladed. 
The caller also raised concerns about the bridge’s low load rating and the possibility that a loaded truck could fall 
through the wooden structure. CR 71 carries substantial truck traffic because it is the paved northbound road just 
south of CR 66. Commissioner Brownell also expressed concern about heavy trucks using the bridges south of 
Dailey. Burri identified another small bridge on a back road beyond the prison. It is only 4½ to 5 feet wide and is 
filled with sand, so a vehicle falling through would drop about 2½ feet. He would like to consult the landowners 
about removing it because cattle no longer pass beneath it. 
 
Commissioner Brownell also mentioned another bridge on CR 67 about a quarter of a mile south of Hwy 6, down 
in the draw, there is a well and a tank and pastures on both sides. They used to run cattle underneath there when he 
was a kid. It has filled in a lot. It’s an old cement bridge. The battery energy storage company will be running trucks 
across there soon. Burri said that he would take a look at that one.  
 
Commissioner Yahn asked whether Highway 59 near Proctor was closed. Mr. Burri said it remained open but that 
he would install concrete barricades while leaving enough room for the North Sterling maintenance crew to pass.  
Alan Samber said that a hearing was unnecessary because the Board has statutory authority over highways, 
including county roads, culverts, bridges, and ditches. He will prepare a resolution. Burri said Travis Somerfield is 
the only person who uses the road because he owns land on the other side , although Somerfield already takes 
another route with his large equipment. The Road and Bridge Department contacted other potential users, who said 
they never use the road. Mr. Burri said that he could close the intersection and make it a T intersection instead of a 
four way and then close it right at the bridge.
