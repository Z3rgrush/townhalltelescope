COUNTY HELD TAX LIENS NEW LAWS – Patty Bartlett met with the Board concerning Senate Bill 26-144 
which was intended to be a cleanup of the treasurer’s deed bill that they passed two years ago. This bill went into 
effect July 1, 2024, and this one went into effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
 
3 
 
In the past, county held liens were good for 30 years, and individual liens were good for 15 years. This law changes 
it so that individual liens are now seven years, but they grandfathered in any liens that are outstanding. So, beginning 
on January 1st, 2026, from now on, individually held tax liens are seven years. County liens, they dropped to five 
years and did not grandfather them in. If an individual had a lien from four years ago, it is still good for 15 years. 
Any lien for an individual that was already issued before January 1 st of 2026 is still good for 15 years, but the y 
didn’t grandfather them in. 
 
The Board signed a resolution last year to cancel 30-year liens, which was the first time any liens had reached that 
age. Mrs. Bartlett presented a li st of 400 liens more than five years old and  requested a resolution directing the 
Treasurer to cancel them, as none of the taxes have been paid. She noted one concern involving a property owner, 
Hazel Miles, whose property is subject to a City of Sterling special assessment. Because the lien was sold in 2009 
and includes a substantial assessment for demolition of a house by the city, Mrs. Bartlett asked what would happen 
to the assessment if the lien were canceled. Although someone is interested in purchasing the lien, they will not do 
so because of the special assessment. Mrs. Bartlett said she has discussed with Alan Samber the possibility of the 
city taking ownership of the lien and applying for a deed, but that  has not yet occurred. She asked the Board for 
direction on this issue. 
 
There are three real property liens. Lots of people are interested in the Irene Prather property but it's landlocked, so 
nobody has picked up that lien. Mrs. Bartlett asked when the liens are cancelled if those taxes are forgiven and the 
properties advertised with the taxes owed. Most of the liens are minerals and don't amount to much. There are some 
mobile homes on the list, one was sold in 2005, which is probably not located here anymore.  
 
Mrs. Bartlett went on to say that if the Board wants to apply for a Treasurer’s Deed, the Board must give her an 
application before November 16th of this year, otherwise they’re not going to be any good. Alan Samber said that 
there was no reason that the county would want to apply for a deed on the undivided mineral s because case law  
says counties cannot own property to hold speculatively if they can’t put it to good use.  
 
Mrs. Bartlet also presented a spreadsheet of county -held liens that were sold in 2022 that will still be good until 
2028. There is no rush to do anything with them, again, they are all minerals. Mr. Samber said that he will need to 
do more research on the new law.  
  
Mrs. Bartlett discussed many of the fees that have been changed in recent years. Prior to two years ago, the fee for 
anything less than $500 in assessed value , which included all minerals, and some  mobile homes the fee was $35. 
Anything over that was $75. Two years ago, they changed the fee to $300 unless it is a severed mineral, then they 
can charge less. People did not want to buy severed minerals because they are cost prohibitive. The legislature has 
promised to correct this . If Logan County charge s $100 and Phillips  County charges $200 and another county 
charges $10, people will go where it is least expensive. Mrs. Bartlett finally settled on $50 after a Treasurer ’s 
Association meeting where it was voted on to charge that amount.  
 
Mrs. Bartlett had one more issue  concerning a T reasurer's Deed on a severed mineral  with no research. She 
researched it. It was sold for .005 and is actually worth .001. There is another one that was sold for .01498, and it's 
.003. She asked what should be done with them. T ax-wise it doesn't make any difference. She does not know if  
the buyer would have purchased them if he had known, in this case if was worth a fifth of what it sold for. The tax 
sale is buyer beware. An application has been applied for a treasurer ’s deed. The certificate of purchase had a  
higher percentage than it turned  out, and she cannot deed him what he bought. The Assessor has looked at it and 
agrees with her research.  
  
The fee used to be $35 for a treasurer’s deed. Now it is $50 application fee plus $60 to issue a deed, so technically 
$110. A deed process on a mineral today will cost between $250 - $300, because one of the things that the legislature 
changed is that they no longer have to mail them certified mail. He may not have purchased this mineral if he had

known it was going to cost him that much. She has not talked to him about the smaller amount. Mr. Samber said 
the buyer should be informed and given the option of a refund and the certificate taken back to a county-held 
certificate.  
 
The Board asked about the typical tax bill for severed mineral interests. Mrs. Bartlett said many are approximately 
80 cents per year. In one year, an administrative fee of $10 could be added when taxes were less than $50, but the 
Commissioners removed that fee. If the taxes become delinquent, the  Treasurer may add a $5 delinquency fee. 
Mailing tax statements costs 78 cents twice a year if the account becomes delinquent. Mrs. Bartlett said this can be 
an effective way to return minerals to the land, provided the records are accurate and landowners are willing to pay 
the costs. Under the new law , the county must apply for a deed , and the Treasurer must then notify the surface 
owner. If the surface owner chooses to exercise the right to acquire the minerals, the Treasurer must deed them to 
the owner at no charge and waive all fees and costs.
