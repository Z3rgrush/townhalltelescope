RURAL HEALTH TRANSFER (RHTP) GRANT – Lonnie Knudsen and Sterling Fire Chief Cody Howell met 
on behalf of Logan County Ambulance  with the Board to discuss the Rural Health Transfer Grant (RHTP ) grant.  
The grant deadline is August 3. Regional partners worked together to write the grant for the whole blood aspect of 
the grant. If the grant is successful, then the grant will be awarded and managed on our behalf. The grant covers all  
costs and expenses. However, there may be training and staff time required. Equipment and blood products for the 
period of the grant is covered. Chief Howell signed a letter of intent to support the grant.   
 
Mr. Knudsen explained that he would like to develop a community paramedic program, or mobile integrated 
healthcare. He feels that it will fit well within the parameters of the grant. It will expand emergency care but whole 
health care. One part of the grant that may be able to get funded is the interfacility transports if it would pay for at 
least the next five years to implement that and get those things in place, it would be worth the effort.  Then there 
would be time to determine how to sustain the service. 
  
The grant does not allow reimbursement for services already being billed. If interfacility transfers are provided, it 
would make sense to bill for those services and use the grant to help recoup costs. An interfacility transfer program 
staffed by paramedics and EMTs could allow the ambulance service to handle billing and help support the overall 
program. 
 
They are also considering ambulance replacement options. Mr. Knudsen said he was unsure whether the grant 
would allow the purchase of ambulances, but i t would be beneficial if two ambulances could be obtained for 
interfacility transfers. The grant application does not require a match; if funded, it would cover 100% of costs for 
five years, although in-kind contributions may be required. 
 
The group discussed using a different type of vehicle for i nterfacility transfers, such as a Sprinter van. However, 
one company Mr. Knudsen contacted said it no longer builds Sprinter vans because they are not worth the time. He 
said it  may be better to re -chassis an ambulance rather than purchase a Sprinter van. He also emphasized the 
importance of ambulance interoperability, so staff does not have to learn different vehicles or equipment setups. 
He noted that transfer ambulances are often used in snowstorms, blizzards, and other adverse conditions, making 
four-wheel drive capability beneficial. 
 
Cost savings and efficiency would come from purchasing quality ambulances that can be re-chassied over multiple 
years. Replacing the chassis is significantly less expensive  than replacing the entire module , and a chassis can 
typically be used for at least 10 to 15 years. The Board also discussed the two ambulances given to Sterling Regional 
MedCenter several years ago. Chief Howell said that when the ambulance service stopped doing transfers, two 
ambulances were given to Banner Health for transports. Neither would have been suitable for long-term use. Both 
were older ambulances, more than eight years old; one was on its third turbo and had a poor engine, while the other 
had an International chassis that was not well suited for interfacility transfers because it was overbuilt and resembled

Mr. Knudsen said that previous CDPHE grant applications had included a 50% match, but he believes they are now 
in a financial position to request  a waiver and apply with a 10% match. He recently received a budget quote of 
$337,000 for an ambulance similar to the Med Five ambulance. If the 10% waiver is approved, the ambulance could 
cost approximately $37,000. If the RHTP grant is also funded, they may be able to purchase three ambulances for 
about $30,000 to $40,000 each. 
 
Mr. Knudsen said EMS reimbursement needs to be addressed at the federal level, noting that fraud and waste 
receive more funds than EMS. He said local governments cannot continue subsidizing EMS and that reimbursement 
should cover the actual cost of each service. He estimated that fully funding community paramedics, interfacility 
transport, and 911 coverage  would cost approximately $5 million with all expenses  included. He also discussed 
repair facilities capable of servicing emergency vehicle s, completing  re-chassis and remount work, and the 
possibility of generating revenue by repairing ambulances for surrounding communities. 
  
At the monthly billing meeting with Morgan last week, she said that the revenue projections were above what they 
say they’re collecting. It was set on 1,400 billable transports, but when they reworked the numbers there were closer 
to 1,900 billable transports.  Knudsen asked if the collection agreement had been signed by the county. Alan 
Samber had some concerns with the original contract. Debbie Unrein had been waiting for the Board’s decision on 
the contract. Alan and Debbie will work on the contract this week. Once that is settled, they will have a better 
picture of the cash that they are getting back in. There is quite a gap between what is billed and what they actually 
collect which is about $560 - $580 per transport.   
 
Mr. Knudsen discussed his ideas for a community paramedic program and its potential benefit to the community. 
Chief Howell noted that there is currently no local urgent care facility or walk-in clinic, and patients often wait four 
to six weeks to see their primary care providers. He said community paramedics could help fill this gap in the 
emergency system by providing timely, efficient, and cost-effective care in patients’ homes. 
 
The RHTP grant is transforming rural health care. Chief Howell told the Board that Dr. Bacon has been working 
through the hospital on this as well as regionally. He’s been a part of a lot of emergency services in Wheatland, 
Wyoming.  He is trying to determine ways to regionalize this and help provide care modules in different places. 
He has been talking about how this can be integrated with the hospital. Paramedics could make house calls to 
prevent sick people from having to go into the doctors’ offices and spread illness.     
 
The community paramedics would need office space, SUV’s and equipment. The police department will be moving 
into the old armory; they could possibly utilize the old police department facilities for the paramedic program and 
police chief and fire chief and public safety offices.  
 
Mr. Knudsen asked if the Board has any issues if he applies for the RHTP grant to include everything discussed. 
The Board agreed that he should apply for the grant for everything allowed and will support the application with 
letters of support if necessary. He will need a Certificate of Good Standing from the County issued by the Secretary 
of State to submit with the grant application.  
 
The Community Paramedic program, if funded, would be approximately $300,000. He was not sure if it would be 
a reimbursable grant or if the funding would be distributed up front. It may require a supplemental budget for the 
expenses. The grant award should be made in September or October, so it could be incorporated in the Ambulance 
budget. However, a lot of the capital expenses  $210 million would need to be expended by the end of 2026 and 
then it would be renewed for next year.

The RHTP grant began with those organizations that wanted to do interfacility transports and how many wanted to 
do whole blood. Getting the whole blood programs onto ambulance is extremely expensive, costing approximately 
$70,000 per year. Logan County Ambulance has protocols and the waiver, but due to the cost, they have never been 
able to carry out the plan. With this grant, there is an opportunity to fund this. The Denver RETAC has taken on   
the grant writing process to encompass up to 30 agencies throughout the State of Colorado. They said they would 
handle everything to create a program  and manage everything. The blood would be shipped to Logan County 
Ambulance from Texas every month, with Logan County Ambulance doing the data reporting and training.    
 
Plains to Peak RETAC did the sa me thing with the interfacility transports. Those that want to be included in that 
grant got with them, but they found out that there were so many differences amongst the region that they said that 
this probably isn’t going to work because of the different ways agencies operate. However, they are writing a grant 
for notification and some type of software that allows for transfers and availability.   
 
LANDFILL – Commissioner Brownell told the Board that Josh Klein has told him that the landfill stays open until 
4 p.m. during daylight savings time and then the rest of the year from 8 a.m. to noon on Saturdays. He says that 
there are a lot of times that they will get some business in the morning in the summer and then there is no business 
for three or four hours. This leaves the operator sitting with nothing to do for several hours. He is wondering if we 
ought to consider setting the hours from 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. year-round on Saturdays. If the vote in the Senate  on 
Daylight Savings Time passes, they will stop having Daylight Savings Time. This has already passed in the House. 
Josh is working on the budget for next year. The Board agreed that this is a reasonable idea.
