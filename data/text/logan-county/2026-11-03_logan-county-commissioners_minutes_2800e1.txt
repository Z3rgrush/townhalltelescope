STERLING COMMUNITY FUND PROPOSED SPLASH PAD – Members of the Sterling Community 
Fund (SCF) Board Rob Nichols and JD Stone, met with the Board regarding a proposed splash pad project at 
the Courthouse Square. Mr. Stone distributed revised renderings of what the splash pad might look like and 
explained the goals of the SCF that are to facilitate some form of a hardscape in the community that can help 
grow and beautify northeast Colorado. 
 
Sterling is a hub of northeast Colorado. The goal is to create more foot traffic downtown and build the tax base .  
He wants to be clear that this is a splash pad, not a water park. There is nothing elevated above ground other than 
the on-off button, there are no slides or water guns. Everything is concrete. People have been worried about 
esthetics. Today, they are looking for advice to move forward and possibly approval from the Board. 
 
Mr. Nichols said that this came up as a suggestion from community members. The fund was approached by citizens 
with a suggestion from people who wanted to see some sort of water attraction. People had seen the splash pads in 
other communities. The SCF was creat ed so that projects like this would benefit the entire community. The fund 
felt this project in particular was important for downtown. The ir vision for downtown is for it to become more 
vibrant and attract more people to do things. The splash pad is designed so that is doesn’t detract from the 
courthouse grounds.  
 
It is designed to be low maintenance, with the bulk of maintenance, other than starting it and stopping it similar to 
a sprinkler system. The water is turned on in the spring, and the system is flushed in the fall, making sure the drain 
grate is drained. There may be leaves that periodically need to be cleaned out. Sterling Comm unity Fund will be 
responsible for funding the splash pad in its entirety. They don’t expect the county to pa y for anything. If it is a 
total failure, the splash pad can be removed and grass planted in its place. It is d esigned in a way that does not 
require additional work.  
 
The location would be central for downtown in general and creates a concentration of activities. People like to walk 
from one event to another. People like to walk from July Jamz, Sugar Beet Days, to restaurants, and other activities. 
It creates a lot of energy just in and of itself. They don’t want it to be a distraction from the Courthouse. Mr. Nichols 
has been involved in historic preservation for much of his adult life.  
 
They don’t believe that there will be a problem with Courthouse restroom use, it should not be any worse than any 
other event. All the water, whether it be the taps, the hookups, the sewer hookups, the cost of the water, will be paid 
for by the SCF . The fund believes i t is a long-term investment in the community . The more people you bring

downtown, the more people that shop, go to movies, buy gasoline, etc.  
 
Mr. Stone said that they have looked at the Fleming splash pad, which is very basic. The amount of foot traffic that 
Fleming gets on a Saturday afternoon is huge. The goal is hopefully that the same idea would come to Sterling. 
People can come to town and watch their kids play, grab some ice cream, and go to the movies.   
 
They have seen a huge outpouring of support from the community. The y ran a quick petition for a couple of days 
and obtained close to 700 signatures in favor of the splash pad. There are a few people against it, but they believe 
it’s about 150 to one. There are some issues, but they have addressed most of them and are willing to discuss them. 
They have also had a private donor offer a $100,000 donation to see the project happen. The donor has hinted at an 
additional donation if it is approved and is moving forward. The donor is a well-known family in the area and when 
she visits the area, she is always looking for something to do. The idea of being able to go downtown and enjoy the 
splash pad with her family would be huge. She’s seen this with her other grandchildren in other locations and thinks 
it would be a great addition.  
 
Initially, when the splash pad was designed as a committee, the vision was to try to bring back the historic pond in 
the same location at the Courthouse which was probably unrealistic. They played with the locations and now have 
moved it to the northwest corner of the Courthouse square. Historically, there was a horseshoe pit in that area. 
There is an urban park across the street. Another major benefit  is the sewer, and water utilities are close by in the 
street. They will have to bore or trench to Fourth Street and that will take care of the water and sewer. There is 
minimal electrical power necessary. They have talked to Tom Kiel about the electrical and will have to determine 
how to pull in the 12-volt power, similar to landscape lighting.  
 
There have been some concerns about parking. At one time the downtown area had metered parking because it was 
so busy. If there is a parking issue, the fund will feel that the goal has been accomplished by drawing more people 
downtown. If there is an issue during business hours , the programming can be changed remotely so it can be shut 
down if necessary. It can be set to start at 2 p.m. or 4 p.m. or whatever is necessary.  
 
Downtown Sterling used to be a vibrant community, and they're really trying to make it like that again. Old Town 
Fort Collins is beautiful on a Saturday with people walking around; there is family time. Landscaping would be a 
natural safety barrier; the splash pad is back far enough from the street that it should not be a safety concern. There 
is only one small tree that needs to be moved. SCF would take care of moving the tree with a tree spade. SCF would 
like to see the splash pad open Memorial Day weekend 2027. SCF has looked at other location s but there are not 
any locations that give downtown Sterling the same effect and energy.  
 
Commissioner Santomaso expressed se rious concerns about kids going into the Courthouse restrooms to change 
clothing and the floors getting extremely wet and slick. Sterling has an older population. It is not very safe for them 
to walk into the restrooms onto a wet floor. The janitorial staff does not have time to clean up water all the time in 
the restrooms. He suggested a 5 p.m. start time for the splash pad and the restrooms be closed.   
 
Mr. Nichols and Mr. Stone explained that the Centaura Mall and the Union Station splash pads did not have exterior 
restrooms readily available except for those restrooms inside Union Station. The restroom problem could be 
addressed with programming and closing the restrooms when it’s going to be busy. The kids aren’t going to be 
submerged; they are mostly getting their feet wet.  
 
Commissioner Yahn said that he has taken his grandchildren to the splash pad  in San Antonio and there were no 
restrooms available, they were just running around, and they didn’t get that wet. There was another one in 
downtown Cheyenne, with no recognizable restrooms nearby, just kids playing while their parents sat and watched. 
Another he saw was in Omaha this summer was similar. They bring a lot of people to the area with lots of kids 
playing.

DEPARTMENT OF LOCAL AFFAIRS ( DOLA) – Greg Etl, Northeast Regional Manager for the Colorado 
Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) met with the Board to discuss Sage Pointe Metro District. He suggested that 
the Board get in touch  with History Colorado and find out what their limitations are with the Courthouse Square 
before looking at the splash pad. He will try to find out who a contact might be and let the Board know.  
 
Mr. Etl also wanted to let the Board know that DOLA has a grant round that opened yesterday. He stated that Logan 
County received about $23,197 of Federal Mineral Leasing ( FML) funding last year. He explained that  even at 
$23,000 of FML, the county can still apply for a grant from DOLA. At most, the county would lose the $23,000. 
 
If the county wants to apply for a grant for the bridges that are out, he asked that the Board let him know and he 
would send an application. The closing date is December 2. Commissioner Yahn asked if bridge design could be 
funded as the Board is looking to put a new deck on the bridge on CR 26. Mr. Etl said bridge design can be funded; 
however; DOLA always wants counties to look at other funding first, such as off-road bridge funding. It helps 
DOLA to understand. If the county could build it cheaper, that’s a reason for DOLA to fund it. Then the county 
could take CDOT out of it with all their requirements.   
 
Commissioner Brownell noted that the county did get turned down last year on the off -road highway bridge fund 
for this particular bridge. Mr. Etl said that he feels that if the county applies for a DOLA grant for the design of the 
bridge and then another construction grant, it increases the chances of getting the construction money. If there were 
two similar projects and DOLA was part of the design and engineering for one and not the other, they would grant 
more money to the one that they were a part of.  
 
Commissioner Brownell asked if the design was used for bridges that span more than 20 feet that could be used in 
different locations, knowing that there will be different soil conditions, different piers, etc., could that qualify as a 
project? Mr. Etl said it would probably have to be site specific for the DOLA grant but then probably could be used 
on other sites.  
 
Mr. Etl explained that there is a $200,000 cut off between tier one and tier two grants. G rants are at least 50 /50; 
however, depending on the competition, it might be a 65/ 35 scenario, based on what the c ompetition is . He 
suggested the 50/50 match. If t he design of this work were  $4250,000 or $450,000, the county could ask for the 
$200,000 and it becomes a tier one, or it could ask for half of that tier two. For the $25,000 - $50,000 difference, 
you drive to do the presentation, wherever that's going to be, and the decision is made about a month later than the 
tier one decisions are. That's the advantage if you want to speed it up because you'll be under contract at least a 
month before the tier twos will be, and you don't have to drive over and do the presentation in person. 
 
The tier one grant is $200,000 and tier two is $201,000 and somewhere between $600,000 to $750,000. 
Commissioner Yahn asked about the $23,000 amount that Mr. Etl mentioned. He said that the state can take all  
DOLA’s severance tax and they have been taking it recently. All they have is FML money and that is the county’s 
federal mineral lease payment for the year. It may mean that the county will not get the FML payment if the county 
gets the grant.

County Attorney Alan Samber noted that the zoning regulations require standard setback for all feedlot or livestock

of the proposed operation, the number of animals to be confined, future expansion plans of the operation, drainage 
characteristics and problems, method of waste disposa l, odor potential, and proximity to an incorporated unit of 
government platted subdivision or surrounding residential units.

Commissioners. Mr. Samber said that this would justify the grant of a variance from the Planning Commission and 
then the Board of Commissioners. The 30-day posting notice would not be necessary. It should be scheduled on the 
next Planning Commission agenda, then for the Board of Commissioners’ meeting for approval of the variance for 
the setback for the pond. The variance would be requested for the 70-foot front line setback in the Ag Zone District.
