EMS BUDGET FOR 2027 – Lonnie Knudsen, EMS Battalion Chief for the Sterling Fire Department  met with 
the board to request guidance on the 2027 budget. He asked about a RETAC grant that was to pay for FTO training 
in the amount of $14,000 in 2026. The grant was not  budgeted in 2026, so he would like to move it to the 2027 
budget. He asked how this would work. The Board asked Debbie Unrein to join the meeting. Mrs. Unrein said that 
it would be best for him to include it with the city bill with the associated documentation or it can be paid for 
directly. The RETAC grant is to be used exclusively for training.  
 
Commissioner Yahn asked who would receive the grant funds. Mr. Knudsen said the training company wanted to 
know who was authorized to sign the contract. Mrs. Unrein explained that, with similar ambulance grants, the 
Commissioners typically sign the contracts, the funds are paid to the County, and the County pays the bill. Mrs. 
Unrein suggested that Mr. Knudsen add a note about the RETAC grant to the budget request. She will create a new 
revenue line in next year’s budget for the RETAC training grant. The grant covers 100% of the cost for developing 
a field training program for approximately 30 students. The program  will be opened to the RETAC, with staff 
trained locally. It is a nationally accredited training program. 
 
Mr. Knudsen also discussed creating a line item for capital expenditures for grants for any kind of capital purchases, 
whether it is an ambulance, an auto pulse, Lucas device, cardiac monitor or any other type of equipment. This year 
$35,000 was budgeted for an EMS vehicle. He suggested that if that money was left to roll over into next year and 
keep building it up until the fund was larger, that way if there was a grant award for an ambulance at the last minute 
there was a fund that could be used for the match. 
 
Mrs. Unrein said the County already budgets this way through  the Capital Expenditures Fund. The funds are 
earmarked for the Ambulance Fund, which restricts their use for other purposes. A  reserve equipment line of 
$100,000 has been budgeted each year. No revenue is budgeted for that line because it is unknown whether a grant 
will be awarded. 
 
The County last received an ambulance grant in 2025 for $127,000, along with other safety equipment. In 2024, it 
purchased an ambulance for $218,000. Mr. Knudsen said he would like to pursue the CDPHE grant with a 10% 
waiver match. If the waiver is approved, the County’s match would be 10%. He estimated that approximately 
$40,000 would be needed because the unequipped ambulance quote is $337,000. The grant application is due after 
the first of the year, and the award will not be announced until June. 
 
Mr. Knudsen said Chief Howell is reviewing the Fire and EMS budgets and considering how to split certain utility 
and facility costs in the 2027 budget. Some expenses appear in both the County’s and City’s EMS budgets, while 
other County-paid items are not paid by the City. He is working to create a budget that reflects those differences.

Mrs. Unrein said those items could be left blank because the County pays for billing services, as well as property 
and liability coverage for  the ambulance. The budget also includes lines for the Medical Director, postage, and 
materials and supplies, including oxygen. Mr. Knudsen said he is working on an oxygen cascade system that should 
be installed soon. 
 
Salary details are still being finalized. Mr. Knudsen will not know whether the community paramedic position grant 
will be funded until the RHTB grant award is announced at the end of September. He will include the position in 
the budget, and if the grant is not approved, the Board will remove it before finalizing the budget. If approved, the 
position would be funded for five  years. It is not yet known whether the grant covers benefits or only the base 
salary. 
 
Lastly, Mr. Knudsen discussed the concern of overtime. One thing that would be beneficial is to have funding for 
part-time paramedics that could work fair standby, school standbys and other events. The standby rates don’t cover 
all the overtime costs. He is looking for ways to try to reduce the overtime. Having part-time paramedics do transfers 
would create some additional revenue. Mr. Knudsen  stated that they are attempting to budget  amounts similar to 
the fire department’s reserve budget, which is around $37,000 - $40,000.   
 
Chief Howell and Mr. Knudsen have been working to get reports built to code specific activities, whether they are 
fire based or EMS based overtime and standby. They would like to have that in place soon to have accurate numbers.  
This year’s budget for overtime is $73,500; however, $92,000 has been spent already. Knudsen explained that July 
had three pay periods and then they covered the 4 th of July holiday and then the fair. That was a huge hit on the 
overtime. The rest of the year the overtime should level out. Mrs. Unrein stated that the bills are coming so late, the 
County does not have the July billing yet.    
 
Mr. Knudsen informed the Board of an issue with one of the new iPad 35 monitors. They were responding to a call 
and the monitor itself wasn’t completely locked into the latching device. The screen was shattered when it fell six 
inches. They are working on getting the screen repaired. There is not a service contract with Stryker to service the 
monitors, so they are working on figuring out what those costs are going to be to get it repaired. He thinks it could 
cost more than $5,000 to repair. Jennifer Crow can file an insurance claim with CTSI if Mr. Knudsen provides the 
make and model and serial number of the monitor and a narrative of what happened. Mr. Knudsen agreed to  
provide the information. The vendor has offered to repair the monitor in exchange for increasing the service contract 
to include the monitors which would cost approximately $6,000.    
 
The ambulances will be inspected today by State inspectors for ambulance licensing.   
 
ROAD AND BRIDGE DEPARTMENT MOTORGRADER MAGNETS , MOWER PARTS  - Mike Burri, 
Road and Bridge Department Operations Manager , met with the Board. Commissioner Santomaso asked him to 
discuss magnets to attach to motorgraders. The grader magnets are either put on behind the graders from the toolbar 
in the back or there are permanent fixed magnets that go behind the blade. They can get in the way if you are pulling 
the ditches. For the guys that are just blading, the magnets can be mounted underneath, and they can pick up a lot 
of nails and metal.   
 
Commissioner Santomaso asked him to look into it more. The cost runs from $3,000 – $8,000 each. The County 
has 19 motorgraders.  If they bought  several sets of the toolbar magnets, they could be switched out and used on 
one section at time. They mount to the toolbar and let it hang behind as they’re blading. All the loose stuff is right 
on the top, and the magnets pick it up. The magnet can be transferred from machine to machine  within about 15 
minutes. The magnets would have to be cleaned off periodically. Commissioner Yahn said he has had several 
complaints on County Road 56 east of Highway 113. Commissioner Brownell said that his neighbor called him 
about using the magnets. They’ve had a rash of flat tires in their neighborhood. The Board agreed that they would 
like to try this out.

getting parts for some of the Rhino mowers. Redland had called in parts almost a month ago and they had not even 
been shipped. Right now, the department is short on mowers instead of tractors. They have a couple of tractors

Additionally, Mr. Burri said that each one of Logan County’s mowers are different models and  different years, so 
he can’t buy two extra gear boxes because each machine is different. They would have to have seven or eight 
different gear boxes just to have one spare for every mower. If they end up getting rid of one mower, then there 
would be a $2,000 - $3,000 gear box sitting there that can’t be used. It would be nice to have a mower that they can 
get parts for quickly.    
 
Commissioner Brownell asked if the department was in good shape with the mower tractors right now. Mr. Burri

Burri asked if it was a one -year or two-year lease. Commissioner Brownell thought it was every year. Mr. Burri

more roads.   
 
Commissioner Brownell will contact 21 st Century and Wickham Tractor to inquire about leasing. Mr. Burri said 
that he would want to keep the tractors Logan County already owns, so the blade operators could mow when they
