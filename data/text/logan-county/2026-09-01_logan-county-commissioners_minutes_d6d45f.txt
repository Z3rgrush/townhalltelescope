LANDFILL SATURDAY HOURS – Landfill Supervisor Josh Klein met with the Board to review  the landfill’s 
Saturday hours. The landfill is currently open from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., and Mr. Klein proposed changing the schedule 
to 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. due to limited afternoon traffic and employee downtime. Staff have tracked Saturday usage since 
May and found that activity drops significantly after about 1 p.m. Mr. Klein also noted that many residents already 
believe the landfill closes at noon, and summer heat may further reduce afternoon visits. 
 
The landfill is typically open on Saturdays from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. during daylight saving time, from May through 
November. Mr. Klein proposed changing Saturday hours to 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. year-round, beginning when the landfill 
returns to winter hours in November. The Board agreed the change is reasonable. Mr. Klein will update the landfill 
sign and Facebook page , and Jennifer Crow will update  the website when the landfill is ready to implement the 
new hours. 
 
SPECIAL TRANSPORT PERMITS – Mike Burri and Kathy Hradecky met with the Board to discuss the Road 
and Bridge Department’s process for issuing oversize and overweight permits. Mrs. Hradecky explained that she 
currently emails an application to the transport company, checks with road supervisors for any route concerns, 
collects credit card payment, prepares the permit, and emails the completed permit back to the applicant. 
 
Although permit volume is usually low, processing can become time-consuming when she is handling other duties 
or multiple permits are requested at once. Another concern is that companies often wait until Friday afternoon to 
request permits needed for Saturday morning, or when the department does not work Fridays during the summer.  
The Board watched a video explaining how Permit Gateway automates the permitting process. 
 
Permit Gateway is free for the County, with carriers paying a service fee when purchasing permits. The system 
provides a current digital map of County roads, conditions, permanent and temporary restrictions, and related road 
information. Permit types and fees can be customized, and reports are available at any time. Fees collected on the 
County’s behalf are disbursed monthly, reducing the need for manual paperwork and spreadsheets. Permits can be 
reviewed and approved from any computer or mobile device, and maps can be updated as road conditions change. 
Using the carrier’s load information, including weight and dimensions, the system routes loads  around applicable 
barriers or restrictions. 
 
The County can work with Permit Gateway’s GIS coordinator to set road parameters,  so trucks are routed around 
bridges with weight limits, narrow roads, hazards, or other restrictions before problems occur. Updates can be made 
within hours. For example, if a weather event washes out a road, the County can contact Permit Gateway to update 
the map. The County can also add chip-sealing information, so transport companies know which roads to avoid.

Logan County charges $15 for each oversize permit, plus another $15 for overweight vehicles. Loads over 200,000 
lbs. incur an additional $5 per axle. Permit Gateway charges applicants a small service fee, and Mrs. Hradecky will 
contact the company to confirm those fees. The Board indicated support for moving forward with the permitting 
system.

NONPROFIT FRAUD - Ryan Apelbaum requested permission to speak with the Board. He stated that he is a 
private journalist and investigator with no accreditation. He is investigating mass nonprofit fraud here in Logan 
County. He stated he has some evidence that he believes if a warrant is issued can lead to more evidence. There are 
some nonprofits such as the Hope House and Cooperating Ministries. He is not saying they don’t do good things; 
they do, but they’re misappropriating funds, they’re misallocating funds, they’re misrepresenting what the money 
they’re getting from private donations, from grants, such as the  El Pomar Grant. He does not know if they’ve 
received that yet, but they’re acting very shady. When he’s asked to speak to their directors, Sean Wilson and 
Shelly, their response has been to ban him rather than to answer his questions, and he feels it’s something that really 
needs to be looked into.  
 
Commissioner Brownell said that his concerns would need to be brought before law enforcement. County Attorney 
Alan Samber said that he would need to report the fraud to the District Attorney, Travis Sides. Mr. Apelbaum also 
said there may be people within the County system involved. He does not have any names to name at the moment, 
but he asked Mr. Samber if that would be something he would look into. Mr. Apelbaum asked if he could meet 
with Mr. Samber privately instead of discussing things in a public meeting.
