CENNTENNIAL SOIL CONSERVATION DISTRICT FIRE PROTECTION GRANT  - Amber Beeson of 
with the Centennial Soil Conservation District and Byron Gillham, former Board member at Centennial met with 
the Board. Ms. Beeson told the Board that Mr. Gillham had the idea to put together this project. She worked with  
Josilyn Lutze to apply for a Resiliency Grant from the Colorado Department of Agriculture.  It was the first time 
it was offered as an option for Colorado. They were awarded $30,000 to complete this project.   
 
They would like to bury a 10,000-gallon water tank for fire suppression at County Road 70 and County Road 11, 
just north of the former Stony Butte School, on Chimney Canyon Grazing Association land. Ms. Beeson has been 
speaking with Dennis Walker, the manager, who proposed the site because a well and stock tanks are nearby. The 
proposed underground tank would provide year-round water storage that could be filled from the well and accessed 
as needed. This would ensure water is available during cold winters when water may not be accessible from North 
Sterling and would also provide the grazing association with a faster backup source if a well went down or the stock 
tanks needed water. 
 
It is a long haul for fire department tenders from a water source to the northwest corner of Logan County. It might 
help with insurance for people in the area, although there are not that many structures in that location. Commissioner 
Brownell mentioned that the Peetz Fire Protection District probably has to file the NFPA form every year. Ms. 
Beeson said she could ask Steve Schumacher who is the Fire Chief, who also used to be Centennial Board President 
if it would affect the insurance rates.   
 
Mr. Gillham first contacted Steve Schumacher to ask whether the fire district could place  a water supply in that 
area. He later suggested meeting with all fire chiefs in the Centennial Soil Conservation District to identify gaps in 
their coverage. The Fleming Fire Department would cover the southeast corner of Logan County, while  Crook 
Rural Fire Department would cover the northeast part of the county toward Jumbo Reservoir. 
 
Underground tanks were recommended because at North Sterling’s south end there is a 20,000-gallon underground 
tank, and the Elks Campground has a 10,000-gallon tank. These fires often occur in the middle of winter, when 
stock tanks may be frozen or drained, making an underground water supply necessary. An above-ground tank could 
also be vulnerable to fire. Fire trucks are equipped with suction to draw water from the tanks.  
  
County Road 11 is not well maintained probably because there are not any residents on that section of the road. 
Ms. Beeson requested that the County maintain that section to include snow removal and also asked if the County 
could prepare a gravel pad in the location where the underground storage tank will be placed. She also asked if 
County personnel and equipment could dig the hole to bury the tank, but the Board told her that it would be better 
if Centennial Soil Conservation District would get bids for that as they do not like to get involved in something that 
private enterprise can do.   
 
Ms. Beeson would like to work with the grazing association to fence the tank area off so that it does not get driven

SAGE POINTE METRO DISTRICT - Heather Gage, James Thatcher, Collin Mielke and  Angela Kana -
Veydovec (via Zoom) met with the Board to discuss issues of the Sage Pointe Metro District. Ms. Kana-Veydovec, 
engineer for Sage Pointe gave a slide presentation on the creation of the district and some of the issues that the 
district is facing.  
 
Amazon was built out in the Sage Pointe area and does not recognize the district. The goal is to identify where the 
County could partner with Sage Pointe to help move forward. Amazon has been operating for a little over two years 
and has not been participating in the district’s wastewater utility service.  
 
Sage Pointe inherited a significant wastewater compliance challenge. Many of the challenges predate the district 
and predate the HOA, when they were first issued their enforcement notice of violations of this order from the 
Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment  (CDPHE), dating back to the early 1990s. Since then, 
there's been approximately 170 discharge violations that led up to their cease-and-desist order. In response to that, 
they moved quickly to procuring a Cloacina unit referred to as the “rental unit” which was ins talled in March or 
April of 2024. The board acted quickly to stabilize treatment. This rental unit allowed them the time to develop a 
permanent solution, but it was never intended to be the long-term solution. This rental unit is built to be temporary 
in nature. For that reason, a lot of wastewater manufacturers will build a mini pilot scale of treatment facilities that 
they end up renting out. These are not meant to be used for more than three to five years. The district is trying to 
move from paying rent for this temporary unit to owning a permanent unit for the wastewater treatment facility. 
 
This is dependent on a sustainable revenue base and a workable financing structure. Sage Point e customers are 
connected to the district system, and currently they are the ones carrying the ongoing costs for operations, 
maintenance, and regulations. Amazon, however, is operating differently. Even though they reside within the 
service area of the district, it is the district’s understanding that water is being hauled in from the city to a cistern, 
and then the wastewater that's being produced out of the facility is then being hauled out. 
 
Amazon has been in operation for more than a year, which is beyond the 90 days outlined in the district's rules and 
regs. T hey're actively operating in violation of those rules and regs by hauling in water and hauling out the 
wastewater rather than connecting to Sage Point e’s systems. They have ignored repeated outreach from Heather  
Gage, Co llin Mielke and  Ms. Kana -Veydovec for nearly a year. Originally, Amazon and their engineer were 
working towards connection, but when the engineer departed or parted ways with Amazon, communication ceased. 
This is a concern for the district, as one significant commercial user is not connected. With a small customer base, 
every user matters, especially a larger commercial user.   
 
This is why the Amazon issue and how the financing issue for the long-term solution of are tied and interconnected.  
Ms. Kana-Veydovec showed the Board the district’s expenses and revenue at the time of the formation  of the 
district. Analysis was developed by the Rural Community Association Corporation, RCAC, which is a nonprofit 
organization that assists small utilities and small communities. The rates were determined where they would need 
to be set to support what’s currently happening and what was anticipated for long-term treatment. This is where the 
$690 per month for residential, and $1,600 per month for commercial users’ rate was developed.  
 
The rates were adopted last November, and have been in place since then, as well as the 94 mills for their mill levy.    
The Amazon issue is important because the effect  is measurable. By Amazon not connecting, cost s shift to the 
existing residential customer base that resides within Sage Point e, of approximately $20,000 per year. That's 
equivalent to $20 per month per tap. It also raises a precedent question, whether large commercial properties within 
the district can routinely avoid participation in the district’s public utility system, those fixed costs continue to be 
pushed onto a very small group of residential owners and other commercial customers.

The district has contacted over 24 lenders. It's been very tough because they are a young district with their credit
