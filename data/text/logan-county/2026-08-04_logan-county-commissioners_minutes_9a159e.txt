LOGAN COUNTY HUMANE SOCIETY – Terry Dowis of the Logan County Humane Society thanked the 
Board for its generous donation of the fairgrounds at no charge for the recent s pay and neuter clinic. He reported 
that the clinic was a “smashing success,” with more than  236 surgeries performed, helping prevent thousands of 
unwanted litters in the future. He also thanked Rick, Shawna, and the fairgrounds staff, noting that the event went 
very well. 
 
IGNITE ATHLETICS USE OF MITCHEK EVENT CENTER  – Meleesa Calderon, Emma Schell, and 
Christine Baseggio of Ignite Athletics met with the Board to request a waiver of the Mitchek Event Center fees for 
a fundraising bingo night scheduled for October 16. They said the fundraiser would help cover travel competition 
costs, allowing athletes to participate without placing the financial burden on families. All proceeds will go toward 
competition expenses. The Mitchek Event Center has been reserved, and the group expects approximately 150 to 
200 attendees. Ignite Athletics is a competitive cheerleading program that has grown from 52 students last year to 
nearly 85 this year. The group has students from Sterling, Sidney, Brush, and Fort Morgan, and has expanded from 
four teams last year to six teams this year, with participants ranging in age from 4 to 19. 
 
The group has previously competed in Sterling and holds showcases at NJC, including an end-of-season showcase 
in March or April that is open to the community. Ignite Athletics and Melissa’s School of Dance also partner with 
Northeast Dance Academy. Some athletes have performed halftime shows at NJC after participating in clinics with 
the NJC cheerleading program. This year , the group plans to compete  in seven or eight competitions across 
Colorado and hopes to attend a competition in Las Vegas if it receives a bid. Last year, one team earned a bid to 
compete in Florida but was unable to attend due to lack of funding. 
 
The Board approved the fee waiver. Commissioner Brownell told the group that , although not yet implemented,  
the Board may eventually need to charge some nonprofit groups a small fee to help cover  wear and tear, utilities, 
and supplies. 
 
SUMMIT CARBON SOLUTIONS – Matt Bright of Summit Carbon Solutions met with the Board to discuss a 
proposed carbon sequestration pipeline that would run from Minnesota, Iowa, and Nebraska to Wyoming, where 
the carbon would be sequestered north of Cheyenne. Mr. Bright, who serves as  the public relations manager for 
this area is from Kimball, Nebraska.  
 
The company hopes to establish as much of the route as possible voluntarily by offering landowner incentives. Mr. 
Bright said larger voluntary participation would result in higher annual payments from the company. He was unsure 
whether landowner outreach had already begun in the area but wanted to inform the Commissioners in advance in 
 
 
 
2 
case they receive calls. The proposed 24- inch pipeline would run parallel to the T allgrass pipeline and include 
compressor stations along the route; however, their exact locations will not be known until the route is finalized. 
He said the project is in the early stages, with surveys beginning and construction not expected until mid- to late 
2027. 
 
Commissioner Yahn mentioned that the County just adopted new fees for pipelines and new setbacks for 
compressor stations.   
 
Commissioner Santomaso asked what federal regs govern the pipeline. Mr. Bright answered FIMSO 195 is what 
the pipeline must  be built to.  He said that carbon pipelines are kind of an oddity because they’re generally not 
state regulated, so it is more difficult.   
 
Commissioner Brownell asked if there were restrictions on how close the pipeline can be to populated areas.  Mr. 
Bright felt sure that they wanted the pipeline as far away from populated areas as possible . In those areas, it is a 
smaller pipeline, probably an 8-inch line coming from the Ethanol plant.   
 
Mr. Bright explained that the main goal is to produce sustainable aviation fuel and create another market to help 
address the surplus of corn. He said that establishing an additional market could help farmers by increasing corn 
prices. He noted that the project is not solely about carbon sequestration or CO2 storage, as captured carbon may 
also be used for enhanced oil recovery and other industri al purposes, rather than only being sequestered 
underground in Wyoming.
