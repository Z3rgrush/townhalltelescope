Discussion 1: Revisiting Incentives 
Council and BOZAR previously reviewed incentive concepts for the Belleview, Sixth Street, and 
Residential Small Lot districts, which have since been further analyzed based on the feedback 
received. At earlier work sessions, staff presented initial incentive structures for Belleview and 
Sixth intended to encourage mixed use development and additional deed restricted units. Based on 
Council’s feedback, particularly the desire to further shift incentives toward deed restricted 
commercial space, staff revisited and refined each incentive to better align with policy goals. 
In reassessing these incentives, staff evaluated how to close the financial feasibility gap that 
emerged in the original proposals. The earlier versions did not fully “pencil out” for a typical 
project, even with the incentives applied. For this round, staff modeled ways to make the 
incentives more realistic for new development while still ensuring that the community benefit 
(particularly deed restricted commercial space) remains meaningful and central. The updated 
recommendations reflect that balancing act: maintaining or increasing the community benefit, 
while adjusting the incentive packages so that they are more viable for property owners and 
developers to utilize. 
Staff is seeking feedback to confirm the direction for the following three zone districts: 
Belleview Incentive 
Staff revisited the Belleview incentive structure with a focus on increasing the amount of deed 
restricted commercial space while improving the overall financial feasibility of a project. Because 
commercial space is costly to construct and difficult to finance, staff tested different configurations 
and determined that requiring 15% deed restricted commercial space (or a minimum of 500 square 
feet (which equates to closer to 25% for a smaller lot development) represents the minimum 
threshold that still allows a project to reasonably pencil out. Further modeling showed that whether 
the residential requirement was fully market rate or included one deed restricted residential unit, 
the impact on feasibility was negligible.  
Recommendation: As a result, staff recommends an incentive package that includes 15% deed 
restricted commercial space and one deed restricted residential unit (in exchange for market 
rate residential and a 20% parking reduction). Please see the attached graphics depicting this 
recommended incentive compared to the proposed baseline.  
Given existing on street parking constraints along Belleview (30 on-street spaces compared to 175 
off-street), staff does not recommend reducing the parking requirement for market rate residential 
below the currently proposed 20% reduction. Maintaining this baseline helps ensure that parking 
demand does not exceed what the corridor can support but could be revisited in the future.  
Finally, staff notes that the feasibility of deed restricted commercial space may still require patient 
capital or potential gap filling by the Town, as it is a new concept and commercial construction 
costs are very challenging in today’s market. Staff recommends implementing this approach for 2–
5 years, monitoring outcomes, and returning with any recommended adjustments.

Council previously expressed interest in increasing the FAR incentive along Sixth Street as 
reducing parking was viewed as a worthwhile tradeoff for more community-serving space. In 
response, staff tested an FAR increase to 1.5, which corresponds to an approximate 40% reduction 
in parking requirements. Staff supports this direction given Sixth Street’s walkability, transit

FAR bonus and parking reduction. This approach aligns with the community’s desire to support 
deed restricted commercial opportunities while maintaining a feasible path for project 
development. The Sixth Street incentive had a positive result for financial feasibility, while 
Belleview was on the bubble. As with Belleview, staff recommends trying this incentive package 
and evaluating its use over time.

Re
sidential Small Lot District Height Incentive 
The incentive in the Residential Small Lot District is designed to support the replacement of an 
existing mobile home with either a modular home or a stick built home in exchange for a deed 
restriction. The deed restriction is proposed in the 5-year Housing Strategy to be structured as 
owner occupied housing that prohibits ownership of other residential property within Gunnison 
County, or as long term rental housing for members of the local workforce. As with all incentives 
in the zoning update, participation is voluntary and comes with meaningful community benefit 
requirements. 
During earlier work sessions, there was mixed feedback on whether a second story option should 
be included to make the incentive more appealing. Staff reiterated that this is an incentive (not a 
by-right entitlement) and that property owners may choose not to pursue it. Given the substantial 
costs of replacing a mobile home with a modular or stick built home, additional “carrots” can help 
broaden participation and increase deed restricted housing supply in this historically naturally 
affordable neighborhood. 
Council discussed whether the incentive should allow a full second story up to 24 feet or whether it 
should instead be limited to approximately 20 feet to accommodate only a loft. To address these 
questions, staff conducted a shading analysis (graphic below) and researched available two-story 
modular options. Most modular home products reviewed ranged from 22 to 24 feet above grade, 
making a full 24-foot allowance more practical and financially viable than constructing a partial 
loft through stick-built methods.

Di
scussion 2: Updates to Sections Not Previously Reviewed 
This section provides a high-level overview of a few updates that have not yet been discussed in 
detail but are important in the zoning code update for modernization, clarity, legality, and ease of 
administration. Staff will continue finalizing the draft of these sections and will discuss the 
technical details of FAR further with BOZAR at their May meeting. Staff is seeking feedback and 
questions on these proposed updates: 
Signs 
The sign regulations are being reorganized and updated for legal compliance, including content 
neutrality and other constitutional considerations. While the core standards are not materially 
changing, the revised structure is clearer, easier to administer, and more consistent with modern 
legal requirements. 
Only Substantive Proposed Changes: The only change being considered relates to digital marquee 
signs. Staff held a focus group with the Crested Butte Community School, Center for the Arts, and 
the Majestic Theatre to unpack feedback about the challenges of maintaining traditional marquee 
signs and the limitations they face in sharing timely information with the community. Based on 
this feedback, staff recommends allowing digital marquee signs for governmental uses to improve 
communication and reduce maintenance burdens. At the same time, to preserve Crested Butte’s 
authentic character and streetscape, the staff proposes continuing to require nondigital marquee 
signs for all other uses. 
The revision also shifts sign permit review to an administrative process, with appeals routed to 
BOZAR rather than requiring BOZAR Chair sign off for all sign permits, improving clarity and 
predictability. 
Lighting 
The lighting section is being modernized to clarify terminology and maintain alignment with dark 
sky best practices. Staff will continue exploring the potential “red light” concept brought up at the 
May 4 Council meeting and return to Council with more information at a later date.  
Only Substantive Proposed Change: Staff proposes simplifying holiday lighting by establishing 
one end date for both residential and commercial holiday lights at the end of ski season (currently 
residential is January 15 and Commercial is April 15), and introducing a time-of-day policy of 
having all lights off by 11 p.m.   This limitation would not apply to the Town’s wreaths on the 
light poles, but could apply to all other Town lighting displays. 
Floor Area Ratio (FAR) 
Staff is proposing a more consistent and easier to administer FAR definition to replace the current 
system, which relies on multiple cutting planes that are difficult to apply and have unintentionally 
created an opportunity for a “free third story” in many residential projects. The revised approach 
shifts to the IBC definition of floor area, which significantly improves clarity and consistency. 
Under this method, floor area is measured from the interior of the wall, enabling thicker walls and 
13
bet
ter insulation, which is an important benefit given current building and energy efficiency codes. 
The proposal also provides a reduction for front porch areas, complementing the proposed 
allowance for porch encroachments into the front setback and helping to encourage a pedestrian-
oriented scale, reduction of the perceived mass of structures, and promoting social interaction.  
The proposed revisions would also exempt from FAR areas such as window well covers and 
covers for mechanical equipment, such as heat pumps, if proposed at a size that is consistent with 
the code requirement.   
Staff tested the revised definition across several development types in town, and results show no 
meaningful change in total building size or scale. The update eliminates the third-story loophole 
and creates a simpler, clearer, and more predictable administrative framework. Additionally, the 
code update includes the proposed FAR incentives of not counting garage space or an ADU, as 
well as allowing a basement for providing a deed restricted unit or ADU remain. A supporting 
graphic is in development and will accompany the version presented at the BOZAR meeting. 
Discussion 3: Subdivision Approach 
Staff and the Town Attorney have been working to simplify and modernize the subdivision process 
while supporting the Town’s housing goals, particularly the creation and use of deed restricted 
microlots or condo-ed ADUs. The proposed framework organizes subdivision into three tiers, each 
calibrated to the scale and impact of the request, with clearer standards, more predictable timelines, 
and rightsized application requirements. 
Staff is seeking feedback and questions on this proposed framework. 
1. Exempt Subdivisions
Exempt subdivisions would include:
• Condominiumization of deed restricted property (for example a single family home and an ADU)
(dividing a building into individual airspace units)
• Creation of a microlot, whether on a vacant lot, a lot with a detached structure, or for the purpose
of dividing an attached two-unit structure into two conveyable units
For exempt microlot subdivisions, one resulting unit or lot would be required to carry a deed 
restriction, consistent with the Residential Small Lot District requirements (e.g., owner occupied 
with a prohibition on ownership of other residential property within Gunnison County, or long 
term rental to a local workforce household). 
Exempt subdivisions would be approved administratively by staff, with appeals routed to BOZAR. 
Application requirements would be streamlined compared to Minor and Major subdivisions. 
2. Minor Subdivisions
Minor subdivisions would include the division of two or more lots that do not qualify as exempt
subdivisions but fall below a yet to be established threshold for Major subdivisions, as well as:

• Vacations
• Lot combinations

Major subdivisions would include any division that:
• Exceeds the lot threshold for Minor subdivisions, or
• Is determined by the Community Development Director to have cumulative impacts significant

Major subdivisions would involve a more rigorous review process, beginning with a staff 
recommendation, followed by BOZAR review, with appeals going to Town Council. These 
applications would require the most robust submittal materials.

Thru:   Dara MacDonald, Town Manager 
 
From: Lynelle Stanford, Town Clerk 
 
Subject:   Renewal of Pitaboy Inc DBA Pitas is Paradise’s Liquor License Expiring May 18, 
2026, Due to Violation of Colorado Liquor and Beer Code. 
 
Date: April 24, 2026   
 
 
 
Background: 
 
Chapter 6, Article 2 of the Town Code establishes the provisions and procedures governing liquor 
licensing within the Town.  Section 6-2-30 specifically outlines the process for renewal 
applications.  The Town Clerk is authorized to review and approve liquor license renewals on 
behalf of the Town Council, provided that no violations of the Colorado Liquor or Beer Code have 
occurred during the preceding year. 
 
Summary: 
 
The Town Clerk received, via email on April 22, 2026, an executed stipulation from the Colorado 
Liquor Enforcement Division concerning Pitaboy Inc., doing business as Pitas in Paradise, related 
to a liquor code violation that occurred on December 18, 2025. 
 
On that date, the Colorado Liquor and Tobacco Enforcement Division conducted compliance 
check operations within the Town using an eighteen-year-old underage operative. During the 
operation, the licensee permitted the sale, service, or provision of an alcoholic beverage, 
specifically, one 16-ounce paper cup of Bud Light, to the underage operative. The beverage was 
provided for takeout in an unsealed container with a lid featuring a straw opening and without the 
required warning label. 
 
The stipulation, included in the packet, was signed by the owner of Pitaboy Inc., Andrew Stichter, 
on April 13, 2026.  As a result of the stipulation Pitas in Paradise agreed to a four-day liquor 
license suspension, from Friday, May 1, 2026, through Monday, May 4, 2026.  Please note that 
May 18, 2026, is the date of expiration for said liquor license. 
 
Recommendation:  
 
32
Staff recommends approval of the liquor license renewal for Pitaboy Inc DBA Pitas in Paradise, 
with the condition that the licensee maintain full compliance with all applicable state and local 
liquor laws. Staff further recommends that any future violations may be grounds for additional 
enforcement action, including potential suspension or revocation. 
 
Recommended Motion: 
 
Motion to direct Staff to renew Pitas in Paradise’s liquor license for the period of May 18, 2026, 
through May 18, 2027.

direction while remaining financially feasible for both current and future tenants, and keeps rates 
fair and reasonable given that some existing leases are still at lower rates. 
 
A standard 5-year lease without an automatic renewal is in the Town’s best interest. 
 
Climate Impact:  None

Summary:  
Staff have received several community requests from ADU residents and same‑owner multi‑unit 
properties to share trash service. In response, staff propose updating Chapter 13 of the Town Code 
to allow these units to voluntarily combine service, with no financial impact to the Town, and ask 
Council for direction on moving forward. 
Background and Discussion 
In 2025, the Town implemented the Save as You Throw (SAYT) program, allowing households 
served under the Town’s single hauler contract to choose 32-, 64-, or 96‑gallon trash bins at 
decreasing rates. All customers receive a standard 96‑gallon recycling bin, and recycling fees are 
included in the total Waste Management (WM) rate. 
Relevant code sections are as follows: 
Sec 13-5-10. Definitions. 
Residential property means a single unit providing complete, independent living facilities for one 
or more persons, including permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating, cooking, and 
sanitation. For purposes of this Article, a residential property may consist of a single or multiple 
building property containing no more than seven dwelling units. Further, a residential property 
may be part of a building with both residential and commercial uses. 
Section 13-5-20. Compulsory refuse collection. 
(a) Every owner or occupant of a premises within the Town shall provide for the collection and 
disposal of refuse in accordance with the regulations of this Article 
(e) The Town shall have the authority to choose the proper pick-up location and type of refuse 
container for residential properties, be it an individual container or dumpster 
Section 13-5-40. Refuse containers and precollection practices. 
35
(a) All residential properties within the Town shall select at least one (1) of the refuse 
container sizes set forth by the Town Council in the fee schedule 
The interpretation of this code section for many years has been that each residential unit must have 
their own trash service.  In effect this means that we have required each home on a property 
(including ADUs) to have their own trash and recycling bins and to be charged for those. 
Trash service is conducted by the Town through a contract with Waste Management.  Both Town 
staff and WM are comfortable with the suggestion that properties with up to 7 units under single 
ownership may combine services for every two units.  This could both provide for a more 
affordable option for residents and encourage participation in the composting program to help 
households accommodate a shared bin.  For households that opt to combine bins, it also frees up 
the space required on the property for an additional trash and recycling bin. Staff would like to 
emphasize that this proposed code update is in response to community requests, and sharing trash 
services is voluntary. If units do not share a trash service, the status quo remains and each would 
receive its own trash and recycling bins.  
 
According to the Town’s GIS database, there are 179 units and 118 ADUs that could decline trash 
service and share with another unit or primary residence (see attachment 1). 
Staff also recommends that the minimum bin size when combining would be a 64-gallon bin to 
ensure sufficient capacity for two households.  It should also be noted that recycling is a 
companion service to trash service, so 96-gallon recycling bins would also be shared along with 
trash. 
There will be some complications for the Town’s utility billing and assigning the responsible 
accounts. This transition and initial enrollment will require some additional attention from both the 
Town and WM staff. 
 
Previous Council Action 
Council adopted Ordinance No. 7, Series 2025 on August 18, 2025, establishing the SAYT 
program and codifying it in Chapter 13, Article 5.

Subject:    Lower Verzuh Amended Proposal Referral Letter 
 
Summary: Gunnison County is seeking a referral comment letter on the amended Lower Verzuh 
proposal, due by May 19. A draft comment letter is attached for Town Council consideration. 
Previous Council Action:  
• March 17, 2025: Town Council submitted a letter to the Gunnison County Board of 
County Commissioners expressing concerns about the Lower Verzuh proposal. The letter 
emphasized the importance of completing the Highway 135 Corridor Plan prior to 
approving major new development and requested a temporary moratorium on new 
subdivisions within the corridor until that plan is complete. 
• June 16, 2025: Town Council approved and submitted a referral letter to Gunnison County 
reiterating the need for the corridor plan and raising concerns related to housing 
affordability and community integration, transportation connectivity, recreation mitigation, 
and utility capacity. That letter is attached for reference.  
• January 5, 2026: Council submitted two letters (one from Council perspective and one 
from Staff perspective) expressing concern about the Lower Verzuh proposal ahead of its 
anticipated public hearing reiterating the same themes as June 16. The applicant 
subsequently withdrew their proposal from the public hearing and has since submitted an 
amended application.  
Background: The Lower Verzuh property is located approximately three miles south of the Town 
of Crested Butte along Highway 135. The amended proposal: 
• Reduces the residential unit count from 557 residential units (301 single family residential 
units and 256 “optional” ADUs) to 433 residential units  
• Increases the Essential Housing component from 46 Essential Housing lots (15%) to 179 
constructed Essential Housing units (41%).  
The attached memo from Gunnison County provides more history on the proposal iterations as 
well as the referral comment request. Additionally, attached are the updated application materials.  
 
 
38
 
Discussion: Gunnison County is the reviewing authority for the Lower Verzuh proposal. Given the 
property’s location within the Town’s three-mile planning area and the scale of potential impacts 
on the Town and the broader North Valley, staff recommends submitting the attached comment 
letter for consideration by the Planning Commission and the Board of County Commissioners. 
The draft letter is framed in alignment with the intent of the sketch plan process, identifying key 
items that warrant more detailed analysis should the application advance. While the applicant’s 
revisions demonstrate meaningful incorporation of feedback from the public and the Planning 
Commission, several questions remain regarding the essential housing component, including the 
feasibility and implementation of the proposed unit mix. In addition, staff continues to have 
significant concerns about transportation, connectivity, and overall access, particularly given the 
project’s location and the internal layout of the amended proposal.  
Lastly, consistent with previous correspondence, the letter reiterates the importance of establishing 
a more comprehensive planning framework for the North Valley Corridor to fully understand and 
mitigate cumulative development impacts. Staff is looking forward to the kick off of the Corridor 
Plan process on May 8.

Background: The Community Plan laid out a clear vision for strengthening the community-
serving spaces and housing that keep Crested Butte livable, functional, and rooted in community. 
Implementation shifted from planning to action in fall 2025 through three interconnected efforts:  
1. Zoning Code Update: The zoning code update is a holistic update to the Town’s 
development regulations to ensure the code incorporates the CP’s recommendations, 
incorporates state model standards, and aligns with the forthcoming Design Standards 
Update (a major outcome of the Historic Preservation Plan). The Zoning Code has gone 
through extensive community outreach since fall 2025 and three joint work sessions with

2026 and adoption targeted by December 2026.

expanding community housing, yet demand continues to outpace supply and the challenges 
remain complex. The CP calls for a more diverse mix of housing types, better integration 
across neighborhoods, and deeper affordability of workforce housing. The 5-Year Housing

Subject:    Consideration of Appointments for Community Outreach and Education 
Subcommittee 
 
 
 
I am requesting that the Town Council consider appointing members for a Community Outreach and 
Education Subcommittee during its meeting on May 18th.  The purpose of this subcommittee will be 
to guide and support work related to communications, public outreach, and potential ballot question 
development.  Per open meetings requirements, no more than two members of the Town Council 
may serve on this subcommittee. 
 
To support this effort, I have contacted Miles Graham with GSBM to provide a proposal for a 
communications plan.  This plan is expected to help distill and communicate key information 
regarding the Town’s financial condition, the connection between financial resources and municipal 
services, upcoming capital and operational needs, prior cost-control efforts, options for revenue 
generation, and the broader challenge of maintaining community affordability. 
 
In addition, I have reached out to Keating Research and Magellan Strategies for proposals to conduct 
polling of the electorate.  Their work would help inform both the communications approach and any 
potential future ballot measure. 
 
Because these efforts will require timely review, input, and coordination, the subcommittee will need 
to meet more frequently than the regular Council meeting schedule allows.  Establishing this 
subcommittee now will help ensure the Town remains on track to develop clear, accurate, and 
effective communication materials for the community.
