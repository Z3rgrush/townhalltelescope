Subject:     Resolution No. 14, Series 2026 - A Resolution of the Crested Butte Town 
Council Authorizing the Town Manager to Sign an Intergovernmental 
Agreement with the Gunnison County Weed District Regarding Undesirable 
Plant Management. 
 
 
 
Background: 
Since 1999 the Town of Crested Butte has entered into an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) for 
the purpose of undesirable plant management with Gunnison County, Hinsdale County, Saguache 
County, the City of Gunnison, and the Town of Pitkin.  It is to the mutual advantage of each of 
these entities to cooperate with one another to meet the obligations imposed by the Colorado 
Noxious Weed Act CRS 35-5.5-101 et seq. (the Act).  This IGA appoints the Gunnison Watershed 
Weed Commission to be the local authority as required by the Act for the Gunnison River Basin 
Watershed.  This IGA promotes the Town of Crested Butte Noxious Weed Management Plan and 
allows for the management of undesirable plants within the municipality as required by Crested 
Butte Town Code Section 7-3-110 et seq. 4. 
 
Summary: 
Gunnison County will charge a rate of $117.50/hour for noxious weed management, including all 
associated labor, equipment, materials, and administration.  Gunnison County will spend 110 hours 
in 2026 managing weeds in the Town of Crested Butte for a total of $12,913.00.  This has been 
budgeted for and is split between the Open Space Fund, Parks, Rec and Trails Fund, and the Street 
and Alley Fund. 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends approving Resolution No. 14, Series 2026 thereby authorizing the Town 
Manager to sign the Intergovernmental Agreement with the Gunnison County Weed District 
Regarding Undesirable Plant Management.

Subject:   Resolution 15, Series 2026 A Resolution of the Town of Crested Butte Town 
Council Authorizing the Purchase of 810 Gothic, Crested Butte, Colorado and  
Authorizing the Town Manager to Execute All Documents Related to the Purchase 
 
 
Summary 
Staff seek approval from the Town Council to purchase 810 Gothic, a deed restricted unit, with 
the intent update to the new form of deed restriction and add income and asset limits and to grant 
the Town the first right of refusal on future sales.  Staff anticipate that Town will take ownership 
of the unit in late July or Early August and intends to sell it by lottery with the revised deed 
restriction shortly thereafter.  Staff will return to the Council to request approval for the sale of 
the unit and present the income restriction.    
 
Previous Council Action: Over the past year, Council has approved the purchase of five units 
with the purpose of updating to the current form of deed restriction to improve administrative 
efficiency, include the Town’s first right of refusal on future sales, and address the exclusion of 
income and asset limits in the deed restriction. 
 
Background 
Based on Town records, previous Staff carefully crafted sales prices based on target AMIs for 
the 30 homes that were constructed and sold by Bywater Construction in 2018-2020. The 
original deed restriction on these homes includes an appreciation cap based on those sales prices 
as well as the requirement that one individual on the deed be a full-time participant in the local 
workforce and the standard prohibition on the ownership of other improved residential real 
estate. However, the restriction does not include an income restriction or a limit on the assets of 
potential buyers. The ongoing expectation is that Town will step in to update the remaining 
Bywater deed restrictions with then current Town policy objectives when their owners are ready 
to sell. 
 
Discussion: Purchasing 810 Gothic enables the Town to release the current deed restriction and 
record a new restriction that targets household incomes that are not served by the housing market 
and establishes asset limits, which ensures that those with high net value do not qualify to 
purchase the home. Additionally, the new deed restriction will grant the Town with a first right 
of refusal to purchase the unit and improve administrative efficiency. 
 
Staff uses a reverse affordability calculation, which converts the maximum allowable sales price 
into an affordable monthly housing cost and then converts that cost into an income and 
9
 
 
corresponding asset limit using standardized assumptions on the loan for the home. A cushion is 
included in the interest rate to help protect against swings that could render the price of the home 
unaffordable at the designated income limit. 
 
Pending the Council’s approval, staff will announce the property on the Town’s “Available 
Properties” webpage, through Town social media accounts, in the CB News, and through the 
Gunnison County Housing Authority’s homeownership interest list.  The home will be sold via 
lottery in accordance with the Town’s Affordable Housing Guidelines at the maximum allowable 
sales price of approximately $265,000.  
 
Climate Impact: N/A 
 
Financial Impact: Staff anticipate that the costs to purchase and resell the property will be 
approximately $4,000 for standard closing costs excluding the Real Estate Transfer Tax which is 
not required for a Town transfer of a deed-restricted property. 
 
Legal Review: Town legal counsel is engaged to support staff in this effort. 
 
Recommendation: For a council member to make a motion, followed by a second, to purchase 
810 Gothic and authorize the Town Manager to execute all documents related to the sale. 
 
Proposed Motion: A Council member should make a “make a motion to purchase 810 Gothic 
and authorize the Town Manager to execute all documents related to the sale” followed by a 
second and roll call vote.

Proclamation in Honor of Principal Sally 
Hensley 
Whereas, Sally Hensley arrived in Crested Butte in the summer of 
1987, fresh from earning her degree in Child Study and Human 
Development from the prestigious Elliott Pearson School of Child 
Study at Tufts University in Boston, drawn to the mountains of 
Colorado in search of a ski town where she could do the work she had known since second 
grade she was born to do - teach and nurture young children; and 
Whereas, upon arriving in Crested Butte, Sally immediately put down roots that ran 
deep, working as a ski instructor in the Town’s Learn to Ski program and volunteering at 
Stepping Stones and the Crested Butte Elementary School, establishing from her very first 
days here a lifelong commitment to the children and families of this community; and 
Whereas, Sally devoted 17 years to Stepping Stones Children's Center, the nonprofit early 
childhood organization that has served the Gunnison Valley since 1987, rising from volunteer 
to teacher to executive director, shaping the foundation of early childhood education in 
Crested Butte and providing generations of the community's youngest children a warm, 
joyful, and nurturing start in life; and 
Whereas, in 2006, Sally joined the Gunnison RE1J School District as a teacher and director 
of Lake Preschool, simultaneously pursuing a master's degree in educational leadership from 
Regis University and earning her principal's license, demonstrating the same drive for growth 
and excellence that she has always inspired in her students; and 
Whereas, Sally served as principal of Gunnison Elementary School beginning in 2011, 
extending her dedication to young learners across the entire Gunnison Valley and building 
her reputation as a thoughtful, caring, and visionary school leader; and 
Whereas, in 2013, Sally returned to her beloved Crested Butte as the first principal of 
Crested Butte Elementary School, a position she has held with distinction for more than a 
decade, guiding the school through periods of growth and change; championing the social-
emotional development and academic success of every student; and embodying the school's 
mission to ensure all learners are successful, safe, and resilient; and 
Whereas, over more than 20 years as an educator with the Gunnison Watershed School

community, building relationships with students, families, teachers, and staff that span 
generations of Crested Butte life; and

the broader community through partnerships with Mountain Roots Farm to School, Celebrate 
the Beat, the Adaptive Sports Center, the Rocky Mountain Biological Laboratories, the CB 
Mountain Heritage Museum, and the CBCS Enrichment Program, ensuring that the unique 
natural and cultural richness of this place is woven into the fabric of every child's education;

Bac
kground and Discussion:  
As outlined in the first reading memo, the Town launched the Save as You Throw (SAYT) trash 
program in 2025, giving residents a choice of 32-, 64-, or 96-gallon trash bins, with recycling 
included in the Waste Management (WM) rate. Historically, the Town has required each home on 
a property (including ADUs) to have their own trash and recycling bins and to be charged for 
those. Residential trash and recycling services are conducted by the Town through a contract with 
Waste Management. 
St
aff discussed with Council allowing properties with up to seven units under single ownership to 
share service between every two units. This option responds to community requests and remains 
fully voluntary. When units share service, the minimum trash bin size would be 64 gallons, and the 
standard 96 gallon recycling bin would also be shared. Approximately 179 units in Town could 
choose to participate.

n sharing may reduce costs for households, encourage composting to help manage volume, and 
reduce the space needed for multiple containers. Some administrative adjustments to utility billing 
17

is attached for Council’s review.

nancial Impact: 
No impact to the Town. WM charges for residential trash service are direct pass‑throughs; fewer 
accounts result in lower total billing.

behalf of the Town Council, provided that no violations of the Colorado Liquor or Beer Code have 
occurred during the preceding year.

dated October 2, 2025, concerning Gas Café LLC DBA Gas Café One Stop, related to a liquor 
code violation that occurred on September 3, 2025.

check operations within the Town using an eighteen-year-old underage operative. During the

specifically, one 8-ounce can of Twisted Tea, to the underage operative.

Tunkey, on October 9, 2025.  Under the terms of the stipulation, Gas Café LLC agreed to pay a 
$500 fine, and the three-day suspension was permanently stayed.
