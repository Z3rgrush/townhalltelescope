Staff Report 
December 15, 2025 
 
To:   Mayor Billick and Town Council 
Prepared By: Mel Yemma, AICP, Community Development Director  
Thru: Dara MacDonald, Town Manager 
Subject:    Community Spaces Strategy Check In 
 
Summary:  
This work session serves as a check in on the Community Spaces strategy, one of the three major 
implementation items of the Community Plan. At this work session, staff is seeking discussion and 
feedback on: 
1. Refining draft definitions for “community spaces,” and 
2. Shaping guiding principles to inform strategy development. 
Previous Council Action: 
The Town Council held a joint work session with BOZAR on October 6, 2025 to kick off the 
Community Spaces strategy, in conjunction with the Zoning Code Update and Housing strategy. 
Background:  
The Community Plan laid out a clear vision for strengthening the community-serving spaces and 
housing that keep Crested Butte livable, functional, and rooted in community. Implementation now 
shifts from planning to action through three interconnected efforts: the Zoning Code update, the 5-
year Community Spaces Strategy, and the 5-year Housing Strategy. 
1. Zoning Code Update: Staff, in coordination with the Town Attorney, are leading a 
comprehensive zoning code update, which includes a holistic review to ensure the code 
incorporates the CP’s recommendations, incorporates state model standards, and aligns 
with the forthcoming Design Standards Update (a major outcome of the Historic 
Preservation Plan). 
To help ensure that new requirements are both community-aligned and market-feasible, the 
Town retained Economic & Planning Systems (EPS) using the remainder of the “Strong 
Communities” grant. EPS is analyzing the financial and market implications of proposed 
zoning incentives to help structure tools that attract private investment while delivering 
community benefit. EPS is also supporting development of both the 5-year housing strategy 
(funded through Proposition 123) and the community spaces strategy, enabling these efforts 
to move forward cohesively. 
3
2 
 
Staff and EPS will return for a joint Town Council and BOZAR work session on February 3 
to review the incentives analysis and check in on the zoning code update. 
2. 5-Year Community Spaces Strategy (Tonight’s Focus): The CP underscores that Crested 
Butte’s livability relies not only on housing, but also on access to the essential services and 
gathering places that hold the community together. While the Town has decades of 
experience advancing community housing, supporting community-serving spaces is new 
territory and defining what “community spaces” or “community-serving businesses and 
non-profits” truly mean for Crested Butte is an important first step. 
The Community Spaces Strategy is intended to help chart that path. It will establish a 
working definition of community spaces, assess current strengths, weaknesses, 
opportunities, and threats, and identify where the Town can most effectively focus its 
efforts. Ultimately, this work will guide how zoning tools, programs, projects, and 
partnerships can help sustain the services and spaces most essential to a functional and 
connected community. 
3. 5-Year Community Housing Strategy: Crested Butte has made significant progress in 
expanding community housing, yet demand continues to outpace supply and the challenges 
remain complex. The CP calls for a more diverse mix of housing types, better integration 
across neighborhoods, and a stronger balance between affordability and workforce housing. 
The 5-Year Housing Strategy will assess past and current accomplishments, identify gaps 
in the community’s housing portfolio, and clarify where the Town should focus its energy 
and resources over the next five years.  
Staff and EPS will return to Town Council on January 20 for a work session dedicated to 
the Housing Strategy. 
Discussion:  
The attached memo from EPS provides an initial market assessment of Crested Butte’s commercial 
environment including its existing conditions, opportunities, and constraints. Using this assessment 
as context, the purpose of this work session is to gather Town Council feedback on two key 
components of the emerging Community Spaces Strategy: 
1. Refining draft definitions for “community spaces,” and 
2. Shaping guiding principles to inform strategy development. 
Discussion 1: Defining Community Spaces 
A central challenge in both the Community Plan and the zoning code update is defining what 
qualifies as a “community space.” The CP offers a strong starting point: “locally serving places 
that meet everyday needs and foster community connection and partnerships,” but recent 
conversations, including the joint Town Council and BOZAR session, have made clear that 
“everyday needs” span a wide spectrum in a rural mountain community. They encompass 
everything from childcare and healthcare to plumbers, electricians, arts and cultural programming, 
basic goods providers, gathering spaces, and more. 
To bring more clarity and structure to this broad category, the joint Council and BOZAR work 
session surfaced two overarching types of community spaces that can help guide both zoning and 
strategic interventions: Core Services and Community Vitality Spaces. Staff has developed the 
following refined draft definitions to support Council discussion: 
4
3 
 
Draft Community Spaces Definition: Community Spaces are locally-serving places that meet 
every day needs and help keep Crested Butte functional, livable, and connected. They include the 
core services that allow residents to live, work, and stay healthy in town, as well as the gathering 
places that spark community vitality, culture, and connection. 
• Core Services: Core Services are the essential, locally focused businesses, providers, and 
community organizations that support Crested Butte’s day-to-day functioning and 
residents’ basic needs.  
o Discussion questions to consider: 
 Is “essential” the right framing, or is there a better term (e.g., foundational, 
every day, locally serving)? 
 Should Core Services focus on daily/weekly needs rather than less frequent 
services that are still important but may be more viable regionally? 
 Should Core Services be defined by frequency of use, impact on basic 
needs, or difficulty of accessing elsewhere? 
 Should we define Core Services narrowly (healthcare, childcare, post office) 
or more broadly (grocery, laundry, auto repair, etc.)? 
 How could this draft definition be improved? 
• Community Vitality Spaces: Community Vitality Spaces are the places—formal and 
informal—that strengthen Crested Butte’s culture, creativity, and collective wellbeing. 
While not strictly “essential,” they are vital to the town’s identity and resilience. These 
spaces foster connection across generations, encourage shared experiences, and offer room 
for learning, expression, play, innovation, and celebration. 
o Discussion questions to consider: 
 Does this definition clearly distinguish vitality spaces from Core Services, 
or does it need sharper boundaries? 
 Should we focus on physical spaces only, or include programming/activities 
that bring them to life? 
 Should vitality spaces meet certain shared criteria (e.g., affordable, locally 
oriented, intergenerational)? 
 Where is the line between a community-serving space and a tourist-serving 
one, and can a space be both? 
 How could this draft definition be improved? 
Discussion 2: Guiding Principles for Strategy Development 
As the Community Spaces strategy begins to take shape, this work session is an opportunity to 
provide initial feedback on guiding principles to guide strategy development. Based on the existing 
conditions and sample strategies memo, and the joint Council and BOZAR work session, and the 
Community Plan, the following are example guiding principles to inform the discussion: 
Draft Guiding Principles 
5
4 
1. Start small and focus on opportunistic, high-impact solutions.
Zero in on a few achievable actions, seize the opportunities that present themselves, and
create meaningful, lasting benefits rather than stretching to solve every need at once.
2. Prioritize where the Town can make the greatest difference.
Given limited tools and capacity, focus resources on gaps where the free market struggles
or needs a partner. Concentrate on what the Town does best (such as public services,
stewardship, and public assets) rather than trying to solve every challenge.
3. Enable community creativity—not replace it.
Crested Butte thrives on grassroots initiative. The Town’s role is to remove barriers, seed
opportunities, and support partnerships rather than control or run every solution.
4. Align tools with balanced outcomes.
Ensure zoning, programs, and investments work together to support both Core Services and
Community Vitality Spaces, maintaining a balance between function and livability.
5. Keep solutions locally-serving and locally-grounded.
Prioritize spaces that meet year-round needs, strengthen community connection, and
support those who live and work here, rather than uses that disproportionately serve short-
term or external demand.
Discussion questions to consider: 
• Are some draft guiding principles more of a priority than others?
• Is anything missing from this list? Are there other principles the Town should consider?
• Do these draft principles feel realistic and actionable for Crested Butte’s context?
• Are these draft principles hitting the right mark for balancing Core Services, Community
Vitality Spaces, and the Town’s role?
• Are there any draft principles that might conflict with each other or need clarification?
• How well do these draft principles support long-term flexibility, learning, and adaptation?

Subject:     Approval of the 2025 Arbor Day Proclamation and Tree City USA Application for 
Certification  
   
 
 
Background:    
The Town of Crested Butte has been awarded the certification of Tree City USA for 24 years through 
the Arbor Day Foundation.  The standards for a community to be officially certified as a Tree City 
USA include: 
1. A Tree Board or Department 
2. A Community Tree Ordinance 
3. A Community Forestry Program with an Annual Budget of at Least $2 Per Capita 
4. An Arbor Day Observance and Proclamation 
 
The Town of Crested Butte has achieved these standards and recognizes the environmental and 
economic value of trees in our community.  The following people serve on the Town’s tree board: 
 
- Janna Hansen (Chair), Director of Parks, Recreation, Open Space, and Trails 
- Mike Tarantino, CO State Forester 
- Astrid Matison, Building Official 
- Josh Staab, Building Inspector 
- Jack Morgan, Parks Manager 
 
The Tree Ordinance can be found in Town code sec. 16-15-10 et seq. In 2025 the Town of Crested 
Butte spent $5.68 per capita on tree maintenance and planting and planted 6 trees in public spaces.  The 
annual Arbor Day celebration with the Crested Butte Community School 5th Graders was observed this 
year on October 10th and included education and activities for 58 students at the Crested Butte 
Community School with our local state foresters and staff.  The students learned about our local forest 
health initiatives, played tree-related games like Tree Jeopardy, learned general tree facts, and 
discussed the importance of trees.  
 
Recommendation: 
Town Staff recommends the approval of the 2025 Arbor Day Proclamation and the signing of the 2025 
Tree City USA application by the Mayor.

Memorandum 
December 15, 2025 
 
               
      
        
 
 
To:   Mayor and Town Council 
 
From: Dara MacDonald, Town Manager 
      Rob Sweeney, Interim Finance & Administrative Services Director 
 
Subject:    Adoption the Revised Town of Crested Butte Employee Handbook   
 
Date:  December 8, 2025   
 
 
Summary: 
The personnel policies of the Town are adopted by the Town Council and administered by the 
Town Manager.  The Council is being asked to consider adoption of the updated personnel policies 
contained within the Employee Handbook.   
 
Prior Council Action: 
• December 3, 2018 – Adoption of current format of the Employee Handbook (Version 1) 
• January 19, 2021 – Adoption of revisions to the Employee Handbook (Version 2) 
• December 19, 2022 – Adoption of revisions to the Employee Handbook (Version 3) 
 
Background:   
Section 2-3-30(b) of the Crested Butte Municipal Code states that, “subject to the approval of the 
Town Council, the Town Manager may from time to time promulgate such rules and regulations as 
may be necessary to implement and carry out the administrative services provided by the Town, 
including a personnel manual; provided, however, that such rules and regulations shall not be 
inconsistent herewith.” 
 
Revisions and updates are made periodically to the Handbook to: 
• Keep policies current and in compliance with employment laws and regulations; 
• Ensure the documents reflect any needed changes in the Town’s employment 
practices/procedures; and 
• Convey the handbook information in a clear and accessible manner. 
 
Discussion: 
Over the past several months, staff researched current handbook policies, Town practices that may 
not be in the Handbook and met with department heads and staff to identify areas that require 
18
language and policy adjustments.  Additionally, staff worked with the Town Attorney and outside 
human resources personnel to review changes to the Handbook to ensure compliance with federal 
and state law as well as industry best practices.   
 
Staff sought to identify areas in the Handbook where the Town could reasonably make changes to 
improve the lives of current employees, ensure fair human resource management practices, help 
attract new employees and retain our current employees.  The substantive changes to the 
Handbook are identified below.   
 
Section 2 – Nondiscrimination Policy  
Fine-tuned language throughout this section to add clarity and be consistent with existing laws.  
D. Lactation Accommodation was relocated to this Section.  
 
Section 3 – Employee Status  
A. Definitions  
• Added definitions for “At Will Employee” and “Continuous Operations 
Environment”  
• Updated Nonexempt Employee definition to acknowledge changing Marshals from 
28-day period to 14-day period.  
• Changed definition of Full time from 30 to 32 hours per week, consistent with 
health benefits.

Section 4 – Compensation Policy  
A. Compensation Administration – Added reference to performance evaluations and 
compensation plan.  
B. Paid Leave & Hours Worked – Removed and incorporated into existing Section 6.B, Hours 
Worked.  
  
Section 5: Pay Periods, Work Schedules, Rest Breaks/Meal Periods & Time Reporting  
A. Pay Periods – Added note that payroll election changes must be submitted a week in 
advance of payroll processing.  
D. Rest Breaks and Meal Periods – added this section to comply with Fair Labor Standards 
Act and Colorado Statutes  
E. Time Reporting – Removed reference to salaried nonexempt employees earning overtime 
as we do not have that situation and don’t see how that can occur given updates to position 
classifications in recent years.  
  
Section 6 – Premium Pay  
B. Hours Worked – relocated from former Section 4.B.  Added clarification that only hours 
actually worked are included in calculating overtime.  
C. Compensatory Time – Added language to try and clarify that employees eligible for comp 
time may elect to be paid for overtime earned during each pay period.  Or they may elect to 
be paid out in full at any time so long as they meet payroll processing deadlines.  
E. Call-Back Pay - Modified to address situations (usually for Utilities on-call) where tasks 
may be accomplished remotely.   
F. On-Call Time – Modified to add a ‘primary’ and ‘secondary’ on-call for utilities, one from 
each utility.  Primary will be paid $100 per weekend or holiday day during their scheduled 
19
on-call week.  Secondary on-call pay is $50 per weekend or holiday day during their 
scheduled on-call week.  Added that if the on-call duty changes from one employee to 
another (someone gets sick or called away), that the compensation is also shifted.  
G. Winter Snow Removal Pay – Clarified that snow pay is specific to streets and parks 
employees working as part of their assigned duties.  Other employees who chose to help 
with snow removal at night will be paid at 1 ½ times the blended rate per Section 6.M.  
I. Jury Duty – Removed the expectation that an employee would reimburse the Town if they 
received compensation for jury duty.  
K. Pay for Required Training/Travel Time – Minor clarifying changes made.  
L.  Seasonal Employee End of Season Bonus – Modified to note that the season should be 
established at time of hire and the rate of $1 per hour worked.  
O.  Marshal Training Time – Added an acknowledgement of pay at 1.5 times regular rate for 
officers coming in early or staying late as part of scheduled trainings (typically 
Wednesdays 2x per month).  
P. Marshal Off-Duty Assignment – New subsection to address detail assignments.  
  
Section 7 – Training & Development  
B. Industry-Based Education & Certification – Clarified that this subsection applies to job-
specific memberships to professional or trade organizations, earning job-required or 
preferred credentials and continuing education related to those certifications or credentials.  
C. Tuition Reimbursement – Similar to above, updated language to clarify difference between 
job-specific certifications/credentials and continuing education that may not be a 
requirement of an employee’s position.  This subsection includes a re-payment provision.  
D. POST Training – Added repayment expectation if Cadet fails to complete POST academy.  
 
Section 9 – Work Related Expenses  
C.  Meal Allowance – Update to reflect current practice of matching GSA per diem rates.  
  
Section 10 – Leave Policy  
A. Paid Vacation Leave –   
• Clarified that employees may only go negative on vacation up to 40 hours.  
• Added a section that recognizes hours worked for previously part-time or seasonal 
employees who move into full-time positions towards length of service.  
C. Holiday Leave –   
• Addressed that someone must be an employee at the time a holiday occurs in order to 
get the holiday (specific to employees who take a season off from regular 
employment).  
For employees in ‘continuous operations’ – generally Marshals officers and streets crews in 
the winter – the holiday is observed on the calendar day of the holiday.  
• If holiday falls on a scheduled workday for employees, the alternate day off needs to be 
taken in that pay period.  
E. Paid Sick Leave –   
• Clarified that accrual rate is tied to hours worked similar to vacation accrual.  
• Added details of sick leave accrual for part-time, seasonal and temporary employees 
consistent with State requirements  
• Updated the uses, notification and verification of sick leave sections to be consistent 
with State law.

• General clean-up throughout this section to better align with FMLA 
requirements/allowances.  
L. Paid Family & Medical Leave Insurance (FAMLI) – Included required language about the

Section 12 – Paid Parental Leave  
B. Eligibility – Noted that the employee does have to have been employed for the prior 
consecutive 12-months in order to be eligible.  
C. Amount, Time Frame, & Duration of Paid Parental Leave –   
• Added a provision that amount of leave is proportional to employee’s regular annual work 
schedule.  
• Per current practice, clarified that parental leave does not need to be utilized in continuous 
period and how arrangements can be made for alternate schedules when employees choose 
to return to work and continue utilizing paid parental leave during the year following the 
qualifying event.  
  
Section 13 – Workers’ Compensation  
D. Compensation & Benefits While on Workers’ Compensation Leave – clarified that for 
prolonged leave, employee may use accrued vacation or comp time.  
E. Modified Duty Assignments – Noted that modified duty as part of work comp injury must 
be agreed upon by relevant parties.  
  
Section 14 – Leave of Absences - Other 
D. Donated Leave Policy  
Added Section D to define a new Donated Leave Policy for employees experiencing an 
extended illness/injury or is seriously incapacitated and has exhausted all their accrued 
leave.  Employees may donate accrued vacation time to a fellow employee.  
  
Section 16 – Conditions of Employment  
A. At Will Employment – Added specific note that employees are at-will.
