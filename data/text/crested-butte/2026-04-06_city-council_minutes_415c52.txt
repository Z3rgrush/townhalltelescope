Discussion 1: Development Review Processes 
All new development, major additions and renovations, and redevelopment in Crested Butte are 
subject to a development review process. Depending on project scope, approvals are granted by 
either BOZAR or Town Council. Both the Historic Preservation Plan and the Community Plan 
identified a clear need to streamline this process and make it more predictable and efficient, 
particularly for projects that advance community goals such as affordability. 
As it stands, larger and higher-end projects are generally better positioned to absorb lengthy and 
complex review timelines, while smaller or mission-driven projects often are not. Refining 
approval authorities, such as delegating certain decisions to staff instead of requiring full BOZAR 
or Chair review, can better align the process with the Town’s policy priorities. 
The zoning code update includes both regulatory changes, covered in the first two joint work 
sessions, and procedural updates. This discussion focuses on the latter. 
Before reviewing potential process refinements, it is helpful to clarify BOZAR’s two primary 
roles: zoning review and architectural review, summarized in the table below. Tonight’s discussion 
will focus solely on zoning and subdivision review. Architectural review will be addressed 
separately in a later series of joint work sessions during late summer/early fall as part of the Design 
Standards update.

Throughout the zoning code update process, staff identified both inefficiencies and vulnerabilities 
in the current development review system. One example is the use of Chair-level approvals, which 
concentrate decision-making authority on a single appointed individual. To improve clarity, 
consistency, and accountability, it is important that decisions are either administrative or made by 
the full BOZAR body.

Staff also held a series of work sessions with BOZAR in summer and fall 2025 to better 
understand what is working well and where the process can improve. These discussions led to the 
following key takeaways, which are guiding proposed updates to both zoning and architectural 
review: 
• Streamline the review process for projects that clearly meet the rules, have lower public 
impact, or advance community goals outlined in the Historic Preservation Plan and 
Community Plan. 
• Continue to provide and encourage meaningful opportunities for public input and feedback. 
• Prioritize BOZAR’s time on projects with the greatest community impact, while using staff 
resources efficiently to keep the process responsive and effective. 
Building on this direction, staff has prepared draft refinements to the Town’s development review 
process, focused on zoning and excluding architectural review. At this meeting, staff is seeking 
feedback from BOZAR and Council on this approach, including which ideas feel most effective, 
which may need further discussion, and where adjustments may be warranted.  
It’s important to note that in all alternatives, the appeals process remains the same. Appeals to staff 
decisions are reviewed by BOZAR and appeals to BOZAR decisions are reviewed by Town 
Council.

Discussion 2: Planned Unit Developments (PUDS) 
 PUDs are intended to allow for greater flexibility in the application of zoning and development 
standards than would otherwise be achieved through a base zoning district in exchange for greater 
benefits to the Town. In practice, a PUD provides flexibility from conventional zoning standards 
such as lot size, setbacks, density, or uses, in exchange for delivering a project that achieves 
broader community goals. PUDs should add value to the Town not just flexibility to a developer.   
In the Town’s current code, PUDs are identified as an overlay district that allows coordinated, 
master-planned development across multiple contiguous lots (with minimum lot thresholds that 
vary by district), with flexibility from certain zoning standards in exchange for enhanced public 
benefits such as open space, design quality, and efficient land use. In today’s code, PUDs are only 
allowed on certain lots within the Town, mostly in Business/Commercial districts.  
The approval process is multi-step and discretionary: 
1. Optional Concept Plan Review: Informal feedback from staff and BOZAR.  
2. General Plan Review (BOZAR): Public hearing and recommendation to the Town Council 
on the overall development plan.  
3. Zoning Amendment (Town Council): Council approval of the PUD overlay district.  
4. Architectural Review (BOZAR): Detailed design review following Council approval.  
5. Permitting: Final approvals and permits prior to construction.  
Larger PUDs may also be subject to subdivision review. Overall, the process is comprehensive and 
front-loaded, requiring significant detail and multiple public hearings before development can 
proceed. 
7
6 
 
The most recent approved PUD in Crested Butte is the Academy Place project. During this 
discussion and to inform the discussion on the proposed refinements, staff is seeking to learn from 
BOZAR and Council what worked well and what didn’t work well during that process. That 
project allowed zoning flexibility in parcel size, FAR, setbacks, and parking requirements, 
including a reduced rear setback and fewer required parking spaces. In exchange, the project 
delivered public benefits including increased open space, context-sensitive building design, an 
additional affordable housing unit beyond ROAH requirements, and a publicly accessible 
easement with preserved mature trees and pedestrian connections through the site. 
Proposed Refinements: Staff is proposing refinements to what qualifies for a PUD, what zone 
provisions can be allowed for flexibility, and to better define public benefits, as well as 
refinements to the approval process to be clearer and more predictable.  Staff is also 
recommending moving away from PUD overlay districts as the concept of underlying zoning plus 
the overlay is both confusing and difficult to administer.  In other jurisdictions PUDs are simply 
rezones of the property with comprehensive standards created as part of the approval process that 
would remain in place unless otherwise rezoned. 
What Qualifies: It is recommended to allow any lot in the Belleview Commercial District, Sixth 
Street Business District, or High Density Residential district to be eligible for a PUD if a developer 
wants to put forward a proposal that is different/more creative than the underlying zone district for 
the purposes of providing public benefits.  
What Zone Provisions Can be Flexible: Rather than prescribe a list of where flexibility can be 
granted, provide an open-ended call for creativity beyond the baseline zoning or design standards 
from the applicant.  
Public Benefits: Refine the requirement for public benefits by noting the definitions and 
prioritization for community-serving housing and community spaces but leave open flexibility for 
creativity from the applicant. 
Proposed Process:  . 
1. Optional Pre-Application Meeting with Town Staff: An informal opportunity for the 
applicant to receive early feedback on proposed zoning flexibility and associated public 
benefits.  
2. Council Direction: Similar to a rezoning proposal from a member of the public, it is 
proposed to have a high level concept discussion resulting in direction to proceed or not 
from Town Council on the proposed flexibility vs. proposed public benefits.  
3. PUD Guide and Zoning Amendment Proposal: If Council gives direction to proceed, the 
applicant submits a detailed PUD Guide outlining proposed zoning amendments and 
corresponding public benefits. A PUD Guide is a project-specific zoning document that 
establishes the rules, commitments, and framework for how a particular development will 
be built and function. 
• The proposal is reviewed by BOZAR during a public hearing, which provides a 
recommendation to Town Council on whether the zoning amendment should be 
considered.  
• Town Council has full discretion to approve or deny the proposed zoning change 
following a public hearing. Approval of a rezoning or PUD is not guaranteed.  
4. Site Plan Review and Approval: The applicant submits a detailed site plan for review by 
BOZAR and decision by Town Council.

• The site plan may be reviewed concurrently with the PUD Guide and zoning 
amendment or separately following approval, at the applicant’s discretion and risk.  
5. Architectural Review: Architectural review will follow the process established in the 
updated Design Standards, with approval by staff or BOZAR depending on the applicable 
building types. 
 
Discussion 3: Zoning Code Process Update and Feedback Recap 
Code Update Process Check In: Town staff had originally anticipated adopting the full zoning 
code update in conjunction with completion of the Design Standards update, currently targeted for 
December 2026. However, given the strong alignment between Council and BOZAR during recent 
joint work sessions, staff is proposing a two-phased adoption approach to maintain momentum on 
the zoning code update while shifting focus toward the Design Standards effort. Staff seeks 
feedback on whether Council and BOZAR are comfortable with this proposed two-phased 
approach.  
Phase 1 (June 2026 Adoption): Zoning Code Update 
Adopt core updates to the zoning code, including general cleanup and legal review, refined zone 
districts and map, updated provisions, incentives, as well as updates to ROAH and the Parking 
Payment in Lieu fee. This phase would also implement revised development review processes 
related to zoning. 
Proposed Timeline: 
• May 18: Council Work Session, draft ordinance and code language  
• May 26: BOZAR meeting, recommendation for adoption  
• June 1: Council first reading  
• June 15: Council public hearing and adoption  
Phase 2 (December 2026 Adoption): Design Standards Integration and Code Refinements 
Adopt the Design Standards update and incorporate related amendments into the zoning code. This 
phase will also establish the Early Recreation Era period of significance and include any necessary 
refinements identified during the design standards process. 
Proposed Timeline: 
• Now through June: Drafting, advisory committee, and focus group work; finalization of 
Recreation Era period of significance survey work 
• August through October (first Council meeting each month): Joint BOZAR and Council 
work sessions on design standards  
• November: BOZAR recommendation for adoption  
• December: Council first reading and public hearing for adoption  
It is also recommended to initiate a 2-year window to assess how the new code is working and 
return to BOZAR and Council with a check in on any tweaks suggested all at once after 2 years 
(rather than incrementally).

ry:   
Staff is requesting that Town Council approve the 2026 Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) 
between the Town of Crested Butte and Gunnison County to continue administration of the 
GreenDeed program through GV-HEAT. The agreement outlines program updates for 2026, 
including expanded eligibility, revised Phase II rebate structures, and shared administrative costs 
among participating jurisdictions. 
Bac
kground: 
The GreenDeed program provides funding and logistical support for energy assessments and 
energy efficiency and electrification upgrades for deed-restricted homeowners in the Town of 
Crested Butte. Launched in 2021 from the Town’s 2019 Climate Action Plan, the program aligns 
with the 2030 Climate Action Plan goal to “drive retrofits in existing buildings by developing 
incentive programs for residential and commercial retrofits.” 
GreenDeed is administered in partnership with the Town by GV-HEAT, which now operates under 
Gunnison County through the Gunnison County Housing Authority (GCHA). The program 
includes two phases: 
Phase I: For a $50 registration fee, deed-restricted homeowners receive a home energy 
audit, assessment report, and up to $5,000 in energy efficiency improvements (including 
the audit). Some project examples include air duct and crack sealing, insulation, appliance 
and lightbulb replacements, and programmable thermostats. GV-HEAT coordinates with 
contractors to complete the work.  
Phase II: Homes that complete Phase I are eligible for additional funding for energy 
efficiency and electrification. Examples include installing insulated cellular shades on 
windows, conversion of appliances to electric (i.e. heat pumps, heat pump hot water heats, 
induction stoves), EV-chargers, or renewable energy system installments (i.e., solar). The 
homeowner contracts with contractors of their choice.  
To date, GreenDeed has served 65 deed-restricted households, averaging 13 homes per year. 
Homes completing recommended improvements increased their energy efficiency by 15% on 
average based on pre- and post-blower door testing. Phase II (launched in 2023) has funded 29 
19
additional individual projects, including heat pumps, window replacements, cellular shades, 
electric appliances, and solar installations. 
 
Previous Council Action:  
On December 16, 2024, Town Council approved a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) with the 
Gunnison Valley Regional Housing Authority (GVRHA) and a $100,000 contribution to continue 
the GreenDeed program. Council later approved the 2026 budget, which includes $158,000 for 
building efficiency and electrification programming. Development and approval of the 2026 MOA 
has been delayed due to the dissolution of GVRHA in late 2025 and uncertainty regarding whether 
GV-HEAT and its governing authority would continue administering the program. 
 
Discussion:  
The proposed MOA is largely consistent with previous agreements but includes several updates 
related to program administration, eligibility, and Phase II funding: 
 
1. Administrative Fees – Previously, the Town paid $400 per home and rebate administrative 
fee to GV-HEAT (scheduled to increase to $800 per rebate in 2026). The County now 
proposes splitting administrative costs evenly among the four participating jurisdictions 
(City of Gunnison, Gunnison County, Mt. Crested Butte, and Crested Butte). The Town’s 
annual share is $32,500.

free energy assessments from Energy Smart Colorado, GreenDeed is proposed to 
additionally be available to:

Summary 
Staff seek approval from the Town Council to purchase 16 Ninth, a deed restricted unit, with the 
intent update to the new form of deed restriction and add income and asset limits and to grant the 
Town the first right of refusal on future sales.  Staff anticipate that Town will take ownership of 
the unit on April 30th and intends to sell it by lottery with the revised deed restriction shortly 
thereafter.  Staff will return to the Council to request approval for the sale of the unit and present 
the income restriction.    
 
Previous Council Action: Council approved the purchase of three other units, 816 Gothic on 
January 20, 2026, 828 Gothic on October 20, 2025, and 822 Gothic on June 3, 2024, addressing 
the exclusion of income and asset limits in the deed restriction on those homes. 
 
Background 
Based on Town records, previous Staff carefully crafted sales prices based on target AMIs for 
the 30 homes that were constructed and sold by Bywater Construction in 2018-2020. The 
original deed restriction on these homes includes an appreciation cap based on those sales prices 
as well as the requirement that one individual on the deed be a full-time participant in the local 
workforce and the standard prohibition on the ownership of other improved residential real 
estate. However, the restriction does not include an income restriction or a limit on the assets of 
potential buyers. The ongoing expectation is that Town will step in to update the remaining 
Bywater deed restrictions with then current Town policy objectives when their owners are ready 
to sell. 
 
Discussion: Purchasing 16 Ninth enables the Town to release the current deed restriction and 
record a new restriction that targets household incomes that are not served by the housing market 
and establishes asset limits, which ensures that those with high net value do not qualify to 
purchase the home. Additionally, the new deed restriction will grant the Town with a first right 
of refusal to purchase the unit. 
 
Staff uses a reverse affordability calculation, which converts the maximum allowable sales price 
into an affordable monthly housing cost and then converts that cost into an income and 
corresponding asset limit using standardized assumptions on the loan for the home. A cushion is 
23
 
 
included in the interest rate to help protect against swings that could render the price of the home 
unaffordable at the designated income limit. 
 
Pending the Council’s approval, staff will announce the property on the Town’s “Available 
Properties” webpage, through Town social media accounts, in the CB News, and through the 
Gunnison County Housing Authority’s homeownership interest list.  The home will be sold via 
lottery in accordance with the Town’s Affordable Housing Guidelines at the maximum allowable 
sales price of approximately $265,000.  
 
Climate Impact: N/A 
 
Financial Impact: Staff anticipate that the costs to purchase and resell the property will be 
approximately $4,000 for standard closing costs excluding the Real Estate Transfer Tax which is 
not required for a Town transfer of a deed-restricted property. 
 
Legal Review: Town legal counsel is engaged to support staff in this effort.

Subject:  Annual Traffic Calming and Parking Management Discussion 
Summary: This agenda item provides the Town Council’s annual review and discussion of 
Parking Management and Traffic Calming. The memo summarizes conditions and program 
performance over the past year and outlines recommended updates. Staff is seeking Council 
feedback on these recommendations, as well as direction on any additional changes to consider. 
Previous Council Action: Town Council held a work session on Parking and Traffic Calming on 
March 2, 2025, to discuss the 2025 plan and priorities. 
Background: The Town’s Transportation Mobility Plan (TMP), adopted in March 2023, 
establishes a roadmap to advance the Compass strategic goal of de-emphasizing cars while 
prioritizing walking, biking, rolling and transit. The TMP is organized around three overarching 
strategies: 
1. Increase mobility choices
2. Manage parking convenience
3. Integrate land use with transportation
Traffic Calming: The Town’s modern traffic calming program began in 2021 (building on earlier 
efforts in the early 2000s), catalyzed by the temporary one-way conversion of Elk Avenue during 
the pandemic. The TMP called for formalizing a policy and program to reduce vehicle speeds 
where warranted in residential neighborhoods to improve the pedestrian experience, a key 
component of increasing mobility choices.  
The Traffic Calming Policy, adopted in winter 2023 (attached), establishes criteria for prioritizing 
and deploying devices such as chicanes and lane narrowings using planters, mid-block signage, 
and speed feedback signs. 
Parking Management: Parking management efforts also began in 2021, initially focused on 
enforcing the existing 2-hour parking limit on Elk Avenue and adjacent half-block side streets. The 
program also includes residential and employee permits (provided at no cost), with a 2-hour grace 
period for non-permit holders on Sopris and Maroon. Enforcement occurs during the winter and 
summer seasons through Park Crested Butte.

2 
 
The program expanded two winters ago to include a free “skier permit” for Gunnison County 
residents. This permit is targeted to neighborhoods near the 4-way stop and Teocalli bus stops to 
reduce skier parking impacts in residential areas. 
The TMP recommends continuing to incrementally expand parking management in areas 
experiencing parking constraints, as alternative mobility options improve (e.g., increased transit 
frequency and regional park-and-ride facilities). The parking management program is a critical 
component of the TMP strategy to manage parking convenience.

I. Draft Parks Decision-Making Framework 
* Please Review Attachment: Parks Decision-Making Framework 
 
This Parks Decision-Making Framework establishes a clear and transparent approach for assessing 
park investments in the Town of Crested Butte. It is designed to ensure that projects align with 
community values, address identified needs, remain financially and operationally sustainable, and 
balance creativity, multi-use, equity, and resilience. The framework serves as a guiding tool to 
support thoughtful, well-rounded decision-making rather than a rigid checklist. 
 
Staff will apply the framework by evaluating each project through the Town’s Community 
Compass values (Authentic, Connected, Accountable, and Bold) while documenting key strengths, 
trade-offs, partnership opportunities, funding strategies, and community input. This process 
promotes consistent analysis and supports clear, transparent recommendations to Council. 
 
Projects are evaluated holistically across four core areas: 
• Authentic: Reinforces Crested Butte’s unique character, culture, and sense of place.  
• Connected: Enhances access, inclusion, community gathering, and system-wide 
connectivity.  
• Accountable: Demonstrates responsible stewardship through financial sustainability, 
operational feasibility, and environmental resilience.  
• Bold: Embraces innovation, creativity, and forward-looking planning to meet future 
community needs.  
Overall, the framework ensures park investments advance community priorities while 
acknowledging practical constraints such as aging infrastructure, staffing capacity, funding, and 
regional demand.
