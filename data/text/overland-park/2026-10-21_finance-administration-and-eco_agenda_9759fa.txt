Finance, Administration and Economic Development Committee - October 21, 2026
ITEM TITLE:
2027 Annual Calendar of Meetings  – Council, Committee of the Whole, Goal Area Committees and 
Boards, Commissions and other Plural Authorities of the City.
POLICY ISSUE:
Resolution No. 5011 establishing Council and Committee Procedures, calls for the development of an 
annual calendar of meetings. Each Goal Area Committee and Board, Commission and Advisory 
Committee  reviews and recommends an annual calendar of its meetings.  A comprehensive Annual 
Calendar is then reviewed by the Finance, Administration and Economic Development Committee (the 
“Committee”), before recommending a final calendar to the City Council for approval.
STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
That the Committee recommend to the City Council approval of the proposed 2027 Annual Calendar.
PROPOSED MOTION:
I move that the Committee recommend City Council approval of the proposed 2027 Annual Calendar of 
Meetings.
BACKGROUND:
Attached is the proposed calendar of meetings of the Mayor and City Council, Committee of the Whole, 
City Council Goal Area Committees and all City Boards, Commissions and Plural Authorities for 
calendar year 2027.
 
Meeting dates for City Council Goal Area Committee meetings have been reviewed and recommended 
by each Goal Area Committee.  On September 16, 2026, the Committee also reviewed a 
recommended schedule of meetings for the Committee and for City Council and Committee-of-the- 
Whole meetings.  Two Committee-of-the-Whole meetings have been added to the schedule reviewed in 
September:  January 25, 2027 and June 21, 2027 - to review the Safe Streets Plan.
Dates for meetings of Boards, Commissions and Plural Authorities have been prepared by the Staff 
Coordinator for each entity in consultation with each Plural Authority and/or the policies of those bodies.  
Each body has approved the schedule of meetings that has been incorporated into this comprehensive 
calendar.
 
Upon Governing Body approval of the 2027 Annual Calendar, dates will be added to the City's website 
under the City Calendar + Events section.

ITEM TITLE:
Authorization to Negotiate Funding Agreement - Dream Theatre, LLC for aid in the Reopening and 
Historic Restoration of the Dream Theatre at the Rio in Downtown Overland Park.
POLICY ISSUE:
The Finance, Administration and Economic Development Committee (the “Committee”) has policy 
responsibility for matters related to Economic Development.
STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends that the Committee direct staff to negotiate a Funding Agreement with Dream 
Theatre, LLC for aid in the reopening and historic restoration of the Dream Theatre at the Rio in 
Downtown Overland Park, and recommend City Council approval of the same.
PROPOSED MOTION:
I move that the Committee direct staff to negotiate a Funding Agreement with Dream Theatre, LLC for 
aid in the reopening and historic restoration of the Dream Theatre at the Rio in Downtown Overland 
Park, and recommend City Council approval of the same.
BACKGROUND:
The Rio Theatre, located at 7204 W. 80th Street in downtown Overland Park, originally opened in 1946 
and was restored in 1993 by The Fine Arts Theatre Group, which operated it until 2020. The single-
screen theater is a recognized landmark on the National Register of Historic Places and serves as a 
cultural and architectural anchor in downtown Overland Park. The theater ceased operations during the 
COVID-19 pandemic. Over the years, the City and the Downtown Overland Park Partnership have 
received numerous inquiries and interest regarding the future of the property and its potential 
reopening.
 
Dream Theatre, LLC approached the City with a proposal for the historic restoration and reopening of 
the venue. Dream Theatre intends to renovate and reopen the location as the "Dream Theatre at the 
Rio." The project includes Art Deco-inspired design elements, a curated film schedule featuring themed 
nights and animated classics for children and adults, concessions, a dedicated cocktail lounge, and 
premium retail offerings.
 
The project aligns with goals outlined in Framework OP and Vision Metcalf by adding an entertainment 
venue to downtown Overland Park. By restoring a historic cultural anchor adjacent to Clock Tower 
Landing, the project supports strategic goals of advancing a high quality of life, establishing a vibrant 
community gathering space, enhancing economic well-being by promoting historic preservation, driving 
local commerce, and stimulating continued economic growth, redevelopment, and investment in 
downtown Overland Park.
 
To make the project financially feasible, proposed terms include:
 
A conditional grant from the City totaling up to $425,000.00. Subject to negotiation, this funding 
may consist of $200,000.00 to assist with the property purchase at closing (estimated for 
Agenda Item #3.
Page 13 of 96
November 2026) and up to $225,000.00 in potential reimbursements for specific exterior 
improvements and building stabilization.
A commitment from Dream Theatre to operate the venue as a public theater for a minimum of 
10 years following the final disbursement.
Dream Theatre provides the City with a mortgage and promissory note to secure the 
investment.
Submission of reports to the City Council’s Finance, Administration and Economic Development 
Committee.
Ensuring the parking lot remains open for public use outside operating hours.
 
In addition to the grant, Dream Theatre has requested Economic Development Revenue Bond (EDRB) 
financing to obtain a sales tax exemption on construction materials, equipment, furnishings and labor. 
Under proposed terms, the City would consider advancing a EDRB Resolution of Intent, waiving its 
application and issuance fees, and paying its bond counsel fees. In turn, Dream Theatre would be 
expected to pay annual trustee fees, redeem the EDRBs within twelve months of project completion, 
and submit quarterly purchase reports.
 
Representatives from STRATA Architecture will be present at the meeting to answer questions about 
the proposed project and renovation.

Amended and Restated Interlocal Agreement - Board of County Commissioners of Johnson County,

The Finance, Administration and Economic Development Committee has policy oversight of information

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
That the Committee recommend to the City Council approval of the Amended and Restated agreement 
with the Board of County Commissioners of Johnson County, Kansas, related to the Radio Room 
located at the Overland Park Sheraton Hotel. 
PROPOSED MOTION:
I move to recommend to the City Council approval of the Amended and Restated agreement with the 
Board of County Commissioners of Johnson County, Kansas, related to the Radio Room located at the 
Overland Park Sheraton Hotel. 
BACKGROUND:
The City has a lease with the Overland Park Development Corporation to  locate, attach, and maintain 
communications and signal equipment—including radio, telephonic, electronic, and video equipment, 
antennas, transformers, and connectors in the Sheraton Overland Park Hotel.  In 2002, the City entered 
into an agreement with the Board of County Commissioners of Johnson County to house county 
communications equipment on the Sheraton Hotel property.  Subsequently, in 2009 the same parties 
entered into an agreement to construct a climate controlled room on the property to house additional 
county communication equipment as authorized by the City's lease with OPDC.  The 2009 agreement 
included a 20-year term with automatic five-year renewals.  This communication hub remains essential 
to achieving City and County-wide goals related to public safety, infrastructure reliability and emergency 
response capability.
In November 2024, City staff initiated discussions with Johnson County to revise the 2009 agreement in 
recognition of shared operational use, expanded equipment, new cybersecurity considerations, 
expenses related to equipment replacement and a general need to clarify the responsibilities of each 
agency.  The proposed agreement addresses these considerations.  Approving the amended and 
restated agreement before automatic renewal in 2029 ensures continuity and reduces operational risk 
for both agencies.

A Kansas City Family Business Since 1945
OUR HISTORY. OUR FAMILY. OUR NEXT CHAPTER.
For more than 80 years, Drenon Jewelry has been a family-owned 
Kansas City business built on entrepreneurship, relationships, 
craftsmanship, and an unwavering commitment to its customers.
Founded in 1945 by Mike and Pansy Drenon in the Fairmount 
neighborhood of Independence, Missouri, Drenon began with 
remarkably humble beginnings. Mike had just $500 to his name.
There was no established showroom, no large inventory, and no 
corporate infrastructure behind him. Mike built his own showcases, 
assembled his merchandise, and personally carried it back and forth 
as he built the business one customer at a time.
What began as a small neighborhood jewelry store became 
something much larger—but the principles established by Mike and 
Pansy never changed. 
Today, more than eight decades later, the fourth generation of the 
Drenon family is involved in the leadership of the business. The 
company remains independently and family owned, while 
representing some of the most recognized names in the international 
luxury industry.
Drenon's move to Overland Park represents not a departure from its 
history, but the continuation of it.

POLICY ISSUE:
Matters related to economic development projects and agreements related to Tax Increment Finance 
District(s) (“TIF”) and Community Improvement District(s) (“CID”) are reviewed by the Finance, 
Administration and Economic Development Committee (the “Committee”). 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
That the Committee direct Staff to draft a resolution authorizing execution of the Notice and 
Acknowledgement of Assignment of TIF/CID Proceeds between Overland Park Development Company 
I, LLC and Capitol Federal Savings Bank and recommend City Council adoption of the same.

Acknowledgement of Assignment of TIF/CID Proceeds between Overland Park Development Company 
I, LLC and Capitol Federal Savings Bank and recommend City Council adoption of the same. 
BACKGROUND:
On December 2, 2019 the Governing Body approved several actions in connection with the proposed 
Brookridge project (now called Meridian) for a multi-phased redevelopment of the Brookridge Golf 
Course into a mixed-use residential, retail, hotel, and office project on the northeast corner of I-435 and 
Antioch Road (the “Project”). These actions included approval of public investments including creation 
of Brookridge CID No. 1, Brookridge CID No. 2, project plans for two TIF project areas, and 
authorization to use EDRBs for the purpose of obtaining a sales tax exemption on construction 
materials, furnishings and equipment. A redevelopment agreement (the “Agreement”) with the Project 
developer, Overland Park Development Company I, LLC (the “Developer”) was also approved, 
establishing various terms and conditions related to the Project including improvements, performance 
milestones, and provisions related to public investments in the Project. 
 
The Development Agreement has been amended, with the last amendment (the Fourth Amendment) 
being approved by the Governing Body on July 6, 2026.
 
Developer is receiving a loan from Capitol Federal Savings Bank (“Lender”).  As part of this transaction, 
Developer and Lender are entering into a Collateral Assignment of Development Agreement, whereby 
Developer is collaterally assigning to the Lender of Developer's rights under the Redevelopment 
Agreement, including Developer's rights to receive TIF/CID Proceeds thereunder. 
 
Developer has requested the City execute the attached Notice of Acknowledgement of Assignment of 
TIF/CID Proceeds.
