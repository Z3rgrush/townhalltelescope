Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) 
Technology ● ALPR cameras photograph visible license plates and record characters, vehicle image, date, time, and camera location:
○ Plate reads are compared with authorized "hot lists," including NCIC information and 
lists connected to documented investigations.
○ An alert identiﬁes a possible match that, by policy, must be veriﬁed and conﬁrmed 
prior to taking action.
○ Historical reads when investigating a reported crime or attempting to locate a 
missing or endangered person can also be searched.
○ Searches are not random and must be related to an authorized law enforcement 
reason.
○ An alert alone does not determine that a crime occurred.

Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) Technology 
Search Records Include:
● A record connected to an 
individual user or account.
● Date and time of search.
● All details of the search.
A Search or Alert Does Not Include:
● Identify a vehicle’s driver or occupants.
● Use facial recognition.
● Display registration information.
● Determine whether a crime has occurred. 
● Attempt to read or save bumper stickers or 
decals*.
*A large sticker or decal placed in legible position could 
be inadvertently recorded as a tag; however, this would 
not be searchable or stored differently than the tag.

ALPR System Overview 
The Overland Park Police Department currently 
owns and/or operates ALPR cameras from 
Genetec and Axon.  
The Department is currently exploring options 
for replacement of our legacy Genetec system 
to ensure alignment of our systems with current 
best practices, to include accountability and 
auditing measures.
Genetec
● Genetec cameras make up the entirety of Overland Park’s 
ﬁxed-site ALPR system (aside from the pre-production test 
listed below). 
● They are deployed at eight sites (37 cameras) within the city.
● Access is restricted to Dispatch Supervisors and members of 
the Technology and Crime Analysis Units. 
Axon
● We are currently beta testing pre-production Axon ﬁxed-site 
ALPR models at one site (8 cameras) within the city. Although 
we do not own the devices, we own the data.
● 110 vehicles are outﬁtted with the Axon Fleet 3 in-car 
cameras.
Flock
● Overland Park does not own or operate any Flock ALPR 
devices.  
● Because several private businesses within the city operate 
their own devices, the Police Department has access for the 
purpose of receiving hotlist alerts from these private devices.
● Access allows the Police Department to conduct searches 
within the Flock system.
● Access is restricted to Dispatch Supervisors and members of 
the Technology and Crime Analysis Units.

Axon “Hit” Example* 
A hit from Axon’s system contains the following information. 
The tag read and that the hit is still active must be 
independently veriﬁed prior to taking any action.
1. Hit Reason:  Reason for the hotlist entry.
2. Context Photo:  Vehicle photo with the tag being read 
highlighted.
3. Tag Photo:  Photograph of the tag only.
4. Read Digits:  Letters and numbers the system 
identiﬁes from the tag photo.
5. Vehicle information:  Present only if this information 
was included in the hotlist entry.  The system is not 
searching for this information on its own.
6. Action buttons:  Users can inform the system that the 
read was correct, the read digits were correct but the 
hit was from another state’s tag, or that the read 
digits were incorrect.

Current ALPR Structure: Half of One-Tenth of One Percent 
● As Overland Park’s ALPR infrastructure has expanded over the years, the active sites 
were selected based on criminal activity or speciﬁc need in the area.  
● ALPR cameras are not installed in a grid system or matrix capable of tracking a vehicle’s 
movements across the city.
● Coverage: 0.00052 or 0.052% of the roadway. Half of one-tenth of one percent.* 
● Under the Kansas Open Records Act, the exact location of ALPRs and all captured 
license plate data are explicitly exempt from public disclosure.
● The city maintains ownership and control over all ALPR data.
● OPPD owned/operated cameras are not within 100 feet within any place of worship.
*This is an overestimation of ALPR roadway coverage. We used the maximum possible amount; therefore, our actual 
coverage is slightly lower than half of one-tenth of one percent.

Statement from the ACLU of Kansas 
“The Overland Park Police Department has engaged in an open, transparent dialogue about its policy 
on the use of ALPRs. Chief Jokerst and the Department have sought the views of a wide range of 
individuals and organizations, including those like ours, who have divergent perspectives. The OPPD's 
willingness to meaningfully engage and take steps to address our concerns is commendable. The 
ACLU of Kansas will continue to advocate for ending the use of ALPRs entirely, but the updated OPPD 
policy is a signiﬁcant step forward in limiting ALPR use, scope, and data retention in ways that seek to 
protect individual rights. We are committed to continuing the respectful, constructive dialogue which 
Chief Jokerst and OPPD initiated about ALPRs, including by calling for reduced data retention periods 
and limits on data sharing."  
- Micah Kubic, Executive Director, American Civil Liberties Union of Kansas

Successful Use Case - Missing Person 
● An external agency entered a license plate associated with a missing person into NCIC 
(National Crime Information Center).
● An Overland Park ALPR generated an alert when the vehicle entered the camera's view.
● OPPD ofﬁcers veriﬁed the information, located the vehicle, and contacted the driver.
● The driver was disoriented and was transported to a hospital for care. 
● The missing person was ultimately reunited with their family, and the license plate was 
removed from NCIC.

Successful Use Case - Armed Robbery 
● An Aggravated Robbery occurred within the city during an attempted online sale 
transaction.
● ALPR data was used to assist in identifying the suspect vehicle as it ﬂed the scene.
● OPPD Detectives were able to use this information to identify the vehicle’s registered

● Police resources were used to conﬁrm the owner was the suspect, the suspect was

Successful Use Case - Burglary Series 
● A suspect targeted a business chain, burglarizing 11 different locations across seven 
states. 
● The same suspect vehicle was seen on video footage at multiple locations. An ALPR read 
from an intersection near one of the scenes revealed the license plate returned to an 
individual who was on probation. 
● The suspect’s probation ofﬁcer reviewed store video footage and positively identiﬁed the 
suspect.
● An ALPR BOLO alert was entered.
● The suspect was ultimately located, arrested, and plead guilty to the burglaries across 
multiple states.

installation services for 13 patrol vehicles to Ka-Comm, Inc. and approval of an agreement with Ka-

BACKGROUND:
The police department purchases police patrol vehicles annually to replace vehicles that meet certain 
criteria for replacement.  After the vehicles are purchased, they must be upfitted with items such as 
emergency lights, sirens, controls, prisoner partitions, push bumpers, etc.  Equipment that cannot be 
reused and must be replaced with new equipment supplied by a vendor.   The equipment specifications 
are created to ensure standardization across the police fleet. 
 
The police department conducted a formal bid process for the equipment and installation services used 
in the 13 patrol vehicles purchased as part of the 2026 equipment replacement program.  There were 
two responsive bidders.  Ka-Comm, which is a vendor located in Olathe submitted the low bid, and the 
police department has had positive experience in the past with this vendor.  The bid amount is in line 
with our original budgeted estimate for this purchase.
