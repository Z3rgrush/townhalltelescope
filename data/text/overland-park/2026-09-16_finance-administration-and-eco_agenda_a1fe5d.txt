ITEM TITLE:
2026 Audit Engagement - RubinBrown, LLP, for audit of 2026 Financial Statements.
POLICY ISSUE:
The Finance, Administration and Economic Development Committee (the “Committee”) has oversight 
responsibility for the City audits and audit services. The auditing firm is subject to annual review by the 
Committee and satisfactory negotiation of an agreement to perform auditing services.
STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
That the Committee recommend to the City Council approval of an audit engagement with RubinBrown, 
LLP to perform the 2026 Audits as described below.
PROPOSED MOTION:
I move that the Committee recommend City Council approval of the 2026 Audit Engagement Letter with 
Rubin Brown, LLP for Audit of the City’s Financial Statements and the 2026 Audit Engagement Letter 
for Audit with RubinBrown, LLP for Audit of the Overland Park Convention Center’s Financial 
Statements.
BACKGROUND:
On October 2, 2023, the City Council approved the Committee’s recommendation to select 
RubinBrown, LLP (“RubinBrown”) as the external auditor for the city for a five-year period (2023 – 
2028), subject to annual renewals.  On November 20, 2023, the City Council approved an agreement 
with RubinBrown for professional auditing services for fiscal years 2023 through 2028.
 
Attached are engagement letters from RubinBrown for the audit of the 2026 City’s Financial Statements 
and a 2026 audit of the Overland Park Convention Center.
 
Below is a summary of the fee for the 2026 Audit in comparison to fee for the 2025 Audit. The total fee 
for the 2026 audit represents an increase of 3.3% or $4,650 over the 2025 fee.

Audit Objective
The objectives of our audit are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial 
statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and 
issue an auditors’ report that includes our opinion about whether your financial statements are 
fairly presented, in all material respects, in conformity with generally accepted accounting 
principles and to report on the fairness of the supplementary information referred to above 
when considered in relation to the financial statements as a whole. 
Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not absolute assurance and 
therefore is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with GAAS and 
Government Auditing Standards will always detect a material misstatement when it exists. 
Misstatements, including omissions, can arise from fraud or error and are considered material 
if there is a substantial likelihood that, individually or in the aggregate, they would influence 
the judgment of a reasonable user made based on the financial statements. 
The objective also includes reporting on:
• Internal control over financial reporting and compliance with the provisions of laws, 
regulations, contracts, and award agreements, noncompliance with which could have

Administrative Requirements, Cost Principles, and Audit Requirements for Federal

PROPOSED MOTION:
I move to recommend City Council approval of an agreement with Carahsoft Technology Corp., utilizing 
the National Association of State Procurement Officers Cooperative Purchasing Agreement #AR-2472, 
for the purchase of Continuous Cyber Testing Services for a one-year term, in an amount of 
$168,700.35.
BACKGROUND:
The City’s Information Technology Department continually seeks to improve Overland Park’s 
cybersecurity posture while empowering municipal operations with safe, efficient technology services. 
Modern cybersecurity standards necessitate shifting from static, annual point-in-time assessments to 
continuous verification to identify and remediate vulnerabilities before they can be exploited by threat 
actors.  
To achieve this, staff recommends procuring a continuous cyber penetration testing platform that safely 
simulates attack strategies against the City's internal and external IT infrastructure. This platform allows 
IT staff to rapidly verify actionable risks, prioritize remediation efforts, and confirm security fixes without 
disrupting daily operations.
 
Funding for this initiative is supported by a State cybersecurity grant. The City of Overland Park applied 
for and was awarded a total of $256,800 in State cybersecurity grant funding, with $135,000 explicitly 
allocated toward this penetration testing software project. The City is contributing an additional 
$33,700.35 to satisfy the local match requirement.
Additionally, the IT Department successfully negotiated pricing with the vendor, securing a 43% 
discount off the original list price of $297,000 to bring the final purchase cost to $168,700.35.
