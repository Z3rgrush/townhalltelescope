REZONING NO. REZ2026-00008 - FoJCDS - Grandview Lane - 8500 Grandview 
Lane. Rezoning requested to RP-3, Planned Garden Apartment District, to allow for the 
conversion of a building into a multi-unit home.  Application made by Friends of 
Johnson County Developmental Supports. 
 (Approved) 
Planner II Najma Muhammad presented Rezoning No. REZ2026-00008, Friends of 
Johnson County Grandview Lane triplex proposal, located at 8500 Grandview Lane.  
The property is zoned R-1, Single-Family Residential District, located on the corner of 
Grandview and 85th Street and primarily surrounded by single-family zoned 
properties and duplexes.  The future land use calls for traditional neighborhood in this 
area, which is intended to provide a mix of housing ranging from small-lot single-
family homes to townhomes, and small neighborhood scale multi-family buildings 
being proposed.  The surrounding areas include the Elmhurst subdivision and to the 
north is the Galway Domain subdivision, which is where the duplex zoning lots and 
parcels are located. 
The existing building was built in 1957, which pre-dates the City, and was historically 
used as Johnson County Med-Act in the past.  The applicant intends to convert this 
building into a triplex using the existing material, which was approved by the Site Plan 
Review Committee of cement block; however, they intend to enhance it with the pitch 
roofing to make it blend in with the surrounding single-family homes and duplexes, 
some wood siding and other small-scale pedestrian scale housing and architectural 
Overland Park Planning Commission Meeting September 14, 2026 
Page 9 
 
elements common to single-family homes and duplexes, which will be completed at the 
final development plan. 
The applicant is requesting two deviations, as this is a current property, they do not 
intend to tear down the existing building.  This building was built prior to the City’s 
zoning code, and the building is not a home.  The applicant is requesting to rezone to 
RP-3, which requires things outside of what the existing building can accommodate.  
There is a parking setback requirement along the east on Grandview Lane, which 
requires 30 feet from the street right-of-way, and a building setback requirement from 
R-1, which affects them on the south abutting the single-family home.  The minimum 
setback for the main street shall be 25 feet from the property line, which they do not 
meet.  The setbacks are more in line with what is seen in R-1 zoning, and the intent 
for the outside of the home is for it to blend in with the neighborhood.  Staff supports 
both deviations, as well as Site Plan Review Committee meeting comments. 
In terms of the deviation criteria, the proposed triplex development intends to reuse an 
existing building and retrofit it to resemble the surrounding single-family homes.  This 
will include updating the roof from a flat roof to a pitch roof, increasing the amount of 
green space and providing personal off-street parking in the front of the home.  The 
granting of the deviation will not adversely affect the rights of adjacent property 
owners or residents, as it is intended to reconfigure the site to provide outdoor 
amenities along the north and east on Grandview Lane.  A strict application of the 
code would require the applicant to demolish the building, which would defeat the 
purpose of adaptive reuse and would not be in alignment with what staff believes is 
best planning practice.  Staff supports what the developer is wanting to do with the 
space.  The proposed development will not adversely affect public health, safety, and 
general welfare, as it is benefitting as there are some additional health concerns that 
will be mitigated through the redevelopment and renovation of this building that has 
been sitting vacant for some time. 
In terms of the relief for the sound planning and urban design principle consistent 
with the general spirt and intent of the code, adaptive reuse, as a design principle 
supported by staff, is a way to reach many of the goals outlined in Framework OP 
relating to infill, increasing the range of housing types and making the City more 
attainable for individuals who live and work here.  The general spirit and intent of    
RP-3 zoning is to provide a quality apartment environment with emphasis on open 
space and access to light and air, which the applicant is seeking to do through 
providing more pervious surfaces, the covered patio area, additional seating, and other 
green space amenities for the potential users.  RP-3 zoning is meant to provide those 
living in apartments amenities generally available to low-density residential 
developments, and the proposed plan successfully outlines this within the context of 
the existing structure while using what is available on the site.  
The applicant is required to provide up to three amenities, and they have provided six 
amenities to include seasonal planting areas, flowering trees, seeding, pedestrian scale 
lighting, covered patios, and accessible garden beds.  Part of the rezoning criteria 
includes the alignment with Framework OP, and there are several policies the 
applicant is reaching with the reuse of this property to a multi-family home, which 
includes infill, increasing housing choices, economic development, and fostering a 
more neighborhood feel in a building that has historically been out of place in this 
area.

feels this can be viewed as a point-of-activity sign.  Staff proposes this can be a point-

sign to direct the person at the location where an activity or transaction occurs, which 
can be freestanding or a menu board product or dispenser.  Staff sees this as being 
open-ended, as this label has been given to other signs across the City, most notably 
the Morton Salt sign.  Staff views this sign as a point-of-activity sign as NBKC Bank 
has taken over the entire 7400 building.  There are two existing towers currently 
under the development plan, but on that particular parcel, NBKC Bank has full 
ownership.  The intent is for it be more of an office campus; however, there are other 
tenants with their branding across the building.  Staff supports the sign as a point-of-
activity sign to direct people to this building for NBKC since the two towers and that 
development look very similar to one another and will allow for another point of 
understanding of where they are on site at a pedestrian level. 
Point-of-activity signs are allowed two per development, not to exceed 32 square feet 
and 8 feet in height. Staff recommends that this sign not only be approved as a point-
of-activity sign, but to allow for the combination of the two allowed signs for a total of 
64 square feet and to remain at the 8 feet in height.  Staff recommends approval of a
