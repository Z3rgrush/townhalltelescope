Mrs. Muhammed stated this property is surrounded by light commercial, office, and 
industrial on all sides, with a mixture of Johnson County and Overland Park zoning. 
The site currently houses a commercial golf simulation recreational space. The Special 
Use Permit was approved in 2024 and the renewal was scheduled to take place within 
the next year. Due to the upcoming renewal, staff believed it was appropriate to 
combine the new proposal with the existing commercial establishment. Should this be 
approved, the previous proposal will be rescinded by way of expiration. 
 
The applicant is now proposing a private indoor sports facility in addition to the 
existing golf track, which is intended to only be used for personal and family uses. 
This will account for approximately 9,154 square feet of the site, and will be referred to 
as the Sallee Indoor Sports Facility. The golf track is to remain, which is to account for 
3,196 square feet, and will remain as a commercial facility. The distinction exists 
because this use is not permitted, which is why the special use permit is required.  
 
Staff would need to conduct an additional review of parking to confirm it can 
accommodate a commercial indoor sports facility of this size, should the private 
portion of the facility be allowed for a commercial use. There are 38 parking stalls on 
the site, which meets the minimum requirement for this district. Five of the spaces are 
used by golf track. The intent to use this location privately, this is an appropriate 
number of parking spots. The applicant has also updated their landscaping to align 
with the City code as much as possible. 
 
There are few exterior changes, and a door will be moved to accommodate the existing 
golf track use and the new use. Staff recommends approval of Special Use Permit No. 
SUP2026-00019, for a five-year period of time, subject to stipulations a through h. The 
five-year period of time was proposed because the existing golf track was set to renew.

REVISED PRELIMINARY DEVELOPMENT PLAN NO. PDP2026-00020 – Walmart 
Online Pickup and Delivery Expansion – 15700 Metcalf. Application made by BFA. 
(Related Case No. REZ1996-00004). 
 
(Approved) 
 
Mrs. Muhammed stated this location is surrounded by commercial uses to the east 
and residential to the west, south, and north. There have been adjustments to this 
Walmart through staff approvals, though significant changes require a public hearing. 
The previously approved plan allowed for an expansion of up to 198,062 square feet; 
however, in the section of the code 18.140.200.A.zch states that for any significant 
decrease in the peripheral setback of more than five percent, this plan must come 
before the commission with a public hearing. The applicant proposed to extend the 
setback greater than the additional 14.5 feet that would have been allowed.  
 
The new plan is to expand a portion of the building to the north, enclosing a portion 
that was a walking path from the employee parking lot to the store. This enclosure is 
to allow for the expansion for the online pickup and delivery service, as well as 
associated parking. The proposed landscaping includes some replacements and larger

the City Forester and Community Services team to intervene. The applicant has 
addressed the concerns through the updated landscaping plan. The landscaping and 
architectural design plans will be reviewed further at the time of the final development 
plan. The proposal will be in line and complementary with the existing Walmart 
architecture. Staff recommends approval of Revised Preliminary Development Plan No. 
PDP2026-00020, for the Walmart online pickup and delivery expansion, subject to 
stipulations a through m.

are at a 6.5 percent peripheral setback, which triggered the need for a public hearing. 
Walmart proposes to increase the online pickup and delivery operations at the site. 
There are currently 11 online grocery pickup and delivery spots. Walmart is proposing 
to increase the parking for dedicated spots for pickup and delivery from 11 to 39. The 
5,300 square foot expansion is for employees and to increase the online pickup 
operations. This allows for employees to pick up items from a consolidated area. They 
are proposing to update the landscaping. They agree with stipulations a through m.

UNIFIED DEVELOPMENT ORDINANCE NO. PLM2026-00040 – Amendments to the 
Unified Development Ordinance (UDO) Chapters 18.140, 18.460, and new Chapters 
18.376 and 18.377, pertaining to the Kansas Senate Bill 418. Application made by the 
City of Overland Park. 
 
(Approved) 
 
Senior Planner Danielle Hollrah stated previously, staff asked this commission for 
permission to publish this for a public hearing in light of the new Kansas Senate Bill 
418. Earlier this year, the state approved and enrolled Senate Bill 418 in regards to 
residential housing, planning, development, and construction law. The bill went into 
effect July 1, 2026. City staff is proposing these amendments and the new code 
section to align with the new state legislation. Until these amendments are adopted, 
staff is administratively following the requirements of the new law since it took effect.  
 
The bill is divided into five sections, which were summarized in the staff report. 
Section one is the By-Right Housing Act, which calls for approval for plans when 
residential development meets the City zoning criteria and are to be approved by right 
administratively. Section two of the bill allows for third party review and inspection. 
Overland Park is not taking advantage of this and it would primarily be used by small 
communities in the state that may not have professional staff to review all plans and 
conduct all inspections; Overland Park is staffed so this does not apply to the City. 
Section three calls for streamlined development for homes under 2,500 square feet. 
Section four revises the notification requirements and eliminates protest petitions for 
single-family zoning requests; there are amendments to provisions in the current code 
to address this. Section five calls for single-family housing to be allowed in all 
locations where residential development is allowed, which includes the City’s multi-
family or apartment zoning districts and there have been provisions added to 
accommodate single-family residential. 
 
In the draft code update, the new Chapter 18.376 responds to section one of the bill 
and calls for administrative approval if the housing application meets the criteria in 
zoning and land-use regulations. To comply with state law, City staff has worked on a 
by-right housing application. There are no code changes proposed for section two. 
Section three of the bill calls for the opportunity for single-family homes under 2,500 
square feet to be built on lots that are 3,000 square feet in area; the proposed new 
Chapter 18.377 addresses this. The City’s standard R-1 Single-Family lot requires a 
minimum of 8,000 square feet. The current RP-1 and Infill district would allow lots 
down to 4,250 square feet; 3,000 square feet is less than anything the City currently 
allows for. The state law allows cities to apply appropriate setback standards. In the 
City draft amendment, there is a lot width requirement of 40 feet and setbacks 
comparable to the RP1-N Infill district to acknowledge how a home should fit onto a 
potential 3,000 square foot lot; this is a minimum requirement and a home under 
2,500 square feet could have a larger lot. Under state law, the City must accommodate 
this.

There are proposed amendments for Section four of the bill in Chapter 18.140, which 
is the applications and procedures area of the code. This removes the notification 
requirement related to single-family rezoning requests and removes the protest 
petition opportunity for single-family rezoning requests. Single-family development is 
allowed where any residential development is allowed under Section five, and the City 
has added a provision in RP-3, RP-4, RP-5, and RP-6 districts that allow for single-
family with the setback and lot size standards proposed for the 3,000 square foot lots. 
The smaller lots for single-family were considered appropriate for multi-family areas. 
 
Other proposed updates include removing the public hearing requirement for 
preliminary plats. The City currently has this requirement and it is not a requirement 
in the state statute. Due to Senate Bill 418, the time frame, and encouragement of 
housing development, the City believed making this change was in line with the other 
provisions of the bill. In line with the smaller housing contemplated under the bill, 
there are two sections in 18.460 for subdivision regulations that the City currently has 
provisions for developers to declare minimum dwelling sizes; these provisions will be 
removed. A subdivision’s Homeowner’s Association (HOA) could still have their own 
private restrictions; however, it would no longer be in City code.  
 
Staff added a provision to the new chapters for any unforeseen conflicts between 
existing provisions in the Unified Development Ordinance (UDO) and Senate Bill 418 
that the Planning Director have authority to interpret the UDO in a manner that 
complies with this new statute. These edits were made in light of the state legislation 
that went into effect July 1, 2026, and the City will keep these changes in mind as 
they rewrite the entire UDO. Staff felt these changes were required to be made 
immediately to be able to respond to any by-right applications they may receive. The 
Ordinance Review Committee considered the text of the amendment prior to this 
meeting, and they voted 4 to 0 to recommend approval as proposed. Staff recommends 
approval of PLM2026-00040, pertaining to Kansas Senate Bill 418, with no 
stipulations.
