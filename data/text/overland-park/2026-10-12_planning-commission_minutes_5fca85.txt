PLANNING MISCELLANEOUS – PLM2026-00071 – 2026-2027 Comprehensive Plan 
Annual Updates. Application made by the City of Overland Park. 
 
(Approved) 
 
Senior Long-Range Planner April Snay presented the 2026-2027 Comprehensive Plan 
annual updates. The Kansas statute for enabling comprehensive plans requires the 
Planning Commission to annually review, or reconsider, the Comprehensive Plan. This 
helps to ensure that the content reflects the Overland Park goals, policies, and that it 
remains up-to-date. The recommended amendments this calendar year are minor.  
 
In 2026, staff proposes an update to the plan document to revise an error and change 
the responsible party for two (2) action items, as well as minor updates to the Future 
Development Character Framework Map. In 2026, the proposed changes include 
amendments to the Official Street Map, which will be considered with a public hearing 
at the November 2, 2026, Council meeting, along with minor cartographic refinements 
that include refining right-of-way and parcel boundaries, without affecting any of the 
designated character areas. No changes are proposed to the designated character 
areas at this time. 
 
Ms. Snay addressed the three (3) proposed changes to the FrameworkOP document. 
One change was a correction to the land use character area matrix that showed 
live/work as a supported use for compact neighborhoods. This change was to align 
with the current wording on page No. 59 of the Comprehensive Plan describing 
character areas. The other two changes are intended to change the responsibility for 
two (2) action items in the mobility chapter, item Nos. MB-6 and MB-7. These pertain 
to an update to the Greenway Linkages Map, guidelines, and update to the Safe 
Bicycle Use Outreach project, otherwise known as Overland Park Bicycle Plan. These 
action items identify the Parks and Recreation Services Department and Public Works 
Department primary responsible parties. Staff was proposing to change the primary

responsible party to the Planning and Development Services Department, which is 
currently listed as a partner. This change is in preparation for the upcoming Active 
Mobility Plan, which will consolidate both planning efforts into one (1) plan for active 
transportation. The Parks and Recreation Services Department and the Public Works 
Department will then be, in turn, proposed to change to partners for these action 
items, which will provide essential support in this multidisciplinary effort. This 
concluded her updates to the plan document. 
 
In addition to the required annual review, Ms. Snay relayed that staff created a 
Comprehensive Plan Progress Report, which they intend for an annual update. The 
overall intent of this report is to provide a concise update of the plan status action 
items and on how the plan will be implemented. They will break the report down into 
three (3) main categories: 1) Status update of the action items; 2) things to come in the 
upcoming years; and 3) how the plan impacts development. 
 
At a high level, Ms. Snay said there was one (1) action item already completed and 19 
in-progress, which meant that significant action had already been taken to implement 
four (4) action items that remain pending for initial discussion and six (6) that have 
yet to begin. The report provided summaries of the action items in-progress or those 
pending. Highlights included Action item No. 1 of the housing chapter, which is the 
implementation of the Permit-Ready Housing Unit Program. In November 2025, this 
was accomplished with the rollout of the Portfolio Homes Program and with the pilot 
program that offers greater flexibility. Other progress updates include continued 
progress of the Unified Development Ordinance (UDO) and the Quality-of-Life 
Dashboard, which staff hopes will be posted online by the end of the year, various 
parks and recreation projects and the recently adopted Playbook OP and Urban Forest 
Plan, as well as other transportation studies. The Solid Waste Study continues to 
move forward, as do many other projects, all of which were summarized in the report.  
 
Ms. Snay continued that the report highlights some things where staff expects signifi-
cant progress within the next couple of years. These things include completing the 
draft of the UDO update and implementation, as well as kicking off the Active Mobility 
Plan, which will address a couple of the action items she mentioned earlier in the 
mobility chapter. Several of the transportation-related studies are nearing completion, 
in addition to the Active Mobility Plan that will inform the finalization of the City’s Safe 
Streets Policy and prioritizations for infrastructure projects. 
 
Additional implementation of Framework OP beyond the action items, includes a 
review of the proposed development applications, which will ultimately align with the 
overall plan and is the focus of the next section of the report. The level of review for 
development applications is dependent on several factors, including the level of change 
in the case of preliminary development plans and if a public hearing is required, and 
in the case of special use permits, if it is a renewal or new construction. All rezonings 
undergo a full Framework OP analysis and include a review of the character areas, 
preferred and supported uses, the site design through the relative building blocks, and 
consideration of the plan’s goals and policies. 
 
In the timeframe of this report, which is August 1, 2025, through July 31, 2026,  
Ms. Snay said that the Planning staff conducted a full analysis of Framework OP for 
15 rezoning cases, four (4) preliminary development plans, and three (3) special use 
permits. The report provides a table with links to each rezoning case, a summary of 
the request, and the Council action. Lastly, the report highlights areas within the

REZONING NO. REZ2026-00010 – Oak Park Mall – Vicinity of the southeast corner of 
95th Street and Quivira. Rezoning requested to CP-2, Planned General Business 
District, and MXD, Mixed-Use District, to allow changes to the existing Oak Park Mall. 
Application made by CBL Properties. 
 
(Continued) 
 
Senior Planning Keith Gooch presented Rezoning No. REZ2026-00010, Oak Park Mall, 
in the vicinity of the southeast corner of 95th Street and Quivira, from CP-2, Planned 
General Business District, and MXD, Mixed-Use District, to allow a new drive-thru 
restaurant to the existing Oak Park Mall. The property is located at 11855 West  
95th Street. This item was continued from the September 14, 2026, Planning 
Commission meeting.  
 
Mr. Gooch presented a vicinity map, noting that the property is entirely zoned 
commercial at this time. There is residential to the east across Nieman, as well as to 
the south, including additional retail. To the north is more retail and the Shawnee 
Mission School District-owned property, and commercial property to the west across 
Quivira, which is all located in the City of Lenexa, Kansas. He presented an aerial 
map, noting the subject piece of property. 
 
Mr. Gooch stated that FrameworkOP designates the mall as a Regional Activity 
District. At the September 14, 2026, Planning Commission meeting, the Planning 
Commission asked staff and the applicant to address the design screening along  
95th Street, the pedestrian access and walkways, and the drive-thru exit on 96th 
Street to the south on the previous On the Border Mexican restaurant site.  
 
Mr. Gooch reviewed the revisions being presented by the applicant. He presented a

slide that showed the mall, the former Chick-Fil-A site, and the newly proposed Chick-
Fil-A site. The old On the Border restaurant will be demolished to build the new 
establishment. This is a 5,157 square-foot Chick-Fil-A restaurant, with 95th Street to 
the north. The entrance off 95th Street is from the west. Access into the drive-thru is 
located at the southeast corner of the site going north and transferring around the 
restaurant. The previously requested deviation involved the applicant not meeting the 
10-foot setback. The new plan shows a 10-foot setback, so a deviation is no longer 
requested. Other changes to the site plan include sidewalks from Cody, which is a 
street not named in the City’s naming system, but is how they referenced it on the 
plan. There is a sidewalk that runs along the north side of the private drive to the 
south, and a pedestrian access was removed so vehicles are able to exit right-out only 
and transverse to the north to get to the Chick-Fil-A site. Other changes relate to the 
architectural design for the 95th Street canopy, as well as on the south side. The knee 
wall and landscaping remain along 95th Street and runs down Cody. Staff stipulated 
that the sidewalk be extended to the property line to allow for future connections if the 
project to the east is ever reconstructed.  
 
Mr. Gooch said the landscape plan is similar to the previous plan and meets ordinance 
requirements. He pointed out a drive on the site map that was previously set at 24 feet 
to allow for two-way traffic. That drive has been reduced to 16 feet, in an attempt to 
keep that area an exit-only. He presented the north elevation changes, which showed a 
wider canopy and faux windows. The west elevation along Cody includes the pick-up 
window canopy and to the north, there is a roof over the outdoor seating area and an 
order canopy. All signage is required to meet code and will be discussed further at the 
time of final development plan. The order area looks southeast and includes three (3) 
faux windows, a thick canopy, outdoor seating area, and knee wall that extends 3- to 
4 feet along 95th Street with additional landscaping. At the corner is another larger 
wall that will help screen the drive-thru from the Cody and 95th Street intersection, as 
well as the knee wall that goes along the property line farther south.  
 
Mr. Gooch stated that staff reviewed the traffic and stormwater analyses and could 
answer any questions. He noted that there is a rezoning for the overall mall that will 
come before the Planning Commission in November, which will impact the Chick-Fil-A 
to some degree, but staff continues to work through the details with the applicant. An 
additional traffic analysis will be conducted with Oak Park Mall during that process. 
Staff stipulated the presented design, as well as the right-out only exit. He presented 
colored elevations of the proposed building. While FrameworkOP defines this area as a 
Regional Activity District with the CP-2 zoning, that district does not allow drive-thrus 
in the plan, which was reviewed in the staff report. The site has been zoned CP-2 since 
the most recent rezoning in 1995, which is a conflict and something staff struggled 
with, and also relates to why they requested that the applicant design the drive-thrus 
in a better way, to align and screen better with some of the newer drive-thrus across 
the City. With these changes, staff was recommending approval of Revised Preliminary 
Development Plan No. PDP2026-00016, Oak Park Mall, 11855 West 95th Street, 
subject to stipulations a through o. He welcomed any questions.
