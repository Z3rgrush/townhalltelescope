ITEM TITLE:
Active Mobility Plan.
POLICY ISSUE:
The Community Development Committee has policy oversight over matters related to the Planning 
Department.
STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
None.
PROPOSED MOTION:
None.
BACKGROUND:
FrameworkOP includes a goal area for mobility, which recognizes that the implementation of the future 
land use vision for Overland Park is only possible with a supportive strategy for mobility and 
transportation. The plan prioritizes a safe, comfortable, and efficient transportation network and 
identifies key initiatives for enhanced trail networks and pedestrian connectivity, improved walkability 
and bikeability, and context appropriate street systems. 
 
In recognition of the multiple modes of transportation the plan intends to bring together in one 
document, staff is recommending calling the effort the Active Mobility Plan. The plan will address three 
action items from FrameworkOP: 
 
A-MB.6 - Update and Implement the Greenway Linkages Map and Guidelines 
A-MB.7 - Update and Implement the Safe Bicycle Use Outreach Project (BikeOP)
A-MB.8 - Update the Complete Streets Policy 
 
In preparation for this planning effort, the staff began reviewing barriers and opportunities to create an 
enhanced mobility system through an integrated active transportation planning workshop led by Happy 
Cities and a bike network benchmarking tour in northwest Arkansas led by Laneshift. These efforts 
have provided a strong interdepartmental foundation from which the plan can be developed and 
implemented. 
 
The Active Mobility Plan is intended to evaluate existing facilities and policies, conduct community 
outreach, and develop a plan that identifies a preferred network, future projects, and implementation 
strategies. Specific objectives include:
Development of a final plan which identifies the Active Mobility Transportation Network to 
integrate biking and walking into a seamless and complete network of on- and off-street routes 
for bikes and pedestrians;
Establishment of a Vision and Policy Framework including guiding principles; definitions of 
success and the review and assessment of existing mobility policies;
Development of criteria for context appropriate mobility options;

Development of network alternatives and project prioritization;
Development of standards for context sensitive walkable, bikeable street designs; 
Development of a phased implementation plan and funding strategy for construction and

The Consultant Team will have experience in transportation planning, specifically for bicycle and 
pedestrian systems, transportation modeling, transportation engineering, policy development, 
community engagement, communications, and other expertise necessary to complete the project

Staff anticipates releasing the RFQ by the end of September, with Consultant Team presentations and

as defined by Governing Body Resolution No. 5007, which requires a Selection Committee consisting

ITEM TITLE:
Approval - Urban Forest Plan.
POLICY ISSUE:
The Community Development Committee has oversight over issues related to the Parks and 
Recreation Department. The Urban Forest Plan sets a strategic vision to preserve and enhance the 
City’s tree canopy, fostering a healthy and resilient urban ecosystem.
STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends the approval of the Urban Forest Plan.
PROPOSED MOTION:
I move to recommend City Council approval of the Urban Forest Plan.
BACKGROUND:
On March 4, 2024, City Council approved an Agreement with Multistudio, Inc. for development of the 
Urban Forest Plan. The purpose of the Urban Forest Plan is to set a strategic vision to preserve and 
enhance the City’s tree canopy, fostering a healthy and resilient urban ecosystem.
 
Multistudio Inc. began by gathering data to inform the plan's development via: public engagement with 
a survey and a table at the Farmers' Market, staff interviews and questionnaires, plural authority 
meetings, data collection from the GIS Section and other publicly available resources, and review of 
existing city and regional policies and related documents as outlined in the Agreement. 
 
The first draft of the plan was received in January 2025, and was subsequently reviewed by high-level 
staff members in multiple departments including: Parks and Recreation, Planning and Development, 
Public Works, and City Manager's Office. The plan has undergone two revisions since that first draft, 
with staff reviews by the same personnel as well as the Communications and the Sustainability 
Divisions. The Legacy of Greenery Committee reviewed the second draft of the plan, and voted to 
endorse the plan during its meeting on May 7, 2026.  The plan presented tonight is the third and final 
draft of the Urban Forest Plan
