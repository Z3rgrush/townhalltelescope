POLICY ISSUE:
The Community Development Committee has policy oversight over matters related to the High Quality 
of Life strategic goal area.  
BACKGROUND:
In 2022, the Overland Park City Council adopted its first set strategic goals, which are now reviewed 
and refined on an annual basis. The City Manager’s office compiles an annual mid-year report 
documenting the city's progress toward these adopted goals.
Released in August, this report offers a comprehensive blend of performance measure data and 
qualitative updates outlining progress toward the Governing Body’s strategic goals. The performance 
metrics included represent the 2025 calendar year, utilizing the 2024 calendar year as a comparative 
baseline unless specified otherwise.

☐ Create and Sustain Safe and Healthy Neighborhoods
☒ Create and Sustain a High Quality of Life

INITIATIVE 3: 
PILOT PARTNERSHIP TO PROVIDE A BEHAVIORAL
HEALTH SPECIALIST IN MUNICIPAL COURT
The role of Specialty Court Clinician was established in early
2025 as a pilot program in partnership with Johnson County
Mental Health to better serve the Court’s population of
defendants struggling with mental health barriers. A licensed
clinician from Johnson County Mental Health is now available
full-time at the Overland Park Municipal Court, with the City
providing reimbursement utilizing Opioid Settlement Funds.
Due to the program's success, this position transitioned to
permanent status and is now a vital component of
operations. 
The Specialty Court Clinician provides direct client support
and crisis intervention, conducting extensive administrative
coordination and case consultations. This has facilitated
more efficient court proceedings, timelier routing, and
enhanced case planning within the Mental Health Diversion
teams at Johnson County Mental Health. Mental Health
Diversion referrals rose from 18 in 2024 to 24 in 2025, marking
the highest referral rate to date, with initial data from the
first six months of 2026 indicating a sustained upward trend. 
Another key achievement of the program is the ability to
review police reports prior to the defendant’s first
appearance, allowing for early identification of those who
need additional support, and screening those who are already
receiving services at Johnson County Mental Health. This
enables proactive coordination with existing case
management plans, and much needed insight into the
defendant’s current challenges. 
Additionally, the program introduced supplementary
resources, including hygiene supply backpacks for unhoused
defendants, free public access naloxone (Narcan), and
gunlocks distributed on an as-needed basis.
We look forward to the continued success of our partnership
with Johnson County Mental Health, and will report future
data points as they become available.

WHAT DOES UTSTEIN MEAN?
Utstein is a standardized template of uniform reporting
guidelines for clinical variables and outcomes for cardiac
arrest patients.
Utstein: A bystander witnessed a cardiac arrest and the
patient was found in shockable rhythm.
Utstein Bystander: A bystander witnessed the cardiac arrest,
intervened through CPR or the use of an AED, and the patient
was found in a shockable rhythm.

City staff selected Burns and McDonnel as consultant
and began work on a comprehensive solid waste study
in July 2025. The study was completed in May 2026. It
includes recommendations for the City Council to
discuss and evaluate updates to the existing system,

organized collection service will be presented to the
City Council in the fall.

The update of the Unified Development Ordinance (UDO)
began in 2025. The update intends to modernize the existing
regulations to better align with FrameworkOP including
consolidating existing zoning districts to align with the
character area recommendations from FrameworkOP. 
In early 2026, the staff presented the proposed zoning
districts and a summary of a market study to the Council
Committee of the Whole and the City’s Planning
Commission. Outreach to the public occurred with an in-
person and virtual public open house, on-line survey and pop-
up information events at the Farmer’s Market and Blue
Valley branch of the Johnson County Library. 
A working draft of the UDO is anticipated by the end of the
year. Staff has outlined a series of policy discussions with the
Governing Body regarding housing, development standards
for commercial uses and open space, procedural standards
and an implementation strategy to assist staff in prioritizing
specific elements to draft in the new UDO. A public comment
period, including input from the Technical Advisory Panel,
will assist with fine tuning a final draft. Public hearings to
consider the final draft are expected in early 2027.

In spring 2024, City staff began work on a set of green

Ordinance No. MW-3522 - Levying special assessments against private properties for unpaid expenses 
incurred by the City, for costs associated with cutting weeds and noxious vegetation, removing trash

POLICY ISSUE:
The Community Development Committee has policy oversight over matters related to the Property 
Maintenance Code.  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends City Council passage of Ordinance No. MW-3522.  
PROPOSED MOTION:
I move to recommend the City Council passage of Ordinance No. MW-3522.
BACKGROUND:
Between June 1, 2025 and June 1, 2026 the Community Services Division responded to thousands of 
requests for service involving property maintenance issues. Pursuant toOverland Park Municipal Code 
7.25.106.5 Abatement of Violation, when property owners fail to correct violations of City ordinances on 
private property, staff issues work orders for cutting weeds and noxious vegetation, and for the 
abatement of other nuisance violations. Failed inspection fees are also assessed for cost recovery 
purposes. Of the total amount billed, there are $35,300.35 in contractor work order charges and failed 
inspection fees that remain unpaid and are scheduled for special tax assessment. To recoup 
outstanding contractor work order charges and failed inspection fees, a special tax assessment must 
be filed with the Johnson County Department of Records and Tax Administration by August 31, 2026. 
Attached is an overview of costs associated with the work orders and failed inspection fee charges as 
well as a chart highlighting the historical data for total assessments by year.

☐ Create and Sustain Economic Well-Being and Housing

The special tax assessment process provides an opportunity to recover costs incurred by the City. 
These outstanding costs are related to unpaid expenses and result from property owners failing to 
correct property maintenance violations. The net effect on the Overland Park budget is neutral in that

assessing outstanding fees for failed inspections encourages compliance from property owners and 
generates revenue for the City for the purpose of cost recovery.
