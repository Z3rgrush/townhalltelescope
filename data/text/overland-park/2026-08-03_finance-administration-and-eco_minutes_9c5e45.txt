Mr. LaSala referred to the phases in the development agreement, noting Phase 1 is the 
only project area they would be advancing at this time and the others would follow.  
This is also the only part of the project that is contractually guaranteed.  The other 
phases are somewhat speculative, but their approach is similar to what they have 
done with other development agreements where they have large scale development 
with multiple phases, which require partnerships with retail and office partners.  This 
is a concept they call minimum improvements, such as how much has to happen.  
This development agreement included negotiations between them and Black and 
Veatch about how many additional phases would come and what the implications are 
if they do not come.  They are not in default if the other phases do not happen, but 
there are significant financial implications in the incentive package that will give them 
two of the four additional phases in addition to the headquarters Phase 1.  They have 
financial implications attached to them getting at least three of the five phases that are 
contemplated in the agreement. 
 
Mr. LaSala referred to the public park, noting there is a planned green space but not a 
public park.  Black and Veatch plans for that would involve some programming and 
good uses.  They also negotiated a public gathering space the City could build on the 
City Hall property.  Black and Veatch has agreed to contribute $4 billion to design, 
development and construction of the public gathering space on the City property as a 
part of a discussion of the evolution away from a public park in the middle of the 
district.  
 
Mr. LaSala presented the timeline for the different project phases.  Phase 1 includes 
project area 1A and 1C, which would occur together.  This portion would start by the 
end of this year and complete by December 31, 2030.  The next two phases with 
deadlines could happen in any of the four project areas, and the first would commence 
by the end of 2032 and complete by the end of 2035.  Another of those phases is to 
commence by the end of 2035 and complete by the end of 2038.  The last two phases 
will commence by 2038 and complete by 2041.  The TIF District work shows 13 project 
areas.  Project Area 1A is Phase 1, including the headquarters portion south of the 
existing building and also includes the OP residences project that Black and Veatch 
does not have control of but it is in Project area 1A.  Project Area 1 has evolved, as 
they have previously discussed splitting up that project into 1A and 1B.   The reason 
for the split is that Copaken would develop the 1B portion, which would likely be on a 
different timeline.  In their discussions with Black and Veatch about this project area, 
they also wanted to add a Project Area 1C, which is where the parking garage would 
be located.  The structure of the financing and the amount of time needed to 
reimburse the cost of that parking garage would make sense to have it be its own 
separate project area.  When they amend the plan for the district, it will include the 
three project areas opposed to what was discussed earlier.

Mr. LaSala referred to the TIF District, stating they had a base year of 2025, and the 
first project area they would begin discussing would include Project Area 1A.  He 
presented the caps on the amount of property tax increment that can be collected in 
the project areas and devoted to the developer to reimburse eligible costs.  The first 
part, which is the headquarters project, is $132.5 million.  The other cap amounts 
relate to the future phases.  If the plan happens as planned, there could be up to a 
$253 million TIF project.  Regarding TIF, up to 100 percent of the increment could be 
available for Project Areas 1A and 1C, which is the headquarters project and the 
associated parking garage.  All the other project areas would get a 90-percent 
increment, not 100 percent.  The reason it is up to a 100-percent increment in the 
first project is because they have negotiated financial implications about getting two 
additional phases.  In areas 1A and 1C, they will start at 90 percent in their contract, 
and each earns up to 95 percent and then 100 percent by completing the additional 
phases.  If they only timely build one of those additional phases, they will get 95 
percent of the increment collected.  If they build both additional phases on time, they 
would get 100 percent of the TIF increment for both of those phases.  The TIF is 
entirely Pay-as-you-Go (PAYGO) with no possibility of bonds.  For each of the project 
areas, they contemplated 20 years of TIF collection based on when they approve the 
applicable project area.  The only exception to that is 1C with the parking garage, 
which may benefit from more than 20 years of reimbursements, because that project 
area might be brought forward at a later date.  It is a way to maximize the amount of 
TIF revenue that could be used to pay for the parking garage costs.  There is a unique 
point of the deal that contemplates TIF sharing contributions from other areas outside 
the Black and Veatch project site, which is part of a negotiated deal that Black and 
Veatch has with its neighbors with Copaken in project area 1B and the Block Galleria 
development to the east, both of which have negotiated with those other partners 
about sharing the TIF increment from these project areas in these amounts.  More 
details will eventually be attached to the development agreement with the Copaken 
deal of 67.5 percent of the increment from that being shared with Black and Veatch.  
That deal contemplates them on a schedule and will finish by April 1, 2032.  With the 
Copaken sharing contemplated, it is capped at $25 million of contributions from the 
Galleria project to the Black and Veatch project.  After that, Galleria would get 100 
percent of the TIF increment from those projects.     
 
Mr. LaSala presented the public/private ratios they agreed to in all of their 
development agreements, which goes by phase and project area.  In Phase 1, the cap 
for project areas 1A and 1C is no more than 20 percent of the project cost, resulting in 
80 percent private investment, 20 percent public at the maximum TIF cap.  The same 
would be true of Phase 2, which shows the public investment in that phase in that 
project area.  These are all within the range of what they typically find, and some of 
them are lower. 
 
Mr. LaSala presented the Community Improvement District (CID) component of the 
project, stating most of the CID sales tax and retail contemplated would come from the 
Black and Veatch site which is a 1.5 percent add-on sales tax for 22 years.  It could be 
increased up to 2 percent when the Transportation Development District (TDD) 
expires, but it is also entirely a PAYGO vehicle with no possibility of bonds.  The TIF 
cap is $11.6 million, but there is emphasis on financial implications to the two 
additional phases they want.  The CID cap starts at zero, and Black and Veatch earns 
half of that CID cap by completing at least one additional phase, and would go to 5.8 
percent if they do that.  If they timely complete two additional phases, the CID cap

improvements for the parking area north of the Convention Center, which are all

Mr. LaSala noted they were taking a similar approach to the TDD, although it is a

Galleria site.  The projects they want to spend the City part of the money on would 
include the headquarters site, road improvements and the parking area to the north.   
 
The final piece of the incentive package would include Economic Development 
Revenue Bonds (EDRB) for the limited purpose of the sales tax exemption on the 
construction materials and equipment, but not for Ad Valorem property tax 
abatement.  It is contemplated to go on a phase-by-phase basis where they are 
submitting EDRB applications for each phase. 
 
Mr. LaSala noted there were some things to discuss about the development agreement 
with respect to the headquarters, and jobs.  They negotiated those provisions, and 
Black and Veatch is committing to have this project, after it builds the nine-story 
headquarters facility, promising it will be Black and Veatch’s world headquarters 
facility, which means a majority of the senior executives and the chief level executives 
will be located at this facility.  They also committed there will not be more employees 
in Kansas City anyplace else, and only focused at this location.  They also have some 
retention provisions about the 2033 fulltime employees Black and Veatch currently 
has.  They would have a minimum average wage of at least $100,000.  If they do not 
maintain that level of employment over the course of the agreement with the average 
wage, then their TIF cap could be proportionally reduced for Phase 1 of the project 
based on how far off they are from 2,033 jobs or how far below the $100,000 average 
wage they are.  There is a cap on the reduction that could happen, and the worse it 
could ever be is a 40-percent reduction.  Although that is a big number at $52.8 
million, it would turn their $132 million TIF for Phase 1 into about an $80 million TIF, 
which is a significant penalty.
