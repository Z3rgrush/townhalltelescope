COUNCIL PRESIDENT MELISSA CHEATHAM 
 
 ACTION AND VOTE: 
Council President Melissa Cheatham provided a report, stating that last week, the Council voted to adopt 
PlaybookOP, the City's long-range Parks and Recreation Plan. At that same meeting, they began 
discussions about how to pay for the Plan's ambitious goals, including improving and maintaining the 
City's existing assets and adding new parks and trails. Tonight, the Committee- of-the-Whole continued 
that discussion to determine how to address the $248 million funding deficit between current funding and 
the community goals embodied in PlaybookOP. PlaybookOP identified several potential funding sources. 
Tonight's agenda included a discussion of one potential funding source: a staff -recommended dedicated 
Parks and Recreation sales tax. Sales taxes are paid by anyone who shops in Overland Park, including 
visitors who use the City's parks and trails. The City Council cannot add new sales taxes on its own and 
must be approved by the voters. The discussion this evening was whether to ask voters to approve a 
dedicated sales tax and if so, at what level. After discussion of three options—a 1/8, 1/4, or 3/8-cent sales 
tax increase—the Committee of the Whole ultimately chose to ask voters to approve a dedicated 1/4- cent 
sales tax. If approved by the voters, this dedicated funding source will allow the City of Overland Park to 
improve and maintain its existing assets and begin investing in new facilities according to the PlaybookOP 
goals. No vote required.
