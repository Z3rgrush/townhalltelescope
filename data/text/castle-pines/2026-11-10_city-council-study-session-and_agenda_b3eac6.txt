CITY COUNCIL COMMUNICATION FORM
ACTION ITEM
TO: Mayor Engerman and City Council
FROM: Donna Ferguson, AICP, Community Development Director
DATE: November 10, 2026
RE: Ordinance No. 26-12, An Ordinance of the City Council of the City of Castle Pines, 
Colorado, Accepting the Conveyance of Certain Tracts From the Canyons 
Metropolitan District No. 7 Pursuant to the Public Improvements Dedication, 
Operation and Maintenance Agreement Between the City of Castle Pines, Colorado 
and The Canyons Metropolitan District No. 7 (Tract Group 1) on First Reading
Policy Question: 
Does City Council support, on first reading, approving the conveyance of specific recreation 
properties from The Canyons Metropolitan District No. 7 (the "District") to the City, entering into 
an easement agreement with the District for certain improvements on those properties that the 
District will retain, and authorizing City staff to record the associated deed and easement 
agreement in the real property records of Douglas County? 
Background: 
In 2023, the City’s voters approved Ballot Item 2F, authorizing the City to increase City ad valorem 
property taxes to fund park, recreation, trail and open space amenities and improvements subject 
to an equal decrease in ad valorem property taxes imposed by Title 32 metropolitan districts on 
properties located within such metropolitan districts.
The District is a Title 32 metropolitan district that has a service area covering what is commonly 
referred to as “Shea Canyons” on the east side of I-25.  The District owns tracts of land within its 
service boundaries that include public parks, recreation, trail and open space amenities and 
improvements (“Recreation Properties”).
To implement the intent of Ballot Item 2F, the City and the District previously entered into that 
certain Public Improvements Dedication, Operation and Maintenance Agreement Between the 
City of Castle Pines, Colorado and The Canyons Metropolitan District No. 7, dated March 11, 
2026, as amended ("IGA"), to memorialize the District's agreement to reduce its mill levies for 
operation and maintenance costs of the Recreation Properties and the City’s agreement to accept 
ownership, operation, and maintenance of the Recreation Properties, excepting therefrom certain 
improvements to be retained by the District.

Pursuant to the IGA, City staff and District staff conducted a review and prepared an inventory of 
all tracts owned by the District to identify the Recreation Properties that the City could accept for 
ownership and maintenance and to identify the types of improvements situated in or attached to 
the Recreation Properties.  The City and the District agreed that certain types of improvements 
that are primarily for private rather than public benefit were not to be included as improvements 
to be owned by the City.  These “Retained Improvements” generally include perimeter fencing 
adjacent to private property, mailbox pads and lighting, and retaining walls that support private 
property.
The Recreation Properties were separated into three (3) groups based on the types of 
improvements in the tracts.  Tract Group 1 includes tracts that only have public improvements 
that will be accepted by the City or improvements that are excepted by category (e.g., all mailbox 
units and pads are excepted and retained by the District).  Tract Group 2 includes tracts that have 
retaining walls that will be retained by the District.  Tract Group 3 includes tracts that have 
retaining wall improvements that will be owned by the City as well as retaining walls that will be 
retained by the District.
In addition to conveying the Recreation Properties to the City, the District has retained easements 
in certain tracts to access and maintain the improvements the District will continue to own.  An 
easement agreement for each Tract Group was prepared between the City and the District to 
clarify the rights and obligations of both parties relating to the easements.
The Ordinance is limited to the City accepting the conveyance of the tracts in Tract Group 1, which 
are identified in Exhibit A to the Bargain and Sale Deed With Reservation of Easement, attached 
to the Ordinance as Exhibit 1 (“Deed”), and approving the Retained Improvements Easement 
Agreement, attached to the Ordinance as Exhibit 2 (“Easement Agreement”), between the City 
and the District related to the District’s retained easements over the tracts in Tract Group 1.
The District’s Board of Directors has authorized the conveyance of the tracts in Tract Group 1 to 
the City by way of the Deed and Easement Agreement.
Staff Analysis:
The City’s Home Rule Charter, Section 14.6, provides for Council action by ordinance to 
purchase, sell, or exchange interests in real property. The conveyance of the tracts in Tract Group 
1, along with the District’s exception and retention of certain improvements as described in the 
exhibits to the Deed and Easement Agreement, implements the agreements between the City 
and the District in the IGA.
Fiscal Impact:
Ballot Item 2F is implemented by this transaction, and the City’s costs of maintenance and 
operations are covered, in part, by the property tax revenues generated by the allowed 12 mills, 
which is offset by the District decreasing its allowed property tax mills by an equivalent amount, 
subject to the IGA’s assessed-valuation adjustments.
Staff Recommendation: 
City staff recommends approving Ordinance No. 26-12 on first reading.

3. Notice and Access.  Except in an emergency, the District shall give the City at least five (5) 
business days prior written notice before performing work within an Easement Area. The

approval requirements of the City for the types of work that require additional City approvals.

a. minimize interference with the City’s ownership, operation, maintenance, and use of the

b. use existing roadways to the extent available to access Retained Improvements; 
c. comply with applicable laws, permits, and governmental requirements; 
d. maintain the Easement Areas in a clean and safe condition during its work;

or in part by acts or omissions of the District, its employees, agents, contractors, 
subcontractors, assigns, lessees, licensees and agents, including, without limitation, 
landscaping, irrigation, trails, sidewalks, utilities, and other City-owned improvements;  
f. remove its equipment, materials, and debris promptly after completing its work; and 
g. not take any action or omit to take any action which could result in a claim for an 
encumbrance, subordination, or hypothecation of the City’s property, including the 
Easement Area, by any lien, pledge, not or other obligation.
