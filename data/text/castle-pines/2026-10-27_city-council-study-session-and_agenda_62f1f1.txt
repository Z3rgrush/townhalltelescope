Re: Ordinance 26-11, An Ordinance of the City Council of the City of Castle Pines, 
Colorado, Vacating a Portion of South Havana Street within the Final Plat 
Canyonside Subdivision Filing No. 4 on First Reading 
 
Policy Question: Does the City Council support the adoption of Ordinance 26-11, which would 
authorize the vacation of a portion of South Havana Street right-of-way (Case No. RV-2026-
001)? 
 
Executive Summary:  North Canyons 
Development Company (the “Applicant”) 
is requesting the vacation of a portion of 
the South Havana Street right -of-way 
following the completion of the roadway 
realignment associated with the 
Canyonside Subdivision Filing No. 4. 
See Vicinity Map. 
 
As part of the public improvements for 
Canyonside Subdivision Filing No. 4, the 
City Council approved the realignment of 
South Havana Street to  create better 
access and traffic flow in the area. The 
new realigned roadway is now open to 
traffic, and the old alignment is no longer 
needed. The Applicant is requesting t he 
City to vacate the portion of the original 
right-of-way that is no longer needed,  
with title to vest in North Canyons LLLP.  
 
Pursuant to the road vacation procedures established in the Castle Pines Subdivision 
Regulations, road vacations associated with a roadway realignment are subject to review and 
approval by the City Council at a public hearing. 
 
Vicinity map: right-of-way to be vacated shown in blue.

Background: On April 8, 2025, the City Council approved Resolution 25- 17, approving the 
Canyonside Subdivision Filing No. 4 (Case No. FPL- 2023-001). The subdivision, generally 
located north of Castle Pines Parkway and east of I -25, established three mixed- use lots, two  
tracts, and dedicated new right-of-way for the realignment of South Havana Street. 
 
The public improvements associated with the subdivision included the realignment of South 
Havana Street, which was previously contemplated through two Intergovernmental Agreements 
(IGAs) approved by the City Council on July 25, 2023: 
 
• Resolution 23-35: Approved an IGA between the City and Douglas County regarding the 
relocation of South Havana Street. 
 
• Resolution 23-36: Approved an IGA between the City, Canyons Metropolitan District No. 
3, and North Canyons LLLP regarding the South Havana Street realignment. 
 
The IGA between the City and Douglas County acknowledged the City's legislative authority to 
vacate all or a portion of the original South Havana Street right-of-way following the dedication of 
a new right-of-way, the completion of roadway improvements, and the City's acceptance of those 
improvements. Similarly, the IGA between the City, Canyons Metropolitan District No. 3, and 
North Canyons LLLP contemplated that the City would process an ordinance to vacate the portion 
of South Havana Street that was replaced by the new alignment. 
 
With the South Havana Street realignment completed, the new roadway open to traffic, and the 
City having granted preliminary acceptance of the public improvements, this Ordinance 26-11 
would authorize the vacation of the portion of the original South Havana Street right -of-way 
contemplated under the IGAs. 
 
Staff Analysis: Staff has reviewed the proposed right-of-way vacation and finds that the portion 
of South Havana Street proposed for vacation is no longer necessary for public roadway purposes 
following the completion of the roadway realignment associated with the Canyonside Subdivision 
Filing No. 4. The new roadway alignment is open to traffic and has received preliminary 
acceptance from the City, providing continued public access and circulation.  
 
The proposed vacation is consistent with the previously approved Canyonside Subdivision Filing 
No. 4 and the IGAs approved through Resolution Nos. 23-35 and 23-36, which contemplated the 
vacation of the original South Havana Street right-of-way following the roadway realignment. The 
vacation would eliminate right -of-way that is no longer needed for its original public roadway 
purpose while maintaining the public roadway network through the newly dedicated alignment , 
and vest in North Canyons LLLP in accordance with §43-2-202 C.R.S. 
 
A request for external referrals  was sent to the following agencies in accordance with the 
Subdivision Regulations: Parker Water and Sanitation District, Canyons Metropolitan District No. 
2, South Metro Fire and Rescue, Colorado Department of Transportation, Douglas County 
Sheriff’s Office , Douglas County Assessor, Douglas County Addressing, Planning, and 
Engineering, and public utility providers. Comments were either no comment, no conflict, or 
approval. See Attachment B.  
 
Pursuant to the road vacation procedures outlined in the Subdivision Regulations, a road vacation 
may be approved by the City Council based upon the following:

705B.01. A platted or deeded road or portion thereof or unplatted or undefined roads 
which have arisen by public usage shall not be vacated so as to leave any land adjoining 
said road without an established public road or private access easement connecting said 
land with another established public road. 
 
Analysis: The proposed road vacation will not leave any adjoining properties 
without access to an established public road. South Havana Street has been 
realigned, and the new roadway is open to traffic, maintaining public access and 
connectivity to the surrounding roadway network. 
 
705B.02. A vacation of a S tate highway must be approved by the Transportation 
Commission. 
  
Analysis: The proposed road vacation is not a S tate highway; therefore, it is not 
applicable.  
 
705B.03. The road vacation, or vacation and replat is in keeping with the spirit and intent 
of the City Subdivision Ordinance, and the City Comprehensive Plan and Transportation 
Plan as adopted by the City. 
 
Analysis: The proposed road vacation is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan 
and the Transportation Plan. The Castle Pines Transportation Master Plan, 
approved in October 2025, contemplates the realignment of South Havana Street. 
 
705B.04. The road vacation will not diminish the ability to provide adequate emergency 
services and will not increase the cost of services. 
 
Analysis: The proposed road vacation will not diminish the ability to provide 
adequate emergency services or increase the cost of services. South Havana 
Street has been realigned, and the new roadway is open to traffic, maintaining 
access for emergency service providers. The roadway realignment was completed 
at the Applicant's expense, and the City will continue to maintain the newly 
realigned roadway in accordance with its prior  maintenance responsibilities. As 
such, the vacation is not anticipated to result in additional service costs to the City. 
 
705B.05. The approval will not adversely affect the public health, safety, and welfare. 
 
Analysis: The proposed road vacation will not adversely affect the public health, 
safety, and welfare. The realigned portion of South Havana Street is open to traffic 
and maintains public access and circulation. 
 
Fiscal Impact: The roadway realignment was completed at the Applicant's expense, and the City 
will continue to maintain the newly realigned roadway in accordance with its prior maintenance 
responsibilities. As such, the vacation is not anticipated to result in additional fiscal impacts to the 
City. 
 
Staff Recommendation: Based on the findings described herein, staff recommends City Council 
adopt Ordinance 26-11.

Evaluation: 
Subdivision Use: The properties subject to this Replat are located within Planning Areas 3, a 
portion of C, and F of The Canyons PD. The Canyons PD, as amended, outlines the zoning for 
Planning Areas 3, C, and F as: 
• Mixed Use – Planning Area 3 
• Open Space Limited – Planning Areas C and F 
 
Mixed Use is intended to allow for a variety of uses, including a range of residential densities

and housing types. Open Space- Limited is intended to be primarily maintained in its natural 
character, providing passive recreational activities, drainage facilities, water and wastewater 
facilities, trails, and primarily native landscaping.  
 
This Replat proposes 69 residential lots, 7 tracts utility, drainage, retaining walls, and open 
space uses, and the dedication of right -of-way. The proposed uses conform to the permitted 
uses for each planning area. Residential lots are generally located within Planning Area 3, and 
tracts for utility, drainage, and open space uses are generally located in Planning Areas C and 
F. There is a small portion where residential extends into Planning Area C, and open space 
extends into Planning Area 3.  Per The Canyons  PD, these types of minor adjustments to the 
planning area boundaries are permitted, provided they do not alter maximum densities, 
minimum open space, or other development requirements. The open space requirements for 
Planning Areas C and F will exceed the minimum dedications required with this Replat.   
 
Subdivision Layout: The Replat will include one new single-family detached neighborhood. New 
right-of-way will be dedicated and constructed to connect to Sweet Creek Lane to the west (from 
the Madison Apartments) and extend east to Cross Canyon Trail. The proposed subdivision will 
include residential lots generally ranging from 5,000 sq. ft. to 9,999 sq. ft., with two lots exceeding 
10,000 sq. ft. These lot sizes are similar to those of most single- family detached neighborhoods 
in The Canyons PD. The northwestern portion of the proposed subdivision will largely comprise 
an open space tract with some new and existing trails, and the southeastern portion includes 
additional open space, creating a buffer between planning areas. Trails are integrated throughout 
the proposed subdivision and connect to the extensive Canyons trail network. Retaining walls are 
proposed adjacent to backyards and open spaces. Access to the proposed subdivision will be via 
new residential streets that connect to the exist ing street network at Cross Canyon Trail and 
Sweet Creek Lane.  
 
Dedications: The Replat does not provide for additional dedications for parkland and schools, as 
these dedications have been satisfied with prior preliminary plans and final plats. The minimum 
dedications required for The Canyons PD have been exceeded.

Terracina Design. The report concludes that the proposed subdivision will conform to the Douglas 
County Storm Drainage Design and Technical Criteria Manual and the Mile High Flood District 
Drainage Criteria Manual, where applicable. As part of the review process, the City’s consultant 
engineers reviewed the report and concurred with the analysis and conclusions. See Attachment 
E.

by Felsburg Holt & Ullevig. The letter provided an update on land uses and trip generation for the 
existing and proposed uses within Planning Area 3, including the proposed subdivision, which 
was initially analyzed in the Canyonside Subdivision Traffic Impact Study, dated November 2020. 
Compared with the development assumptions evaluated in the November 2020 traffic impact 
study, the proposed subdivision is anticipated to generate fewer daily, AM peak -hour, and PM 
peak-hour trips. As  such, the proposed subdivision can be accommodated within the existing 
street network, and no additional traffic mitigation is necessary. As part of the review process, the

outlined in the letter. See Attachment F.

A. Site Location 
This Phase III Drainage Report provides remediation for changes in the drainage patterns 
resulting from the construction of Canyonside Filing 1 - 3rd Amendment , from here on 
known as “Site” . The Site is currently undeveloped and future development will include 
single family residential lots, open space, local residential roads, and utility infrastructure.  
 
Canyonside Filing 1 - 3rd Amendment  is bound to the north by Canyon Forge Drive , 
Canyonside Boulevard to the west , Cross Canyon Trail to the east, and Newlin Gulch to 
the south. It is located within portions of Planning Areas 3 and C within Section 2, Township 
7 South, Range 67 West of the 6 th Principal Meridian, City of Castle Pines, Colorado.  Tract 
A, Canyonside Filing No.1, 2nd Amendment, together with Lot 3, Canyonside Filing No.1 was 
platted with the Canyonside Filing No.1 Plat, reception No, 2021054776. A vicinity map for 
the Site can be found in Appendix A.

II. DRAINAGE BASINS AND SUB-BASINS 
A. Major Drainage Basins 
A majority of the existing drainage patterns within the major basins will generally follow 
historic patterns . The development lies within Newlin Gulch Basin . Once the overland 
discharge from the Extended Detention Basin s (EDB) reaches Newlin Gulch, the flows will  
be conveyed through  Newlin Gulch to Reuter-Hess Reservoir then, to Cherry Creek and 
eventually they will become a part of the South Platte River. 
 
Historically, the H-Basins, identified in the Canyonside Preliminary Plan No. 1 Phase II 
Drainage Report, dated December 18, 2020, contained approximately 26.2 acres draining 
to Newlin Gulch. Canyonside Filing 1, 3 rd Amendment accounts for 26.67 acres which 
includes 0.28 acres from Basin OS1 from the Peak at Canyons Phase III Drainage Report 
Dated November 5, 2025. This runoff will be captured by Pond H which will be discharged 
through an outfall pipe to the south to Newlin Gulch. Offsite basins account for 2.86 acres 
which will directly sheet flow into Newlin Gulch.  The studies and analysis can be found in 
Appendix B. Refer to Appendix G for the proposed drainage map.

III. STORMWATER MANAGEMENT FACILITY DESIGN 
A. Stormwater Conveyance Facilities 
Low Impact Development (LID) practices and strategies have been applied to the 
comprehensive land planning and engineering design approach to managing 
stormwater runoff. The primary objective of the Project is the preservation of the property’s 
natural features  by arranging the development to minimize site grading, impacts to 
existing vegetation and wetlands, as well as providing large open space areas. The 
drainage design will generally maintain the historic drainage patterns and release rates 
for the Site. The EDB’s on Site have been located to minimize subsurface systems and 
control the developed discharge prior to entering the established waterways thus 
reducing the impact to Newlin Gulch.  
 
Runoff will be captured by 5 -foot, 10-foot, and 15-foot Type R inlets at on-grade and in 
sump locations, along with Type C  & D  inlets for area drains. The runoff will then be 
conveyed via a subsurface system toward the proposed EDB. The EDB will discharge less 
than historical rates via a pipe from the outlet structure to Newlin Gulch. 
 
B. Stormwater Storage Facilities 
The Site will utilize one proposed EDB (Pond H) for water quality and detention storage. 
The basin was designed as an EDB (WQCV, EURV, 100-year) and will be released at 90% 
or less of historic rates per the criteria. EDB and 100-year detention pond volumes were 
calculated using the MHFD detention basin design workbook (MHFD-Detention, Version 
4.07, June 2025). A copy of the output file for the ponds has been included in Appendix 
D.  
 
The proposed EDB, Pond H, includes two designed forebays that will improve the pond’s 
water quality. Forebay Calculations can be found in Appendix D. The proposed grading 
and outlet structure design of EDB Pond H, that can be found in Appendix D, will support 
the basin delineation and discharge going into Newlin Gulch. Based on the Rational 
Method calculations for predevelopment conditions, the historic peak flow into Newlin 
Gulch from the H basins was calculated at 37.30 cfs for the 100-year storm event. Pond H 
and the outlet structure are now designed to release a peak flow of 32.10 cfs during the 
100-year storm event. Downstream protection of the spillway shall be included in the 
form of a rip rap rundown to reinforce the embankment of the ponds spillway. As a result, 
the EDB Pond H outfall should not cause any impacts to Newlin Gulch, since the 
proposed conditions will detain more flow than was historically released.   
 
The design of the EDB is compliant with the Colorado Revised Statute 37 -92-602 (8) 
pertaining to stormwater and detention facilities. The applicable MHFD spreadsheet and 
calculations are in Appendix D. In addition, per the new statute, the maximum micropool 
volume will be no greater than 1% of the 100-year volume including water quality. 
 
C. Water Quality Enhancement Control Measures  
Water quality measures have been included in the design of the proposed EDB. The basin 
has been designed to incorporate a structure that releases flows for the water quality 
capture volume (WQCV), excess urban runoff volume (EURV), and the 100 -year storm 
event. The design of these structures has been provided in Appendix D.

drainage basins throughout the Site. These basins were delineated based on the natural

was sized for a 100 -yr storm and will be released  below historic rates. The storm sewer 
system will be designed to capture the minor (5-year) and major (100-year) storm events.
