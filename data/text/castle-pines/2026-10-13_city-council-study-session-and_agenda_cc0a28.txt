CITY COUNCIL COMMUNICATION FORM 
ACTION ITEM 
 
TO: Mayor Engerman and City Council 
 
FROM: Donna Ferguson, AICP, Community Development Director 
 
DATE: October 13, 2026 
 
RE: Ordinance 26-10, An Ordinance of the City Council of the City of Castle Pines, 
Colorado, Amending the Boundaries of the Three Districts of the City in 
Accordance with Section 2.3 of Article II of the City Home Rule Charter on 
First Reading 
 
Policy Question: Does the City Council support the redrawing of electoral district 
boundaries? 
 
Background: Article II, Section 2.3 of the Castle Pines Home Rule Charter (City Charter) 
creates three voting districts. This same Article and Section of the City Charter also 
provides that boundary changes must be finalized at least 180 days before any election.  
 
There is no hard rule regarding when redistricting is required due to population differences 
between districts. However, the general rule is that redistricting should occur when 
population differences between districts exceed 10%. Many municipalities trigger 
redistricting when the population differences between districts by 2% to 5%. Considering 
this, the City can redistrict when population differences between districts exceed 5%, but 
should redistrict when they exceed 10%. 
 
Analysis: Ongoing development within The Canyons and Castle Pines Town Center has 
resulted in changes in the population of the three voting districts. As of September 1 , 
2026, the total population for the three voting districts is as follows: District 1 = 5,667; 
District 2 = 5,989; and District 3 = 6,378 (Attachment B). The percentage change in 
population between the district with the highest population (District 3) and the one with 
the lowest population (District 1) is 11.8%.  
 
Because the population difference between Districts 3 and 1 is greater than 10%, staff 
recommends that the Council redraw the electoral district boundaries to achieve a 
population distribution for the three voting districts as follows: District 1 – 6,002; District 2 
= 6,066; and District 3 = 5,967 (Attachment C). For this proposed redistricting, the

In redrawing the district boundaries, staff considered constitutional, judicial, state, and 
City Charter requirements . Constitutional requirements include both mathematical and 
racial equality in redistricting. The Equal Protection Clause of the U.S. Constitution 
requires each district to be drawn as nearly as practicable so that one person's vote is 
worth the same as another’s, though mathematical precision is not required. Further, the 
Equal Protection Clause requires racial equality, ensuring that minority voting strength is 
not purposefully diluted by redistricting. A final constitutional consideration is whether the 
redistricting results in political gerrymandering, where redistricting divides a group of 
voters who share common ethnic, racial, or religious backgrounds, resulting in "common 
interests…strong enough to…manifest in political action."  
 
In addition to the above constitutional considerations, the following judicial redistricting 
factors were considered: compactness, continuity, and communit ies of interest. 
Communities of interest include, but are not limited to, geography, demography, ethnicity, 
culture, and socioeconomic status.  
 
State law requires all municipalities to set boundaries for election districts and finalize 
them at least 90 days before any election. However, the City Charter provides that 
boundary changes must be finalized at least 180 days before any election, superseding 
the state's 90-day requirement.  
 
Fiscal Impact: There is no financial impact to the City.  
 
Staff Recommendation: Staff recommends that the Council redraw the electoral district 
boundaries to balance the population across the three districts.

ORDINANCE NO. 26-10 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF CASTLE PINES, 
COLORADO, AMENDING THE BOUNDARIES OF THE THREE DISTRICTS OF THE 
CITY IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 2.3 OF ARTICLE II OF THE CITY HOME 
RULE CHARTER 
WHEREAS, the City of Castle Pines (the “City”) is a home rule municipality with all 
powers granted by Article XX of the Colorado Constitution and the City’s Home Rule Charter 
(the “Charter”); and 
WHEREAS, Section 2.3 of the Charter divides the City into three districts, requires the 
districts to conform to constitutional requirements, and authorizes the City Council to change 
district boundaries by ordinance; and 
WHEREAS, the current boundaries of each district are established pursuant to Ordinance 
No. 24-05; and 
WHEREAS, pursuant to Section 2.3 of the Charter, changes in the boundaries of districts 
must be made by ordinance adopted by the City Council at least one hundred eighty (180) days 
prior to any regular or special election to be effective for such election; and  
WHEREAS, constitutional principles require that voting districts have roughly the same 
number of residents (within a 5-10 percentage differential) with equal representation under a one 
person-one vote standard; and  
 
WHEREAS, the general rule is that redistricting should occur when population differences 
are greater than 10%; however, most Colorado municipalities trigger redistricting when the 
population difference between districts is between 2-5% (See White v. Register, 412 U.S. 755, 
763 (1973)); and 
 
WHEREAS, development within the Canyons and the Castle Pines Town Center has 
changed the distribution of residents among the three districts; and 
WHEREAS, City Staff has reviewed the latest available data and has determined that 
redistricting is warranted because the percentage change in population between the district with 
the highest population (District 3) and the lowest population (District 1) is 11.8%; and  
 
WHEREAS, the City Council has considered the population information and proposed 
redistricting presented by City staff, together with the applicable constitutional requirements and 
the considerations of contiguous and reasonably compact districts and communities of interest; 
and 
WHEREAS, the City Council desires to adopt the district boundaries depicted in Exhibit 
A, attached to and incorporated into this Ordinance, in conformance with constitutional 
principles and the Charter. 
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF CASTLE PINES, 
COLORADO, THAT:

district boundaries shall continue to govern. The City Clerk shall determine and document the 
applicable boundaries before administering each affected election.

by this Ordinance, including updating City election materials and coordinating with the Douglas 
County Clerk and Recorder as appropriate. Administrative implementation shall not alter the 
boundaries depicted in Exhibit A.

Policy Question: Does the City Council support approving a p rofessional services 
agreement with Alfred Benesch & Company to provide construction management 
services for Phase 1 of the Happy Canyon Road and I-25 interchange project? 
 
Background: The Happy Canyon i nterchange project will require a small construction 
management (CM) team to support the City in managing all aspects of construction. This 
will include pay item documentation, quality control, traffic control management, and 
materials management, among other tasks.  
 
The City  went through the request for proposals (RFP) process, soliciting for a 
construction management team. Through the process, four proposals were received and 
evaluated.  A panel, comprised of City, County, and Colorado Department of 
Transportation staff, all agreed that Benesch was the most qualified and supported 
pursuing a CM contract with them.   
 
Staff Analysis: Not only was Benesch selected by the RFP review panel, but the City 
has been working with Benesch through the engineering on- call agreement for the last 
18 months. During that time, Benesch has proven it has the required expertise, 
experience, and staffing to efficiently and effectively help deliver the Happy Canyon 
interchange project. Additionally, the same Benesch team that was selected for Happy 
Canyon also delivered and managed the much larger Crystal Valley i nterchange project 
in Castle Rock . City staff contacted Douglas County and the Town of Castle Rock for 
references, and both resulted in strong endorsements from those agencies.

contract, an independent cost estimator reviews and analyzes the contractor's unit 
pricing. The cost estimating staff from Stanton models and analyzes the project 
independently to develop a construction schedule and unit prices. This is then compared 
to the Contractor’s  construction schedule and unit prices . The City and the project 
management team review the comparative analysis and identify items that need further 
discussion. Often, the discrepancy stems from a minor difference that can be easily 
resolved or justified with project-specific information.  Either way, each item is discussed 
until both parties reach an agreement, with the goal of keeping the independent cost 
estimate within 5% of the construction pricing.

to justify and present their costs for the project, including labor, equipment, materials, and 
overhead. This analysis helps the City and Douglas County know that the construction

PUBLIC HEARING: Ordinance 26-08, An Ordinance of the City 
Council of the City of Castle Pines, Colorado, Approving the 
Conveyance of Certain Recreation Properties from the Castle Pines 
North Metropolitan District to the City of Castle Pines Pursuant to 
the Intergovernmental Agreement Between the City of Castle Pines 
and the Castle Pines North Metropolitan District Regarding 
Operation, Maintenance and Transfer of Recreation Properties on 
Second Reading 
 
Policy Question: Does City Council support the adoption of Ordinance 26- 08, which 
would authorize the conveyance of several recreation properties from Castle Pines North 
Metro District (CPNMD) to the City?  
 
Background: In March 2023, the City Council approved an intergovernmental agreement 
(IGA) with CPNMD where the City and CPNMD agreed to transfer ownership and 
operations of the District's parks, open space, trails, recreation facilities, buildings and 
associated improvements, and fixtures from CPNMD to the City subject to, among other 
terms, the City receiving voter authorization to impose a property tax of 12 mills to fund 
the City's obligations. The City was successful in passing ballot measure 2F, and voters 
approved a 12- mill property tax for maintenance and capital improvements to parks, 
recreation, open space, and trails.  
 
In July 2024, an amendment to the IGA was approved, which authorized the necessary 
survey and engineering work to complete the administrative tasks of accurately identifying 
the properties, creating and developing the necessary water and sewer easements, and 
presenting those documents for approval. As part of the process to identify the properties 
to be conveyed to the City, it was discovered that some properties would require a replat 
in order for CPNMD to maintain ownership of its water and sewer infrastructure while 
being able to convey any park, open space, trail, recreation facilities, buildings and 
associated improvements, and fixtures to the City.

Staff Analysis: Through Resolution Nos. 26 -42 through 26- 47, City Council approved 
the replat applications necessary to subdivide the remaining CPNMD properties. The 
approved replats place the applicable open space and water and sewer infrastructure on 
separate tracts, al lowing CPNMD to retain its utility infrastructure while conveying the 
remaining open space tracts to the City in accordance with the IGA. 
 
The newly created tracts proposed for conveyance to the City total approximately 76.37 
acres and include: 
• Tract R1, Romar West 3rd Amendment 
• Tract E-1, CPN No. 27, 7th Amendment 
• Tracts G1-1 and Tract CC1-1, CPN No. 27, 8th Amendment 
• Tract A-1 and Tract A-3, CPN No.  17A , 1st Amendment 
• Tract A, CPN No. 30 
• Tracts C-1 and C-3, CPN No. 16, 1st Amendment 
 
Approval of Ordinance 26- 08 will allow the City to accept ownership of these tracts and 
complete this portion of the property transfer contemplated by the IGA. 
 
Fiscal Impact:  Upon conveyance, the City will assume ownership and maintenance 
responsibility for approximately 76.37 acres of open space, including existing open space 
and associated drainage infrastructure. The costs associated with maintaining and 
improving these properties are anticipated to be funded through the 12- mill property tax 
authorized by voters through Ballot Measure 2F. 
 
Staff Recommendation: Based on the terms of the IGA between the City and CPNMD 
and the findings described herein, staff recommends that the City Council approve 
Ordinance 26-08.
