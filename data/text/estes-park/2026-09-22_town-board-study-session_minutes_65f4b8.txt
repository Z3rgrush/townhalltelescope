Annexation of Enclaves Follow Up.
At the March 24, 2026 study session, the Board directed staff to provide additional
information on potential costs/benefits in the annexation of two (2) enclaves: Fawn
Valley Enclave and Highway 7 Enclave. Planner Hornbeck reminded the Board the
Municipal Annexation Act of 1965 allows a municipality to annex an enclave with or
without property owner consent when an area has been completely surrounded,
excluding right-of-way, by a municipality for a minimum of three (3) years. He stated the
Town annexation policy indicates enclaves should be evaluated on a case-by-case
basis. He further noted research determined; the Fawn Valley would not qualify as an
enclave due to a gap in the Town Boundary. This was due to an "orphaned" strip of land
separated from the adjoining properties on either side of the river that was never
annexed and has no apparent ownership. He stated a possible explanation could be
previous legal descriptions of adjacent properties referred to the bank, or centerline of
the river, which subsequently shifted course. If the Town Board desires to pursue
annexation of the Fawn Valley properties, the gap would first need to be annexed. Staff
stated the Highway 7 area was eligible for annexation as an enclave. Per the Board's
desire for public input, staff mailed letters to owners of all 19 properties within the
Highway 7 enclave. Staff received four (4) responses in opposition to annexation and
one (1) in favor. Financial impacts of annexation were reviewed including: a one-time
cost to hire a consultant to create an annexation map and legal description estimated at
$50,000; an annual increase of $12,750 in Town sales tax and $1,900 in property tax;
cost of $2,400 annually for 25 years for road maintenance to bring Chalet Ridge Court
roadway up to Town standards, and reviewed residential water rates which varied by
usage. Advantages and disadvantages remained consistent as identified by staff in
March 2026. Staff requested Board direction regarding the annexation of the enclaves.
Board comments and questions have been summarized: it was clarified Trustee Brown
brought this item forward at a previous meeting where the Board directed staff to
provide and update after further research; concerns were heard related to the tax
increase to property owners which may be a significantly higher than estimated by staff;
staff were directed to contact Larimer County Assessor to provide assistance in
identifying the owner(s) of the gap in the parcels; highway maintenance responsibility;
the number of commercial and residential properties within the enclaves; whether there
were any anticipated development projects in the area which may require annexation;
concern was stated related to gaps in the areas maintained by the Town; responses
were primarily dissenting and this was a good exercise to hear community sentiment;
equity and fairness were mentioned related to properties benefiting from Town services
while not annexed into the Town, therefore they are not paying the taxes. Staff would
contact Larimer County Assessor to identify the owner(s) within the gap identified in the
Fawn Valley enclave.

Feather Flag Sign Code Review.
Through adoption of Ordinance 15-25, the Board approved temporarily allowing feather
flags in certain areas of Town, subject to specific requirements, and ending January 6,
2027, unless otherwise terminated or extended by the Board. Highlights of the
ordinance were reviewed including: the feather flag would not require a sign permit or
fee; were temporary in nature; were not permitted in any residential zoning district or
downtown; limited to only one (1) flag per lot not to exceed 12 feet in height; the flags
would be considered a freestanding sign included in max signage calculations, and
required a setback of at least 12 feet from any street, alley or sidewalk. Director
Careccia reviewed advantages including the ability for businesses to utilize the flags for
additional advertising at a low cost and disadvantages including the signs are prone to
damage from the elements, they can be visually impactful or distracting and establishing
standards can help mitigate those disadvantages mentioned. Staff sought Board
direction on extending the temporary allowance, allow the temporary use to terminate,
or incorporate feather flags within a permanent code revision. Board discussion ensued
and has been summarized: feather flags appear to be a simple, helpful and effective
marketing tool for business owners; it was mentioned two (2) feather flags may be
visually appealing versus one; mixed Board support for no fee versus fee and permit
required versus no permit; whether staff had received complaints; these signs should be
treated like banners; ensuring the allowance of feather flags was equitable and fair for
all business owners and well defined in the code; and the importance of ensuring signs
do not impede the visibility for vehicles and pedestrians. Staff affirmed a single sign per
lot would be manageable from an enforcement standpoint and mentioned on occasion,
there are multiple businesses housed on a single lot. Discussion ensued related to
chalk which staff responded the Estes Park Municipal Code prohibits chalk advertising
which has had to be enforced in the past. Town Administrator Machalek stated staff
have been discussing opportunities to re-establish and re-educate on signs overall. He
stated there had been a proliferation of signs which are not allowed per the code. Staff
are planning an education campaign on signage in early 2027 and informed the Board
the community would see a more proactive approach next season on sign enforcement.
Staff confirmed Board consensus was to continue to allow feather flags for another year
and review a more long-term decision thereafter.
Black Canyon Water Treatment Plant Property.
Town Administrator Machalek reviewed the background of the Black Canyon Water
Treatment Plant ("the property"). This item was requested by the Town Board at the
June 23, 2026 study session. The property consists of two (2) Town-owned parcels of
land adjacent to Rocky Mountain National Park (RMNP) and MacGregor Ranch.
Combined, the properties are roughly 1.65 acres and contain the decommissioned
Black Canyon Water Treatment Plant and water tank. Neither asset held current or
future use as it related to the Town's water system. He stated discussions regarding
disposal of the property date back to at least January 28, 2014, when the Town Board
held a study session to discuss an inventory of Town-owned property. At that time, the
Board agreed to allow staff to move forward with the disposal of the Black Canyon
property to the neighboring property owners. Since that time, efforts to dispose of the
property have proceeded in fits and starts. MacGregor Ranch approached the Town
with interest in the property at least twice and recently, since publishing the agenda item
for discussion, RMNP also stated interest in the property. Staff affirmed the Town has
no identified future use for the property and the 2014 rational for disposal remained
valid. Staff recommended disposal of the property through donation. Disposal would
allow the Water division to avoid costs associated with demolishing the existing
structure, as well as ongoing maintenance costs. Staff requested Board direction on the
desired type(s) of access or conservation easements on the property in order to engage
in conversations with MacGregor Ranch representatives. The Town has not typically
held conservation easements in the past and would need to work with a third party if the
Board was interested in pursuing this option. RMNP currently holds conservation
easements on MacGregor Ranch land adjacent to the property. Staff requested
feedback from the Board on whether the property should be disposed of as previously
determined or complete the full process identified in Policy 226 and options to donate.
Board discussion ensued and has been summarized: questioned if both entities were
willing to accept the property in its current state; it was mentioned there are trails on the
