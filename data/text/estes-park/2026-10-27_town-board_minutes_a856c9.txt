Ron Wilcocks/County Resident expressed concern about the cost of the proposed Public
Safety Facility but agreed a new facility was warranted. He referenced a new facility in

more rapidly to emergencies. He recommended eliminating the second floor of the
proposed facility and instead expanding the first floor or continuing to house some public
safety personnel, such as dispatch, in Town Hall in order to reduce construction costs.

1.A. Ordinance 16-26 Amending Section 3.3 (Code Amendments) of the Estes
Park Development Code. Mayor Hall reopened the public hearing. Director
Carrecia reviewed the information presented at the October 13, 2026 meeting. He
emphasized the proposed change of Review Criteria 1, which would evaluate
requested zoning amendments by first determining if the, "amendment is
substantially consistent with the Future Land Use Plan of the Estes Forward
Comprehensive Plan". Substantially was added to the proposed criteria at the
request of the Board at the October 13, 2026 meeting. This determination would
be made by the Town Board. If the requested amendment is not substantially
consistent with the Future Land Use Plan, then it must be either, "necessary to
address a change in conditions since the establishment of the Code, in the vicinity
of the amendment area, which area's extent is subject to the interpretation of the
Board of Trustees - October 27,2026 - Page 3
Board of Trustees in its reasonable discretion" or "correct a technical error in the
text of Official Zoning Map of this Code". Director Carrecia emphasized zoning
amendments would be permissible if they met one of the above criteria, they would
not need to meet both. Review Criteria 2 considered potential adverse impacts to
surrounding properties. The sentence, "such mitigation shall satisfy this standard
of review," was removed from the proposed review criteria after the October 13,
2026 meeting. The third amendment to Section 3.3 would codify the Town Board's
existing authority to legislatively establish townwide and large-scale changes to
the text of the Development Code or the Official Zoning Map. The Planning
Commission unanimously recommended approving the amendments.
Board comments ensued and have been summarized: questioned the rationale of
codifying the Board's existing legislative authority, Town Attorney Kramer stated it
was a common practice among municipalities to codify the Town Board's authority
to establish townwide and large-scale development or zoning changes through
legislation in order to differentiate between the Board's quasi-judicial authority for
other land development proceedings, he stated Fort Collins, Loveland, and
Longmont had similar clarifications in their municipal codes; requested additional
information on the difference between the Board's quasi-judicial and legislative
authorities, Town Attorney Kramer explained the distinction had been extensively
established in Colorado case-law and further described how a legislative decision
created rules and quasi-judicial decisions applied the rules; Town Attorney Kramer
reiterated public input on quasi-judicial items could only be heard during the public
hearing, but that did not apply to public input on legislative items; discussed the
process to rezone a property including a pre-application meeting, hosting at least
one (1) neighborhood meeting, technical review by Town staff, public hearing
before the Planning Commission, and public hearing before the Town Board;
discussed projects that may necessitate development plans considered by the
Planning Commission or building permits approved by Town staff; discussed the
definition of "a change in conditions" in the proposed amendment to Review
Criteria 1(a), Town Administrator Machalek stated the Board would need to further
define "change in condition" if both criteria (a) and (b) would need to be satisfied
under Review Criteria 1 for rezonings that the Board deemed were not
substantially consistent with the Future Land Use Plan; Town Administrator
Machalek reaffirmed for the Board the current zoning map was established through
a legislative process in 1999; stated the Comprehensive Plan (Future Land Use
Plan) was the most thorough data the Town had on community opinions about land
use; and discussed the role of the Land Use Plan as a guiding document.
Public comment was heard from Marty Miranda/Town Resident, Jed Eide/Town
Resident, Christy Jacobs/Town Resident, Kristine Poppitz/County Resident, and
Steve Wende/Town Resident. Comments have been summarized: concerned the
proposed amendments and ongoing Development Code update reduced public
input; stated the passing of Ballot Initiative 300 clearly demonstrated resident's
desire for increased participation in land use decisions; urged the Board to
consider changes in condition with a 500 foot vicinity of the property as a criteria
for all rezonings; urged the Board to vote against the amendments; concerned with
the Board making land use decisions through legislative processes rather than
quasi-judicial processes; stated all proposed criteria should be considered for all
properties; requested the Development Plan rewrite be voted upon by the citizens;
and requested more community involvement in the remaining stages of the
Development Plan rewrite to build on prior public engagement.
Additional Board comments were heard and have been summarized: discussed

2. Ordinance 18-26 Amending the Water Rate Schedules of the Town of Estes Park.
Mayor Hall opened the public hearing. Superintendent Fredericks presented the rate
study. The purpose of the study was to establish a financial plan that would support
reliable daily operations and identified improvements. The study analyzed how cost
could be equitably shared among customers. An assessment of the distribution
system found the condition to be between good to poor and supported an ongoing
pipe replacement program. The pumping and storage condition assessment found
tank conditions to be moderate to poor with some pump facilities possessing limited
backup equipment and controls. Water treatment plant conditions were rated fair to
poor with aging equipment, treatment limitations, and limited redundancy found at both
Marys Lake and Glacier Creek Water Treatment Plants. The Integrated Water Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) includes replacing the Glacier Creek Water Treatment Plant,
Board of Trustees - October 27,2026 - Page 5
the Thunder Mountain Storage Tank, the Big Thompson Tank, Fall River Estates
Pump Station, and the aging water mains. Implementing the CIP was estimated to
cost $110 to $150 million. The last rate increase occurred in 2022. AE2S conducted
a rate study to determine the level of funding required to implement the CIP. AE2S
Project Manager Shawn Gaddie presented the findings of the rate study. He shared
the recommendation for a two (2) phase rate increase. In Phase 1, AE2S conducted
a Multi-Year Revenue Adequacy Analysis. They recommended increasing rates 15%
every year for the next three (3) years. The second phase of the study would make
recommendations to equitably distribute the cost of system upgrades based on
updated revenue needs assessments and would consider rate differences for
residential and non-residential customers as well as evaluate tap fees. He stated
Estes Park's limited population growth would not account for a significant revenue
increase, therefore rate increases were needed to complete the CIP.
Board discussion ensued and has been summarized: questioned the required debt
coverage ratio compared to the projected debt coverage ratio, Project Manager
Gaddie explained if these rate increases were adopted the debt coverage ratio would
be higher than average in the second to fourth years of the plan and would then reduce
to a more average debt coverage ratio in later years of the project, this would prepare
the utility to service the projected debt incurred five (5) years into implementation;
questioned when current rates would be doubled, Project Manager Gaddie agreed the
rates would be two (2) to two and a half (2.5) times greater than current rates by the
end of the recommended rate increases; and asked if the recommended rate
increases would be sufficient to fund the CIP, Project Manager Gaddie stated the 15%
increase every year for the next three (3) years was the minimum amount
recommended by the model and that higher rate increases could be detrimental to
successfully funding the CIP.
It was moved and seconded (Eshelman/Brown) to extend the meeting past 10:00
p.m., and it passed with Trustee Igel voting "No".
Board discussion continued and has been summarized: discussed the need to accrue
funds in order to apply for loans to fund the remaining construction; questioned the
cost of the CIP, Project Manager Wesley explained cost estimates would be further
refined and a funding package would be defined in 2027; discussed determining raw
water sources in 2027; questioned when the rate increase would be in effect, staff
stated if approved by the Board on November 10, 2026 the 15% rate increase would
be effective January 1, 2027; discussed further opportunities for public engagement
in 2027; discussed the necessity of completing the CIP; and requested additional
information on water rates in comparable communities, Project Manager Gaddie
agreed current water costs in Estes Park were slightly higher than average. It was
moved and seconded (Hazelton/lgel) to continue the public hearing for
Ordinance 18-26 to November 10, 2026, and it passed unanimously.

5. Ordinance 13-26 Third Amendment to the 2016 Verizon Wireless Land Lease
Agreement Mayor Hall opened the public hearing. Deputy Town Administrator
Damweber shared Verizon had leased land from the Town at the Wiest Parking Lot
since 2016 for a cellular tower facility. The agreement renewed every five (5) years at
which time the annual lease payment increased by 15%. The agreement was in the
third five (5) year term. Verizon requested a third amendment to extend the next
renewal period to 10 years to provide them with assurance prior to investing in
improvements to the existing facility. The 15% increase of annual lease cost would
continue to occur every five (5) years and the renewal period would revert to every
five (5) years at the end of the 10-year lease period. The lease rate would be $23,805
per year for the next five (5) years. Board discussion was heard and summarized:
requested information about the footprint of the lease, staff stated it was 16 feet and
9 inches by 52 feet or 666 square feet; discussed the viability of the site for other uses;
questioned how the lease price was determined, staff stated the rate was competitive
for the space; inquired if other parties had the opportunity to apply for the lease at
renewal, staff explained the site was leased to Verizon through Vertical Bridge who
sought the 10-year lease to make technological upgrades and provide improved
cellular coverage; questioned the process to solicit other offers for the lease, staff
stated the current contract would need to be terminated prior to soliciting other offers;
expressed desire to limit the contract to five (5) years; discussed options to use the
site for other purposes; expressed support for extending the contract to 10-years to
encourage Verizon to update the infrastructure in order to provide improved coverage;
and asked if the Town would receive additional revenue if the land was subleased by
Vertical Bridge, staff stated the Town would not. Mayor Hall closed the public hearing.
It was moved and seconded (Igel/Brown) to deny Ordinance 13-26, and it failed
with Trustees Brown and Igel voting "Yes".
