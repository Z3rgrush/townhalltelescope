Mayor Hall called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m.
2027 Budget Presentation
Town Administrator Machalek stated the 2027 budget has been prepared with an
estimated $90.255 million in revenue and $92,030 million in expenses. The Town plans
to expend $1.775 million in fund balance for capital expenditures. The budget has been
prepared with a revenue budgeted at 5% over 2025 actuals. The Town has been
trending at approximately 5.57% above 2025 for 2026 sales tax collections. The
proposed budget expenditures do not exceed the ongoing revenues in the General
Fund and meets Policy 660 targets for fund balances. He noted that one-time funding
has been used to fund one-time expenses, not ongoing expenses. The budget contains
$400,000 in the Community Reinvestment Fund for the Public Safety facility, increase
unreserved General Fund, and establish a Revenue Stabilization Fund using the
previous Workforce Housing Reserve funds which were established prior to the 6E
funds. This new fund strengthens the Town's financial position when issuing future
debt.
Director Zimmerman reviewed the 2027 budget process, reviewed sales tax allocations
including the 1A sales tax which expires in 2034, reviewed discretionary equipment
replacement reserves, outlined other reserve funds, noted the proposed budget
contains the conversion of a .4 FTE Records Technician to a full-time position in 2027,
and reviewed 2027 transfers between funds.
Community Improvement Plan 2027 - 2031
The Community Improvement Plan (CIP) was developed by the departments submitting
projects, the Town Board completing and setting a Strategic Plan, development of an
operating budget, project prioritization, and the final adoption of the CIP by the Town
Board during the budget review and approval process. The CIP annual summary for
2027 contains $37.660 million in projects including Events Center safety lighting
improvements, rooftop replacement over the Police department, Visitor Center log
restoration and preservation, brown sand tent repair, stormwater management program,
STIP overlay and patch program, STIP parking lot rehabilitation, Wonderview Avenue
trail extension, and Children's Park remodel.
Compensation and Benefits
Manager Lizotte stated the annual compensation survey was conducted by Graves
Consulting LLC. The 2026 market study proposes adjustments to the pay families as
follows: Labor & Trade - 2%, Technical & Professional - 2%, Public Safety - 2%,
Lineworker - 3%, and Management - 2%. The budget also includes a 2% merit pool to
move employees through the pay range.

Staff worked with the Town's insurance broker Howden to review the Town's benefit
package for 2027. She noted the budget proposal contained a 20% increase for medical
in 2027 for both the Town and employee premiums; however, the with the assistance of
Howden and plan design changes the Town was able to secure a 15% increase.
Spousal medical coverage would remain the same for 2027 with the inclusion of
spouses that do not have access to employer coverage. Dental and vision insurance
premiums would see no increase in 2027. All other benefits remain unchanged at the
time of the meeting. PERA has increased employer contributions from 15.8% to 16.84%
in 2027 with the employee contribution remaining at 9%.
Medical Fund
The fund was established as an internal service fund for premiums collected for the
Town's partially self-insurance fund. This fund budgets for estimated premiums, third-
party administration for claims processing and network management, and for stop loss
coverage. The budget contains $5.685 in revenues and expenditures for 2027.
General Fund
General fund accounts reviewed included Human Resources, Legislative, Judicial,
Town Clerk, Executive, Attorney and Finance with additional information noted. Trustee
Lancaster questioned the percentage of the overall budget that accounts for personnel
versus operating costs, to which Town Administrator Machalek noted the personnel cost
make up 30.2% of the overall expenses and in the General Fund personnel and benefit
cost make up 50.2% of the budget. The Legislative budget would reflect a reallocation
of the Platte River Power Authority (PRPA) community funding of approximately
$15,000 to the Outside Entity funding moving forward because the funds are intended to
be used for economic development.
Mayor called a 10-minute break at 10:10 a.m. and reconvened the meeting at 10:23
a.m.
Community Reinvestment Fund
The allocations provide funds for specifically targeted capital projects with funding
primarily through transfers from the General Fund and grant funding. The revenues for
2027 would include $2.657 million from the General Fund and minor interest. Proposed
expenses have been projected at $2.657 million which include debt services for the
Event Center and Visitor Center parking garage, O&M, and capital projects such as the
Town Hall roof replacement and HVAC improvements, Public Safety facility resen/e,
Visitor Center improvements, and Event Center improvements.
Wildfire Mitigation EVFPD Fund
It was noted these funds are a portion of the 1A funds approved by the voters dedicated
to the Wildfire mitigation performed by the Estes Valley Fire Protection District
(EVRPD). The funds to be transferred to the EVFPD are estimated at $438,800.
Workforce Housing and Childcare Fund
Manager Speedlin stated the fund supports the advancement of strategies and
investment for workforce housing and childcare to improve the overall availability,
affordability, and quality of each. The fund contains the revenues from the 6E Lodging
Tax estimated at $6.3 million in 2027 and rent from Mountaintop Preschool for the Town
owned facility. The revenues split 80% to address housing through the Estes Park
Housing Authority (EPHA) and 20% for childcare. A portion of the 6E funds are
maintained annually to fund personnel cost for the Housing and Childcare Manager and
a portion of the Grant Specialist and other administrative costs associated with the
implementation of the annual strategies.
Workforce Housing Regulatory Linkage Fee Fund
Manager Speedlin stated the special revenue fund was established to collect the annual
linkage fee for vacation home licenses. The fund supports local workforce housing
programs and initiatives managed through the EPHA. The 2027 revenue was estimated
at $810,000 with a transfer to the EPHA of the funds through a five (5) year agreement
approved in 2025 and ending in 2029.

Community Services
Visitor Service: Manager Bucci noted revenues of $192,300 received by the Rocky
Mountain Conservancy from the operations of the nature store within the Visitor Center
to develop an interruptive project within the Center in 2027. Overall expenses decrease
with refinement of cost such as janitorial services. Trustee Igel questioned how the
Center measures the guest community relationship, with Town Administrator Machalek
stating the Visit Estes Park Tourism Sentiment Survey could be used to measure the
strategic plan objective.
Events: The division budgeted expenses at $3.6 million and revenues of $982,700.
The budget includes decision packages for Event Center Complex outdoor lighting,
arena groomer, First Peoples Festival expansion with revenue increase to offset the
additional cost, asphalt barn aisles in Barns T, U and V and additional funding for
temporary labor to assist during the summer season.
Police
The Police Department contains four funds for Operations, Communications, Records
and Restorative Justice, and Code Enforcement for a total budget of $9.074 million in
2027 with just under 80% of the budget funding personnel costs. The budget contains
the conversion of a current part-time Records Technician to a full-time Evidence and
Records Technician to manage the storage and disposition of property and evidence as
well as provide quartermaster duties and records support. This position would address
the past audit and report recommendations and meet best practices.
Outside Entity Funding Requests
Management Analyst Simpson presented the 2027 funding for outside entities including
base funding and community initiative funding, and contribution to the Fire District and
Sister Cities dues as outlined in Policy 671. Staff brought forward for the Board's review
and discussion $330,000 in Outside Entity funding, $30,000 in Community Initiative
funding, and no funding for event sponsorships. Board discussion followed and has
been summarized: questioned if the Estes Chamber would be funded with base funding
in 2027, Town Administrator Machalek noted the Town Board discussed the issue
earlier in 2026 and declined to add the Chamber in 2027; noted that Estes Valley
Investment in Early Childhood Success has been provided base funding to support
operations and questioned if 6E funds are strictly being used to support programs and
not administrative costs, Deputy Town Administrator Damweber noted 6E funds are
strictly used for programming; it was stated the Arts District has accumulated other local
support and questioned if the base funding need remains, to which Town Administrator
Machalek noted the ongoing need to support the District; and stated the Senior Center
request should be fully funded at $30,000 as the organization continues to struggle to
meet costs and the Board consensus was to reallocate $5,000 from the fund balance to
fully fund the Senior Center's full request in 2027.
