increase in tobacco use amongst youth, including vaping and nicotine products. She

products on the market have a magnetic appeal come in various flavors appealing to kids,
with some featuring built-in video games in increase usage. She urged the Board to join
other communities in considering policies against these products to help protect the youth
in the community.

Town Administrator Report.
Town Administrator Machalek reported Policy Governance non-compliance issue report
with regard to 3.4.5 specifically purchasing practices contracty to purchasing policies. He
stated the non-compliance was related to inventory purchases for the Power and
Communications and Water Divisions. There has been a longstanding practice to procure
inventory through a competitive quote process, regardless of the size of the order. He
stated this practice goes back to at least 2005 and did not change when original
purchasing policies came into effect in 2011, nor did it change in February 2024 when a
minor update to raise the purchase order threshold occurred. To summarize, he stated
the Divisions were not adhering to the $30,000 bid process outlined in the policy and were
instead accepting competitive quotes. He stated inventory purchases are commonly
treated separately than other puchases which communities make, however, as this was
not addressed explicitly in the policies, the default would be the bid process. This would
be discussed when the Town revises the purchasing policies which was underway. In
the meanwhile, and in order to stay in compliance, staff would obtain one quote from the
public bid sources where a public bid process has taken place to satisfy the requirements
of the policy. In the future, staff would undergo procurement training and use the new
Tyler Technology checklist. Town Administrator Machalek would report again next March
during the regular reporting schedule.

1.A. Ordinance 16-26 Amending Section 3.3 (Code Amendments) of the Estes
Park Development Code. Mayor Hall opened the public hearing. On March 18,
2025 and August 7, 2025, the Town Board and Estes Park Planning Commission
(EPPC) held joint study sessions to discuss review criteria for rezonings. The
Board considered draft amendment language at the May 26, 2026 and June 23,
2026 study sessions and regular meeting on September 8, 2026. Additionally, the
EPPC discussed the item at their August 18, 2026 regular meeting and held a
public hearing on September 15, 2026 recommending approval of the changes
with a minor revision to sentence structure. The draft proposed two (2) new review
criteria along with the carryover of one (1) existing criterion. This revision would
establish procedures for town-wide and large-scale changes and would add
procedural clarity and alignment with the Colorado State Law and practice. Director
Carrecia reviewed all three (3) review criteria including: 1) Consistency with the
Future Land Use Plan of the Estes Forward Comprehensive Plan, 2) The
amendment would not adversely affect surrounding properties or the community
and any potential adverse impacts would be adequately addressed and mitigated,
to the maximum extent practicable, 3) The Town and other relevant service
providers shall have the ability to provide adequate services and facilities.
Board comments ensued and have been summarized: Clarification and examples
of quasi-judicial versus legislative review; how these changes would apply to
rezonings; concerns the changes would create a way to get around Ordinance 11-
25 or have unintended consequences; whether these changes should wait until
the Development Code re-write has been completed; reflection of mitigation under
D.2 may not satisfy the Board or public opinion; and it was mentioned the
Comprehensive Plan had significant public input.
Public comment was heard from Rebecca Urquhart/County Resident, Jed
Eide/County Resident, Kristine Poppitz/County Resident, and Steve Wende/Town
Resident. Comments have been summarized: Questioned the Board's legislative
ability; citizens spoke with the passage of Ordinance 11-25 noting they do not want
the Town to rezone neighborhoods; it was suggested the code be modified to
change "immediate area" to: the area within 500 feet from the area under
consideration; interest in more civic engagement on the issue; and concerns were
heard why this item was not included in the Development Code re-write.
Mayor Hall closed the public hearing. It was moved and seconded
(Brown/Lancaster) to approve Ordinance 16-26 adding the word
"substantially" in section D.1 to read "...substantially consistent with future
land use plan..." and remove "Such mitigation shall satisfy this standard of
review.", in section D.2, and it failed with Mayor Hall and Trustees Igel and Mieras
voting "No".
It was moved and seconded (Lancaster/Hall) to continue Ordinance 16-26 to
the October 27, 2026 Town Board meeting, and it passed unanimously.
1.B. Ordinance 17-26 Amending Section 3.2 (Standard Development Review
Procedures) of the Estes Park Development Code. Mayor Hall opened the
public hearing. Director Careccia presented a proposed text amendment to Section
Board of Trustees - October 13,2026 - Page 4
3.2 of the Estes Park Development Code to modify certain provisions related to
Step 2: Neighborhood and Community Meeting. He reviewed the current process,
a survey of nearby jurisdictions and their neighborhood meeting process and
changes directed by the Town Board during their July 14, 2026 study session and
the EPPC at their August 18, 2026 meeting. Code changes were reviewed

4. Resolution 102-26 2026 Housing Action Plan. The 2026 Housing Action Plan (HAP)
and Housing Needs Assessment Addendum would provide an updated, data-informed
framework for addressing housing needs in the Estes Valley. Manager Speedlin
Board of Trustees - October 13,2026 - Page 6
stated the plan and addendum was completed by Root Policy Research who
conducted the research, updated housing needs data, and facilitated community
engagement. Frankie Lewington/Root Policy Research provided an overview of the
addendum to the 2023 Housing Needs Assessment. He stated State Bill 24-174
mandated Colorado communities to develop a housing needs assessment which
translates findings into strategies and actions to address local housing needs. Since
the Town developed the plan prior to the bill in 2023, some of those requirements had
not been met, and therefore, triggered the need for the addendum. DOLA has
developed a list of affordability strategies and requires plans to have at least two (2)
standard affordability strategies, one (1) long-term and one (1) displacement
mitigation strategy. DOLA requires communities complete a MAP every six (6) years
and submit and update on efforts every three (3) years. He stated completing a HAP
by the end of 2027 would give the Town funding priorities at the State level for grant
opportunities. Molly Fitzpatrick/Root Policy Research provided a review of the housing
needs findings including: Housing prices and rents have increased substantially
reducing affordability for many locals; gaps remain significant for lower-income and
moderate-income residents; high rates of cost burden; extensive affordable housing
waitlists; persistent shortages of workforce and affordable housing; median rent was
up 62% from 2018; average home price is $707,000 more than twice the affordable
price at 100% AMI, and nearly half of Estes Park renters 49%, and 21 % of owners are
cost burdened. Core needs were identified as supply, affordability and security.
Community engagement included: information from the Development Code
engagement and outreach summary; 6E Community Survey to inform 2027 Annual
Funding Priorities; two (2) 6E and HAP community meetings open to the public;
stakeholder interviews; two (2) resident focus groups with Estes Park Housing
Authority residents and waitlist applicants, and conversations with the Estes Chamber
of Commerce Construction Alliance and the Estes Next Generation Network (ENGN).
Lewington/Root Policy Research stated the idea was to build upon the progress and
momentum of the strategies identified in the 2023 plan. He stated the 2023 housing
strategies were identified in broad categories and have been reworked into action
statements over four (4) objectives as follows: expanding and diversifying housing
options; preserving existing workforce housing and improving housing stability;
aligning housing growth with community character, infrastructure, and environmental
stewardship, strengthening partnerships, and community engagement and
accountability. A summary of key changes included: reorganized strategies under key
outcome-oriented objectives; continue most strategies from 2023 with updates to
action items and wording revisions to better reflect progress and directions; direct
integration of the Regional Homelessness Strategy Across Larimer County to ensure
connectivity between HAP and homelessness; and the addition of a new objective and
strategies to address extensive community input around balancing housing growth
with conservation goals and the unique character of the Estes Valley. Trustee Brown
asked if zoning issues and limited multi-family areas are issues for other communities,
to which Fitzpatrick noted it was dependent upon a number of factors. It was moved
and seconded (Brown/Hall) to approve Resolution 102-26, and it passed
unanimously. Manager Speedlin stated staff would present all public comment on this
item and the resolution to DOLA as required by the State.

Reports and Discussion Items:
Reimbursement of Vacation Home Fees for 440 Valley Road. Town Clerk Williamson
presented a review of the licensing history of 440 Valley Road and licensing changes as
a result of the rezoning which was effective on July 26, 2026. The property contains three
(3) individual units, a triplex identified by the Larimer County Assessor. The property held
one (1) vacation home license starting in 2011 and three (3) separate vacation home
licenses, one for each unit, beginning in 2017. The Town Board approved the rezoning
of the property from RM - Residential Multi-Family to A-1 Accommodations on June 23,
2026. During the rezoning hearing, Trustee Igel questioned the course of action to refund
a portion of the licensing fee and staff responded the current Town ordinance would need
to be reviewed as it does not contain a mechanism for refunding fees. She stated the
Town has not refunded business license or vacation home license fees, including the
linkage fee once paid, if a business has been active for any portion of the year. The
vacation home licenses for 440 Valley Road were active in 2026, could be booked and
were receiving reviews. Staff were available for questions and requested Board direction
on refunding any portion of the fees. Trustee Igel supported a refund and considering a
policy which would allow the refunding of licensing fees. He stated concerns with the
reference to level of public interest identified in the report and staff's ability to remain
neutral on items and accurately gauge unbiased public interest which reflects both sides
of a discussion. Board discussion ensued and has been summarized: Questioned the
total licensing fees paid in 2026; whether reapplication fees were charged to the property
owners for the rezoning; concerns a refund would set a precedence; some support was
heard for a pro-rated refund; interest in fostering better community relations; and the
situation was identified as a one-off which may not merit a code amendment. Town
Administrator Machalek stated the contents of the staff memo could be reviewed and the
advantages and disadvantages and public interest could be removed from memos and
reports. He stated staff work diligently to ensure the facets of the facts are presented in
the memos and reports. He was curious and open to Board suggestions on how to call
attention to the opposing/altemate side of topics. Town Attorney Kramer reaffirmed the
code does not have a mechanism to refund fees; noted the need to identify under what
circumstances a refund would be allowed; and the importance in avoiding potentially
arbitrary decisions on whether a refund should be afforded or not. He added the Town
has a constitutional responsibility to only hand out public money when a basis to do so
can be identified through a specific set of criteria. Staff would develop r a resolution for a
partial refund of the vacation home licensing fees for 440 Valley Road for future Board
consideration which includes the public purpose for the refund.
