Reports and Discussion Items (Outside Entities):
1. Support Our Firefighters Estes Valley Fire Protection District - Ballot Question
6D Overview. Randy Repola, Chairperson of the Estes Valley Fire and Safety
Coalition provided an overview of Ballot Question 6D. Ballot Question 6D would
establish a 0.5% sales tax within the Estes Valley Fire Protection District (EVFPD) to
fund the EVFPD. The top three priorities of the EVFPD were wildland firefighting,
firefighter support, and timely response and apparatus. Anticipated future expenses
included ongoing training of new and existing volunteers, replacing outdated gear,
replacing self-contained breathing apparatuses (SCBA) and a SCBA fill station,
Board of Trustees - September 22,2026 - Page 3
purchasing new tires for Type 3 Engines, and replacing vehicles. Repola discussed
existing and proposed funding mechanisms. EVFPD's existing property tax of 2.069
mills was over four (4) mills lower than comparable communities. Other fire protection
districts around Colorado have adopted a 0.5-1.5% sales tax, in addition to mill levy's
ranging from 2.199 -16.621 mills. He noted that both fire calls and sales tax revenue
fluctuate with visitation. Board comments and questions have been summarized:
asked if additional EVFPD staff would be hired if Issue 6D passed, Repola stated his
understanding was the EVFPD Board was committed to maintaining the EVFPD as
a volunteer organization; questioned the cost of replacing a fire engine, Repola
estimated replacement cost at $1 million with the ladder vehicle due for replacement
in 2039 estimated to cost $5 million; discussed the status of EVFPD's capital
replacement fund, Repola believed a fund had existed previously but was not aware
if it had been depleted and stated if Issue 6D passed some of the revenue would
contribute to a capital replacement fund; questioned if any changes were planned to
the wildland mitigation efforts currently supported by a portion of the 1A sales tax,
Repola stated there were several opportunities to expand mitigation efforts; inquired
if EVFPD had a strategic plan; stated the Town did not plan to stop funding the EVFPD
if Issue 6D failed; supported Ballot Issue 6D to enable EVFPD to be financially
independent from the Town; and explained a sales tax shared the responsibility of
funding EVFPD with visitors and all of those within the EVFPD boundaries, including
residents beyond town limits.

1. Resolution 99-26 Supporting Estes Valley Fire Protection District Ballot Issue
6D. Town Administrator Machalek provided information on the Town's current support
of EVFPD. The Town committed 7% of the Town's then 4% sales tax to the EVFPD
at the formation of the district in 2009. Senate Bill 24-194 authorized fire districts and
other emergency services districts to levy sales tax beginning in May 2024. The Town
Board had clearly stated no redirection of the Town's existing contribution to the
EVFPD would be considered unless the EVFPD secured alternate financing.
Ron Wilcocks/County resident expressed concern sales tax was not an equitable
funding option. He stated increasing sales tax had a greater negative impact on those
with fewer means and questioned the boundaries of the sales tax and the effect on
non-profit organizations. Funding the EVFPD through an increase in property taxes
would distribute the burden to those who benefited the most from fire protection in the
Estes Valley. The existing sales taxes resulted in people purchasing necessities
outside of town limits. He expressed his support for EVFPD and the services they
provide and encouraged the EVFPD to consider alternative funding mechanisms.
Board discussion was heard and has been summarized: discussed the EVFPD's
previous unsuccessful attempts to increase the existing mill levy; reminded fellow
Board members of the correlation between the increase in tourism and the increase
in calls for the EVFPD; suggested EVFPD ask voters to increase the mill levy and
decrease the proposed sales tax in the future; supported Resolution 99-26; discussed
the lack of success of EVFPD's previous attempts to distribute the cost of service by

4. Ordinance 15-26 Prospect Mountain Water Project Repayment Surcharge. Mayor
Hall opened the public hearing for Ordinance 15-26. Manager Wesley presented an
overview of the proposed repayment program for the Prospect Mountain water
customers. The Town of Estes Park Water Division began operating the Prospect
Mountain Water System in 2016. The Voluntary Transfer Agreement between the
Bankruptcy Trustee for Prospect Mountain Water Company (PMWC) and the Town
transferred RMWC assets to the Town in 2019. At that time, former PMWC customers
became Town customers subject to Town rules and billing. It was identified the water
system required updating. The Town secured over $10 million worth of grant funding
for the project in addition to a USDA-RD low-interest loan payable over 40 years. A
surcharge would be applied to former PMWC customers and customers connecting
before the completion of the project to repay the USDA loan. Customers connecting
after the completion of the project would not be charged the project surcharge. The
estimated benefited customer repayment was $150 per month, accounting for
administrative costs and the interest rate of the USDA loan. The final surcharge
amount would be calculated following project completion. A one-time payoff process
would be established for interested parties. Board discussion was heard and has been
summarized: questioned how the continued weeping of the water storage tank would
be resolved, staff stated it was the contractor's responsibility to deliver a completed
water tank and staff were in negotiation with the contractor to fix the weeping,
complete other remaining work, and resolve liquidated damages; staff confirmed there
was a surety bond; supported the $150 per month maximum surcharge; discussed the
expressed desire of Prospect Mountain water customers to update the water system
to ensure it remained operational; and confirmed the Voluntary Transfer Agreement
was between the Town and the Bankruptcy Trustee for PMWC rather than individual
property owners, and therefore, annexation of the properties was not considered as
part of the Water Division's acquisition of the infrastructure. Mayor Hall closed the
public hearing. It was moved and seconded (Eshelman/Hazelton) to approve
Ordinance 15-26, and it passed unanimously.
