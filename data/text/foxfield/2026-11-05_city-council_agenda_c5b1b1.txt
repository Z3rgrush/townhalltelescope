5. For Discussion 
a. Resolution 2026-09 A Resolution of the Board of Trustees of the Town of 
Foxfield Approving the Bid Between the Town and Vance Brothers for 
Additional Crack Seal  
Town Administrator Karen Proctor presented a request to approve additional 
funding for the Town roads crack seal program. She explained that the original two-
ton estimate of crack seal material was insufficient to complete the entire Towns 
roads. Representatives from SEH, including Blayne Risk and Scott Jardine, attended 
the meeting to answer questions regarding cost estimates, material quantities, 
roadway maintenance practices, and future planning. Discussion included the 
challenges of accurately estimating crack seal quantities due to variations in crack 
width, depth, roadway age, traffic patterns, weather conditions, and pavement 
conditions. Concerns were raised by Trustees regarding the second consecutive year 
in which material estimates proved insufficient to complete the Town. SEH discussed 
the differences between mobilization costs, traffic control costs, overall project 
administration costs, future strategies for estimating material quantities, and 
potentially adjusting the Town’s maintenance approach. Trustees generally agreed 
Item #4a
that completing the roadway maintenance was necessary. Board members 
acknowledged that had the larger quantity been estimated initially, similar 
expenditures would likely have been approved as part of the original contract.  
Mayor Jones moved to approve Resolution 2026-09 Approving the Vance Brothers 
Bid for Additional Crack Seal, seconded by Trustee Cockrell. Passed unanimously.

  Item # 5a 
 
 
MEMORANDUM 
 
TO:  Mayor Jones and Members of the Board 
 
FROM:  Monica Torres, Town Clerk 
 
DATE:  November 5, 2026  
 
RE: Foxfield AI policy Draft 
 
DISCUSSION: 
Due to the increased use of AI, many municipalities have seen the need to implement an AI 
policy to protect their employees, establish the manner in which AI can be used, and have 
guidelines in place for protected information. This policy establishes the appropriate use of AI 
for the Town of Foxfield and its employees and contractors. AI chats do fall under information 
that can be CORA requested, therefore a policy to create guidelines for its usage will protect 
the Town in the case that this information is requested from us in the future.  
SUGGESTED MOTION: 
No motion at this time.  
ATTACHMENTS: 
Attachment A: Foxfield AI Policy Draft 
 
 
 
 

 
 
Town Wide 
Revision #1 
Last Revised: 10/23/26 
                           
  
1.Purpose 
This document establishes acceptable and prohibited uses of artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) 
systems used by, or on behalf of, the Town of Foxﬁeld (Town) to enable ﬂexibility and 
innovation to beneﬁt the community while safeguarding against harm.  
2.Scope 
This document applies to Town employees (full-time, part-time, seasonal), contractors, 
consultants, and Town Trustee members (each a "Covered Party") using AI Systems to 
provide services or information to the Town, its residents, employees, local businesses, 
and other community stakeholders. Service Inquiry Chatbots are excluded from the scope 
of this document.  
3.Deﬁnitions 
This section contains deﬁnitions of terms used within this document, as well as a few 
common terms used when discussing AI. Additional deﬁnitions can be reviewed in the ‘AI 
Resources’ section located on the employee intranet.  
3.1 AI-Assisted Decision – Any decision that impacts one or more individuals where AI is 
used to make, guide, assist, or materially inﬂuence the outcome.  
3.2 Artiﬁcial Intelligence (AI) - A machine-based system that can, for a given set of human-
deﬁned objectives, perform tasks commonly associated with human intelligence, such as 
reasoning, learning, and problem solving. AI is not a substitution for human intelligence.  
3.3 AI System - Any software, app, plug-in, cloud service, automated workﬂow, 
development tool, etc. that includes AI. There are various types of AI Systems, including, 
without limitation, the following:  
3.3.1 Agentic AI – An AI System capable of performing multi-step tasks, taking 
actions, or interacting with digital systems with digital systems with partial or full 
autonomy. Agents may plan, execute, and adjust self-directed actions based on 
objectives provided by a user. 
Attachment A
3.3.2 Generative AI – An AI System that can generate digital content such as text, 
images, audio, video, documents, or code. Content is created in response to user 
Prompts, and is based on patterns learned from data it is trained on.  
3.3.3 Large Language Model (LLM) – An AI System that utilizes Machine Learning and 
is trained on large amounts of textual data to process and generate human-like text. 
They are a type of Generative AI specializing in natural language processing. LLMs 
can draft text, summarize documents, answer questions, translate languages, and 
support research or communication tasks.  
3.3.4 Predictive AI – An AI System that uses Machine Learning and statistical 
analysis to identify patterns, anticipate behaviors and forecast outcomes.  
3.3.5 AI-Enhanced System – Software that incorporates AI features to assist with 
tasks the software was originally designed to perform. Examples include: a virtual 
meeting platform feature that generates meeting summaries or action items, a 
project management software containing an AI schedule risk assessment feature, 
an email or document creation tool that contains an AI feature to create drafts 
based on a human’s prompt.  
3.3.6 Service Inquiry Chatbot – A vendor-supplied online assistant embedded within 
a service provider’s website that provides information speciﬁc to the service 
provider’s products or services and does not process sensitive information. They are 
designed for customer service-related activities, such as requesting information, 
troubleshoot issues, report outages, and research products.  
3.4 Creative Content – Output produced by AI that enhances communications, 
presentations, or visual imagery to support marketing, public outreach, or employee 
engagement. Examples include, without limitation, graphic designs, logos, presentation 
visuals, marketing materials, social media posts, signs, banners, brochures, etc.  
3.5 Machine Learning – A subset of AI where algorithms learn and adapt from data without 
explicit programming.  
3.6 Foxﬁeld User Account – A Town email address or digital account, typically associated 
with a login and password, used to access the Town’s network, Town issued devices, or 
Town software systems.  
3.7 Prohibited Information – Highly sensitive information that must be protected and 
handled with the greatest care. Items in this category may share overlapping parts of the 
Restricted Information deﬁnition but are included as Prohibited to direct appropriate usage 
restrictions. Examples include, without limitation:  
3.7.1 Personally Identiﬁable Information (PII), such as: social security number; 
personal ID number; government-issued driver’s license or ID card number; 
passport number; health and biometric data; employer, student or military ID 
number; and ﬁnancial information (account numbers, credit card numbers, PINs, 
etc.).  
3.7.2 Names, addresses, telephone numbers, and personal ﬁnancial information of 
past or present users of public utilities, public facilities, or recreational or cultural 
services owned by the City.  
3.7.3 System access credentials, such as: logins, passwords, PINs, and passcodes.  
3.7.4 Personnel ﬁles and the information within personnel ﬁles attributable to an 
individual.  
3.7.5 Conﬁdential attorney-client privileged communications.  
3.7.6 Information protected by copyright or trademark.  
3.7.7 Criminal Justice Information Services (CJIS) data attributable to an individual.  
3.8 Prompt - Input provided by a user that instructs or guides the AI system on what 
response, action, or output to generate. Input can include, without limitation: textual 
instructions, uploaded documents or datasets, sample language, etc.  
3.9 Public AI System - Any AI system not licensed by the Town and that has not been 
approved as a Town AI System. For the purposes of this policy, Town AI systems include 
systems licensed individually or personally by a Covered Party.  
3.10 Restricted Information – Sensitive information that falls into at least one of the 
following conditions: is not public information under the Colorado Open Records Act, is 
conﬁdential, is not allowed to be shared under the Town’s Municipal Code or could post 
risk to public safety if exposed to parties without an authorized need-to-know. Examples 
include, without limitation:  
3.10.1 Details related to critical infrastructure, such as: detailed diagrams of city 
utility systems, information regarding technology networks or system design, 
emergency response plans, physical or cyber security arrangements, etc.  
3.10.2 Trade secrets, conﬁdential commercial, ﬁnancial, geological, or geophysical 
data, including this type of information provided to the Town by third parties.  
3.10.3 Information collected and maintained regarding individual businesses such 
as sales tax, contact information, etc.  
3.10.4 Locations of marijuana cultivation operations.  
3.10.5 Voter registration information attributable to an individual.  
3.10.6 Details of bids, proposals, and contracts, including staff scoring data.  
3.11 Vibe Coding – The act of creating software applications using an AI System, typically a 
LLM, where a human describes the desired functionality in plain English and asks the AI 
System to generate the code itself to produce the software application.  
4. Guiding Principles  
These over-arching principles of a responsible AI framework apply to the use of AI Systems.  
4.1 Human-led - AI Systems are tools to augment, not replace, professional judgment and 
expertise. Humans will remain the primary drivers of their work.  
4.2 Human-veriﬁed - Humans are accountable for the use and impact of AI Systems. 
Meaningful human inspection will be applied to the information input into AI Systems 
(including Prompts), as well as to the output from AI Systems, to assure accurate, safe, and 
ethical outcomes that adhere to local, state and federal law.  
4.3 Fair and unbiased - Although many AI Systems are designed to follow best practices to 
minimize bias, they typically do not guarantee complete compliance with anti-
discrimination laws. Meaningful human oversight is required to ensure unbiased, equitable 
outcomes. If using AI Systems for personnel performance evaluations, employee relations, 
or conﬂict resolution, restrict AI involvement to reﬁning drafts originally written by a human.  
4.4 Transparency- The use of AI Systems must be disclosed to your supervisor.  
4.5 Security and privacy – Unauthorized access to or use of Prohibited Information and 
Restricted Information will be prevented by adhering to Section 5 of this Policy. In addition, 
Covered Parties must take precautions to redact or anonymize Prohibited Information from 
content uploaded to or entered in any AI System. Covered Parties are responsible for 
requesting that the Information Technology department apply appropriate permission-
based access restrictions to City AI Systems.  
4.6 Sustainability - Each user of an AI System is expected to exercise appropriate 
judgement to avoid unnecessary processing so that energy consumption is minimized.  
5. Prohibited Uses of AI Systems  
The following are prohibited uses of AI Systems. Exceptions to these prohibited uses must 
be approved in writing by the Director of Technology.  
5.1 Exposing Prohibited Information to any AI System, either Public AI or Town AI, by 
including such content in Prompts or by uploading ﬁles containing Prohibited 
Information. An AI-Enhanced System speciﬁcally designed to securely store and 
process certain types of Prohibited Information may be allowed if it is approved as a 
Town AI System.  
5.2 Exposing Restricted Information to Public AI Systems by including such content 
in Prompts or by uploading ﬁles containing Restricted Information.  
5.3 Connecting Public AI Systems to the Town network, Town software systems or 
data sources (on-premises or cloud-based), or Town-issued devices (e.g. laptop, 
desktop, tablet, mobile phone).  
5.4 Installing Public AI Systems onto the Town network, Town issued devices or 
integrate with Town software systems.  
5.5 Creating an account with a Public AI System using a Foxﬁeld User Account.  
5.6 Using a Public AI System for purposes other than Creative Content or research of 
publicly available information.  
5.7 Using an AI System to make AI-Assisted Decisions affecting an individual’s 
eligibility for, access to, or the cost or terms of essential government services or 
public beneﬁts, including renewals, or services in the following categories: 
employment, purchase or lease of housing, ﬁnancial or lending, insurance, health 
care, legal, or educational opportunities.  
5.8 Using AI Systems for hiring evaluations, termination or promotion decisions.  
5.9 Using AI Systems to alter reality in a manner that could pose a risk to public 
safety, could be used to commit fraud, that harms another person, or that attempts 
to inﬂuence a decision or action with false information.  
5.10 Using Agentic AI.  
5.11 Vibe Coding.  
6. Use of Public AI Systems  
A Covered Party who elects to access a Public AI System through a personal account using 
their individual credentials is personally accepting the vendor's terms and conditions for 
use. In doing so, a Covered Party is personally responsible for their usage of AI and its 
outcomes. The Town accepts no liability.  
7. Public Records  
The Town is subject to the Colorado Open Records Act (CORA), C.R.S. § 24-72-200.1, et 
seq. Use of AI Systems may result in public records that are subject to CORA.  
8.  Town Systems Approval  
Written approval from the Town Administrator is required in advance to purchase or install 
any Town AI System. This includes, without limitation, on-premises and cloud-hosted 
systems, software subscriptions, and any software system where artiﬁcial intelligence is 
either the main component or is a feature. The review process will include, at a minimum:  
• RFP/RFQ speciﬁcations  
• Vendor assessment of data privacy and cybersecurity policies and practices; and  
• Contract review of terms protecting sensitive information.  
Approval of an AI System through this process does not grant an exception to any 
prohibited use and does not exempt a Covered Party from adhering to the guiding 
principles outlined in this guideline.  
A list of approved Town AI Systems will be kept up to date by the Town Administrator and 
will be made available to Covered Parties.  
9. Enforcement  
Employees who violate this standard operating guideline may be subject to the following 
actions, as applicable:  
• Training and guidance on AI and this standard operating guideline.  
• Loss of, or reduced access to, AI Systems or Sensitive Information.  
• Disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

AGREEMENT FOR LAW ENFORCEMENT SERVICES 
 
 THIS AGREEMENT made and entered into by and between the TOWN OF 
FOXFIELD, a municipal corpor ation of the State of Colorado, ( hereinafter referred to as 
“Foxfield”) and BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF THE COUNTY OF 
ARAPAHOE, on behalf of the ARAPAHOE COUNTY SHERIFF’S OFFICE  
(hereinafter referred to as “County”).  
 
WITNESSETH: 
 
 WHEREAS, Section 30- 11-410, C.R.S. as amended, authorizes the County to 
contract with a municipality for the purpose of providing law enforce ment by the Sheriff 
within the boundaries of the municipality; and 
 
 WHEREAS, Foxfield is without monetary means to provide for the employment 
of any full-time law officers; and    
 
 WHEREAS, Foxfield has requested that the County, through the Arapahoe 
County Sheriff, provide law enforcement services which the parties agree are 
contemplated in section 30-11-410, C.R.S., 
 
 WHEREAS, the County, in the interest of the health, safety and welfare of the 
residents of the Town of Foxfield, deems it advisable to enter into this Agreement for 
Law Enforcement Services; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the County has determined to execute future agreements on a fiscal 
year basis. 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the premises, it is agreed as follows: 
 
1. The Arapahoe County Sheriff (“Sheriff”) shall provide law enforcement within 
the boundaries of Foxfield within Arapahoe County commencing January 1, 2027. 
 
2. The type of law enforcement services to be provided by the Sheriff, within the 
boundaries of Foxfield situated in Arapahoe County, shall be similar to the law 
enforcement services provided in unincorporated areas of the County of Arapahoe and 
will include law enforcement communication center services, but shall not include 
enforcement of municipal ordinances.  The Sheriff shall utilize, to the best of his ability 
and judgment, the manpower and equipment presently at his disposal in order to provide 
said law enforcement services.  Offenders shall be cited into the Town of Foxfield 
Municipal Court, District Court or the County Court of Arapahoe County as appropriate. 
 
 
 
 
 2 
A. The law enforcement services provided under this Agreement shall not 
include non-routine law enforcement services associated with special events 
sponsored by or approved by Foxfield.  For such events, it is expressly 
understood that Foxfield shall consult with the Sheriff’s staff regarding such 
event(s) and any law enforcement needs associated with the events that 
exceed routine service levels.  Foxfield shall negotiate a separate agreement 
with the Sheriff’s Office for any temporary or off -duty services required for 
such events that exceed routine service levels.  For purposes of this 
Agreement, “special events” and/or “non -routine service levels” shall mean 
events sponsored or approved by Foxfield with an expected or actual 
attendance of more than five thousand people.

end as of December 31, 2027.

dispatch services.  Payment of said $130,340.17shall be made to the County in full upon

MEMORANDUM 
TO: Town of Foxfield 
 
FROM: Blayne Risk, PE (Lic. CO) 
 
DATE: October 16, 2026 
 
RE: 2027 Pavement Maintenance Plan 
  
 
 
This memo summarizes the Town of Foxfield’s pavement maintenance program since 2012, provides 
recommendations for the 2027 pavement maintenance program, and initial planning for future years.  
Background 
Since 2012, the Town of Foxfield has maintained its streets through a formal pavement management 
program, including annual crack sealing, patching, and periodic chip seal and slurry seal applications. 
The goal has been to keep pavement condition at fair (PCI of 55) or better. 
2026 Field Observations 
Field observations conducted in 2026 indicate that the Town’s roadways are generally in fair to good 
condition. No significant deterioration was noted that would warrant chip seal, slurry seal, or mill-and-
overlay treatments for 2027. Routine inspection and maintenance remain important to preserve pavement 
condition. No crack patch was done in 2026; larger cracks were treated with mastic crack repair. This has 
been utilized for the past 4 years and has shown to hold up well in the Town.  
 
2026 Maintenance Program crack seal quantities were increased from the previous year's program totals 
in an effort to address the crack condition needs for the Town and perceived shortcomings in the actual 
applied field application coverage achieved in the previous year. Crack seal quantity was increased from 
1.5 Tons to 2 Tons. However, application rates again fell short of addressing full Town roadway crack 
coverage. An additional 2 Tons were subsequently added to the program through a construction change 
order. It is anticipated this additional volume will address the remaining targeted streets, but the work is 
not yet completed to confirm that result. 
 
Estimation of accurate crack seal quantities remains limited by a number of variables, including: 
representative street condition review applied to the Town's overall roadway mileage, traffic use, 
irregularity of crack depth, width and uniformity, environmental considerations, and application conditions. 
The Town and SEH have visited the details of this challenge in estimating and concluded the current 
approach will be maintained in an effort to budget and spend money as much as possible on the annual 
work needs rather than additional estimate refinement and planning costs.

Recommended 2027 Maintenance Program 
The 2027 Maintenance Program expectations are based on progress completed in 2026, feedback 
provided by the Town, and consideration of work completed in recent annual programs. 
 
Recommendations for 2027 are as follows: 
No chip seal, slurry seal, or mill-and-overlay is recommended for 2027 at this time. 
Maintenance activities include: 
 Annual crack sealing 
 Mastic crack repair 
 
A preliminary quantity and construction cost estimate has been prepared for budget planning purposes, 
focusing on crack maintenance only. The 2026 Maintenance Program's aim was to address crack seal 
needs for the entirety of the Town. As of the time of this Memo, success of meeting that goal remains 
unconfirmed. Since we do anticipate the program will cover the majority of the Town's roadways, the 
quantity of crack seal needed next year is likely less. However, the Town has expressed a desire to be 
conservative to try to provide full coverage. SEH believes an estimate of 3 Tons should be conservative 
for coverage in 2027. Based on the success of estimating mastic needs in 2026, SEH recommends the 
2027 mastic quantity remain 1 Ton. SEH recommends conducting a field survey in late winter/early spring 
(weather dependent) to identify and inventory recommended work areas on Town roadways and verify 
that the 3 Ton estimate should be maintained or revised. Deliverables will include an exhibit illustrating 
work areas and an updated quantity and construction cost estimate. After Town review and approval, 
SEH can assist with bid documents, contractor selection, and construction administration. 
Roadway Surfacing: Future Planning 
Surface evaluation and/or surface work may be needed again in approximately 2–7 years. Annual 
inspection and maintenance for crack seal and patching are recommended. The Town should anticipate a 
full roadway evaluation, including PCI ratings, by 2030, or as early as 2028 depending on conditions, to 
inform future pavement program alternatives (chip seal, slurry seal, or mill-and-overlay).
