RE: 2025 Hinsdale Corridor Traffic Calming Recommendations 
PROJECT BACKGROUND 
SEH previously analyzed traffic conditions in the Town of Foxfield and evaluated traffic calming options 
for implementation. The Town installed traffic control gates at Richfield St and Fremont Ave that help 
prevent traffic along Parker Rd and Arapahoe Rd from using the Town’s roadways as a cut-through 
during the morning and evening peak periods.  
Recently, the Town of Foxfield has received complaints from residents about speeding along some of the 
Town’s roadways. SEH was tasked to review recently collected speed and traffic data, and outline traffic 
calming recommendations along Hinsdale Ave. Any speed calming recommendations along Hinsdale Ave 
may also be applied to Richfield St, Easter Ave, and Waco St.  
DATA COLLECTION AND SPEED ANALYSIS 
The Town collected speed and daily traffic volumes along Hinsdale Ave, Richfield St, Easter Ave, and 
Waco St by utilizing radar speed feedback signs that were moved to various locations along the corridor 
in the spring and summer of 2025. Table 1 below displays the speed results at each location.  
Hinsdale Ave 
Hinsdale Ave speed and traffic data were collected from June 22 to June 29, 2025, for eastbound traffic 
near 18150 Hinsdale Ave. The reports indicate that the average vehicular speed was 23 mph, the 50 th 
percentile was 24 mph and the 85th percentile speed was 30 mph. Approximately four percent of vehicles 
traveled 10 mph above the posted speed limit.  
Richfield St 
Richfield St speed and traffic data was collected from March 22 to April 15, 2025, for southbound traffic 
near 7388 Richfield St and from April 27 to May 17, 2025, for northbound traffic near 6818 Richfield St. 
The reports indicate that the average vehicular speed was 23 mph for both the northbound and 
southbound directions, the 50th percentile was 24 mph in the southbound and 25 mph in the northbound 
direction, and the 85th percentile speed was 30 mph for both directions. Approximately two to three 
percent of vehicles traveled 10 mph above the posted speed limit.

TOWN OF FOXFIELD TRAFFIC CALMING ANALYSIS 
Hinsdale Ave was identified by the Town as the focus for traffic calming implementation. The Arapahoe 
County Neighborhood Traffic Management Program (NTMP) and Federal Highway Administration 
(FHWA) guidance for traffic calming were utilized to develop roadway appropriate traffic calming 
recommendations for Hinsdale Ave. Both the NTMP and FHWA’s ePrimer outline appropriate applications 
for traffic calming measures and effectiveness.  
According to the NTMP the following minimum threshold criteria are required for a roadway to qualify for 
traffic calming treatment: 
 Average Daily Traffic (ADT) Volumes: the measured volume is over 500 vehicles per day AND 
Speed Observations: the measured 85th-percentile speed exceeds the posted speed limit by six  
miles per hour or more.   
OR 
 Safety Analysis: the roadway segment has documented traffic safety issues that are considered 
correctable by traffic calming treatments.  
Currently, the Hinsdale Ave exhibits a one-way ADT of 175 vehicles per day (vpd) in the eastbound 
direction and 85th percentile speed of 30 mph, not meeting the criteria of the NTMP. However, 
neighborhood support for traffic calming measures is high and residents’ concerns with speeding have 
been documented.  
Hinsdale Ave mostly fits the FHWA category of “residential collector” as displayed in Table 3.1 of the 
ePrimer. Table 3.1 outlines the appropriate traffic calming measures for different roadway categories. Of 
the traffic calming measures displayed in the ePrimer and outlined in the NTMP, the following may be 
appropriate for the Hinsdale Ave corridor:  
Horizontal Deflection 
 Lateral Shift (FHWA only) 
 Chicane (FHWA and NTMP) 
Vertical Deflection 
 Speed Hump (FHWA and NTMP) 
 Speed Cushion (FHWA and NTMP) 
 Speed Table (FHWA only) 
 Offset Speed Table (FHWA only) 
Street Width Reduction 
 Choker (FHWA and NTMP) 
 Median Island (FHWA and NTMP) 
Routing Restriction 
 none

TOWN OF FOXFIELD TRAFFIC CALMING RECOMMENDATION 
After comparing the potential traffic calming measures for the project, speed hump, speed cushion, speed 
table, and offset speed table are the most appropriate for the Hinsdale Ave corridor. Speed humps or 
cushions with cutouts appear to be the best suited traffic calming measures for the corridor. Both traffic 
calming measures operate in a similar way; however, the cutouts on speed cushions allow for emergency 
response vehicles to straddle the speed humps and may help better accommodate residents using horse 
trailers and larger vehicles. 
 
All other traffic calming measures outlined by the FHWA and NTMP for a residential collector roadway 
generally require a more urban roadway cross section with curb and gutter and the installation of raised 
medians. Raised medians do not fit with the more rural cross section of Hinsdale Ave and may introduce 
safety and maintenance issues.  
 
It is recommended to install either speed humps or speed cushions along Hinsdale Ave at the 
recommended 260’ to 500’ in a series along the corridor to control speeds. A conceptual layout is 
included as an attachment to this memo. The layout outlines approximate locations of speed humps or 
cushions along Hinsdale Ave from west of Richfield St to just west of Yampa St. 
 
The same speed hump or speed cushion traffic calming measure can be applied to Richfield Ave, Waco 
St, and Easter Ave. Each of those roadways have a similar cross section to Hinsdale Ave making speed 
humps or cushions appropriate for speed management. Additionally, implementing uniform traffic calming 
measures throughout the Town will standardize driver expectations and contribute to consistent speed 
reduction along similar roadway types.

E: 2026 Pavement Maintenance Plan 
T
his memo summarizes the Town of Foxfield’s pavement maintenance program since 2012, provides 
recommendations for the 2026 pavement maintenance program, and initial planning for future years.  
Background 
Since 2012, the Town of Foxfield has maintained its streets through a formal pavement management 
program, including annual crack sealing, patching, and periodic chip seal and slurry seal applications. 
The goal has been to keep pavement condition at fair (PCI of 55) or better. 
2025 Field Observations 
Field observations conducted in 2025 indicate that the Town’s roadways are generally in fair to good 
condition. No significant deterioration was noted that would warrant chip seal, slurry seal, or mill -and-
overlay treatments for 2026. Routine inspection and maintenance remain important to preserve pavement 
condition. No crack patch was done in 2025, larger cracks were treated with mastic crack repair. This has 
been utilized for the past 3 years and has shown to hold up well in the Town.  
Recommended 2026 Maintenance Program 
Recommendations for 2026 are as follows: 
No chip seal, slurry seal, or mill-and-overlay is recommended for 2026 at this time. 
Maintenance activities include: 
• Shoulder repairs as needed
• Annual crack sealing
• Mastic crack repair
• Crack patch
A
 preliminary quantity and construction cost estimate has been prepared for budget planning purposes, 
focusing on shoulder and crack maintenance only. SEH recommends conducting a field survey in late 
winter/early spring (weather dependent) to identify and inventory recommended work areas on Town 
roadways. Deliverables will include an exhibit illustrating work areas and an updated quantity and 
construction cost estimate. After Town review and approval, SEH can assist with bid documents, 
contractor selection, and construction administration.

Surface evaluation and/or surface work may be needed again in approximately 2– 7 years. Annual 
inspection and maintenance for crack seal and patching are recommended. The Town should anticipate a 
full roadway evaluation, including PCI ratings, by 2030, or as early as 2027 depending on conditions, to 
inform future pavement program alternatives (chip seal, slurry seal, or mill-and-overlay).
