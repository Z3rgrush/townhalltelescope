5. For Approval
a. Resolution 2026-06 A Resolution of the Board of Trustees of the Town of
Foxfield Approving a Quote Between the Town and Terracare for the Waco
Inlet Backfill and Asphalt Repair
Town Administrator Proctor presented updated recommendations regarding the
Waco drainage repair project. The revised proposal removed the previously
discussed culvert cleanout work after further review indicated that the culvert
appeared functional and did not currently require cleaning. The updated proposal
retained seeding and watering components while reducing overall project costs.
Trustees discussed project timing, erosion control alternatives, riprap installation,
seeding and watering costs, and engineering recommendations. The Board agreed to
restore the area and evaluate drainage conditions before considering additional
improvements. Staff were directed to coordinate project scheduling with the
contractor for the fall.
Item #4a
Mayor Jones moved to approve Resolution 2026-06 approving the quote from 
Terracare Associates for the Waco Inlet Backfill and Asphalt Repair, seconded by 
Trustee Cockrell. Passes Unanimously.  
b. Resolution 2026-08 A Resolution of the Board of Trustees of the Town of
F
oxfield Approving the Purchase of Solar Powered Radar Speed Signs and a
Quote Between the Town and Terracare for the Installation of the Signs
T
own Administrator Proctor presented updated pricing for four solar-powered radar
speed signs, including installation costs, for a total project cost of approximately
$22,373. The Board discussed the effectiveness of radar signs, speed humps,
targeted enforcement, and overall traffic-calming strategies. Trustees questioned
whether the signs represented the most effective use of Town funds and reviewed
prior traffic-study findings. The Board reached consensus to defer the radar sign
purchase and instead pursue a resident reporting tool on the Town website to
identify speeding trends, locations, and times. Information collected would be used
to support targeted law-enforcement efforts and future traffic-mitigation decisions.
Trustee Pakanati urged the board to reconsider their decision and to bring this
discussion back to the board when all Trustees could be present.

RE
: Solar Powered Radar Speed Signs 
DISCUSSION: 
As requested, staff is presenting the solar powered radar speed signs for further discussion and 
reconsideration.  Below is information provided by Trustee Pakanati as to why he strongly 
disagrees with deferring this low-cost, high-value pilot and an explanation as to why the signs 
are the most prudent first step for addressing resident speeding concerns. 
Reasons why the radar signs are necessary now: 
• Objective, continuous data: The signs provide 24/7 speed monitoring and automated
reporting so we can verify whether resident complaints reflect a real, recurring problem
or isolated incidents. This evidence is essential before committing to more expensive,
permanent measures.
• Surgical enforcement: With time-stamped, location-specific data we can deploy officers
only when and where speeding actually occurs. That targeted approach is far more
efficient than relying on ad hoc complaints or broad enforcement.
• Limitations of the complaint portal approach: Asking residents to file complaints on the
website and then staging off-duty officers has three inherent problems: low
participation, delayed reporting that misses peak offending times, and recurring cost for
off-duty officers. Those factors make the portal approach unreliable and expensive.
• Cost effectiveness: The signs cost a fraction of major traffic calming measures and will
reduce the need for repeated traffic engineering studies (which can run about $5,000
per instance). The pilot will pay for itself if it prevents even one unnecessary capital
project or repeated consultant visits.

• Immediate behavioral impact: Drivers commonly slow when they see their speed
displayed. The signs can reduce speeds immediately while we evaluate longer-term
solutions.
• Budget availability: The 2026 budget includes $120,000 for speed mitigation. Approving
$22,373.52 uses a small portion of that allocation and preserves funds for future
measures if the data supports them.
• If approved/installed, staff should compile and present a 1/3/6-month report that
includes average speeds, 85th-percentile speeds, and peak-time speed patterns for each
location. Trustee Pakanati volunteered to assist with data collection and analysis. If the
data identifies problem areas, we can authorize targeted enforcement and evaluate
follow-up measures. If speeds are within acceptable limits, funds can be reallocated
accordingly.
This measured, evidence-based approach protects public safety, respects taxpayer dollars, and 
gives residents a clear, transparent response to their concerns.
