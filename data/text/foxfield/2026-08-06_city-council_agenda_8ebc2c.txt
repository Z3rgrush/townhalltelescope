5. Public Hearings and Possible Action 
A. Ordinance 2026-03 Adopting the Wildfire Resiliency Code 
Mayor Jones opened the public hearing regarding Ordinance 2026-03 adopting the Wildfire Resiliency 
Code by reference. She explained that the State developed the code to establish minimum standards 
intended to safeguard life and property from wildfire hazards and reduce fire spread between 
structures and surrounding wildland areas. No public comments were received and the hearing was 
closed. The Board discussed prior review of the code and the State's recommendation that 
municipalities adopt the standards. Mayor Jones moved to approve Ordinance 2026-03. Trustee 
Thompson seconded. Motion passed unanimously. 
B. Ordinance 2026-02 Land Use Code Update 
Mayor Jones opened the public hearing regarding amendments to the Land Use Code. The proposed 
revisions were intended to improve usability and ensure consistency with current laws and regulations. 
No public comments were received and the hearing was closed. Mayor Jones moved to approve 
Ordinance 2026-02. Trustee Shultz seconded. Motion passed unanimously.

C. Resolution 2026-05 CLI Services Street Light Repairs 
The Board reviewed a proposal from CLI Services to replace three roadway light fixtures and one fuse

D. Waco Street Drainage and Culvert Repairs 
Staff presented engineering recommendations concerning erosion and roadway deterioration near 
Waco Street. Trustees discussed observations from the engineering report, the condition of the 
culvert, potential future roadway damage, and the possibility that continued erosion could eventually 
create a sinkhole. The Board noted that engineers reported the culvert itself appeared clear and that 
the issue was more likely related to drainage patterns and embankment erosion. Trustees expressed 
support for proceeding with the initial recommended repair option to further evaluate the source of 
the problem. Board members discussed replacing the proposed seeding with riprap rock to provide 
additional stabilization near the roadway and reduce future erosion. Staff was directed to obtain 
clarification regarding culvert-cleaning costs, investigate adding riprap stabilization, and return with 
updated information. 
E. Resolution 2026-07 2026 Pavement Repair and Surface Treatment Program 
The Board reviewed the Vance Brothers proposal for crack sealing and pavement maintenance. 
Trustees discussed quantities of sealant and mastic material, lessons learned from the previous year's

additional repairs are identified. Board members stressed the importance of ensuring needed repairs 
are completed and that staff is notified promptly if additional materials are necessary. Mayor Jones 
moved to approve Resolution 2026-07 and authorize up to an additional $5,500 for contingency 
purposes. Trustee Thompson seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously.

MEMORANDUM 
 
TO:  Mayor Jones and Members of the Board 
 
FROM:  Karen Proctor, Town Administrator 
 
DATE:  August 6, 2026  
 
RE: Resolution 2026-06 A Resolution of the Board of Trustees of the Town of Foxfield 
Approving the Quote Between the Town and Terracare for the Waco Inlet 
Backfill and Asphalt Repair 
 
DISCUSSION: 
The Town’s Engineers, SEH, provided the following comments and recommendation regarding 
their evaluation of the inlet hole deterioration located on Waco.  
 
From a visual review it seems that the deterioration is caused from surface flows, rather than a 
subsurface pipe dislocation. The culvert appears connected to the inlet and the inlet appears 
stable, even though there has been soil loss and deterioration of the subgrade around the inlet.  
 
See attached link for some photos: 20260622-Waco \u0026 Arapahoe Rd Pavement \u0026 
Drainage Issues-Photos 
  
The inlet top is about 6-8 inches lower than the adjacent pavement. The pavement is obviously 
damaged at the edge of the road. It looks like about 12” of pavement was previously patched 
along the edge. Reviewing Google Earth photos suggests this may have occurred somewhere in 
the 2020-2021 timeframe.  So, this may be a reoccurring problem.  
  
Since there is a missing subgrade (hole is about 5’ deep and extends 4’ south of the inlet), it 
would seem that ditch flows are seeping down to the pipe trench and likely migrating fines 
through the soil and perhaps along the pipe trench. You can also see that there is a pavement 
asphalt crack forming along the culvert pipe trench that extends to the middle of the road, but 
it isn’t a significant crack, nor does it currently propagate the full width of the road. You can 
also see that the nearby concrete pavement panels appear to dip in the center of the road. This 
may or may not be a related issue. 

                                                  Item # 5a 
  
Finally, at the inlet and outlet ends of the culvert, you can see there is sediment in the inlet and 
pipe, but it isn’t inhibiting flows and there looks to be a “full moon” looking at the light at the 
other end. So, it doesn’t seem the pipe is deflected or compromised, with that being a 
contributing factor in degrading the subgrade.  
  
SEH’s recommendation is based on deductive reasoning versus engineering certainty. Since we 
don’t know the extents of the current subgrade situation, and since things could get quite 
costly, they recommend doing minimal repairs and monitoring things more closely over the 
next year.  
  
Option 1 
1. Sawcut the damaged asphalt 3’ beyond the limits of the damaged pavement and 
remove the asphalt. Remove and dispose of the asphalt. Est. 4’W x 6L’= 24 sq. ft. 
2. Excavate around the inlet area to expose the culvert pipe and inlet to undisturbed 
subgrade.  
3. Backfill and recompact the pipe bedding and inlet with native material.  
4. Have the Contractor include scope for a geotech engineer to confirm subgrade density is 
compacted to 95% standard proctor density. 
5. Replace the pavement section with min 5” HBP over 4” of compacted Class 6 roadbase, 
or match existing, plus 1” of asphalt, whichever is greater. 
6. Reestablish the finished grade around the inlet to 2-3” above the inlet to slope to the 
top of inlet.  
7. Place seeded erosion control blanket min 4’ around the inlet top and min 10’ upstream 
of the inlet in the ditch swale. 
8. Contractor to hand water to reestablish seeding. 
9. Contractor to jet the existing storm inlet and culvert and dispose of mud, dirt and 
debris. 
  
Option 2 – Options 1-10 above, with a more robust approach to #7.  
      7a. Reestablish finished grade around the inlet with 2’ wide, 6” thick concrete apron around 
the inlet, with a 2% slope. Use joint sealant and epoxy coated dowels to tie the apron into the 
inlet.  
  
Option 3 – Option 2, and modify steps 1, 3, 5: 
     1 a, 3a, 5a. Expand the pavement removal and replacement along roadway culvert alignment 
to min 2’ beyond horizontal crack in pavement a minimum of 3’ wide. Excavate to pipe bedding 
and backfill and recompact to subgrade and replace pavement trench patch section.  
  
SEH suggested we start with Option 1, with monitoring and periodic surface fill placement 
around the inlet top to keep the ditch positively flowing to the inlet. If the issue seems to be a 
reoccurring maintenance issue, then the Town could add a concrete apron around the inlet in 
the future. And, lastly, if there seems to be an expanded area of pavement deterioration in the 
                                                  Item # 5a 
future, we could excavate the culvert alignment, remove and recompact the pipe trench and 
repatch the pavement surface.     
 
Based on the Boards comments and questions from the last Board meeting, SEH stated that If 
the Town wants to defer the jetting, that is a maintenance consideration. There does appear to 
be some sediment in both the inlet and outlet ends, but the capacity of the pipe is likely not 
significantly compromised. It should be cleaned, but the Town can decide when that happens 
or wait to make these repairs and see if the next significant storm clears out the line.   
 
SEH has concerns with the rip rap approach, stating that the rip rap acts as erosion protection 
for larger storm events, and is susceptible to water infiltration if the water ponds. The rip rap 
doesn’t extend below the flowline of the pipe. So, normal storm evens flow along the ditch 
grass and enter the pipe.  
 
SEH’s proposal is to build up the existing ditch flowline slightly (while still maintaining positive 
flow) so that water flows into the top of the inlet. The erosion blanketing helps establish and 
protect the ditch bottom while seeding takes place. And the well compacted subgrade helps to 
limit infiltration around the inlet. We are trying to prevent saturation around the inlet and 
prevent persistent ponding before getting to the inlet top.  
 
Placing rip rap around the inlet, rather than a concrete apron suggested in Option 2 has several 
challenges. One is that it doesn’t look like there is much room between the repaired pavement 
and the inlet. If rip rap was a viable solution, I’d recommend a width of least 2-3x the width of 
the largest rock side. It doesn’t look like we have that spacing. The second issue is we don’t 
want the water to persistently pond, and really don’t want it to pond below the inlet top. The 
road isn’t significantly higher than the inlet, so we don’t have room to build up rip rap to pond 
water. Rip rap around and roughly level with the inlet top means ditch flows will saturate 
around the inlet and pond water until it can spill into the inlet. Saturation and ponding is what 
is causing the current problem. 
 
If the drainage swale improvements aren’t enough, a possible substitute to the concrete apron 
would be to create the same with asphalt. However, a narrow band of roadway asphalt 
connecting to an adjacent area a 10’ wide asphalt around the inlet won’t likely hold up if a truck 
or snowplow drives along the road edge. 
 
Based on the Boards comments and SEH’s recommendations, attached for the Boards approval 
is a Resolution approving a quote from Terracare in the amount of $11,890.40 for Option 1 with 
the jetting and culvert clean out removed.  
This expense was not budgeted for in the 2026 budget.  However, $120,000 was approved in 
the budget for speed mitigation. It is anticipated that the actual cost for speed mitigation will 
be significantly less, therefore some of these budgeted funds could be used to cover this repair.
