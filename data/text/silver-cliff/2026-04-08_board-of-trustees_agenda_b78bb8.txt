 Decommissioning Report 
  
 
1.0 GENERAL PROJECT INFORMATION 
At the request of CBEP Solar 10, LLC, Coffman Engineers has prepared this Decommissioning 
Report for the proposed 4.25 MWac (megawatt alternating current) Silver Cliff Solar project. The 
project is located due south of the Town of Silver Cliff, Colorado just north of the Silver Cliff 
cemetery. The site is on a ~36.5-acre parcel with solar arrays occupying the eastern portion of 
the site with an approximate footprint of 22 acres. The project includes the installation of a 
central access gravel road with a vehicle turnaround and electrical equipment sourced by new 
underground conduits.  
 
The purpose of this decommissioning report is to provide procedures and an opinion of probable 
construction cost for decommissioning the solar facility and restoring the site in accordance with 
authorities having jurisdiction and industry standards upon the end of the project’s life. The 
Silver Cliff solar facility will have an expected life span of around 40 years. 
2.0 ANTICIPATED CONDITIONS FOR DECOMMISSIONING 
This plan assumes that the project will be decommissioned under any of the following 
conditions: 
1. The land lease ends and will either not be renew ed, or a new lease will not be entered 
for the project. 
2. The system does not produce power for sale, exce pt for instance when the site is 
undergoing repairs and restorations. 
3. The system is damaged and will not be repaired o r replaced. 
4. The system requires an upgrade, and the existing  system will be removed from the site 
and replaced with new. 
3.0 SYSTEM DESCRIPTION  
The project is assumed to consist of the following components: 
 
• 17 inverters  
• 9600 PV modules  
• 96 PV Trackers  
• Access roads to the arrays – 20’ wide of gravel base  
• 4,089 linear feet – 8’-high security fencing  
• 20’ wide – 8’-high double swing gate  
• 28,423 linear feet – underground electrical conduit 
• 2 Transformers  
• 17 Inverters  
• 10,000 Gallon Fire Cistern  
• 4 surge poles  
 
 Decommissioning Report 
  
4.0 DECOMMSISIONING  
Upon the end of the project’s life, the decommissio ning and site-restoration process detailed in 
this plan would begin and be completed within approximately 40 weeks; see the Decommissioning 
Estimate and Site Remediation section for more info rmation. The following general 
decommissioning would occur: 
• Removal of solar arrays, inverters, and electrical distribution equipment. 
• Removal of electrical cable raceways and electrical appurtenances. 
• Removal of storage containers, fencing, fuel break and access road. 
Solar Array 
Decommissioning of the solar array will consist of dismantling and transporting solar components 
off the project site. The solar arrays will need to  be dismounted from the PV Tracker and fluid 
collected through the drainage holes will need to be drained. Modules that are in good condition 
will be collected from this process and transported to a local or regional buyer of used solar arrays.  
If no local or regional buyer exists at the time of decommissioning, the arrays will be shipped to a 
national recycler such as WeRecycleSolar for value recovery. Salvageable metal and electronics 
will be transported to American Iron, and the remai ning materials to dispose of will be removed 
from Custer County for recycling and disposal.  
 
Salvage Yard (Metals) 
• American Iron – 1801 S. La Crosse Ave Pueblo, CO 8 1006, approximately 57 miles from 
the site. 
o Light Steel Re-Value: $80 / Ton 
o Aluminum Re-Value: $0.55 / Pound 
 
Recycling Center (Metals and Electronics) 
• Pueblo Recycle Works – 1595 Stockyard Rd. Pueblo, CO 81001, approximate 58 miles 
from the site. 
o Scrap Metal Fee: $1-$30 
o Recyclable Electronics Fee: $1 / Pound 
 
Landfills (No Electronics) 
• Southside Landfill – 5715 W State Highway 78, Pueb lo CO 81005, approximately 57 miles 
from the site. 
 
These facilities may not be functioning at the time of decommission but it is expected that facilities 
in the area will be operational, rates are also subject to change. 
 
All modules should be transported upright whenever possible to decrease the amount of pressure 
on the glass surface to prevent possible cracking i n transit. Should the modules be transported 
flat, a layer of protection must be added between the modules to ensure safe packing and transit. 
Modules sent for disposal will be sampled and teste d using accepted test methods to determine 
whether they must be managed as a resource conservation and recovery act (RCRA) Hazardous 
Waste. 
 Decommissioning Report 
  
Electrical System 
Decommissioning the electrical system will consist of the removal of customer power poles, 
inverters, switchboard, transformers and roughly 28 ,423 linear feet of underground electrical 
conduit and conductors.  
 
Transformer dielectric fluids shall be disposed of in accordance with the type of dielectric fluid 
installed. Dielectric fluids that contain polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), less-flammable dielectric 
fluids like FR3, the hazardous waste requires speci al disposal methods in accordance with 
federal, state, and local regulations. Ground grids  shall be removed unless the depth of 
decommissioning is above the depth of the ground grid. Salvageable metals are to be transported 
to American Iron. Electronic waste is to be taken to Pueblo Recycle Works. 
Site Features 
Additional site features that will need to be removed as part of the decommissioning plan are the 
game fence, double swing gate, fuel break, storage containers and gravel access road with 
vehicle turnaround. The game fence is estimated to be 4,089 linear feet and 8 feet tall with 4’x4’ 
cedar posts in concrete bases. Wood posts and concrete bases will be transported to Southside 
Landfill for waste. Scrap metals from the gate and game fence can be taken to American Iron for 
salvage. Storage containers are expected to be returned to the Owner for reuse at another facility 
or transported to a local reseller of used storage containers.  
 
The proposed gravel road is estimated to be 1,051 linear feet and 20 feet wide; the access road 
is equipped with a vehicle turnaround with 30-foot radius. The construction of the access road will 
include aggregate base and surfacing as well as reinforcing fabric. Once the portions of the project 
that are serviced by the access road have been deco mmissioned. The access road will be 
decommissioned, with the removal of aggregate materials to be transported to Southside landfill. 
Site Remediation 
Site remediation will include the restoration of the land to a condition as close to pre-development 
conditions. The restoration will include the de-compaction of topsoil by tilling as well as reapplying 
topsoil to match surrounding grade and maintain existing drainage patterns. Mass grading is not 
anticipated since the initial project will not alte r topography significantly. The topsoil shall be 
seeded with naturally occurring grass unless the ag reed upon land-use at the time of 
decommissioning requires different vegetation. Unde rground utility trenches shall be backfilled 
and compacted, dependent on soil conditions.  
Erosion and Sediment Control 
Erosion and sediment control Best Management Practi ces (BMP’s) will be implemented to 
minimize erosion throughout the decommissioning process. A silt fence will be erected along the 
perimeter of the site to control storm runoff gener ated from the site during demolition. Vehicle 
tracking pad will be installed at the entrance of t he gravel access road to minimize sediment 
tracking off-site.
