Planet Bluegrass Annexation and Zoning Review
EVC members discussed the potential economic vitality benefits of the proposed Planet Bluegrass 
development and the scope of any future EVC recommendation. The commission discussed focusing its 
comments on economic development considerations, while recognizing that parking, traffic, utilities, 
and other land-use matters would be evaluated through the Town's established review processes. The 
topic will return for further discussion in October in terms of referral and whether to draft a letter for 
BOT future consideration for when the issue is brought to the BOT.
2027 EVC Budget and Lodging Tax
The EVC discussed forming a subcommittee to develop recommendations for the 2027 budget, including
potential uses of lodging tax revenue to support marketing, tourism, and economic vitality initiatives. 
Commissioners discussed how lodging tax revenue could benefit the broader community and the role of 
the EVC in recommending funding priorities. The subcommittee will review potential budget requests, 
including marketing and festival-related initiatives, and develop recommendations for consideration by 
the full commission. The subcommittee plans to meet before the October EVC meeting, with the goal of 
presenting preliminary recommendations for discussion.
EVC and Chamber Coordination
Commissioners discussed opportunities to strengthen coordination between the EVC and the Lyons 
Chamber of Commerce while avoiding duplication of efforts. Marily, the Chamber Administrator, is 
expected to attend the October meeting to discuss the Chamber's initiatives and opportunities for 
collaboration.
Downtown Vacant to Vibrant Incentive Program
The EVC discussed a potential incentive program – Vacant to Vibrant to encourage businesses to occupy 
vacant commercial spaces in downtown Lyons. Discussion included possible funding sources, eligibility 
requirements, and administrative approaches. Members considered whether incentives should be 
limited to retail or other businesses generating sales and use tax revenue, as well as whether the 
program should include existing businesses seeking to expand. Potential approaches included an 
application-based program or rebates for certain eligible fees and improvements.  The discussion also 
included a potential sliding-scale incentive structure, with higher funding percentages for smaller 
projects and lower percentages for larger investments. Preliminary consideradtions included funding up 
to 75% for projects under $5,000 and approximately 10% for projects up to $25,000. These percentages 
were discussed as potential options and do not represent an approved funding structure.
They also discussed the possibility of developing a policy to provide rebates or other incentives for 
business improvements and expansions that may generate additional sales tax revenue.
No final program structure or eligibility criteria were established. Further work is needed to evaluate 
program eligibility, available funding, administrative requirements, and potential economic benefits 
before a formal proposal is considered. The EVC will continue developing the proposed business 
incentive program for further review and consideration.
Funding Incentives
The EVC continued its discussion of other business incentives intended to support existing businesses, 
encourage investment, and strengthen economic vitality in downtown Lyons.
Commissioners discussed opportunities to better promote existing resources, including business 
recovery loans and facade improvement funding.
