Chair Mike Jackson called the meeting to order and confirmed a quorum. Present: Mike Jackson, Sandra Haber, 
Julia Herz, Eva Guenther, Eric Friedman, and Glen Delman, who arrived during roll call. Jennifer Kovarik was

No September meeting was held. The August 10 gathering was not an official meeting because the agenda was 
not submitted for public notice. Dave advised that its notes could be posted as draft notes, but actions taken

3  Audience business
 Kara apologized for her role in past tensions, raised concerns about insufficient Confluence neighborhood

 Pam Barnes criticized personal conflicts within the commission, urged members to put community service

public statements and encouraged members to watch the recent BOT workshop on board and commission roles.
4  Procedural business
Roles, responsibilities, and expectations remain pending further town leadership guidance. Members requested 
clearer advance notice of terms ending, vacancies, and appointments. Dave said notices should come from the 
front office and clerk’s office. Annual training and renewed quarterly meetings of commission chairs or their 
substitutes were being pursued. Julia will confirm her anticipated year-end term expiration and intends to 
continue the dark sky work.
No new decision on meeting space or remote access was recorded. Dave reported difficulty connecting to Zoom 
and took written notes.
5  New and continuing business and events
A  Word about town
Members raised concerns about visitor-built rock barriers in the creek. Dave said spring runoff generally 
disperses them; staff intervene when obstructions create significant wildlife, flow, or safety issues. Dave will ask 
about the stalled kayak shipping container painting project, for which concepts and funding had been under 
discussion. Memorial requests are handled through the town’s online tree program, with GPS locations and 
certificates; the town has moved away from memorial benches following flood losses.

B  Dog waste pilot and continuation
Eva reported that the six-month dog waste composting pilot had operated smoothly, with limited contamination
and positive community engagement. Staff responded to user suggestions about container placement and 
compostable bag access. The program diverted waste from landfill, but there were no data showing whether it 
reduced uncollected dog waste. Ongoing education remains necessary.
Dave confirmed service and bags would continue through the end of 2026 within the parks budget. He will 
address an unlocked bag dispenser. Eva is tentatively scheduled to report to the BOT in early November and will 
include final costs from Dave, recommendations, and follow-up community communication.
Continuation at the dog park was supported. Expansion to other collection stations may be explored, but 
volume, contamination, and staff logistics require further review. Members also discussed grants and better 
outreach about dog registration fees supporting dog park improvements.
C  Dark Sky Places application and initiative
Julia is preparing an Urban Night Sky Places application with lighting inventories and seasonal sky readings. 
DarkSky International encouraged a broader park application. Julia and Dave will meet with its representative in 
October to clarify eligible parks and the distinction between parks and open space before finalizing the scope, 
mapping, and documentation.
The application will require a lighting management plan, potential signage, and a phased approach to 
improvements. Costs and funding remain to be determined. Julia aims to submit in 2026, subject to completing 
the required information and town review before submission. Summer and winter night sky events are being 
planned with the library and coalition, including weather alternatives.
D  PRC budget requests
The commission reviewed and unanimously approved its proposed priorities and requests, including the added 
$500 request for the Lyons Community Farm Project. A motion and second were recorded. These are 
recommendations for BOT consideration; final town funding remains subject to the budget process.
1. Dog waste composting continuation. 2027 total of $9,850: $5,000 proposed within the parks budget and 
$4,850 in additional funding requested. Staff are exploring grants and sustainability funding.
2. Lights Out Lyons event support. $1,200 proposed within the parks budget for community events and night 
sky education.
3. Dark Sky Places application fee. $250 proposed within the parks budget. Julia and Dave will coordinate 
reimbursement.
4. Youth scholarships and leagues. $1,500 discussed for recreation access. Dave said this amount generally 
covers scholarship demand; free clinics and camps are funded separately.
5. Second double gated dog park entrance. $9,500 estimate includes materials, gates, hardware, and some 
signage. Staff recommended the project, but it was not included in the draft budget and requires BOT 
consideration.
6. Dark sky preparation and lighting improvements. Retained as a planning placeholder for 2027 and beyond, 
with costs to be determined.
Additional request from Jennifer. $500 requested for the Lyons Community Farm Project nonprofit overseeing 
the orchard and related groups, rather than the orchard alone. Members supported the request while discussing
intended use, equitable distribution, and the property’s designation. The request was not in the draft budget.

7  Staff liaison update
 Seasonal winterization is underway, including restroom shutdowns and irrigation blowouts.
 A seasonal, trailer-mounted sauna proposal had been approved for LaVern M. Johnson Park. The third-party 
vendor will pay the town a fee, staff the operation while open, and supply its own utilities. Two potential 
locations near river access were under consideration.
 Staff are preparing holiday lights and events. Volunteers are needed for the Halloween parade and Parade of
Lights; interested members should contact recreation staff or Town Hall.
 An ice rink appears unlikely this season because of weather and the effort required for limited usable ice. 
Staff are monitoring conditions and have considered non-ice alternatives.
