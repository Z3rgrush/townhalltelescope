• Meter Collar Policy 
• Municipalities with less than 5000 residents don’t have to allow meter collars, so 
Lyons doesn’t have to allow these by law. 
• Lyons does, however, need to allow plug-in solar by law. The current LMC does 
not allow for this and must be modified. 
• Examples: Enphase meter collar is allowed by many towns. Fort Collins permits 
the Tesla version. Las Animas does not allow them at all.  
• Meter collars are getting attention now to ease the use of plug-in solar. However, 
they can also be used to expand service for older houses that may be limited to 
50-100 amps. For electrification and EV chargers, a meter collar could be used if 
the installed panel doesn’t have the capacity. 
• Expansion of service would require the customer to underground, so a meter 
collar would be an attractive option to avoid that. 
• Kerr expressed concern that plug-in solar could impact the town’s bill from 
MEAN. If customers are not required to install production meters (which are more 
expensive), then MEAN would be relying on the nameplate rating, which would 
increase costs to the town. 
• It is not clear how plug-in solar would work with net-metering. 
• The consensus of the UEB was to allow meter collars in Lyons, though policy 
details still need to be worked out.

Water Supply Element
Lyons serves a population of just over 2,100 through its municipal water and 
wastewater utility. The service area generally follows the Town of Lyons 
boundaries, except for approximately 40 properties along Apple Valley Road in 
Boulder County. The Town receives treated water from the City of Longmont under 
an intergovernmental agreement first established in 2003 and reviewed and 
updated several times over the past two decades. That agreement helped 
incorporate water planning into the Town municipal code and long-range growth 
planning. In fall 2022, Lyons staff attended a WaterSmart workshop and developed 
an action plan to integrate water efficiency and water supply considerations into 
planning and development processes. The Town’s comprehensive plan was most 
recently updated in 2023 and in 2025, the Town reviewed its water supply and 
demand with Longmont.
Lyons retains it's own water rights portfolio, which includes native waters from the 
South St. Vrain, Ralph Price Reservoir and Lake MacIntosh as well as supporting 
water rights from Northern Water Conservancy District's Colorado Big-Thompson 
Project. We have maintained a strong water rights portfolio through our initial 
agreement with Longmont and it's requirement for the dedication of water rights 
based on actual water service connections and not the amount of land that might be 
annexed into the water utilities service area.  This water rights dedication policy is 
also found in our municipal code.  In 2019 we worked with Longmont to amend our 
IGA so we could accept a more affordable water right for development that included 
affordable housing.
Water Conservation Policies
Lyons currently experiences approximately 8% water loss. In 2027, the Town plans 
to budget for a capital improvement project to replace about 50 aging water meters. 
This upgrade will allow the AMI water-reading software to measure usage in gallons
rather than increments of 1,000 gallons, enabling faster leak notifications for 
customers.
The 2026 drought conditions have increased coordination with neighboring 
jurisdictions on water resource concerns, conservation messaging, and potential 
drought restrictions.
In 2026, Lyons developed a Water Shortage Action Plan. For water supply planning, 
the Town evaluates the system using 3/4-inch service connection equivalents and 
B3
,
estimates 46,000 gallons per 3/4-inch connection. The Town is working to establish 
a reduction goal for this amount.
Demand and Supply Estimates
Lyons provides Longmont with an annual letter estimating projected water demand 
increases over the next five years, as required under the 2003 agreement. In 2025, 
Lyons reviewed its water supply and demand with the City of Longmont. The 
analysis showed a current supply of 438 acre-feet and current demand of 361 acre-
feet. It also projected a future supply of 534 acre-feet and future demand of 464 
acre-feet if Lyons reaches the maximum number of 3/4-inch equivalent water 
connections allowed under the intergovernmental agreement. Lyons currently has 
115% capacity relative to current demand, and water rights required from 
development are expected to maintain that capacity through buildout.
Lyons will incorporate more detail into its comprehensive plan about how it 
estimates future water demand and monitors its water supply portfolio. As a small 
town with limited growth expectations and as the sole water provider in town, 
Lyons will use the Low-Tech pathway.
Lyons Water Utility staff will review the Water Supply Element of the 
comprehensive plan with the Lyons Utility and Engineering Board in 2030 and again
when the comprehensive plan is updated in 2033. After that, the element may be 
reviewed with each comprehensive plan update and at the midpoint of each 10-year
update cycle. Staff will then present recommendations for updating the element to 
the Town Board of Trustees.

Integrating with the Colorado Water Plan
Lyons works with two conservancy districts and is exploring how its water rights could 
support nearby agriculture and improve environmental stream flows. Town staff serve on 
boards and participate in working groups for water-focused organizations, including the 
Watershed Center and Left Hand. The Town has also discussed potential water storage 
options within its growth area, is participating in the St. Vrain WRAP, and supports 
efforts to protect and improve riparian and wetland habitat in undeveloped floodplain 
areas.
Lyons has evaluated infill and accessory dwelling unit development as a potential water-
conservation strategy because it may reduce turf-grass irrigation. The Town is also 
working to add Green Stormwater Infrastructure requirements to development standards 
and capital project construction requirements. Staff toured Boulder County Green 
Stormwater Infrastructure projects to learn from and collaborate with regional partners.

Guidelines for Review 
W
hy do staff, boards, commissions, and other interested parties need to review their 
strategies from the comprehensive plan? 
- The comprehensive plan states that each assigned party should review their assigned
strategies. This helps determine relevance, priority, and can also help with budgeting
requests and funding for the year. It can also give boards, commissions, and staff some
areas to focus on when there is a slow period or help keep everyone focused.
Reviews can help with budget requests and the annual report 
- Progress on implementation strategies outlined in Chapter 4 should be reviewed
annually to set priorities to allocate limited resources. A brief status report regarding
near-term strategies should be provided by Town departments and boards and
commissions in the Town’s Annual Report (Page 14).
Update priorities 
- This chapter should be reviewed and updated on an annual basis to update the status of
near-term strategies; review and refine the anticipated timing of mid-term and long-term
strategies, or make other adjustments as needed. Progress on implementation
strategies should be communicated by Town departments and boards and commissions
in the Town’s Annual Report (Page 124).
Track Progress 
- Establish a framework for monitoring progress over time. Progress that is made toward
achieving the community’s goals must be monitored, and the results of that monitoring
be made available to the community at large (Page 125).
o Near-Term – these strategies should be completed within one to two years
o Mid-Term – these strategies should be completed within three to five years
o Long-Term – these strategies should be considered within six to ten years
o Ongoing – these strategies will be completed incrementally as opportunities arise
- Strategies may be implemented sooner if the opportunity arises (funding becomes
available) or as community priorities change over the planning horizon (Page 127)
- Only the near-term and ongoing will be populated in the priority/resource required
column.
- There will need to be a yearly priority setting exercise by the BoT to set Near-Term
strategies that reflect current Board priorities, funding constraints and input from other
Town Boards and Commissions. (Page 127)
What are the steps for Boards and Commissions? 
1. Receive a reminder or set a reminder to begin reviewing assigned strategies
a. At the beginning of July (or sooner), each B&C will receive a reminder to begin
working on their comprehensive plan review. The reviews must be complete and
back to staff by September 31 so that it is available prior to budgeting
2. Add a new column to the spreadsheet for the year
a. Each Board and Commission will have a spreadsheet they can use each year to
track progress. The spreadsheet will contain the feedback from previous years.
Each B&C should add a column with a new year and begin to fill in the sheet. 
Depending on the amount of goals assigned to the B&C, the review may take a 
couple of meetings but we hope that this will become easier as strategies are 
reviewed more often, completed, etc. 
b. Evaluations can be used by B&C to ask for money during budgeting and can 
assist with tracking progress and activities for the annual report 
3. Review the lead/partner 
a. Each B&C should review every strategy they are assigned either a lead or a 
partner. Boards can and should make comments if they do not believe the 
strategy should be assigned to them or if changes should be made to the 
lead/partner. 
4. Review strategies for priority 
a. If a B/C believes a Near-term or Ongoing strategy should NOT be worked on 
now, they can suggest to remove the priority. If there is a goal the B/C believes 
should be a priority in the near-term or is actively being worked on the B/C 
should note this and make it clear such as, “We believe this should be moved to 
near-term and we anticipate a lot of progress this year…” 
5.  Make progress updates or general comments 
a. This can include very thorough reporting about activities, priorities, etc. so that 
they can update others on their progress, or even what they do not believe is 
relevant. Comments can be brief or can give details about grants, work being 
done, discussions, etc. 
6. Make recommendations for new goals 
a. B&C’s can give themselves steps to achieve goals, suggest other changes or 
comments and these will be added into a separate section of the review.  
7. Make additional comments to the comprehensive plan 
a. B&C’s may suggest something that may be missing, that needs to be added 
based on recent events, or that they feel is a priority for the year or hindered 
progress in the previous year 
b. These suggestions can be placed on the separate tab of the spreadsheet 
8. B&Cs may select up to 3 high priority strategies to focus on for the year. 
a. This can be added to their webpage or become a focus for the B/C 
b. Not all Boards may wish to do this 
c. Some Boards may wish to break down the strategy into measurable short-term 
goals as well which may assist with implementation 
d. Example from Nederland’s Sustainability Board
