September 22, 2026
Water Conditions
Summary - WY 2026
Observed temperature
Temperatures in Water Year 2026 ( Oct 2025 - August 2026) continue
to be firmly the warmest on the Colorado Climate Center ’ s 131- year
record.  
August 2026 ranked as the warmest August on record. This was the
fourth record- breaking month during Water Year 2026: December, 
February, March, and August. 
So far, temperatures in September 2026 have been well above normal
across most of the state. Parts of the Eastern Plains experienced
temperatures up to 8° F above normal. 
Observed precipitation
This Water Year has been the 6th driest on record. Nowhere in the state
experienced an unusually wet water year through August.  
August 2026 was the 47th driest August on the period of record. 
Southern Colorado was dry in August, parts of northwest Colorado and
the eastern Plains were wetter than average. 
So far, September 2026 has been much wetter than normal across most of the West Slope and the Eastern Plains. 
Statewide September month- to- date precipitation is well above normal at 167% of the median. 
Snowpack
Statewide snow water equivalent ( SWE) was amongst the lowest on record. Peak SWE statewide occurred a
month early ( early March) at just 51% of the median. 
Snowpack has melted out in all basins. Median melt- out timing averaged 36 days ahead of normal. 
Streamflows, Soil Moisture, & Reservoir Storage
Observed streamflow from April through July was 28% of median statewide. August flows were 26% of the median
and many runoff points established new periods of record minimums. 
Soil moisture conditions have increased across gauges along the West Slope and parts of the Eastern Plains due to
increased precipitation events. 
Reservoir storage was 38% of capacity and 56% of median on September 1. 
Storage is lower than last year in every basin except the South Platte. The
Gunnison and Colorado Headwaters have the largest departures from last year. 
Drought conditions
Drought continues to impact Colorado, with record or near- record low
streamflows and reservoir levels. 
Approximately 88% of the state is in at least moderate drought ( D1).  
Approximately 72% of the state is in at least severe drought ( D2).  
Seasonal outlook
El Niño is in place and strengthening, which tilts the odds toward wetter
conditions for the rest of fall. However, the influence of El Niño on mountain
snowpack remains uncertain. Colorado has never had a bad snow drought during
a strong El Niño, but only one out of three Super El Niños ( WY 1983) had a huge snow year and
well- above- average streamflows. The other two were closer to average ( 1998, 2016). There is also a small sample
size of past strong El Niño years ( 1983, 1998, and 2016), and this year’ s may be stronger than all of them, so it is
unclear if history is a good guide for making water management decisions. 
The next Water Conditions Monitoring Committee Meeting will be on November 17, 2026. 
Chair: Emily Adrid, CWCB
