September 11, 2026 
Be it re membered that the Vice Mayor and members of the Board of Aldermen met in a Work 
Session on Friday, September 11, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. in the second-floor conference room of City 
Hall located at 110 West Main Street, Starkville, MS.  Present were Vice Mayor Roy A’. Perkins, 
Alderwoman Kim Moreland, Alderwoman Sandra Sistrunk, Alderman  William Pochop, 
Alderman Kyle Skinner, and Alderman Henry Vaughn, Sr., as well as City Attorney Berk 
Huskison and City Clerk Joanna M cLaurin were also present.  
Vice Mayor Perkins opened the work session.  The work session was properly noticed pursuant to 
Miss. Code Ann. §25-41-13 and a copy of that notice is included in the minutes. The work session 
was live streamed on Facebook.  The public and media were welcomed and attended. The session 
is also available for viewing on the City website.   
Edward Kemp gave a presentation on the Electric Department projects and rate structure. 
The Vice Mayor and members of the Board discussed items on the upcoming September 15, 2026, 
agenda.  Department Directors were available to answer questions related to the items. All agenda 
items were discussed.   
Vice Mayor Perkins closed the work session

MUNICIPAL CAPITAL ASSET POLICY 
1. Purpose and Objective 
The purpose of this policy is to ensure that the City of Starkville accurately records, 
classifies, depreciates, and safeguards its capital assets. This policy establishes 
capitalization thresholds, useful lives, and control procedures to ensure compliance with 
Mississippi state laws and GASB guidelines. 
2. Definition of a Capital Asset 
To be classified as a capital asset, an item must meet all of the following criteria: 
1. It must be owned or contractually controlled by the municipality. 
2. It must have an expected useful life of more than one (1) year. 
3. It must meet or exceed the capitalization threshold established in Section 3 of this 
policy. 
3. Capitalization Thresholds 
The City of Starkville will capitalize individual assets at their historical cost (or estimated 
historical cost if actual records are unavailable). Donated assets will be recorded at their 
estimated fair market value at the time of donation. 
The following thresholds shall apply for financial reporting purposes:

Infrastructure (Roads, Bridges, Water/Sewer Lines) $50,000 
Machinery, Equipment, and Vehicles $5,000

Aggregate Asset Capitalization  
Aggregate Assets (also referred to as "group" or "bulk" assets) are defined as a collection of 
homogenous, low-value items that are acquired simultaneously, possess similar useful 
lives, and are managed collectively as a single functional asset class. 
To qualify for aggregate capitalization, the asset group must meet all of the following 
criteria: 
1. Simultaneous Acquisition: The items are purchased concurrently (typically under a 
single project, contract, or fiscal year budget allocation). 
2. Systemic Utility: The items operate dependably together as an integrated system, or 
represent a complete, bulk refresh of standard municipal equipment. 
3. Materiality: The combined total cost of the initial aggregate purchase meets or 
exceeds the Aggregate Group Purchase Threshold. 
4. Useful Life: The items have an estimated useful life of more than one (1) year. 
To balance administrative efficiency with precise financial reporting, the following

4. Depreciation Method and Useful Lives 
Depreciation will be calculated on a straight-line basis over the estimated useful life of the 
asset. Asset salvage value will generally be assumed to be zero unless a significant 
residual value is anticipated. 
The following ranges of useful lives will be utilized: 
• Buildings: 40 years 
• Building Improvements: 20 years 
• Water and Sewer Systems: 30 to 50 years 
• Electric Utility Plant: 10 to 50 years 
• Streets, Sidewalks, and Bridges: 20 to 50 years 
• Heavy Machinery and Equipment: 10 years 
• Vehicles (Police/Fire/Public Works): 5 years 
• Office Equipment and Furniture: 7 years 
• Computer Hardware / Software: 3 years 
5. Inventory and Control Threshold (Non-Capitalized Assets) 
Important Note on Safeguarding Property: Certain items do not meet the dollar threshold 
for financial capitalization but are highly susceptible to theft or loss. 
Items costing between $1,000 and $4,999 will not be depreciated on financial statements 
but must be tagged, logged in a data inventory system, and verified annually by department 
heads. 
Some equipment is required to be included on the City’s inventory list regardless of the 
price paid by the City such as: weapons, audiovisual equipment, power tools, refrigerators, 
televisions, lawn maintenance machinery, chain saws, air compressors, welders, 
generators, computers, motorized vehicles 
6. Disposal and Transfer of Assets 
• Property Transfers: No capital asset may be transferred from one department to 
another without the prior completion of an Inter-Departmental Asset Transfer Form 
submitted to the City Clerk and approval by the Board. 
Page 54 of 371
• Disposal/Surplus: Capital assets may only be sold, traded, destroyed, or declared 
surplus in accordance with Mississippi Code Ann. § 31-7-13 and related statutes. 
All disposals require formal Board approval before the asset is removed from the 
inventory tracking system. 
• Theft or Loss: Any suspected theft or unaccounted loss of a municipal asset must 
be reported immediately to the Mayor, Police Department, Finance Department, 
and, if required, the State Auditor's Office. 
7. Roles and Responsibilities 
• Chief Financial Officer: Responsible for maintaining the master capital asset ledger, 
calculating annual depreciation, and coordinating with external auditors. 
• Department Heads: Responsible for the physical custody of all assets assigned to 
their department, reporting any new purchases or disposals promptly, and 
participating in the mandatory annual physical inventory. 
8. Amendment and Review 
This policy shall be reviewed annually by the Finance Department to ensure compliance 
with evolving state auditing requirements and economic factors.
