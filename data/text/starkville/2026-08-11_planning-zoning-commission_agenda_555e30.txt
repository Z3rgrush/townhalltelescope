The purpose of this report is to provide information regarding a Special Exception request 
by Avery Walton, on behalf of Coopers Land LLC , to allow for the use of a “Data Center - 
Crypto Currency Generation Facility.” The facility’s location is being proposed on the east 
side of the Industrial Park Road approximately 350 feet south of the Miley Road intersection 
in a n I ( Industrial) zoning district . This report evaluates the request under the Special 
Exception review criteria contained in Section 3.4.1 of the Unified Development Code 
(UDC). The property has the parcel number 102N-00-010.01. Please see attachments 1- 3. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION 
 
The applicant, Avery Walton, on behalf of Coopers Land LLC, is requesting a Special 
Exception to establish a “Data Center - Crypto Currency Generation Facility” on an 
approximately 8.60-acre parcel. The submitted site plan depicts data/mining containers, 
associated electrical equipment, gravel access and circulation areas, and a storage 
building. No separate operational narrati ve has been submitted describing the proposed 
equipment, projected electrical demand, anticipated water demand, cooling technology, 
hours of operation, staffing, maintenance activities, anticipated sound levels, or other 
operational characteristics of the facility. In the absence of these operational specifics, staff 
cannot establish whether the application complies with the approval criteria in Section 3.4.1 
of the UDC. 
 
The property fronts Industrial Park Road on the west and Glen Creek on the east. Existing 
industrial development is located to the north, including warehouse development and City 
utility infrastructure. Portions of the southern and eastern boundaries of the subject property 
adjoin property zoned SD -6, Suburban Detached residential. Based on the information 
available to staff, the nearest existing residential structure east of the subject property is 
approximately 1,430 feet from the easternmost portion of the site. Although existing 
residences are se parated from the proposed operational area, the subject Industrial 
property directly adjoins residentially zoned property. Section 8.2 of the UDC requires a 50-
foot side or rear building/structure setback where Industrial zoning adjoins residential zoning. 
The proposed site plan does appear to meet this requirement.  Section16.2 of the UDC 
establishes sound standards when the subject use immediately abuts residential zoning. 
 
The subject property also contains significant environmental constraints. The submitted 
survey identifies portions of the property within FEMA Zone AE, and the civil plans depict the 
regulatory floodway associated with Glen Creek across a substantial portion of the eastern

Section 1.8 of the UDC places the burden on the applicant to demonstrate that an 
application or development complies with the applicable review and approval standards. 
The burden is not on the City, staff, neighboring property owners, or other parties to establish 
compliance on the applicant's behalf. Accordingly, when the record lacks information 
reasonably necessary to make an affirmative finding required by Section 3.4.1 of the UDC, 
staff has identified the applicable criterion as “not established.” Such a finding does not 
mean that staff has affirmatively determined that the proposal will violate the criterion; 
rather, it means that the present application record is insufficient to  demonstrate that the 
criterion has been satisfied. 
 
Based on the application materials, departmental review comments, findings of fact, and 
the Special Exception approval criteria contained in Section 3.4.1 of the UDC , staff 
recommends denial of SE 26-08 based on the record currently before the City. The applicant 
has not provided sufficient information for staff to make all of the affirmative findings 
required for approval.

1. Site suitability. The proposed location of the structure and use has adequate space 
for development, adequate access to the site, fits contextually with the surrounding 
area, and has been properly designed for any environmental constraints. 
2. Traffic. There is no undue nuisance or serious hazard to pedestrian or vehicular traffic 
in the surrounding area by the proposed structure and use. 
3. Immediate neighborhood impact. The proposed structure and use is not detrimental, 
injurious, obnoxious, or offensive to other properties in the neighborhood. Negative 
impacts can include excessive trip generation, noise, vibration, dust, glare, heat, 
smoke, fumes, gas, odors, and inappropriate hours of operation.

use; however, compliance with the full Site Suitability criterion has not yet been 
established because the requested wetland delineation and final floodplain review 
have not been completed. 
2. Traffic. Staff has not identified evidence of an undue traffic nuisance or serious 
hazard.

adjoin SD -6 residential zoning. The applicant has not provided equipment 
specifications, an operational sound analysis, information concerning cooling and 
fan systems, anticipated heat rejection, vibration characteristics, or proposed hours

Section 3.4.1.C of the UDC, including noise, vibration, heat, and inappropriate hours
