A. PUBLIC HEARING AND CONSIDERATION OF VA 26-04 A REQUEST FOR 
A VARIANCE FROM FRONT YARD SETBACKS FOR A FENCE AND 
WORKSHOP/GARAGE LOCATED AT 235 BROOK AVENUE IN AN SD -2 
ZONING DISTRICT. 
Assistant City Planner Lyle MeCaskey presented the request by Jimmy McPherson 
for front yard setbacks for a fence and workshop/garage located at 235 Brook 
Avenue in an SD-2 zoning district.  
 
The applicant is planning a future workshop/garage. The proposed location for the 
structure is on the east side of the house adjacent to Gardenia Drive. The lot is 
bordered by public right-of-way on three sides. This request exceeds the maximum 
permitted height of 8-foot. The current issue is that the applicant would like to place 
a workshop/garage structure in the approximate location of the existing play set 
and would also like to have the ability to replace the existing privacy fence with 
another privacy fence in the fut ure. To allow for this, a dimensional variance from 
the setback requirements within the front yard would be needed.  The applicant is 
requesting relief from Sections 13.9.1.B.1.c and 13.9.1.B.6.b of the UDC to allow a 
20’x36’ detached workshop/garage within the required front yard setback of 
Gardenia Drive and to permit replacement of the existing privacy fence within 
areas where fence height is otherwise limited to 3½ feet. If the request for Variance 
is recommended for approval or denial, the applicant’s requests will be heard by 
the Board of Aldermen at the June 2, 2026, meeting. 
 
The Planning Department requests the following conditions of approval: 
1. The accessory structure shall substantially conform to the site plan submitted 
with VA 26-04. 
2. Any replacement fence within the variance area shall not exceed eight (8) 
feet in height. 
3. No portion of the structure or fence shall encroach into public right-of-way or 
utility easements.

parking setbacks of his property, but also paved the parking setbacks of the 
adjacent property . This made both properties illegal nonconformities due to the 
expansion of an already nonconforming parking lot.  The applicant also failed to 
remove the concrete from the City’s right -of-way from the old driveway that was 
part the permit.

SUMMARY 
 
The applicant requests a variance of +/-6’ feet from the maximum building height for 
proposed three -story multi -dwelling residential buildings within The Row development. 
Section 6.3.4 of the Unified Development Code (UDC) list s the maximum height for an 
apartment building in a C zoning district at 3 stories  and 38’ maximum. The proposed 
building, as measured from the ground, is +/ - 43’-8” (Attachment 3). The variance applies 
to the height of the proposed residential buildings and does not increase the proposed 
number of stories, dwelling units, bedrooms, or overall residential density of the 
development. 
 
The Row is proposed as part of a larger master-planned development that includes a new 
hotel and public road currently under construction. The submitted site plan depicts multiple 
residential buildings organized around internal parking areas, landscaped open spaces, 
and a centralized recreational and amenity area.  The buildings in question are shown in 
orange on the conceptual site plan (Attachment 4).

porches, and ten -foot interior ceiling heights. The applicant identifies these building 
elements as the basis for the requested dimensional relief (Attachment 5). According to the

building height, including reducing the architectural base and modifying other building 
components. The applicant states that further reductions would require changes to the roof 
design, ceiling heights, stair systems, structural framing, roof trusses, exterior elevations, 
windows, and other architectural elements. 
 
The applicant states that the additional height is intended to maintain the proportions and 
architectural character of the proposed three -story buildings rather than accommodate

CRITERIA FOR VARIANCE REVIEW AND APPROVAL (Section 3.7.1) 
 
3.7.1. Criteria for variance review and approval. 
A. Special Conditions.  That special conditions and circumstances exist which are 
peculiar to the land, structure, or building involved, and the same conditions are not 
applicable to other land, structures, and buildings in the surrounding area.   
B. Literal Interpretation. That the literal interpretation of the provisions of this Code would 
deprive the applicant of rights commonly enjoyed by other properties in the same 
district under the terms of this Code. 
C. Hardship. That the hardship has not resulted from the actions of the applicant. 
D. Special Privilege. That granting the variance requested will not confer on the 
applicant any special privilege that is denied by this Code to other lands, structures, 
or buildings in the same district. 
E. Minimum Variance.  That granting the variance is the minimum variance that will 
make possible the reasonable use of the land, building, or structure. 
F. Consistency with Comprehensive Plan . That the granting of the variance will be 
consistent with the general purpose, intent, goals, objectives, and policies of the 
Comprehensive Plan and this code and will not be injurious to surrounding areas or 
otherwise detrimental to the public welfare. 
 
STAFF ANALYSIS OF CRITERIA FOR APPROVAL 
 
A. Special Conditions. Staff finds that the building -specific conditions identified by the 
applicant provide support for the requested dimensional relief . The requested relief 
relates specifically to the configuration and architectural composition of the 
proposed buildings rather than an increase in the scale or intensity of the 
development. 
B. Literal Interpretation . Limited relief from the maximum height standard allows 
reasonable flexibility in the design of the proposed buildings while retaining the 
underlying three-story development form. 
C. Hardship. The requested relief is associated with the integrated design of the 
proposed buildings and is not being requested to obtain additional residential 
density or development intensity

The purpose of this report is to provide information regarding Variance request by IMS 
Acquisitions LLC for a Variance from front building setbacks for a proposed new multi -unit 
residential development referred to as Beacon Place. The development is located on the 
west side of Carter Blvd approximately 0.4 miles south of the Abernathy Dr intersection in an 
MDU-20 zoning district with the parcel number 103J-08-001.00. Please see attachments 1- 5. 
 
SUMMARY 
 
The applicant requests a variance from Unified Development Code (UDC) Section 6.3.4 to 
reduce the required front building setback along a proposed public street from 20 feet to 
10 feet. The proposed development encompasses approximately 64.7 acres and incl udes 
252 dwelling units in one -story buildings containing one and two  bedroom units with 
attached garages. 
 
The proposed street provides the  required connection for future access to the adjoining 
property to the north. Because the connection will function as a public street, the apartment 
buildings fronting the street are subject to the 20-foot front building setback in Section 6.3.4. 
The requested variance would reduce that setback to 10 feet only for the affected building 
frontages identified in Attachment 4. 
 
The applicant identifies grading conditions along the southern and northern portions of the 
property as the basis for the requested dimensional relief. Moving the affected buildings 
closer to the proposed street allows the site plan to better accommodate t hose grades 
while retaining the required street connection (Attachment 5). 
 
Based on the information contained in the application and staff report, staff finds that the 
requested variance satisfies the criteria for variance review and approval in UDC Section 
3.7.1. The site-specific combination of the required public-street connection and identified 
grading conditions supports limited relief from the 20-foot front building setback. Reducing 
the setback to 10 feet for the affected building frontages provides flexibility to address those 
conditions while preserving the public -street connection and maintaining the remaining 
applicable development standards. Staff recommends approval of a variance from UDC 
Section 6.3.4 to authorize a minimum 10-foot front building setback for the affected building 
frontages identified in Attachment 4.

peculiar to the land, structure, or building involved, and the same conditions are not 
applicable to other land, structures, and buildings in the surrounding area.   
B. Literal Interpretation. That the literal interpretation of the provisions of this Code would 
deprive the applicant of rights commonly enjoyed by other properties in the same 
district under the terms of this Code. 
C. Hardship. That the hardship has not resulted from the actions of the applicant. 
D. Special Privilege. That granting the variance requested will not confer on the

STAFF ANALYSIS OF CRITERIA FOR APPROVAL 
 
A. Special Conditions . Staff finds that special conditions exist due to the site’s 
topography, lot shape, and the location of the required street connection to the 
adjoining property. These conditions uniquely affect the building sites and are not 
generally found on surrounding properties. 
B. Literal Interpretation. Strict application of the 20 -foot setback, combined with the 
site’s grade and required street connection, would significantly limit the placement 
of permitted MDU -20 buildings, and restrict the reasonable development flexibility 
available to other properties in the district. 
C. Hardship. The conditions supporting the variance result from the site’s existing 
topography and required street connection, not from actions by the applicant. 
D. Special Privilege. The variance provides limited setback relief and does not change 
the property’s permitted use, density, height, or other MDU -20 development rights. 
Staff finds that approval would not grant a special privilege. 
E. Minimum Variance. The requested 10-foot setback provides the flexibility needed to 
address grading while maintaining the proposed public street and development 
layout.  
F. Consistency with Comprehensive Plan and UDC .  The proposed development 
provides additional housing and incorporates a public street connection intended 
to improve connectivity. The Comprehensive Plan supports a variety of housing types 
and increased street connectivity and promotes development patterns that support 
walkability.
