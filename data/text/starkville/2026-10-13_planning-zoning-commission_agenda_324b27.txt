City Planner Daniel Havelin presented the request by Avery Walton on behalf of 
Coopers Land LLC for a Special Exception to allow a Data Center -  Crypto 
Currency Generation Facility. The proposed site includes mining/data 
containers, electrical equipment, a storage building, gravel circulation areas, 
fencing, landscaping, and stormwater detention. 
The property is industrially zoned but directly adjoins residential zoning along 
portions of the site. Staff identified noise, vibration, heat, hours of operation, and 
buffering as important considerations. Environmental constraints include 
mapped floodplain and floodway areas and potential wetlands. Floodplain 
review remained pending, and the requested wetland delineation had not 
been submitted.

street subbase requirements.

C. PUBLIC HEARING AND CONSIDERATION OF SE 26-09 A REQUEST 
UNDER UDC SECTION 13.3.2 TO REMOVE THE ADDITIONAL 
STANDARD PROHIBITING A SPECIAL EVENT FACILITY ON THE FIRST 
FLOOR OF A BUILDING IN THE T-5D DISTRICT AT 200 SOUTH 
MONTGOMERY STREET, SUITE C. 
 
City Planner Daniel Havelin presented the request by Bonner Whittington on 
behalf of Boardtown Venue Co LLC for a Special Exception to waive the 
additional standard prohibiting a Special Event Facility on the first floor in the T -
5D district. The applicant leased Suite C at 200 South Montgomery Street within 
the Creamery at Central Station, formerly occupied by Boardtown Bikes. The 
mixed-use development includes commercial space on the first floor and 
residential condominium units above, including six units above Suite C. 
The applicant proposed weddings, receptions, meetings, fundraisers, workshops, 
showers, birthdays, and similar events by reservation, primarily during evenings 
and weekends. The applicant stated that the facility would not operate as a 
nightclub or continuous entertainment venue. Mr. Havelin explained that 
Special Event Facilities are generally allowed in T -5D and that the request 
concerns waiver of the first -floor restriction. Other UDC standards, including 
parking and hours of operation, would continue to  apply. Because single-family 
residential property is nearby to the southeast, applicable operating hours 
would be limited to 9:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. 
Staff reviewed the approved 2009 site plan, noting that the current parking 
layout differs significantly. Minimum parking requirements can be met for the 
existing mix of uses, but availability could be limited by event size and 
simultaneous activities. Staff also reviewed the Phase I condominium plat 
showing Suite C and the Phase II plat showing the six residential units above it.

6 
The Fire Marshal established an occupancy of up to 225 people with tables and 
chairs. 
Staff presented the following findings under the Special Exception review 
criteria: 
1. Site Suitability. Minimum parking can be met, but adequate parking during 
peak or overlapping uses has not been demonstrated. 
2. Traffic. No traffic management plan addresses circulation, parking, or 
emergency access. 
3. Immediate Neighborhood Impact. The applicant has not demonstrated 
that events will avoid impacts on residential units above or nearby homes. 
4. Availability of Public Services. Utilities are available, but unobstructed 
emergency access has not been demonstrated. 
5. Site Plan. No site plan or Development Review Committee review was 
required because no exterior or site modifications were proposed. 
6. Impact on Property Values. No evidence establishes a positive or negative 
effect on surrounding property values. 
7. Consistency with the Comprehensive Plan. The applicant has not 
demonstrated that removal of the first -floor restriction is consistent with the 
Comprehensive Plan. 
8. Additional Standards. Key operational requirements, including traffic 
management and operating hours, have not been adequately 
addressed. 
The request was noticed in accordance with the UDC. Thirty property owners of 
record within 160 feet were notified by mail; a legal advertisement was 
published in the Starkville Dispatch on July 24, 2026; and a sign was posted 
conspicuously on the property. The Planning Office received one emailed letter 
against the request, primarily concerning parking. Staff found insufficient 
evidence to support all required affirmative findings for approval.

13.5.8 Dwelling, Manufactured And Modular Home 
A. Definition: 
1. Manufactured home- A factory-built single-unit structure that is manufactured 
under the authority of 42 U.S.C., Sec. 5401, the National Federal Manufactured 
Home Construction and Safety Standards Act, is transportable in two (2) or 
more sections, is built on a permanent chassis, and is used as a place of 
human habitation; but which is not constructed with a permanent non- 
removable hitch or other device allowing transport of the unit other than for 
the purpose of delivery to a permanent site, and which does  not have non- 
removable wheels or hitch-axles, permanently attached to its body or frame. 
Manufactured homes shall not include travel trailers, camper trailers, 
campers, self-contained motor homes, or camper buses. A mobile home shall 
be defined as a residential dwelling that was fabricated in an off -site 
manufacturing facility, designed to be a permanent residence, and built prior 
to enactment of the Federal Manufactured Home Construction and Safety 
Standards. Mobile homes shall not be placed or moved to another lot within 
the city limits of Starkville. Manufactured homes with more than one (1) unit 
within one (1) structure are not permitted in any zoning district.  
2. Modular home- A dwelling built and delivered to a site in two (2) or more 
sections, meeting City’s current adopted building codes when connected to 
the required utilities. 
B. Parking: 
1. One (1) parking space per bedroom for all newly placed manufactured or 
modular home.  
2. See zoning district base dimensional standards for parking location and 
setback requirements. 
C. Loading: Loading/unloading areas accommodating delivery of materials to and 
from the premises are not permitted or required.  
D. Additional Standards: 
1. Homes shall be anchored according to International Building Code 
requirements.  
2. Homes shall bear the FMHCCS Label or Seal of Compliance.  
3. Homes shall have horizontal siding. At a minimum, the exterior siding shall 
consist predominantly of vinyl or aluminum lap siding whose reflectivity does 
not exceed that of flat white paint, wood or hardboard, comparable in

CRITERIA FOR SPECIAL EXCEPTION REVIEW AND APPROVAL (Section 3.4.1) 
 
1. Site suitability. The proposed location of the structure and use has adequate space 
for development, adequate access to the site, fits contextually with the surrounding 
area, and has been properly designed for any environmental constraints. 
2. Traffic. There is no undue nuisance or serious hazard to pedestrian or vehicular traffic 
in the surrounding area by the proposed structure and use. 
3. Immediate neighborhood impact. The proposed structure and use is not detrimental, 
injurious, obnoxious, or offensive to other properties in the neighborhood. Negative 
impacts can include excessive trip generation, noise, vibration, dust, glare, heat, 
smoke, fumes, gas, odors, and inappropriate hours of operation. 
4. Availability of public services. The proposed structure and use is adequately served 
by sewer, water, electricity, fire protection, police protection, and provides for any 
stormwater requirements. 
5. Site Plan. A site plan shall be reviewed by the Development Review Committee prior 
to review by the Planning and Zoning Commission. This review shall be to determine 
if elements have been adequately provided on the plan. These elements can 
include, but are not limite d to: parking areas, loading areas, buffers, screening, 
landscaping, and signage. Additional approval by the Development Review 
Committee may be required for site plan approval after approval of a special 
exception. 
6. Impact on property values. The proposed location of the structure and use will not 
cause or contribute to a decline in property values of surrounding properties. 
7. Consistency with Comprehensive Plan. The proposed special exception is consistent 
with the goals, objectives, and policies of the Comprehensive Plan.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION 
 
The a pplicant is seeking a Special  Exception to exceed the 25% maximum for units 
containing four or more bedrooms for a proposed multi dwelling residential development. 
Section 13.5.7.D.4 states “A special exception shall be required for an apartment building if 
more than twenty-five percent (25%) of the proposed units have four (4) or more bedrooms”.  
 
The Row is proposed as part of a larger master-planned development that includes a new 
hotel and public road currently under construction. The submitted site plan depicts multiple 
residential buildings organized around internal parking areas, landscaped op en spaces, 
and a centralized recreational and amenity area. The buildings in question are shown in 
orange on the conceptual site plan (Attachment 3).  
 
The applicant is requesting a Special Exception to allow a greater percentage of four -
bedroom units than otherwise permitted for student housing. The applicant states that the 
proposed unit mix responds to demand from students seeking to live with established friend 
groups and allows The Row to provide larger four -bedroom residences within a boutique, 
amenity-oriented student housing development. 
 
Based on the application, submitted plans, and findings contained in this report, Staff 
recommends approval of SE 26 -11 to allow more than twenty -five percent (25%) of the 
proposed apartment units to contain four (4) or more bedrooms.

The purpose of this report is to provide information regarding a Special Exception request 
by KBR Hotel Partners, LLC for a reduction in the amount of required parking for a proposed 
hotel project on the east side of Mill Street directly east of the Cotton Mill Road intersection 
with the parcel numbers 101D -00-339.00, 101D-00-340.00, 101D-00-341.00 within a C zoning 
district. Please see attachments 1-5. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION 
 
The applicant is designing a proposed 102 -room TownePlace Suites hotel on the east side 
of Mill Street, directly east of the intersection with Cotton Mill Road. The property is located 
within the Commercial (C) zoning district. 
 
UDC Section 13.7.12.B.1 requires hotel developments within the C district to provide 1.25 
parking spaces per guest room. Based on the proposed 102 guest rooms, the development 
is required to provide approximately 128 parking spaces. The submitted site plan  provides 
103 on -site parking spaces, or approximately one parking space per guest room.  The 
applicant states that a ratio of one parking space per guest room is commonly considered 
an industry standard and that its experience with multiple operating hotels indicates that 
this ratio is adequate. 
 
UDC Section 3.6.1 authorizes the City Planner to approve an Administrative Adjustment of 
up to 10 percent of the required parking. The reduction necessary to accommodate the 
proposed 103 space parking plan exceeds that authority and therefore requires approval 
through the Special Exception process. 
 
The applicant has indicated that additional parking could potentially be provided through 
a perpetual off-site parking easement on the Cottonmill Marketplace property immediately 
west of the site in accordance with UDC Section 14.10.6. Staff has concerns that requiring a 
perpetual parking easement could unnecessarily encumber the adjoining commercial 
property and affect its future redevelopment or use. Approval of the requested Special 
Exception would allow the hotel to satisfy its parking requirement with the 103 spaces 
provided on the hotel site without relying on the adjoining property.
