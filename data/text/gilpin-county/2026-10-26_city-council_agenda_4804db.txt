Dear Chris,  
 
Re: Request to Extend EIAF Grant No. 9953 –  Gilpin County Rollinsville Plan and 
Infrastructure Feasibility Study 
 
Gilpin County respectfully requests a nine -month extension of EIAF Grant No. 9953, from 
November 30, 2026, through August 31, 2027, with corresponding adjustments to the 
Project Final Report deadline and reporting schedule. The request does not change the 
approved scope or grant amount. 
 
The project has experienced several setbacks, beginning with a near- complete loss of 
Community Development staff in May and June 2025. The current Director assumed the 
role in July and spent her first months rebuilding the department and managing several 
ongoing grant projects. 
 
In September, staff discovered that the consultant had conducted no public engagement 
for the Area Specific Plan and had no plans to do so. The County redirected the work to 
prioritize public participation before proceeding with drafting, holding two commu nity 
meetings in October and conducting a survey through November and December. 
 
More substantial concerns emerged regarding the consultant's interpretation of the project 
scope. Although the grant contemplated both an Infrastructure Feasibility Study and an 
Area Specific Plan, the consultant treated them as a single document focused a lmost 
exclusively on technical infrastructure analysis. Drafts lacked land -use planning 
components expressly included in the grant agreement, such as regulatory barriers and 
historic preservation goals. Staff repeatedly identified these omissions and direc ted 
revisions, but significant feedback was not incorporated. 
 
The technical work also contained questionable assumptions, particularly regarding water 
demand. For example, at two public meetings, the Board directed the consultant to replace 
generic water-use estimates for the local brewery and distillery with actual data provided 
by the business owners. Despite acknowledging the oversight, apologizing, and assuring 
the County that corrections would be made, the consultant's March 2026 draft still relied on 
the original assumptions. 
 
Nevertheless, the feasibility work substantially improved the County's understanding of 
Rollinsville's infrastructure options. Its roadway, parking, pedestrian safety, and civic -
improvement findings provide useful direction for future planning. The water and 
wastewater analyses identif y potential solutions, although certain calculations require 
validation before supporting infrastructure design or investment decisions. 
 
By March 2026, only two of the original seven consultant team members remained engaged 
with the project. After months of repeated requests and unresolved concerns, the County

concluded that further revisions were unlikely to produce the comprehensive Area Specific Plan required by the grant. The 
parties agreed to end the contract without additional work or payment. County staff assumed responsibility for preparing 
the Area Plan  in-house, building on the technical findings while addressing the original scope and priorities of the   
grant, the community, Board, Planning Commission, and Historic Preservation Commission. 
 
Planning Commission work sessions through April 2026 established direction on the Area Plan's vision, goals, and strategies. 
In May, staff paused drafting to prioritize the Land Use Code update, which carried a separate September 30, 2026, DOLA 
deadline. S taff is now prepared to resume the Area Plan, integrating infrastructure findings, community input, and 
outstanding land-use and policy components into a cohesive document. 
 
An important remaining consideration is the potential passenger rail stop, which has significant implications for roadway 
access, parking, pedestrian improvements, civic development, and economic development. The County first sought a 
meeting with Union Pacific in February 2026. Despite repeated follow-ups and CDOT's assistance, an initial meeting did not 
occur until September 1, seven months later. Shortly before a September 17 follow-up, Union Pacific disclosed that a formal 
inquiry and an estimated $75,000 reimbursement agreement would be required for its review. The County had not previously 
been advised of this requirement or budgeted for it in 2026; the estimated cost is included in the proposed 2027 budget, with 
Board approval anticipated in December. 
 
The County did not anticipate the time required simply to establish communication with Union Pacific and initiate review of 
potential station locations. Although the Area Plan can be completed without a determination from the railroad, 
understanding the li kelihood of approval would improve the practicality of its recommendations and reduce the potential 
need for substantial amendments shortly after adoption. 
 
This is the principal reason for requesting nine months rather than six. We recognize that nine months is a substantial 
extension to the original 24 -month grant term. However, the additional time would allow the County to complete the 
planning process more deliberately while advancing railroad discussions. Under the preferred nine- month extension, staff 
would complete a comprehensive draft in the first quarter of 2027, conduct public and Commission review during the spring, 
and target adoption by summer 202 7. This would provide more time to refine outstanding planning components, evaluate 
public feedback, and incorporate meaningful information from Union Pacific, if available. 
 
If DOLA considers nine months excessive, the County could follow an accelerated six-month schedule, through May 31, 2027. 
Staff would target a working draft by the end of 2026, public and Commission review during the winter and spring, and 
adoption by May.  This is achievable, but provides less opportunity to incorporate railroad information or refine the plan 
before adoption. 
 
Under either scenario, planning and railroad coordination would proceed concurrently, without making adoption contingent 
upon Union Pacific's decision. Unresolved rail questions would be identified as contingencies in the adopted plan. 
 
We prefer to request sufficient time now rather than risk a second extension should the railroad review take longer than 
anticipated. However, we hope not to need the full nine months and would proceed with adoption and grant closeout earlier 
if the work is satisfactorily completed. 
 
The County remains committed to fulfilling the original grant objectives and producing a credible, useful, and implementable 
long-term plan for Rollinsville that builds on the technical feasibility analysis already completed. We appreciate DOLA's 
continued support and consideration of this request.

preparedness and its ability to assist communities affected by a disaster.
