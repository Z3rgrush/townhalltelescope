4. Discussion 
  
1. PAC Budget 
Budget. Staff reported that the committee's budget of about $91,000 is unapproved but 
equal to last year's. Staff advised the Town Manager that spending would be cut 

Page 2 of 3 
substantially because the Town expects to be over budget in 2027. Contributing factors 
were sales tax revenue running about 6–7% below last year (down 20% early in the 
summer), and major projects such as Clayton Court and the planned public works 
building. By consensus, the committee agreed to hold off on sculpture purchases in 
2027. A sculpture is being donated, the Downtown Development Authority has 
expressed interest in a Fraser sculpture near the train station, and members wanted to 
wait until Clayton Court is finished. Staff will also reduce swag and band spending for 
Picnic in the Park. The committee expressed support for setting up an online, drop-
ship Fraser merchandise store. It would be priced at cost or with an optional voluntary 
$5 donation to the Town or the art center, and could feature artwork from the mural 
festival winner. 
2. Fire and Ice 2027 
 
Sculptures and layout. The committee reviewed plans for the February 6 event. Artist 
Aaron will build a lighted cube and a kinetic flame sculpture, at about $1,000 more 
than the previously considered dome (about $1,200), which yields two sculptures. A 
couple with a fire heart sculpture and tiki-style street lamps will also participate. The 
Town will not pay for their lodging. Placement will be finalized on a map to be 
circulated, taking into account snow conditions, the photo booth, food vendors, the 
bike park and the bonfire. A designated backup location near the road, photo booth or 
restrooms was supported for the hot air balloon basket and burners in case of high 
winds. The balloon glow is scheduled for 4:30–5:30 PM. Two shuttles will run per 
location. The elementary school lot, Valley Life Church and the ball fields and 
recreation center will be used, with the last to be reconfirmed. 
 
Entertainment and amenities. The Homestead circus lighted sway pole was rejected as 
too expensive ($5,000 for one, $7,000 for two). Lava Box is unavailable. John Henley 
Designs will sponsor a warming tent with fire pits. Members will bring portable 
propane and wood fire pits, to be placed near the info tent and the snow sculpture. One 
member will contact a maker of ammo-can fire pits. The sauna provider plans to attend 
Friday and Saturday. Gather Collective will build a business burn-off, and a form was 
sent to other businesses. Staff will ask Girl Scouts to sell cookies under the Town tent. 
 
Vendors and logistics. Confirmed vendors are River Wolf, Mountain Mary's cookies, 
Idyllwild (with the liquor permit process coordinated through the Town Clerk) and 
Alex's food truck. Rooted and a coffee truck are pending, and members will contact 
local coffee options so more than one hot beverage is available. Music will begin at 
4:00 PM, and vendors will open an hour earlier than last year. Fire pits will be lit at 
about 5:00 PM, with sunset at 5:37 PM and dark at 5:54 PM. Members will pursue a 
fireworks contractor confirmation and the snow sculptor, who still needs direction on 
the sculpture and kids' slide. Hay bale availability is uncertain because of high prices. 
A meeting with the high school on Monday will address a possible drum corps 
appearance. KFFR's new director is Michael Solomon, and the technical lead will 
remain unchanged. 
 
3. Other Business

The committee reported no progress in soliciting artists for next year's mural. Members 
agreed to print a single flyer and personally distribute it to downtown businesses, meeting 
at Town Hall to divide the territory. Calls for all events will open January 1, and returning 
artists may be contacted individually. Staff reminded members of a Fraser Valley Arts 
open house on Friday, November 13, from 3:00 to 5:00 PM at the library, which gathers 
community input for the design of the new building. An online form is also available, and 
the display will remain through the end of the month. 
Members were reminded that nominations and reappointments for the Public Arts 
Committee are due November 16. About nine applications had been received for the 
eleven seats. The incoming Board will make the decisions after being sworn in in 
December. Staff will send another call for applicants and invite those who cannot serve to 
join the volunteer list.
