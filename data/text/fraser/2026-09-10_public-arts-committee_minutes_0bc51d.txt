4. Discussion 
a) Mural Festival Mural Updates 
Kelly shared that 2026 was the best year yet for the Mural Festival, highlighting the following: 
● The silent auction brought in $17,000, up from $11,000 the previous year, with total online revenue 
reaching $21,000 
● Several murals sold for over $1,000 for the first time 

 
 
● All 30 murals sold, including the final one being purchased recently 
● Artist feedback was very positive overall 
Commission Murals 
Sarah shared renderings for two commission murals currently in progress: 
● Christy Canon's mural — A rendering was shared; the other side of the wall is planned to feature a 
nighttime version with northern lights. Steve noted the artist is available from October 6th 
● Stephanie's mural — The current design needs tweaking, as Stephanie wasn't happy with the bird-
focused concept. The artist plans to visit the first week of October to paint 
The group discussed covering travel and lodging for the artists, noting that for a $3,000 commission from 
Oregon, the pay wouldn't be worthwhile without some support. Steve offered to potentially provide his short-
term rental unit if October dates aren't already booked. The group agreed to coordinate on logistics once dates 
are confirmed. 
Wall Ride Mural 
Sarah shared that Bobby McGee was hired to repaint the wall ride. The rendering features a large "Fraser" 
text with a simple sunrise/sunset and trees background. The group loved it, noting its visibility from the road. 
b) Mural Festival 2027 
The group discussed a potential shift for 2027 toward painting building walls instead of panels, an idea 
originally raised by Steve. 
Key discussion points: 
● Sarah proposed putting out a call to businesses to gauge interest in having their walls painted, along 
with a social media form asking about wall size and availability 
● Steve suggested targeting 10–15 properties as potential canvases, with a mix of invited mural artists 
and a competitive selection process, while potentially still including some 8x8 panels 
● The group agreed the budget needs to increase to support larger stipends for building murals. Sarah 
noted the current Art in Public Places budget sits around $91,000, with unspent funds in recent years 
that could be applied. Steve suggested bumping the budget by roughly $50K relative to what was 
spent 
● Kelly noted that building murals would reduce auction revenue that typically goes to Fraser Valley 
Arts, but the group agreed the trade-off was acceptable given the community beautification goals 
● Steve emphasized the importance of tying the initiative to Fraser's vision as an art-centric community 
and noted potential announcements related to the Fraser Valley Arts center coming in the next year or 
two 
● The group discussed the Granby experience as a cautionary tale, noting that public pushback there 
was less about business owners and more about general community reaction to certain art styles. The 
group felt Fraser's community is generally more receptive 
● Steve and Kelly agreed to do direct outreach to businesses in addition to the social media call 
● Sarah will create a flyer for businesses to share with committee members for lobbying
