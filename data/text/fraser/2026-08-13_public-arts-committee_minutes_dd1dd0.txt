4. Discussion 
1. Fire and Ice 2026 Recap 
The committee conducted a comprehensive review of the Mural Festival. The event was widely 
regarded as the smoothest in its history, with strong volunteer performance and positive public 
reception. Key outcomes and areas for improvement were identified across several themes. 

 
 
Financial Performance Silent auction revenues increased by approximately $6,000 over the prior 
year. Four murals sold for over $1,000, with one reaching $2,100 — a notable improvement over 
previous years. Donate-to-vote revenue via the online platform was slightly lower than the prior 
year, but is expected to balance out once all figures are reconciled. 
Awards Distribution The committee noted that award distribution was handled effectively this 
year, with efforts made to spread recognition across more artists. One minor shuffle was required in 
the Fraser's Favorite category, as the top two vote-getters had already received other awards. The 
committee affirmed a general preference for distributing awards broadly rather than allowing one 
artist to win multiple categories. 
Artist Tracking and Data A committee member offered to build a comprehensive spreadsheet 
tracking participating artists by year, awards won, mural subject matter, and repeat attendance. This 
was well-received as a tool to inform future jurying, interview talking points, and strategic 
invitations. It was noted that having this data would also support discussions about balancing 
returning and new artists. 
T-Shirts and Merchandise The committee discussed reintroducing Mural Fest branded T-shirts for 
sale, noting the strong positive response to the tie-dye activity. A proposal was made to use plain 
white shirts with Fraser Valley Arts branding printed in a resist technique, so unsold inventory could 
carry over between years. Presale options and a limited-quantity model were suggested to avoid the 
storage and staffing challenges experienced in past years. It was agreed that at minimum two 
volunteers would be needed at the info tent to support merchandise sales alongside ticketing. 
Volunteer Staffing and Incentives The committee discussed refining volunteer slot structures to 
better align coverage with peak attendance periods, and to reduce over-staffing during slower 
periods. A member raised the question of whether PAC members could receive additional 
compensation for extended hours worked during the festival, noting that other contractors and public 
works staff are paid for their time. It was acknowledged this would require review of bylaws and 
further conversation with town staff. A general consensus supported the concept of exploring 
stipends or acknowledgment for high-hour volunteers. 
PAC Membership and Bylaws A member formally raised for the record that the committee is 
currently at capacity, while interested community members have been unable to join. It was 
suggested that attendance expectations be codified in the bylaws, such that members missing a 
threshold number of consecutive meetings without notice could have their participation reviewed. 
The possibility of term limits or reapplication requirements was also raised as a mechanism to ensure 
active, engaged membership. 
Wayfinding and Maps The committee identified wayfinding as an area for improvement. Printed 
maps were not available at the event, and while most attendees adapted, some confusion was noted 
— particularly around murals tucked behind buildings or off the main corridor. Proposals included 
reusable yard-sign style maps with removable dot stickers for mural locations, QR codes at each 
mural cluster linking to a digital map, numbered flag markers corresponding to map zones, and 
printed map inserts attached to each artist's sign. It was acknowledged that QR codes present a 
barrier for some older attendees, and a multi-format approach was recommended. 
Signage and Flags Feedback was received from locals that the volume of event flags felt excessive, 
particularly during the week before the festival. The committee agreed to consider reducing flag 
 
 
density in the days leading up to the event and concentrating placement around key mural sites rather 
than along the full corridor. 
Mural Locations Discussion was held regarding the logistical challenges of the Fraser River Beer 
Co. location, which was identified as difficult for setup, takedown, and artist visibility. The 
committee agreed to reduce placement there to one mural going forward. The EMS station's red wall 
was identified as a promising future commission site. The parking lot across from the gas station was 
discussed but concerns about prior conflicts with neighboring businesses were noted. No formal 
location changes were decided. 
Community Participation Mural An artist provided written feedback suggesting the addition of a 
facilitated community mural, similar to a paint-by-number format, where attendees could participate 
under artist guidance. The committee expressed support for the concept in principle but noted it 
would require a dedicated on-site facilitator. The idea of partnering with a local professional artist or 
business — such as a tattoo artist or gallery — to staff this role was proposed as a way to also 
promote local arts businesses. 
Library Mini-Murals The library's mini-mural activity was noted as a significant success, with 
approximately 250 participants. The committee discussed the need to identify funding for supplies in 
future years, as this year's materials were donated from an estate. Lower-cost alternatives to canvas, 
such as cut plywood or hardboard, were suggested. 
Unsold and Past-Year Murals The committee discussed options for the one mural that did not sell 
at auction. Ideas included raffling past-year murals at future events, displaying them at community 
spaces such as the tennis court fencing, or incorporating them into Alpine Art Affair programming. 
No formal decision was made. 
Joe's Departure The committee acknowledged and expressed appreciation for the contributions of 
Joe, a Public Works representative who has been instrumental in the physical execution of Mural 
Festival. Joe's last day with the Town of Fraser was noted to be the day of the meeting. The 
committee expressed hope that he would return in a consulting capacity in future years, and noted 
that efforts to document his processes and institutional knowledge are ongoing. 
Next Steps and Upcoming Events The committee noted the following upcoming events: 
• Locals Landscape Exhibition — August 22, in conjunction with Tame Community Fest. 
Tie-dye activity will also be offered, with a portion of proceeds shared with the hosting 
organization. 
• Plein Air Festival — Cocktail reception on September 11; public viewing on September 12. 
Volunteers are needed for host duties at painting sites, gallery hang, reception, and takedown. 
Members were encouraged to confirm their inclusion on the Fraser Valley Arts volunteer 
email list. 
• Fire and Ice — Tabled for discussion at the next meeting. The committee expressed a 
preference for keeping the event scaled back, consistent with recent years, rather than 
returning to the competition format.
