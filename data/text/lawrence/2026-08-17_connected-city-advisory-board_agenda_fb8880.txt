Recommendations  
 Recommend approval of the 2026 Lawrence Bikes plan to replace the 2019 Lawrene Bikes 
plan. 
  
Executive Summary  
 The Lawrence Bikes plan is a guide to achieve the vision of “A bikeway network that 
supports safe and comfortable riding for all.”  
  
Through technical analysis, public feedback, and committee collaboration, this vision reflects 
Lawrence’s broader values around health, equity, mobility, climate resilience, economic 
vitality, and quality of life. The plan advances these values through:  
  
 A connected network that provides access to schools, parks, jobs, and daily 
destinations  
 Continued growth of programs that build skills, confidence, and safety  
 Infrastructure that reduces crashes and supports the City’s Vision Zero commitment  
 Partnerships that strengthen education, encouragement, and community engagement  
 An approach that integrates biking within the city’s multimodal transportation system  
 The Lawrence Bikes plan positions the city to make steady, meaningful progress 
while aligning with funding opportunities, policy frameworks, and long-term strategic 
goals.  
 The Plan provides recommendations on the general location and types of bicycle 
facilities, projects, policies and programs that support the goals and vision. The Plan 
includes a vision for a bikeway network that can be developed over time.  
  
Steering Committee Collaboration  
The Bike Plan Update Steering Committee played a central role in shaping the plan’s 
direction and final recommendations. Appointed by the Lawrence-Douglas County MPO 
Policy Board, the committee represented a wide range of bicycling interests and 
experiences—including neighborhood associations, local cycling clubs, Safe Routes to 
School champions, university representation, and community safety groups.  
  
The Steering Committee met over the last 18 months to review public input, existing 
Page 1 of 277
Page 18 of 294
conditions data, evaluate bikeway network options, and develop strategies across 
Engineering, Education, Encouragement, Enforcement, and Evaluation. Their insights 
ensured the plan remained grounded in local needs and community values.   
  
Public Engagement  
Public participation directly shaped the recommendations. Engagement included:  
 Three open houses and multiple tabling events  
 Three online surveys with more than 1,000 cumulative responses  
 Identification of network gaps, barriers, crash concerns, and preferred loop alignments  
 Focus groups with “plus users” (e-bike, scooter, one-wheel) and downtown parking 
users  
  
Several strong themes emerged from public feedback:  
 Riders consistently expressed that separation from vehicle trafficis the most important 
factor in comfort.  
 Community members emphasized the need to close gaps between existing 
bikeways—especially in the Lawrence Loop.  
 Respondents described intersections as key stress points, reinforcing the need for 
safe crossings and consistent design.  
 Many riders highlighted that the current network often feels discontinuous, reducing 
both comfort and usability.  
 Public comments stressed the importance of designing for all ages and abilities, not 
just confident or experienced riders.  
 Several respondents expressed the importance of fiscal responsibility in both the 
construction and long-term maintenance of bicycle infrastructure, noting that 
improvements should balance community benefits with thoughtful stewardship of 
public resources.   
  
The Steering Committee used this feedback to refine alignments, prioritize projects, and 
elevate strategies that directly address community concerns.  
  
A Shared Vision for Our Community  
The Plan envisions the development of a bikeway network that supports safe and 
comfortable riding for all. The plan’s goals include:   
 Improve Safety – Eliminate traffic fatalities and serious injuries for all bicyclists by 
2050.  
 Increase Ridership – Increase the bike to school percentage by 1% per year through 
2030.  
 Increase Access – Increase the percentage of the population within ¼ mile of a Level 
of Comfort 2 or below bikeway network to 80% by 2030.   
 Low Stress Network – Increase the mileage of low-stress bikeways (LOC 1 & 2) to 
55% by 2030.  
 Bicycle Friendliness – Achieve League of American Bicyclists Silver level Bicycle 
Friendly Community or higher by 2030.  
  
Prioritizing and Designing Comfortable Bikeways  
A major update in this plan is the refined Bicycle Level of Comfort (LOC) methodology. The 
Page 2 of 277
Page 19 of 294
revised LOC matrix aligns with national best practices and NACTO All Ages and Abilities 
guidance. It incorporates updated speed and volume thresholds and places greater 
emphasis on bikeways that provide predictable, separated, and comfortable environments. 
This focus on comfort is foundational to increasing ridership, expanding access for youth and 
seniors, and supporting mode shift goals outlined in Transportation 2050.  
  
The Plan does not detail what specific type of bikeway a future bikeway should have. Instead 
during project development, the road’s level of comfort will be analyzed using the Plan’s Bike 
Level of Comfort model. To determine the level of comfort, the speed and number of vehicles 
and the facility type are evaluated. Based on this result, the amount of physical separation 
between bicycle riders and motor vehicles will be determined.   
  
Building out the entire bikeway network is not financially feasible in the short term. Priority 
and Secondary Funding Networks were identified to feed projects in the Non-Motorized 
Project Prioritization program to prioritize and implement the 5-year Bike/Ped Standalone 
projects. The future bikeways are network links to be built as private development or road 
construction/maintenance occurs.  
  
Implementation  
Developing a truly bicycle friendly community takes more than building bikeways. 
Furthermore, it is not solely a municipal government function; it requires the entire 
community working together to implement non-infrastructure policies and programs. The 
Plan’s policies and programs are split into categories: Education and Enforcement; 
Engineering and Enforcement Operations; and Evaluation.  
  
This Plan will guide investments and decisions, helping Lawrence continue to grow as a 
safe, accessible, and bike-friendly community.

Fiscal Impact  
 Implementing the Lawrence Bikes Plan will require financial investment. However, adopting 
the plan does not commit the current City Commission, or future City Commissions to make 
specific investments. Future implementation recommendations for capital investments will 
require separate City Commission action throught the Capital Improvement Plan.
