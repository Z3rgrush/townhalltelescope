 4. Lawrence City Band Stage for South Park Proposal -  
  
  
Desired Action:  Recommend City Commission approval of the Lawrence City 
Band Stage for South Park Proposal. 
 
John Esau, representing the Lawrence City Band, presented a proposal to 
construct a small, elevated, ADA-accessible concrete platform near the South 
Park gazebo. The platform would address challenges associated with the 
band's expanded size, accessibility, drainage, equipment placement and use of 
the grass while allowing the band to remain at its historic location. 
The City Band intends to fully fund the project and estimates the cost at 
approximately $150,000. Funding of nearly $109,000 has already been raised, 
including a $50,000 donation designated for the project. The proposed platform 
would also be available for other community uses. 
Board members discussed the area's drainage issues and the possibility of 
incorporating future park improvements into the project. Members also 
suggested working with the Watkins Museum and Douglas County Historical 
Society to highlight the history of the gazebo and Lawrence City Band. 
The Board unanimously endorsed the proposal and encouraged the City 
Commission to approve it. Staff will prepare a memo for consideration by the 
City Commission.

approval of the Lawrence City Band Stage for South Park 
Proposal.

Arneill presented a proposal for the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board to 
serve as the review body for the City's Unmistakable Events Grant Program. 
The program, funded through transient guest tax revenue, provides $150,000 
annually to Lawrence-based events that demonstrate economic and tourism 
benefits and contribute to the City's character and reputation. 
Following the dissolution of the former Transient Guest Tax Grant Program 
Advisory Board, staff has used an ad hoc committee to review applications. 
The proposal would establish a process similar to the Cultural Arts 
Commission's review of community arts grants. 
Applications are expected to be accepted October 2 through November 1. 
Board members would independently review and score applications through 
Submittable, with results brought back to the Board for discussion and funding 
recommendations before being forwarded to the City Commission. 
Board members discussed the review timeline, diversity of perspectives, 
potential participation by youth, conflicts of interest and the existing scoring 
rubric. Porter noted that Submittable limits the number of reviewers to seven 
and offered to provide a practice opportunity using a sample application. 
The Board unanimously recommended that the City Commission approve the 
Parks and Recreation Advisory Board serving as the review body for the grant 
program. 
 
ACTION: Motion and Second: Recommend to the City Commission 
that the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board serve as the 
reviewing body of the Unmistakable Event Grant program, 
which would have the advisory board evaluate submitted 
applications and make funding recommendations to the City 
Commission for final approval.
