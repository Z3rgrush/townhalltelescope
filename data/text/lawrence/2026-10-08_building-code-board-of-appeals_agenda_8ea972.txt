1. Permit Abandonment - 904 Highland Drive (RES-24-0585) 
 
The letter asserts that Mr. Cesare repeatedly attempted to schedule inspections but 
was rebuffed or delayed by City staff, and that the homeowner (not VBC 
Construction) bears responsibility for the failure to complete the work. 
 
The Department disagrees on both points. 
 
The Department’s records (or lack thereof) directly contradict this assertion. Our 
digital permitting system indicates that these inspections remain outstanding, with 
no inspection requests submitted by the applicant (VBC Construction): See 
Attachment 1.   The Department's inspection scheduling system (whether initiated by 
phone or online) generates a digital record of every inspection request at the time it 
is made. Inspections are conducted the same day or the following business day. 
There is no digital record of Mr. Cesare having scheduled inspections that were not 
honored. If Mr. Cesare had requested inspections and the Department had failed to 
appear, that record would exist. It does not. The claim that City staff repeatedly failed 
to respond to his scheduling requests is unsupported by any documented evidence 
presented to us (by VBC Construction) to date.  Please be advised that the City’s 
permitting system (EPL) requires applicants to submit all documentation and

placed a call to  our Administration staff to schedule.  Had Mr. Cesare followed the 
required procedure (or by contacting our admin staff directly), the inspection request 
would appear in the system as an outstanding item. As evidenced by the attached 
record, no such request was submitted. 
 
 
Regarding the homeowner’s decision not to allow VBC Construction onto the 
property after purchase: A licensed contractor’s obligations under City Code… 
 
 Sec. 5-317. - Inspection requests. 
(a) It shall be the duty of the holder of the building permit or of any duly authorized

reminder: VBC construction was the owner of the property during work and prior to 
selling to this new owner.  The contractor remains responsible for ensuring that all 
permitted work is properly inspected and closed out in compliance with code. The 
fact that the home was sold in Spring 2025 with numerous inspections left open, 
resulting in the new owner receiving a structure with uninspected, non–code-
compliant work, is a matter of documented record: See again Attachment 1. and 
constitutes a failure to meet licensee responsibilities, regardless of any contractual 
dispute between the parties. The new owner is not obligated to allow the contractor 
to return to complete work or schedule inspections, especially considering VBC 
Construction was the previous owner and seller. VBC Construction had the 
responsibility to facilitate and close out all required inspections before the sale, 
regardless of any preferences or directions expressed by the then-prospective or 
eventual new owner.

contractor: See Attachment 2. The letter also argues that Mr. Schmidt's informal

If made (Matt S. does not recall this statement), an informal comment by a staff 
member during the course of an ongoing compliance effort is not a formal finding, a 
written determination, or a release of any licensing obligation. It does not preclude 
the Department from considering this conduct as part of a documented pattern of 
violations.

2. False Statements Regarding Licensed Subcontractors - 110 Fall Ridge Lane 
(RES-24-0498) 
The letter takes issue with the Department's use of the words "repeatedly" and "in 
many instances," noting that the Notice of Probation specifically documented one 
instance involving electrical work. The Department acknowledges that the electrical 
work is the most thoroughly documented instance.. The language in the Notice 
reflects the Division’s assessment of the overall pattern of conduct across this 
project. 
 
On the substance: your letter offers a notably convoluted explanation for how Hemel 
Electric LLC came to confirm to the Department that it did not perform the finish 
electrical work. The explanation - that Brad Hemel may have said he was not 
personally on site and therefore may have told the City he did not do the work, when 
in fact Hemel Electric as a company did - is not supported by the facts as the 
Department documented them. When the Department contacted Hemel Electric 
directly on multiple occasions, the response was unambiguous: Hemel Electric did 
not perform the finish electrical work.  
 
Furthermore, the letter's account is directly contradicted by the evidence. The 
Division can present multiple written accounts of verbal confirmation from Hemel 
Electric Ownership (See Attachment 3) confirming that Hemel Electric's involvement 
concluded at rough-in, that Hemel Electric did not perform the finish electrical work, 
and that the finish work subsequently performed by Mr. Cesare without a license 
was initially visibly and materially non-compliant. That statement is simple and will 
speak for itself.  Further to this point, and in lieu of requiring Mr. Cesare to uncover all 
uninspected electrical work, we (including Sean Cesare via conference call) engaged 
Hemel Electric to assist him through the final inspections related to electrical 
systems. Hemel Electric inspected a limited number of outlets and switches by 
removing faceplates and reviewing work they had not performed. After completing 
their review and providing guidance on necessary upgrades to meet code, Hemel 
Electric issued a letter (Attachment 4) confirming that, to the best of their knowledge, 
the work complies with all applicable codes. 
 
More fundamentally, the argument that Mr. Cesare was entitled to perform licensed 
electrical work himself because his subcontractor was temporarily unavailable has 
no basis in City Code, state licensing law, or standard practice. Subcontractor 
unavailability is not an exception to licensing requirements nor a defense to do the 
work without a required city trade license. The only lawful options available to Mr. 
Cesare when his licensed subcontractor could not complete the work were to wait, 
or to hire a different licensed electrician. Performing the work himself without a trade 
license  - and then falsely representing to a code official that a licensed contractor 
had completed the work - is precisely the conduct the licensing framework exists to 
prevent.

3. Repeated Code Violations and Inappropriate Conduct - 110 Fall Ridge Lane 
(RES-24-0498) 
The letter argues that failing inspections multiple times is not, by itself, grounds for 
probation under City Code. The Department agrees that repeated inspection failures 
alone are not the sole basis for probation. The guardrail failures are cited as part of a 
broader documented pattern of conduct; not as an independent standalone basis. 
The probation is grounded in the totality of the pattern described across all three 
sections of the Notice. 
 
Regarding the police report: the letter frames Mr. Cesare's decision to call law 
enforcement on Inspector O'Brien as a protected right exercised on the advice of his 
‘risk management company’. The Department does not dispute that filing a police 
report is a legal right. What the Department documented is the context: a contractor 
who had just failed a guardrail inspection responded by alleging that the inspector 
intentionally damaged his property. That allegation was investigated by the 
Lawrence Police Department, during which Patrick O’Brien was interviewed. The 
investigation determined there was no criminal intent to cause damage, and the case 
was closed. In addition, the Code Official and Deputy Code Official did not find any 
statements from Mr. O’Brien indicating conduct beyond what is considered 
professional. 
 
The letter provides an alternative account of the inspection, alleging that Mr. O’Brien 
struck the wires with his hand, causing the break, and further asserting that his 
conduct was unprofessional. This account contradicts Mr. O’Brien’s description of the 
events. However, the differing accounts are ultimately secondary to the central 
issue: even if the wires had been subjected to this force, the guardrails should not 
have failed. They are required to withstand reasonable impact without breaking and 
are required to be tested as such. 
 
 
4. Definition of "Major Violation" and “Due Process” 
The letter raises two additional arguments: (1) that City Code does not define "major 
violation" or "unethical conduct," and therefore the Department lacked authority to 
impose probation on those grounds; and (2) that VBC Construction was not given a 
warning or opportunity to cure before probation was imposed. 
 
 On the definition question: City Code Section 5-1717 grants the building official 
authority to place a contractor license on probation in the case of "a major 
violation, a series of minor violations, or similar conduct." The conduct 
documented in the Notice of Probation - performing unlicensed electrical 
work, making a false statement to a code official, abandoning a permitted 
project, and filing a retaliatory police report against a city inspector 
performing lawful duties - collectively constitute major violations within the 
plain meaning of that term and within the Division’s authority under the Code.

 On due process and the right to cure: Section 5-1717 does not require the 
Department to issue a warning or provide an opportunity to cure prior to 
imposing probation in the case of major violations. The notice and appeal 
process provided under Section 5-1716 (which VBC Construction is now 
exercising) constitutes the due process afforded under the Code. The 
Department followed that process. 
Sec. 5-1716. - Appeal. 
a) Any licensee aggrieved by the action of the building official in issuing a 
notice of denial or notice and order shall have the right to appeal that 
action to the building code board of appeals. Such appeal shall be taken 
by filing with the department of planning and development services a 
notice of appeal within 14 days of the date of the notice of denial or notice 
and order. The notice of appeal shall be in writing and shall set forth in 
sufficient detail why the licensee believes that the notice of denial or notice 
and order was issued in error or why the penalty is excessive, inappropriate, 
or unreasonable. After the notice of appeal is filed, the building code board 
of appeals shall set a time and place for a public hearing. Notice of 
hearing shall be given to the licensee in the same manner as the notice of 
denial or notice and order. To prevail on appeal, the licensee must prove 
that it is more probably true than not true that the notice of denial or notice 
and order was issued in error or that the penalty is excessive, inappropriate, 
or unreasonable. 
b) There shall be a docketing fee, as provided in the city fee schedule, due 
and payable at the time that any notice of appeal is filed. 
c) Except where there are exigent circumstances, which exigent 
circumstances will be noted in the notice and order, the filing of a notice of 
appeal shall stay all administrative action on the notice and order until the 
appeal is heard and decided by the building code board of appeals. 
d) On appeal, the decision of building code board of appeals shall be a final 
order. Any person aggrieved by a final order of the building code board of 
appeals shall have the right, in accordance with state law, to appeal that 
final order to the district court of the county. 
 
Sec. 5-1717. - Probation. 
 
The building official or, in the case of an appeal from a notice and order, the 
building code board of appeals, shall, in the case of a major violation, a series of 
minor violations, or similar conduct, have the authority to place a contractor 
license on probation. Probation may be conditioned to include a reasonable 
time period to remediate violations, reasonable reporting requirements, the 
completion of continuing education, or other reasonable requirements 
necessary to bring the licensee into compliance with this Code. Failure of a 
licensee to successfully complete any and all conditions of probation shall be 
grounds for suspension or revocation of the contractor license.
