process, which requires compliance with the review criteria listed in Section 20-1108(d) of the Land 
Development Code.  When proposed lots are adjacent to streets  that do not meet the minimum 
right-of-way standar ds listed in Section 20 -1110(e), criteria 4  of Section 20-1108(d) requires 
subdividers to dedicate one -half the additional land necessary to bring the adjacent street into

The property fronts W. 23 rd Street, a principal arterial street, to the north. Section 20-1110(e)(5) 
requires principal arterial streets to have a 150-foot minimum right-of-way width. The adjacent W. 
23rd Street has an existing right-of-way width which varies between 128 and 102 feet. The applicant 
requests a variance (pursuant to  Section 20 -1112(g)) from Section 20 -1110(e)(5)(i) of the 
Subdivision Regulations to maintain W. 23rd Street’s existing right-of-way width and not dedicate 
additional right -of-way. If t he variance were denied , the property owner would be required to

the minimum right -of-way standards. The street previously had 30 feet of its total right-of-way 
width vacated on along the subject property. 
 
REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Criteria 1: Strict application of these regulations will create an unnecessary hardship upon 
the subdivider. 
 
APPLICANT’S RESPONSE: Strict application of the 150-foot right-of-way dedication requirement 
would impose an unnecessary hardship because the existing right -of-way configuration is the 
result of previous public actions, including right-of-way vacations and historical roadway planning 
decisions, rather than circumstances created by the Applicant. Requiring additional dedication 
would create an inconsistent right -of-way condition along a corridor that has already been 
established by the City and would unnecessarily reduce the deve lopable area of the site without 
providing a proportional public benefit.  
 
Furthermore, the requested variance addresses an existing condition that is unique to this 
property. The irregular right-of-way widths cannot be reasonably corrected by the Applicant alone,

As such, strict application of the standard would place an undue burden on the Applicant while 
providing limited practical benefit toward achieving the intended arterial right-of-way width.

respecting the existing public infrastructure and maintaining the intent of the City's transportation 
network. 
 
STAFF FINDING: The purpose of the Subdivision Regulations is to ensure that the division of land 
will serve the public interest and general welfare. The existing right-of-way was established under 
South Hills plat in 1960. The Alabama Street corridor classified as a collector street is all 60 feet in 
width. As such, the property setback and development pattern is well established along the existing 
60-foot right-of-way. The current design standard for right-of-way width was adopted under the 
current Subdivision Regulations  to accommodate for a variety of wider street designs that are 
unlikely to be implemented in existing developed areas, such as along Alabama Street. 
 
Maintaining the existing right-of-way width at this location would in turn maintain the established 
development pattern and setbacks along Alabama Street. Granting a variance from the requirement 
to dedicate additional right -of-way would be in harmony with the intended purpose of the 
regulations given the context of the surrounding subdivision and development pattern.   
 
Criteria 3: The public health, safety, and welfare will be protected. 
 
APPLICANT’S RESPONSE: Approval of the requested variance will not adversely affect the public 
health, safety, or welfare. Alabama Street will continue to function as an existing public roadway 
serving adjacent properties, and the proposed development will comply with all applic able City 
standards related to access management, emergency vehicle access, stormwater management, 
utilities, and pedestrian circulation.  
 
The existing right-of-way provides adequate space for the current roadway and associated public 
infrastructure. Maintaining the established right -of-way width will not reduce the safety or 
operational efficiency of Alabama Street or negatively impact the public's use of the transportation 
system. Rather, approval of the variance will recognize the existing conditions while allowing for 
the orderly redevelopment of the property in a manner consistent with the surrounding urban 
context. 
 
STAFF FINDING: Maintaining the existing right-of-way width was not found to negatively impact 
public health, safety, or welfare. 
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
Staff recommends approving the variance requests to maintain the existing right-of-way widths of 
the portions of W. 23 rd Street and Alabama Street along 1015 W. 23 rd Street, related to PLAT-26-
0052.

re: Naismith Commons, Plat-26-0052, agenda item #1
The 23rd Street corridor is one of the few that spans the entire city from east to 
west.  For most of it, it provides multi-modal features for motor vehicles, transit, 
bicycles, and pedestrians.  West of Iowa St. and east of Learnard Ave. there are 
shared use paths on both the north and south sides of 23rd St.
The 2018 Lawrence Bikes Plan, and the pending 2026 Lawrence Bikes Plan, both call 
for a bikeway to be added from Iowa St. to Learnard Ave.  T o achieve this goal, the 
variance request along 23rd St. should be denied, and require the applicant to 
dedicate one-half the additional land necessary to bring 23rd St. into compliance.
In January 2019, the Proxi Apartment project just west of Naismith Dr. made a similar 
ROW variance request.  It was denied so as to retain enough room for the future 
planned 10 foot wide bikeway.  Officials recognized that, in all likelihood, such a 
bikeway will be on the south side of 23rd St. because there are almost twice as 
many curb cuts on the north side that would create dangerous bicycle-auto conflicts.
Criteria #1:
Applying the regulations will not create unnecessary hardship.  If anything, reduced 
parking would result in a cost saving, whereas the planned bikeway would provide a 
proportional public benefit.
Criteria #3:
Granting the variance would endanger the public health, safety, and welfare.  
According to the 2026 Lawrence Bikes Plan, this very section of 23rd St. is currently 
rated at Level 5 (the worst) for bicycling comfort level, and is flagged as “Vulnerable 
Road User High Injury Network”.  The applicant fails to include “bikeway” in their 
claim that 23rd St has “adequate current public infrastructure”.

There have been multi-year calls for a corridor study of this section of 23rd St., but 
staff has not scheduled it nor funded it.  In lieu of a comprehensive design for this 
corridor, securing ROW for a bikeway relies on either a parcel-by-parcel adjustment, 
or on eminent domain.  Either way, it’s absolutely foolish to cede existing ROW.
Given that Proxi Apartments bit the bullet by the denial of their variance request, 
that precedent will be for naught if this variance request is granted.
Another factor to consider is that some ten years ago, KDOT granted access 
management funds to Lawrence to consolidate adjacent curb cuts along 23rd St.  
The project at this very site would have combined two access points into one, but it 
was never pursued.  Now is the time to realize that option, and reduce the risk to 
bicyclists from turning vehicles.
It is irresponsible for staff to review applications such as this one while wearing 
historical blinders.  Either they are not doing adequate research, or they are 
accepting applicant’s claims at face value.

Dear Planning Commissioners,  
My name is Sheri Hamilton. I have lived in Lawrence for 34 years, and my professional career has 
been closely tied to the University of Kansas and KU Endowment. My family and I have called Old 
West Lawrence home since 2017. My husband, Alex Hamilton, is a lifelong Lawrence resident who 
has spent more than 40 years in the hospitality industry. 
On behalf of both of us, I am writing to express our concerns regarding the proposed rezoning of 
833 Indiana Street from M-3 to M-2 and the potential long-term consequences of granting such an 
exception. 
Old West Lawrence is a distinctive neighborhood known for its historic homes, some dating to the 
early 1860s and surviving Quantrill's Raid. Maintaining these properties requires significant 
dedication and investment, often with little expectation of financial return. Most homes lack 
driveways and rely heavily on street parking. 
The neighborhood sits at the intersection of several active and evolving areas. Downtown is just a 
few blocks to the east, while Pinckney and the Kaw River lie to the north. To the south, the University 
of Kansas campus and the new football stadium development create both opportunities and 
challenges as student life and family life coexist in close proximity. 
When this commission and the City Commission adopted the new zoning districts and map last April, 
significant thought and planning went into those decisions. OWLA and neighborhood residents also 
invested substantial time and energy in reaching consensus around the current R-2 low-density 
zoning. While concerns remained about how ADUs might ultimately be interpreted, limiting ADUs to 
owner-occupied properties was a reasonable and well-considered compromise. 
Based on discussions with City staff and information shared at a recent OWLA meeting, my 
understanding is that the rezoning request for 833 Indiana is intended to allow an Accessory 
Dwelling Unit (ADU) without an on-site owner. The out-of-state investors who recently purchased the 
property are effectively seeking relief from the very standards adopted last year to prevent that type 
of development. 
I understand the property is currently zoned M-3 Mixed Corridor, serving as a transition between 
low-density Old West Lawrence, higher-density Oread student housing, and the commercial uses 
along 9th Street. Approving spot zoning just over a year after adoption of the new map risks 
undermining the planning process and setting a precedent that may be difficult to reverse. It is not 
difficult to envision additional M-2 rezonings along the boundary between the M-3 corridor and R-2 
neighborhoods throughout Old West Lawrence if this request is approved.

immediately. Street parking became noticeably more congested, pushing vehicles farther down the
