Mara Strayer
My proposed design is a rusted stalk of wheat, sitting on a platform, 
rendered in a bold, geometric style. The form is intentionally graphic and 
simplified, while the material gives it a sense of permanence. The weight 
of the sculpture is important to me—not only physically, but 
symbolically. I want each recipient to hold something that feels 
substantial, as though the recognition itself has real presence.

My concept centers on birds in flight painted in the same palette

the awards would be an original painting on an oblong wood 
panel (substantial — roughly ½"–¾" thick wood), depicting 
fanciful — not literal — birds carrying a ribbon reading "2026 
Phoenix Award" against an expansive Kansas sky.

The proposed artwork is located on the exterior of the Midco building
at  644 New Hampshire Street, Lawrence, Kansas 66044 . The
project consists of the ﬁrst phase of a multi-year Indigenous public art

This application requests approval for Phase 1, consisting of two

painted surface and establish the visual foundation for future phases
extending around the building.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THEPROPOSED ARTWORK LOCATION 
The Midco building is a one-story commercial structure located within downtown Lawrence along
New Hampshire Street. The proposed murals are located on existing painted exterior masonry walls
and do not require alterations to the building's structural systems. 
The A1 mural occupies the southernmost section of the east-facing wall overlooking the alley and the
adjacent Native Plant Park. The A2 mural wraps around the southeast corner of the building onto the
south-facing elevation, creating visual continuity between the two primary public viewpoints. 
The proposed mural surfaces currently include: 
Existing painted masonry wall 
Exterior service door(s) 
Downspouts 
Exterior lighting ﬁxtures 
Security cameras 
Electrical and communication equipment mounted to the building 
The mural design anticipates incorporating selected architectural elements (such as doors,
downspouts, and lighting ﬁxtures) into the composition where appropriate while preserving the
functionality of all building systems. Security cameras and other operational equipment will remain
fully functional and will not be obstructed.

The mural will be painted directly onto the existing exterior walls of the Midco building at 644 New Hampshire Street.   
The concrete walls have an  existing layer of exterior primer and should provide a durable surface for a long-term exterior mural. 
Before painting,  we will clean and prepare the walls as needed, including pressure washing.    
The project will use: 
Primer: KILZ All-Purpose Exterior Primer 
Exterior mural paint: Nova Color 
Protective ﬁnish: Nova Color Satin Varnish/Finish

The project will not change the building structure, grading, drainage, sidewalks, landscaping, seating, or the site's normal use. Existing doors, lighting, drainage features,
security equipment, and utilities will remain functional. Any nearby landscaping will be protected during installation. 
The mural is designed to complement the adjacent Native Plant Park and reﬂect the prairie, waterways, plant life, and Indigenous presence connected to this region. No
excavation, tree removal, or permanent environmental disturbance is anticipated. The project will not remove or alter any known historic architectural features.

MONA CLIFF’S RESUME & PREVIOUS WORK 
PROFESSIONAL PROFILE 
Mona Cliff (Hanook-gah-neeh/Spottedcloud) is an enrolled member
of the Gros Ventre Tribe, A’aninin/Nakota Nations, of Fort Belknap,
Montana. She is a Lawrence-based multidisciplinary visual artist and
muralist whose work explores contemporary Indigenous identity,
joy, resilience, cultural continuity, and generational knowledge. Her
practice incorporates mural painting, printmaking, beadwork, fabric
appliqué, installation, community engagement, and traditional
Indigenous making practices. Cliff holds a Bachelor of Fine Arts in
Printmaking from Cornish College of the Arts in Seattle, Washington. 
Her public-art practice frequently connects visual artwork with
workshops, ecological education, Indigenous visibility, and
opportunities for knowledge to be shared across generations. Since
2023, she has completed multiple murals in Lawrence, including
projects at or near Haskell Indian Nations University, as well as
public-art projects elsewhere in Kansas.

RELATIONSHIP TO THE SITE 
Rather than functioning as a separate decorative element, the mural extends the ecological vision of the current Native Prairie Park being 
installed by Native Lands Restoration Collaborative onto the building and creates a uniﬁed relationship between the landscape, architecture, 
and public artwork. 
The imagery will draw  from the prairie, waterways, native plants, and interconnected living systems of this region, while afﬁrming  the
longstanding and continuing presence of Indigenous peoples in and around Lawrence. 
The project also responds to the social character of downtown Lawrence as a shared civic, commercial, and pedestrian space. The A1 mural will
serve as a prominent visual anchor overlooking the Native Plant Park, while A2 will carry the composition around the building’s southern corner.
Together, they will make Indigenous contemporary art visible within the everyday life of downtown and create a welcoming point of connection
for residents, visitors, Midco employees, and people using the park. The broader project is intended to expand paid public-art opportunities and
mentorship for Native artists and emerging Indigenous apprentices. 
The mural is planned as the ﬁrst portion of a larger 2026–2027 project sponsored by Midco that may ultimately extend across additional
building surfaces. A1 and A2 will therefore establish a cohesive visual language that can be continued in later phases while remaining complete
and meaningful as an initial installation. The result will connect ecological restoration, Indigenous cultural continuity, contemporary public art,
and downtown placemaking in a manner speciﬁc to this site.

FUTURE MAINTENANCE 
Midco will be responsible for the ongoing maintenance of the mural a fter installation. Routine m aintenance is expected to be limited and may include
periodic inspection, gentle cleaning, removal of dirt or biological growth, minor paint touch-ups, and renewal of the protective coating as it wears. More
substantial repairs could be needed if the wall develops moisture problems, cracking, peeling paint, vandalism, or damage from building maintenance. 
Anticipated future costs may include: 
Routine inspection and cleaning: approximately $250–$750 every one to three years. 
Minor artist touch-ups: approximately $500–$2,000, depending on the area and whether lift access is required. 
Protective coating renewal: approximately $2,000–$5,000 for A1 and A2, likely every ﬁve to ten years depending on the product used and 
 weathering. 
Larger restoration or damage repair: cost to be determined based on the extent of damage, wall preparation required, equipment access, and artist 
 labor. 
These ﬁgures are planning allowances rather than ﬁrm quotes. Actual frequency and cost will depend on the selected paint and protective-ﬁnish
system, exposure to weather, wall condition, and access requirements. 
Consistent with the LCAC owner–artist agreement, Midco should contact Mona Cliff before undertaking any repair, restoration, repainting, alteration, or
removal that could affect the appearance or artistic integrity of the mural. Mona should be offered the opportunity to complete the work under a
separately negotiated, paid agreement covering her time, materials, travel, equipment, and any assistants needed. 
When Mona is unavailable or declines the work, Midco may retain another qualiﬁed professional only after receiving Mona’s written agreement
regarding the proposed treatment and the person who will perform it. Emergency work necessary to address an immediate safety or building-integrity
concern may proceed as needed, but Midco should make reasonable efforts to notify Mona in advance and minimize changes to the artwork.
