2026 Lawrence Bikes Plan 
The Lawrence Bikes plan is a guide to achieve the vision of “A bikeway 
network that supports safe and comfortable riding for all.”  The Bike Plan 
Update Steering Committee played a central role in shaping the plan’s 
direction and final recommendations. The Steering Committee met over 
the last 18 months to review public input, existing conditions data, 
evaluate bikeway network options, and develop strategies across 
Engineering, Education, Encouragement, Enforcement, and Evaluation. 
Public tabling Engagement included three open houses & surveys, 
events, and focus groups. A public comment period was held June 17 
through July 1. 
  
Desired Action: Recommend approval of the 2026 Lawrence Bikes Plan 
to replace the 2019 Lawrence Bikes Plan.

C. PUBLIC COMMENT: 
Lisa Hallberg and Cynthia Courtney provided public comment regarding the proposed 
Lawrence Loves Parks Foundation, a grassroots community organization focused on 
supporting Lawrence parks, recreation facilities, and green spaces. 
Hallberg shared that the group has been meeting since February to explore the 
creation of a community foundation that could support park improvements, 
community-led initiatives, and capital projects. The group has developed draft bylaws 
and standing rules, engaged with community organizations, and is exploring 
establishing a nonprofit organization in partnership with the Douglas County 
Community Foundation. 
Courtney shared support for the effort and discussed the potential benefits of bringing 
together community members, organizations, and resources to support parks and 
recreation services. She expressed appreciation for Parks, Recreation and Culture 
staff and the challenges of providing services with limited resources.

Chip Blaser, Executive Director of the Douglas County Community Foundation, 
presented information on how the foundation operates and how existing Parks, 
Recreation and Culture-related funds are structured. 
Blaser explained the purpose of endowed and designated funds, donor-
directed gifts, and the process for managing and accessing funds. He reviewed 
the current Parks, Recreation and Culture-related accounts, including the 
general endowment fund, the Nalbandian fund supporting recreation access, 
and the Municipal Stadium project fund. 
Board members asked questions regarding: 
 How donors may designate the use of funds.  
 The process for requesting funds and communication between the 
foundation and Parks, Recreation and Culture staff.  
 How endowed funds grow and how annual distributions are determined.  
 Examples of previous donations supporting specific programs or 
projects.  
 How a potential Parks foundation could complement existing funding 
resources.  
Staff and Blaser discussed how a future foundation could work alongside 
existing resources while maintaining coordination with the department and City.

Board member Nalbandian provided an update on the North Lawrence Corridor 
Study. The consultant team continues to focus on community engagement and 
gathering resident input. Three primary themes have emerged:  
 Transportation and infrastructure: Addressing access, flooding, and 
connectivity issues, particularly along Second Street.  
 Placemaking: Exploring ways to enhance the corridor as a welcoming 
area and improve connections to parks and public spaces.  
 Economic revitalization: Considering future development opportunities 
while balancing the history, culture, and character of North Lawrence. 
   
The Board discussed potential opportunities related to parks, recreation, river 
access, and possible reuse of existing spaces.

Sports Field Scheduling 
Hart provided follow-up regarding youth sports field scheduling. She explained 
that field assignments are completed weekly due to changing game schedules, 
weather impacts, and field-specific needs. Teams participating in City leagues 
receive an earlier reservation window than the general public to assist with 
scheduling. 
  
Playground Accessibility Update 
Steinbrock provided an update on playground accessibility within the park 
system for Hecker, who was not in attendance. Lawrence currently has 33 
playgrounds: 
 19 playgrounds are fully accessible with ADA-compliant sidewalks, 
accessible surfacing, and accessible play amenities.  
 9 playgrounds have ADA-compliant sidewalks and accessible amenities 
but use wood chip surfacing.  
 5 playgrounds currently lack required accessibility features.  
The Board discussed the importance of continuing to improve accessibility 
beyond minimum ADA requirements and asked for additional information 
regarding accessible amenities and future improvements. 
  
Other discussion 
Bussinger asked about spraying occurring near the levee area and concerns 
from residents regarding notification, timing, and impacts on pets, saying signs 
were posted the same day as the spraying.  Hart will have Hecker provide 
information regarding current notification procedures and provide additional 
information on the spraying process. 
  
The Board discussed the proposed Lawrence Loves Parks Foundation and 
expressed interest in learning more about how the organization could 
complement existing Parks, Recreation and Culture funding resources. 
  
The Board discussed inviting additional presentations from other organizations, 
including the Lawrence Public Library Foundation, to better understand 
different foundation structures and partnerships.

Culture 
Arneill highlighted the impact of World Cup-related arts and culture activities 
throughout Lawrence. Events included public art, music performances, 
community celebrations, and watch parties. 
Arneill also recognized the Lawrence Music Alliance and local artists who 
contributed to World Cup programming. The Board discussed how the

Recreation/Director 
Hart provided updates on summer programming and recent recreation 
activities: 
 South Park Summer Camp has served 94 unique participants.  
 Prairie Park Nature Center Camp reached full capacity with 45 
participants each week.  
 Unified Recreation is launching a new intuitive art class.  
 A walking soccer program was held at Sports Pavilion Lawrence with 
more than 30 participants.  
 Staff created daily programming throughout July in recognition of 
National Parks and Recreation Month and World Cup activities.  
 The Algerian soccer team borrowed a PRC ping pong table during their 
stay in Lawrence and signed the table, which staff plans to display.  
 Adult swim events continue to be successful, with the June event 
attracting 78 participants.  
 Tournament activity is returning to Sports Pavilion Lawrence following 
World Cup-related scheduling.  
 New cardio equipment has been installed at Sports Pavilion Lawrence.  
 July included 16 third-party camp offerings, including specialty camps.  
 Adult sports leagues are underway with 163 teams participating.  
 The Kansas City Royals will host a youth baseball and softball clinic at 
the Youth Sports Complex with 180 participants.
