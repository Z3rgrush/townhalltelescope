Executive Summary  
 Section 20-1213(d) of the Land Development Code requires all off-street parking areas and 
driveways shall be surfaced with one of the listed options in Section 20-1213(d)(1), or a 
permitted alternative surfacing material listed in Section 20-1213(d)(2).

materials to permit a non-listed surfacing material, loose rock and gravel. The property is 
platted Lot 1, Block A of Southwest Addition No. Two (book 4/page 10). The property is 
zoned R-2 (Residential Low Density 2) District and is encumbered in the FEMA mapped 
regulatory floodplain (Zone AE – 1% annual chance of a flooding event). The properties to 
the north, south, east, and west are zoned R-2 (Residential Low Density 2) District.  
  
Fiscal Impact  
 There is no fiscal impact to the City.  
  
Action Requested

loose rock and gravel, in the R-2 (Residential Low Density 2) District based upon the findings 
in the staff report concluding that the request does meet the conditions outlined in Sections 
20-1604(f)(7)(A) of the Land Development Code.

2. That the granting of the variance will not adversely affect the rights of adjacent property 
owners or residents. 
 
Applicant response – “There is an important distinction between the crushed limestone gravel used in 
typical parking lots and the decorative Kansas River Rock we used.  
According to the Kansas Geological Survey, Kansas River Rock is Hard and Durable. Kansas River 
gravel and rock consist largely of tough quartz, granite, and chert (flint). Because these materials 
were washed down from the Rockies over millions of years and tumbled smooth, they score very high 
on the Mohs hardness scale (quartz alone scores a 7). They do not break down, weather, or dissolve 
easily.  
The typical crushed Kansas Limestone found in most parking areas is softer. Much of the local 
limestone found in the Kansas City area and eastern Kansas is a sedimentary rock composed of 
ancient skeletal fragments and calcite. It typically only scores around a 3 on the Mohs scale, meaning 
it is relatively soft, porous, and highly susceptible to erosion and acidic weathering. 
While river rock is much harder, crushed limestone is often preferred for driveways and concrete 
because its angular edges lock together tightly when compacted, whereas rounded river rock tends 
to shift and roll. 
Based in part on this knowledge we provided a new concrete driveway area where most wheel turning 
occurs and limited the decorative rock to the areas where cars sit. The decorative rock is bound on 
the sides by either concrete driveway or landscape timbers. These limit rolling and displacement. And 
the decorative rock reduces the amount of impervious surface immediately adjacent to the flood plain 
and the section of concrete driveway we were required remove. Together this works well and provides 
an appealing aesthetic.  
At the time of this writing, Pine Landscape Center was selling washed limestone ¾” and  1 ½” @ 
$70/ton and Kansas River Rock 1 ½” @ $121ton. Another reason folks typically choose the limestone. 
The neighbors have complimented us on the design and installation.”.  
 
In staff’s opinion, the requested variance would not adversely affect the rights of adjacent property 
owners or residents. Approval of the requested variance to permit a non-listed surfacing material, 
loose rock and gravel in the R-2 (Residential Low Density 2) District would not restrict or impact the 
rights of adjacent property owners to continue using their properties. 
 
Per Sections 20-1603(g) and 20-1604(f)(4) of the Land Development Code, newspaper and mailed 
notice, via letters dated 07/13/2026, were provided to property owners within 400 feet of the subject 
property informing them of the application filed by the applicant. At the time of this writing, staff has 
not received any phone call, written, or in-person communications on this item. Staff has received 
email communications from neighbors on this item, see Communications.

4. That the variance desired will not adversely affect the public health, safety, morals, order, 
convenience, prosperity, or general welfare. 
 
Applicant response - “We agree a typical crushed limestone gravel parking lot does represent a 
danger and nuisance to the residents of Lawrence. Given the likelihood the smaller, jagged edge 
gravel lodges in a tire tread, get transported to a public street where the gravel can dislodge, become 
a dangerous missile or occlude the storm water drainage system, the prohibition on this type of 
gravel makes sense. Given limestone deteriorates and produces dust, chalk and mud, the prohibition 
makes even more sense.  
With the harder, larger, rounded rock we are proposing the likelihood of the migration of the rock and 
subsequent danger and nuisance is greatly reduced. Same with the fact the Kanas River Rock does 
not weather/deteriorate like the limestone and does not produce dust, chalk or mud.  Additionally, 
the concrete driveway and the stretch of pervious pavers create a buffer tires must travers before 
they reach the public right of way. This further reduces the chance any rock would migrate and 
subsequently become a danger or nuisance. 
A concrete parking lot creates a significantly larger urban heat island effect than a gravel parking 
lot. While both materials absorb and radiate heat, concrete typically has a higher heat storage 
capacity and conductivity, whereas gravel allows for natural cooling through evaporation and 
airflow. 
Gravel allows water to seep into the ground. As this moisture evaporates, it provides a natural cooling 
effect that prevents the ground from heating up as drastically. Concrete is completely sealed,

5. That granting the variance desired will not be opposed to the general spirit and intent of 
this chapter. 
 
Applicant response – “We do not dispute that the general prohibition of certain gravel driveways and 
parking areas upholds the spirit and intent of the Development Code. It is our contention that a more 
nuanced application of the regulations can uphold the same spirit and intent while providing a greater 
range of acceptable options. With a broader range of acceptable alternatives, the community can find 
ways to move the city towards a greener and more aesthetically pleasing uncompromised future. 
We respectfully ask for approval to use 1 ½” Kansas River Rock at the defined and separated portion 
of the parking area where cars are parked.”.  
 
In staff’s opinion, granting the variances would be opposed to the general spirit and intent of the Land 
Development Code. Granting the requested variance would permit the loose rock and gravel parking 
area to remain at the site.  
 
Off-street parking located on an improved surface has been a requirement of the Land Development 
Code dating back to 1979. (Code 1979, 20-904; Ord. No. 5177, Sec. I; Ord. No. 5658, Sec. I; Ord. No. 5791) 
New residential developments located within city limits have been required to install improved 
surfaces.  
 
Per Section 20-1217 of the 1966 Land Development code, parking areas were subject to the following: 
 
(a) All off-street parking areas, including single-family and duplex residential driveways, 
shall be surfaced with a minimum of: 
(1) Four (4) inches of reinforced Portland cement concrete; 
(2) Five (5) inches of granular rock base with two (2) inches of asphaltic concrete; 
(3) Seven (7) inches of granular rock base with a double asphaltic prime and sealer; 
or,  
(4) Five (5) inches of full depth asphaltic concrete.  
(b) As an alternative to subsection (a), all off-street parking for uses allowed by right 
within residential districts or areas of low off-street parking use as determined by 
the City Engineer (Such as fire safety lanes or overflow parking areas, may be 
surfaced with following alternative methods of paving: 
(1) Grid unit paver with grass installed per the manufacturer’s recommendations 
with the pavement and base designed by a licensed professional engineer in the 
State of Kansas. The pavement cross-sections shall demonstration the structural

The intent of parking area surfacing regulations as expressed by Sections 20-1213(d) of the 2024 Land 
Development Code is to ensure parking areas (driveways) are constructed in a manner that prevents 
wear-and-tear to public improvements (streets, sidewalks, bike lanes) and maintains orderly property 
maintenance. The existing loose rock and gravel parking area at the site does not satisfy the above 
stated goals for parking areas (driveways).  
 
Conclusion: 
 
Staff’s analysis of this variance application finds the request does not meet all conditions set forth in 
Sections 20-1604(f)(7)(A) of the Land Development Code that the Board must find existing to grant a 
variance. 
 
Recommendation: 
 
Staff recommends denial of the requested variance to permit a non-listed surfacing material, loose 
rock and gravel, in the R-2 (Residential Low Density 2) District based upon the findings in the staff 
report concluding that the request does meet the conditions outlined in Sections 20-1604(f)(7)(A) of 
the Land Development Code.
