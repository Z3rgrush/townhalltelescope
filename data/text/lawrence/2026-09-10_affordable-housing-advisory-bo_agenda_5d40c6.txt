 Report 8/6/26 
Members of the AHAB exploring municipal land for affordable housing met with Jeff Crick, Director of 
Planning and Development Services, Danelle Walters, Assistant Director PDS and Mark Hecker, Assistant 
Director Parks and Recreation.  As a group reviewed and discussed the six sites that the subcommittee 
previously identified as potential building sites for affordable housing.  The subcommittee also asked city 
staff if there were any other sites that should be considered.   
Members are recommending further study of the first two opportunities listed below.  We have included a 
brief description and a few comments about each parcel for consideration.

Oak Hill Cemetery Parcel:

of those lots on the east side of this parcel.  However, with a short extension of Prairie Ave, Prospect Ave,

directly north is also owned by the city and provides further opportunities for development of housing if

detail.

Chair Soukup and Board Members:
Our neighborhood association, as well as residents, consider the Brook Creek Woods (aka 
The Nature Area, The Woods) to be a treasure, similar to the Lawrence Nature Park, the 
Prairie Park Prairie Parcel, and the Haskell-Baker Wetlands.
For decades, we have not only mounted a defense against encroachments into The 
Woods, but also have proactively proposed collaboration with the City to preserve them.  
The attached documents will provide a glimpse of our extensive efforts.
We don’t view The Woods as a commodity stockpiled by the City, as mere real estate to 
be exploited, or as planning documents would commonly call them “vacant”.
Indeed, The Woods are, in their highest and best use, a riverine ecozone or wet 
woodland, providing wildlife habitat as well as stress-reducing relaxation for urban 
dwellers.  We would argue that if the richest man in Lawrence can have the Lawrence 
Nature Park as his back yard, we are entitled to have the Nature Area as ours.
Indicative of our depth of commitment to The Woods is our proposal from 11 August last 
month to expand The Woods.  Since MSO is moving their Public Works activities from 
11th and Haskell to their new campus at 19th and O’Connell, we asked that MSO and 
Parks, Recreation, and Culture follow through with their frequent promises over the years 
to convert the 11th and Haskell site to more parkland.
In conjunction with The Brook Creek Woods, the original nature of the 11th and Haskell 
site was either wetlands, wet prairie, or wet woodlands, or a combination.  It’s also the 
convergence of the Burroughs Creek watershed and the Brook Creek watershed.  Being 
100 year floodplain, stream corridor, and native woodland, it qualifies as “sensitive land” 
under the Plan 2040 Comprehensive Plan.
We want the Brook Creek Woods to be preserved intact, and the 11th and Haskell site to 
be ecologically restored as part of a larger Brook Creek Woods.  We recommend that the 
Affordable Housing Advisory Board cease consideration of taking some nine acres of the 
Brook Creek Woods for housing.

Brook Creek Nature Area Preservation and Management Plan
Proposal for a Memorandum of Understanding Between the Brook Creek Neighborhood
Association and the Lawrence Parks and Recreation Department
Introduction:
The 43 acres of natural woods that constitute the northern portion of Brook Creek Park have been 
considered a nature preserve by the neighborhood residents for two decades or more.  These natural 
woods are an ecological preserve, a buffer of the neighborhood from disturbances by the BNSF 
Railroad and the City Public Works operations, and have historical origins in the Woodland Park 
amusement park from 1910 to approximately 1930.  Approximately half of these natural woods 
are within the FEMA 100 year floodway, and a smaller portion are in the FEMA 100 year 
floodplain.
The City of Lawrence purchased the property from Mr. Park Hetzel, Jr. in 1996 for use as 
parkland.  Fred DeVictor, the Director of Parks and Recreation from 1975 to 2007, made a 1996 
agreement with then Brook Creek Neighborhood Association President, Mark Taylor, to keep the 
woods natural and not formally developed.  Mr. DeVictor later said “The intent was to keep it 
natural, which is what the neighbors wanted.  It’s a beautiful woodland.” (quote, Fred DeVictor, 
Lawrence Journal-World).
On at least five occasions, the Brook Creek Neighborhood Association (BCNA) has reaffirmed 
this intent.  The first was at the BCNA meeting of May 1998, and the second was at the December
2012 BCNA meeting, attended by Parks & Recreation staff.  A February 2013 letter to the City 
Commission was the third statement, requesting Parks and Recreation to create a woodland 
management plan covering uses and amenities of the Nature Area.  Fourthly, in November 2015, 
BCNA wrote a letter to the interim Director of Parks and Recreation, again asking for a 
management plan.  And at the April 2016 BCNA meeting, Assistant Director of Parks and 
Recreation, Mark Hecker, agreed to enter into discussions about a management plan.
In recent years, these natural woods have suffered encroachment by the City Public Works 
operations, pollution from illegal dumping, unannounced brush hog trail cutting by the City Parks 
and Recreation Department, and random presence of illegal rifle game hunting, among other 
disturbances.  For BCNA, it is important what steps are taken, as well as how any corrective 
measures are conducted, to deal with situations like these.  Therefore, BCNA is proposing the 
following principles for inclusion in any Nature Area Management Plan.

Public record:
The 43 acres of woods that act as a buffer between the northernmost residences of Brook Creek 
Neighborhood and the Public Works Yard and the BNSF railroad tracks were the site of Woodland Park, 
an amusement park started in 1910.  Originally owned by the land developer, N.P. Dodge, who first 
developed the residential area north of 15th St., it was purchased by the Lawrence Amusement Company 
who built a roller coaster, ferris wheel, dance parlor, and other attractions. 
At some time in the late 1920's or early 1930's, the amusement attractions fell into disuse and disrepair.  
The land was offered to the City of Lawrence for free, under the condition that it remain a park.  The City 
turned down the offer because they had ideas to make use of it for industrial purposes.  It's unclear how 
long the stalemate went on, but by the 1950's the natural growth of trees were becoming reestablished, and 
by the 1970's, some houses were being built on part of the land.
The owner at that time was Park Hetzel Jr., a longtime independent crane operator and house mover.  He 
owned most of the land east of Haskell Ave. on the north and south side of 11th St. (except for the City 
Public Works Yard) including the now wooded nature area.  This is when the Brook Creek Neighborhood 
Association (BCNA) President, Mark Taylor, precipitated the City buying the Nature Area.  Douglas 
County had plans to build the new County Jail on the site, but Mark led the fight to stop that.  The City 
then bought the land, and the Director of Parks & Recreation, Fred DeVictor, said “The intent was to keep 
it natural, which is what the neighbors wanted.  It's a beautiful woodland”.  His quote is near the end of 
this Journal World article - http://www2.ljworld.com/news/2010/mar/28/daisy-dozers-day-lore-
surrounding-bygone-roller-co/. 
BCNA record:
The history of the intent to preserve the Brook Creek Woods as “The Nature Area” appears in several 
BCNA documents.  Mark Taylor's recollections that appear in the April-May-July 2009 Brook Creek 
Breeze include the following: 
“The jail would have been directly to the east [of his house] and would have taken up most of what 
we consider the Nature Area”.  He further relates “The land under consideration was privately 
owned at the time.  The City purchased the land for a park”.  The article concludes “Mark described 
taking many walks through what is now the Nature Park with Fred DeVictor, then head of the City's 
Parks & Recreation Department, who decided that the City would keep the park in its natural state 
and not put in things like asphalt trails”.
The next reference appears in the September 1998 Brook Creek Breeze, within the minutes of the 12 May 
1998 BCNA meeting.  It reads 
“John Sullivan presented a proposal to create a Langston Hughes Memorial Trail in the park, 
including color blazes on the trees . . . General consensus was no objection to the [trail] markers, but 
trails should not be cleared or widened . . . no benches or rest points allowed . . . and the agreement 
with the City must be in writing”.
The City didn't put forth a written agreement still, and BCNA failed to follow through and push the issue.  
Then problems arose in 2012 when the Parks & Rec Supervisor took it upon himself to strike a deal with 
the K.U. Running Club (without informing or consulting with BCNA) to cut wide trails through “The

woods, and got him to stop.  He agreed to meet with the neighbors to explain what he was up to.  A 
number of us met with him on 19 November 2012, following which he attended the next BCNA meeting.

3. The goal of this agreement is that the Nature Area be preserved and maintained according to 
all conditions as enumerated in this Nature Area Management Plan.
4. It is the desire of the Brook Creek Neighborhood Association: 
A. That this Nature Area Management Plan follow industry best practices of stormwater 
management for Burroughs Creek and Brook Creek to maintain natural stream contours, 
remove all blockages, and minimize flooding.
B. That “improved” infrastructures not be placed in the Nature Area, including but not 
limited to: area lighting, paved trails, picnic shelters, grills, water service and fountains, 
rest rooms, play equipment, BMX bicycle tracks, streets, motor vehicle parking, seating 
except for intermittent rustic benches, and signage, if not done in consultation with, and by
the agreement of, the  Brook Creek Neighborhood Association, or it’s successor agency.
C. That adjacent activities not be expanded so as to encroach, either physically or by light 
pollution, into The Nature Area, including but not limited to: Public Works operations, 
Utilities operations, Oak Hill Cemetery or other cemeteries, and residences, businesses, or 
other structures, if not done in consultation with, and by the agreement of, the Brook 
Creek Neighborhood Association, or it’s successor agency.
D. That natural footpaths be maintained by City personnel for safety and erosion control, 
with routine vegetation removal being done mechanically but not chemically, no more
