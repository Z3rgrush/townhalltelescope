Re: Regulatory Considerations for Battery Energy Storage Systems 
INTRODUCTION 
On July 31, 2024, the Board of County Commissioners initiated a text amendment to 
address emerging land uses, including Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS), in the 
Unincorporated Area. The County’s zoning code currently permits BESS only when 
accessory to a utility-scale solar installation and has limited regulations. Since the text 
amendment has been initiated, County staff has been actively engaged in researching 
BESS to ensure the most well-informed regulations possible are presented to the 
Planning Commission and the Board of County Commissioners.  Specifically, staff have 
used resources from the American Planning Association, conferred with planners in 
peer communities, studied regulations, reviewed packet materials for submitted projects 
elsewhere, been monitoring relevant technological developments, and met with industry 
experts.    
 
The purpose of this document is to serve as an outline for discussion among Planning 
Commissioners. The background is informed by staff research, the layout of the 
County’s zoning code, and safety standards. 
 
What is a BESS? 
Battery Energy Storage Systems are arrays of batteries used to store electricity for later 
use. They can range in scale from small in-home/office systems to utility-scale projects 
that can be used to power entire cities. Utility-scale systems are typically housed in 
shipping container-like structures and are often associated with substations, wind/solar 
installations, other power generation facilities, and industrial land uses. The batteries 
can take energy from the power generation source or the electrical grid and supply the 
energy later when needed, such as during a peak time.

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK 
DEFINITIONS, APPLICABILITY, AND INTENT 
This section would divide BESS into at least two tiers. Based on the framework of 
existing regulations on wind and solar installations, these tiers would likely be based on 
a “small and large” scale like in the wind regulations or a “personal, limited, and 
commercial/utility” scale like in the solar regulations. The threshold of the tier would be 
based on energy capacity in kilowatt-hours. The lower tier would require minimal 
permitting and would likely not require a public hearing, while the larger tier may require 
a conditional use permit, rezoning, or both. These are also referred to as “BTM” 
systems, or “behind the meter.” The regulations would only apply to larger-scale “FTM” 
or “in front-of-the-meter” utility/commercial-scale systems. 
 
Existing regulatory framework: 
In the wind regulations, small-scale wind energy conversion systems are defined as 
producing up to 50 kW. Both wind and solar regulations emphasize that the smaller tier 
is for on-site or personal use. For smaller scale, in-home BESS, a building permit is 
required, but there is no additional zoning permission necessary. 
 
Thresholds from peer communities: 
- Allen County, Kansas and Pratt County, Kansas have three tiers that separate at 
80 kWh and 600 kWh.1 
- American Clean Power’s model ordinance: 4,000 kWh2  
- Saline County, Kansas: 1,000 kW3 
- Solano County, California: 1,000 kWh4 
 
KEY DISCUSSION QUESTION: 
1. What should be the threshold for dividing between a smaller and larger scale 
BESS?

Existing Regulatory Framework: 
In the wind and solar regulations, these considerations include visual impact, noise

Douglas County has an existing requirement that limits sound generated to 60 dBA. Per 
Section 12-306-49.04(n)(1) of the Zoning Code, “The operational noise generated from 
… battery energy storage systems, components, and associated ancillary equipment 
shall not exceed a noise level of 60 decibels (60 dBA) as measured at the property line 
or 500 feet from an existing residence.” 60 dBA is comparable to a normal conversation 
or office environment.5 
 
Additional examples from peer communities: 
- Johnson County, Kansas has locational criteria that apply to BESS:  
 “Due to their potentially combustible nature and possible large footprint, the 
siting of Battery Energy Storage Facilities (BESF) shall endeavor to: 1) locate the 
BESF in nonresidential areas, 2) avoid locating the BESF in areas used by the 
public (e.g. parkland), 3) buffer the BESF from the surrounding areas by siting 
toward the interior of the parcel and through the use of greater parcel sizes and 
setbacks, 4) take advantage of existing topography, structures and vegetation to 
provide extra screening, 5) locate and design the BESF so that it mitigates the 
potential detrimental impacts to the general health, safety and welfare of the 
community, 6) locate the BESF in areas where the potential adverse impact on 
the community is minimal, and 7) design and configure the BESF in a way that 
minimizes adverse impacts such as views, noise, vibration and the like.”6 
- Saline County, Kansas has a requirement that the project be sited to ensure the 
viability of agriculture in the immediate vicinity, minimize impacts to neighboring 
properties and the environment, and allow for decommissioning.7 Saline County 
also requires projects to be sited to accommodate the growth of cities, limit 
developing on slopes, and make use of brownfield sites.8 Saline County has a 
permitted noise range of 60-80 dB within 100 feet and 60 dB at neighboring 
properties.9

DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS 
This section lists specific standards that would need to be satisfied. This can include 
limits on size, location, height, land disturbance, setbacks, screening, lighting, noise, 
and other requirements. 
 
Existing Regulatory Framework: 
The existing BESS regulations within the solar regulations require: 
- Compliance with NFPA 855 and all other local, state, and federal regulations; 
- Enclosure by a fence with a self-locking gate; 
- The area within 10 feet of the BESS cleared of vegetation and covered by non-
combustible surfacing; 
- Posting warning signage, and; 
- A setback of 500 feet from an existing residence. 
In the solar regulations, there is a provision for “participating” and “non-participating” 
landowners. It allows for participating landowners to waive certain buffer and screening 
requirements.  
 
Additional examples from peer communities: 
- American Clean Power’s model ordinance requires the system to comply with 
NFPA standards. It also allows for reduced setbacks if an engineered solution is 
presented.11  
- Johnson County, Kansas requires battery energy storage systems and other 
components of a solar facility to “be screened when visible from any road, 
Residential District, Planned Residential District, Planned Rural District, and any 
dwelling located outside of the Project Area.”12 Johnson County also requires a 
150-foot setback from the project boundary or street line.13 
- Solano County, California requires a 300-foot setback from schools, hospitals, 
and those with increased sensitivity to air pollution. Solano County also requires 
a 30-foot-wide fire rated access road around the entire system inside a security

APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS 
This section will outline the specific materials that will need to be submitted with a 
development application. This can include specific additional plans or maps beyond 
those generally required to be submitted with a Conditional Use Permit, Site Plan, or 
Building Permit application. This may include public outreach plans, landscaping or 
screening plans, construction impact assessments, stormwater management plans, 
traffic and road maintenance plans, emergency response plans, decommissioning and 
restoration plans, and bond requirements. Most importantly, this section would require 
the application to include documentation that any proposed project comply with 
applicable safety standards established by the National Fire Protection Association, UL, 
Fire Code, and Building Code. 
 
Staff have noted that in other communities, there may be a requirement that certain 
technical aspects of the application and required materials be reviewed by third-party 
qualified professionals at the applicant’s expense.17 Douglas County’s existing 
regulations for solar facilities, for example, require certain reviews be conducted by a 
County-selected third-party at the applicant’s expense. 
 
Existing regulatory framework: 
Existing regulations for wind and solar require one-mile notification, exceeding the 
requirement of ½ mile for typical Conditional Use Permits. Existing BESS regulations 
require compliance with NFPA 855.

PERMITTED USE TABLE 
The Permitted Use Table is located toward the beginning of the County’s zoning code 
and it lists the uses allowed in the unincorporated area and in which zoning districts 
they are considered as permitted, conditional, accessory, or not permitted uses.

systems, as standards are listed for them there. Battery Energy Storage Systems are 
often associated with solar and wind installations, substations, other generation
