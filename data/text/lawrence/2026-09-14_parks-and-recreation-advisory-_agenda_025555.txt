 1. Presentation of the 2026 Lawrence Bikes Plan - Bailey Waters/ Stephen Mason 
2026 Lawrence Bikes Plan 
The Lawrence Bikes plan is a guide to achieve the vision of “A bikeway 
network that supports safe and comfortable riding for all.”  The Bike Plan 
Update Steering Committee played a central role in shaping the plan’s direction 
and final recommendations. The Steering Committee met over the last 18 
months to review public input, existing conditions data, evaluate bikeway 
network options, and develop strategies across Engineering, Education, 
Encouragement, Enforcement, and Evaluation. Public Engagement included 
three open houses & surveys, tabling events, and focus groups. A public 
comment period was held June 17 through July 1.

Lawrence Bike Plan 2026 DRAFT 
  
 
Stephen Mason with the Lawrence-Douglas County Metropolitan Planning 
Organization and Bailey Waters, City Engineer, presented the draft 2026 
Lawrence Bikes Plan. The plan establishes goals and strategies for improving 
bicycling in Lawrence, including safety, increased ridership and access, 
development of a low-stress bikeway network, and maintaining the city’s 
bicycle-friendly designation. 
  
The presentation covered proposed bikeway improvements, funding priorities, 
the Lawrence Loop, education and enforcement efforts, and the use of a 
revised level-of-comfort system for evaluating bicycle facilities. The Board 
discussed how implementation and funding would be coordinated among City 
departments and community partners. Members also discussed the need to 
engage non-sport cyclists, Public Health, schools, and other community 
organizations, as well as the possibility of having a staff position or ongoing 
group dedicated to coordinating bicycle-related efforts. 
  
The Board recommended approval of the 2026 Lawrence Bikes Plan by the 
City Commission.

Parks Report 
Staff reported that the Indoor Aquatic Center was closed for annual 
maintenance and would reopen Sunday. Work included draining and cleaning 
the pools, inspecting equipment, and replacing flooring in the family changing 
rooms. 
  
Mowing has been challenging due to the combination of heat and frequent rain,

continues to work through the mowing cycle.

and other conditions. Staff also began a multi-year effort to address newly

September 7. The project will include replacement of the pool deck and 
portions of the pool, new slides, and other improvements, with completion 
anticipated before the 2027 pool season.

Complex, including construction of additional access from the west and south 
sides of the complex. The project has been bid and is expected to begin within

Background
The City of Lawrence has a legacy and tradition of live band music almost from its founding. In
the pioneer days, militia bands and small musical groups entertained citizens along the river, in
the Turnhalle building and in open air concerts at various locations in and around Lawrence.
South Park is the present location and home to the Lawrence City Band summer concert series.
The gazebo band stand was erected in the early 19005 and for over 100 years various iterations
of our municipal band have made South Park their home. The night before the infamous
Quantrill’s raid on Lawrence a small band of men gave a band concert on the banks of the
Kansas River. Some of them were killed the next day.
Organizational Structure
The Lawrence City Band is a not—for—profitorganization that has historically been led by local
musicians passionate about high quality band music and keeping the nostalgic feeling of open
air music alive in our area. Longtime leaders included BillKelley (for whom the gazebo is
named) and Bob Foster, longtime director of bands at KU.A volunteer-led board of directors
ensures that our legacy continues, providing guidance for our eight week summer concert
series and an annual holiday concert.
The makeup of the LCBis selected by a personnel director to assure that we have top-tier local
musicians. Many ofthe musicians are KUfaculty (active and retired) and area music educators,
all of whom are talented and share a passion for band music.
Our Audience
Lawrence City Band summer concerts in South Park are a slice of Americana. Young and old
patrons from every walk of life enjoy the free concerts each Wednesday evening from 8—9pm
during the summer concert series. Vans full of residents from Pioneer Ridge, Brandon Woods
and other retirement communities look forward to sharing a pleasant evening in this
community setting. Families with children take a break from their screen time and others
simply take a breather from stresses of life to enjoy the egalitarian mix of fellow citizens in a
relaxed outdoor setting. Downtown businesses appreciate the additional pre—concert and post—
concert activity. The band has recently included a Holiday Concert at the Lawrence Public
organization LBC local regional visitors on a consistentbasis.

Financial Support
The City of Lawrence’s Department of Parks, Recreation & Culture has generously provided
annual support and the use of South Park for many years, and we are very grateful for that
ongoing support. The Rice Foundation is our other major source of annual revenue to sustain
the organization. The LCBhas a partnership with KU Bands (providing rehearsal space, sound
equipment and some instruments) and the LiedCenter (our inclement weather venue).
Minuteman Press has provided free programs for many years. We employ sound technicians
and a music librarian for the eight week concert series and band members are paid a small
stipend for each concert played. As an organization, we have historically managed and
leveraged relatively modest resources to sustain a top—notchcommunity asset. Several years
ago the LCBset up a fund at the Douglas County Community Foundation to professionally
manage memorial funds and contributions as we look to thefuture. That relationship has been
wonderful and we are grateful to generous supporters of the arts who have directed gifts our
way.
The Stage Project
During the Covid era we had to cancel one summer series when assembly of band members
and the public posed a significant health risk. During that year, the Rice Foundation continued
its support of the LCBby providing funding for the replacement of our aging sound system.
Likewise,the City of Lawrence pivoted annual support to replace badly needed chairs and chair
racks. When we resumed the concert series after a one—year absence, the band ”socially
distanced” by moving to the lawn in front of the gazebo. The band was able to expand its
membership and it was more convenient for many of our aging members to navigate rather
than lugging instruments and equipment up and down the stairs of the bandstand. We grew to
like the arrangement and the audience liked it too. We still have the historic bandstand as a
backdrop and an homage to our past, but we now have an area that allows expanded space for
the band and guest artists that the gazebo did not allow. Altering the gazebo was not an option
due to the historic nature of that structure.
Being at ground level has created some issues. Soggy grounds meant putting tennis balls on
chairs to keep them from sinking into the ground. Uneven ground and cables for microphones
tovarious options and discussed those with the leadership at Lawrence Parks, Recreation &Culture. These options included moving the concerts to a different location and setting up atemporary stage for the summer series. The most viable and preferred solution was toconstruct a slightly elevated permanent stage platform in front (east side) of the Gazebo. it

would be elevated by around 14 inches, envisioned to have a brick face to complement the
gazebo and a concrete (stamped or scored) surface with ADA—accessibledesign.
Longtime band member and LCBboard member, the late Tom Hornbaker and his wife Shelley,
generously donated $50,000 to start a fund to support this project. Subsequently, without any
public announcement, additional support and private funding has grown to around $109,000.
The LCBintends to fully fund this project through donations to the Douglas County Community
Foundation.
Paul Werner Architects has provided a conceptual drawing and preliminary site layout ofthe
project. We have engaged an engineering company at our expense to survey and locate
topography for the exact siting of this platform. Working with Parks, Recreation & Culture, we
envision a multiple-use stage that can enhance many other community annual events (Art in
the Park, Fiddling & Picking Contest, etc.) and special events such as weddings, rallies, and
other events that take place in South Park.
Next Steps
Gaining support from the Lawrence City Commission and with the cooperation of Parks,
Recreations & Culture, our goal is to complete the land survey, develop construction
specifications and documents, obtain the additional needed funding (estimated at $150Ktotal),
select a contractor and break ground as soon as possible so we can have this ready for the
Summer 2027 concert series. This is an ambitious but achievable timetable as long as we keep
multiple tracks going. Support for this project by the Lawrence City Commission is vital to
keeping the Lawrence City Band the communityjewel it has been through its history and for
many years to come.

Background
Background
• The Transient Guest Tax Fund (TGT) is supported by an 8% tax collected on 
all short-term overnight stays in Lawrence. 
• The TGT Fund, by Code, is used to promote tourism and convention promotion 
in Lawrence. 
• Charter Ordinance 30 states that the TGT fund should be expended for the 
purposes as the City Commission determines promotes, enhances, maintains, 
or improves the tourism, visitor, or convention business of the city.

Current Advisory Bodies
Current Advisory Bodies
• Parks, Recreation and Culture (PRC) and the City’s Unmistakable Identity
commitment are supported by two City advisory bodies: the Parks and 
Recreation Advisory Board and the Cultural Arts Commission. 
• Both bodies are composed of members appointed by the City Commission 
and provide community perspective, guidance, and recommendations to 
the City. 
• The Cultural Arts Commission provides oversight of the annual 
Community Arts Grant Program and serves as the review body 
responsible for evaluating applications and making funding 
recommendations.

Proposal
Proposal
• The Unmistakable Events Grant Program similarly advances the City’s 
Unmistakable Identity commitment by supporting community events and 
experiences that contribute to Lawrence’s identity, vitality, and sense of 
place. 
• Following this established model, it is appropriate for the Parks and 
Recreation Advisory Board to serve as the review body for the 
Unmistakable Events Grant Program, evaluating applications and making 
funding recommendations to the City Commission. 
• This approach creates a consistent and transparent advisory-board 
structure for the City’s grant programs while more directly connecting the 
Parks and Recreation Advisory Board’s work to the Unmistakable Identity
commitment in the City’s Strategic Plan.
