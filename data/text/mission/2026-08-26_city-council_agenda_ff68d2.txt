The Tomorrow Together 2040 Mission Comprehensive Plan includes several 
recommendations that support the adoption of a Complete Streets ordinance for the City 
of Mission. The Complete Streets concept is a way of planning, designing, and building 
streets so they are safe and accessible for all users, including pedestrians, bicyclists, 
motorists, and transit riders of all ages and abilities. Complete Streets is a tool for ensuring 
a city’s transportation network reflects community values  in providing equitable forms for 
transportation for all residents and visitors. Staff first introduced this concept to the City 
Council at a work session in August of last year. Since this time staff has been working on 
developing a Complete Streets policy. Staff will present a draft Complete Streets policy at 
this work session for Council’s input and guidance.

RE: Review of a Draft Complete Streets Policy for the City of Mission 
DETAILS: The Tomorrow Together 2040 Mission Comprehensive Plan includes three 
recommendations related to the adoption of a Complete Streets ordinance and 
transportation planning process. In addition to recommendations found in the 
comprehensive plan, our 2021 DirectionFinder Survey shows residents want more 
transportation options and safer, more connected streets. 
Staff presented an overview of the Complete Streets concept to the City Council at a 
work session in September of last year. A link to that presentation can be found here. At the 
work session staff explained that Complete Streets is a way of planning, designing, and 
building streets so they are safe and accessible for all users, including pedestrians, 
bicyclists, motorists, and transit riders of all ages and abilities. This approach is not 
about a single design standard or an immediate overhaul of our streets. Instead, it is a 
policy framework for making long-term, thoughtful decisions that serve all users. Complete 
Streets is a tool for ensuring our transportation network reflects our community’s values. 
Adopting a Complete Streets policy will: 
• Provide safer options for people who walk, bike, or roll.
• Support transportation choice for residents and visitors.
• Improve public health and safety.
• Create attractive, business-friendly corridors.
• Reduce long-term maintenance costs by planning proactively.
After a thorough and thoughtful discussion, the City Council directed staff to move 
forward with developing a draft Complete Streets policy for the Council to consider at a 
future work session. 
An internal team has been developed consisting of representatives from Administration, 
Public Works, Police, and Community Development to develop a Complete Streets 
policy that best fits the needs of the City in guiding staff and consultants in the future 
planning, designing and construction of streets and other transportation infrastructure 
improvements for the City that best incorporate Complete Streets concepts. Staff would 
like to present this draft Complete Streets Policy to the City Council at tonight’s work 
session to garner feedback and answer any questions. The draft policy is included for 
review.

Mission, Kansas SECOND DRAFT Complete Streets Policy 
August 26, 2026 
 
Vision and Intent 
The City of Mission’s (“the City”) Complete Streets policy establishes a framework for 
creating a connected, safe, accessible, and equitable multimodal transportation 
network. This policy directs the design and implementation of transportation and 
mobility projects with the goal of improving access for all users of all ages. It 
emphasizes modes that have historically received less priority in transportation planning 
such as walking, rolling, bicycling, and transit. 
Complete Streets elements may include, but are not limited to: 
 Intersection safety improvements – right turn on red prohibition, 4-way stop 
control, dedicated left-turn lanes/signal, raised intersections 
 Traffic calming measures – advanced warning signs, curb extensions, speed 
feedback signs, on-street parking, vertical deflectors, chicanes 
 Pedestrian safety improvements – lighting, leading pedestrian interval, raised 
crosswalk, RRFBs, HAWK signals, pedestrian refuge islands, high visibility 
crosswalks, sidewalks and paths 
 Traffic safety improvements – driveway access control, raised median 
 Bike safety improvements – sidewalks, shared paths, sharrows, painted bike 
lanes, bike detection, protected bike lanes 
A connected, multimodal network improves route directness, reduces barriers, and 
supports efficient navigation throughout Mission. A Complete Streets approach 
advances the City’s goals for public safety and equitable access while also delivering 
community benefits including improved public health and air quality, expanded 
opportunities for outdoor recreation, and reduced long-term maintenance costs. 
 
Diverse Users 
The City of Mission recognizes that all users of the transportation network deserve safe, 
accessible facilities. These users include: 
 Pedestrians 
 Bicyclists 
 Transit riders 
2 
 
 Motorists 
 Emergency responders 
 Freight and commercial vehicles 
 People of all ages and abilities  
Additionally, some users face disproportionate transportation burdens and limited 
access to community resources, particularly low-income individuals, people of color, 
older adults, children and youth, people with disabilities, and households without a 
private vehicle. These groups rely more heavily on walking, rolling, cycling, and transit 
and face higher safety risks when using these modes. This policy prioritizes these 
populations in transportation planning and design to reduce existing inequities and 
create a safer, more accessible network for all. 
 
Full Commitment 
This policy applies to all phases of transportation projects within the public right-of-way, 
whether publicly or privately funded. This includes: 
 New construction and reconstruction 
 Retrofits and rehabilitation 
 Maintenance operations, such as repaving, resurfacing, and restriping 
 Private development projects that require City approval. 
During any construction, routine maintenance, or emergency repair work that 
encroaches on the right-of-way, appropriate accommodation must be provided to 
maintain safe movement for all road users, including pedestrians and cyclists. Private 
streets, driveways, and parking lots should also adhere to this policy.  
 
Clear Exceptions 
A project may only be excluded from Complete Streets requirements if: 
1. Accommodation is unnecessary on corridors where specific users are prohibited 
(e.g., interstate freeways) 
2. The cost of accommodation is documented as excessively disproportionate to 
the current or future need 
3. There is a documented absence of current and future use based on review of 
technical documents and plans adopted by the City of Mission, such as the 
Mission Connections Plan, the Pavement Condition Index, and the Safe Streets 
for All Action Plan  
3 
 
Exceptions to the Complete Streets policy will be reviewed and documented by the City 
Administrator or their designee and approved by the City Council. The City 
Administrator or their designee will add requested exceptions to the City Council 
meeting agenda for discussion in open forum. Documentation of the proposed 
exception will be published in the City Council meeting minutes and accessible to the 
public via the City website. 
 
Jurisdiction 
The City will establish and maintain an internal Complete Streets Committee composed 
of representatives from relevant City departments. The Committee will meet regularly to 
review transportation projects, evaluate them for consistency with this policy and other 
adopted City plans, identify opportunities to improve multimodal access and safety, and 
develop recommendations for implementation. Recommendations from the Committee 
will be presented to the City Council by the City Administrator or their designee to inform 
project prioritization and decision-making. 
City staff will coordinate with regional partners, including the Mid-America Regional 
Council (MARC), the Kansas Department of Transportation (KDOT), Johnson County, 
utility providers, emergency services, and neighboring jurisdictions, as needed to 
promote connectivity, consistency, and coordination across jurisdictional boundaries. 
To support effective implementation of this policy, the City will encourage training and 
professional development opportunities for staff and decision-makers related to 
Complete Streets principles, best practices, and emerging design guidance, as 
resources allow. 
 
Design 
Complete Streets design principles shall be incorporated into all publicly and privately 
funded projects, as appropriate. All transportation infrastructure and mobility projects 
requiring funding or approval by the City of Mission as well as projects funded by the 
State and/or Federal government will adhere to Mission’s Complete Streets ordinance. 
The Mission Complete Streets ordinance will focus on developing a connected, 
integrated network that serves all transportation users. Complete Streets principles will 
be integrated into policies, planning, and design of all types of public and private 
projects, including new construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, repair, and 
maintenance of transportation facilities in public right-of-way and private projects. 
4 
 
The City will utilize the following guidelines to inform the planning, design, and 
construction of Mission’s streets: 
 American Association of State Highway Transportation Officials (AASHTO) 
 National Association of City Transportation Officials (NACTO) Design Guides 
 Small Town and Rural Design Guide, Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) 
 Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD) 
 Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Standards for Accessible Design 
o Public Rights-of-Way Accessibility Guidelines (PROWAG) 
o Americans with Disability Act Accessibility Guidelines (ADAAG) 
 Documents and plans created for the City of Mission including, but not limited to: 
o Comprehensive Plan (Tomorrow Together 2040 Mission Comprehensive 
Plan) 
o Zoning Code 
o Bicycle and Pedestrian Plan (Mission Connection Plan) 
o Comprehensive Safety Action Plan 
o Rock Creek Trail corridor studies 
o Pavement Condition Index 
 
Land Use and Context Sensitivity 
Reorienting Mission toward Complete Streets principles requires integrating 
transportation considerations into all land use decisions. Accordingly, all new or updated 
land use policies, plans, zoning ordinances, and related documents must identify how 
they support the City’s Complete Streets objectives. 
The physical, economic, and social setting of Mission must be given full consideration 
when implementing the Complete Streets ordinance. Community context should be 
integrated into the decision-making process by giving significant consideration to 
stakeholder and community values. The goal of Complete Streets implementation is to 
preserve and enhance Mission’s unique scenic, aesthetic, historic, and environmental 
resources while also improving safety, mobility, and infrastructure conditions for all users 
of our transportation network.  
 
Performance Measurement 
The City will collect and publicize data every two years to monitor progress. Metrics for 
evaluating performance will include: 
 Number of crashes resulting in fatalities and/or serious injuries and their location 
 Linear feet of new sidewalks, bike lanes, and trails 
5 
 
 Linear feet of repaired or replaced sidewalks, bike lanes, and trails 
 Number of safety projects completed 
 Number of Complete Streets Projects implemented within 1/4 mile of schools, 
health care services, grocery stores, retail and service stores, banks, parks or 
recreational facilities.

Project Selection Criteria 
Mission will establish criteria to encourage funding prioritization for Complete Streets 
implementation with at least the following principles in mind: 
 Connectivity – connecting existing infrastructure to existing infrastructure and 
creating additional connections to community assets (including, but not limited to, 
6 
 
businesses, schools, community gathering places, government facilities, medical 
facilities, parks, voting locations) 
 Multimodality – implementing projects that expand infrastructure for modes of 
transportation other than the car 
 Economic Development – proposed project supports broader efforts to enhance 
economic development 
 Equity – project will broaden transportation options for residents with limited 
connectivity to community assets 
 
Implementation 
Mission will integrate Complete Streets practices into routine operations when feasible, 
treat every transportation project and program as an opportunity to improve the network 
for all users, and coordinate with relevant departments, agencies, and jurisdictions to 
achieve these goals. 
Mission will update all related procedures, plans, regulations, and processes to align 
with this ordinance within five years of its adoption. Further, Mission will also ensure this 
ordinance remains aligned with the latest standards established by the National 
Complete Streets Coalition. 
Finally, the City of Mission will organize an annual Complete Streets focused community 
work session to update the community on its progress. This work session will include 
City of Mission staff, elected and appointed officials, and representatives from outside 
stakeholder groups to provide community input.  
Through this Complete Streets Policy, the City of Mission affirms its commitment to 
creating a transportation network that is safe, connected, accessible, and equitable for 
all users. By integrating Complete Streets principles into planning, design, construction, 
maintenance, and decision-making, the City will continue to enhance mobility, improve 
public safety, strengthen community connectivity, and support a high quality of life for 
current and future residents. This policy serves as a long-term commitment to building a 
resilient transportation system that reflects Mission's values and meets the evolving 
needs of the community.
