Comprehensive Land Use Plan 
 
The Tomorrow Together 2040 Mission Comprehensive Plan includes a future land use 
map that identifies the entire west side commercial district, including these parcels of 
property, as “Mixed-Use High Density.” 
 
“Mixed-Use High Density” is defined in the plan as “pedestrian-friendly mix of housing, 
office, and retail uses in either a multi-story building (vertical mixed-use) or in a 
cohesive development of separate or attached buildings (horizontal mixed-use) with a 
floor area ratio (FAR) of 3 to 10 and density of 50 or more dwelling units per acre.   
 
The proposed development is requesting a deviation from the mixed-use requirement, 
but does have the following densities that meet the definition included in the 
Comprehensive Plan: 
 
• Building #1 – has a FAR of 2.2 and a density of 46 units/acre  
• Building #2 – has a FAR of 3.3 and density of 138 units/acre  
• Building #3 – has a FAR of 3.4 and density of 87 units/acre 
 
Zoning  
 
The two parcels included in the PDP sit within the Form-Based Code (FBC) overlay 
district. The FBC lays out an implementation plan from the West Gateway Vision study 
that was adopted by the City in 2005. This visioning document calls for re-imagining the 
entire commercial district on the west side of the City from what has been primarily 
suburban, auto-centric type land uses to more urban, pedestrian oriented land uses. 
The vision document puts forth the notion of multi-story buildings, with a variety of uses 
within, that are situated at the public realm (or sidewalk) with parking behind or in 
parking structures. The intended result is a more walkable, pedestrian-oriented area 
that allows for shopping, dining, work, and living all within a few blocks.  
 
The FBC breaks out the west side commercial area into specific blocks and then 
identifies the types of buildings that would be permitted within each block. It goes 
further into materials and general design parameters for the building types. It also 
identifies specific corridors and how buildings should be situated on those corridors as

Building Height - Building #1 (5665 Foxridge) is located in Block “B” of the FBC.

Mixed-Use - The FBC stipulates that high-rise buildings should be mixed use with

Buildings #2 nor #3 offer a mixed-use element.

Impact to Infrastructure 
 
Storm Water - A storm water study was submitted as part of the PDP. The entire site, 
both 5665 Foxridge and 5700 Broadmoor, is mostly impervious surface because of 
buildings or parking areas. Storm water currently sheet flows to the northwest toward 
Foxridge and 56th Street or the southeast toward Broadmoor and 58th Street.  
 
If fully built as proposed, the project would result in a slight increase to the overall 
impervious surface area. The plan calls for the construction of stormwater structures 
which will collect storm water and direct it to either the northwest or southeast. Storm 
water flowing to the northwest will be captured in an underground row separator 
beneath the front parking lot of Building #1 where it would be filtered, detained, and 
slowly released into the existing public storm sewer. Storm water flowing to the 
southeast will be collected and directed to the public storm sewer system at 58th 
Street.  
 
The stormwater improvements proposed by BRES comply with the provisions of the 
City’s code.   
 
Traffic Impact Study - A traffic impact study (TIS) was also submitted as part of the 
PDP. A TIS shows the impact that the additional traffic generated from the proposed 
development will have on the surrounding road system during peak hours – morning 
and afternoon rush hour.   
 
The July TIS, completed by the developer’s traffic engineer and reviewed by the City’s 
consulting engineers, shows that at full buildout, all 647 apartment units will generate 
2,926 trips over a 24-hour period. Moring rush hour trips equate to 264 and afternoon 
rush hour trips are 246.   
 
Most intersections (56th/Foxridge, 58th/Foxridge, 58th/Broadmoor, Johnson 
Drive/Broadmoor) surrounding the development site currently operate at an “A” or “B” 
level of service (LOS). Some intersections, most notably Johnson Drive and Broadmoor

CFAA CONSIDERATIONS/IMPACTS: Housing is an important component of the 
Community for All Ages initiative. A variety of housing helps to meet the needs of a 
variety of people. Multi-family housing is not only attractive for young adults first 
entering the housing market, but older adults who want to downsize to secure, 
maintenance free housing. Housing in the downtown core, within walking distance of 
retail and services, promotes less reliance on automobiles, which is ideal for those that 
cannot or prefer not to drive.

FROM: William A. Block, CCIM, Vice President | Principal, Block Real Estate Services, LLC 
DATE: September 24, 2026 
RE: Current Economic Conditions Affecting the Feasibility of Required Retail or 
Commercial Space 
Planning Commissioners and Staff:
This memorandum responds to the City Councils September 16, 2026 remand directing the 
Planning Commission to give greater consideration to current economic conditions affecting 
the feasibility of mixed use at 5700 Broadmoor. It is limited to that mixed-use and retail issue; 
the applicants separate traffic and exterior-material submissions address the other remand 
topics.
1. APPLICANTS CONCLUSION
The available evidence does not support requiring new speculative retail at this location. The 
Citys own November 2025 Land Use Code Audit concludes that Mission may already be over-
zoned for retail and that planned commercial space requires either substantial residential 
growth or a greater share of spending from outside the City. Current CoStar data and recent real 
world experience within Mission further reinforce the risk that required retail space may remain 
vacant even when the residential portion of a mixed-use project is successful.
The proposed redevelopment responds directly to the demand-side need identified by the 
Audit: it adds 340 homes, adaptively reuses a largely vacant office building, supports existing 
West Gateway businesses, and creates an active pedestrian frontage without constructing 
commercial space for which no tenant or demonstrated demand has been identified. 
Importantly, the adaptive reuse portion of the project will also recycle construction materials 
and reduce waste significantly in favor of a more environmentally-focused approach to 
development.
2. CITY OF MISSION LAND USE CODE AUDIT FINDINGS
The City of Mission Land Use Code Audit Report, dated November 2025, provides the clearest 
market evidence:
The findings suggest that Mission may already be over-zoned for retail. 
Without significant increases in residential density or broader regional market share, new commercial 
areas risk underperforming, leaving space underutilized and reducing investment returns. 
The Audit identifies 307,000 square feet of planned West and East Gateway commercial space and 
estimates that 4,715 additional residents would be required to support it under the Audits sales-
based methodology. 
The Audit concludes that the planned retail exceeds what the local market can reasonably support 
without significant outside spending or residential growth. 
These findings do not suggest abandoning retail. They indicate that the City should first strengthen 
the customer base for existing and planned commercial areas. The 340 additional homes proposed 
at 5700 Broadmoor advance that objective.
3. MISSIONS RECENT MIXED-USE EXPERIENCE
The Lanes  The January 21, 2026 City Council minutes report that four live/work units were leased, 
but none was being used for business operations; the other apartments were rented. The same 
discussion noted other vacant commercial space in Mission, including Mission Mart across the street. 
Accurate characterization  The Lanes units were designed as live/work units from the outset; they 
were not conventional storefront retail later converted to apartments. The relevant local evidence is 
that the live/work format has not produced actual business occupancy even though the units 
themselves are leased. Our team has independently confirmed these details. 
The Locale  Representatives of EPC Real Estate Group confirmed that The Locale opened in 2020 
with two commercial spaces. A 604-square-foot space leased within approximately one year. The 
approximately 5,600-square-foot corner space remained vacant for nearly six years while EPC sought 
a restaurant tenant and was finally leased in summer 2026 to a salon-studio user. 
Implication  Residential demand and commercial demand are not interchangeable. A successful 
apartment component does not establish a market for required ground-floor retail, and constructing 
new commercial space can place it in direct competition with existing vacancies. 
4. APPLICANT MARKET OUTREACH
To supplement the Citys Audit and current CoStar data, the applicant reviewed the leasing 
experience associated with recent Mission projects, including The Lanes, The Locale, and Mission 
Crossing, and consulted with developers and retail professionals familiar with the local market. The 
Lanes experience is documented in the City Councils January 21, 2026 minutes and was separately 
confirmed by the applicant, and The Locale leasing history was confirmed directly by representatives 
of EPC Real Estate Group on September 23, 2026. Those examples and discussions were consistent 
with the published data: successful retail depends heavily on visibility, access, parking, proximity to 
an established commercial corridor, and rents sufficient to support development costs.
Mission Crossing illustrates the importance of those fundamentals. Its commercial space benefits 
from Metcalf frontage, surface parking, convenient access, adjacency to Target, and an established 
retail setting. The Locale likewise occupies a prominent Johnson Drive location. By comparison, 
5700 Broadmoor lacks direct frontage on or visibility from a primary commercial corridor and has 
more constrained parking and access.
The applicant does not have a prospective tenant and the market itself has not dictated sufficient 
demonstrated demand for new commercial space at 5700 Broadmoor. Requiring speculative 
commercial space at this location would therefore create a material risk of prolonged vacancy rather 
than the active street frontage intended by the Comprehensive Plan.
Jeff Berg, Founding Partner of Catalyst Property Group, LLC and an experienced local retail real 
estate professional, has provided a letter based on his experience developing and operating retail 
property in Mission, including Mission Crossing. His letter, attached as Exhibit B, addresses local 
leasing conditions, the sites retail fundamentals, the compatibility of walk-out residential units, and 
the viability of newly constructed speculative retail at 5700 Broadmoor: 
Under current market conditions, I would expect newly constructed speculative retail at this 
location to face meaningful leasing and economic challenges. In this location, walk-out units 
would likely be more compatible with surrounding uses. Importantly, additional residents in 
the corridor would support existing and future retail uses. 
5. CURRENT COSTAR MARKET EVIDENCE
The September 23, 2026 CoStar reports provide current, third-party market evidence for Mission and 
the surrounding Northeast Johnson County retail submarket. The figures do not show that all Mission 
retail is failing. They do show that existing space remains available, commonly requires more than a 
year to lease, and commands rents that do not support the cost of speculative new construction. 
The relevant findings are summarized below; the full reports are cited as sources and can be 
provided upon request.
Mission inventory  The Mission search covers retail properties within Mission, Kansas. It reports 
approximately 1.6 million square feet of inventory. 
Mission rents  CoStar reports a $21.51 per-square-foot market asking rent across Mission and a 
lower $17.21 per-square-foot asking rent for space currently available. 
Leasing time  CoStar reports 12.9 months on the market and 13.8 months to lease for Mission 
retail. Those figures demonstrate that even existing space offered at prevailing rents is not absorbed 
immediately. 
Northeast Johnson County  The broader submarket contains approximately 410,000 square feet 
listed as available, and recorded negative 12-month net absorption of approximately 41,300 square 
feet. 
Neighborhood centers  Neighborhood-center vacancy is 7.8%, with a market asking rent of $20.98 
per square foot. This is a particularly relevant comparison for storefront-oriented space serving nearby 
residents. 
Mission Mart  CoStar reports 30.8% vacancy at Mission Mart, notwithstanding substantial leasing 
activity during the prior 12 months. This confirms that meaningful existing retail inventory remains 
available within Mission. 
6. RETAIL RENT COMPARED WITH NEW-CONSTRUCTION COST
Retails share of the mixed-use building cost could be $500 per square foot or more if the retail 
component is allocated its proportionate share of the structure, foundations, vertical circulation, 
life-safety systems, façade, and other shared building costs. This is an allocation of mixed-use 
building costs to the retail component - not a standalone retail construction rate or a detailed project 
pro forma. 
 
At the $17.21 per-square-foot asking rent for currently available Mission space, annual gross rent 
equals approximately 3.4% of a $500 per-square-foot allocated building cost. 
Even at Missions broader $21.51 per-square-foot market asking rent, annual gross rent equals only 
approximately 4.3% of that allocated building cost. 
Both comparisons are before vacancy, operating expenses, tenant improvements, leasing 
commissions, financing costs, land cost, and any return on equity. 
Conclusion: requiring speculative retail at those economics would create space whose achievable 
rent does not justify its cost, increasing the risk of prolonged vacancy or later conversion.

8. SITE-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS
The site is within the West Gateway district but is not a Johnson Drive storefront location. 
The principal existing asset is an older office structure whose adaptive reuse accounts for more than 
half of the additional residential units. 
The proposal creates active frontage through individual entrances, stoops, landscaping, residential 
amenities, pedestrian and bicycle connections, and its relationship to Broadmoor Park. 
Requiring a conventional retail bay without an identified tenant could produce the opposite of the 
intended result: vacant space with dark or papered windows rather than active street frontage.  
A conventional retail use would also likely require changes to the projects parking design, potentially 
including additional access points and dedicated, highly visible parking areas for retail tenants and 
their customers. 
9. WHY THE PROPOSAL REMAINS CONSISTENT WITH THE COMPREHENSIVE PLAN
The evidence above explains why requiring retail at 5700 Broadmoor is not feasible under current 
market and site conditions. Importantly, that conclusion does not place the proposal at odds with 
the Comprehensive Plan. The Plan expressly recognizes both flexible mixed-use development and 
horizontal mixed use across separate or attached buildings within a cohesive development.
Tomorrow Together 2040 defines Mixed-Use High-Density as a pedestrian-friendly mix of uses in a 
multi-story building or a cohesive development of separate or attached buildings (horizontal mixed-
use). The Plan also states that mixed-use developments should be flexible so they can 
accommodate changing needs. (pp. 18 and 89.)
The project does not claim that this residential site alone contains every component of a horizontal 
mixed-use development. Rather, it advances a horizontal mixed-use pattern across West Gateway 
by adding the residential density needed to support existing nearby retail, office, service, park, and 
civic uses. It also supplies the active, pedestrian-oriented frontage contemplated by the Plan 
through building entrances, stoops, landscaping, amenities, and improved connections. The 
surrounding uses and connections are illustrated in the Horizontal Mixed-Use Context Exhibit 
attached as Exhibit A.
This interpretation gives effect to both parts of the Plan: its support for mixed-use districts and its 
express recognition that mixed-use development must remain flexible as retail and office markets 
change.
I have reviewed the general location and proposed redevelopment at 5700 Broadmoor. In my 
opinion, the property presents greater retail-leasing challenges because it is not directly 
adjacent to or readily visible from an established commercial corridor and would have more 
constrained customer parking. Under current market conditions, I would expect newly 
constructed speculative retail at this location to face meaningful leasing and economic 
challenges. Furthermore, retail in this location would demand visible parking near and/or along 
the street, which would need to be considered in the context of the surrounding neighborhood. 
In this location, walk-out units would likely be more compatible with surrounding uses. 
 Jeff Berg, Founding Partner, Catalyst Property Group, LLC

Accordingly, the requested mixed-use deviation responds to documented market and site 
conditions while maintaining the Comprehensive Plans intent through flexible land use, residential 
density, active frontage, and support for the broader West Gateway mixed-use district.

that:
Current economic conditions do not support requiring speculative retail or commercial space at 5700
