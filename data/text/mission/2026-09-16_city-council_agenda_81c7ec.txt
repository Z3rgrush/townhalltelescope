RE: Consideration of PC Case #26-15, a Preliminary Development Plan for the 
Foxridge/Broadmoor Development at 5665 Foxridge and 5700 Broadmoor  
RECOMMENDATION: Approve PC Case #26-15 for a preliminary development plan 
for three separate apartment buildings totaling 647 units to be located at property 
addressed as 5665 Foxridge and 5700 Broadmoor with stipulations 1-17 as indicated in 
the Ordinance.  
DETAILS: Block Real Estate Services (BRES) received approval in 2022 for a 
preliminary development plan to construct a five-story, 307-unit apartment building at 
5665 Foxridge, the site of the former JC Penny Call Center. 
BRES submitted an application in May 2026 for a new preliminary development plan 
(PDP) that includes revisions to the initial apartment building approved in 2022, a 
repurposing of the current office building at 5700 Broadmoor to an apartment building, 
and construction of a new apartment building at the corner of 58th Street and 
Broadmoor. The entire development plan totals 647 apartment units between the three 
buildings. 
Development Plan Summary 
Building #1 - 5665 Foxridge - BRES initially proposed construction of a five-story, 
307-unit apartment building that would encompass the entire site at 5665 Foxridge. The
building was approved for four-stories of residential use over a ground floor, podium
parking structure. The building faced the west with a visitor parking lot at Foxridge and
an interior courtyard with residential amenities to include a swimming pool, bocce court,
cooking grills, and sitting areas.
The new PDP removes the ground floor podium parking structure and replaces it with a 
five-level parking structure immediately to the south. This results in the height of the 
building being reduced by one story and allows the ground floor apartment units to 
have a more direct connection to the sidewalk surrounding the building. This building is 
designated as a mid-rise structure under the City’s Form-Based Code. The parking 
structure is wrapped with a four-story apartment structure on the east and south sides. 
Building #2 - 5700 Broadmoor - This is currently a ten-story, 175,000 square foot 
office building. BRES intends to repurpose this building into a 176-unit apartment 
building. Part of this repurposing entails “reskinning” the exterior of the building to allow 
for bigger windows and balconies. BRES has proposed cladding the bottom two stories

Comprehensive Land Use Plan 
The Tomorrow Together 2040 Mission Comprehensive Plan includes a future land use 
map that identifies the entire west side commercial district, including these parcels of 
property, as “Mixed-Use High Density.” 
“Mixed-Use High Density” is defined in the plan as “pedestrian-friendly mix of housing, 
office, and retail uses in either a multi-story building (vertical mixed-use) or in a 
cohesive development of separate or attached buildings (horizontal mixed-use) with a 
floor area ratio (FAR) of 3 to 10 and density of 50 or more dwelling units per acre.  
The proposed development is requesting a deviation from the mixed-use requirement, 
but does have the following densities that meet the definition included in the 
Comprehensive Plan: 
• Building #1 – has a FAR of 2.2 and a density of 46 units/acre
• Building #2 – has a FAR of 3.3 and density of 138 units/acre
• Building #3 – has a FAR of 3.4 and density of 87 units/acre
Zoning 
The two parcels included in the PDP sit within the Form-Based Code (FBC) overlay 
district. The FBC lays out an implementation plan from the West Gateway Vision study 
that was adopted by the City in 2005. This visioning document calls for re-imagining the 
entire commercial district on the west side of the City from what has been primarily 
suburban, auto-centric type land uses to more urban, pedestrian oriented land uses.

The vision document puts forth the notion of multi-story buildings, with a variety of uses 
within, that are situated at the public realm (or sidewalk) with parking behind or in 
parking structures. The intended result is a more walkable, pedestrian-oriented area 
that allows for shopping, dining, work, and living all within a few blocks. 
The FBC breaks out the west side commercial area into specific blocks and then 
identifies the types of buildings that would be permitted within each block. It goes 
further into materials and general design parameters for the building types. It also 
identifies specific corridors and how buildings should be situated on those corridors as 
well as streetscape for those corridors. 
Building Height - Building #1 (5665 Foxridge) is located in Block “B” of the FBC.  
Block “B” allows for mid-rise buildings, which is defined as structures between two and 
four stories. Building #1 has now been revised to four stories. 
Buildings #2 & #3 (5700 Broadmoor) are located in Block “C” of the FBC. Block “C” 
allows for high-rise buildings, which is defined as structures five stories or greater in 
height. Building #2 (the existing office building) is ten stories and Building #3 is 
proposed to be seven stories. 
Both blocks permit structured parking at the back of the property or screened with 
ground floor retail use. The PDP provides a parking structure that is wrapped with a 
residential use around the east and south sides, as opposed to the ground floor retail 
required by the FBC. 
Mixed-Use - The FBC stipulates that high-rise buildings should be mixed use with 
ground floor retail or office space and residential or office space above. Neither 
Buildings #2 nor #3 offer a mixed-use element. 
Exterior Materials – The FBC stipulates and/or prohibits certain exterior cladding 
dependent on the building type. Stone, brick, stucco, wood siding, fiber cement siding, 
and even metal are all permitted materials for mid-rise buildings. Stone, brick, metal or 
light-weight concrete panels are permitted materials for high-rise buildings. Building #1 
is compliant with the exterior cladding provisions outlined in the FBC. However, 
Building #2 is not. The applicant is proposing to use EIFS, which is not a permitted 
material. Building #3 included stucco, brick and stone, but the FBC does not allow for 
the use of stucco high-Rise buildings

Impact to Infrastructure 
Storm Water - A storm water study was submitted as part of the PDP. The entire site, 
both 5665 Foxridge and 5700 Broadmoor, is mostly impervious surface because of 
buildings or parking areas. Storm water currently sheet flows to the northwest toward 
Foxridge and 56th Street or the southeast toward Broadmoor and 58th Street. 
If fully built as proposed, the project would result in a slight increase to the overall 
impervious surface area. The plan calls for the construction of stormwater structures 
which will collect storm water and direct it to either the northwest or southeast. Storm 
water flowing to the northwest will be captured in an underground row separator 
beneath the front parking lot of Building #1 where it would be filtered, detained, and 
slowly released into the existing public storm sewer. Storm water flowing to the 
southeast will be collected and directed to the public storm sewer system at 58th 
Street. 
The stormwater improvements proposed by BRES comply with the provisions of the 
City’s code. 
Traffic Impact Study - A traffic impact study (TIS) was also submitted as part of the 
PDP. A TIS shows the impact that the additional traffic generated from the proposed 
development will have on the surrounding road system during peak hours – morning 
and afternoon rush hour.

The July TIS, completed by the developer’s traffic engineer and reviewed by the City’s 
consulting engineers, shows that at full buildout, all 647 apartment units will generate 
2,926 trips over a 24-hour period. Moring rush hour trips equate to 264 and afternoon 
rush hour trips are 246. 
Most intersections (56th/Foxridge, 58th/Foxridge, 58th/Broadmoor, Johnson 
Drive/Broadmoor) surrounding the development site currently operate at an “A” or “B” 
level of service (LOS). Some intersections, most notably Johnson Drive and Broadmoor 
drop to a LOS of “C” in the afternoon rush hour. A LOS of “A”, “B”, and “C” is 
considered acceptable. 
The exceptions would be the intersections of 56th Street/Metcalf and 58th 
Street/Metcalf. The July TIS indicates that both intersections have experienced a 
relatively high number of traffic accidents over the past few years. 
The highest number of accidents (rear-end collisions) have occurred with the right turn 
movement from 58th Street to northbound Metcalf, which currently has a LOS of “D” for 
the morning rush hour and “F” for the afternoon rush hour. However, improvements to 
Metcalf that are currently underway by KDOT include the extension of the acceleration 
lane between 58th Street and 56th Street, which will address this. 
The second highest number of accidents (rear-end collisions) have occurred with the 
right turn movement from 56th Street to northbound Metcalf, which currently has a LOS 
of “C” for the morning rush hour and “D” for the afternoon rush hour. The July TIS 
indicates that there will not be any change in the LOS with the new proposed 
development. 
The lowest number, but more severe, accidents (angle collisions), have occurred with 
the southbound, left turn movement from Metcalf to eastbound 56th Street, which 
currently has a LOS of “C” in the morning rush hour and an “E” in the afternoon rush 
hour. With the additional trips generated from the proposed development project, the 
July TIS projected this movement would drop to a LOS “D” and LOS “F” respectively. 
The July TIS offered recommendations for improvements to the intersection of 56th 
Street and Metcalf to address the low service levels.

The Planning Commission held a public hearing on the revised PDP (Case #26-15) at 
its July 27, 2026 meeting. The Commission had numerous questions about the 
proposed development plan most of which focused on the application of the FBC to the 
project and the traffic study. The Planning Commission was also concerned that KDOT 
had not responded to the applicant’s July TIS, though attempts had been made to 
engage them. 
Additionally, the Planning Commission wanted to see renderings of Building #2 (5700 
Broadmoor), which were not available at the time of the meeting to have a better 
understanding of the exterior materials that were proposed. Several residents were 
also in attendance and provided testimony against the development. 
After nearly two hours of discussion and testimony, the Planning Commission voted to 
continue the public hearing to the August 24, 2026 Planning Commission meeting so 
that: a) renderings and a description of exterior building materials for Building #2 could 
be provided, and b) staff could engage with KDOT to determine if they were aware of 
the proposed development plan and had reviewed the July TIS.

Chapter 410 of our zoning code states that the Form-Based Code governs all 
development in this study area, and it really utilizes cumulative zoning.  And what that 
means is that if you have something that is zoned for industrial, which is the highest 
intensity of use we have in our zoning code, cumulatively that allows residential and 
commercial uses in that zoning district without requiring a rezoning. 
 
Traditional zoning regulations that we're used to are really intended to dictate the land 
use.  So, you can put a residence here.  You can't put a commercial business there.  
You can, can't do this based on the use.  Form-Based Codes are really about the 
physical form of the development.  What does the building look like?  How big is it? 
What does it look like from the street?  How does it interact with the street?  So, it's 
much more visual.

appropriate type of building that would be sited.  And this project falls within Block B.

structures.

district.  So, Broadmoor is considered the primary frontage for that block.
