V. New Business / Action Items 
A. Review of New Cemetery Documents:  
M. Frey presents the new forms and documents that Town Clerk Nihiser has put together. 
These forms include a Cemetery contract that replaced the older “cemetery deed”, and three 
brand new forms: an Open/Close request, a monument application, and a transfer request. The 
contract is filled out when the plot is purchased, providing all the necessary information to 
sell a plot. The open/close request is filled out anytime a grave is opened and/or closed. This 
allows the Town to have clear communication on when services are being held, when graves 
need to be opened, and when they need to be closed. Additionally, it is a mechanism that will 
prompt the sexton and clerk to obtain all the necessary documents prior to any bodies being 
interned, such as a cremation certificate or a declaration of remains. M. Frey asks if anyone 
can open or close a grave, and the clerk informs the committee that per the cemetery 
ordinance only the Town of Silver Plume, or a vendor previously approved by the Silver 
Plume Sexton can excavate within the cemetery, meaning that Mickey can open/close graves, 
or anyone that Mickey/TOSP authorizes, but any random excavation company cannot dig 
graves. The monument application is another regulatory measure, ensuring that only 
authorized people, i.e. monument companies/families, are allowed to touch/tamper with grave 
sites. This also allows the Town to regulate the type of headstone. Currently, there are not any 
regulations on monuments, however Mac states it would be in the Town’s best interest to 
prohibit any flagstone and/or concrete. Otherwise, the committee would like to allow families 
to have flexibility choosing their loved ones monuments/headstones.  The last document is a 
transfer request, and this is used to transfer a contract from one person to another. Marty asks 
if there are dedicated spaces for urns, which there is not a current section specifically for urns. 
However, the way the ordinance is currently written, you can buy a plot and bury either one 
casket or four cremains, so one could purchase a single plot and allow three other people to be 
buried with them. In this case, the contract would need to be filled out to explicitly state this is 
the wishes of the owner of the contract. The cemetery committee approves the new forms 
unanimously.  
B. Review of New Cemetery Section Proposal:  
Selling cemetery plots is currently a bit of a stressful situation, as there is not a section of 
available plots ready for purchase. If someone were to walk in right now and want to buy a 
plot, Mickey and Chelsea have to walk through the cemetery with the interested party, look at 
where someone might be able to be buried, and hope that there isn’t an unmarked grave 
already there. Mickey and Chelsea both feel very strongly that a new section needs to be 
opened up for present-day burials. This will allow the clerk to do all the plotting and data 
entry for the historic sections separately from selling new plots. It also allows the historic 
sections to remain as they are, with only maintenance needing to be done and no 
opening/closing occurring. Mickey suggests opening a new, residential public burial section 
behind Section II, the Historic Italian Section. This land is well within the cemetery property 
and does not interfere with the Xcel right of way. It is already flat, so the prep work is limited 
to removing the existing trees. M. Frey estimates approximately 200 graves to fit in the new 
section. The existing access road can be extended with very little difficulty. The cemetery 
property goes north all the way to the train tracks, and all the way west to the homes located 
on S. Charles, so there is plenty of room to expand without needing to purchase additional 
land in the future. It is noted that a large percentage of this land is probably not usable to dig 
into, being comprised of hard rock, but there is a lot of room to explore for future sections. 
Mac states that if this is the case, and the cemetery does have usable room, the Town should 
consider opening a “non-resident” section as well, and charge market value for these plots. 
This is a way to get a little more revenue for the cemetery without making it to financially 
burdensome for the Town residents to purchase plots.  
C. Discussion of Cemetery Clean Up:  
M. Frey is in contact with a church group that wants to send up to 20 volunteers to the 
cemetery prior to snowfall to do manual labor. They have offered to trim bushes, cut off tree 
limbs, rake, and stack slash. Mickey plans to invite them up on October 24th. Mac suggests 
renting a porta-potty for the volunteers, Clerk states M. Frey should see if Timberline would 
donate the use of one before we pay to rent it. Mac states folks can park on his blacktop 
behind the A frame and can also utilize the trailhead parking on Mountain Street, and Clerk 
states that they can also park along her driveway so that we can keep the cemetery parking lot 
clear. Additionally, will have to determine if we are getting a roll off to dispose of the slash, 
have the CCFA come and chip the slash, or if we should make slash piles that will be burnt in 
the winter months. Mac states that this is an acceptable form of disposal, and M. Frey states 
that we could turn the bonfire situation into a fundraiser in the Winter. Mickey will confirm 
with the volunteer group if the 24th will work for them. If it does, clerk will post for additional 
volunteers to join the effort, and if it does not, hopefully M. Frey will be able to set up an 
alternate date. 
D. Discussion of Cemetery Gate:  
Mac states he has some ideas for the cemetery gate but needs to know where they will be 
placed. M. Frey states that instead of a few individual gates, he would like one single gate that 
restricts vehicle access to all of the entrances. He states he would like an archway still, but 
one that opens with the gate to allow heavy equipment through such as cranes for 
monuments/caskets. Mac states this simplifies things a bit, and he will start to mockup some 
plans for this. Hopefully the gate can be placed sometime in 2027.
