SUBJECT:  Ordinance Regulating the Residential Location of Registered Sex Offenders 
   
Report in Brief 
  
City Council has requested that Legal draft an ordinance regulating the residential location of registered sex offenders. Included 
within this request Legal was tasked with researching whether this type of regulation was lawful for the Arvada City Council to 
undertake. This session is designed to summarize the state of the law for City Council, and request Council provide direction on 
several key policy questions. 
  
Background 
  
Arvada does not currently have an ordinance that restricts or regulates the residency of sex offenders or sexually violent 
predators. Several City Council Members have expressed an interest in passing an ordinance that would restrict or regulate the 
residency of sex offenders and sexually violent predators. 
 
The purpose of this session is for Legal to report on the current state of the law, clarify terms, and provide some policy 
guidance to the City Council. With regards to guidance Legal will be asking Council to consider three specific questions: 
 
1. Who should be restricted or “governed” under this ordinance? 
2. What areas do we want to restrict sex offenders from? 
3. How far do we want to restrict sex offenders from certain areas? 
 
These questions will be discussed in the context of the overarching question whether City Council wants to pursue the drafting 
and consideration of such an ordinance. 
 
In preparation for this project, Legal has conducted legal research, reviewed ordinances from other comparative local 
municipalities, and drafted several legal memoranda. 
  
Strategic Alignment 
  
This potential ordinance serves to forward the City of Arvada’s Safe Community strategic alignment. 
  
Next Steps 
  
Once Council has provided feedback regarding the parameters of the ordinance, the City Attorney's Office will prepare a draft 
ordinance for City Council consideration at a business meeting.

Establishment of a Sex 
Offender Residence 
Ordinance
This presentation is Attorney Work Product
Questions to be Answered by Council: 
If we proceed with drafting an ordinance, the following questions should be 
answered: 
1. Who do we want to restrict? 
2. Where do we want to restrict them from? 
3. How far away do we want to restrict them from establishing residence? 
Does Arvada have the legal authority? 
Yes. This is what is referred to as a topic of 
“mixed local and state concern.” 
• Thus, Arvada is not outwardly “pre-empted” 
from establishing an ordinance regulating 
where a registered sex-offender may 
establish residency in the City of Arvada. 
• Ryals v. City of Englewood
What we cannot do is: 
• Create a blanket ban on any sex offender 
from establishing residence in Arvada. 
• Creating an ordinance that has a restrictive 
distance that has the practical effect of 
prohibiting a sex offender from establishing 
residence anywhere in the City. 
• Applying a new ordinance retroactively to 
sex offenders already in the City. 
• Apply this ordinance to juveniles. Only 
adults. Juveniles are regulated by the 
State. 
1. Who do we want to restrict? 
• “Sex Offender” means any person who is convicted of a sex offense or any criminal 
offense with an underlying factual basis. 
• Includes a wide variety of offenses at both the misdemeanor and felony 
level.
• Currently 216 RSOs in Arvada 
• A “sexually violent predator” is a ‘sex offender’ but one who has been found a “high 
likelihood or risk to reoffend”, and been ruled to be classified as a sexually violent 
predator by a court of law.
• Currently 3 of those 216 RSOs are classified as SVPs
• By offense-type: Legal recommends avoiding this type of criteria.
• Determinate vs. indeterminate vs. lifetime registration
• Crime classifications vary significantly. 
• Specific conditions of criminal sentence and SOMB requirements
2. Where do we want to restrict them from?
• Common examples include: 
• Schools
• Playgrounds
• Pools
• Daycare or childcare facilities
• Be cautious of “overly-restrictive” locations and overly vague locations
• Parks
• Libraries
• “recreational areas” or “recreational trails” 
3. Distance of Restriction? 
• Survey of comparative cities most are in the 500 to 1000 foot range. 
• Legal recommends no further than 1000 feet. 
• Be cautious of “overly-restrictive” or a distance that has the practical effect of 
preventing any sex offenders from establishing residence in the City.
