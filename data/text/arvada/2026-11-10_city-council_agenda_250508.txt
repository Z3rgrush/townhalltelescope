SUBJECT:  Discussion Regarding Potential Commercial Drone Regulations 
   
Report in Brief 
  
The City team will provide an overview of commercial drone operations; describe what aspects of the operations a local 
government can regulate; and present possible Land Development Code regulations. 
  
Background 
  
Drone use for retail deliveries is expanding across the United States.  Major retailers, such as Walmart, are requesting approvals 
from local governments to provide such services. 
 
Currently, the City does not have any specific regulations regarding commercial drone delivery use. 
 
As part of this workshop, the City team will provide an overview of commercial drone operations; describe what aspects of the 
operations a local government can regulate; and present possible Land Development Code regulations. 
  
Strategic Alignment 
  
Bolster a vibrant local economy by delivering efficient and cost-effective services and support for business in Arvada. 
  
Next Steps 
  
Based on City Council input, the City team will draft regulations for commercial drone delivery operations and present the 
proposed Land Development Code regulations to the Planning Commission and City Council at a future date.

Commercial Delivery Drones
Typical delivery scenario:
● Package prepared and delivered to drone “nest” enclosure
● Package is either attached to the drone on the ground or the drone hovers and drops a tether 
to attach package
● Drone cruises at about 150 feet and at speeds up to 65 mph
● Drone arrives at destination and lowers to about 20 feet 
● Package is either dropped or tethered to the ground
Commercial Delivery Drones

Regulatory Framework
FAA Authority - Airspace
The Federal Aviation Administration has exclusive jurisdiction over the National Airspace System, 
including the operation, flight paths, altitude, and safety certification of unmanned aircraft.
Commercial drone delivery operators are certified by the FAA under 14 CFR Part 135 and must 
obtain an exemption or waiver to provide drone package deliveries.
Municipalities have no legal authority to regulate where drones fly, how high they fly, their flight 
paths, or their schedule of flights once airborne. 
 
Regulatory Framework
Local Authority - Ground-Based Operations
Municipalities can regulate the land use at the point of distribution, such as the pad, structure, 
fencing, screening, ground-operation hours, parking, lighting, and security of the staging area.
 
Regional Response
City of Northglenn recently adopted regulations
City of Thornton has regulations under consideration
Towns of Castle Rock and Parker have imposed moratoriums (six and nine months)
Most other communities have not yet addressed commercial drone delivery
 
Regulation Options
Require Conditional Use approval utilizing the criteria found in the LDC (8-3-5-
1) for primary and/or accessory uses:
● Neighborhood meeting
● Planning Commission recommendation
● City Council decision
Regulation Options
Require specific site design regulations for the “Nest”, such as:
● Maximum facility size
● Hours of operation
● Location
● Setbacks from other types of uses (residential, schools, etc.)
● Landscaping
● Screening/fencing
● Noise limitations
Regulation Options
Potentially allow in the following zone districts:
● Accessory use in the MX (S, U and T), CG, IL and IG zoning districts
● Primary use in the IL and IG zoning districts
