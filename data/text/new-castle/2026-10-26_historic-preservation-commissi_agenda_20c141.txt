Town seeks citizens to serve on the 
Historic Preservation Commission 
The Town of New Castle is seeking applications from citizens interested in 
serving on the Historic Preservation Commission. 
The Historic Preservation Commission reviews proposals to designate 
properties as historic landmarks, advises property owners on physical  
and financial aspects of property preservation, advises Town Council on 
matters related to preserving the historic character of the Town, and 
performs related duties. People with expertise in a preservation-related 
discipline, such as history, architecture, planning, or archeology, can be 
especially valuable Members, but such expertise is not required. 
This is a non-paid position. The person appointed to the Historic 
Preservation Commission will fill a vacancy created when a member 
resigned and will serve until April, 2026.  
The Commission meets monthly, 3rd Monday of that month at 6:30 p.m. 
The deadline for submitting letters of interest is January 10, 2027.

Consider Review of Videography Project 
Chair Riddile provided an update on the Historic Preservation Commission videography 
project. Chair Riddile reported that the videography partner at Colorado Mountain College 
had contacted her and offered two rooms for interviews on September 23, 2026.  
Chair Riddile also reported that other potential interview subjects had been explored, 
including Kent Jolley, but a scheduling conflict prevented participation at this time. Chair 
Riddile noted that Grady Hazelton had expressed interest in participating in a future 
session together with Kelly Cox, formerly Kelley Hazelton, and members agreed that it 
would be a strong future pairing. 
The Commission discussed obtaining signed consent forms in advance of interviews. Chair 
Riddile reported that releases were needed for prior interviews and that efforts were 
underway to complete that documentation. 
Commissioners also discussed the presentation format of the recorded interviews. Chair 
Riddile said in the current format, viewers sometimes hear an answer without hearing the 
original question, which can make the final video confusing. Professional Assistant Huster

Consider HPC Applying for Grants for Survey of Historical Properties from 
Sandborn Maps  
The Commission discussed grant application planning for additional historic property 
surveys using Sanborn maps and related research materials. Commissioner Ruth Fletcher 
said the first step would be to identify a project area and determine how many properties 
might be included, after which the Commission would need to secure consultant quotes. 
Commissioner Ruth Fletcher noted that the grant would pay for consultant work. 
Commissioner Ruth Fletcher asked if multiple quotes were required for a new application. 
Professional Assistant Huster would research and discuss the specifics at the October 19, 
2026, meeting.  
Commissioner Ruth Fletcher said that the grant application was due in mid-January and 
that funding, if awarded, would not likely become available until the fall of 2027. Staff 
confirmed that a match was not required for the grant. 
The Commission also discussed public concerns related to historic designation.  
Consider Draft of the CLG (Certified Local Government) Audit 
Professional Assistant Huster said the purpose of Certified Local Government status was to 
improve access to preservation-related funding and gave the community a more formal 
role in historic preservation. Alternate Commissioner Fuller added that Certified Local 
Governments are also important points of contact for agencies such as the Forest Service 
when projects may affect historic resources. Staff confirmed that the Commission was 
current on required webinar participation and explained that training notices were 
forwarded to commissioners as they were received. 
In response to a question about the Commission’s greatest challenge over the past year, 
commissioners agreed that it was difficult getting property owners interested in historic 
designation and overcoming fear or misunderstanding about what designation would 
mean for private property owners. 
When asked about the Commission’s most significant accomplishment during the year, 
commissioners said the cemetery designation was a major success. Commissioner Parks 
said the cemetery was a special and an important part of New Castle’s history. 
Commissioner Parks noted his appreciation that it received recognition on Memorial Day 
and Founder’s Day. Members also identified the videography project as an important 
accomplishment. 
The commission said the goals for the upcoming year were to continue the videography 
project, pursue the survey grant discussed earlier in the meeting, and expand efforts to 
secure additional property designations. Commissioner Richard Parks suggested inviting 
small groups of property owners to future meetings to discuss designation opportunities 
directly. Members supported that idea.
