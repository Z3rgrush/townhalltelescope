           Memorandum 
 
To:    Mayor & Council 
 
From:   David Reynolds 
 
Re: Workshop Item: Building and Planning and Police Department Updates 
 
Date: 09/01/2026 
 
Purpose: 
The purpose of this Workshop agenda item is to provide time for Town Planner Paul Smith and Police 
Chief Stu Curry to present Town Council with a requested update regarding the accomplishments, 
current needs, priorities, and long-term vision of the Building-Planning Department and the Police 
Department. 
As part of the annual Town Council Retreat Work Session, Council traditionally receives updates from 
Department Heads outlining departmental accomplishments, challenges, operational needs, and future 
priorities. These reports help Council better understand Town operations and provide context for 
discussions regarding the Town’s future direction and allocation of resources. 
This year, Council has requested that Department Head reports be provided in advance of the annual 
retreat rather than being presented for the first time during the retreat. Providing these materials 
beforehand allows Council Members time to review the information, consider the needs and priorities 
identified by staff, and formulate questions and ideas in advance. 
This approach is intended to make the retreat more productive and strategic. Rather than using valuable 
retreat time to receive background information, Council can arrive familiar with the major 
accomplishments, challenges, needs, and opportunities facing each department. This allows the retreat 
discussion to focus more directly on strategic priorities, long-term planning, resource allocation, and 
aligning Council’s goals with departmental needs.

Police Department Update 
From: Chief of Police 
To: Town Council 
I would like to provide the Town Council with an update on one of the Police Department’s 
primary areas of focus this year: training and professional development. 
We have made a deliberate effort to increase the amount and variety of training available to our 
personnel. Our goal is not simply to meet minimum training requirements, but to provide our 
officers and staff with the knowledge, skills, and tools necessary to perform at a high level and 
continue providing professional service to our community. 
We have also been intentional about taking advantage of grant funding and regional training 
opportunities whenever possible. A significant portion of the training listed below has been 
funded through our local training region’s Colorado POST grant, allowing us to provide this 
professional development while minimizing the financial impact on the Town. 
In-Service Training 
Throughout 2026, the New Castle Police Department has continued its commitment to 
professional development by conducting in-service training every month. Training topics this 
year have included emergency vehicle operations and driving, arrest control, firearms, 
search and seizure, high-risk vehicle stops, active shooter response, and Tactical Combat 
Casualty Care (TCCC).  
This consistent training ensures our officers maintain and continually improve the skills 
necessary to safely and effectively serve our community. We are also fortunate to have some of 
the best law enforcement instructors in this area of the state within our own department. 
Several of our instructors teach at the law enforcement academies in Glenwood Springs and 
Breckenridge, allowing us to bring a high level of experience and expertise directly back to our 
officers. Our commitment to monthly training reflects our belief that maintaining a highly trained 
department is essential to providing professional, safe, and effective police services to the Town.

Continued Investment in Our Personnel 
Training is one of the most important investments we can make in our department. The situations 
our officers encounter are constantly changing, and we want our personnel to be prepared—not 
only to respond to those situations, but to respond professionally, safely, and effectively. 
I am proud of the commitment our officers and supervisors have demonstrated toward continued 
professional development. Their willingness to attend training, become instructors, and bring that 
knowledge back to our department directly benefits the entire organization and, ultimately, the 
residents we serve. 
One of our current leadership development priorities has been the continued professional 
development of Sergeant Contreras. Sergeant Contreras has been actively pursuing advanced 
leadership training through the FBI-LEEDA Trilogy, which consists of three progressive 
courses: the Supervisor Leadership Institute, Command Leadership Institute, and 
Executive Leadership Institute. This training provides law enforcement leaders with the 
knowledge and skills necessary to effectively supervise personnel, manage organizational 
challenges, develop employees, and lead an agency through change and growth. 
In addition to the FBI-LEEDA Trilogy, Sergeant Contreras recently completed the CIRSA 
Leadership Academy, an intensive 80-hour leadership program designed specifically to 
develop leadership skills and strengthen organizational effectiveness. We were fortunate that this 
training was fully funded by CIRSA, including meals, lodging, and transportation.  
I am proud of Sergeant Contreras’ commitment to professional development and leadership. His 
willingness to pursue this level of training demonstrates the type of initiative we want to 
encourage throughout the organization. We will continue to look for opportunities to invest in 
our personnel through quality training, grant funding, and partnerships with organizations such 
as FBI-LEEDA, CIRSA, and Colorado POST. 
Ultimately, these investments are not simply about training individual employees. They are 
investments in the future of our department and, most importantly, in the service we provide to 
the residents of our community. 
 
2026 Community Events 
One of the things we are particularly proud of this year is the department’s ability to be actively 
involved in our community. Maintaining full staffing levels has allowed us to dedicate personnel 
to community events while continuing to provide the level of police service our residents expect. 
This is an important benefit of having a fully staffed department. It gives us the capacity to do 
more than simply respond to calls for service—it allows our officers to build relationships with 
the people they serve. 
Page 8 of 13
A major focus of our community engagement efforts has been developing positive relationships 
with the youth in our community. We believe that getting to know our young residents outside of 
an enforcement setting helps build trust and creates positive interactions that can last well into 
the future. 
Bike Rodeo 
The department participated in our annual Bike Rodeo, providing an opportunity for children to 
learn about bicycle safety while interacting with our officers in a fun and positive environment. 
Events like the Bike Rodeo allow officers to engage with children in a setting where the primary 
focus is education, safety, and having fun. 
Junior Police Academy 
The department hosted another successful Junior Police Academy, focused on youth ages 10–
14. 
The academy provides young people with an opportunity to see firsthand what law enforcement 
is about and develop relationships with the officers who serve their community. Participants 
were exposed to a variety of police functions, including: 
• K9 demonstrations and training 
• SWAT 
• Driving 
• Arrest control 
• Firearms 
• Obstacle courses 
• Team-building activities 
The goal of the Junior Police Academy is not to encourage young people to become police 
officers, but to give them a better understanding of law enforcement, introduce them to the 
people behind the badge, and provide positive experiences with the department. 
 
Coffee with a Cop 
We also held Coffee with a Cop, which continued to provide residents with an informal 
opportunity to meet and talk with our officers. This year, we added a youth component with a 
Mario Kart tournament between local youth and our police officers. 
The tournament was a great example of how we can take a traditional community event and find 
new ways to engage young people. It gave kids the opportunity to interact with officers in a 
relaxed environment where everyone was there simply to have a good time.

National Night Out – Battlement Mesa 
The department participated in National Night Out in Battlement Mesa, working alongside the 
Garfield County Sheriff's Office and Parachute Police Department. 
National Night Out is an annual community-building campaign that promotes police-community 
partnerships and neighborhood camaraderie to make neighborhoods safer. It provides an 
opportunity for residents and law enforcement officers to interact in a positive, informal 
environment and strengthen relationships within the community. 
And, of course, the NCPD once again provided what many would consider to be the best snow 
cones around. 
Upcoming Events 
We still have several opportunities to engage with our community as we close out 2026, 
including: 
• BBQ with a Cop 
• Trick or Treat Street 
• Shop with a Cop 
These events provide additional opportunities for our officers to interact with residents and, 
particularly, with young people in our community. 
Community engagement is an important part of our overall policing philosophy. Our officers 
spend their days responding to calls, conducting investigations, enforcing laws, and dealing with 
people during some of the most difficult moments of their lives. Community events give us an 
opportunity to interact with residents under completely different circumstances. 
For our young residents in particular, we want their experiences with police officers to include 
more than seeing us during an emergency or traffic stop. We want them to know our officers as 
members of their community—people they can talk to, ask questions of, and trust. 
I am proud of the effort our officers have put into these events throughout 2026. They 
consistently demonstrate that being a police officer is about more than enforcement. It is about 
being present, building relationships, and being part of the community we serve.

Lerma and Nick Martin to Corporal. We are fortunate to have experienced officers who have 
chosen to lateral to our agency from other departments in the area, bringing valuable knowledge, 
experience, and leadership to our organization.

Police Department Remodel 
The New Castle Police Department has also made several significant upgrades to our facility this 
year to improve functionality, training, and the work environment for our employees.  
We created a new training and meeting room, which features our department badge 
prominently displayed on the wall along with our mission and vision statements, providing a 
dedicated space for training, meetings, and department functions.  
Patrol staff moved into the former conference room, which was updated to better meet their 
operational needs, and we created two additional offices to improve our use of space.  
We also converted the former juvenile holding cell into a Lieutenant’s office. CBI was pleased 
with this change, as they prefer that juveniles not be held in a cell and instead remain in a 
conference room while awaiting release to a parent or guardian.  
Additionally, we converted the former supervisor’s office into a department gym. Looking 
ahead, we have received Colorado POST grant funding and a generous donation from 
Alpine Bank that will allow us to create a permanent mat room for arrest control training. 
These improvements allow us to make better use of our existing facility while creating a more 
professional, functional, and training-focused environment for our employees. 
 
Significant Accomplishments  
One of the New Castle Police Department’s significant accomplishments in 2026 has been our 
continued focus on active shooter response and preparedness. We met with the principals of 
all three schools in our community to strengthen our relationships, identify areas for 
improvement, and develop a comprehensive reunification plan for use during a critical incident. 
We have also increased active shooter response training for our patrol staff so our officers are 
prepared to respond quickly and effectively when seconds matter.

In addition, all officers receive eight hours of Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC) 
training and carry an IFAK (Individual First Aid Kit), a compact trauma kit designed to 
provide immediate treatment for life-threatening injuries such as severe bleeding and airway 
emergencies until additional medical assistance arrives.  
Another significant accomplishment was our work helping develop the Town’s new e-bike 
ordinance. Prior to summer break, our officers completed an educational campaign at all three 
schools to help students understand the new regulations and safe operation of e-bikes. We also 
purchased an e-bike for the department, allowing officers to conduct bike patrols and further 
engage with our community.  
Our K9 program continues to grow and develop as well. Our K9 handler regularly trains with a 
regional group of handlers from Glenwood Springs, Silt, and Garfield County, providing 
valuable opportunities to maintain and improve the team’s capabilities. In addition, Officer 
Brooks is trained as a K9 decoy, allowing him to work directly with Sergeant Alcorta to conduct 
in-house training in both search and bite work. These efforts reflect our commitment to being 
proactive, prepared, and continually improving the services we provide to our community. 
 
Looking ahead to 2027 
As we look ahead to 2027, the New Castle Police Department has identified several priorities 
that will strengthen our capabilities and better serve our community. One of our immediate 
priorities is replacing the current K9 vehicle, which was generously donated to the Town by 
Garfield County approximately a year and a half ago. We are evaluating several options, 
including purchasing an existing K9 vehicle or converting one of our current vehicles to meet the 
needs of the K9 program.  
We are also pursuing grant funding to establish a department drone program. We already 
have an officer trained as a drone pilot, and New Castle is one of the few agencies in our area 
that does not currently have this capability.  
In addition, we are working towards an MOU with the AHRT team, our area multi-
jurisdictional SWAT team, which would allow one of our officers to participate on the team and 
bring additional specialized capabilities back to our department.  
Finally, we are evaluating the creation of a full-time Investigator/School Resource Officer 
position. Investigative capacity is an identified need within our organization, and we currently 
do not have a dedicated investigator. Because many of our more complex investigations involve 
child-related crimes, combining the investigator role with an SRO position would provide 
significant benefit to both the department and our schools. These initiatives represent our 
continued effort to build the department strategically and ensure we are prepared to meet the 
needs of our community in the years ahead.
