9. Presentation and Distribution of Pavement Condition Index (PCI) 
 
Dan Merkh, Public Works Director, and Rhoderic Montgomery, CIP Project Manager, presented 
this item. The Citywide Pavement Condition Index (PCI) score is currently 86, which is 
excellent. This score signifies that 88% of street miles are rated as good or excellent. City staff 
reviewed maintenance strategies, focusing on the importance of timely interventions to manage 
costs effectively, and explained deterioration curves that show how roads degrade over time. 
He gave Council a handout to help them determine what PCI really means on the streets. 
  
Councilmember Longanecker stated he drove those roads. Below 70 PCI really needed repairs. 
It won’t take long to drive the score of 86 down. 
 
Mr. Merkh gave a sample scenario, because one question came up of how long these scores 
last. Hypothetically, with a roadway that starts at 100 when it gets built, it has a period which 
hits degradation. For instance the chip-seal treatment that was just completed in the last couple 
of weeks would bring the PCI back up around roughly 5 to 10 points and it’ll help keep the 
roadway in better condition for the next five or so years. Like all industry averages, there’s a lot 
of parameters that fall into that. Hard winters make it shorter, soft winters make pavement last 
a lot longer. When you hit another 5 years or so, then we hit a thin overlay, like a UBAS and 
then it would give us approximately 7 – 12 years. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker asked if there were any roads as poor as the area rated 27 on 3rd 
Street.  
Mr. Merkh stated no, nothing near that severity. 88% if our miles rate at good or better. We did 
some research to find what other cities’ averages and goals were, 65-75 where most 
recommend. Staff recommendations fall to a range of 70-74. When we get your range 
recommendation, we will be able to prepare and build a street preservation program annually 
and identify any limitations to the goal. This can grow and change as often as council have the 
direction to do so. 
 
Mayor asked how often the PCI takes place. 
 
Mr. Merkh stated that it is every 5 years. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker is comfortable with staff recommendations. Bad streets are 
terrible, especially in the wintertime. He stated that we could always revisit this if needed.

Background/Description of Items: 
Annually the City of Edgerton updates the Fee Resolution to reflect the changes in rates for 
the water/wastewater charges for the next calendar year. Staff is also recommending changes 
to fees for Parks and Recreation and special events.    
 
Water/Wastewater Rates for 2027 
Staff have worked closely with Raftelis Financial Consultants in the last few years to complete 
a biennial rate study for both water and wastewater utilities. In their report, Raftelis 
recommends a small rate increase each year of the forecast period to avoid larger rate 
increases. The rate increases recommended for 2027 are listed below. The 2027 Budget 
adopted by the Council on September 10, 2026, included these rate increases. There will be 
another rate study in 2028. The attached fee resolution formally adopts the rate increases and 
sets the effective date as the January 1, 2027 utility bills.

Background/Description of Item: 
Each year the City of Edgerton requests that the financial statements and financial reports of 
the City of Edgerton for the year ending December 31st be prepared in compliance with the 
cash basis and budget laws of the State of Kansas, and requests waiver of the requirements of 
K.S.A. 75-1120a(a). 
 
K.S.A. 75-1120a(a) is the uniform system of fiscal procedure, accounting and reporting for 
municipalities. It requires the use of Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP). The 
Governing Body can, by resolution, each year make a finding that the financial statements and 
financial reports prepared in conformity with the requirements of GAAP are of no significant 
value to the Governing Body or members of the general public of the municipality. The 
Governing Body can then request a waiver from the Director of Accounts and Reports to 
prepare the City’s financial statement on the basis of the cash receipts and disbursements as 
adjusted to show compliance with the cash basis and budget laws of this State. 
 
Another requirement to receive the waiver is that the City of Edgerton may not have any bond 
ordinances or other ordinances or resolutions which require financial statements or reports to 
be prepared in conformity with K.S.A. 75-1120a(a). The City of Edgerton currently does not 
have any such ordinances or resolutions.

Background/Description of Item: 
The outdoor warning siren system is operated and activated by Johnsons County, while the 
ownership of the outdoor warning sirens within the network is compiled of cities, townships, 
Johnson County Parks and Rec., and the Johnson County Airport. This system consists of 190 
sirens strategically placed throughout the county. Each of the sirens in the system are 
manufactured by Federal Signal, installed and maintained by Blue Valley Public Safety, and 
activated on the Johnson County, county-wide warning system. According to the National 
Weather Service, outdoor warning sirens are “designed only to alert those who are outside 
that something dangerous is approaching.” Individuals are always encouraged to utilize 
multiple methods of alert for dangerous weather conditions such as cell phone alerts, local 
media, weather radios, Notify JoCo, etc.  
 
Edgerton’s Siren Locations and Coverage: 
The City currently has three outdoor warning sirens within its jurisdiction: 
 
• First Street and West Martin Street (Manor Park), identified as Siren ED02; 
• 1100 West Braun Street, identified as Siren ED01; 
• The Intermodal Lift Station site at Logistics Park Kansas City (LPKC), identified as Siren 
ED03. 
 
The map on the next page shows the approximate outdoor coverage area for each siren. 
These boundaries do not guarantee that a siren will be heard at every location within the 
area. Actual audibility varies with weather, surrounding buildings, and background noise. Siren 
ED01, located at 1100 West Braun Street and shown in the lower-left portion of the map, 
currently has a smaller coverage area than the other two sirens. Its coverage area is expected 
to increase with installation of the replacement unit. 
 

 
 
Siren ED01 was installed on August 6, 1994, and is approximately 32 years old. The unit has 
reached the end of its useful life. Replacement parts are increasingly difficult to obtain, and 
some components can no longer be serviced or replaced. These limitations make continued 
maintenance more difficult and affect the City’s ability to keep the unit operating reliably. 
In 2025, City staff coordinated with Blue Valley Public Safety to evaluate the location for a 
replacement siren. The review considered advances in siren technology, anticipated coverage, 
and development in Dwyer Farms. Based on that evaluation, the existing location at 1100 
West Braun Street remains the preferred location for the replacement unit. 
  
On August 28, 2025, City Council approved the purchase of the replacement Outdoor Warning 
Siren with the recommended 2026 Budget Approval. At this time the cost for the replacement 
unit was $29,500. In early 2026 staff reached out to Blue Valley Public Safety to get the 
updated quote for the replacement unit, due to cost increases for the equipment that were 
received during the request for quote process, staff postponed the purchase to update the 
cost in the 2027 Budget. At the September 10th City Council meeting, City Council approved 
the 2027 Budget, as part of this approval process the Outdoor Warning Siren was included as 
an updated project cost. The new budget for the replacement siren was estimated at $36,000, 
to align with the current cooperative cost structure.  
 
The Vehicle and Equipment Policy requires the preparation of the specifications for the 
replacement of equipment with an effort for those to be as “standard” as possible to provide 
for economical and efficient repairs. It also requires that the City first use cooperative 
purchasing partnerships. For the purchase of the replacement Outdoor Warning Siren at 1100 
W Bruan St, staff used the Kansas City Regional Purchasing Cooperative through Mid America 
Regional Council (MARC) and Mo-Kan Council of Public Procurement (MKCPP). The 
MARC/KCRPC Contract No. 109/1R includes cooperative purchasing agreement language for 
Federal Signal, the siren manufacturer, and Federal Signal’s local distributer Blue Valley Public 
Safety. Per Contract No. 109/1R the total price for the replacement siren at 1100 W Bruan St 
is $33,539.

Background/Description of Item: 
On September 14, 2023, City Council approved the Senior Property Tax Rebate program with 
$65,000 budgeted for the 2024 Fiscal Year. The budgeted amount remained the same for 
2025 and 2026 because not all funds were depleted in previous years. 
 
This year, the City received significantly more applications – 33% more - than the prior years.  
• 2024 – 71 applications 
• 2025 – 75 applications 
• 2026 – 100 applications 
 
Staff believe this increase is due, in part, to the postcards that were sent out to every 
household in late August and increased social media reminders. 
 
However, due to the increased interest, the $65,000 allocated was not enough to cover every 
application that was received. Staff is requesting Council approve an additional $4,900.25 to 
be taken from the City’s unencumbered fund balance in the General Fund to fully cover the 
seven remaining applications. Sufficient reserve funds are available. 
 
If approved, staff will be requesting additional funds for the 2027 fiscal year as part of the CIP 
process tonight.
