Background/Description of Item:  
During the 2025-2029 CIP Work Session, City Council identified the 2025 Street Preservation 
Program to include new curbs in selected areas as determined by City Staff using tools in the 
Pavement Management Program.  The Program also included a refresh of the City’s Pavement 
Condition Index (PCI), completed under the KDOT Innovative Technology Program grant.  
This item brings the 2025 Street Preservation Program before City Council for final acceptance 
as a complete program. 
 
On May 8, 2025, City Council awarded the construction contract to Sunflower Paving, Inc. for 
a total contract amount of $87,440.02. 
 
On September 25, 2025, City Council approved the KDOT Innovative Technology Program 
grant agreement (Project No. N-0846-01) for the Pavement Condition Index (PCI) portion of 
the 2025 Street Preservation Program.  The agreement provides up to $34,000.00 in grant 
funding through KDOT with a $12,202.00 City cash match. 
 
Also on September 25, 2025, City Council approved a contract with Alfred Benesch & 
Company in the amount of $20,372.00 for review and analysis of the pavement condition 
data, GIS coordination of the PCI results and a 10-year Pavement Management Plan, and a 
contract with Streetscan/CityLogix in the amount of $25,830.00 for vehicle mounted scanning 
and 360 imagery of the road system, pavement condition data processing and three years of 
road asset management software. 
 
On December 11, 2025, City Council approved Change Order #1 with Sunflower Paving for an 
increase of $10,554.24.  The new contract amount is $97,994.26. 
 
On January 16, 2026, City Administrator approved Change Order #2 with Sunflower Paving 
which included the final quantity changes for the 2025 Street Preservation Program (Curb 
Replacement) for $1,155.47.  The new contract amount is $99,149.73.  
 
The 2025 Street Preservation Program was budgeted at $240,715.85. Gas Tax received was 
less than the budgeted amount by $1,858.30, resulting in available funds of $238,857.55. 

Final billings under the Benesch and Streetscan/CityLogix contracts were $20,312.40 and 
$20,870.00, which are $59.60 and $4,960.00 under their respective contract amounts.  
Program costs also included $5,281.50 for City Engineer project manual review and 
construction observation (BG Consultants, Inc.) and $40.25 for advertising.  Total program 
expenditures are $145,653.88.  On October 6, 2026, the City received the final reimbursement 
of $30,474.98 through the KDOT Innovative Technology Program grant, for a net City cost of 
$115,178.90 and a budget savings of $123,678.65.

City Council Action Item 
 
 
Council Meeting Date: December 11, 2026 
 
Department: Public Works 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Background/Description of Item: 
On Dec 11th, 2025, the City Council approved a multiyear agreement with Weather or Not® to 
provide dedicated weather forecasting services. These forecasts support key operational staff 
by improving the ability to accurately schedule projects and initiate emergency operations 
when necessary. Weather or Not provides forecasts and lightning notifications through email, 
a web portal, text messages, and phone calls. Under normal conditions, staff receive updates 
twice daily. During significant or severe weather, Weather or Not increases communication 
with directly contacting staff by phone. These calls are essential to the City’s Winter Weather 
Operations and enhance staff’s readiness during inclement weather events. 
The City’s purchasing policy allows and encourages the practice of cooperative purchases. 
Traditionally this has been most frequently utilized with Vehicle and Equipment. The 
purchasing policy allows for the City to “piggy-back” on contracts entered-into by other 
jurisdictions. Section 2.14 of the agreement between the City of Prairie Village and Weather or 
Not® includes the cooperative purchasing provisions to allow the City of Edgerton to piggy-
back on an existing contract.  
 
The City of Prairie Village renewed its contract with Weather or Not on December 1, 2025, this 
contract runs from 2026 to 2028 with no price increase. The 2027 contract price of $12,700, 
which is the same as the price for 2026, includes Premier Plus (*including A+ Weather Post 
Storm Reports), Sferic Maps total lightning service, and the Mid-Range Forecast (weekly 
delivered month-long outlook).  
 
The 2027 Budget includes sufficient funds to cover the annual contract costs. 
 
The contract runs from January 1, 2027, to December 31, 2027.

(UDC) for regulations regarding emerging industries.

focused on independent community engagement efforts and the other specifically for technical 
code writing for updates to the UDC based on the information received during the community 
engagement efforts.  
 
On September 1, 2026, the City of Edgerton issued a Request for Proposals seeking firms with 
expertise in community engagement to conduct a community engagement campaign specifically 
related to emerging industries in the planning field. The Project highlights include: 
• Identification of a list of emerging industries that will be the main topic of community 
engagement. 
• Minimum of two larger approach community engagement events. 
• Small feedback engagement activities to meet the community where they are. 
• Hosting of a project website to provide project updates. 
• Attendance at least one Planning Commission and City Council meeting. 
• Final Public Engagement Report upon project completion.

short-listed firms were conducted on October 5 and 6,  2026.  The selection committee 
recommends The Nash Group, LLC as the best, most qualified team for the project.

5. TASK 2. LIST OF EMERGING INDUSTRIES
City objective (RFP Exhibit A). Develop a list of emerging industries increasing in development nationwide, such as data centers, battery energy storage
systems, energy production facilities and vertical farming; develop a common understanding in the community of what these industries are, their common
traits and characteristics, and possible impacts from development.
Activities
Step 1, screening a long list. Review national development trends, peer community codes and Edgerton's own context, including LPKC, the BNSF
intermodal facility, utility infrastructure and available land, to identify uses increasing in development nationwide and plausibly relevant to Edgerton.
The City has not identified a list in advance; the list is a product of this task. Research begins with, but is not limited to: data centers; battery energy
storage systems; energy production facilities, including utility-scale solar, wind, natural gas, on-site generation and emerging technologies such as
small modular reactors; electrical substations, transmission and microgrid infrastructure associated with large loads; vertical farming and controlled
environment agriculture; heavy-duty electric vehicle charging and alternative fuel hubs serving freight; cryptocurrency mining and other high-density
computing; hydrogen production and fueling facilities; and highly automated and robotic distribution, cold storage and advanced manufacturing
facilities, including battery manufacturing.
Each candidate is screened for likelihood of an Edgerton application, degree of community concern elsewhere, and whether the current UDC defines
or addresses it.
Recommendation of a short list of up to ten uses for full profiling, confirmed with staff before public engagement begins.
Step 2, plain-language industry profiles of two to four pages for each short-listed use, written for residents, covering: what the use is and what it does;
typical site, building and equipment characteristics; power, water and fuel requirements; noise, light, traffic and emissions characteristics; fire and
safety considerations; jobs and fiscal effects; decommissioning and end-of-life issues; and how other communities have regulated it.
Each profile includes a "What we know and what is still debated" section that separates well-established facts from contested claims on both sides,
with sources cited, and states plainly what the use does not do.
Profiles are reviewed by staff for accuracy before release and serve as the backbone of the project website and event displays.

Agenda Item: Consider Ratification of the 2026 Quiet Zone Regulations

City Council Action Item 
 
 
Council Meeting Date: October 22, 2026 
 
Department: Public Works  
 
 
 
 
 
 
Background/Description of Item:  
The City's 24-hour Quiet Zone at the BNSF Railway crossings on East Nelson Street (DOT 
006177M) and West 199th Street (DOT 006183R) have been in effect since January 2015. 
During a May 13, 2025 inspection, the Federal Railroad Administration (FRA) found that the 
medians at both crossings do not meet 49 CFR Part 222, which requires non-traversable 
medians at least 100 feet long with curbs at least 6 inches high. The medians at both 
crossings are shorter than 100 feet, and the East Nelson Street medians are less than 6 inches 
tall. The FRA has advised staff that once the City receives its formal notice, the City will have 
30 days to complete the corrections to keep the Quiet Zone in place. 
 
The 2026 Quiet Zone Regulations Improvement project was presented to City Council at the 
October 8, 2026 CIP Work Session. City Council provided direction to staff to proceed under 
Section 4 of the City's Purchasing Policy, which provides exceptions for emergency 
maintenance of city infrastructure that cannot be postponed until action can be taken by the 
Governing Body and for situations where the safety of the public is at risk. This was necessary 
to meet the deadlines required by FRA to keep the quiet zone designation. As directed by City 
Council, Staff now requests that City Council ratify the following: 
- Creation of the 2026 Quiet Zone Regulations Improvement project with a budget of 
$200,000.00 funded by the PIF Maintenance Fee. 
- Procurement of the work under the emergency purchase provisions of the Purchasing 
Policy in place of formal bidding. 
- Work outside the standard hours in Edgerton City Code Section 4-203(d), including 
nights, weekends and Sundays, when coordinated with BNSF work windows. 
- Authorization for the Mayor to sign the permits, contracts and agreements necessary 
to complete the work. 
 
The project will extend the medians at both crossings, construct new median noses, place a 
concrete cap on the existing Nelson Street medians to achieve an 8-inch curb height, and 
relocate the median object marker signs, so that every median meets or exceeds the FRA's 
100-foot requirement. Staff posted the Project Manual on the City's website and solicited 
proposals directly from qualified contractors. Proposals were due at 2:30 PM on Friday, 
October 16, 2026, and were opened by City staff after the closing time. A total of 2 proposals
