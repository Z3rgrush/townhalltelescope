7. Presentation by Staff and Alfred Benesch & Company of a Pavement Management Plan 
including an updated Pavement Condition Index (PCI)  
 
Dan Merkh, Public Works Director, explained the Pavement Condition Index (PCI) is a 
score ranging from 0 – 100 that measures the conditions of roadways. This index is 
determined by using modern technologies like LIDAR and 3D imagery, which assess 
various aspects such as block cracking, potholes, and ride quality. The City’s current 
overall PCI was reported to be 86, indicating that most roads are in excellent 
condition. Specific areas such as the industrial zone scored a PCI of 91 due to newer 
concrete roads, while residential zones scored 79.  
 
Three budget scenarios were discussed: (1) with an annual investment of $147,000, 
the average PCI could decay to 56 over ten years, (2) a $500,000 annual investment 
would see a decreased average PCI to 62 over ten years, and (3) maintaining an 
average PCI of 86 requires an annual investment of $1.8 million, preventing PCI 
degradation.  
 
The PCI software allows the City to perform data analytics on roadway conditions, 
budget scenarios, and different maintenance strategies. The software helps in 
planning out which roads need what type of maintenance and when, potentially 
optimizing costs and extending the lifespan of roadways. This presentation highlighted 
the need for the city council to set a target PCI range to help guide future budgeting 
and maintenance prioritization. It was acknowledged that even with a high target PCI, 
roads in poor condition would still require immediate attention.  
 
Mr. Merkh introduced Ed Lewis with Benesch. Mr. Lewis stated that Edgerton is 
starting with a very strong network. To maintain is more cost effective than trying to 
recover a deteriorated network. Now there is more advanced technology to allow more 
functionality to use the data to determine what you are looking at. You can get this 
data at any time and 360 imageries.  
 
The council requested more information such as pictures, bullet points, and examples 
of roads at different PCI levels, along with potential funding implications for a various 
target PIC range of 60, 65, 70, and 75. Further discussion and consensus will help 
frame future budget allocations and maintenance strategies.

8. Presentation by Burns & McDonnell of the Concept Development Report for Dwyer Farms 
Sanitary Sewer Improvements - Site 2 & 3 
 
Councilmember Josh Lewis removed himself during this presentation as he works for 
Burns & McDonnell so there were no questions about a conflict of interest.  
 
Mr. Merkh provided a map handout to go along with the presentation for the 
preliminary design for sanitary sewer improvements at sites referred to as Dwyer Sites 
2 and 3, which were originally identified in the 2020 Wastewater Master Plan.  
 
Miranda Hamrick and James Farrell from Burns & McDonnell presented their findings. 
The existing manual screen at the City’s pump station poses safety concerns, and the 
replacement cost is estimated to be around $900,000. The pump station was designed 
for a third pump, however, to meet capacity needs, swapping the impellers for the two 
existing pumps, adding a third pump, and rebuilding pump number two were 
considered at an estimate of approximately $160,000. The current standby generator 
can support only the two existing pumps. An additional $153,000 is needed to 
enhance the generator capacity for the third pump that complies with KDHE 
requirements. Installing a permanent flow meter, including a vault and bypass 
pumping is essential to measure wastewater flows through the station, estimating a 
cost of $110,000. Due to wet weather capacity issues, a proposal was to either install 
a new holding tank or repurpose the old lagoon for storage, estimating at $680,000.  
 
At site 3, several equipment items, like the grit removal pump, motor platform, UV 
disinfection system, and others have reached the end of their useful like and need 
replacement. The costs of these items vary, with recommendation for new parts kits 
and agreements with manufacturers for timely service and repair. The radio 
communication system has reliability issues due to aging equipment, with a proposed 
solution to replace the county line coax cable and raise some antennas, estimated at 
$135,000. The SCADA system currently operated by a single person is under 
consideration for replacement to enhance operational efficiency. Various options for 
new SCADA systems were evaluated, each with different upfront and annual costs.  
 
Mr. Merkh outlined the next steps for the project, including determining the 
components to move forward with final design, preparing a final design scope and fee 
for council approval, and progressing towards construction.

14. PUBLIC HEARING FOR 2027 RECOMMENDED BUDGET 
 
Beth Linn, City Administrator, stated that this is just a quick summary and highlighted sections 
for the 2027 budget. The foundational priorities are centered around community engagement, 
fiscal stewardship and family-oriented services. The budget supports increased programming at 
the community’s Greenspace, maintenance of events like the Summer Block Party, and the 
Motors and Monsters cars how. It also includes new initiatives like a bike safety rodeo. Local 
events are what brings the community together.  
 
The proposed 2027 budget maintains the mill rate at 29.592, which is significantly lower than 
the average for other third-class cities in Kansas. This represents a continuation of efforts to 
reduce the financial burden on residents, a strategy that has been pursued over the past 
decade. The budget reflects a strategic reduction in the residential tax burden from 92% in 
2011 to 19% today. It includes programs to rebate a portion of the city property taxes to 
residents, with a 40% rebate for the owner-occupied residential property in 2027. Edgerton’s 
mill rate is among the lowest for all similar properties in Johnson County. Substantial growth in 
assessed valuations, largely due to the addition of non-residential properties like those in the 
Logistics Park Kansas City (LPKC), is a key feature. The budget aims to capture these new 
revenue sources while supporting ongoing services and improvements without increasing the 
burden on residential properties. Clarifications were provided regarding the distinction between 
changes in the revenue neutral rates and the city’s actual tax levy strategy, which allows 
capturing new non-residential valuations. 
 
Mayor opened the Public Hearing for 2027 Recommended Budget at 9:03 PM. Having no public 
comments, Mayor closed the Public Hearing for 2027 Recommended Budget at 9:04PM. 
Councilmember Lewis stated he has been on council for approximately 10 years. Council has

reduce the tax burden on those who live here. He would like to see it continue to go down. He 
is glad to see we are doing 40% rebate instead of 10% to residents in 2027. That is huge.
