8. Public Comments.  
 
William LaFalce, an Edgerton Resident, criticized the mayor for sitting with the plaintiff’s 
counsel during a recent court case involving the City of Edgerton. He argued that this 
action undermined public trust and indicated alignment against constituents. The court 
had ruled the City’s lawsuit as a SLAPP and ordered the City to pay the defendant’s 
attorney fees. Mr. LaFalce requested the mayor to issue a public statement explaining 
the choice of seating and also provided letters of resignation for the council members 
and staff, should they choose to resign. He also provided a Dubai flag, stating that is 
who was being worshipped. 
 
Kim Twente, an Edgerton resident, expressed frustration regarding the city’s approach 
to citizen engagement. She criticized the city for hiring outside consultants and 
conducting surveys that she believes are designed to suppress citizen input. Ms. Twente 
argued that these steps are a waste of taxpayer money and do not provide a true 
platform for residents’ voices to be heard. She also claimed a lack of open comment 
section in city surveys and insufficient documentation of public meeting comments, 
which she believes limits meaningful citizen participation.

10. Presentation by ElevateEdgerton! Of Award from Southern Economic 
Development Council (SEDC) 
 
James Oltman, President of ElevateEdgerton!, presented an award from the Southern Economic 
Development Council (SEDC) recognizing the city for its Dwyer Farms Housing Development. 
This project was awarded the SEDC Community Economic Development Award in the under 
5,000 population category. Dwyer Farms is set up to add 279 single-family homes, increasing 
Edgerton’s housing inventory by nearly 45%. The project was commended for addressing 
workforce housing needs and for influencing legislative changes that made more communities 
in Kansas eligible for housing development incentives. It represents over $21 million in new 
residential construction, supported by nearly $1 million in federal funding for infrastructure. The 
award highlights Edgerton’s collaborative efforts and a significant impact on local and regional 
economic development.

11. CONSIDER A SPECIAL EVENT PERMIT FOR WELLSVILLE 1ST ANNUAL 
RZR/RANGER RUN WITHIN THE CITY OF EDGERTON, KANSAS 
 
Trey Whitaker, Assistant to the City Administrator, stated in early July 2026, Mystic Mastin, 
proposed this event with the third stop being in Edgerton. The event is set for October 3,, 2026, 
the “run” would arrive around 4:15 PM and leave around 5:30 PM. This event aims to raise 
money for USD 289 negative school lunch balances. If granted, staff recommends all other 
requirements of Ordinance 2086 be required for participants and their vehicles, and that 
vehicles participating in the event be marked or identified. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker asked what the route into town would be. 
 
Ms. Mastin stated they would ride in on backroads, right to Full Draw and then go right back 
out the same way. There is a form circulating online for all participants information to get 
names, machine counts and head counts, which was required by Eudora. Payments are asked 
to be made to the USD 289. 
 
City Administrator Beth Linn suggested the UTV’s be marked in some way, like a flag and 
sticker, to help law enforcement easily identify participant vehicles. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker motioned to approve, seconded by Councilmember Lebakken, 
business action item nine approving the special event and requiring some type of identifier per 
UTV for the event, waive the annual permit, and retain all other requirements of Ordinance 
2086. The motion carried 5-0 by the following vote: 
 
Yes: Longanecker, Lewis, Lebakken, Conus, Malloy

Karen Kindle, Finance Director, stated staff began on May 28, 2026, for funding requests from 
outside agencies. On July 9, 2026, was the budget work session and recommended mill rate 
discussion. During the work session, council consensus was to use same mill rate from 2026 to

13. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 09-10-26A AUTHORIZING THE CLOSURE OF 
CERTAIN PUBLIC STREETS FOR MOTORS AND MONSTERS 
 
Brittany Paddock, Recreation Superintendent, introduced a new event called Motors and 
Monsters. This is a community car show and trunk-or-treat event taking place on October 10 
from 11 AM to 4:30 PM. The plan includes road closures and no parking zones to accommodate 
these activities. Key closures include parts of Nelson Street from 8 AM to 6 PM, with certain 
intersections remaining open for emergency vehicle access. The event is free to attend for local 
community members, the $20 car entry fee will go towards Mayor’s Christmas Tree Fund to 
contribute to local families in need during the holiday season. There will be an owl 
demonstration for families while kids can enter the Halloween costume contest.  Taco Via will 
have food available for purchase starting at 11 AM and live music will begin at Noon. Families 
can then participate in trunk-or-treating with the cars entered into the contest and even cast 
their own vote for the “People’s Choice” car award. There will be additional flyers for 
information and registering, if car show participants would like to guarantee their spot.  
 
Ms. Linn stated she is interested to see what the turn out this event will be.  
 
Ms. Paddock stated staff will notify surrounding residents who would be affected, as well as the 
Johnson County Sheriff’s Office, Johnson County Consolidated Fire District #1 and Johnson 
County Med-Act of the proposed closings. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker motioned to approve, seconded by Councilmember Lewis, 
business action item thirteen. The motion carried 5-0 by the following vote: 
 
Yes: Longanecker, Lewis, Lebakken, Conus, Malloy

14. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 09-10-26B FOR TEMPORARY NO PARKING 
RELATED TO MOTORS & MONSTERS 
 
Ms. Paddock discussed Resolution No. 09-10-26B, focusing on establishing temporary no 
parking zones for the Motors and Monsters event on October 10, 2026. The no parking zones 
are to support the event logistics while ensuring emergency vehicles have access. These zones 
will be effective from 7 AM to 6 PM on the event day. Staff asked Council to decide how to best 
assess the parking on the Horseshoe near Nelson Street, considering its proximity to the car 
show site. The council debated whether to implement parking restrictions on just one side of 
the street or leave it open this year to observe the outcome. They decided to leave it open this 
year to not inconvenience residents who already park on both sides of the road. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker motioned to approve, seconded by Councilmember Malloy, 
business action item fourteen with the exception to remove the restrictions on lines 13 through 
15 on Resolution 09-10-26B. The motion carried 5-0 by the following vote: 
 
Yes: Longanecker, Lewis, Lebakken, Conus, Malloy
