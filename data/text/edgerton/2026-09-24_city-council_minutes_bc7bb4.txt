8. Public Comments.  
 
Clara White, an Edgerton resident, expressed concerns about alleged foreign influence in local 
activism related to anti-AI and data center activities. She highlighted a network reportedly 
funded by Neville Roy Singham, who is under federal investigation for potential tax crimes and 
violations influencing these activities. Ms. White pointed out the use of fake online accounts 
promoting false narratives to stall US technology. She emphasized the positive potential of AI in 
various sectors and cautioned against losing American freedom to advancing foreign 
technological influence.  
 
William LaFalce, an Edgerton resident, raised concerns about high salaries paid to City 
leadership staff in Edgerton, which he believes are excessive for a community of approximately 
1,924 residents. He compared these salaries to those of federal employees in demanding 
positions, noting that the compensation for the city staff exceeds what many professionals earn 
at similar levels. Mr. LaFalce pointed out that while government salaries distort the perceived 
financial well-being of the community, some families still rely on food assistance. Mr. LaFalce 
called for transparency regarding the source of revenue funding these salaries and the financial 
relationships between the city and various development organizations.  
 
Carrie Schmidt, a Gardner resident, criticized the process used to add data centers to the zoning 
rules without public input, alleging this violated legal statutes. She accused city staff and 
developers of having undue influence over city decisions, bypassing normal procedures such as 
public hearings and city council votes. Ms. Schmidt expressed frustration with the city 
leadership, accusing them of not acting in the interests of Edgerton residents and allowing 
external entities to control key decisions. She called this a lack of transparency and 
accountability in the process.

9. Presentation and Distribution of Pavement Condition Index (PCI) 
 
Dan Merkh, Public Works Director, and Rhoderic Montgomery, CIP Project Manager, presented 
this item. The Citywide Pavement Condition Index (PCI) score is currently 86, which is 
excellent. This score signifies that 88% of street miles are rated as good or excellent. City staff 
reviewed maintenance strategies, focusing on the importance of timely interventions to manage 
costs effectively, and explained deterioration curves that show how roads degrade over time. 
He gave Council a handout to help them determine what PCI really means on the streets. 
  
Councilmember Longanecker stated he drove those roads. Below 70 PCI really needed repairs. 
It won’t take long to drive the score of 86 down. 
 
Mr. Merkh gave a sample scenario, because one question came up of how long these scores 
last. Hypothetically, with a roadway that starts at 100 when it gets built, it has a period which 
hits degradation. For instance the chip-seal treatment that was just completed in the last couple 
of weeks would bring the PCI back up around roughly 5 to 10 points and it’ll help keep the 
roadway in better condition for the next five or so years. Like all industry averages, there’s a lot 
of parameters that fall into that. Hard winters make it shorter, soft winters make pavement last 
a lot longer. When you hit another 5 years or so, then we hit a thin overlay, like a UBAS and 
then it would give us approximately 7 – 12 years. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker asked if there were any roads as poor as the area rated 27 on 3rd 
Street.  
Mr. Merkh stated no, nothing near that severity. 88% if our miles rate at good or better. We did 
some research to find what other cities’ averages and goals were, 65-75 where most 
recommend. Staff recommendations fall to a range of 70-74. When we get your range 
recommendation, we will be able to prepare and build a street preservation program annually 
and identify any limitations to the goal. This can grow and change as often as council have the 
direction to do so. 
 
Mayor asked how often the PCI takes place. 
 
Mr. Merkh stated that it is every 5 years. 
 
Councilmember Longanecker is comfortable with staff recommendations. Bad streets are 
terrible, especially in the wintertime. He stated that we could always revisit this if needed.

10. CONSIDER PROJECT CLOSEOUT FOR THE 2026 STREET PRESERVATION 
PROGRAM 
 
Dan Merkh, Public Works Director, stated the 2026 Street Preservation Program includes 
three categories of work: the Concrete Project, the Pavement Marking Project, and Chip 
Seal Project. On May 28, 2026, NewCo Concrete LLC was awarded the construction 
contract for the Concrete Project. The final construction for all three projects is 
$131,214.75. After all project costs, this project is within budget, with the remaining 
$15,785.25 recommended to be moved to the 2027 Street Preservation Project.
