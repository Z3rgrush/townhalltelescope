To: Board of Trustees 
 
From: Joseph Rivera 
 
RE: AN ORDINANCE OF THE TOWN OF FAIRPLAY, COLORADO REPEALING 
AND RE-ENACTING SECTION 8-72 OF THE FAIRPLAY MUNICIPAL CODE 
TO ADJUST THE PENALTIES FOR SPEEDING VIOLATIONS DETECTED BY 
THE TOWN’S AUTOMATED VEHICLE IDENTIFICATION SYSTEM 
DATE: November 2, 2026 
 
 
The Colorado General Assembly intended the enforcement of AV I S  citations to be uniform 
across Colorado. C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(1) (“The general assembly hereby finds and declares that 
the enforcement of traffic laws through the use of automated vehicle identification systems under 
this section is a matter of statewide concern and is an area in which uniform state standards 
are necessary.”  
 
The Town must publish the number of AVIS citations and amount of revenue collected 
through AVIS citations. C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(2)(g)(IV) (“The state, a county, a city and county, 
or a municipality implementing an automated vehicle identification corridor pursuant to 
subsection (2)(g)(I) of this section shall annually publish a report on its website disclosing the 
number of citations and amount of revenue generated by the automated vehicle 
identification corridor.”). The Town has two (2) AVIS corridors. See below.  
 
Under AVIS, the pursuit of the collection of fines requires personal service. C.R.S. § 42-4-
110.5(2)(a)(IX) (“The state, a county, a city and county, or a municipality shall not initiate or 
pursue a collection action against a registered owner of a motor vehicle for a debt resulting from 
an unpaid penalty assessed pursuant to this section unless the registered owner is personally 
served the notice of violation or the final order of liability.”). Because AVIS violators are not 
stopped by Fairplay PD, AVIS violators are not personally served.  
 
Colorado law establishes a uniform penalty scheme. AV I S  penalties are determined by the 
vehicle’s speed, number of previous AVIS citations, and whether the offense occurred in a 
School Zone or Construction Zone. 
 
 
 
 
Page 5 of 78
• penalties for traveling less than six MPH over speed limit 
 
If a vehicle travels less than 6 miles per hour over the posted speed limit, then the 
Town may mail the registered owner a warning regarding the violation. C.R.S. § 42-
4-110.5(4)(a)(I). 
 
If a vehicle travels less than 6 miles per hour over the posted speed limit, and the 
violation occurs in a School Zone or a Construction Zone, then the Town may impose 
a penalty of up to $40, including any surcharge. C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(4)(a)(I). 
 
• penalties for traveling six to nine MPH over speed limit 
 
If a vehicle is traveling at least six and less than ten miles per hour over the posted 
speed limit, and the violation is the first speeding violation by the registered owner 
that the Town has detected using an automated vehicle identification system, the 
Town shall mail the registered owner a warning regarding the violation and shall not 
impose a penalty or surcharge for such first violation. C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(4)(a)(II). 
 
If a vehicle travels at least six and less than ten miles per hour over the posted speed 
limit, and the violation is a second or subsequent speeding violation, then the Town 
may impose a penalty of up to $40, including any surcharge.  C.R.S. § 42-4-
110.5(4)(b)(I)(A). 
 
If a vehicle travels at least six and less than ten miles per hour over the posted speed 
limit, and the violation occurs in a School Zone or a Construction Zone, then the 
Town may impose a penalty of up to $40, including any surcharge. C.R.S. § 42-4-
110.5(4)(a)(III). 
 
If a vehicle is traveling at least six and less than ten miles per hour over the posted 
speed limit, and the violation is a second or subsequent speeding violation, and the 
violation occurs in a School Zone or a Construction Zone, then the Town may 
impose a penalty of up to $80, including any surcharge. C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(4)(b)(II).  
 
• penalties for traveling ten to twenty-four MPH over speed limit 
 
If a vehicle travels at least ten and less than twenty-five miles per hour over the 
posted speed limit, then the Town may impose a penalty of up to $40, including any 
surcharge. C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(4)(b)(I)(A).  
 
If a vehicle travels at least ten and less than twenty-five miles per hour over the 
posted speed limit, and the violation occurs in a School Zone or a Construction 
Zone, then the Town may impose a penalty of up to $80, including any surcharge. 
C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(4)(b)(II).

ORDINANCE NO. 6 
(Series of 2026) 
 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE TOWN OF FAIRPLAY, COLORADO REPEALING 
AND RE-ENACTING SECTION 8-72 OF THE FAIRPLAY MUNICIPAL CODE 
TO ADJUST THE PENALTIES FOR SPEEDING VIOLATIONS DETECTED BY 
THE TOWN’S AUTOMATED VEHICLE IDENTIFICATION SYSTEM 
WHEREAS, the Town of Fairplay, Colorado ("Town") is a statutory town, duly organized and 
existing under the laws of the State of Colorado; and 
WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 31 -15-401, the Town by and through its Board of Trustees 
("Board"), possesses the authority to adopt laws and ordinances within its police power in 
furtherance of the public health, safety and welfare; and 
WHEREAS, pursuant to this authority, the Board previously adopted Article 4 of Chapter 8 of 
the Fairplay Municipal Code (“Code”) and established an automated vehicle identification 
system (“AVIS”) enforcement system within the Town to detect certain traffic violations; and 
WHEREAS, subsequent to the Board’s adoption of Article 4, Chapter 8, the State of Colorado 
updated the penalties and rules relating to enforcement of AVIS detected speeding violations ; 
and 
WHEREAS, the Board finds, determines, and declares that the adoption of updated penalties 
for AVIS detected speeding violations is in the best interests of the citizens of the Town and 
furthers the protection of the public health, safety, and welfare. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE 
TOWN OF FAIRPLAY, COLORADO AS FOLLOWS: 
Section 1. Section 8-72 of the Code, concerning speeding violations detected by automated 
vehicle identification systems, is hereby repealed and replaced to read in its entirety as follows: 
Sec. 8-72 – Speeding. 
(a) Violations and Civil Penalties. The Town is hereby authorized to detect 
speeding violations and issue various civil penalties for same in accordance 
with the limitations and authorizations provided by C.R.S. § 42-4-110.5(4)(a)–
(c). 
(b) Signage. The Town shall place an appropriate temporary or permanent sign 
in conspicuous place not fewer than 300 feet before the area in which the 
automated vehicle identification system is to be used to notify the public that 
an automated vehicle identification system is in use immediately ahead. 
 
Section 2. Safety Clause. The Town Board hereby finds, determines and declares that this 
Ordinance is promulgated under the general police power of the Town of Fairplay, that it is 
Page 9 of 78
  
promulgated for the health, safety and welfare of the public, and that this Ordinance is 
necessary for the preservation of health and safety and for the protection of public 
convenience and welfare. The Board further determines that the Ordinance bears a r ational 
relation to the proper legislative object sought to be obtained. 
Section 3. Severability. Should any one or more sections or provisions of this Ordinance or 
of the Code provisions be judicially determined invalid or unenforceable, such judgment shall 
not affect, impair or invalidate the remaining provisions of this Ordinance or of such Code 
provisions, the intention being that the various sections and provisions are severable. 
Section 4. This Ordinance shall become effective 30 days following publication.

Background/Analysis: 
November 16 is the 2nd Public Hearing on the 2026FY Amended and 2027FY Proposed Budget. The 
Mayor will open the hearing that was continued on November 2, request Staff’s presentation, 
accept public input and vote to continue the hearing to December 7, 2026 for final adoption. 
 
At this meeting, Staff will review updates to revenues and expenses, and the Board will discuss and 
provide direction to Staff on which capital expenditures (programs and equipment) and capital 
projects (paving, renovations, etc) to include in the 2027FY budget. 
 
At this time, Plante Moran has already preliminarily reviewed the new spreadsheet format that Kelly 
created and where numbers are pulling from, and they will begin final review following our meeting 
on November 16 in order to confirm all calculations and help in preparation of finalizing the 
associated resolutions for adoption on December 7. 
 
As always, Staff invites the Board and members of the public to stop by Town Hall at any time to 
review the budget detail and ask questions or provide input.  Our goal is for complete and total 
transparency and for a budget that addresses the needs and desires of our community. 
 
Recommendation: 
Open the publichearing that was held and continued on November 2, request Staff’s presentation, 
and accept public input, discuss various matters and then provide direction to Staff as necessary. 
Upon completion of the public hearing for the evening, the Baord should then conduct a vote to 
continue the hearing to December 7, 2026 for final adoption.

ARTICLE II — AUTHORITY AND PURPOSE 
2.1 Authority. This Agreement is entered into pursuant to Article XIV, Section 18(2) of 
the Colorado Constitution and §§ 29-1-201 through 29-1-205, C.R.S., which authorize 
political subdivisions of the State of Colorado to cooperate and contract with one 
another to provide any function, service, or facility lawfully authorized to each of the 
cooperating or contracting parties. 
2.2 Purpose. The purposes of this Agreement are to: 
(a) Establish a framework for the cooperative operation, administration, and financial 
support of the PCCC; 
(b) Provide for the delivery of high-quality, reliable, and efficient emergency services, 
dispatch to the Participating Members; 
(c) Ensure the continuation of PCCC services in a manner that promotes public safety 
and the effective coordination of emergency response resources; 
(d) Define the roles, responsibilities, and obligations of the Participating Members with 
respect to the funding, and operation of the PCCC; 
(e) Promote compliance with all applicable federal, state, and local laws, regulations, 
and standards governing emergency communications and criminal justice information 
systems. 
ARTICLE III — SERVICES 
Page 37 of 78
Page 4 of 19 
3.1 Scope of Services. The PCCC shall provide the following Services to the 
Participating Members: 
(a) Twenty-four-hour emergency communications and dispatch services for fire, 
emergency medical services, law enforcement, and other public safety agencies; 
(b) Receipt and processing of emergency calls, including 911 calls and administrative 
calls for service; 
(c) Dispatch of appropriate emergency response resources in accordance with 
established protocols and procedures; 
(d) Access to and management of federal, state, and local criminal justice information 
systems, including National Crime Information Center (NCIC), Colorado Crime 
Information Center (CCIC), and other systems subject to CJIS requirements; 
(e) Radio communications support and coordination for emergency response 
operations; 
(f) Maintenance of computer-aided dispatch (CAD) and records management systems; 
(g) Call-taking and dispatch quality assurance and training programs; and 
(h) Coordinate radios, radio channels, communication towers, and related 
communications support services, are assigned to Park County after considering 
Council input and remain subject to appropriations, available resources, and County 
operational authority. This is a defined County-control function.  
3.2 Services to Non-Members. Subject to available capacity and resources, the PCCC 
may provide Services to public safety agencies that are not Participating Members, 
provided that: 
(a) The provision of such services does not adversely affect the quality or availability of 
Services to Participating Members; 
(b) The non-member agency enters into a separate written agreement with The Park 
County Board of County Commissioners, governing the terms of the dispatch services; 
and 
(c) The provision of services to non-members is approved by Park County through its 
authorized officials.

EXHIBIT A 
DISPATCH FEE 
The Dispatch Fee shall be $50.00 per billable dispatched incident attributable to a Fee-
Paying Special District. For purposes of invoicing, a billable dispatched incident is one 
unique incident to which the PCCC dispatches that District, regardless of the number of 
units dispatched by that District to the same incident. If more than one Fee-Paying 
Special District is dispatched to an incident the agency responsible for the incident or 
call nature will be charged.    
DISPATCH CALL TRACKING 
The PCCC will provide a report of the specific calls dispatched to the Special District for 
the fiscal calendar quarter with the DISPATCH FEE invoice.  
The invoice will reference the quarter, the number of calls dispatched and the per call 
fee.  
RECORD MANAGEMENT FEE 
The Record Management Fee shall be $50.00 for each entry into the CJIS/NCIC and a 
CJIS record completed and filed by the PCCC on behalf of the Fairplay Police 
Department. For purposes of invoicing, a billable CJIS record filing report will be 
provided with the invoice.  
CJIS FILED REPORT TRACKING 
The PCCC will provide a report of the specific calls answered where a CJIS report is 
taken and filed on behalf of the Fairplay Police Department for the fiscal calendar 
quarter invoice.  
The invoice will reference the quarter, the number of CJIS reports filed and the per call 
fee.
