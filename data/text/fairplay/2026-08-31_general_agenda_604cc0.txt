 C. Presentation from Plante Moran regarding Government Accounting and Budgeting.  
Amanda Karger, Accounting Manager at Plante Moran, provided a presentation on government accounting, 
budgeting, and best practices in preparation for the Town's upcoming 2027 budget process. The presentation 
emphasized that the municipal budget serves as both a legal authorization to spend public funds and  a 
management tool for establishing priorities and planning for future needs. The Board was advised to consider 
Page 12 of 116
 
long-term financial forecasting, community priorities, measurable results and outcomes, appropriate fund 
balances, financial policies, and public engagement when developing the budget.  
 
Karger discussed fund balance considerations, noting that the statutory emergency reserve is 3% of applicable 
operating expenditures and that approximately two months, or 16%, of expenditures is generally considered a 
healthy baseline for fund balance. The Board also discussed the use of fund balance for significant, one-time 
expenses and the importance of maintaining sufficient reserves for unexpected costs. 
 
The presentation also emphasized matching one-time revenues with one-time expenditures and avoiding the 
use of temporary revenues to support ongoing operating costs. The Board also discussed establishing 
additional financial policies and reserve targets to provide guidance duri ng the budget development process. 
 
Karger also touched on priority-based budgeting, which would provide an additional framework for aligning 
Town expenditures with community priorities and desired outcomes. She  recommended a phased approach 
rather than attempting a full implementation in one year, noting that implementation could take approximately 
12–24 months. The process would include identifying community priorities, evaluating programs and services, 
quantifying costs, and developing a scoring framework to assess alignment with priorities. 
 
The Board discussed the timing of the 2027 budget process and expressed interest in receiving an early 
indication of the Town's financial position and potential budget gap before the fall budget adoption period. Staff 
indicated that financial information would be provided as the year progressed  and that Board priorities should 
be identified before staff finalized proposed budget figures. 
 
The discussion also identified water and sewer infrastructure as significant upcoming needs, including potential 
improvements associated with the wastewater lagoon and other infrastructure deficiencies. Staff noted that 
capital improvement planning was in process, and that additional evaluation of funding and potential revenue 
needs may be required. The Board and Staff discussed the value of maintaining an unfunded -needs list to track 
projects and prioritize future expenditures while protecting fund balance. 
 
The Board thanked Karger for the presentation and information and Sciacca recognized and also expressed 
sincere appreciation for all Plante Moran’s support and oversight of the Town’s finances and accounting 
systems. Karger also expressed appreciation for the relationship and indicated significant improvements had 
been made in the past 2 years.

XI. Other Business  
 
 A. Discussion of the municipal utilities impact fee report, parks impact fee report, law enforcement impact 
fee report, storm drainage impact fee report, and streets impact fee report and next steps.  
The Board received a presentation from Deron Dircksen and Zach Wolken of SGM regarding proposed impact 
fees for municipal facilities, parks, law enforcement, storm drainage, and streets. Dircksen explained that 
impact fees are intended to offset the impacts of new development and must maintain a direct relationship to 
the impacts created, with collected funds segregated and used only for the specific purposes for which they are 
assessed. He explained that the study uses residential square-footage tiers and existing Town assets/capacity 
to calculate the maximum defensible impact fee for each category. The Board discussed existing fees in the 
Town Code, including park and street-related fees, as well as the Town's inclusionary/affordable housing 
requirements. Members emphasized the need to balance having new development contributes  toward 
infrastructure costs while maintaining sustainable growth and avoiding unnecessarily high costs for 
development. They generally agreed that outdated or duplicative provisions should be reviewed as part of the 
Town's broader code and development regulation updates. No action was taken to adopt the proposed impact 
fees and Staff recommended the Board continue reviewing the study and consider the fees as part of the Unified 
Development Code update and budget process. Staff reminded the Board that the Impact Fee Study was 
partially funded through a $25,000 DOLA grant.  
 
 B. Discussion regarding potential donation of parcel R0001381 to Boys & Girls Club of the High Rockies.  
Town Administrator Sciacca presented a verbal inquiry from the Boys & Girls Club regarding the potential donation 
of a Town-owned parcel adjacent to the Club’s existing property. She reported t he Club would be conducting a 
capital campaign for a new facility and indicated that ownership of the additional parcel could strengthen its 
position when seeking grants and other funding. The Board discussed potential safeguards, including a 
reversionary clause requiring the property to return to the Town if specified development conditions were not met. 
A long-term lease was also discussed as an alternative to conveying ownership. The Board indicated that it was 
willing to further consider the request but requested additional information regarding the Club’s existing debt, 
property ownership, fu nding plans, and the potential acquisition of an additional parcel from a private property 
owner. Staff will continue discussions with the Boys & Girls Club and will consult with the Town Attorney regarding 
available options and appropriate conditions.  
 
 C. Discussion regarding potential purchase of parcel R0050215 from Dale & Katherine Fitting.  
Town Administrator Sciacca reported that Staff was proposing the Town acquire a parcel located across from 
the Visitor Center that the Town has historically used for events. She added that t he property owner indicated a 
willingness to sell and a recent valuation discussion placed the estimated market value in the range of 
approximately $70,000–$90,000. She further explained that acquiring the parcel would allow the Town to 
incorporate it into an existing paving project and provide additional flexibility for future public uses, including 
perpetual event space.  
 
MOTION: To authorize the Town Administrator to make an offer for purchase of the property in an amount 
between $65,000 and $75,000 was made by Trustee Permakoff  and seconded by Trustee Baum . Passed 
unanimously.

PROVISION TO COMPLY WITH RECENT COLORADO SUPREME COURT CASE LAW AND TO DECRIMINALIZE 
TOWN CODE VIOLATIONS."? (L) 
Mayor Douglas introduced Ordinance No. 3, Series of 2026, and provided a brief summary of the format of the 
public hearing.  
 
Town Attorney Rivera advised Ordnance No. 3 would amend the Town’s general penalty provisions to comply 
with recent Colorado Supreme Court case law and House Bill 26 -1134. He explained that recent legal changes 
limit municipal penalties for offenses that parallel state offenses and require municipalities to provide public 
defense when incarceration remains a potential penalty. The proposed ordinance would therefore align 
municipal penalties with applicable state penalties and remove the possibility of jail time for Town code 
violations, effectively decriminalizing those violations. Discussions ensued about the potential costs and 
administrative implications of retaining incarceration as a penalty, including the Town’s responsibility to provide 
a public defender and potential costs associated with incarceration in the county jail. Staff noted that serious 
offenses are generally referred to County Court and that incarceration for Town code violations is uncommon. 
 
Staff also updated the Board regarding new requirements for municipal court proceedings to be accessible by 
video. The Town will provide a link on its website allowing the public to view municipal court proceedings 
remotely. 
 
Mayor Douglas opened the floor to public comment. Questions were raised regarding the public defender 
requirement and whether the cost would be borne by the Town. The Town Attorney confirmed that, if 
incarceration remained a potential penalty, the Town would be responsible for providing appropriate public 
defense services. Douglas closed the floor to public comment and advised again that Trustee Baum has lodged 
a request to have the ordinance continued. 
 
MOTION: To continue Ordinance No. 3, Series of 2026, to July 20, 2026 to allow for additional review time and 
clarifications as discussed was made by Trustee Permakoff and seconded by Mayor Pro Tem Bossert. Passed 
unanimously.

consistent with the Town Center Overlay Design Standards which are attached to this report for 
reference. Per the Design Standards, the following statements are relevant to the Town Board of 
Trustees’ review: 
The Fairplay Town Center Overlay District has been established to ensure that high 
standards of design are maintained to protect the aesthetic qualities of Fairplay’s historic 
building stock. 
And,  
The Design Standards serve as a tool for preserving the Town Center by informing property 
owners and builders about compatible and appropriate design for new construction within 
the District. The Standards also provide design expectation for additions and alterations 
made to the District’s historic buildings and give property owners direction in preserving 
their historic commercial buildings and houses. 
The Design Standards do not address color schemes or repainting within the District. The 
standards are aimed at new construction and additions/remodels to existing buildings to

Standards
