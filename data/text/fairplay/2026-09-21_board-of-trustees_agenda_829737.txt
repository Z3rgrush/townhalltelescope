Staff Memorandum  
To: Board of Trustees 
From: Kelsey Sprys, Special Events Coordinator 
RE: Consider Burro Days Funding Request Application from South Park School District RE-
2 for Nature-Based Learning Program. (A) 
Date: 9/21/2026 
 
 
Background/Analysis: 
The Town of Fairplay established the Burro Days Funding program in 2005 as a way to event donate 
proceeds back to the local School to support services and programs or fund projects that the 
School District might not have the financial ability to. Since 2005, the Town has provided funding in 
the amount of $158,377.17 to South Park Schools as shown in the attached table.  
 
For 2026, the Town solicited applications and Superintendent Sellers responded advising that the 
School felt one program was most important to support, and they are therefore requesting funding 
for the Nature-Based Learning program, lead by Taya Mastrobuono. The request for $5,000 is 
attached and would help purchase a green house, shaded seating, plants and other outdoor 
classroom resources. 
 
Town Staff supports the request and recommends approval of the request. We also included a 
copy of the application form this year so new Board Members have this information and are better 
informed for future years when requests come in. 
 
Recommendation: 
$5,000 in funding was included in the budget this year for the Burro Days program and Staff 
recommends approval of the funding fo the South Park School District for the Nature-Based 
Learning program as requested by motion, second and a roll-call vote.

Burro Days Funding Criteria 
 
When first starting the Burro Days funding process back in 2006 our committee agreed that we 
wanted our proceeds after expenses to go to the school. They are a huge supporter of Burro Days 
and that is where most of our 50+ volunteers come from each year.  
Historically we have looked for the following things when funding requests: 
• How many students will the request impact? We look for requests that impact as many 
students as possible. 
• Are we meeting a long-term goal or a short-term goal? The longer our money is put to 
use the better. 
• Is the request educational in nature? We tend to give more weight to educational vs. 
recreational or fun. Alternative Education and Special Educations requests will vary 
though depending on what those students need. 
• Has the staff member requested funds in the past and put to good use? 
• Is there another way for us to help other than just funding a request? 
• We have always funded the teachers/organizations directly, not the school because they 
spend their own money to get things for the students that are needed once their budgets 
are depleted. 
 
Our deadline to submit applications varies and we do try to give out funding checks before the 
school closes for Christmas break.

Staff Memorandum  
To: Board of Trustees 
From: Janell Sciacca, Town Administrator 
RE: Discussion regarding Park County's use of Town of Fairplay Water for wells 
contaminated by landfill. 
Date: 9/21/2026 
 
 
Background/Analysis: 
The Town became aware earlier this year that Park County was allowing a Bulk Water Service 
Company to take water from their Public Works shop on Castello / County Road 16 when Staff 
observed the truck going in and out of the Public Works yard. When asked about it, Town Staff was 
told this has been going on since Otober 2025 and the water is being delivered to two homeowners 
outside the Town limits where their wells were contaminated by the Park County landfill. When an 
inquiry was made to an Board member of Park County he indicated that he believe the Water 
Service Company was supposed to be providing the water.  However, that does not appear to be 
the case. 
 
To date, Park County Public Works Director Nick Bredsnajder has indicated the County has 
provided 24,000 gallons (2,400/month) of Town water to 248 and 435 Foxtail Lane since October 
2025. He has also indicated this will continue "indefinitely or until continued well testing indicates 
otherwise." 
 
Bredsnajder has also indicated the Town is a responsible party even though Park County assumed 
the lease and operation of the landfill from the Town in the 1970's.  I have shared the documents 
provide to me by Bredsnajder to Town Attorney Rivera for review and advised he may want to reach 
out to the County Attorney to further discuss the matter. 
 
Town Staff has additional concerns about how the truck is being filled.  I have asked three separate 
times for Bredsnajder to provide this information indication there could be a potential backflow 
issue and we needed to know how the fills were occurring so we could protect the integrity of our 
system, but no such information has been shared.  
 
In my responses to the County I have stressed that an agreement should have and needs to be 
worked out for this water usage and that the Town is certainly willing to work with them in the spirit 
of intergovernmental relations and for protection of public health, safety and welfare. However, 
they could not continue filling and paying in-town water rates if they are paying at all.  Since we do 
not know where and how they are filling the truck, these fills could be contributing to our water 
loss figures and they could receiving the water free of charge.

move forward, since the County is without a Manager and the Assistant Manager, who has been

Recommendation: 
There is no recommendation, and Staff is seeking direction from the Board.

Re: Closed Fairplay Landfill – Notice of Liability and Demand for Payment 
Dear Mr. Shoop: 
Davis Graham & Stubbs, on behalf of Park County, Colorado (“Park County”), is notifying the 
Bureau of Land Management (“BLM”), that BLM is a responsible party under the Comprehensive 
Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (“CERCLA”) at the former solid waste disposal 
site known as the “Closed Fairplay Landfill” (hereinafter referred to as the “Landfill”), located 
approximately one-half mile west of Highway 285 and three-fourths mile south of the Town of Fairplay, 
in Park County.
1  Park County has spent, and will continue to spend, public funds to investigate and 
respond to releases of hazardous substances at the Landfill.  Therefore, Park County has a claim for 
contribution from the BLM under section 113(f) of CERCLA to recover Park County’s past and future 
response costs related to the Landfill. 
 
Landfill Background 
 
The Landfill is located just north of the Foxtail Pines Heights Subdivision (“Subdivision”), which 
was under construction while the Landfill was operating.  Groundwater flow direction is to the east and 
the south of the Landfill.  The Landfill began operating in 1967 on land leased by the Town of Fairplay 
from the BLM.  The Landfill operated until it was permanently closed in the early-1990s. 
 
In Spring 2015, Park County initiated an investigation of the conditions at the Landfill under the 
oversight of the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (“CDPHE”).  In 2016, site 
activities were expanded to include onsite and offsite groundwater monitoring.  That monitoring 
detected the presence of 1,4-dioxane at concentrations in excess of the Colorado groundwater standard 
of .35 ug/l in certain Landfill-related monitoring wells and now in two residential water supply wells in 
the Subdivision.  Park County has provided the affected residents with an alternate drinking water 
supply.  Sampling and other response activities are ongoing.

Current Claims 
 
 On March 14, 2018, Park County received a Notice of Intent to Sue from Kaplan Kirsch & 
Rockwell, LLP on behalf of certain landowners in the Subdivision and the Foxtail Pine Heights Owners 
Association.  The Notice alleges claims arising under the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act, 
CERCLA, and the Clean Water Act regarding alleged groundwater contamination.  In October 2018, Park 
County entered into a Compliance Order on Consent with CDPHE to define and provide CDPHE oversight 
of the ongoing response actions at the Landfill.  In December 2019, CDPHE issued BLM a unilateral 
Compliance Order to the same effect.  Both orders established compliance requirements and criteria for 
closure and post-closure care of the Landfill. 
 
Explanation of BLM’s Potential Liability 
 
CERCLA allows a performing responsible party to sue other responsible parties in a cost recovery 
or contribution action.  Responsible parties include current and former owners and operators of a 
contaminated site, as well as persons who arranged for treatment and/or disposal of any hazardous 
substances found at the site, and persons who accepted hazardous substances for transport and 
selected the site to which the hazardous substances were delivered.  Park County believes that BLM is 
liable under section 107(a) of CERCLA with respect to the Landfill, as a generator of hazardous 
substances disposed of at the Landfill.   
 
Demand for Reimbursement 
 
Park County has claims under CERCLA against BLM and other responsible parties for its current 
and future response costs and pre-judgment interest.  The approximate total response costs incurred 
through at the Landfill through the year-end of 2018 are approximately $360,000.  Park County hereby 
makes a demand for payment from BLM and other responsible parties for the above amount, plus all 
interest authorized to be recovered under section 113 of CERCLA.  A list of the other responsible parties 
identified by Park County to receive notice of this claim is attached as Exhibit A. 
 
Please ask your legal counsel to contact me to discuss this notice and demand and to answer 
any questions.
