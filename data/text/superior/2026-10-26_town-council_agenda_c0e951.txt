Parks and Recreation Advisory Committee
(PARC)
Board Details
PARC meetings, agendas, notes, and videos
The purpose of the Parks and Recreation Advisory Committee (PARC) is to consider and provide input to the Town Council on
park and recreation programs, services, amenities, and facilities, as well as applicable Parks, Recreation and Open Space
Department projects. PARC is charged with advising the Town Council on the master plan addressing parks and recreation
services, goals and objectives for parks, recreation, and trails in the Comprehensive Plan, and work cooperatively with OSAC and
other advisory groups on open space and related information.
PARC was formerly called the Parks, Recreation, Open Space and Trails Advisory Committee (PROSTAC) and was created by the
Board of Trustees at their regular meeting on August 25, 2003; with the purpose of forming a long-range plan concerning parks,
trails, open space, and recreation within the Town. The committee’s name was changed to Parks and Recreation Advisory
Committee (PARC) by the Town Board on March 8, 2021.
Committee members are expected to attend and actively participate in one standing meeting each month, with an additional
time commitment of approximately 10–20 hours as needed based on committee demands.

General Instructions:
Superior residents interested in serving on a Town of Superior Advisory Committee
must complete this application. This form is designed to facilitate the application
process for any of the seven committees.  Appointments are made by the Superior
Town Council and applications are reviewed as vacancies arise. Interviews are required
as part of the selection process and scheduled periodically as appointments are
typically made quarterly, corresponding with Town Council meetings. You may apply to
multiple committees, however, once a vacancy opens, you must choose only one
committee to interview for. An advisory committee member shall be eligible to serve
two consecutive three-year terms. Following their second term, interested members
may then reapply if they wish to continue their committee membership.
Applicants must be residents of the Town of Superior, with the exception of
the Superior Historical Commission.
All applicants are asked to attend or virtually view at least one committee meeting to
become familiar with the advisory committee’s work plan, requirements, and processes
prior to being scheduled for an interview. All Town meetings are open to the public.
Committee meetings, agendas, notes, and videos can be found on the meetings and
agendas homepage.  Please also review the advisory committee handbook to ensure
there are no conflicts of interest and you are comfortable with all policies and protocols.
All committees and committee members must also comply with the Town’s Municipal
Code and State open government rules.

What do you see as most important for the present and future state of parks
and recreation services for the Town?
As Superior continues to grow and become more diverse, I believe our parks and recreation
services need to grow with the community. It’s important that we create welcoming,
inclusive, and accessible spaces and programs that reflect the needs of residents of all ages,
backgrounds, and abilities. I also believe we need to listen to our community, especially
voices that may not always be represented, and make sure everyone feels a sense of

our open spaces will be important. Ultimately, I want to see Superior continue to be a
community where everyone has opportunities to connect, recreate, and thrive.

Why do you want to participate on PARC?
I believe that one of the key, if not the key, attraction of Superior is its parks and access to
recreation. I live in a condo complex (Saddlebrook), and Town parks are my main
opportunity for spending time outdoors within the Town. I am particularly interested in

Park/the Rock Creek cycleway to access the Coalton Trail on my way to work at the National
Laboratory of the Rockies Flatirons Campus (located off of Highway 128 a mile or so east of

highlights of my job. Finally, my wife and I have recently had a son, and while he is not quite

bring him. I have spent 5 years living around and enjoying Superior's parks and recreation
opportunities, and I feel it's time I try to give back to the cause.
Question applies to Parks and Recreation Advisory Committee (PARC)
What do you see as most important for the present and future state of parks
and recreation services for the Town?
Expanding on the (already great) network of connected parks and green spaces so that
Superior kids can travel and play safely within the Town. As a cyclist and cycle commuter, I
am passionate about providing safe multiuse paths for children and adults to use. The Town
has done a great job in recent years building out paths along Coal Creek and Rock Creek,
along Highway 36, through Miner's Park, etc. I strongly appreciate this and would like to
expand this network to better connect to paths in the Coyote Ridge Open Space, across 88th
Street to Monarch High School and Louisville, and to the western parts of the Superior
Marketplace. Superior could become a model for bike and walk-friendly infrastructure! As
part of this connectivity, I'd also like to expand on recreation opportunities for older kids
within Superior's Parks, possibly by adding public sports courts (e.g. basketball courts, tennis
courts) and expanding the bike park/pump track in Autrey Park.

Background:  
In 2023, the Superior Cultural Arts and Public Spaces advisory committee (CAPS) worked with 
Town staff to hire 23.4 Degrees LLC, to engage Superior residents about the possibility of a 
Marshall Fire Memorial. In January 2024, the Marshall Fire Memorial Subcommittee was 
formed, comprised of representation from Superior’s advisory groups, to further engage residents 
and to deeply assess the collective data. In the fall of 2024, the subcommittee and project were 
renamed to Marshall Fire Remembrance Project (MFRP) and the group conceived of a potential 
location and concept plan for how a Remembrance might take shape. 
 
The MFRP subcommittee worked with staff on the scope of an RFQ for the design of a 
remembrance project that included a Place to Gather, Pet Remembrance, and Dispersed 
Remembrance Components, collectively called the Marshall Fire Remembrance Project (MFRP). 
Design firm finalists were interviewed and Davis Partnerships + LandDesign were selected to 
create a design concept. Town Council reviewed the preliminary design from the consultants in 
November 2025 with support to continue moving forward with the design and fundraising 
planning. 
 
In early 2026, the Town contacted Boulder County to initiate needed approvals for locating the 
MFRP in a Town-owned open space parcel that includes County Conservation Easement 
requirements. The County responded with a letter of general support, with some design 
parameters and the request to keep them informed on the progress of the project. The Town has 
completed a survey of the project site and geotechnical report, which have been shared with the 
design firm.  
 
Page 37 of 249
Town staff met with the City of Louisville Cultural Services staff in February 2026, to bring 
them up to date on the project and inquire about Louisville's interest and involvement in the 
project. Design concepts were shared with Louisville understanding that the Marshall Fire 
impacted all of our residents and the MFRP would be important to all. The project team of Davis 
Partnerships + LandDesign presented the MFRP concept design to Louisville City Council on 
August 11th, 2026. Louisville staff indicated they were conducting a public survey for residents 
to explore priorities for expenditure of Louisville City funds in support of Marshall Fire events 
and/or projects. The results of this survey were presented to the Louisville City Council on 
September 10th. Following the review of the survey results and feedback from the Louisville 
City Council, the City of Louisville is planning to allocate $25,000 to the Marshall Fire 
Remembrance project.  
Town staff began a search for a local non-profit to serve as the fiscal partner to collect and hold 
donations for the Marshall Fire Remembrance Project. After conducting inquires with qualified 
non-profits interested and capable of providing this service, the Colorado Gives Foundation was 
identified and selected as the best match to provide this service. This Fiscal Sponsorship 
Agreement outlines the scope of services to be provided by the Colorado Gives Foundation 
including: collecting, holding, and investing donated funds, providing appropriate 
communication and receipts to donors, and distribution of the funds to the Town for the 
implementation of the project. Once the fundraising is completed, the agreement with the 
Foundation will conclude and there is no provision for additional fundraising to support 
maintenance or any ongoing operational expenses for the Project. In the event that the Project is 
altered or canceled because of unforeseen circumstances or lack of funding, funds will be used 
for a similar project commemorating the Marshall Fire with final decision on the scope and form 
of any altered project to rest with Superior Town Council.  
 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommend approving the Fiscal Sponsorship Agreement with the Colorado Gives 
Foundation. 
 
Budget Implications: 
The Colorado Gives Foundation will invest the donated funds prudently and will allocate 
investment earnings and losses to the Marshall Fire Remembrance Disbursement Account. The 
Foundation may retain Administrative Fees up to 5% of the amount of all donations received.

Move to adopt a Resolution approving the Fiscal Sponsorship Agreement with the Colorado

Background:  
Town staff are exploring alternative funding through the Colorado Park and Wildlife (CPW) 
Non-Motorized Trail Grant program to complete planning documents for a segment of the 
McCaslin Multi-use Trail, a trail along the west side of McCaslin Blvd. The planning grant will 
support hiring a contractor to complete construction drawings and other pre-construction reports. 
The proposed trail extension will run along the west side of McCaslin Blvd. from the driveway 
of the Superior Water Treatment facility to High Plains Dr. The trail segment is envisioned to be 
an 8 ft concrete multi-use path to accommodate pedestrians and cyclists. This .35-mile trail 
extension is needed to improve the safety of pedestrians, cyclists and vehicles who now share the 
asphalt of McCaslin Blvd. which has steep slopes, limited sight-lines and higher vehicular 
speeds. McCaslin Blvd. was identified as having several areas of safety concerns in the 2025 
Vision Zero Action Plan. The CPW planning grant will fund survey, geotechnical testing, design 
and construction drawings needed to seek additional funding for future construction.  
 
The Town has already secured funds to construct the McCaslin Multi-use Trail from McCaslin 
and Rock Creek Parkway to the Town’s Water Treatment Facility. This segment will be 
constructed in 2027 through Congressionally Directed Spending funds already approved.  
 
The outcome of this project will include design for the .35-mile segment of the McCaslin Multi-
use Trail following site survey, engineering, environmental assessment and additional public 
engagement. The planning of the trail segment is anticipated to take place between Summer 2027 
to Spring 2028. The Town intends to pursue grant funding to support construction in 2029.
