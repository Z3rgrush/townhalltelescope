3. OSAC Discussion Items  
a. Pointe Strategies Coyote Ridge Recommendations (6:10) Presenters (Annie & 
Joe) provided an update on some of their recent activities. Notably, they had 
community engagement at the September 12th Chili and Beer Fest, and received 
considerable resident input. They also met with the Trails Subcommittee on 
September 30th where they reviewed the Final Trail Condition Report, incorporated 
an additional maintenance item and reviewed the final prioritization matrix. All trail 
designations and names were identified. Pointe Strategies emphasized their 
completion of Phase 1 of this project and that they will send Town Staff a final 
report and list of projects and priorities. Moving forward into Phase 2 of this project, 
the focus will be the implementation of certain items, i.e. Braiding and Erosion 
concerns (on Barn Swallow and Prickly Pear trails), build a bridge to improve the 
cattail crossing, and widening portions of Prairie Dog Trail to make it adaptive-
friendly. 
b. Staff & Town Council Liaison Updates (6:30) Council Member Mike Foster 
provided an update from the recent Town Council meeting, confirming the approval 
of the 2027 Budget. Included in this budget approval is the addition of a full time 
Park Ranger. Leslie Clark provided Town Staff updates. The town is moving to 
make improvements along the fenceline and gate of the 299 South 3rd Avenue 
Open Space parcel. The town is hosting a Volunteer Reception event on November 
12th at the Superior Civic Space at 6pm. Additionally, the town will commemorate 

the 5th Anniversary of the Marshall Fire on December 18th at the Superior 
Community Center, 6-9pm. 
c. Coyote Ridge Subcommittee Trail Clarification Recommendations (6:50) Todd 
Gleeson made a motion to approve the proposed Coyote Ridge Trail Names, Rita 
Trainor seconded the motion and it was approved unanimously by OSAC members.  
Next steps will include building trail maps and trail signs, coupled with a robust 
campaign from the town’s communications team to engage and inform residents. 
d. Prairie Dog Management Plan (6:55) Town staff is working with ERO to update 
the 2005 Prairie Dog Management Plan, with input from OSAC. The intent is to 
have a draft prepared for review by the end of November 2026. Amber Greves 
provided an update on the implementation of the Prairie Dog Playbook in regard to 
the Zaharias parcel and received unanimous confirmation from OSAC members 
that the committee’s intention remains to pursue re-location of the prairie dogs to 
the Harney Lastoka parcel. At this time, that’s the only viable relocation site. 
e. Wildfire Mitigation-Habitat Conservation Update (7:05) Leslie Clark informed 
OSAC of the ongoing work to update the Wildfire Mitigation Plan, developed by 
Bindel. The Plan currently exhibits the following significant shortcomings: Mowing to 
below 4” is recommended twice a year or more, depending upon growth; there is 
insufficient attention to seasonality; there is no acknowledgement that grazing must 
be carefully monitored to prevent negative impacts; it relies on calendar deadlines 
rather than providing guidance on how to tailor mitigation to accommodate 
variables; it does not address the importance of protecting drainages and riparian 
areas and the recognition that some mitigation strategies require reseeding efforts 
seems absent. Leslie proposed the following recommendations be filtered through 
a set of operational guidelines that reflect the following standards: Defensible strips; 
No blanket mowing; Seasonal wildlife protection; Mowing behavior; Grazing floor; 
Grazing utilization; Rotation and recovery; Riparian protection; Native restoration; 
Habitat trees. Leslie also provided an update on the status of the Goat Grazing 
Program. The FEMA HMGP funding program for vegetative fuels reduction is 
closing out, but the Town received funding through the BOCO Strategic Fuels 
Mitigation Grant for 3-years of goat grazing. In 2027, efforts will take place at 
Coyote Ridge and other small areas. In 2028, focused attention will take place at 
Autrey Park Open Space, Harney Lastoka and Vista Corridor. In 2029 efforts will be 
on Community Park Open Space, Fire Station Open Space and Castle Peak Open 
Space. 
f. OSAC Endorsement of McCaslin-High Plains Multi-Use Trail Proposal (7:15) 
The Town is applying for a grant through Colorado Park and Wildlife to fund the 
planning, design and survey work for a segment of trail along the west side of 
McCaslin. This CPW Non-Motorized Trail Grant is specifically for planning the 
segment of trail from the driveway of the Water Treatment Plant south to High 
Plains Drive. Town staff is asking for support from both OSAC and PARC for 
this important trail project. OSAC members discussed, and Todd Gleeson, (the 
OSAC Chair) signed the document in support of the effort.  
g. 2027 Trail Counter Recommendations (7:20) Committee members discussed 
the value trail counters provide in determining trail usage. There are three 
currently in place along Coyote Ridge. Todd Gleeson made a motion to 
purchase three additional counters from the 2027 Budget, Rita Trainor 
seconded that motion and it passed unanimously. OSAC members will decide 
in a future meeting the location of these new trail counters.  
h. Raptor Monitor Program Summary Discussion (7:35) Kara Neuse made a 
motion that OSAC approve the Town of Superior Raptor Monitoring 2025-2026 
Summary and recommend that it be delivered to the Town Council. Cristi 
Verbeck seconded that motion and it passed unanimously by OSAC members. 
i. 2027 Work Plan Discussion (7:40) OSAC members continued to make 
suggestions and updates to the 2027 Work Plan. The intent is to approve the 
plan for submission to the Town Council during the November OSAC meeting.  
4. Standing Updates (7:50)  
a. Raptor Program: Kara Neuse reported that the October 10th Raptor Walk was a 
success. Within a two-hour window, volunteers observed seven Red-tailed Hawks, 
two Ferruginous Hawks, one Golden Eagle, one juvenile Bald Eagle and one 
American Kestrel. The next walk will take place in November, date still to be 
determined. Kara also requested town staff assistance in mending one raptor nest. 
b. Coyote Ridge: No additional update.  
c. Wildlife Monitoring Program: No additional update.  
d. Town Wildlife Management Plan: Next subcommittee meeting is October 22. 
e. East County Open Space Liaison: The quarterly meeting will take place on 
Friday, October 16. 
5. OSAC Roundtable/New Business. Amber Greves mentioned a conversation she 
had with the proposed developer of the Zaharias parcel and indicated the town will be 
able to influence input concerning the surface type used in the design’s 8ft trail 
adjacent to the reservoir. OSAC will discuss further in a future meeting. 
Todd Gleeson raised the topic of the McCaslin-Rock Creek culvert and the need to 
improve drainage through the culvert to allow for wildlife movement under McCaslin 
during the wetter months of the year.  The wildlife monitoring team have used camera 
data to document animal movement during dry periods, but passage through the 
culvert when standing water is present.
