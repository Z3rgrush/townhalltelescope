GUEST REPORTS: 
Manager Roy Cue and Engineer Randy Hendrix with Lower Arkansas Water Management Association 
(LAWMA) presented on how water shares and allocations work through the state and how this affects the water 
shares the Town of Hugo owns. The Town owns common share, not preferred shares. The State Division of Water 
Resources sets the allocation amounts for every user in the state . The allocation is 20% this year. The Arkansas 
basin is in the worst shape it has ever been in since they have been keeping records. In 2013 the allocation was 
10%. At that time, the State Division Engineer deemed the Town a non -curtailable entity. The current Division 
Engineer has not asked for those designations for towns and cities. The Town of Hugo is being very proactive by 
trying to address these issues now. Noncompliance will result in wells being tagged by the Divion Engineers 
Office. Wells will be t agged monthly. We should continue to look for share s to lease and purchase. We should 
also look into converting our common shares into preferred shares with LAWMA. 
 
Troy McCue with Lincoln County Economic Development presented the monthly report highlighting Rural 
Philanthropy D ays July 8-10, 2026, EDCC Regional Roundtable is at the Fairgrounds Wednesday, April 29, 
SBDC is trying to find a new home within a Community College, the LCEDC Annual Meeting is April 22 at the 
Fairgrounds, the Governor was in Arriba to highlight Big Ben’s RV Park as a new Rural Jumpstart designee, and 
there will be a spring social media campaign for the new businesses in Hugo. 
Vicks came in and would like to offer the Town a lease for 1 preferred share for 1 year. They would like a 10 day 
period to come up with a fair market value. They will follow up with the Clerk Administrator.

TOWN OF HUGO 
 
CDBG PUBLIC HEARING ON 2025 WASTEWATER SYSTEM IMPROVEMENTS 
 
April 13, 2026 
 
• The Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) program, administered by the U.S. Department of 
Housing and Urban Development, provides federal funding to small communities for projects such as 
water, sewer, streets, and other public infrastructure, with the primary goal of benefiting low - and 
moderate-income (LMI) residents, improving public health and safety, and supporting overall community 
development. 
 
• The population of the Town of Hugo is 55.5% low-to-moderate income. 
 
• The Town of Hugo owns and operates a wastewater treatment facility (WWTF), built in the early 1960s. 
Much of the sanitary sewer system is still in its original condition.  The Town's sanitary sewer collection 
system consists of gravity -flow pipelines and lift stations that pump sewage to the WWTF.  The Town’s 
WWTF is a three-cell, stabilization, evaporative lagoon system.   
 
• The Town of Hugo has undertaken smoke testing, sewer cleaning, and videoing to determine the condition of 
its collection system.  The results identified one infiltration source, 42 inflow sources, and three possible 
blockages.  Inspected segments were in poor condition and require replacement.  Approximately one-third of 
the system was video-inspected.   
 
• This phase replaces pipelines in alleys between 4th –5th Streets (1st –4th Avenues) and 5th –6th Streets 
(1st–5th Avenues), totaling about 4,000 feet of pipe, 12 manholes, and approximately 80 service 
connections with full surface restoration.  
 
• The lift station, built with the WWTF in 2022, can connect to a portable generator but does not have a 
permanent standby unit. The Town is concerned about power reliability and sewer backup risk; therefore, 
the Town may install a permanent generator as part of this project. 
 
• The proposed improvements will be beneficial to the protection of public health as the replacement sewer 
main will prevent future sewer main collapses and sewage backup.  The estimated cost for the project is 
$2,167,000.
