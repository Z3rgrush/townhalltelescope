DEVELOPMENT OF DATA CENTERS IN NEIGHBORING JURISDICTIONS: 
The City’s land use authority is substantially limited outside Westminster’s city limits, with 
comprehensive planning and land use regulations only being applicable within Westminster’s city 
limits and in accordance with the City’s Home Rule Charter. However, there are three mechanisms 
the City may utilize to influence development in adjacent jurisdictions. 
Referrals
The primary means for influencing land use and associated entitlements outside the city is through 
the opportunity to comment on “referrals” from other jurisdictions, including Adams County, Arvada, 
Broomfield, Federal Heights, Jefferson County, Northglenn, and Thornton. A map of neighboring 
jurisdictions is included as Attachment 3. On occasion, these neighboring jurisdictions “refer” details 
of private development proposals, utility projects, and other capital improvement projects so 
Westminster City staff may review them for potential concerns.

Most referrals received by Westminster City staff are minor routine projects such as additions to 
residential homes or a zoning change to allow establishment of a new business; however, 
occasionally more impactful referrals are received. City staff makes recommendations on such 
matters and provides comments back to the referring agency; however, Staff feedback is received as 
a courtesy and there is no legal requirement for another jurisdiction to address Westminster’s 
feedback. For example, the City received a referral several years ago from Jefferson County 
regarding a proposal to develop an auto repair facility adjacent to a single-family residential 
neighborhood in Westminster. Staff responded to the referral with a respectful request that the 
developer consider adding additional dense landscaping and berming along the property line shared 
by the homes. In this case, the developer agreed to the request although not legally obligated to do 
so. 
Intergovernmental Agreements (IGAs)
The City has two IGAs in effect with adjacent jurisdictions that address land development. The first is 
an IGA with Thornton for the North Interstate 25 (I-25) area. This IGA previously included land use 
limitations, however it was amended several times, and the only remaining land use provision is that 
both Westminster and Thornton are to only approve development in compliance with their respective 
comprehensive plans. Another IGA is with Arvada, which establishes future boundaries for ultimate 
annexation for the Unincorporated Jefferson County area located west of Standley Lake, however the 
IGA does not include specific land use provisions. With the consent and agreement of another 
jurisdiction, the City may enter into an IGA to limit data venter development in the future. 
Utility Service in Neighboring Jurisdictions 
The City’s utility service regulations are found in Title VIII of W.M.C. and, in accordance with existing 
utility service agreements, the City provides water and wastewater service to certain areas outside of 
the city limits within the Utility Service Area as shown in Attachment 4.  These areas include an 
enclave in unincorporated Jefferson County (Jeffco), the City of Federal Heights (Federal Heights), 
and the unincorporated community of Shaw Heights, located in Adams County (Adco):
Jeffco Enclave
Jeffco enclave properties used to be within the Standley Lake Water and Sanitation District 
(SLWSD), but SLWSD was dissolved in 1971 with Westminster taking over the service 
area, see Attachment 5. This “dissolution” agreement established the City’s price premium 
of an additional 25 percent for out-of-City water service. When these out-of-City properties 
are developed and not already Westminster water/wastewater customers, the City has the 
option to require their annexation into the City as a condition of providing water service. 
This is most common when a single-family home with a failing well or septic seeks to 
connect to City services. It is not the City’s preference to force the annexation of a single-
family property under these circumstances, so the City typically allows the property to 
remain in Jeffco and connect to the City water and wastewater system. In such cases, the 
property owner is required to construct the infrastructure to City standards and must pay a 
premium charge for the services. To date, the extension of utility services outside of the 
City limits has been strictly limited to individual single-family homes. The continuation of 
this policy is supported by Public Works and Utilities Staff. 
Federal Heights
The 2007 agreement with Federal Heights is a Distributor’s Contract, which commits a 
specified amount of treated water to be conveyed through a master meter, see Attachment

DATA CENTER REGULATION IN UDC: 
Staff is currently finalizing the draft W.M.C. Title XI update for the Unified Development Code, 
commonly referred to as the UDC, which, if adopted by City Council, will update, revise, and 
consolidate various existing land use and design regulations in alignment with the Comprehensive 
Plan. In conjunction with this effort, Staff intends to add clarifying language regarding modern data 
centers to ensure that the current prohibitions are extended to the UDC with additional clarity. 
Specifically, Staff will include the following four key regulatory elements: 
1) Definitions for data centers that are consistent with State and national definitions to ensure that 
the City is in alignment with current industry standards regarding this rapidly evolving land use. 
Currently, the draft definitions are as follows:
Data center: A facility used primarily for the storage, management, processing, and 
transmission of digital data, which houses computer or network equipment, systems, servers, 
appliances and other associated components related to digital data operations. The facility 
may also include air handlers, power generators or similar back up power supply, water 
cooling and storage facilities, utility substations, and other associated utility infrastructure to 
support sustained operations. A data center may include but is not limited to the following:
oManaged data centers. Also known as colocation data centers, these third-party 
facilities provide a shared rented space for organizations who may lack the space, staff, 
or IT resources to own and operate their own data centers. 
 
Page     5     of     11115

oCloud (computing) data centers. Data center resources are hosted by a cloud 
services provider. These types of online service providers operate geographically 
distributed and interconnected data centers and allow multiple users to remotely access 
computing resources hosted by these data centers. 
oEdge data centers. Smaller data centers operated and maintained by cloud computing 
service providers physically closer to users for a number of benefits, including reduced 
network latency and data communication delay, faster content distribution, optimized 
workloads, and improved application performance and user experience. 
oHyperscale data centers. Massive data centers that are designed to support very 
large-scale IT infrastructure and so provide extreme scalability capabilities for purposes 
that include AI development, automation, and big data computing tasks. These facilities 
were developed in large part to address increasing demand for data storage and 
processing capacity.
2) Definitions for related non-data center uses to ensure they are clearly distinguished from data 
centers while recognizing other data processing operations that have historically been allowed 
and have contributed to the success of our business community. Currently, the draft definitions 
are as follows:
Data Processing Office: Offices where electronic data is processed by employees on the 
premises including, but not limited to, data entry, storage, conversion or analysis, subscription 
and credit card transaction processing, telephone sales and order collection, mail order and 
catalog sales, and mailing list preparation. A data processing office is not synonymous with a 
data center. 
Data Processing, Enterprise: An accessory space, room, or structure housing information 
technology equipment of limited size and intensity of operation that stores and processes a 
single organization’s data for the sole use of the business or tenant on that specific property. 
This accessory use is not synonymous with a data center.
3) UDC Subsection titled “Uses Strictly Prohibited.” This subsection will list uses that City Council 
intends to strictly prohibit in Westminster. A purpose statement will be included in this 
subsection to clarify that uses listed within are not allowed under any circumstances and the 
Planning Manager will not have the authority to allow a listed prohibited use using any type of 
codified discretionary authority that may exist elsewhere in the UDC. Data centers will be 
included in this subsection as strictly prohibited, along with any other uses that City Council 
identifies in the future. Data centers will not be included in the permitted use table.
4) Companion text amendment to W.M.C. Title VIII. – Health and Sanitation stating that municipal 
utility services shall not be provided for a data center regardless of jurisdiction.

Background Information:
From a historical perspective, data centers began as privately-owned, on-premises facilities housing 
traditional information technology (IT) infrastructure for the exclusive use of one company. In recent 
years, data centers have evolved into remote facilities or networks of facilities owned by cloud service 
providers (CSPs). These CSP data centers house virtualized IT infrastructure for the shared use of 
multiple companies and customers.
Although data centers date as far back as the 1940s, the modern era of data centers as we know 
them began in the mid-2000s with the onset of cloud computing and the subsequent opening of the 
first hyperscale data center in 2006 in The Dalles, Oregon. Data centers have rapidly evolved in 
recent years. New, hyperscale data centers are larger than traditional data centers, with some 
exceeding 10 million square feet, including the largest in the world in Hohhot, China, at 10.7 million 
square feet. However, the typical data center covers about 100,000 square feet, and recent data 
shows that the average size is trending upward as newer facilities come online. 
According to a March 5, 2026, State of Colorado Legislative Council staff memorandum titled “Data 
Center Impacts on the Environment, Public Health, and Energy Costs,” data centers fall into the 
following types, generally based on ownership, scale, and intended purpose (see Attachment 1):
Enterprise Data Centers: Private, on-premises data centers generally run by a large 
company. All IT infrastructure and data are hosted on premises. 
Managed Data Centers: Also known as colocation data centers, these third-party facilities 
provide a shared rented space for organizations who may lack the space, staff, or IT 
resources to own and operate their own data centers. 
Cloud (Computing) Data Centers: Data center resources are hosted by a cloud services 
provider (e.g., Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google Cloud Platform (GCP), Microsoft 
Azure, and IBM Cloud). These types of online service providers operate geographically 
distributed and interconnected data centers and allow multiple users to remotely access 
 
Page     1122     of     11115     
 
Page     1122     of     11115     

Page 3 of 5
computing resources hosted by these data centers. 
Edge Data Centers: Smaller data centers operated and maintained by cloud computing 
service providers physically closer to users for a number of benefits, including reduced 
network latency and data communication delay, faster content distribution, optimized 
workloads, and improved application performance and user experience. 
Hyperscale Data Centers: Massive data centers that are designed to support very large-
scale IT infrastructure and provide extreme scalability capabilities for purposes that include 
AI development, automation, and big data computing tasks. These facilities were 
developed in large part to address increasing demand for data storage and processing 
capacity.
Municipalities across the country have become increasingly concerned about the environmental 
impacts of data center development. These concerns are primarily in the areas of energy usage, 
water usage, air pollution, and noise pollution.  
For the purposes of this memorandum, Managed, Cloud, Edge, and Hyperscale data centers as 
defined above are hereby referred to as “modern data centers.” This recognizes that Enterprise data 
centers, which are private, on-premises facilities serving a single company on its campus, are 
considered an accessory use to the primary use allowed on the property. This intentionally 
distinguishes Enterprise data centers from modern data centers by acknowledging that modern data 
centers, regardless of size, scale, or function, are a primary, stand-alone use of a property that serves 
one or more clients that are not located on the site of the facility.

Comprehensive Plan Analysis
The 2040 Comprehensive Plan (Plan) accounts for the physical buildout of the City with goals and 
policies organized around four Plan Cornerstones: Thriving City, Healthy Places, Great 
Neighborhoods, and Managed Growth. 
In recognition of the City’s substantially built-out condition, the Plan’s goals seek to fulfill the needs of 
Westminster residents through policies to facilitate diversity in housing, experiential retail, and 
employment development.  
A key concern of City Council leading to adoption of the Plan was the City’s ability to remain within its 
existing utility resources and ensuring no single type of development uses a disproportionate share of 
such resources. These policy objectives are supplemented through establishment of several unique 
Land Use Character Areas that are used to define the City’s vision and intent for development 
citywide and provide overarching direction for land use on a specific site. Each Character Area 
includes a defined statement of intent and a general list of primary and secondary uses, built form 
and guidelines, and density limitations. According to Section 11-3-2(C), W.M.C., compliance with the 
Plan is mandatory. The Plan includes several non-residential Character Areas, each of which is 
distinct from the other in terms of land use: Commercial, Service Commercial, Commercial Mixed 
Use, Neighborhood Office, Employment-Flex, and Employment-Office/Institutional Campus. 
The following summarizes the allowed primary land uses in each of these non-residential Character 
Areas:  
Commercial: 
Retail Stores, Eating Establishments, Banks, Supermarkets, Professional Offices

The Plan does not define data centers and makes no reference to data centers as a land use. Based 
on the defined types of modern data centers referenced in this memorandum, there is no correlation 
between modern data centers and the permitted uses and Character Areas listed above. Modern data 
centers cannot be equated to retail stores, eating establishments, offices, medical facilities, research 
and development labs, or any of the uses contemplated in the Plan. Modern data centers do not 
provide substantial on-site employment and do not lend themselves to the placemaking policies 
embedded throughout the Plan’s policies. Therefore, Staff determines modern data centers are not 
compliant with the Plan. 
Zoning Analysis
W.M.C. Title XI – Land Development and Growth Procedures (Title XI) establishes all development-
related regulations, processes, and procedures for development of property in Westminster. This 
includes but is not limited to comprehensive planning and growth management, zoning, public 
improvements, site development standards, and building and fire codes. Title XI does not define or 
reference data centers as a land use. However, there is an undefined use called “data processing 
service” listed in the Permitted Use Table and allowed in the T1, B1, C1, C2, and M1 zoning districts. 
Although this use is not defined in the W.M.C., this is a reference to older, more historical types of 
data processing services that were in operation decades ago at the time of the original adoption of 
this Title XI and substantially predate modern data centers. This type of business is best defined as 
follows:
Data Processing Service: A facility where electronic data is processed by employees on site 
including, but not limited to, data entry, conversion or analysis, subscription and credit card 
transaction processing, telephone sales and order collection, mail order and catalog sales, and 
mailing list preparation.
Based on the above analysis, modern data centers do not fall within this land use category. 
Summary
Based on analysis of the Plan and the W.M.C., Staff concludes that modern data centers are not 
permitted in the City. Staff does not find that modern data centers are clearly identified as a land use 
or acknowledged as a permitted land use. Land use regulations are permissive by nature. The listing 
of a use as being allowed in any particular district shall be deemed to be an exclusion of such use

from any other district, unless such use is specifically allowed in the other district. Additionally, if a 
use is not listed as permitted, and the use does not fall into the definition of a listed use, it is 
considered prohibited. Modern data centers are not comparable to data processing centers and 
cannot be accepted as being aligned with this land use and are therefore a prohibited land use in 
Westminster. 
Staff is currently finalizing the draft Unified Development Code (UDC), which, if adopted by City 
Council, will update, revise, and consolidate various existing land use and design regulations in 
alignment with the Comprehensive Plan. In conjunction with this effort, Staff intends to add clarifying 
language regarding modern data centers to ensure that the current prohibitions are extended to the 
UDC with additional clarity.  
The attached resolution proposed for City Council’s consideration affirms the opinion of the Planning 
Manager that data centers are a not permitted land use within the City of Westminster as the use 
does not comply with the City’s Comprehensive Plan and is neither listed as, nor falls within the 
definition of, a use permitted under the Westminster Municipal Code.
