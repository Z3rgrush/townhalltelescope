On April 13, 2026, City Council enacted an ordinance establishing a six-month moratorium 
on the issuance of land use approvals, building permits, or business licenses for new event 
centers in multi-tenant commercial developments or within 300 feet of a residential district, 
residential dwelling unit, or another event center.

Does City Council wish to receive a presentation and provide feedback and direction regarding

City Council could choose not to receive a presentation or provide feedback to Staff regarding

Background Information:
Traditionally, event centers in the city have been smaller, community-focused venues. The intensity, 
size, and scope of these facilities have generally been limited, with the event centers primarily hosting 
smaller, private gatherings such as weddings, graduation celebrations, birthdays, business meetings, 
and conferences.
 
In recent years, the City has experienced a pronounced shift in the nature of event centers. 
Increasingly, venues are becoming larger and are hosting higher-intensity events such as concerts, 
indoor athletic competitions, other ticketed live entertainment events, and very large parties and 
celebrations, sometimes attended by hundreds of patrons. This shift has translated to greater 
demands on public safety operations, water and sewer infrastructure, and parking and traffic systems, 
as well as conflicts with neighboring businesses and land uses. In consideration of these impacts, on

March 23, 2026, City Council enacted a six-month moratorium on the issuance of land use approvals, 
building permits, or business licenses for new event centers in multi-tenant commercial 
developments, or where located within 300 feet of a residential district, residential dwelling unit, or 
another event center. This moratorium was enacted with Ordinance 4339 on April 13, 2026, and 
expires on October 13, 2026, see Attachment 1.
At the June 8, 2026, City Council meeting, Staff presented draft regulations to mitigate the impacts of 
this land use, see Attachment 2.  The goal was to regulate new event centers so they would be 
located in appropriate areas and be successful without causing concerns for neighboring uses and 
properties or undue stress on City services and infrastructure. Passage of this ordinance would have 
also lifted the moratorium. City Council elected to table this ordinance, and provided additional 
direction to Staff, which focused on 10 different topic areas.
At the July 6, 2026, Study Session, Staff provided responses, options, and alternatives to City Council 
based on the 10 areas identified for additional discussion, see Attachment 3. City Council provided 
additional direction and also asked Staff to engage with the Chamber to seek any feedback that 
member event center businesses might have. Council directed Staff to bring further analysis and 
alternative solutions back at a future study session.
Revised Event Center Ordinance
Per City Council direction, Staff partnered with the Chamber, which hosted a meeting with Staff and 
two of its member event center operators. At this meeting, Staff received comments and feedback on 
the June 8 draft event center ordinance. In general, the feedback received included suggestions to 
keep the new regulations simple and easy to understand, retain flexibility for event centers to host a 
variety of events including concerts and other live entertainment on occasion, and incorporate 
regulations that ensure event centers maintain appropriate security measures depending on the size 
and scale of event. Based on the direction Staff gathered from City Council, Chamber, and event 
center operator input, a revised ordinance was drafted to be simpler, provide reasonable flexibility to 
event center operators, and incorporate more direct oversight of event center land use approvals by 
City Council through a Special Use Permit process with clear criteria for City Council consideration, 
see Attachment 4. 
The revised ordinance includes the following elements:
A single definition encompassing all types of event centers has been created. Event 
centers have been separated from places of religious assembly;
Event centers would become a permissible type of Use by Special Permit (also known as 
Special Use Permits), and new event centers would require an approved Special Use 
Permit (SUP);
In the current W.M.C., SUPs are considered and acted upon by the Planning Commission.  
Event center SUPs, however, would be considered and acted upon by City Council;
Updated parking requirements based on the rated occupancy of the proposed event center;
Five mandatory requirements are proposed for all new event centers:
1. All applicable permits and licenses must be obtained prior to the event center operating;
2. The event center must comply with applicable portions of the W.M.C.;
3. The event center must provide the required parking;

4. The operation of the event center may not unreasonably interfere with the operation, 
use, or enjoyment of other properties or businesses;
5. The event center may generate no adverse off-site impacts due to dust, fumes, odors, 
smoke, vapor, noise, lights, or vibration.
Additionally, nine review criteria are proposed for City Council consideration of an event 
center SUP:
1. The proposed event center is compatible and harmonious with existing public and 
private development in the surrounding area.
2. The proposed event center is of an appropriate size and scale relative to surrounding 
uses.
3. The proposed event center is reasonably separated and buffered from surrounding land 
uses to ensure compatibility.
4. The proposed event center sufficiently mitigates or attenuates noise using methods 
such as sound walls along boundaries with residential or other incompatible land uses, 
soundproofing, or limitations on outdoor activity.  
5. The proposed event center appropriately limits the hours of operation.
6. The proposed event center prioritizes design elements such as location of entries; 
automobile, pedestrian, and transit connections to and through the site; and access to 
loading areas and trash receptacles to enhance compatibility with adjacent land uses.
7. The proposed event center includes appropriate standards to control outdoor 
amplification and light trespass.
8. The proposed event center provides a security plan that adequately addresses security 
of patrons and surrounding properties before, during, and after events.
9. The proposed event center provides a traffic control plan that anticipates and manages 
traffic before, during, and after events.
It should be noted that the requirements introduced with this proposed new ordinance will not affect 
existing properly licensed and operating event centers. Existing event centers may continue to 
operate in accordance with their current approvals and licenses. 
Staff welcomes any additional feedback and direction that City Council may elect to provide. The 
updated draft ordinance will be brought back to City Council for consideration at a future meeting, and 
if City Council approves the ordinance, the moratorium in place since March 23, 2026, would also be 
lifted.
The City’s Strategic Plan Priority of Healthy and Safe is supported by ensuring that event centers are 
properly managed, thus reducing the likelihood of adverse safety conditions. Vibrant and Diverse 
Economy is supported by ensuring that event centers are successful without becoming detrimental 
nuisances to neighboring businesses and residents. The Resilient Infrastructure priority is supported 
through ensuring that event centers are located and operated so as not to unduly impact City 
infrastructure and services.

Subject: Second Reading of Councillor’s Bill No. 9 Enacting a Six-Month Moratorium on 
the Issuance of Land Use Approvals, Building Permits, or Business Licenses for 
New Event Centers Meeting Certain Locational Criteria to Operate in the City of 
Westminster
Prepared By: Lindsey Kimball, CEcD, EDFP; Community Services DirectorJohn McConnell, Planning Manager
David German, AICP, Development Services Coordinator
Recommended City Council Action:
Pass Councillor’s Bill No. 9 on second reading to enact a six-month moratorium on the issuance of 
land use approvals, building permits, or business licenses for new event centers meeting certain 
locational criteria to operate in the City of Westminster.
Summary Statement:
The City recognizes that the nature of event centers has rapidly evolved and changed in the 
past several years, representing potentially significant and not previously experienced impacts 
to City resources and infrastructure as well as to nearby residents and businesses.
 
Page 8 of 182 
 
Page 8 of 182 

Page 2 of 2
It is further recognized that, due to the speed of the evolution that has occurred, City 
regulations and requirements have not been updated to keep pace with providing needed 
mitigations and protections or to otherwise address the impacts experienced.
A six-month moratorium is proposed to allow for thoughtful and careful development of 
appropriate regulations to mitigate the impacts of event centers. The regulations will be 
introduced and included in the forthcoming Unified Development Code (UDC), or as stand-
alone regulations introduced and incorporated into the existing Westminster Municipal Code 
(W.M.C.).
The moratorium shall apply to all event centers meeting the definition provided in this memo 
that are proposed to be situated within multi-tenant commercial developments or located within 
300 feet of a residential district, residential dwelling unit, or another event center. The 
moratorium shall not apply to lawfully existing event centers that are fully and properly 
permitted, licensed, and operating at the time of the moratorium adoption.  
The goal of this moratorium and subsequent revision to the W.M.C. is not to eliminate or 
prevent event centers, but to ensure that those allowed to operate in the City in the future are 
of an appropriate size and scale for their surroundings, and that any potential negative impacts 
they generate are properly mitigated.
City Council unanimously passed Councillor’s Bill No. 9 on first reading by a vote of (7 to 0) on 
March 23, 2026.

THE CITY OF WESTMINSTER 
 
WHEREAS, the City recognizes that Event Centers located in the City have traditionally been 
smaller, community-focused venues.  The intensity, size, and scope of these facilities have generally been 
limited, as the Event Centers were focusing on private gatherings such as weddings, graduations, birthdays, 
and various types of business meetings and conferences; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City has experienced a pronounced shift in the nature of Event Ccenters in recent 
years.  Increasingly, venues are becoming physically larger, and are hosting higher-intensity events such as 
concerts, indoor athletic competitions, other ticketed live entertainment events, and very large parties and 
celebrations, attended by hundreds of patrons; and 
 
WHEREAS, this shift has translated to greater demands on public safety operations, water and 
sewer infrastructure, and parking and traffic systems.  Neighboring residents and businesses have 
complained about excessive noise, intrusive lighting, litter, public drinking/ intoxication, traffic snarls, and

moratorium shall mean duly submitted, reviewed, and approved applications for all needed building 
permit(s), including, if applicable, a Certificate of Occupancy, and any needed Fire or Life Safety permits.  
“Business license” under this moratorium shall mean duly submitted, reviewed, and approved applications

Pass Councillor’s Bill No. 19 on first reading, an Ordinance amending Title XI of the Westminster

Summary Statement:

City regulations have not kept pace with recent changes in the nature of event centers. As a 
result, on April 13, 2026 City Council enacted a six-month moratorium on the processing and 
approval of new event center land use applications to allow for development of appropriate 
regulations to mitigate the impacts of event centers and live entertainment venues on City

The regulations proposed in Councillor’s Bill No. 19 will apply to all new event centers and new

Alternative(s):
City Council could choose not to pass Councillor’s Bill No. 19. Staff does not recommend this option

Background Information:
Traditionally, event centers located in the city have been smaller, community-focused venues. The 
intensity, size, and scope of these facilities have generally been limited, as the event centers were 
focused on smaller, private gatherings such as weddings, graduation celebrations, birthdays, and 
various types of business meetings and conferences. 
In recent years, the City has experienced a pronounced shift in the nature of event centers. 
Increasingly, venues are becoming physically larger and are hosting higher-intensity events such as 
concerts, indoor athletic competitions, other ticketed live entertainment events, and very large parties 
and celebrations, sometimes attended by hundreds of patrons. This shift has translated to greater 
demands on public safety operations, water and sewer infrastructure, and parking and traffic systems. 
Neighboring residents and businesses have complained about excessive noise, intrusive lighting, 
litter, public drinking/intoxication, traffic snarls, and vandalism, attributed to the larger crowds 
attending some events. Where event centers are located in inline/strip retail centers and other unified

shopping centers, neighboring businesses have complained about overcrowding and parking 
demands far in excess of what the retail center was designed for, reducing parking availability for the

The reason for the proposed regulations is to mitigate the impacts of this land use and to regulate 
new event centers so they will be located in appropriate areas and be successful without causing 
concerns for neighboring uses or properties, or undue stresses on City services and infrastructure.
The proposed Ordinance consists of updates to multiple sections of the Westminster Municipal Code 
and establishes a new section, 11-4-20, to specifically regulate and provide definitions for Event 
Center and Live Entertainment Venue, which can be found below:
Event Center shall mean an establishment that hosts restricted-access 
gatherings, such as student recitals, weddings, parties, banquets, and 
corporate events, which are not open to the general public, and may 
require invitations to, or pre-registration of, guests. Event centers typically 
offer full or partial venue rental options to third parties hosting private 
events. Facility types may include, but are not limited to, convention 
centers, conference or reception facilities, banquet halls, and similar 
venues. “Event center” does not include “live entertainment venues.”
Live Entertainment Venue shall mean an establishment primarily 
designed, intended, or used for hosting events that are open to the 
general public, whether or not a fee is charged for entry. Live 
entertainment venues may include sporting competitions or events, 
musical concerts, whether performed by live musicians and/or vocalists, or 
by a disc jockey or similar performer, or performances by dancers, stand-
up comedians, or other performance artists. Facility types may include, but 
are not limited to, concert halls, arenas or stadiums, dance halls, 
nightclubs, and similar establishments. “Live entertainment venue” does 
not include event centers.
Changes will include adding event centers and live entertainment venues to the list of land uses that 
are subject to approval as a Use By Special Permit (also known as a Special Use Permit or SUP). 
Spacing requirements and parking analyses will be required for consideration and approval of these 
land uses, and new parking standards will apply depending on whether the use is stand-alone or 
included within a shopping center.

Summary Statement:
In response to concerns about the location and regulation of Event Centers (EC), on March 
23, 2036 City Council enacted a six-month moratorium on the issuance of land use

approvals, building permits, and business licenses for new ECs in multi-tenant commercial

At the June 8, 2026 City Council meeting, City Council considered an Ordinance to amend 
Westminster Municipal Code (W.M.C.) with Councillor’s Bill No. 19 that, if approved, would

During consideration of the ordinance, City Council identified several items needing

Policy Issue(s):
Does City Council wish to review the options outlined in this memo to fine-tune a future Ordinance 
regarding ECs and LEVs?
Alternative(s):
City Council could choose not to review the options for EC and LEV regulation. This is not

Background Information:
Traditionally, event centers located in the city have been smaller, community-focused venues. The 
intensity, size, and scope of these facilities have generally been limited, as the event centers were 
focused on smaller, private gatherings such as weddings, graduation celebrations, birthdays, and 
various types of business meetings and conferences.
In recent years, there has been a pronounced shift in the nature of event centers in the city and the 
greater metropolitan area. Increasingly, venues are becoming larger in size and are hosting higher-
intensity events such as concerts, indoor athletic competitions, other ticketed live entertainment 
events, and very large parties and celebrations, sometimes attended by hundreds or thousands of 
patrons. This shift has translated to greater demands on public safety operations, water and sewer 
infrastructure, and parking and traffic systems, as well as conflicts with neighboring businesses, 
residents, and land uses. In consideration of these impacts, on March 23, 2026 City Council enacted 
a six-month Moratorium on the issuance of land use approvals, building permits, and business 
 
Page 34 of 182 
 
Page 34 of 182 

Page 3 of 7
licenses for new event centers in multi-tenant commercial developments, or where located within 300 
feet of a residential district, residential dwelling unit, or another event center.
At the June 8, 2026, regular City Council meeting, City Council considered regulations to mitigate the 
impacts of this land use and to regulate new event centers so they will be located in appropriate 
areas and be successful without causing concerns for neighboring uses or properties or undue stress 
on City services and infrastructure. Passage of this ordinance would have also lifted the Moratorium.
City Council Direction and Additional Information from Staff:
During the June 8, 2026 discussion, City Council identified 10 items requiring further discussion and 
directed Staff to bring further analysis and alternative solutions for consideration at a Study Session.
1) Approval Authority: City Council has requested approval authority for the “Use by Special 
Permit,” also known as a Special Use Permit (SUP) when required. Currently, SUPs are 
approved by the Planning Commission and only elevated to City Council upon appeal or by 
a City Council call-up that may be initiated if desired by at least four City Council members.
Alternative 1: Create new code language that would require SUPs for all ECs and LEVs 
regardless of size or location. Refer the SUP to the Planning Commission for a 
recommendation and to City Council for the approval decision.
Alternative 2: Create new code language that would require SUPs for all ECs and LEVs 
regardless of size or location. Refer the SUP directly to City Council for the approval 
decision with no prior review by the Planning Commission. 
Alternative 3: Create new code language that would require SUPs for all ECs and LEVs, 
regardless of size or location. Refer such SUPs to the Planning Commission for the 
approval decision unless a modification of a requirement or standard is sought by an 
applicant, in which case such request for SUP would be referred to City Council for the 
approval decision. 
Analysis and Recommendation: Staff recommends Alternative 3 as it requires only those 
applications seeking a modification of a requirement or standard be approved by City 
Council. Applications seeking a modification are most likely to cause the greatest impacts 
to surrounding uses. Alternative 3 also requires public hearings for all applications, which 
maximizes transparency. Alternatives 1 and 2 provide the most direct City Council 
oversight. Alternative 1 may feel burdensome for the applicant due to the imposition of a 
new step and the associated additional time. Alternative 2 removes the Planning 
Commission step but also removes the review and recommendation that the Planning 
Commission would otherwise provide to City Council. 
2) Expanded Locational Opportunities for Live Entertainment Venues: Allow LEVs in 
multi-tenant retail centers with the approval of an SUP.
Alternative 1: Create new code language to allow LEVs in retail centers, with SUP 
approval options as described above.
Alternative 2: Create new code language to allow LEVs in retail centers, with SUP 
approval options as described above. Introduce more restrictive size, parking, and other 
limitations as minimum requirements. Any application seeking a relaxation of a requirement 
or standard would be referred to City Council for the approval decision.
 
Page 35 of 182 
 
Page 35 of 182 

Page 4 of 7
Alternative 3: Do not allow LEVs in retail centers under any conditions.
Analysis and Recommendation: Staff recommends Alternative 3 because retail centers 
were not designed to accommodate such large impacts, and most of the complaints 
received regarding existing LEVs are those located in retail centers. If LEVs are to be 
allowed in retail centers, Staff recommends Alternative 2 to attempt to mitigate, to the 
greatest extent possible, the impacts anticipated. Alternatives 1 and 2 both would allow 
LEVs in shopping centers, and both would require public hearings which maximizes 
transparency.
3) Maximum Sizes for Live Entertainment Venues: Add maximum sizes for LEVs.
Alternative 1: Create maximum sizes for LEVs based on the historic experiences of the 
City. This would be based on research of previously established LEVs and basing the 
acceptable size limits on such past experiences.
Alternative 2: Create maximum sizes for LEVs based upon anticipated impacts to parking, 
traffic patterns, water usage and sewer capacity, impacts to emergency services, impacts 
to neighboring uses, and similar considerations.
Alternative 3: Do not create maximum sizes for LEVs.
Analysis and Recommendation: Staff recommends Alternative 3 for several reasons. 
First, based on experience, if a maximum allowable size is established, unless it is a very 
large size, there will likely be an application that seeks to exceed the maximum, forcing the 
consideration of a variance or other relief mechanism. Second, not all establishments are 
managed in the same fashion. Having a one-size-fits-all maximum could lead to 
undesirable and unintended outcomes, for example, the ability to host multiple events 
simultaneously. In its analysis of other jurisdictions, Staff found that maximum sizes for 
LEVs and/or ECs were rarely established. Instead of maximum sizes, Staff suggests that 
standards of review be followed and analyzed on a case-by-case basis to ensure that—no 
matter what the size of the facility—the anticipated associated impacts are recognized and 
mitigated to the greatest extent possible.
4) Expanded Zoning Opportunities for Live Entertainment Venues: Allow LEVs in 
industrial districts.
Alternative 1: PUD Zoning allows for LEVs, assuming that proper entitlements, licenses, 
and permits have been obtained. LEVs may be added into the M-1 (Industrial) zone, as 
well. As with all ECs, LEVs, and other land uses, compliance with the Comprehensive Plan 
would also be required.
Alternative 2: Do not allow LEVs in the M-1 zoning district. At this time there are only 14 
properties designated M-1, constituting less than one percent of the City’s land area. Two 
of these properties are City Open Space and all but one are concentrated in the area 
between 69th Place and 72nd Avenue between Lowell Boulevard and Raleigh Street, 
which could lead to a concentration of such uses in Historic Westminster. The only other M-
1 property, and the largest, is the Vantor (formerly Maxar) campus on 120th Avenue.
Analysis and Recommendation: The Comprehensive Plan does not allow for the 
construction of ECs or LEVs in either of the City’s industrial designations, Employment - 
 
Page 36 of 182 
 
Page 36 of 182 

Page 5 of 7
Flex and Employment - Office/Institutional Campus, as those uses are not listed as a 
permitted use. Westminster has little M-1-zoned land left, which may be a reason not to 
allow LEVs in this zoning district. Preserving opportunities for light industrial businesses is 
critical to maintaining a balanced economy. Additionally, allowing ECs or LEVs in M-1-
zoned areas would also require a Comprehensive Plan Amendment. For these reasons, 
Staff recommends Alternative 2.
5) “Backwards Compatibility” of Land Uses: Allow LEVs to host private events such as 
those commonly held in ECs, but not the reverse.
Alternative 1: Allow LEVs to host the types of events that would typically be associated 
with ECs. Do not allow ECs to host the types of events typically associated with LEVs.
Alternative 2: Do not allow LEVs to host the types of events that would typically be 
associated with ECs.
Analysis and Recommendation: Staff recommends Alternative 1, as this reinforces the 
idea that the types of gatherings held at LEVs are typically much more impactful than those 
held at ECs and that, if a site is properly geared to handle LEV-level events, it should be 
able to also handle EC-level events. The Planning Manager would be authorized to 
determine if a proposed event would fall into the EC or the LEV level, whenever the 
distinction might be unclear. 
6) Adequate Parking: Ensure that parking requirements are adequate to support the use. 
Under the rules considered by City Council on June 8, 2026, LEVs were not to be permitted 
in retail centers, and ECs are subject to the currently existing parking rules for retail 
centers. Stand-alone ECs and LEVs would achieve correct parking levels because their 
required parking would be based upon the maximum occupancy levels, including staff and 
guests, established by the Chief Building Official and the Fire Marshal.
Alternative 1: Retain parking requirements as currently established in W.M.C.
Alternative 2: Establish parking requirements on maximum occupancy levels (staff plus 
guests) established by the Chief Building Official and the Fire Marshal. The applicant must 
provide a parking study to demonstrate that parking is adequate both for the intended EC 
or LEV use, as well as any other users of the parking lot.
Analysis and Recommendation: If an EC or LEV locates in a retail center, Alternative 1 
does not provide a mechanism to evaluate the EC or LEV parking requirements separately 
from the overall retail center’s established parking requirements. Staff recommends 
Alternative 2, as it ensures that parking is properly accounted for, whether in a retail center 
or stand-alone setting.
7)  Spacing Requirements: Ensure that spacing requirements and separation from 
residential uses are adequate while not being too stringent. It is noted that there are two 
types of spacing being considered: spacing between the EC or LEV and nearby residential 
uses, and spacing between ECs and LEVs to each other.
Alternative 1: Require spacing between ECs and LEVs. Base spacing on anticipated 
impact levels. It is noted that LEVs are more impactful than ECs, and thus, should have 
greater spacing requirements.
 
Page 37 of 182 
 
Page 37 of 182 

Page 6 of 7
Alternative 2: No spacing requirements. Instead of fixed distances, rely on other 
development standards and the SUP process to evaluate compatibility.
Alternative 3: Apply spacing only between ECs/LEVs and residential areas. Do not require 
spacing between ECs and LEVs themselves. This alternative focuses on protecting 
neighborhoods from potential impacts, while preserving flexibility within commercial areas.
Alternative 4: Use a tiered or flexible spacing approach. This alternative provides a 
“baseline” spacing requirement but allow applicants to reduce the distance if they can 
demonstrate adequate mitigation such as sound control, design features, operations plans, 
etc. This creates predictability while still allowing case-by-case adjustment.
Analysis and Recommendation: The ordinance considered by City Council on June 8, 
2026 proposed 1,000 feet of space between stand-alone LEVs and residential uses and 
300 feet of space between ECs and LEVs to each other. If City Council desires to allow 
LEVs to be established in retail centers, a spacing requirement between residential and 
ECs and LEVs could make finding suitable locations more challenging. The spacing 
between residential uses and nearby commercial areas has been established for many 
years. A LEV might be blocked from locating in an existing retail center simply because of 
the proximity of existing homes even if the homes may not experience any impacts. 
Additionally, distance may not provide the mitigation desired: loud noises, for example, can 
travel long distances without abatement. 
Spacing between ECs and LEVs, meanwhile, is more straightforward to administer and 
enforce, but may result in restricting new ECs or LEVs in areas where they are highly 
compatible. No matter what kind of spacing is sought, applicants are likely to seek relief 
from the requirements, so a relief mechanism should be included in the regulation if any of 
these alternatives are chosen.
Rather than relying on spacing requirements, Staff recommends Alternative 2, which would 
require applicants to submit a detailed vicinity map with clearly measured distances to 
residential areas and to other ECs and LEVs as part of the SUP process. Instead of strict 
spacing requirements, mitigation strategies would be used to address impacts such as 
sound walls for sound mitigation, denser landscaping for visual mitigation, and hours of 
operation for sound and behavior mitigation. This approach provides flexibility, focuses on 
actual impacts, and avoids unintentionally blocking EC or LEV opportunities that may 
otherwise be compatible with the surrounding area.

how existing ECs or LEVs renewing their permits and licenses may be impacted by the new

Staff Response: Businesses renewing their existing licenses are grandfathered under the 
regulations and standards that were in place at the time that the original license was 
issued. They remain subject to the regulations that were in place at the time of their original 
approval. 
When a new business applies for a license, the application is routed through multiple City 
departments such as Sales Tax, Planning, Building, and Fire to ensure full compliance with 
tax compliance, zoning rules, building codes, and safety regulations. This coordinated 
review process ensures that all newly issued business licenses meet the most current 
standards and requirements.

Staff Response: The Specific Plans of Westminster Station and Downtown Westminster 
will be updated so they are consistent with the EC and LEV regulations that City Council 
enacts. Staff is supportive of both ECs and LEVs being allowed within the SPDs. 
Next Steps
If so directed by City Council, an updated ordinance will be drafted incorporating feedback provided 
by City Council. The updated ordinance will be brought back to City Council for consideration at a 
future meeting and, if City Council approves the ordinance, the Moratorium in place since March 23, 
2026 would be lifted.
The City’s Strategic Plan Priority of Healthy and Safe is supported by ensuring that ECs and LEVs 
are properly managed, thus reducing the likelihood of adverse safety conditions. Vibrant and Diverse 
Economy is supported by ensuring that ECs and LEVs are successful without becoming detrimental 
nuisances to neighboring businesses and residents. The Resilient Infrastructure priority is supported 
through ensuring that ECs and LEVs are located and operated so as not to unduly impact City 
infrastructure and services.

The following new Section is added to the Westminster Municipal Code: 
W.M.C. §11-4-20 Event Centers: 
 
(A) Statement of Intent: The intent of this section is to establish land use regulations that will provide for 
the harmonious integration of event centers into the City of Westminster. 
 
(B) Applicability: This section applies to all event centers proposed within the City of Westminster that 
were not fully and properly permitted at the time of enactment of this Code Section.  Note that event 
centers proposed for Specific Plan Districts will be separately governed by the Specific Plan 
regulating document for the applicable District. 
 
(C) Permitted Uses: Event centers may be permitted in the City of Westminster in the following zoning 
districts with an approved Use by Special Permit: B1, C1, C2, and PUD. 
 
(D) City Council Approval Required: All event centers require approval through a Special Use Permit 
(SUP), as outlined in W. M. C. 11-4-8.  The City Council will review and consider event center SUP 
proposals and render a decision thereon; the event center will not be presented to the Planning 
Commission as provided for in Section 2-2-2(G) of the W.M.C. The City Council shall render a 
decision to deny, approve, or approve with conditions, an event center SUP based upon its findings. 
  
(E) Standard Requirements Applicable to All Event Centers: All event centers are subject to the following 
requirements: 
a) No event center shall operate that is not fully and properly permitted with applicable permits 
and licenses including but not limited to all needed Building / Fire permits, business licenses, 
alcohol sales permits, Planning / zoning approvals and similar; 
b) Event centers shall fully comply with all applicable titles and code sections within the 
Westminster Municipal Code, or the Specific Area Plan associated with the facility’s 
location, as applicable; 
c) All event centers shall meet the applicable parking requirements found in Section 11-7-4 of 
the W.M.C. A parking study prepared by a Colorado licensed professional engineer or similar 
professional deemed acceptable by the Planning Manager or assigned designee that 
demonstrates an increased or decreased need for parking may modify the parking requirement 
for a given event center. 
d) Entrance queueing of patrons and other activities of event centers shall not unreasonably 
interfere with the operation, use, or enjoyment of other properties or businesses. 
e) No event center that generates adverse off-site nuisance impacts due to dust, fumes, odors, 
smoke, vapor, noise, lights, or vibration shall be permitted to operate in the City, pursuant to 
Section 11-4-6(J) of the W.M.C.  Operating event centers that introduce these nuisances are 
subject to revocation or termination of applicable licenses or permits. 
 
(F) Review Criteria: In reviewing an application for approval of an event center SUP, the following 
criteria shall be considered: 
a) The proposed event center is compatible and harmonious with existing public and private 
development in the surrounding area. 
b) The proposed event center is of an appropriate size and scale relative to surrounding uses. 
c) The proposed event center is reasonably separated and buffered from surrounding land uses 
to ensure compatibility. 
d) The proposed event center sufficiently mitigates or attenuates noise using methods such as 
sound walls along boundaries with residential or other incompatible land uses, sound-
proofing, or limitations on outdoor activity.   
e) The proposed event center appropriately limits the hours of operation.

Event Center Stakeholder Meeting
August 10, 2026 – Community Services Staff met with the Westminster 
Chamber of Commerce and two of its member event center operators.
Key takeaways from meeting:  
• Traditional event centers occasionally host concerts and other live 
entertainment events. Flexibility to host a wide variety of event types is 
key to an event center’s success. 
• Life safety and event security is important. Event centers should be 
required to provide security resources proportional to the size/scale of 
the event.
• Regulation of event center size and location is acceptable to ensure 
compatibility with other land uses. 
• Simple is better. Regulations should be easy to implement and 
understand. Businesses need to understand “why” rules exist.
