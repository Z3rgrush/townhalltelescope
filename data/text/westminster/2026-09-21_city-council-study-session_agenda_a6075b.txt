2027 Community Requests and 2026 Status Update
As part of the 2027 budget process, the City received 55 unique community requests. Staff 
researched each request and developed a recommendation for consideration through the budget 
process. When multiple requests were submitted regarding the same item or topic, Staff combined 
those requests into a single response to provide a clear and consistent recommendation. The 
requests generally centered around several key focus areas, including safe and connected 
transportation; parks, trails and recreation; maintaining existing infrastructure and services; 
sustainability and resilience; and affordability and community access.
Staff also provided an update on the 58 unique community requests received during the 2026 budget 
process. As part of the Proposed 2027 Budget, the status of each request was updated as of July 1, 
2026. At that time, approximately 45 percent of requests had been completed or required no 
additional action beyond the initial Staff recommendation, 52 percent were in progress, and 3 percent  
were paused pending further evaluation of available resources. These updates provide continued 
transparency of how prior community requests are being addressed and tracked over time.
Proposed 2027 City Council Budget
Nested in the proposed 2027 Budget is the proposed City Council budget totaling $353,550, a 
decrease of $17,600 from the 2026 budget of $371,150. The lower 2027 budget reflects actual 
expenditures and anticipated spending needs, aligning the budget more closely with historical use 
while maintaining existing service levels. The proposed budget continues existing community 
commitments, including $62,700 for sponsorships, supporting 27 organizations, events, and causes 
and maintaining a $5,000 contingency for unanticipated requests.

3. The 2026 Revised Budget planned a $19.6M General Fund drawdown, but 
the 2026 projection now shows just $209K. Given that $19.4M miss, what 
makes staff conﬁdent the $12.9M drawdown forecast for 2027 is realistic 
rather than overly conservative? 
 
Staff Response:  
The $19.6 million reﬂects the amount of General Fund balance authorized 
for use in the 2026 Revised Budget based on the revenues and expenditures 
known at the time. Appropriations set the maximum authority to spend 
rather than a projection that every dollar will be spent, so the $19.6 million 
represents the potential drawdown if budgeted expenditures were fully 
realized and revenues performed exactly as projected, not a precise 
estimate of the year-end change in fund balance. In practice, actual results 
come in under that ceiling, which is why the budgeted drawdown is 
consistently larger than the amount ultimately used.

Current projections now indicate a much smaller drawdown of 
approximately $209,000. The difference is primarily attributable to 
approximately $3.8 million in revenues projected above budget and 
approximately $15.6 million in budgeted expenditures that are not currently 
projected to be spent. Part of that expenditure variance reﬂects the 
movement of Outcomes and Tasks funding from capital into the operating 
budget during 2026, which increased the operating budget; the portion not 
spent by year-end lapses, and Staff has proposed re-appropriating an 
estimated $2.7 million of it into 2027. More broadly, these types of variances 
occur over the course of the year as actual revenues, vacancies, project 
timing, and departmental spending become clearer. 
 
The proposed $12.9 million drawdown for 2027 should be viewed in the 
same context. It reﬂects the resources needed to support the full proposed 
spending plan using revenue assumptions based on the information 
currently available. Actual year-end results may differ as revenues and 
expenditures develop throughout the year. 
 
Staff will continue to monitor General Fund performance closely during 
2027 and provide updated projections as additional information becomes 
available. As in 2026, favorable revenue performance, vacancy savings, 
project timing, or other expenditure savings could result in an actual 
drawdown that is lower than the amount reﬂected in the adopted budget. 
 
4. Through June, General Fund revenues are ahead of budget, sales and use 
tax is up 9.8%, and retail sales tax is up 11.3%. Which indicator is actually 
trending badly enough to justify framing 2027 around belt tightening? 
 
Staff Response:  
General Fund revenues are performing well in 2026, and Staff recognizes 
that positive trend. Through June, sales and use tax collections are both 
ahead of budget, and the 2027 budget assumes that growth continues. 
Sales and use tax revenue is projected at approximately $149 million in 
2027, about 6% above the current 2026 projection and generally consistent 
with the City’s independent economic forecast from University of Colorado’s 
Leeds School of Business.

The Proposed 2027 Budget reﬂects a balance between recognizing the 
current strength in revenues and avoiding the assumption that the 
approximately 10% year-to-date growth rate will continue at the same pace 
throughout 2027. Revenue collections can vary because of economic 
conditions, timing, seasonality, and other factors, so the forecast 
incorporates continued growth at a level Staff believes is sustainable for 
ongoing budgeting purposes. 
 
The primary reason for the more disciplined 2027 budget approach is the 
relationship between ongoing revenues and ongoing expenditures. Even

10. What justiﬁes moving about $1.3M of parks operations onto POST and

Staff Response: 
The alignment of additional Parks Operations costs to the POST Fund was 
driven by a broader review of available funding capacity. Staff determined 
that the POST Fund could support a greater share of direct Parks Operations 
costs, allowing those eligible expenses to be shifted from the General Fund.  
 
Cost allocations included in the budget are based on the results of the City’s 
Cost Allocation Plan (CAP). A CAP is a structured ﬁnancial document that 
identiﬁes, aggregates, and distributes central administrative overhead costs 
to departments, programs, and different funds. It estimates the costs that 
are associated with supporting operating groups – for example Human 
Resources (HR), Information Technology (IT), payroll, accounts payable and 
receivable, etc. supports the entire City and the CAP distributes their costs 
amongst other work groups and funds. 
 
The City ﬁrst incorporated a CAP in 2018 into the budget process to properly 
account for the fund transfers from the utilities to General Fund. Staff 
update the CAP as needed. The CAP update using 2026 budget numbers 
fully incorporated the City’s reorganization. 
 
Best practices state that cost allocation transfers should be done for all 
funds, but full implementation can be phased out with sensitivity to the 
relative ﬁnancial health and sustainability of individual funds. For 2027, the 
POST Fund transfer reﬂects approximately half of the amount identiﬁed 
through the CAP, allowing the allocation to be phased in while supporting 
the fund’s future balance. Cost allocations were also introduced for the new 
Paramedics and Pavement funds in the 2027 Budget and are similarly being 
phased in over time. 
 
The POST fund cost allocation was calculated in the same way as other 
funds. The allocation includes part of the costs from City Attorney’s Ofﬁce 
(CAO), City Manager’s Ofﬁce (CMO), General Government or Central Charges, 
Policy and Budget, Finance, HR, and IT. The costs of these groups were 
spread out in ways that best aligned with how they function. For example, 
Finance’s Procurement Division’s cost allocations were calculated by

Staff Response: 
The Rental Assistance Program aligns with the City’s Strategic Plan 
Opportunity to Thrive priority, speciﬁcally Outcome and Task 1.5/1.5.1. The 
City shares the goal of using local funding strategically and avoiding 
duplication with other available assistance programs. Through its 
partnership with the Community Economic Defense Project (CEDP), eligible 
households are connected ﬁrst with available federal, state, and other 
homelessness-prevention resources, including the Colorado Emergency 
Rental Assistance Program. City funding helps address eligible needs when 
other resources are unavailable or insufﬁcient. Temporary Assistance for 
Needy Family (TANF) rental assistance is the ﬁrst option (federal). Those 
funds are only available under certain limited circumstances. The Colorado 
Emergency Rental Assistance (CERA) (state) is the second option. CERA 
makes rental assistance available to anywhere from 100 to 200 households 
per month. Westminster's Homeless Prevention Program can be considered 
if a household is not eligible for TANF or CERA. 
 
To date in 2026, the City’s rental assistance program has assisted 28 
Westminster households, with an average assistance amount of 
approximately $6,500 per household. An additional 18 Westminster 
households are currently in the assistance process, which would bring the 
total number of households served in 2026 to 46. 
 
The Proposed 2027 Budget includes $375,562 to continue the program. 
Staff recognizes the importance of establishing appropriate parameters 
around the amount and duration of assistance and ensuring City resources 
complement, rather than duplicate, other available programs.
