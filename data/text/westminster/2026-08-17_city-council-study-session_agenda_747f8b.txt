Subject: Overview of Proposed Updates to the Westminster Municipal Code to Modernize 
Business Tax Administration
Prepared By: Ezequiel Vasquez, Chief Financial Officer
Mark Harrelson, Sales Tax Manager
Recommended City Council Action:
Receive a presentation on proposed updates to the business tax administration provisions of the 
Westminster Municipal Code and direct Staff to return with an ordinance amending Chapters 1 and 2 
of Title IV to modernize notice, filing, and publication requirements and to authorize the Finance 
Director to specify the manner and form for the filing and payment of tax returns. 
Summary Statement:
The proposed updates to the City’s tax administration apply only to business tax filings, which 
are typically monthly filings, and do not affect resident taxpayers.
The Westminster Municipal Code (W.M.C.) currently prescribes methods for business tax 
return filing and taxpayer communication that are rooted in paper processes and are outdated, 
inflexible, and inefficient considering advances in communications technology and the ways 
that modern business taxpayers engage with the world.

In 2025, Westminster businesses filed 77,424 City tax returns, and an estimated 94 percent 
were filed online through the Westminster tax system or the Colorado Department of Revenue 
Sales and Use Tax System. 
The proposed Ordinance would authorize the Finance Director to specify the manner and form 
for filing business tax returns, modernizing the system for taxpayers and allowing the City to 
more efficiently keep up with best practices and technological updates. Under this authority, 
the City would require online filing and payment of all City tax returns effective January 1, 2027 
through published tax compliance guidance.
Businesses who wish to do so will still be able to file a paper submission by seeking an 
exception, preserving access for taxpayers without internet connectivity.
Because the filing method would be specified by the Finance Director rather than fixed in the 
W.M.C., future changes in technology or best practice would not require additional Ordinance 
amendments.
The proposed Ordinance would also permit electronic delivery of taxpayer notices, remove the 
requirement to post distraint sale notices at county courthouses, which the Jefferson and 
Adams County Courts no longer accommodate, and clarify the exemption language for certain 
medical items without any change in tax policy and without any change in tax revenue.
At least 12 self-collecting Colorado home-rule municipalities, including Arvada and Lakewood, 
already require online filing of sales and use tax returns, and 10 of the 12 also require 
electronic payment. The Colorado Department of Revenue is phasing in mandatory electronic 
filing of state retail sales tax returns, with all returns required to be filed electronically beginning 
in January 2028.

Background Information:
History
Title IV of the W.M.C. governs the administration of the City’s taxes, including the filing of returns, the 
delivery of taxpayer notices, refund claims, assessments, protests, and enforcement. Much of this 
language predates current communications technology and prescribes paper-based methods, 
including first-class mail as the default delivery method for notices and the posting of distraint sale 
notices at county courthouses.
In 2025, taxpayers filed 77,424 City tax returns across three channels, as shown below. An estimated 
94 percent of returns arrived online, and the remaining 4,951 returns arrived on paper through the 
City’s bank lockbox.

Proposed Amendments
The proposed Ordinance would amend Subsection 4-1-7(A), W.M.C., to provide that the manner and 
form for the filing of returns shall be as specified by the Finance Director. This structure places the 
filing method in administrative guidance rather than in the W.M.C. itself, which allows the City to 
adopt improved methods more quickly and efficiently in the future without further Ordinance 
amendments and enables the City to keep up with peer municipalities.
Businesses who wish to do so will still be able to file a paper submission by seeking an exception, 
preserving access for taxpayers without internet connectivity.
The proposed Ordinance would also update the definition and delivery of notices to permit electronic 
delivery or any other verifiable delivery method reasonably calculated to provide notice to the 
taxpayer; remove redundant uses of the phrase “written notice” throughout Title IV, since notices are 
defined as written; permit publication of distraint sale notices in a newspaper of general circulation or 
on the City’s official website, with courthouse posting becoming discretionary; and clarify the 
exemption for prosthetic devices, prescription drugs, and items dispensed pursuant to a written order 
of a licensed practitioner of the healing arts. The exemption clarification would ease the interpretive 
and administrative burden on taxpayers without increasing taxes, without implementing any change in 
tax policy, and without any resulting significant increase in tax revenue.

Implementation and Business Outreach
Under the authority provided by the proposed Ordinance, the City would issue tax compliance 
guidance requiring that, effective January 1, 2027, all City tax returns be filed online through the 
Westminster tax system or the Colorado Department of Revenue Sales and Use Tax System and that 
all associated payments be submitted electronically. A business seeking to file or pay by an 
alternative method could submit a written statement to the Finance Director at least 30 days prior to 
the end of the applicable filing period. Businesses that fail to comply without an approved exception 
may be subject to late filing or late payment penalties, interest, and assessment based on estimated 
tax liability.
In advance of the January 1, 2027, effective date, the City would notify all licensed businesses of the 
online filing and payment requirement and the process for requesting an exception, and would publish 
the tax compliance guidance on the City’s website.
The proposed amendments support Strategic Priority 2: Connected and Inclusive Community by 
improving access to taxpayer communications and maintaining accommodations for businesses that 
require alternative filing methods.  These amendments also support Strategic Priority 5: Resilient 
Infrastructure by modernizing tax administration processes, reducing administrative costs, and 
providing the flexibility to adapt to changing technology while delivering efficient, high-quality services.

Background Information:
City Council asked Staff to bring forth an ordinance that would regulate excessive and disruptive 
noise in the City of Westminster. A proposed ordinance was presented to City Council on June 22, 
2026; this ordinance was not passed by City Council.
The proposed ordinance set maximum noise levels in decibels measured on the “A” scale of a 
standard sound level meter having characteristics defined by the American National Standards 
Institute (ANSI) publication S1. 4 - 1971. These maximum decibel levels were adjusted for time of day 
and zoning district, with the tightest restrictions set for residential areas.

reviewed for how they address noise in their respective municipal codes. Twelve jurisdictions enforce

Licensing Applications (dance halls) – Section 5-16-3, W.M.C.
Home Occupations – Section 11-4-10, W.M.C.
Oil and Gas Regulations – Section 11-4-14, W.M.C.
Zoning and Standards for Official Development Plans – Section 11-5-15, W.M.C.
The ordinance reviewed on June 22, 2026 is consistent with similar and neighboring municipalities 
and mirrors Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.) Section 25-12-103, which provides reasonable noise 
decibel standards for enforcement purposes and sets the minimum level of noise restriction that may 
be enacted by municipalities.
From the time period of July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026, the City of Westminster Police 
Department responded to 77,637 calls for service. Of those calls, 1,189 (0.015 percent) calls dealt 
with noise.

Proposed Framework for the Utility Affordability Advisory Board (UAAB) 
Purpose 
On August 3, 2026, City Council reviewed proposed 2027–2028 water, sewer, and stormwater 
rates. As part of that discussion, Staff recommended transitioning the WATER Task Force into an 
ongoing Utility Affordability Advisory Board (UAAB) to ensure continued community involvement in 
rate setting, system planning, and affordability initiatives. The Task Force model strengthened 
transparency, community understanding, and trust while building member expertise in utility 
operations, infrastructure needs, rate design, and affordability challenges. An ongoing advisory 
board would continue this value. 
UAAB Roles and Responsibilities 
• Provide regular input on water, sewer, and stormwater ﬁnancial planning.
• Review cost-of-service studies and proposed utility rates and fees, including water budget-
based billing.
• Continue to evaluate rate structures, including single-family residential, multi-family and
non-residential.
• Offer guidance on long-term system resiliency and investment priorities.
• Support public understanding of utility operations, trade-offs, and constraints.
• Understand and support continued energy efficiency and utilization at City facilities.

Understanding City-Owned Water Meters and Water Rate Classes 
Residential buildings are billed based upon their city-owned water meter per WMC 8-7-7. 
The rate is determined by the city-owned meter serving the property, not by the type of 
housing alone. 
• A meter serving one single-family detached or attached unit is billed at the single-
family residential rate. This includes:
o Conventional single-family detached homes
o Newer single-family attached homes (townhomes)
• A single meter serving two units (duplex) or three units (triplex) is billed at the single-
family residential rate with a ‘multiplier’ to increase the volume allotted within each
tier
o A duplex will have 2x the water volume in Tier 1/2/3/4 by comparison to a
single unit; a triplex will have 3x the water volume
• A single meter serving a building with four or more units is billed at the multi-family
rate. This includes:
o Apartment buildings
o Older townhome buildings served by a single city-owned meter for the
building.
 In 2010, the City began requiring individual townhomes to have their
own city-owned water meter, rather than one city-owned meter for the
building as a whole. As a result, newer townhomes are billed under
the single-family rate structure while older townhomes are billed
under the multi-family rate.
o Building code requires the installation of private submeters for new multi-
family residential construction
 The City does not own, nor do we bill based upon these private
submeters. Utility bills are based only on the city-owned water meter.
 Property managers can use the private meter readings to apportion
utility bill costs to individual units
 Submetering requirements were initiated in 2018, but older
complexes may have private submeters installed as well because the
property manager thought it was a good idea

City Council could choose not to receive a presentation regarding an overview of Municipal Court

Background Information:
The Westminster Municipal Court (WMC) adjudicates thousands of criminal and civil code violations 
and receives over 18,000 visitors annually. To accomplish these tasks, the WMC collaborates with 
several departments and partners with outside agencies, such as the Office of Alternate Defense 
Counsel. 
In addition, the WMC has developed several innovative programs including the:
Rehabilitation Assistance and Support Program (R.A.S.P.) – provides voucher assistance to 
low-income individuals with court ordered treatment and/or education requirements.
Westminster Wellness Court – an Adult Treatment Court employing national AllRise best 
practices to address high-risk, high-need offenders whose substance and/or mental health 
issues contributed to their underlying crime.
Universal Community Service – provides a non-financial alternative for all court participants to 
resolve their court ordered fines/fees.
Adult Diversion – a newly implemented program which will provide individuals charged with 
low-value shoplifting and similar crimes an opportunity to have their case dismissed. 
Participants will receive education on available community resources and mental health 
services in partnership with the Community Reach Center.
Resource Navigation – the WMC’s in-house resource navigator meets with individuals on an 
as needed basis before or after their appearance in court to provide convenient and immediate 
assistance.
Juvenile Diversion – the WMC partners with the Jefferson County Assessment Center and 
I.S.A.E./CIMA to provide opportunities for appropriate juveniles charged with substance related 
offenses to resolve their cases with or without a court appearance.
The presentation will provide an overview for City Council and the public of the WMC, its departments 
and partnerships, as well as several of its active programs.
The City’s Strategic Plan priorities of Healthy and Safe and Opportunity to Thrive are met by ensuring 
City Council and the Westminster public have a transparent understanding of the WMC’s operations 
and programs that help individuals achieve success and reduce recidivism.
