Genesis Lee spoke in support of adopting the International Property Maintenance Code (IPMC)

corporate landlords accountable.

rental housing regulations. She stated that landlords have a responsibility to provide safe, 
code-compliant housing and that tenants should have mechanisms to address unresolved 
habitability problems. She supported amendments addressing mold, potential impacts to 
unhoused individuals, and clarification of owner and renter responsibilities, and encouraged 
City Council to pursue a City rent escrow program.

11. PUBLIC HEARINGS 
A. Public Hearing and First Reading of Councillor’s Bill No. 39 Amending the 2040 
Comprehensive Plan to Establish a Density Bonus and Revised Definition for Affordable 
Housing; First Reading of Councillor’s Bill No. 36 Amending Sections 11 -2-1 and 11-7-4, 
Title XI, Westminster Municipal Code to Amend the Definition of Affordable Housing 
and Establish a Parking Reduction 
Carmelia opened the public hearing at 8:01 p.m. 
 
Principal Planner Andrew Spurgin and Housing and Economic Development Manager 
Vivi Del Pizzo presented proposed amendments to the 2040 Comprehensive Plan 
related to affordable housing. Staff explained that approximately 2.3% of the City’s land 
remains vacant and available for development and that the 2024 Housing Needs 
Assessment identified density bonuses for infill development as a strategy to support 
affordable housing production. The proposed amendment would allow a 30% density

5 
bonus for qualifying affordable housing developments and revise the definition of 
affordable housing to align with state and federal funding requirements, including 
Proposition 123. The proposed definition included rental housing serving households up 
to 80% AMI and owner-occupied housing up to 120% AMI. Staff noted that affordability 
would be maintained through mechanisms such as deed restrictions, covenants, or 
agreements. 
 
Staff also discussed a companion Municipal Code amendment establishing a 15% 
parking reduction for affordable housing developments. The Planning Commission had 
recommended approval of the Comprehensive Plan amendment but recommended 
requiring only 15% of units within a development to be affordable rather than staff’s 
recommended 50%; Staff did not support that change. 
 
Nurmela asked about the methodology used to establish the 30% density bonus, how 
it compared with other jurisdictions, and whether it should be implemented before 
completion of the Unified Development Code. She questioned the proposed 80% AMI 
threshold for rental housing, noting that some market-rate housing is already available 
at that level, and suggested 60% AMI would better target the City’s affordable housing 
needs and align with funding programs. She also discussed establishing a minimum 
duration for affordability restrictions associated with use of the density bonus. Staff 
stated that 60% AMI could be used while maintaining eligibility for funding and agreed 
to further evaluate requirements for affordability restrictions. 
 
Ireland asked whether former school or church properties could be developed as 
housing and whether multifamily developments were still required to have access to 
transit. Staff explained that properties designated public/quasi-public would require a 
Comprehensive Plan amendment approved by Council and that the Comprehensive 
Plan continues to require multifamily development to have access to transit and major 
thoroughfares. Councillor Ireland expressed concern about reducing parking 
requirements and stated that she would not support the proposal. 
 
Barajas sought clarification regarding whether affordability restrictions needed to be 
incorporated directly into the affordable housing definition. Staff clarified that a term 
for a covenant or deed restriction could instead be established as a condition for 
utilizing the density or parking incentives and did not necessarily need to be 
incorporated into the general definition. 
 
Four individuals signed up to provide public testimony. 
 
Kathy Stroud opposed the proposed density bonus and parking reduction, expressing 
concerns regarding increased residential density, inadequate parking, impacts on 
existing neighborhoods, development of former school properties, and the need for 
additional homeownership opportunities rather than high-density rental housing. 
 
Grace Heaney expressed concerns regarding the impacts of affordable and higher-
density housing on schools, public safety, fire protection, and property maintenance. 
She encouraged consideration of affordable homeownership models such as Habitat 
for Humanity rather than additional high-density rental development. 
 
Jim Fenimore opposed reducing parking requirements and increasing residential 
density, stating that Westminster residents remain dependent on automobiles and 
expressing concern about impacts on infrastructure and water resources.  
 
Mary Berg opposed the proposal and encouraged Council to delay action until 
additional information was available regarding infrastructure, utilities, taxation, and the 
financial impacts of affordable housing development on existing property owners.  
 
Carmelia closed the public hearing at 8:38 p.m.

Background Information:
Beginning in the early 1800s and lasting well into the 20th century, hundreds of thousands of 
American Indian, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian children were removed from their families and 
communities and placed in more than 500 government and church-run Indian boarding schools, 
where many endured the loss of culture, language, traditions, and identity, and many children never 
returned home. These schools caused intergenerational trauma that continues to affect Indigenous 
peoples, families, and communities today, while Indigenous nations, families, and cultures continue to 
demonstrate resilience, strength, and survival. 
Orange Shirt Day began in 2013 to honor survivors of residential and boarding schools, remember 
the children who never came home, and recognize the enduring impacts of these institutions. The 
wearing of an orange shirt symbolizes the experience of Phyllis Webstad, whose orange shirt was 
taken from her on her first day at a residential school, and stands as a powerful reminder that every 
child matters. 
September 30th is recognized across Indigenous communities as Every Child Matters Day, a time to 
reflect on the truths of United States and Canadian boarding school histories, honor survivors, and 
commit to healing, reconciliation, and education.
The Inclusivity Board supports the attached proclamation.
Recognizing Orange Shirt Day supports the City's Strategic Priorities of Healthy and Safe and 
Connected and Inclusive Community by affirming our city’s commitment to guide efforts toward 
healing, equity, and belonging for all.

Summary Statement:
Annually, the President of the United States proclaims the week in October that includes 
October 9 as Fire Prevention Week. October 9 is the anniversary of the Great Chicago Fire of 
1871. Fire Prevention Week is the longest-running public health observance in the United 
States.  
The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) designates the theme for National Fire 
Prevention Week every year. The NFPA theme for 2026 is “Charge into Fire Safety: Safe 
Charging Is a Superpower.”
To enhance a fire safe community, Westminster extends the celebration of Fire Prevention 
Week for the entire month of October. The Westminster Fire Department promotes the 
campaign with fire safety outreach at several community events with residents and 
businesses. A fire station open house will be held during Fire Prevention Month on Saturday, 
October 3.

City Council could choose not to proclaim October 2026 as Fire Prevention Month for the City of

the importance of fire prevention and planning. Additionally, tying into the national theme provides 
greater visibility and continuity in educating the public.  
Background Information:
Fire Service Professionals throughout the United States will celebrate Fire Prevention Week on 
October 4-10, 2026. Fire Prevention Week was established to commemorate the Great Chicago Fire, 
the tragic October 9, 1871, conflagration that killed more than 250 people.  
In 1920, President Woodrow Wilson issued the first National Fire Prevention Day proclamation. Since 
1922, Fire Prevention week has been observed on the Sunday through Saturday period in which 
October 9 falls.  
During Fire Prevention Month, the Westminster Fire Department will promote the theme “Charge into 
Fire Safety: Safe Charging Is a Superpower” throughout the community. The 2025 theme revolved 
around lithium-ion battery safety. The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) chose a battery-
related theme again in 2026 because lithium-ion battery fires are rapidly increasing nationwide, 
making it a critical public safety crisis. This theme focuses on everyday habits that can help reduce 
the risk of lithium-ion battery fires and keep homes and communities safer.
We can each do more by: 
Using the cord and charger that came with the device or one approved by the manufacturer.
Charging on a hard, flat surface like a table, desk, or bare floor.

Stopping use of the device if it feels very hot, starts to swell, smells strange, leaks, smokes, or 
makes popping or hissing sounds.
Unplugging the device when it is fully charged.
Charging large items like e-bikes, scooters, and power tools outdoors when possible, with care 
not to block exits while charging. 
Buying products listed by a nationally recognized testing laboratory such as UL.
Having working smoke alarms and a home fire escape plan.
The Westminster Fire Department wants to bring a sense of safety and empowerment to the 
Westminster community and the best way to start is to have people ask themselves a simple 
question: What can I do to prevent fires?

strengthen property maintenance and rental housing standards, improve transparency and 
consistency in enforcement, and establish clear minimum standards for the maintenance of 
existing residential and non-residential properties.
The proposed Ordinance follows City Council's July 6, 2026 Study Session, at which Staff 
presented the final outcomes and recommendations of the Rental Housing Ad Hoc Committee 
(Committee) and received City Council feedback and direction regarding adoption of the 2021 
International Property Maintenance Code (IPMC) and related amendments.

Committee recommendations included key updates to local property standards such as 
maintenance requirements, inspection procedures, responsibilities for owners and tenants, and 
addressing issues including unsafe structures, snow/ice removal, landscaping viability, debris 
standards, pest control, and minimum heat requirements. These updates are intended to 
provide increased clarity, consistency, and transparency. 
This Ordinance adopts the IPMC by reference, with local amendments addressing 
Westminster-specific conditions, policies and operational practices, and enacts a 
comprehensive Residential Rental Code establishing standards and requirements specific to 
residential rental properties.
The IPMC applies to every property in the city while the Residential Rental Code applies only 
to residential rental properties.
The IPMC and Residential Rental Code provide the Community Services Department’s 
Property Standards Officers with a nationally recognized and comprehensive set of minimum 
property maintenance standards while retaining Westminster-specific amendments to address 
local conditions and policy objectives.
The proposed Ordinance is intended to provide residents, property owners, property 
managers, tenants, and City staff with a clearer and more consistent framework for 
understanding property-maintenance and rental-housing requirements and for addressing 
conditions that affect health, safety, habitability, and neighborhood quality.

A BILL 
FOR AN ORDINANCE AMENDING SUBSECTION 1-23-1(B) AND TITLE VIII OF THE 
WESTMINSTER MUNICIPAL CODE TO ADOPT THE INTERNATIONAL PROPERTY 
MAINTENANCE CODE WITH AMENDMENTS AND ENACTING THE RESIDENTIAL RENTAL 
CODE 
 
 WHEREAS, the City of Westminster has an interest in protecting the health, safety, welfare, and 
quality of life of persons who reside, work, and conduct business within the City, including ensuring that 
existing residential and nonresidential properties are maintained in a safe, sanitary, and habitable condition; 
and the 2021 International Property Maintenance Code (“2021 IPMC”) establishes comprehensive 
minimum standards addressing existing structures and premises, including light, ventilation, occupancy, 
sanitation, protection from the elements, fire safety, plumbing, mechanical systems, and other conditions 
affecting health and safety; and 
 
 WHEREAS, Westminster's existing property-maintenance and rental-housing regulations provide 
important minimum standards for residential rental properties, licensing and registration requirements, and 
inspection authority, but the City has determined that a more com prehensive and consistent property-
maintenance framework is appropriate to address the City's aging housing stock, improve the effectiveness 
and clarity of property standards, and promote safe, stable, and well -maintained neighborhoods; and the 
City's existing rental-property program identifies the prevention of deteriorating conditions, blight, and 
unsafe or unsanitary housing conditions as important objectives of its rental-housing regulations; and 
 
 WHEREAS, during 2025 and 2026, the City convened the Rental Housing Ad Hoc Committee to 
examine rental-housing standards and enforcement, including inspection-model alternatives, a public rental 
registry, landlord education and resources, program capacity and training, and adoption of the 2021 IPMC; 
the Committee's work included participation and engagement with residents, City Council representatives, 
property owners and managers, housing and community organizations, City staff, and other stakeholders, 
with meetings offered at multiple locations and with accessibility, childcare, and translation services; and 
 
 WHEREAS, the Rental Housing Ad Hoc Committee and City staff have evaluated the 2021 IPMC 
in relation to the Westminster Municipal Code and have developed proposed local amendments intended 
to preserve Westminster -specific policies and address local conditions, incl uding provisions concerning 
property maintenance, pest elimination, heating, doors and locks, snow and ice, trash and debris, inspection 
frequency and notice, owner certification of corrections, public rental-registry information, and reinspection 
fees; thereby providing a locally tailored property- maintenance standard rather than adopting the model 
code without modification; and 
 
 WHEREAS, City Council has identified as a 2026 strategic priority the expansion of awareness 
and resources concerning rental -housing standards and codes, the strengthening of renter stability and 
rental-housing quality through inspection expansion and improved standards awareness, and the finalization 
of a Property Standards Code Update for Council consideration with the assistance of the Rental Housing 
Ad Hoc Committee; and the City therefore finds that adoption of the 2021 IPMC, as amended to address 
Westminster's local circumstances, together with a comprehensive Residential Rental Code, will advance 
the public health, safety, welfare, housing stability, and neighborhood-quality objectives of the City.

CHAPTER 2. – RESIDENTIAL RENTAL CODE 
 
8-2-1. Residential Rental Code  
 
(A) Purpose: The purpose of this Residential Rental Code is to provide minimum standards to safeguard 
life or limb, health, property, and public welfare by regulating and controlling the use and occupancy, 
location, and maintenance of all residential rental structures within the City. This Residential Rental Code 
establishes minimum standards for basic equipment and facilities, for light, ventilation and heating; for 
safety from fire; for the use and amount of space for human occupancy; and for the safe and san itary 
maintenance of residential rental properties.  
 
(B) Scope: The provisions of this Residential Rental Code shall apply to all existing residential rental 
buildings, and structures, excluding manufactured homes, and all existing premises, or portions thereof 
used, designed, or intended to be used for dwelling purposes on a rental basis, as well as the site, including 
parking lots, driveways and landscaping, and accessory structures, such as fences, retaining walls, sheds, 
and other such structures. Rooming houses, congregate residences or lodging houses shall comply with all 
the requirements of this Residential Rental Code. Except as provided herein, properties, including buildings, 
or portions thereof, equipment, devices and safeguards that were required by the building codes shall be 
maintained in conformance  with the building codes under which they were installed, provided such 
continued use is not dangerous to life. Where there are conflicts between the building codes and this 
Residential Rental Code, the provisions of this Residential Rental Code shall apply.  
 
8-2-2. Minimum Standards. 
 
No person shall lease to another for occupancy any structure that does not comply with the requirements of 
this Residential Rental Code and the Westminster Municipal Code Title VIII, Chapter 1 Property Standards. 
Existing structures and premises that do not comply with these provisions shall be altered or repaired to 
provide a minimum level of compliance as required herein.  
 
8-2-3. License Required, Rental Property.  
 
Prior to leasing any rental property for occupancy, an owner shall obtain and maintain in good standing a 
rental property license from the City pursuant to Title V, Chapter 12, of the Westminster Municipal Code. 
 
8-2-4. Registration Required, Rental Dwellings.  
 
Prior to leasing any rental dwelling for occupancy, an owner shall obtain and maintain in good standing a 
rental dwelling registration with the City pursuant to Title V, Chapter 12, of the Westminster Municipal 
Code. 
 
8-2-5. Inspections. 
 
(A) General: 
 
(1) The City Manager, acting by and through the building and community standards division, 
shall establish a regular and orderly schedule to inspect all rental property within the City. Such schedule 
shall not preclude inspection of a rental property at other times, based on a request of a resident, owner or 
manager, or other complainant. 
 
(2) Rental dwellings shall be inspected by the City in accordance with the schedule established 
in this section, on a complaint basis, or otherwise as conditions warrant 
 
(3) Any violations shall be documented, and a photographic record may be made of the 
property or of any violations discovered on the property. 
 
(B) Right of Entry

(1) When necessary to make an inspection for compliance with the provisions of this 
Residential Rental Code as part of scheduled inspections, or when the City has probable cause to believe 
that there exists upon any premises any condition that constitutes a vi olation of the provisions of this 
Residential Rental Code, the city manager or authorized representative, hereinafter referred to as the 
"inspector," may enter a premise at all reasonable times to inspect or to perform any duty imposed on him, 
provided that the following procedure has been followed: 
 
(a) If a premise is currently leased, the inspector shall first present proper credentials 
and request permission to enter from  a tenant or other person having common authority over the 
premise. If the leased premise is currently unoccupied, the inspector shall make a reasonable effort 
to locate the tenant and, upon locating the tenant, shall present proper credentials and request  
permission to enter. If a tenant or other person having common authority over the premise cannot 
be located after a reasonable effort, as determined by the Municipal Judge, a notice of intent to 
inspect shall be posted on the premises, giving notice that an inspection may proceed after a court 
order is obtained from the municipal judge of the City. 
(b) If entry is refused, or 24 hours after the premises have been posted, the inspector 
may appear before the Municipal Judge and shall request, pursuant to Rule 241 of the Municipal 
Court rules of procedure, as amended, a court order entitling the inspector t o enter upon the 
premises. Upon presentation of the court order and proper credentials, or possession of same in the 
case of unoccupied premises, the inspector may enter upon the premises, using such reasonable 
force as may be necessary to gain entry. 
 
(2) For purposes of this section, "probable cause" for a court order exists either: 
 
(a) Upon a showing that the inspection is part of the systematic schedule of inspections 
that is a condition of licensing the rental property, and that the tenant or other person having

(b) Upon a showing that the facts and circumstances within the inspector's knowledge

(3) Unlawful Resistance: It shall be unlawful for any owner, tenant, or person having common 
authority of a rental property or rental dwelling to deny entry of an inspector acting pursuant to a court 
order that has been issued according to the procedure outlined in this section. 
 
8-2-6. Inspection Procedures. 
 
(A) Authority: The City Manager, acting by and through the building and community standards 
division, may inspect rental properties and rental dwellings, individual units thereof, and their associated 
properties, in order to determine compliance with the provisions of this Residential Rental Code. 
 
(B) Scheduled Inspections of Rental Properties: 
 
The frequency of inspections on rental properties shall be as follows: 
 
(1) Properties less than six years old shall not be scheduled for inspection. 
 
(2) Properties between six and 20 years old shall be inspected every four years. 
 
(3) Properties older than 20 years shall be inspected every two years. 
 
(4) Inspections may be increased in frequency upon a determination that violations of this 
Residential Rental Code, revealed during an inspection, individually or in combination, demonstrate a 
failure to maintain the rental property in a decent, safe, and sanitary condition.

(6) Any rental property may be inspected at any time due to complaints or as conditions

It shall be the responsibility of the owner or agent to notify the individual tenants of the property of the

(2) Notice in writing to the owner or agent at least seven days in advance of the scheduled 
inspection to verify the time and date. 
 
(3) If it is necessary for the city to cancel a scheduled inspection, it may send a cancellation 
notice to the owner or agent of the affected property at least three days prior to the scheduled inspection 
date. 
 
8-2-7. Notice of Non-Compliance; Inspection Report. 
 
(A) When the City determines that noncompliance with this Residential Rental Code exists, a notice of 
noncompliance and order to correct shall be issued. The notice of noncompliance shall be in writing and 
shall describe the violation with sufficient detail fo r it to be properly addressed and corrected. The notice 
of noncompliance shall provide a reasonable time for correction, no less than seven and no more than 90 
days. 
 
(B) Inspection Report: A report of inspection results, including notice of noncompliance, if any, shall 
be sent to the property owner or agent within seven days of completion of an inspection. 
 
(1) The inspection report shall include the results of the inspection, including code sections 
herein in violation thereof, the period of time for correction of any noncompliant conditions, and the 
scheduled reinspection date and time. The reinspection report shall be posted to a publicly available rental 
registry database. 
 
(2) An inspection report containing only satisfactory results with no notice of noncompliance 
need not be personally served and may be mailed to the owner or agent at the address currently on file with 
the City as part of the licensing or registering of the pr operty pursuant to Title V, Chapter 12, of the 
Westminster Municipal Code. 
 
(C) Any notice of noncompliance and order to correct, and any inspection report containing a notice of 
noncompliance and order to correct, shall be served by one of the following methods: 
 
(1) Personally, upon the owner or agent, in which case service shall be deemed complete on 
the date such service occurs.

(D) Reinspection’s may be conducted to verify that the violations identified in a notice of

affirmation, but may instead insist upon reinspection as provided for in this Section.

assessed for each follow -up inspection required after the initial reinspection due to an owner's failure to 
correct satisfactorily the identified violations. Reinspection fee may be waived at the discretion of the code 
official or their designee if they determine that the property owner is working in good faith. For each follow-
up inspection required after the initial reinspection, a reinspection fee of $50.00 per unit and common area 
may be assessed, which fee amount may be amended by subsequent resolution of City Council . 
Reinspection fees not paid in full within 30 days of assessment shall constitute a lien on the property and 
shall be recorded as such with the County Clerk. 
 
(G) Extensions: If an owner cannot complete the required corrective action in the time set forth in the 
notice of non-compliance, the owner or agent may request an extension to the completion date, which may 
be granted upon a determination by the property standards administrator or designee that substantial 
progress is being made to correct the violation(s). Such request shall be made in writing and shall contain 
the reasons that an extension is necessary and the requested length of extension. A request for ext ension 
shall be made no less than three days prior to a scheduled reinspection or the required completion date, 
whichever is earlier. 
 
(H) No Show Fees: If an owner or agent fails to attend an inspection or reinspection, fails to request a 
timely extension, or fails to provide notice to tenants of the City's request for permission to enter, a no-
show fee of $50.00 per missed appointment may be assessed. The fee amount may be amended by 
subsequent resolution of City Council. 
 
8-2-8. Unlawful Conduct; Public Nuisance.  
 
(A) It shall be unlawful for any owner or agent to lease or to allow the use, maintenance, or occupancy 
of any residential dwelling or residential property that does not comply with the requirements of this 
Residential Rental Code. 
 
(B) Securing Structures: Any residential dwelling or residential property that is abandoned or 
uninhabited and is dilapidated, deteriorated or has become a place frequented by trespassers or transients 
or has otherwise been declared as a hazard shall be deemed a public nuisance. 
 
(C) The City Manager or authorized representative is hereby deemed a peace officer for the limited 
purpose of enforcing the provisions of this Residential Rental Code and shall have the power to issue 
complaints and summons for violations of these provisions, pursuant to Rule 204, Municipal Court Rules 
of procedure, as amended, and Chapter 22 of Title 1 of the Westminster Municipal Code. 
 
(D) Any person found guilty of violating any of the provisions of this Residential Rental Code shall, 
upon conviction thereof, be punished by a fine or imprisonment or both, pursuant to Section 1-8-1, W.M.C.

(F) In addition to all other penalties available, a violation of the provisions of this Rental Code may

8-2-9. Enforcement. 
 
If, after notice and order to correct, an owner fails to timely correct the violation and fails to timely appeal 
the notice and order, the City manager or authorized representative may issue a complaint and summons 
for prosecution in Municipal Court or for abatement as a nuisance. 
 
8-2-10. Appeal. 
 
(A) An owner may appeal a notice of noncompliance and order to correct to the Building and Fire Code 
Appeals Committee established in Title XI, Chapter 14, of the Westminster Municipal Code. Any such 
appeal shall be filed in writing with the City Manager within 30 days of the date of service of the notice of 
noncompliance. 
 
(B) The Building and Fire Code Appeals Committee shall hear the appeal within a reasonable time. 
Procedure for the hearing shall be as established in Title XI, Chapter 14, of the Westminster Municipal 
Code. Compliance with a notice of noncompliance shall be stayed until the committee has met and issued 
its decision. 
 
(C) Any appeal of the decision of the Committee shall be made to the District Court. The appellant 
shall pay for the costs of preparing a transcript and other expenses of preparation of the record of hearing 
before the committee. 
 
8-2-11 Remedies; Recovery of Costs. 
 
The cost of enforcement proceedings, together with the cost of abatement, if so ordered, shall be assessed 
in any judgment rendered. If the costs identified are not paid, they shall constitute a lien upon the property. 
 
8-2-12. Imminent Danger. 
 
(A) If any structure, premise or portion thereof is found to present an imminent hazard to life or health, 
the premises shall be posted, and the property shall be ordered vacated. Upon order to vacate, the property, 
or portion thereof, shall be posted as "Dangerous, Do Not Occupy" and written notification of the violations 
that deem the property, or portion thereof, as an imminent hazard shall be served as required in this Chapter. 
 
(B) It shall be unlawful for any person to remove or deface the posted notice, or to occupy the property 
or to enter the structure except for the purpose of repair. The violations identified as causing the property 
or portion thereof to be an imminent hazard shall be corrected and reinspected before the posting is removed 
and the property or portion thereof is reoccupied. 
 
(C) The owner or agent may appeal the order to vacate to the Building and Fire Code Appeals 
Committee in the same manner as stated in Section 8 -2-10, W.M.C., except that the duty to comply with 
the order to vacate shall not be stayed pending a hearing. 
 
(D) The provisions of this section are in addition to any other available procedures provided by law.
