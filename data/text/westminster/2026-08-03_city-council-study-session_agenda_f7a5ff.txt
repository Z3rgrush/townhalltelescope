Recommended City Council Action:
Receive a presentation outlining water, sewer, and stormwater rates and structures and provide 
direction for the 2027 and 2028 budget years. 
Summary Statement:
The City is focused on implementing the highest standards of responsible fiscal planning as 
outlined by the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) when developing the City’s

Page 2 of 3
Budget.
City Council completed a review of the 2026 budget development process with Staff in 
November 2025. Based on those discussions, Staff updated the budget development process 
including a new community-driven engagement process exploring water rates.
City Council established the Water Affordability To Everyone Resident (WATER) Task Force 
on April 6, 2026 to serve in an advisory capacity to the City with respect to water rates and 
affordability.
On July 6, 2026, City Council received a presentation and report from the WATER Task Force 
detailing the Task Force’s recommendations on water utility rates and rate structure.
City Council’s direction is sought on the 2027–2028 water and sewer utility rates and 
stormwater fees, incorporating the WATER Task Force’s water rate and design 
recommendations along with additional Staff recommendations for water rates based on City 
Council feedback, in addition to sewer rates and stormwater fees recommendations.
The City is fully committed to financial transparency and providing the public with easy access 
to financial information. To explore the City’s financial data, including the interactive OpenGov

 $0 in expenditures.

Not applicable.
Policy Issue(s):
Should City Council receive a presentation and provide direction on the City’s water and sewer rates 
and structure, as well as stormwater management fees, to support the sustainable and affordable 
operation and maintenance of the City’s utility infrastructure?
Alternative(s):
City Council could choose not to receive the presentation or provide direction on the City’s water, 
sewer, and stormwater rates and structure. This alternative is not recommended as Staff is preparing 
the budget for 2027 and the rates and fees are necessary components of the budget to sustainably 
and affordably support the City’s utility infrastructure operation and maintenance.
Background Information:
Following the adoption of the 2026 budget, City Council conducted a review of the budget 
development process with Staff to assess strengths and identify opportunities for improvement. 
Incorporating this feedback along with input from the budget development best practices 
presentation, department priority presentations, and Strategic Plan development and adoption, the 
2027 Budget Development Workflow was actively updated to reflect the leadership and direction of 
City Council.

Page 3 of 3
The City’s water, wastewater, and stormwater systems support the community every day, and their 
effectiveness depends on active monitoring of infrastructure conditions and planning for ongoing 
preventative maintenance, repair, and replacement. Providing safe, reliable utility infrastructure is 
central to the Resilient Infrastructure Strategic Priority. While emergency repairs do occur, the City’s 
goal is to provide uninterrupted, affordable, and dependable service to residents, businesses, and 
visitors. Consistent preventative maintenance, supported by adequate funding, is essential to 
achieving that goal.
In February 2026, City Council initiated the creation of the Water Affordability for Everyone Resident 
(WATER) Task Force to strengthen community understanding and transparency around water 
affordability and rate-setting. The WATER Task Force was established to learn about how water 
systems are funded and managed, which includes the complex trade-offs involved in water rate 
setting, infrastructure investment, and maintaining reliable service along with considering 
updates/expansion of affordability programs. The WATER Task Force was formally established in 
April 2026. During the July 6, 2026 Study Session, the Task Force presented a report outlining its 
recommendations for City Council’s consideration on the City’s affordability programs, rates, and rate 
structure for the 2027 and 2028 budget years to sustain the City’s water systems.
The presentation seeks City Council’s direction on the following items:
1. Transitioning the WATER Task Force into a permanent Utility Advisory Board to continue its 
advisory work on water rates and affordability, specifically the multi-family rate, while 
expanding its scope to include sewer and stormwater systems.
2. Water rates and structures for 2027 and 2028, incorporating WATER Task Force 
recommendations and adjustments based on City Council feedback from the July 6, 2026 
Study Session.
3. Sewer rates for 2027 and 2028.
4. Stormwater management fees for 2027 and 2028.
City Council’s direction and leadership to guide the development of the budget and establish the 
funding structure needed to support sustainable utility operations and capital projects supports the 
City’s Strategic Priorities of Connected and Inclusive Community, Healthy and Safe, and Resilient 
Infrastructure.

Recommended City Council Action:
Discuss the current noise regulations and provide Staff with direction on amending the Westminster 
Municipal Code to provide a regulatory ordinance for unlawful noise within the City.
Summary Statement:
City Council requested Staff to prepare an ordinance regulating excessive and disruptive noise 
within the City of Westminster.
A proposed ordinance to regulate unlawful noise was presented to City Council on June 22, 
2026 (Attachment 1). City Council postponed consideration of the ordinance and directed Staff 
to return with information for a follow-up discussion regarding the City's current approach to 
addressing noise concerns.
As a part of the research in preparation for the requested discussion, 21 surrounding 
jurisdictions were reviewed for how they address noise in their respective municipal codes. 
Twelve jurisdictions enforce noise concerns through a stand-alone unlawful noise ordinance, 
while nine jurisdictions enforce noise through multiple code sections similar to Westminster.
Currently, noise and noise-related impacts are addressed through different sections in

The proposed noise ordinance under consideration establishes decibel limits for properties in 
the city based on zoning and land use designations and is consistent with the limits set by 
state law and with comparable and neighboring jurisdictions that have similar ordinances.
Air traffic and ground airport noise are exclusively regulated by the Federal Aviation

Does City Council wish to direct Staff to bring back for consideration an ordinance amending the 
W.M.C. to address unlawful noise, or continue addressing noise concerns through education, 
outreach, and enforcement of existing ordinances?
Alternative(s):
1. City Council could choose not to further discuss this matter, however, this is not recommended

2. City Council could choose to continue to address and enforce noise concerns by connecting

services, neighborhood outreach and project coordination.
3. City Council could choose to proceed with developing an unlawful noise ordinance.
Background Information:
City Council asked Staff to bring forth an ordinance that would regulate excessive and disruptive 
noise in the City of Westminster. A proposed ordinance was presented to City Council on June 22, 
2026; this ordinance was not passed by City Council.
The proposed ordinance set maximum noise levels in decibels measured on the “A” scale of a 
standard sound level meter having characteristics defined by the American National Standards 
Institute (ANSI) publication S1. 4 - 1971. These maximum decibel levels were adjusted for time of day 
and zoning district, with the tightest restrictions set for residential areas.

Noise would have been assessed, measured, and enforced using ANSI certified and calibrated 
decibel monitors/readers. The proposed ordinance was consistent with similar and neighboring 
municipalities and mirrored Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.) Section 25-12-103, which provides 
reasonable noise decibel standards for enforcement purposes and sets the minimum level of noise 
restriction that may be enacted by municipalities. 
Following a review of the draft ordinance, City Council directed Staff to return with information for a 
follow-up discussion about current noise abatement ordinances and efforts.
As a part of this research in preparation for this discussion, 21 surrounding jurisdictions were 
reviewed for how they address noise in their respective municipal codes. Twelve jurisdictions enforce 
noise concerns through a stand-alone unlawful noise ordinance, while nine jurisdictions enforce noise 
through multiple code sections similar to the City of Westminster.
The Westminster Municipal Code currently contains eight separate sections that address noise and 
noise related impacts. Those include the following: 
Vehicle noise – Model Traffic Code section 225, adopted by the City in Section 10-1-1, 
W.M.C., and modified by Section 10-1-3(D) and (E), W.M.C.
Disturbing the peace – Section 6-2-3, W.M.C.
Animal noise – Section 6-7-7, W.M.C.
Noise within Parks, Recreation, Libraries and Open Space – Section 13-1-5, W.M.C.
Licensing Applications (dance halls) – Section 5-16-3, W.M.C.
Home Occupations – Section 11-4-10, W.M.C.
Oil and Gas Regulations – Section 11-4-14, W.M.C.
Zoning and Standards for Official Development Plans – Section 11-5-15, W.M.C.
The ordinance reviewed on June 22, 2026 is consistent with similar and neighboring municipalities 
and mirrors Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.) Section 25-12-103, which provides reasonable noise 
decibel standards for enforcement purposes and sets the minimum level of noise restriction that may 
be enacted by municipalities.
From the time period of July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026, the City of Westminster Police 
Department responded to 77,637 calls for service. Of those calls, 1,189 (0.015 percent) calls dealt 
with noise.
It is important to note that air traffic and ground airport noise are exclusively regulated by the Federal 
Aviation Administration and federal law. Similarly, train noise is regulated by a complex combination 
of state and federal laws. For this reason, a municipal ordinance will not address airport, air traffic, or

As City Council considers future options, there are two general paths. The first is to continue to 
address and enforce noise concerns with current ordinances and connecting and educating the 
community through calls for service, community events, mediation services, neighborhood outreach, 
and project coordination. The second is to pursue a stand-alone unlawful noise ordinance. Staff 
seeks direction from City Council as to the preferred next steps.
The City’s Strategic Plan Priority of Healthy and Safe is met by having a discussion and giving Staff 
direction on how to proceed with future noise complaints in the City of Westminster.

Subject: Overview of Proposed Updates to the Westminster Municipal Code to Modernize 
Tax Administration
Prepared By: Ezequiel Vasquez, Chief Financial Officer
Mark Harrelson, Sales Tax Manager
Recommended City Council Action:
Receive a presentation on proposed updates to the tax administration provisions of the Westminster 
Municipal Code and direct Staff to return with an ordinance amending Chapters 1 and 2 of Title IV to 
modernize notice, filing, and publication requirements and to authorize the Finance Director to specify 
the manner and form for the filing and payment of tax returns. 
Summary Statement:
The Westminster Municipal Code (W.M.C.) currently prescribes methods for tax return filing 
and taxpayer communication that are rooted in paper processes and are outdated, inflexible, 
and inefficient considering advances in communications technology and the ways that modern 
taxpayers engage with the world.
In 2025, taxpayers filed 77,424 City tax returns, and an estimated 94 percent were filed online 
through the Westminster tax system or the Colorado Department of Revenue Sales and Use

Tax System. 
The proposed Ordinance would authorize the Finance Director to specify the manner and form 
for filing tax returns, allowing the City to more efficiently keep up with best practices and 
technological updates. Under this authority, the City would require online filing and payment of 
all City tax returns effective January 1, 2027 through published tax compliance guidance.
Taxpayers seeking to file or pay by an alternative method could request an exception by 
written statement to the Finance Director at least 30 days prior to the end of the applicable 
filing period, preserving access for taxpayers without internet connectivity.
Because the filing method would be specified by the Finance Director rather than fixed in the 
W.M.C., future changes in technology or best practice would not require additional Ordinance 
amendments.
The proposed Ordinance would also permit electronic delivery of taxpayer notices, remove the 
requirement to post distraint sale notices at county courthouses, which the Jefferson and 
Adams County Courts no longer accommodate, and clarify the exemption language for certain 
medical items without any change in tax policy and without any change in tax revenue.
At least 12 self-collecting Colorado home-rule municipalities, including Arvada and Lakewood, 
already require online filing of sales and use tax returns, and 10 of the 12 also require 
electronic payment. The Colorado Department of Revenue is phasing in mandatory electronic 
filing of state retail sales tax returns, with all returns required to be filed electronically beginning 
in January 2028.

$0 in expenditures

Background Information:
History
Title IV of the W.M.C. governs the administration of the City’s taxes, including the filing of returns, the 
delivery of taxpayer notices, refund claims, assessments, protests, and enforcement. Much of this 
language predates current communications technology and prescribes paper-based methods, 
including first-class mail as the default delivery method for notices and the posting of distraint sale 
notices at county courthouses.
In 2025, taxpayers filed 77,424 City tax returns across three channels, as shown below. An estimated 
94 percent of returns arrived online, and the remaining 4,951 returns arrived on paper through the 
City’s bank lockbox.

Proposed Amendments
The proposed Ordinance would amend Subsection 4-1-7(A), W.M.C., to provide that the manner and 
form for the filing of returns shall be as specified by the Finance Director. This structure places the 
filing method in administrative guidance rather than in the W.M.C. itself, which allows the City to 
adopt improved methods more quickly and efficiently in the future without further Ordinance 
amendments and enables the City to keep up with peer municipalities. Any method specified by the 
Finance Director would preserve a filing avenue for taxpayers without internet connectivity.
The proposed Ordinance would also update the definition and delivery of notices to permit electronic 
delivery or any other verifiable delivery method reasonably calculated to provide notice to the 
taxpayer; remove redundant uses of the phrase “written notice” throughout Title IV, since notices are 
defined as written; permit publication of distraint sale notices in a newspaper of general circulation or 
on the City’s official website, with courthouse posting becoming discretionary; and clarify the 
exemption for prosthetic devices, prescription drugs, and items dispensed pursuant to a written order 
of a licensed practitioner of the healing arts. The exemption clarification would ease the interpretive 
and administrative burden on taxpayers without increasing taxes, without implementing any change in 
tax policy, and without any resulting significant increase in tax revenue.

Neighboring Arvada requires sales and use tax returns and payments to be completed online. 
Lakewood’s municipal code requires returns and payments through its online filing system unless the 
Chief Financial Officer approves another method, a process similar to that proposed for Westminster. 
The Colorado Department of Revenue is also phasing in mandatory electronic filing of state retail 
sales tax returns based on prior-year activity, with all returns required to be filed electronically 
beginning in January 2028.
Implementation and Business Outreach
Under the authority provided by the proposed Ordinance, the City would issue tax compliance 
guidance requiring that, effective January 1, 2027, all City tax returns be filed online through the 
Westminster tax system or the Colorado Department of Revenue Sales and Use Tax System and that 
all associated payments be submitted electronically. A business seeking to file or pay by an 
alternative method could submit a written statement to the Finance Director at least 30 days prior to 
the end of the applicable filing period. Businesses that fail to comply without an approved exception 
may be subject to late filing or late payment penalties, interest, and assessment based on estimated 
tax liability.
In advance of the January 1, 2027, effective date, the City would notify all licensed businesses of the 
online filing and payment requirement and the process for requesting an exception, and would publish
