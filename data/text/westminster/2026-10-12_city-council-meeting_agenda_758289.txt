1. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE 
Mayor Pro Tem Nurmela led City Council, Staff, and the audience in the Pledge of Allegiance. 
 
2. ROLL CALL 
Mayor Pro Tem Nurmela, and Councillors Barajas, Ezeadi, Hott, and Ireland were present at roll 
call.  Councillor Johnson was absent. Mayor Carmelia joined the meeting virtually following roll 
call.  
 
Also present were Acting City Manager Larry Dorr, Deputy City Manager Barbara Opie, City 
Attorney Dave Frankel, and City Clerk Abby Fitch.

Background Information:
On Indigenous Peoples’ Day, the sovereignty, resilience, and immense contributions that Native 
Americans have made to the world is honored.
For centuries, Indigenous Peoples were forcibly removed from ancestral lands, displaced, 
assimilated, and banned from worshiping or performing many sacred ceremonies. Yet today, they 
remain some of our greatest environmental stewards. They maintain strong religious beliefs that still 
feed the soul of our Nation. Native peoples challenge us to confront our past and do better, and their 
contributions to scholarship, law, the arts, public service, and more continue to guide us forward.
We acknowledge the painful history of wrongs and atrocities that many European explorers inflicted 
on Tribal Nations and Indigenous communities. It is a measure of our greatness as a nation that we 
do not seek to bury these shameful episodes of our past - that we face them honestly, bring them to 
light, and do all we can to address them. For Native Americans, western exploration ushered in a 
wave of devastation: violence perpetrated against Native communities, displacement, theft of Tribal 
homelands, the introduction and spread of disease, and more. We recognize this painful past and 
recommit ourselves to honoring Native communities, upholding our solemn and sacred commitments 
to Tribal sovereignty, and pursuing a brighter future centered on dignity, respect, justice, and 
opportunity for all people.
Indigenous Peoples’ Day was first proposed in 1977 by a delegation of Native Nations to the United 
Nations-sponsored International Conference on Discrimination Against Indigenous Populations in the 
Americas. The United States also endorsed the United Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous 
People on December 19, 2010, and Article 15 of that declaration reads:
Indigenous peoples have the right to the dignity and diversity of their cultures, traditions, histories and 
aspirations, which shall be appropriately reflected in education and public information.
The City of Westminster recognizes that Indigenous Peoples have lived and flourished on the lands 
known as the Americas since ancient time, and that the City of Westminster is built upon the 
ancestral homelands of Indigenous tribes. The City values the vast contributions made to our 
community through Indigenous Peoples’ arts and culture, knowledge, and skills and, through these 
contributions, the City of Westminster has developed and thrived.
The Inclusivity Board reviewed and supports the attached proclamation.
Recognizing Indigenous Peoples’ Day supports the City's Strategic Priority of Connected and 
Inclusive Community by fostering equitable opportunities that help residents feel at home and 
connected in their community and empowered to live their best lives as we celebrate indigenous 
history and honor Native Americans for shaping the contours of this country since time immemorial.
Respectfully submitted,

WHEREAS, the City of Westminster recognizes that Indigenous Peoples have lived and flourished on the lands 
known as the Americas since ancient time, and that the City of Westminster is built upon the ancestral 
homelands of Indigenous tribes; and  
WHEREAS, the semi-permanent encampment of the Arapaho peoples in the vicinity of Gregory Hill gave 
course to future settlements that would become the birthplace of the City of Westminster; and  
WHEREAS, Colorado encompasses the ancestral homelands of 48 tribes and currently the City of Westminster 
is home to descendants of Indigenous tribal nations; and  
WHEREAS, the City of Westminster recognizes and values the vast contributions made to our community 
through Indigenous Peoples’ arts, culture, knowledge, and skills and through these contributions the City of 
Westminster has developed and thrived; and  
WHEREAS, Indigenous Peoples’ Day was first proposed in 1977 by a delegation of Native Nations to the 
United Nations-sponsored International Conference on Discrimination Against Indigenous Populations in the 
Americas; and 
WHEREAS, the United States endorsed the United Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous People on 
December 19, 2010, and Article 15 of that declaration reads: Indigenous Peoples have the right to the dignity 
and diversity of their cultures, traditions, histories and aspirations, which shall be appropriately reflected in 
education and public information; and 
WHEREAS, the City of Westminster desires to strengthen our community and increase resilience by 
acknowledging the good, as well as detrimental past; and  
WHEREAS, the City of Westminster stands with all who stand for justice and humanity, and condemns all acts 
of othering in the shape of overt acts or microaggressions; and 
WHEREAS, by recognizing marginalized groups, the City brings awareness to and provides education about 
such groups with the ultimate goal of unifying a community through understanding and acceptance,  
NOW, THEREFORE, I, CLAIRE CARMELIA, Mayor of the City of Westminster, on behalf of the entire City 
Council and Staff do hereby proclaim the second Monday of October as 
Indigenous Peoples’ Day 
in the City of Westminster and call upon the people of Westminster to join together in honoring the resilience 
and contributions of Indigenous Peoples in our community and across the country. 
Signed this 12th day of October, 2026.

Dear Mayor and Members of Westminster City Council,
As a former member of Westminster City Council, a longtime member of this community, and a parent
who has personally experienced the impact of a horrific crime in our neighborhood, I am deeply
disappointed by the direction Council appears to be considering regarding Automated License Plate
Reader (ALPR) technology.
I understand and respect the concerns surrounding privacy, government overreach, and the potential
misuse of technology. Those concerns deserve serious consideration. But I also believe there is an
important distinction between protecting our residents from the misuse of technology and denying
our police officers access to tools that can help protect those same residents from crime.
Our responsibility as elected officials is not simply to eliminate something because it carries
potential risks. It is to do the difficult work of establishing responsible policies, meaningful
oversight, transparency, and accountability so that valuable technology can be used
appropriately.
This issue is deeply personal to me.

The City supports and encourages business development by providing advocacy, technical 
assistance, connections to partner resources, trainings, informational workshops, events,

A core component of business development is business retention and expansion (BRE) 
activities. According to the International Economic Development Council (IEDC), 
approximately 80 percent of all new jobs created in a community come from existing 
businesses. While BRE services are applicable to all industries and sizes of businesses, 
the City places a special emphasis on supporting locally owned small businesses, since 
these businesses create the authentic fabric of the community and, according to the U.S. 
Small Business Administration (SBA), are responsible for creating 61 percent of all new 
jobs in a community. 
Westminster is home to more than 3,000 licensed businesses that provide a stable sales and

diversified local economy, and convenient access to a robust offering of goods and

successful and grow in the community, they are less likely to relocate or close. These

Background Information:
Since 2018, the City’s Economic Development Division (ED) has conducted a biennial survey of 
licensed businesses in Westminster. The survey provides an opportunity for business owners to 
share their perspectives on the current and future economic health of Westminster and provides the 
City with information about business needs, challenges, and opportunities. The results are used to 
inform business retention and expansion initiatives and support overall local economic resilience.
Through a competitive process completed in 2024, the City contracted with Denver-based Corona 
Insights (Corona), a market and research firm specializing in surveys. The 2026 survey was 
developed with guidance from Corona and an interdepartmental City team. Only slight modifications 
were made to the survey this year in order to preserve questions and track year-to-year 
comparisons.
The survey was open from June 1 through June 29, 2026. Invitations were mailed to the city’s 3,089 
licensed businesses, and respondents could participate online through a website link or QR code. 
The survey was offered in English and Spanish, with additional options available to businesses who 
contacted the City. The City received 154 responses, representing a response rate of 5.9 percent.  
While lower than in previous years, this is considered an acceptable response rate for business 
surveys. The survey margin of error is +/- 7.5 percent, which provides a 95 percent level of 
confidence in the results. The data collected was analyzed by industry type, legal structure, 
revenue, plans for employee growth or reduction, number of employees, zip code, and title of 
respondent. Results were also compared with the 2024 survey. Complete survey results are 
included, see Attachment 1.

like to cite rules and regulations without any 
explanation or helpful commentary.”

Q31. What, if anything, could the City do better to support the business 
community in Westminster? (Open-ended question. Coded responses 
shown.)
One third of businesses suggested the City could better support 
the business community by offering financial support. 
• Specific financial support suggestions: 
- “Small grants and loans to keep competing with big 
retail chains and grocery stores.”
- “Lower taxes and fees”
• Specific communication suggestions:
- “Individual departments would benefit from better 
listening skills (a touch of empathy would be 
welcomed), become more solution oriented and at 
least appear to want to help.”
• Specific suggestions to understand and support 
small businesses:
- “Support for minority in women owned businesses, 
creating low barrier minority supported business 
opportunities so that they can sustain their work in 
restaurants and consulting.”
- “Support those businesses that have been here a long 
time and with a decent revenue history. No need to 
squabble with businesses over fickle things like a 
barbed fence when trying to eliminate vandalism and 
property destruction. “
