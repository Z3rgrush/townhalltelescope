CITY OF WESTMINSTER, COLORADO 
MINUTES OF THE CITY COUNCIL MEETING 
HELD ON MONDAY OCTOBER 26, 2026, AT 6:30 P.M. 
 
1. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE 
Mayor Carmelia led City Council, Staff, and the audience in the Pledge of Allegiance. 
 
2. ROLL CALL 
Mayor Carmelia, Mayor Pro Tem Nurmela, and Councillors Barajas, Ezeadi, Hott, and Ireland, were 
present at roll call.  Councillor Johnson was absent.   Also  present were  City Manager Jody 
Andrews, Deputy City Manager Larry Dorr, City Attorney Dave Frankel, and City Clerk Abby Fitch. 
 
Nurmela moved, seconded by Barajas, to excuse Johnson due to being out of town.   
 
The motion passed on a (6-0) voice vote. 
 
3. CONSIDERATION OF MINUTES 
Nurmela moved, seconded by Hott, to approve the minutes of the October 12, 2026, City 
Council Meeting as presented.  
 
The motion passed on a (6-0) voice vote. 
 
4. PRESENTATIONS 
A. Proclamation for Energy Efficiency Month 
Carmelia presented the proclamation declaring October as Energy Efficiency Month.  
Sustainability Administrator Bridger Tomlin, Energy Policy Analyst Faith Ryan, and 
Ashley Firetag of Energy Outreach Colorado participated. 
 
5. PUBLIC COMMENT 
Rachel and Margo Gale expressed concerns regarding an ongoing homeless encampment 
near their home in South Westminster, including trash, neighborhood safety, and the well-
being of individuals experiencing homelessness. They requested additional City assistance to 
address the encampment and help those individuals relocate to a safer location. Rachel also 
expressed appreciation for the City's reporting system and Staff's ongoing efforts.   
 
Hannah Brady, President of the Skyline Greens Homeowners Association, expressed concerns 
regarding recurring incidents involving individuals riding motorized dirt bikes through 
neighborhood streets and common areas, including reports of vandalism, intimidation, and 
harassment of residents. She requested increased police patrols, enforcement of unsafe 
motorbike activity, and collaboration between the City, Police Department, and residents to 
address ongoing safety concerns.   
 
Avishek Mishra expressed concerns regarding repeated harassment, vandalism, and 
intimidation directed at his family by individuals riding motorized dirt bikes in the Skyline 
Greens neighborhood. He described incidents involving property damage, threatening 
behavior, and concerns about possible racial targeting. He requested that the Police 
Department investigate the incidents as potentially bias-motivated crimes, identify those 
responsible, and address the unsafe operation of motorized dirt bikes.   
 
Adyasha Mishra expressed concerns regarding ongoing harassment and intimidation of her 
family in the Skyline Greens neighborhood, including vandalism and incidents involving 
motorized dirt bikes. She described the emotional impact on her family and concerns that the

incidents may be racially motivated. She requested increased police patrols, investigation of 
potential racial bias, and accountability for those responsible to help restore neighborhood 
safety.   
 
Mark Kaiser expressed opposition to discontinuing the City's use of automated license plate 
reader (ALPR) technology, citing its value in criminal investigations and public safety. He 
encouraged City Council to evaluate the effectiveness of existing policies and consider stronger 
safeguards, including restrictions on data access, retention limits, and accountability measures, 
rather than eliminating the technology. He also expressed appreciation for the Police 
Department's efforts addressing homelessness near 72nd Avenue and Sheridan Boulevard.  
 
David DeMott, former Westminster City Councillor, expressed opposition to eliminating ALPR 
technology, citing its effectiveness in criminal investigations, including homicide, missing 
persons, and property crimes. He emphasized the importance of balancing public safety with

strengthen oversight, accountability, and data-use policies while retaining the technology. 
  
Julianne Freeman expressed appreciation for City Council's decision to withdraw the City's

emphasized the importance of establishing privacy protections and appropriate oversight

Jim Fenimore expressed appreciation for the Westminster Police and Fire Departments and 
their service to the community. He discussed concerns regarding homelessness and its impact 
on police resources, encouraging City Council to consider additional housing solutions and

improvements, and property maintenance concerns along 84th Avenue near Lowell Boulevard 
and the Uplands development.  
 
Tom Lampo offered a prayer for the City of Westminster, its elected officials, Staff, and 
residents. He prayed for unity, guidance, and wisdom for the community and the nation, 
particularly in advance of the upcoming election.   
 
Nancy McNally expressed opposition to eliminating the City's ALPR technology, citing its 
effectiveness in supporting criminal investigations and public safety. She encouraged City 
Council to evaluate existing policies, consider appropriate privacy protections, and work with 
Police Department leadership to establish safeguards and accountability measures rather than 
discontinue the technology.

City Manager Andrews highlighted the Fire Department's ongoing Fire Prevention Month 
activities and upcoming community outreach events. He recognized the Westminster Police 
Department's partnership with Raising Men and Women Lawn Care Service, through which

Overview of 2026 Drought Response Actions, Results, and Future Improvements; Westminster 
Housing Authority 2026 Budget Update; and Update on Regional Building Policy Collaborative 
Roadmap and Westminster Building Policy Collaborative Grant 
 
He announced upcoming City meetings and events, noted that City Council would not meet 
on November 2 due to the election, and reminded residents of the City's Access Westminster 
reporting resources. 
 
Nurmela thanked the City Manager for connecting residents of Skyline Greens with Police 
Chief Haubert following their public comments. She also asked whether there had been

7. CITY COUNCIL COMMENTS 
Nurmela thanked residents for their public comments and discussed the importance of 
ensuring all community members feel safe in their neighborhoods. She acknowledged 
concerns regarding homelessness and encouraged continued evaluation of City policies and 
collaboration with Staff and the Police Department to identify opportunities for improvement. 
She also discussed automated license plate reader (ALPR) technology, recognizing both its 
public safety benefits and concerns regarding potential misuse. She encouraged City Council 
to move forward promptly with discussions regarding appropriate policies and safeguards that 
could allow the responsible use of the technology. 
 
Ireland thanked residents for their comments and discussed her opposition to ALPR 
technology, citing concerns regarding government surveillance, privacy, and potential misuse 
based on experiences involving her family in other countries. She emphasized that her position 
did not reflect a lack of support for the Police Department. She also acknowledged concerns 
regarding homelessness and neighborhood safety, emphasizing the need for additional 
resources and solutions at the local, state, and federal levels. She thanked residents for 
identifying property maintenance and blight concerns in South Westminster and expressed 
support for maintaining safe and attractive neighborhoods throughout the City. 
 
Barajas discussed recent tours of the City's stormwater, water treatment, and wastewater 
facilities, expressing appreciation for Staff's expertise, infrastructure maintenance, and 
cybersecurity efforts. He highlighted advancements in stormwater inspection technology and 
encouraged consideration of equitable funding approaches for future infrastructure 
improvements. He recognized the Municipal Court's Wellness Court program and its efforts to 
support individuals experiencing homelessness, and highlighted the City's Business 
Appreciation Awards. He also discussed a recent North Area Transit Alliance meeting, 
including upcoming opportunities for public input on proposed improvements to Interstate 25 
between US 36 and 104th Avenue. He shared his experience participating in a Fire Department 
ride-along and thanked Staff for their work. He acknowledged concerns raised by Skyline 
Greens residents and reiterated their requests for increased police patrols, enforcement of 
unsafe motorized dirt bike activity, improved reporting procedures, and coordination between 
City departments, the Police Department, and the homeowners association to address 
neighborhood safety. 
 
Carmelia highlighted a recent panel discussion with Waterloop and WaterNow celebrating the 
City's new water conservation garden at City Hall. She emphasized the importance of native 
landscaping and responsible outdoor water use, noting that the garden is estimated to save 1.5 
million gallons of water annually. She also discussed Westminster's participation in a 
Waterloop documentary series highlighting municipal water conservation efforts. She 
addressed concerns regarding motorized dirt bikes and e-bikes, noting that Staff would bring 
the issue back to City Council for further consideration and expressing hope for statewide 
regulations. She emphasized the importance of creating a welcoming and inclusive 
community, particularly for immigrant families, and expressed concern regarding the 
harassment experienced by Skyline Greens residents. She also discussed the challenges of 
balancing public safety with individual freedoms in considering automated license plate 
reader technology, acknowledging the suggestions offered by residents while expressing 
concerns regarding data security and the limitations of policies in preventing data breaches.

8. CITY COUNCIL REQUESTS FOR FUTURE AGENDA TITLES 
Ireland requested a Study Session to explore a potential City branding initiative titled 
“Westminster Takes Flight,” highlighting opportunities to promote tourism, economic 
development, community identity, and partnerships with organizations such as the Butterfly 
Pavilion. Community Services Director Lindsey Kimball provided additional information 
regarding potential economic development benefits. Barajas and Ezeadi expressed support for 
further discussion, with Barajas emphasizing the importance of community engagement in 
any rebranding process. The request received sufficient support to move forward. 
 
Nurmela requested an update on proposed regulations for electric bicycles and motorized dirt 
bikes, emphasizing safety concerns involving higher-powered vehicles and the need for timely 
action. City Manager Andrews confirmed that Staff was evaluating regulatory options and 
practices in neighboring jurisdictions and would return to City Council with recommendations. 
 
Carmelia requested that City Council consider a future role in selecting the City's state and 
federal lobbyists, including potential participation in interviews following the procurement 
process. Ezeadi expressed support for discussing the selection process but cautioned against 
establishing a precedent for Council involvement in other consultant selections. The request 
received support for further discussion. City Manager Andrews confirmed that Staff would 
provide an overview of the current lobbyist selection process during the November 16, 2026, 
discussion of the City's legislative policy statement, allowing Council to provide direction for 
future selections. 
 
Barajas emphasized the importance of distinguishing higher-powered electric dirt bikes and 
motorcycles from lower-speed pedal-assist bicycles when considering future regulations. He 
requested that the distinction be addressed during Council's upcoming policy discussions. No 
separate poll was conducted.

Dear City Council,
The Environmental Advisory Board wishes to express its support for the proposed changes to 
the Westminster Municipal Code as it concerns the latest eﬀorts to modernize and expand its 
residential recycling and waste diversion opportunities.
Overhauling our city’s waste management system with the proposed framework will ensure 
that Westminster joins its neighbors in expanding service accessibility and encouraging 
eﬃcient resource management in a manner that preserves the existing, competitive and 
privately-operated hauling system that currently serves our residents without subjecting them 
to additional economic burdens.
Moreover, requiring haulers to oﬀer curbside compost collection is a reasonable extension of 
existing recycling requirements and the proposed phased approach will ensure that service 
providers can readily adapt while aﬀording our residents the freedom to determine how 
composting services meet their individual needs. Mandating documentation and clarifying 
reporting requirements from haulers will ensure that these policies remain subject to reliable 
sources of current and ongoing data to better inform any future reassessments.
Finally, as we gain a better of understanding of widespread environmental contaminants such 
as microplastics and their health eﬀects, Westminster should endeavor to remain aligned with 
state laws such as the Plastic Pollution Reduction Act; additional measures such as a “Skip 
the Stuﬀ” ordinance can ensure that both residents and businesses are united in combating 
these issues with ecologically-sound waste management approaches.
We encourage this City Council to advance the proposed code changes while adhering to 
principles that prioritize environmental stewardship, maintaining aﬀordability, preserving 
consumer choice, and encouraging private-sector competition. We believe these changes will 
establish measurable outcomes by which their long-term eﬀectiveness can be evaluated and 
ultimately serve as a guide for other Colorado communities.
Sincerely,
Westminster Environmental Advisory Board
Maria Rotunda, EAB Chair

Background Information:
Annually reviewing and updating the Legislative Policy Statement and Lobbying Protocols ensures 
the City’s legislative program remains aligned with the goals and priorities of the City’s Strategic Plan. 
These guiding documents also provide a framework for City Council and Staff to respond quickly to 
proposed legislation, strengthening the City’s ability to effectively advocate for Westminster’s interests 
throughout the legislative session.
Staff, along with the City’s state lobbyist, reviewed and updated the 2027 draft Legislative Policy 
Statement (Attachment 1) and Lobbying Protocols (Attachment 2). The proposed revisions to the draft 
Policy Statement and Lobbying Protocols seek to incorporate City Council feedback from the 2026 
legislative session and recent discussions while strengthening clarity and consistency throughout the 
documents.
The intent of the City’s Legislative Policy Statement is to identify general legislative issues of interest 
to the City along with the City's policy principles on these issues. The policy statements within the 
document are broad while being as inclusive as possible to capture important issues to the City. Staff 
will use the Policy Statement as a guide when reviewing and analyzing legislation that may have an 
impact on the City's interests.
Official positions are not automatically assumed simply because a bill aligns with the policy 
statements within the Legislative Policy Statement. Staff and the City’s lobbyists review proposed 
legislation, and when significant legislation is identified, Staff will provide City Council with a brief 
overview of the proposed legislation and a recommendation regarding an official City position that is 
consistent with the principles of the Legislative Policy Statement.

OVERVIEW 
This Legislative Policy Statement identifies the state and federal legislative issues of 
greatest interest to the City of Westminster and the principles that guide the City's 
positions on them. These statements are necessarily broad and are not all-inclusive. It is a 
discretionary reference tool: City Council approves the Legislative Policy Statement 
annually, Staff briefs City Council on significant legislation as it arises, and the City takes an 
official position only by concurrence of a majority of Councillors — no position is 
automatically assumed based on this document alone.

POLICY PRINCIPLES 
AFFORDABLE HOUSING   
The City of Westminster recognizes that access to affordable housing — both rental and 
ownership — is essential to a healthy, inclusive community and a stable local economy. 
The City supports a range of tools, including transit-oriented development, land use 
flexibility, and dedicated funding, to expand housing supply and affordability, while 
preserving the local control needed to implement them responsibly. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports a dedicated statewide fund for affordable housing development and 
preservation, including ownership options, and fair, transparent leasing practices 
that protect renters without imposing unreasonable burdens on housing providers. 
➤ Supports state efforts that facilitate Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) and 
housing in Transit-Oriented Communities (TOCs), provided they include adequate 
funding, flexible compliance paths, and respect for local planning authority. 
➤ Opposes legislation that imposes undue burdens on housing development or limits 
the City's local land-use and redevelopment tools, including growth impact fees, 
tax increment financing, and eminent domain. 
ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT 
The City of Westminster strives to develop and maintain a dynamic and diverse economy. 
Whether maintaining a healthy retail base, retaining and expanding targeted businesses 
and primary employers, or furthering affordable and workforce housing opportunities, the 
City recognizes the importance of a healthy economic climate to overall quality of life. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports legislation and funding that strengthen the local economy — business 
development and retention, workforce training, and expanded access to affordable 
utilities, childcare, and other essential services. 
➤ Opposes legislation that imposes undue burdens on local businesses or otherwise 
weakens the City's economic vitality. 
GENERAL FINANCE 
The City of Westminster is a full-service community. While sales and use tax revenue 
comprises the primary funding source for general government services, the City's wide 
variety of services are also funded through a balanced array of other taxes, user fees, and 
other financial resources. Consequently, the City of Westminster is impacted by state and 
federal financial policies.

GOVERNMENTAL IMMUNITY 
The City of Westminster recognizes that the complexity and diversity of City operations 
and services required to meet the needs of residents and businesses may expose the City, 
its officers, and its employees to liability for damage and injury. The City believes public 
officers and employees need assurance that municipal liability will not impair the lawful 
and proper provision of necessary services to the public. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports legislation that protects municipalities, officers, and employees from 
undue liability in the lawful performance of their duties, and preserves affordable 
access to liability insurance, including through self-insurance. 
➤ Opposes legislation that expands municipal liability or narrows governmental 
immunity. 
HOME RULE AND LOCAL CONTROL 
The City of Westminster believes strongly in the principles of home rule authority and local 
control. Article XX of the Colorado Constitution grants home rule municipalities such as 
Westminster “the full right of self-government in local and municipal matters.” The City of 
Westminster believes that home rule authority increases the effectiveness and efficiency 
of local government services, enhancing the quality of life in the community and the value 
provided to local taxpayers. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports legislation that upholds and strengthens home rule authority and local 
control,  
➤ Opposes legislation that weakens it or mandates unnecessary state intervention in 
local matters.

Employees are the City of Westminster's most important resource. The City is an equal 
opportunity employer and works to ensure excellent and fair salaries and benefits for 
employees, along with growth and development opportunities. Like most other 
organizations, the City's budget is continuously pressured by increasing compensation

LAND USE, DEVELOPMENT, AND REVITALIZATION 
The City believes that local control of land use planning contributes greatly to the creation 
of a vibrant, inclusive, and engaged community and the overall quality of life in 
Westminster. The City supports increasing housing supply and density — including near 
transit — and believes redevelopment and revitalization succeed best when appropriate 
urban renewal tools are preserved and strengthened, and implementation remains locally 
tailored, adequately funded, and flexible rather than driven by rigid state mandates.   
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports local control of land use planning and the tools needed for
redevelopment, including tax increment financing and eminent domain.
➤ Supports state efforts that facilitate Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) and
housing in Transit-Oriented Communities (TOCs), provided they include adequate
funding, flexible compliance paths, and respect for local planning authority.
➤ Opposes municipal service provisions to data centers due to their environmental

➤ Opposes rigid, one-size-fits-all state land-use mandates and legislation that is
inconsistent with Westminster policies and plans.
➤ Opposes mandates or incentives for land uses that place an undue burden on
natural or environmental resources such as water supply, air quality, and the power
grid, or conflict with the City’s sustainability policies and priorities.

The City of Westminster recognizes the importance of supporting mental health initiatives 
that enhance the safety and well-being of the City's residents and businesses, as well as 
the City's employees who may, in the course of their work, encounter residents in need of

PUBLIC SAFETY 
The City of Westminster works diligently to keep residents and businesses safe anywhere 
in the City. The Police Department enforces the law in a fair and impartial manner, 
recognizing both the statutory and judicial limitations of police authority and the 
constitutional rights of all persons. The Fire Department works to minimize injuries and 
property losses due to fire, provides quality emergency medical care and transport 
services, and provides other services including fire inspections and emergency 
preparedness planning. The Municipal Court enforces the City's ordinances in an effective 
and judicious manner. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports resources for crime victims, behavioral and mental health response, and 
effective, adequately funded law enforcement, fire, and court services.
➤ Supports an integrated approach to fentanyl and opioid possession that combines 
enforcement, accountability for sellers and distributors, and treatment and 
diversion programs, while preserving officer discretion to take offenders into 
custody when necessary for public safety.
➤ Supports legislation to prevent auto theft, copper/wire theft, and street racing.
➤ Supports legislation that restricts and limits automated license plate reader 
technology and data retention by law enforcement.

SALES AND USE TAX 
The City of Westminster levies, administers, and collects its own sales and use taxes under

actions at the federal, state, and local levels must preserve or enhance this critical local 
revenue. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports local control over the imposition, collection, and administration of sales
and use taxes, including on remote sales
➤ Opposes legislation that preempts this authority.
STATE AND FEDERAL MANDATES 
Programs and regulations mandated by state or federal governments have the potential 
to adversely impact the financial resources of the City of Westminster. If additional costs 
brought about by these mandated programs or regulations are not paid by the state or 
federal governments, they can have a direct negative impact on the City budget and can 
prevent the City from meeting the needs of residents and businesses and achieving the 
City's strategic priorities. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports full state reimbursement for the cost of state mandates, consistent with
Taxpayer Bill of Rights (TABOR)
➤ Opposes unfunded state and federal mandates that impose unfair financial

and private partners to implement and manage efficient, cost-effective, and 
environmental sustainability and climate protection programs. It is the intent of the City to 
meet or exceed compliance with all applicable environmental laws and regulations. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports legislation and funding that promote pollution prevention, environmental

TELECOMMUNICATIONS AND UTILITIES 
The City of Westminster recognizes the importance of telecommunications and utility 
services — including broadband, cable, wireless, and digital services, as well as a reliable 
energy supply — to economic development and the quality of life in the City. The City 
supports increased competition in the telecommunications market and has taken steps to 
work with providers interested in serving the community, while ensuring residents and 
businesses have access to reliable, reasonably priced utility service. The City's view is that 
statewide franchising should not override the City's authority to ensure the best possible 
service to its residents and businesses and to regulate the use of public right-of-way. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports preserving municipal franchising, rights-of-way, and regulatory authority 
over telecommunications systems including the installation of fiber. 
➤ Opposes state or federal restrictions on franchising, rights-of-way, and regulatory 
authority over telecommunications systems. 
➤ Opposes legislation that fails to ensure a reliable and sufficient energy supply for 
residents and businesses, and increases ratepayer burdens.    
TRANSPORTATION AND MOBILITY 
The City of Westminster believes the safe movement of goods and people is vital to the 
continued economic success of the State of Colorado and to the maintenance of the high 
quality of life that Coloradans enjoy. To preserve them, the state legislature must make 
significant investments to maintain and improve the state's transportation network, 
including roads, bridges, transit, and multimodal systems. The City opposes efforts to pass 
along state roadway construction or maintenance responsibilities to local governments 
without increased and adequate funds to meet these additional responsibilities. 
Therefore, the City of Westminster: 
➤ Supports adequate, sustained state and federal funding for regional transportation, 
transit, and multimodal infrastructure, including completion of the B Line. 
➤ Supports Regional Transportation District (RTD) rail and bus service. 
➤ Opposes shifting state transportation funding or maintenance responsibilities to 
the City without adequate resources.

This document describes the manner in which City Council participates in legislative 
matters that impact City operations, both with federal and state legislative bodies, as 
well as with local, regional, state and federal agencies. 
Information regarding the City’s legislative program may be find on the City’s website:  
City of Westminster - Legislative Action 
The Lobbying Protocols are set forth below in detail, and a “Quick Reference” chart is 
included as “Appendix A” and may be found at the end of this document. 
1. Roles
• City Council. City Council is the only body that can adopt an official City position.
Council sets priorities through the adopted Legislative Policy Statement.
• Staff. The Assistant City Manager and Policy & Budget Staff (Staff) track
legislation, prepare analysis and position recommendations, run Council polls,
record positions, and coordinate the City’s lobbyists.
• Legislative Lobbyists. The City retains a state and a federal legislative lobbyist to
monitor legislation, advise on issues, and advocate for the City’s registered
positions.
• Subject matter experts (SMEs). Department-level Staff provide technical
analysis on legislation that affects their areas.
• City Council designee. City Council may name a Councillor as the point person
for a specific bill or policy area. The designee may work directly with Staff and the
lobbyists.

2. Position Types 
City Council may adopt any of the following positions on state or federal legislation, or 
on a related policy, regulation, or funding matter. 
Position Meaning 
Support The City supports the bill as written. 
Oppose The City opposes the bill as written. 
Support if Amended The City will support the bill if specific amendments are 
made. 
Oppose Unless 
Amended 
The City opposes the bill unless specific amendments are 
made. If the specific amendments are made, the City's 
position will shift to monitor, support, or oppose. The shift to 
monitor, support, or oppose will depend on the 
amendments and can occur without another Council vote. 
Amend The City seeks specific changes to the bill without taking a 
support or oppose position. Council may revisit the position 
upon passage of amendments.  
Monitor (Official 
Position) 
Council is neutral and has chosen not to support or oppose 
the bill, but wants it tracked officially. The bill is listed on the 
City and Secretary of State websites as Monitor. 
 
Monitor (Pending Review). Separate from the official Monitor position, Staff and the 
lobbyists may place a bill in Monitor (Pending Review) status when they need more 
information before recommending a position. This status is not a City Council position 
and does not authorize communication on a position with legislators or agency staff 
on the City’s behalf. 
Amend-type positions can build negotiating leverage and strengthen relationships 
with bill sponsors and other key stakeholders. 
3. How City Council Adopts a Position 
3.1 Staff Review and Recommendation 
Staff reviews each bill for its potential impact on the City and its alignment with the 
Legislative Policy Statement. Staff then gives Council a brief summary, the 
recommended position, and the response deadline.

3.2 Polling Methods 
Staff brings a recommendation to City Council by one of two methods: 
• Council meeting poll. City Council is polled during a meeting to show agreement 
or disagreement with Staff’s recommendation. 
• Email poll. Staff emails the summary and recommendation. Each Councillor 
replies directly to Staff, not to other Councillors, by the stated deadline. 
Legislation is often time-sensitive, so timely replies are essential to the City’s 
ability to affect the outcome. 
3.3 Majority Required 
An official position requires the agreement of a majority of the full City Council (four of 
seven members), whether polled at a meeting or by email.  If four members do not 
agree during polling done in a meeting or by the deadline stated in the email, the City 
takes no position, and Staff continue to actively track the legislation. 
3.4 Reporting Poll Results 
For email polls, Staff notifies City Council which members agreed and which 
disagreed with the recommendation before the position is published. 
3.5 Recording Positions 
Staff enters each official position on the “Legislative Action” page (link above) of City’s 
website as soon as practical and notifies the lobbyists at the same time. The lobbyist 
enters the official position on the Secretary of State website. The City website is 
available to legislators and the public. 
3.6 Changing Votes 
• A Councillor who wants to change a vote cast in a meeting or email poll must 
state the change on the record at the next City Council meeting. 
• An individual change does not change the City’s position unless it changes the 
majority outcome. If it does, Staff and the lobbyist update the respective 
websites as soon as practical.  
• If a bill is significantly amended after City Council acts, Staff may bring it back to 
City Council with a revised recommendation. 
3.7 Monthly Report 
Staff provides a monthly “Information Only” Agenda Memo listing positions adopted 
by email poll during the previous month.

4. Federal Positions 
The same process applies to federal legislation and related policy, regulatory, or 
funding matters. City Council generally supports or opposes federal issues but may 
suggest amendments. The City may partner with other municipalities or organizations 
for a stronger collective position. Once Staff SMEs have evaluated and provided 
necessary information, the City’s federal legislative lobbyist reviews such feedback, 
provides advice, and coordinates communication with the congressional delegation 
and federal agencies. 
5. Monitoring and Advocacy 
5.1 Monitoring 
Staff and the lobbyists may monitor any bill at any time without City Council action. 
Monitoring includes tracking bills, analyzing impacts, attending hearings, and 
coordinating with the Colorado Municipal League (CML) and other partners. 
5.2 Advocacy 
Any communication on the City’s position with state or federal legislators, their staff, 
or state or federal agency staff on the City’s behalf requires a registered City position. 
This applies to Councillors, Staff, and the lobbyists. When the registered position is 
Monitor, communication is limited to saying the City is tracking the bill. Support or 
opposition may not be expressed. 
Anyone who contacts a legislator or agency on specific legislation must notify Staff, so 
the lobbyists stay informed and City messaging stays consistent. 
5.3 Coordination with the Lobbyists 
• Central point of contact. Staff coordinates the City’s work with the lobbyists, 
including tracking, obtaining City Council positions, and research. 
• Council contact with the lobbyists. Councillors may reach the lobbyists either 
through Staff or directly. When a Councillor contacts a lobbyist directly, it is the 
lobbyist’s responsibility to inform Staff. 
• Council contact with SMEs. When needed, especially on time-sensitive matters, 
Councillors may contact department SMEs directly. SMEs inform Staff of these 
communications.

6. Representing the City at CML, DRCOG, Metro Mayors Caucus, 
and Other Organizations 
Councillors represent the City on organizations including the Colorado Municipal 
League (CML), the Denver Regional Council of Governments (DRCOG), and Metro 
Mayors Caucus. They may be asked to vote on legislation, sometimes before the City 
has acted. 
• City position exists. Vote in line with City Council’s official position. 
• No position but covered by the Legislative Policy Statement. Use your best 
judgment to vote in line with the Legislative Policy Statement and City Council’s 
likely position. 
• No position and not covered. State that the City has no official position and that 
the matter will go to City Council first. Abstain if needed. 
A Councillor who votes in a public forum on an issue without a City position must 
report that vote to City Council and Staff at the next meeting. 
7. Testimony at the Colorado State Capitol 
Councillors and Staff are encouraged to testify on the City’s behalf on bills with a 
registered City position. Before testifying, notify the City Manager’s Office so it can 
confirm the City’s position and notify City Council and the lobbyists for awareness. 
Testimony should be coordinated with the lobbyists and, where appropriate, with 
other partners. 
City Council or Staff whose testimony does not match the City’s position must state 
that they are testifying on their own behalf, or on behalf of another group or coalition, 
and not for the City.

Background Information:
In 2018, the City began development of its first Sustainability Plan as a guiding framework for the City 
and its community partners to help Westminster become a more sustainable and inclusive 
community. The resulting plan, which focuses on actions that can be implemented within the next six 
to 10 years as part of the City’s Strategic Plan, was completed in May 2021. It is organized around 
seven sustainability themes: energy, economic resilience, health and wellness, housing and 
neighborhoods, materials and waste, transportation and mobility, and natural resources and 
environment. The Plan also includes five cross-cutting concepts: education, equity, climate, 
resilience, and safety. Each of the themes contain objectives, targets, and strategies that can be 
applied by Staff to guide annual work plans, by elected officials to prioritize policy action, and by 
community partners to identify opportunities to collaborate and contribute.
Since completion of the Plan, the City has tracked and reported progress on targets and strategies 
annually. Data has been collected across all departments and organized into a spreadsheet for 
internal analysis and tracking, which has served as the basis for an annual assessment. An annual 
report was provided to City Council via Information Only memos and to the community through a 
resident-friendly report that highlighted notable outcomes and results (see Attachment 2: 2025 
Sustainability Plan Report). This report was available on the City’s website and in print at events and 
City Hall. Though thorough in their design and content, these annual reports provided little opportunity 
for the community to interactively engage with the content.
This year, the City modernized the reporting process and transparency of its sustainability targets and 
strategies. Through the virtual WISE dashboard, residents and businesses can now see progress on 
Plan goals, keep up to date with new projects, and better understand the commitments the City has 
made for the future. The dashboard also provides an entirely new backend data tracking system, 
allowing for more consistent, standardized, and transparent data collection. The screenshots included 
in Attachment 1: WISE Dashboard Screenshots showcase the visual design and interactive nature of 
the platform. 
By transitioning to the WISE dashboard, the City has transformed a once static document into a living 
resource for residents, businesses and Staff improving our internal data tracking. The City is one of 
only four other municipalities in Colorado that has such a dashboard to transparently display progress 
on sustainability plan strategies and targets.
The dashboard can be accessed at: wise.westminsterco.gov.
Staff will continue to report annually to City Council the key outcomes of and progress on our 
sustainability strategies and targets through Information Only memos. Staff will also continue to 
create physical materials communicating such outcomes to ensure that residents and businesses 
receive essential information on the City’s sustainability targets and strategies regardless of their 
ability to access the virtual WISE dashboard. 
The WISE Dashboard meets the City’s Strategic Plan priority of fostering a Connected and Inclusive 
Community by providing a new, interactive way for residents to engage with the City’s Sustainability 
Plan and up-to-date data and information on our city-wide progress.

Policy Issue(s):
Should City Council approve the proposed opioid settlement with Albertsons?
Alternative(s):
City Council could choose not to approve the proposed opioid settlement with Albertsons. This

Background Information:
The City of Westminster is participating with numerous cities and counties throughout Colorado and 
nationwide in a joint litigation against manufacturers and distributors of opioid medications. The 
litigation seeks to recover resources to help the City deal with the fallout from the ongoing nationwide 
opioid crisis. City Council previously approved a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the 
Colorado Attorney General’s Office and many of the most important municipal and county 
stakeholders in Colorado. The MOU governs how any resulting settlement funds from the opioid 
litigation are to be distributed and managed among the state, local, and municipal governments, 
including the currently proposed Albertsons opioid settlement.
Under the proposed opioid settlement with Albertsons, the City could expect to receive approximately 
$69,362.50 in additional funding over the course of nine years. The funds would be used in 
accordance with the MOU to respond to the ongoing opioid crisis. In addition to these funds that 
would come directly to the City of Westminster, additional funds will be made available to other 
government entities in the region that will also be used by those entities to respond to the opioid crisis 
and thus benefit the citizens of Westminster.
Approving the proposed Albertsons opioid settlement would support the City’s Strategic Priority of 
Healthy and Safe by providing additional funds that can be productively used to help the City respond 
to the ongoing opioid crisis.

 Given the importance of having data-driven analysis, Staff recommends purchasing the

2. City Council could direct Staff to not collect additional sidewalk and ADA ramp condition data. 
Staff does not recommend this option. Updated condition information is needed to ensure proper 
drainage, extend the life of roadway surfaces, maintain accessibility for all residents, and support 
accurate budgeting and planning. Up-to-date data also helps the City make the best use of 
available funding.
Background Information:
A pavement management system is an essential tool that helps the City maintain its roadway assets, 
evaluate their condition, and plan long-term maintenance. The Public Works and Utilities Department 
has used such a system for more than 35 years. In 2017, City Council authorized the purchase of 
RoadMatrix, a pavement asset management system owned by Stantec. RoadMatrix collects and 
analyzes data on the City’s 1,138 miles of asphalt pavement. The data provided through RoadMatrix 
for the asphalt has been invaluable in maximizing City resources for roadways. As the City focuses 
on improving neighborhood streets, assessing the concrete that needs replacement before asphalt 
work begins is becoming more important for effective planning and accurate budgeting.
The City maintains approximately 740 miles of sidewalk. Based on positive experience with 
RoadMatrix data for asphalt maintenance, Staff recommends purchasing the sidewalk assessment 
module to fully integrate decision-making for concrete and asphalt repair and replacement. The 
sidewalk assessment module through RoadMatrix will support data-driven analysis for managing 
sidewalk and ADA ramp assets. The system uses up to 20 condition measurements to determine an 
SCI.
Data is collected using an e-trike or scooter equipped with cameras that capture high-resolution, 
time-lapse images every half-second as shown in Attachment 1. Sidewalk conditions are rated, and 
ADA ramps are measured for slope and dimensions to determine compliance. The SCI information 
will be added to the City’s existing RoadMatrix pavement database and used together with the 
Pavement Quality Index (PQI) to develop cost-effective concrete replacement and asphalt 
rehabilitation programs.
Stantec’s fees include quality control, data uploading, and verification that the RoadMatrix system 
functions correctly with the new data. No other vendor is qualified to perform this work as RoadMatrix 
is a proprietary asset management tool owned and maintained by Stantec. Staff recommends 
awarding a sole-source contract to Stantec.
Adequate funds have been budgeted for these services, not to exceed $315,550 for fiscal years 
2027–2030. The total 2027 cost for concrete and ADA ramp data collection and software services is 
$296,575, plus $15,000 contingency. The software licensing for data collection is $1,325 annually in 
each of the years 2028, 2029, and 2030. Annual license renewals beyond 2027 will be subject to 
future appropriation.
This sole-source contract supports the City’s Strategic Priority of Resilient Infrastructure by ensuring 
the City’s street and sidewalk networks are properly maintained.
