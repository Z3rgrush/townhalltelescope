Mayor Carmelia, on behalf of City Council, presented the Government Finance Officers

5. PUBLIC COMMENT 
Alan Farb spoke regarding homelessness in Westminster, particularly in South Westminster 
and around the Irving Street Library. He described his six and a half years of volunteer street 
outreach providing food, water, hygiene supplies, clothing, information about ava ilable 
resources, and personal support to unhoused residents. He acknowledged concerns associated 
with homelessness but emphasized the need to treat unhoused individuals with dignity and 
compassion and provide safe housing and access to medical, mental hea lth, dental, and 
substance-use services. He encouraged community members to join the City in being part of 
the solution.   
 
Christine Wares expressed concerns regarding ongoing public safety issues associated with the 
Rodeo Convention Center near the Downtown Westminster residential area. She reported 
hearing multiple gunshots over the weekend and stated that shell casings were subsequently 
recovered. She also described a separate incident involving an individual with a bat that was 
de-escalated by one of her staff members before police arrived. She questioned what would 
need to occur before additional action is taken and asked the City to address the continuing 
safety concerns.   
 
Dorothy Chaffee expressed concerns regarding public safety and activities associated with the 
Rodeo, including the type of music performed at the venue, which she stated promotes illegal 
and criminal activity. She referenced the recent gunfire and shell casings recovered near the 
property and stated that nearby residents are fearful for their safety. She asked Council for 
assistance in addressing the ongoing behavior associated with the venue before someone is 
seriously injured or killed.

Jeremy Parker expressed concerns regarding continued late-night disturbances and public 
safety issues associated with the Rodeo. He described being awakened at approximately 1:30 
a.m. by gunfire and having to duck for safety on his balcony while his eight -year-old son was

George Miller expressed concerns regarding homelessness in Westminster and questioned 
why police do not immediately remove individuals residing in public spaces. He referenced 
provisions of the Westminster Municipal Code and criticized the City's approach to 
homelessness, including what he described as the "continuum of compassion" policy. He 
described experiences involving homelessness, drug use, encampments, litter, and sanitation

whether the City plans to streamline the process for removing homeless individuals from 
public spaces.   
 
Wendy McKendrick expressed opposition to the use of automated license plate readers and 
other surveillance cameras. She stated that she supports public safety and law enforcement 
but is concerned about the collection and potential future use of information regarding the 
movements of individuals who have not committed crimes. She discussed constitutional 
privacy protections, the potential expansion or misuse of surveillance technology, and 
questions regarding whether the public-safety benefits justify the collection of large amounts 
of data. She asked the City not to allow the surveillance cameras.    
 
Brandon Gigous expressed opposition to automated license plate readers, specifically 
discussing cameras manufactured by Flock Safety. He described concerns regarding the 
collection and storage of license plate information and vehicle characteristics, nationwide 
database searches, warrantless access, sharing of information with other agencies, and the 
potential for data breaches or misuse. He stated that although he supports law enforcement 
and trusts the Westminster Police Department, he is concerned about the broader sur veillance 
system and opposed the use of automated license plate reader cameras.   
 
Tom Lampo offered a prayer for the City of Westminster. He prayed for the safety of children 
returning to school, encouraged drivers and others to remain aware of their surroundings and 
consider the interests of others, and prayed for clarity and understanding for students. He also 
prayed for school resource officers, asking that they be given discretion, understanding, and 
sound judgment when responding to challenging situations in schools.

Background Information:
In 1955, DAR petitioned Congress to set aside September 17 though 23 annually to be dedicated to 
the observance of Constitution Week. The resolution was later adopted by the U.S. Congress and 
signed into Public Law #915 on August 2, 1956, by President Dwight D. Eisenhower.
Goals of the celebration include the following:
Encourage Study: Promote learning about the historical events that led to the framing of the 
Constitution in 1787.
Highlight Heritage: Remind citizens that the document is the basis for American’s great 
heritage and the foundation of our way of life.
Emphasize Responsibility: Call attention to citizens’ responsibilities to protect and defend the 
Constitution.
The United States of America functions as a Republic under the Constitution, which is the oldest 
document still in active use that outlines the self-government of a people. This landmark idea that 
individuals had the inalienable right to be free and live their lives under their own governance was the 
impetus of the American Revolution. Today, the Constitution stands as an icon of freedom for people 
around the world. Constitution Week is an opportunity to learn more about the Constitution and to 
celebrate the freedoms it bestows. 
Proclaiming Constitution Week is symbolic of the freedoms granted in the United States Constitution 
and enjoyed by the residents of Westminster. This recognition meets the City’s Strategic Plan priority 
of Connected and Inclusive by celebrating the connection of community and the inclusivity of shared 
freedoms.
Respectfully submitted,

Background Information:
City Council approved the 2026 CDBG Annual AAP on July 27, 2026. The current AAP includes 
projects established in the City’s filed 2025-2029 Consolidated Plan, which is required by the U.S. 
Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The CDBG Consolidated Plan sets the goals 
for the City’s CDBG investments and programming over five years. The 2025-2029 CDBG 
Consolidated Plan includes the establishment of a grant program to ensure the continued availability 
of existing affordable residential properties. 
The Housing LIFT grant program can provide up to $500,000 to local, non-profit, affordable housing 
providers to improve and maintain existing properties that house residents who earn between 30 
percent and 80 percent of Area Median Income (AMI). These grants are intended for non-profit 
housing providers to make property improvements and address health and safety needs for the 
residents and ensure that the property owners can address critical repairs and maintenance. The 
grant program meets HUD’s Housing National Objective of providing safe and affordable housing by 
investing CDBG proceeds to improve aging, affordable housing infrastructure for residents. 
The Housing LIFT program will provide an appreciable return-on-investment for the City by increasing 
the value of existing residential properties, preserving healthy and safe housing, facilitating aesthetic 
upgrades to properties, and advancing community pride via a “LIFT” to the curb appeal of older 
housing in City neighborhoods. The Housing LIFT program will also support local non-profit housing 
partners by providing additional gap funds to address the needs of aging housing infrastructure. 
It is common for local non-profit partners who provide affordable housing to Westminster residents to 
limit rent increases to keep residents housed. Because of this funding dilemma, non-profit property 
owners run into critical property maintenance needs with limited operating funds to pay for them. In 
response to this demonstrated need and specific funding requests, the Housing LIFT program 
addresses the needs. All participating property owners must commit to long term affordability 
restrictions in exchange for the opportunity to improve their property.
Participation in the Housing LIFT grant program also requires recipients to set up and fund a reserve 
account to maintain the improvements, therefore protecting the CDBG investment in the property for 
20 years. Both Maiker and Foothills will execute a Development Agreement with the City to access 
the Housing LIFT funds, and their contractors will work closely with residents to ensure minimal 
disruption during construction.

CITY OF WESTMINSTER PLANNING COMMISSION 
BYLAWS AND RULES OF PROCEDURE 
Applicability. In addition to the Charter and Ordinances of the City of Westminster and 
applicable state statues, these Bylaws and Rules of Procedure (the “Rules’’) shall govern all 
proceedings before the City of Westminster Planning Commission (the “Commission”). In 
the event of a conflict between these Rules and any of the above-cited laws, such laws shall 
prevail. These Rules replace and supersede any previously adopted bylaws or rules of 
procedure. 
Officers — Election and Duties. 
1. 
ze 
Officers. The officers of the Commission shall consist of a Chair and a Vice-Chair. 
Secretary. The City Manager shall designate a member of City staff to serve as 
Secretary and perform the duties assigned thereto as set forth herein. The Secretary 
shall not have a vote in the proceedings of the Commission. 
Officer Elections. At its first regular meeting of each odd-numbered calendar year, the 
Commission shall elect one of its members as Chair and one of its members as Vice- 
Chair, each for a two-year term. In the event either position becomes vacant before the 
end of the two-year term, the Commission shall, at its first regular or special meeting 
after such vacancy occurs, elect a regular member to serve as Acting Chair or Acting 
Vice-Chair, as applicable, until the end of the term. No member shall be eligible for 
election as Chair or Vice-Chair unless that member has previously served at least one 
year as a member of the Commission; provided, however, that if no member is so 
eligible, the Commission shall elect the member it deems best qualified. Elections shall 
be by secret ballot conducted by the Secretary. 
Absence of Chair, Vice-Chair. The Vice-Chair shall assume the duties of the Chair in 
the Chair’s absence. In the case of the absence of both the Chair and the Vice-Chair, 
the Secretary shall call the Commission to order and call the roll of members. If a 
quorum is found to be present, the Commission shall choose, by a majority vote of 
those present, a chair of the meeting to act until the Chair or Vice-Chair appears. 
  
Duties of the Chair. The Chair shall preside at all meetings, shall rule on all points of 
order or procedure and on the admissibility of evidence, unless, on motion duly made, 
the Chair is overruled by a majority vote of the members present. The Chair shall sign 
required documents for proceedings of the Commission over which the Chair presided. 
Duties of the Secretary. In addition to any other duties set forth in these Rules, the 
Secretary shall record the minutes of the meetings; receive and process all applications, 
petitions, and other business of the Commission; store the Commission’s records; and 
ensure Commission members receive meeting agenda materials.

Alternate Members. 
1. Alternate members of the Commission shall attend Commission meetings and shall 
have all of the rights and powers of regular members, except that alternate members 
shall not vote or otherwise participate in a matter before the Commission unless fewer 
than all regular members are in attendance at the meeting. 
If an alternate member attends a meeting in a voting capacity, and a public hearing on 
a matter is continued, the alternate member shall vote on the matter unless the regular 
member whose place was taken by the alternate listens to the recording of the prior 
public hearing, reviews the exhibits, and becomes familiar with the matter before the 
Commission. Ifa different regular member is absent at the meeting to which the public 
hearing has been continued, the alternate may vote on the matter in the absent member’s 
place. 
If voting, alternate members shall cast votes according to their rank: if one vote is 
needed, the first alternate shall cast a vote; if the first alternate is not in attendance, the 
second alternate will cast a vote; if more than one vote by an alternate is needed, both 
first and second alternates will cast a vote. 
Member Attendance. Whenever a member is absent from two (2) consecutive regular 
meetings or any three (3) regular meetings in a six-month period, the member shall receive 
notification thereof from the Chair. If a member is absent from four (4) consecutive regular 
meetings or twenty-five percent (25%) of the regular meetings in a fiscal year, unless 
excused by the Commission, the matter shall be referred to City Council. 
Meetings. The Commission shall not take any official action, except at a regular or special 
meeting.

Special Meetings. Special meetings may be called by the Chair with one other member, 
by a majority of the members at a regular meeting, or by a poll of all members with the 
majority assenting. All members shall be notified of the special meeting at least 
twenty-four (24) hours prior to the session. 
Meeting Location and Time. Meetings shall be held at the Westminster City Hall and 
begin at 7:00 P.M., unless proper notice otherwise is provided to the Commission and 
the public. 
Rules of Procedure. ROBERTS RULES OF ORDER shall be used as a guide at all meetings. 
Quorum. A majority of the members of the Commission in office at the time shall be a 
quorum for the transaction of business at all Commission meetings. All motions, decisions, 
and other actions of the Commission shall be by majority vote of those present. If the vote 
is a tie, the action is defeated.

All motions presented by members require a second. The Chair shall have the same 
rights and privileges of making motions and voting as any other member. Whenever 
possible, a motion shall be made prior to discussion by the Commission. All motions, 
decisions, and other actions of the Commission shall be by majority vote of those 
present. If the result of the vote is a tie, the action is defeated. 
A roll call vote may be taken on any question at the discretion of the Chair or upon 
demand of any member. The vote shall be entered upon the minutes of the Commission 
proceedings. It shall not be in order for members to explain their votes during the roll 
call, but members may do so after the roll is complete. 
A member, whether regular or alternate, may not vote on a matter for which the public 
hearing has been continued in the member’s absence unless the member has listened to 
the recording of the prior public hearing, reviewed the exhibits, and become familiar 
with the matter before the Commission. 
No member of the Commission shall vote on any question in which the member has a 
financial interest other than the common public interest, or on any question concerning 
the member’s own conduct, but on all other questions, each member present shall vote 
unless excused by the unanimous consent of the remaining members present. 
Rules of Evidence. Formal rules of evidence need not be followed, but the evidence should 
be of such nature that it would be commonly accepted by reasonable and prudent persons in 
the conduct of their affairs. 
Public Hearings. 
1. The Chair shall announce the application or other matter to be considered, and briefly 
explain the procedure to be followed. 
The hearing shall be conducted in such manner to provide a reasonable opportunity for 
all interested parties to speak. Public testimony shall be limited to five (5) minutes per 
person on a given agenda item except when modified by a majority vote. The Chair 
may limit testimony which tends to be repetitive, cumulative, speculative, or irrelevant.

The Chair will open the hearing.

c. The applicant or representative thereof will make a presentation. 
d. City staff may make additional comments. 
e. The Chair will open public testimony. 
(i) Public testimony will be heard from proponents of the application or 
petition. 
(ii) Public testimony will be heard from opponents of the application or petition.
