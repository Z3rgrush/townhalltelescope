Summary Statement:
At the direction of City Council, the proposed Down Payment Assistance Program (DPA) is 
intended to provide support to first-generation homebuyers, first-time buyers, young 
professionals, and workforce households earning between 80 percent and 120 percent of the 
Area Median Income (AMI). 
On July 31, 2026, the City issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) for a qualified contractor to 
administer the DPA, which aims to expand sustainable homeownership opportunities for 
moderate-income residents. The RFP closed on September 3, 2026, and Staff is currently 
finalizing the selection of a DPA contractor.

On February 23, 2026, Staff presented an update on current efforts to support affordability and 
income-aligned housing, as well as an overview of future initiatives to consider such as the

Staff recommends that the DPA be structured as a loan with highly favorable terms, used to

also consistent with other municipal DPA programs across the Front Range.

paid a set monthly amount, a percentage of a closed DPA Loan, upon repayment, or a 
combination of these for administering the DPA and how the administrative costs are captured 
with the repayment.

Based on the findings of the City’s 2023 HNA, first time home buyers with 120 percent AMI or below 
continue to face significant barriers to achieving homeownership, particularly related to rising housing 
costs and limited savings for upfront expenses. The HNA also identified a shortage of attainable 
“Missing Middle” for-sale housing types and noted that many residents spend more than 30 percent of 
their income on housing, further constraining their ability to save enough funds for a down payment to 
transition into homeownership. These findings align with the broader regional trends identified in the 
August 2025 RHNA, which highlights affordability challenges for moderate-income households across 
the region. The City was a participating municipality in the RHNA.
In response to and support of the City’s Strategic Plan priorities around housing stability and 
opportunity, Staff has advanced several housing initiatives, including development of a DPA. On 
February 23, 2026, Staff presented a housing update and recommendations to City Council, including 
researched DPA options designed to reduce upfront costs and support long-term owner occupancy. 
In July 2026, City Council approved the transfer of $500,000 from the General Capital Improvement 
Fund to the 2026 General Fund to support implementation of a DPA. The proposed DPA is currently 
funded through 2027 at $500,000. Staff is also pursuing state, federal, and other grants for further 
funding. The DPA will need an additional $2.0 million to be fully funded. 
Staff proceeded with issuing an RFP on July 31, 2026, to identify a qualified contractor to administer 
the program and ensure compliance with regulatory and funding requirements as outlined in the DPA 
Scope of Work (Attachment 1). The selected contractor will manage applicant intake, underwriting, 
loan processing, closing coordination, servicing, compliance, financial management, recordkeeping, 
and reporting, and will also be responsible for any compliance and grant management of any state, 
federal, or other agency grants received to fund the DPA.
The proposed DPA structure provides up to 20 percent of the purchase price, requires a minimum 
one percent buyer contribution, and utilizes a deferred second-loan model with no payments for the 
first five years. Repayment begins in year six or at sale or refinance, and the property must remain 
owner-occupied for the first five years of the term to promote long-term stability in Westminster 
neighborhoods. This is also consistent with other municipal DPA programs across the Front Range.
Several components of the DPA are still under development, such as whether the contractor is paid a 
set monthly amount, a percentage of a closed DPA Loan, upon repayment, or a combination of these 
for administering the DPA. Similarly, there are several ways to approach capturing this administration 
cost within the DPA Loan repayment, such as being built into the monthly loan repayment, being due 
as a lump sum upon sale or refinancing of the property, or a combination of these. Staff would like to 
incorporate the selected vendor’s expertise to help shape these details and present a 
recommendation to City Council for their final approval. It is important to note that any future state, 
federal, or other agency grants provided to the City for the DPA may require DPA parameters that 
differ from the City’s. Staff is required to seek City Council approval of these grants, and these 
potential differences could be explored at that time. 
By reducing upfront costs for eligible homebuyers, the program expands access to sustainable 
homeownership and directly responds to the affordability and housing equity gaps identified in the 
HNA and the RHNA for households earning up to 120 percent AMI. Operationally, the program 
benefits from third-party administration by leveraging experienced partners to manage underwriting, 
compliance, and servicing. This positions the City to apply for and incorporate external funding 
sources such as state and federal grants to increase program sustainability and its capacity for future 
expansion.

1. Project Overview
The City of Westminster (“City”) seeks proposals from qualified organizations to administer the City-
sponsored Down Payment Assistance (DPA) Program designed to expand sustainable 
homeownership opportunities for Westminster residents. The program supports moderate-income 
households—specifically first-generation homebuyers, first-time buyers, and young professionals—
who have stable income but face barriers in saving for upfront homeownership costs. 
The selected Contractor shall administer the program in alignment with City Council’s Strategic 
Priorities and in full compliance with federal, state, and local regulations. Responsibilities include 
applicant intake, underwriting, loan processing, closing coordination, servicing, compliance, financial 
management, recordkeeping, and comprehensive reporting. 
Currently the DPA program is being funded by the City, however, the City anticipates applying for 
state and/or federal grants when available. The Contractor must show experience with compliance 
and grant management. 
The Contractor must demonstrate experience in administering down payment assistance programs 
within Colorado and must possess the infrastructure necessary to launch and operate the program 
efficiently and successfully.

Homelessness Prevention
In December 2025, the City signed a $175,000 contract with the Community Economic Defense 
Project (CEDP) for 2026 to distribute rental assistance and case management to Westminster 
residents who would otherwise be evicted for financial reasons only. In April 2026, City Council 
approved expanding that contract to $350,000 for rental assistance, with another $25,000 for move-in 
assistance for Westminster Navigator clients. 
The partnership with CEDP focuses on upstream homelessness prevention services, including 
eviction defense, benefits navigation, and stabilization support. Westminster residents are actively 
being referred through the Navigation Team, municipal courts, and various affordable housing 
property managers.

Community Resource Navigator Team
The City’s Homeless Navigator Program was created in partnership in a Memorandum of 
Understanding (MOU) with Jefferson County, Arvada, Edgewater, Golden, Lakewood, and Wheat 
Ridge in 2020. Starting with one temporary FTE Navigator in 2020, the program transitioned to two 
permanent FTE Navigators in mid-2022 and, in 2025, the program was expanded to four FTE.
Through re-purposing of an existing 1.0 FTE in Recreation Services, the Parks, Recreation and 
Libraries Department (PRL) added a fifth navigator position in 2026. This increased the resource of 
the Community Resource Navigator Team (Team) to 6.0 FTE, including the Senior Management 
Analyst who leads the program. As part of the Proposed 2027 Budget, PRL is proposing to reclassify 
and upgrade the Senior Management Analyst that leads this Team to a Community Resource 
Administrator. This position will focus on policy, intergovernmental and non-profit partnerships, 
special projects, and leadership of the Team. In addition, a 1.0 FTE Navigator position is proposed to 
be reclassified and upgraded to serve as the Community Resource Supervisor, overseeing day-to-
day operations of the Team while also managing contracts with non-profits. The remaining 4.0 FTEs 
titles will be updated to Community Resource Navigators. 
  
From January 1 to August 17, 2026, the Navigator Program positively exited 22 people to housing 
situations, while providing 2,277 services to 125 clients.
In addition, the Westminster Navigator Program provides several versions of hotel stays: medical 
respite, family transitional shelter, bridge housing once a housing voucher is obtained, and severe 
weather sheltering. 
As of August 1, 2026, the Navigators have provided their clients more than 3,100 hotel room nights 
paid for by the City at a cost of $341,000. In addition, the Navigators have distributed Adams County 
Severe Weather Activation Program (SWAP) and Jefferson County Severe Weather Vouchers 
whenever either county activated hotel rooms to provide shelter when the weather met their criteria of 
32°F with precipitation or 20°F or below with or without precipitation.

Safe Camping 
In 2022, the Continuum of Compassion (CoC) as it currently stands was created, allowing people 
experiencing homelessness (PEH) to camp on parks and open space land within Westminster with 
the stipulation they meet certain standards and expectations. Those expectations include:
Currently a Navigator client pursuing services;
Not camping in a restricted area, such as a floodplain, reservable space, locked area, 
foundation properties such as the Metzger Farm, areas where maintenance needs to be 
performed, sensitive wildlife habitat, or blocking a trail; and
Not creating a health or safety risk to themselves or the surrounding community.
To effectuate “Safe Camping,” PRL Ranger staff have developed a new proposed Camp Standards 
Agreement and are working with the City Attorney’s Office on finalization. This draft agreement 
outlines expectations and resources made available to clients temporarily residing on PRL land to 
more clearly communicate with both clients and the surrounding community. This agreement maps 
out expectations related to eligibility, occupancy, fire restrictions, sanitation, camp locations, and 
conduct. Clients are expected to sign this agreement to be eligible to temporarily camp on PRL land.
The draft agreement is based on years of practical experience, including which temporary camp sites 
generate community complaints versus those that go unnoticed. The intent with Safe Camping is to 
establish a baseline of health standards for public safety.

Staff has explored what are typically known as Sanctioned Encampments and/or Micro-Communities. 
Those that were previously operating in the Denver metro area have closed because the sites were 
difficult to maintain, especially when it came to behavioral health and sanitation concerns. 
The Westminster model of dispersed temporary site camps allows people to stay near where they 
have informal support and can walk to their jobs. 
Another constraint around formally adopting certain locations for Sanctioned Encampments is that 
there are street politics and histories of personal interactions, making it best to have options for 
individuals to self-determine where and with whom it is safest to stay.
Safe Parking
Throughout 2026, Staff has engaged Adams County, the Salvation Army, Commerce City, the 
operators of the former Colorado Safe Parking Initiative that permanently closed in December 2024, 
and faith-based organizations that have operated Safe Parking Lots in other jurisdictions.

Irving Street Park Area and Citywide Challenges 
Westminster Navigator clients have largely been pushed out of the Irving Street Park area by new 
people. Most of the new people will not engage and/or have been belligerent with the Navigators and 
with other Staff. These individuals shout at other residents and have a great deal of bottles, needles, 
and other signs of drugs present. In the few instances of the newer people engaging with Navigators 
and enrolling in the program, all have been ejected from the City’s partner hotels for breaking multiple 
rules on a repeated basis. 
In the camps, the newer people have bullied, threatened, assaulted, and destroyed property of 
Navigator clients. The street politics of this dynamic present many challenges to the people 
experiencing homelessness seeking services, the rest of the community, surrounding businesses, 
and Staff.
Based on feedback from community stakeholders, including the business community, residents, 
school districts, and others, summary themes include:
Growing business impacts: Businesses across several Westminster corridors are experiencing 
increasing disruption tied to the unhoused population, especially along West 120th Avenue 
and in areas near parks and transit, including Irving Street.

Irving Street Park Area and Citywide Opportunities – Response to the Community  
Staff strongly recommends the following items move forward:
1. Creating an Ordinance or Regulation to restrict certain activities in developed parks;
2. Applying Continuum of Compassion to open space and not developed parks;
3. Adjusting City-funded Summer Severe Weather Activation Program (SWAP);
4. Working with Adams County’s Project Connects (Harm Reduction) to move from their current 
operation adjacent to Irving Street Library and Park; and
5. Exploring a potential new approach that the City of Lakewood is evaluating.
Information on each of these options is below:
1. Creating an Ordinance or Regulation to restrict certain activities in developed parks; and
2. Applying Continuum of Compassion to open space and not developed parks
This would ultimately mean that Navigator clients would be allowed to temporarily camp within 
open space areas, but not within developed parks.
 
These changes are recommended to improve and clarify the policies for people experiencing 
homelessness and the community at large. Camping within developed parks puts unnecessary 
burden on all involved. Ongoing maintenance, like irrigation and mowing within parks, can 
cause disruption to unhoused individuals. Police Officers are met with challenging scenarios 
within developed parks. Parks are well lit and easily accessible, creating an environment 
where illicit behavior is more noticeable. Parks are nestled within neighborhoods, causing 
significantly more interaction between traditional park users and unhoused individuals. Parks 
are also designed and intended for active recreation. 
Creating an ordinance or regulation restricting certain activities in developed parks will provide 
greater clarity for PRL Rangers and Police Officers in managing developed parks. Applying the 
Continuum of Compassion to open space provides clarity regarding where temporary camping 
is allowed. The Safe Camping Agreement, which is in development, would set clear 
expectations related to eligibility, occupancy, fire restrictions, sanitation, camp locations, and 
conduct. Navigator clients would be expected to sign this agreement to be eligible 
to temporarily camp in open space.
3. Adjusting City-Funded Summer SWAP
Continuing to operate as the City has this summer is not sustainable from a community, 
staffing, or financial perspective. Earlier this year, Adams County changed the Hot Weather 
SWAP criteria, which has created significant challenges.
Staff strongly recommends a change from the current Adams County Hot Weather SWAP 
program and recommends no longer offering City-paid hot weather hotel stays. Westminster 
Navigators would still offer the Adams County provided SWAP hotel stays when weather 
dictates, which in 2026 was limited to eight (8) vouchers per hot weather SWAP activation. 
Under this recommended change, the City would not offer additional weather-related hotel 
vouchers beyond those provided by Adams County SWAP for hot weather. The Navigator 
Program will continue to provide hotel stays for medical respite, family transitional shelter, and 
bridge housing once a housing voucher is obtained throughout the entire year, regardless of 
weather.

Since 2020, Westminster Navigators have activated hotel stays for shelter whenever either 
Adams or Jefferson County activate their programs, distributing county-provided hotel 
vouchers. This summer, City Navigators have done the same. However, Adams County 
adjusted their hot weather hotel stays from projections of 98° Fahrenheit for two or more days 
in a row to 95° Fahrenheit for two or more days and/or when lows do not fall below 70° 
Fahrenheit at night. At the same time, Adams County staff members recognized that they 
would not have enough funding to cover the estimated doubling or more of hot weather nights 
using the new criteria, so they lowered the number of vouchers they provide each municipality 
from 20 to eight for each activation. 
Under the new criteria Adams County is using, hot weather hotel stays have been available to 
eight (8) Westminster clients on 24 nights, 14 in July and 10 in August. During the Strategic 
Planning Session on February 28, 2026, City Council directed Staff to provide more 
compassionate weather sheltering. In attempts to meet that direction, City Navigators have 
offered hot weather hotel stays using the new Adams County Hot Weather SWAP criteria with 
City funds beyond the eight vouchers per activation provided by Adams County. This has 
applied to Navigator clients throughout the City, regardless of their county affiliation. 
Westminster Navigators provided hotel stays to the remaining number of their clients not 
already in hotels for other reasons on these 24 nights over a six-week timeframe. For 
comparison, from October 2025 to May 2026, cold weather hotel stays activated for a total of 
34 nights. The average number of nights hotel stays activate over an eight-month cold weather 
period is 47.
To date this summer, the invoices for the July 2026 activations have been fully processed. 
During those activations, Westminster paid for 378 hotel nights for their clients in addition to 
those they already had in hotels for other reasons. The hot weather hotel nights cost $32,039. 
August and the remainder of the summer will add to that total. 
Unfortunately, hot weather hotel stays have not gone well. As of late-August, 27 clients have 
been banned from at least one hotel with room damages being charged on top of the 
bookings. As there have only been one-to-two-day intervals between the first four activations, 
hotels were not always able to provide documentation and alert the Navigators that someone 
had been banned from their premises due to their behavior before another booking was made 
at a different hotel for the client. Unfortunately, those secondary bookings frequently resulted in 
a ban from a second hotel for the same behavior. During the last activation in August, five 
clients did not check in, so the City was charged for vacant rooms.
In addition to hard-dollar costs, soft dollar costs include Navigators spending the first two days 
of an activation arranging for rooms, bookings, and transportation to the hotel. The day of de-
activation includes arranging transportation, replenishing supplies left at their prior camp, and – 
in the case of egregious damages – calls from hotel managers. Typically, documentation of 
damages to the hotel arrive over the next two to three business days. Then, invoices for all 
rooms and damages are processed over the next week with corrections being sought the 
following week. Time spent on these tasks means that Navigators are unable to meet with their 
clients to help them achieve their goals, including employment and housing. This has 
understandably challenged the morale of the Westminster Navigators.
At the same time, the hotel bans will result in fewer sheltering options for the clients during the 
upcoming cold weather season. While the navigators are willing to continue to work with the

clients, the hotels will not welcome them back. 
Additionally, there are more active unhoused camps than we have seen in the past five 
summers in Westminster. Qualitative data suggests that at least some of this is a result of 
word getting out that Westminster is offering all their clients hotel stays. Adams County is the 
only county in the region offering hot weather hotel stays. Westminster is the only municipality 
offering hot weather hotel stays to all their clients. Staff has encountered several people 
wanting to switch from working with Thornton, Northglenn, Arvada, or unincorporated Adams 
County to Westminster.
The hottest weather occurs during daytime hours when City and other facilities are open and 
available for people to come into for free. During cold weather months, facilities are closed 
during the coldest temperatures. Staff strongly recommends prioritizing resources for cold 
weather and discontinuing City-paid hot weather hotel stays.
 
4. Working with Adams County’s Project Connects (Harm Reduction Street Outreach) to move 
from their current operation adjacent to Irving Street Library and Park
Staff has reached out to the Adams County Harm Reduction Team, known as Project 
Connects, to request that they consider one or more other sites rather than Irving Street for 
their weekly street outreach. They provide many services and supports to the community at 
large, including prevention of, testing for, and referrals for treatment of sexually transmitted 
diseases, educating community members on drug use prevention, where to go for treatment, 
and how to safely store and dispose of substance use paraphernalia when it is used. With 
current challenges, Irving Street is not an ideal environment for their outreach.
5. Exploring a potential new approach that the City of Lakewood is evaluating
Lakewood is considering nuisance abatement zones and encumbrance zones as they shared 
during their August 17, 2026, Lakewood City Council Study Session. Nuisance abatement 
zones would be aimed at sites that consistently have camps pop up and grow quickly on a 
recurring basis, sometimes with the same people, or with a new person or people each time. 
This would create a “cooling off period” for a set number of weeks so that the abatement and 
trespass processes would not have to recur daily. 
Lakewood’s other topic of discussion was creating permanent encumbrance zones for an as-
yet-undetermined space around their new Navigation Center. There are questions about the 
viability and legality of that approach. It appears that the City of Lakewood will have more 
conversations on both ideas in the coming weeks. Staff continue to track their work.
