Background Information:
The City of Westminster 136th Avenue General Improvement District was established by the City of 
Westminster Council on August 14, 2000, with the primary purpose of financing a new interchange at 
136th Avenue and Interstate 25 (1-25). Pursuant to the creation ordinance, City Council serves as the 
ex-officio Board of Directors of the District. The Westminster City Manager serves as the Executive 
Director of the Board and hereby submits to the Board this proposed budget.
Subsequently, on November 7, 2000, an election was held with the property owners in the District 
approving a ballot initiative that included the following: 
1. Authorized the District to issue $11,000,000 of debt;
 
2. Approved a mill levy not to exceed 15.0 mills for debt repayment, which must end in 2021 or 
when the aggregate collections of principal equals $11,000,000, whichever occurs later;
 
3. Approved a mill levy, not to exceed 1.0 mill as is necessary to generate up to $10,000 annually 
for general operating expenditures; and
 
4. Authorized the District to collect, keep and spend all revenues it receives as a voter approved 
revenue change under the TABOR Amendment. However, an emergency reserve of $10,546 
is still required and has been established.
On December 4, 2002, the City issued Sales Tax Revenue Bonds to finance the City's portion of a 
joint construction project with the City of Thornton for an interchange to be constructed at 1-25 and 
136th Avenue. In consideration of the City financing and constructing the interchange, the District 
agreed to repay to the City up to $11,000,000 principal from the District's levy of ad valorem taxes on 
real and personal taxable property. As of the last audited period of December 31, 2024, the District 
has paid $4,759,209 toward this liability.
On January 26, 2004, City Council approved the Westminster Economic Development Authority 
(WEDA) North Huron Urban Renewal Area (URA) to encourage and support the redevelopment of 
properties within its boundaries. The District lies within the North Huron URA area. The WEDA North 
Huron URA is a tax increment financing authority that receives incremental property tax revenues 
attributable to urban renewal development from overlapping taxing jurisdictions. Only property taxes 
attributed to the base valuation of the District are received by the District to pay its annual expenses 
and obligations.
Because the URA captures all property tax increment within its boundaries, an Intergovernmental 
Cooperation Agreement (ICA) between WEDA, the City, and the District was necessary. Pursuant to
the ICA, the WEDA budget includes an estimate of the amount of incremental property tax related to 
the District's mill levy that will be released to the District. This amount is labeled as intergovernmental
revenue in the District's budget document. Additionally, the District's budget includes an amount to be 
sent to the City which may then be applied by the City towards debt service for the interchange
construction, as originally contemplated in the financing plan for the interchange project. This

Revenues
The attached proposed budget for 2027 is based on certifying a mill levy of 16.0 mills, which remains 
unchanged from the prior year. Such levy on the preliminary net assessed valuation for the tax year 
2026 is estimated to generate property tax revenue of approximately $11,833. In addition to property 
taxes, the District receives a proportionate share of the specific ownership taxes collected within 
Adams County. Ownership taxes are estimated to be $14,000. Incremental revenues released by 
WEDA to the District as described above are estimated to be $325,078. And interest earnings are 
estimated at $639. Total revenues in 2027 are estimated at $351,550.
Expenditures
The District's estimated revenues described above fund all expenditures in 2027 associated with 
fulfilling the District's contractual obligations including an amount to be sent to the City to assist with 
the repayment of the debt issued for the construction of the interchange as described above. This 
payment to the City is included in the budget as a transfer-out and is estimated to be $341,000. 
Property tax revenues are collected by Adams County and distributed to the District each month. To 
compensate for collecting the District's property taxes, the county retains a 1.5 percent fee which is 
estimated to be $332. The District does not employ their own professional staff. In an effort to contain 
costs, the District utilizes the expertise of City Staff to provide various necessary administrative and 
record keeping services. These services include but are not limited to legal (provided no conflict of 
interest exists between the City and the District), accounting, management, and clerical, as well as 
the use of the City's financial management system. The District benefits by having the City provide 
these services, as the services would be costlier if outsourced. Additionally, as a blended component 
unit of the City, the District's financial activities are audited as part of the City's annual financial audit 
by the City's external auditors. In consideration of the City providing such services, the District 
entered into an intergovernmental service agreement (IGSA) to formalize the services provided and 
the annual payment due to the City for such services. The amount allocated to pay the City for such 
services was set at $10,000 annually as part of the election in November 2000. Total revenues for the 
District for 2027 are estimated to be $351,550. Total expenditures and transfers for the District for 
2027 are estimated to be $351,332.
A budget message describing the important features of the proposed budget and a description of 
services to be delivered during the budget year is required by Budget Law. Additionally, the District is 
required to submit a proposed budget that includes actual figures for the last completed fiscal year 
and estimated figures projected through the end of the current fiscal year. The attachments to this 
agenda memo include the required budget message, the proposed budget with all the required 
elements, and a map of the District. These documents along with a certified copy of the 2027 
budget must be filed with the Division of Local Government no later than 30 days following the 
beginning of the fiscal year.
This action supports the City's Strategic Priorities of Opportunity to Thrive, Vibrant and Diverse 
Economy, and Resilient Infrastructure. These goals are met by ensuring a balanced budget where 
revenues are appropriated to expenditure accounts so the funds can be utilized as the District 
intended, as well as to efficiently and effectively administer the operating and maintenance activities 
of the District. This action also supports the City's mission statement to provide core services and 
foster economic resilience to give our community the opportunity to thrive by providing well 
maintained destinations with a resilient environment.

CITY OF WESTMINSTER 136TH AVENUE GENERAL IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT
BUDGET MESSAGE
The attached 2027 budget for the City of Westminster 136th Avenue General Improvement 
District (District) includes these important features:
The City of Westminster (City) Council established the District on August 14, 2000, with the 
primary purpose of financing a new interchange at 136th Avenue and Interstate 25 (I-25). 
Pursuant to the creation ordinance, City Council serves as the ex-officio Board of Directors of 
the District.
The District is a blended component unit of the City. In accordance with Governmental 
Accounting Standards Board Statement (GASB) No. 14 and as amended by GASB Statement 
No. 61, component units are legally separate entities for which the City is considered to be 
financially accountable. Financial accountability means that the City appoints a voting majority 
to the governing board and has the ability to impose its will upon the entity and/or accepts 
potential responsibility for the entity’s financial benefits and burdens. Additionally, because the 
District does not employ its own professional staff, the City and the District entered into an 
intergovernmental service agreement on November 26, 2001, that provides for the City to 
provide administrative services, including but not limited to legal, accounting, management, 
clerical, information technology, contract management, and similar services in exchange for 
consideration to the City.
In a given year, when revenues exceed expenditures the fund balance for the District will 
increase. Those funds will be available for future appropriations when needed by the District. 
Conversely, when expenditures exceed revenues in any given year, prior year unspent revenues, 
or fund balance, is used to meet the District’s current year obligations. Use of prior year unspent 
revenues for the District in a given year would be identified in the revenue section below.
As part of the budget process for the District, the preliminary certification of valuation issued 
annually by the respective county assessor in August is used to project the property tax revenue 
for the District. The final certification of valuation issued in December is then used to certify the 
mill levy. The District will certify a 16.0 mill levy, which will generate property tax revenues of 
approximately $11,833 in 2027, based on the preliminary net assessed valuation of $739,544 for 
tax year 2026. This mill levy remains unchanged from the prior year. Total projected revenues 
for 2027, which also include ownership tax, intergovernmental revenue, and interest earnings, 
are estimated at $351,550.
Projected expenditures for 2027 are $10,332 for administrative and property tax collection fees, 
and $341,000 for transfers to the City's General Capital Improvement Fund. Total expenditures 
and transfers are estimated at $351,332 for 2027.
An emergency reserve of $10,546 as required under Article X, Section 20 of the Colorado 
Constitution is included in the ending fund balance.

Background Information:
The City of Westminster 144th Avenue General Improvement District (GID) was established by the 
City of Westminster City Council on August 30, 2004, with the primary purpose of paying debt 
associated with public improvements within or without the District. The District is generally bound by 
144th Avenue, Huron Street, 146th Avenue and I-25. Pursuant to the creation ordinance, City Council 
serves as the ex-officio Board of Directors of the District. The Westminster City Manager serves as 
the Executive Director of the Board and hereby submits to the Board this proposed budget.
Subsequently on November 2, 2004, an election was held with the property owners in the District 
approving a ballot initiative that included the following: 
1. Approved a mill levy, not to exceed 20.0 mills, to pay for annual operating expenditures of the 
District;
 
2. Approved an unlimited mill levy to repay up to $20,000,000 of debt incurred to finance 
improvements in the District; and
 
3. Authorized the District to collect, keep and spend all revenues it receives as a voter approved 
revenue change under the TABOR Amendment. However, an emergency reserve or $3,011 is 
still required and has been established.
On January 26, 2004, prior to the establishment of the District, City Council approved the 
Westminster Economic Development Authority (WEDA) North Huron Urban Renewal Area (URA) to 
encourage and support the redevelopment of properties within its boundaries. The WEDA North 
Huron URA is a tax increment financing authority that receives incremental tax revenues attributable 
to urban renewal development from overlapping taxing jurisdictions. These tax revenues are pledged 
for debt service purposes. In 2005, the North Huron URA issued tax increment adjustable rate 
revenue bonds that have subsequently been refunded with a loan. The initial bond proceeds 
were used to fund the redevelopment in the North Huron URA. This redevelopment benefits the 
District. Incremental property tax revenues are considered pledged revenues for this debt issue. 
Additionally, the City's financing arm, the Westminster Building Authority, issued Certificates of 
Participation originally in 2005 (2005 COPs) for the construction of the east side of the interchange at 
I-25 and 144th Avenue. This interchange also benefits the District. The 2005 COPs were 
subsequently refunded in 2013.
The 144th Avenue GID lies within the North Huron URA area. Annually, the District's mill levy on any 
valuation above the District's base valuation is captured by WEDA as property tax increment for debt 
service. This increment is allocated to the 2013 North Huron URA debt. The amount estimated to be 
retained by WEDA from the District's proposed mill levy for 2027 is forecasted to be $952,308.
During 2004, as part of the City's continued effort to accomplish the redevelopment of the project at 
144th Avenue and Huron Street in northern Westminster, now known as The Orchard, Staff 
addressed with City Council the policy issues surrounding the formation of a general improvement 
district (GID) co-terminus with the Urban Renewal Area. These discussions included adding a GID as 
a method to finance some costs without an added burden on the tax increment financing area. It was 
determined that the GID provided the ability to raise property taxes in this area to facilitate additional 
public improvements while ensuring that the area remained competitive with respect to tax levies. The 
project's developer, Forest City agreed to the formation of the GID. The District was established to

assure that property benefiting from the construction of the development would participate in paying 
any debt incurred for the development.
In consideration of the City and WEDA constructing the public improvements benefiting the property 
owners and furthering WEDA's redevelopment goals, an Intergovernmental Cooperation Agreement 
(ICA) between the City, WEDA, and the District was required to formalize the City's agreement to 
undertake the construction of the public improvements; to establish how project funds would be paid 
to vendors; and to establish how the revenues of the City, WEDA and the District would be shared to 
finance the debt issued to construct the improvements. This ICA was entered on October 10, 2005, 
and accomplished the following: 
The City agreed to enter into one or more contracts for the construction of the improvements.
 
The City agreed to pay for the portion of the cost of improvements that relates to the North 
Huron project constructed outside of the boundaries of the North Huron URA. This was 
accomplished with the issuance of the 2005 COPs as described above.
 
WEDA agreed to pay for the cost of that portion of the North Huron project improvements 
located within the URA. This was accomplished with the original 2005 WEDA bond issue 
described above.
 
The District agreed to annually levy a property tax mill levy not to exceed 20.0 mills and to 
remit to the City that portion of the property tax revenue that the District collects on the base 
valuation.
Based on the ICA, the flow of funds enables WEDA to retain the property tax received on 
the  District's incremental value and enables the District to use the property tax revenue on the 
base valuation for operating expenses and contractual obligations, which include payment to the City 
to assist with the repayment of the 2005 COP's. The District does not have any direct debt but does 
have an obligation to repay WEDA and the City. As of the last audited period, December 31, 2024, 
the District has paid $1,232,499 to the City. Additionally, the District has contributed $11,398,599 
toward the total debt liability through the District's incremental taxes captured by WEDA. Pursuant to 
the amended ICA, the District's obligation has been accruing interest at the actual interest rate paid 
by WEDA which has ranged from 4.51 percent to 2.97 percent. The current rate is set at 3.51 
percent. The balance of the total obligation due by the District as of December 31, 2024, 
is $21,168,902 which includes both principal and the unpaid accrued interest.

The District does not employ their own professional staff. In an effort to contain costs, the District

adjusted annually based on an analysis of the various components required to manage the day-to-
day operations of the District. These components include salary and benefits for City Staff, 
maintenance costs for the City's FMS, and independent financial audit fees. Based on this analysis, 
the amount allocated to pay the City for such services was set at $23,600 for 2027, which is reflective 
of the increasing costs to administer the District.

The primary purpose of the District is to fund the maintenance of landscaped rights-of-way, open

consideration to the City.

increase. Those funds will be available for future appropriations when needed by the District.

or fund balance, is used to meet the District’s current year obligations. Use of prior year unspent

Background Information:
The City of Westminster Downtown General Improvement District was established by City Council on 
August 10, 2015, with the primary purpose of maintaining the public improvements in the District, 
funding operating costs for the District, and funding future capital improvements in the District. The 
District is located on the former Westminster Mall site which is generally bound by 92nd Avenue on 
the north, 88th Avenue on the south, Harlan Street on the west, and Interstate 36 on the east. 
Pursuant to the creation ordinance, City Council serves as the ex-officio Board of Directors of the 
District. The Westminster City Manager serves as the Executive Director of the Board and hereby 
submits to the Board the proposed budget.
On November 3, 2015, an election was held with the property owners in the District approving a ballot 
initiative that: 
1. Approved a mill levy not to exceed 50.0 mills to pay the cost of such improvements and 
services as the District is authorized to provide by law; and
 
2. Authorized the District to collect, keep, and spend all revenues it receives as a voter approved 
revenue change under the TABOR Amendment. However, an emergency reserve of $37,113 
is still required and has been established as part of the 2026 budget.
On April 13, 2009, prior to the establishment of the District, City Council approved the Westminster 
Economic Development Authority (WEDA) Westminster Urban Reinvestment Plan Area (WURP, also 
known as the Downtown Westminster URA) Urban Renewal Area (URA) Plan (Plan). The Plan was 
established to achieve the goals for the area as previously outlined in the Westminster 
Comprehensive Land Use Plan through a project or projects that would create a new Westminster 
Center. The Plan was amended on October 28, 2013, to authorize the utilization of property tax 
increment financing to finance the projects undertaken in furtherance of the Plan. The Downtown 
Westminster URA is a tax increment financing authority that receives incremental revenues 
attributable to urban renewal development from overlapping taxing jurisdictions.
This District lies within the boundaries of the URA. As such, the District's mill levy on any valuation 
above the District's base valuation is captured by WEDA. An intergovernmental cooperation 
agreement (ICA) with WEDA was necessary to release to the District all related property tax revenues 
generated by the District's mill levy. The payment by WEDA to the District is included in the budget 
annually as intergovernmental revenue. These revenues may then be applied by the District for 
funding the costs associated with maintaining the public improvements, operating costs, and future 
capital improvements in the District.

administrative services, including but not limited to legal, accounting, management, clerical, 
information technology, contract management, and similar services in exchange for 
consideration to the City.
In a given year, when revenues exceed expenditures the fund balance for the District will 
increase. Those funds will be available for future appropriations when needed by the District. 
Conversely, when expenditures exceed revenues in any given year, prior year unspent revenues, 
or fund balance, is used to meet the District’s current year obligations. Use of prior year unspent 
revenues for the District in a given year would be identified in the revenue section below.

Background Information:
The City of Westminster Mandalay Town Center General Improvement District (GID) was established 
by the City of Westminster Council on September 8, 2003, with the primary purpose of financing a 
portion of the costs of street improvements and other necessary and related appurtenance facilities 
within the District. The District is generally bound by US 36, Church Ranch Boulevard, and the 
Burlington Northern/Santa Fe railroad line. Pursuant to the creation ordinance, City Council serves as 
the ex-officio Board of Directors of the District. The Westminster City Manager serves as the 
Executive Director of the Board and hereby submits to the Board the proposed budget.
On November 4, 2003, an election was held with the property owners in the District approving a ballot 
initiative that: 
1. Approved a mill levy not to exceed 35.0 mills to pay the annual expenses of the District; and
 
2. Authorized the District to collect, keep, and spend all revenues it receives as a voter approved 
revenue change under the TABOR Amendment. However, an emergency reserve of $2,655 is 
still required and has been established.
On March 17, 2003, prior to the establishment of the District, City Council approved the Westminster 
Economic Development Authority (WEDA) Mandalay Gardens Urban Renewal Area (URA) to support 
the redevelopment of properties within its boundaries. The Mandalay Gardens URA is a tax increment 
financing authority that receives incremental tax revenues attributable to urban renewal development 
from overlapping taxing jurisdictions. These tax revenues are pledged for debt service purposes. This 
WEDA URA issued taxable tax increment bonds in 2003 that have subsequently been refunded as 
tax-exempt tax increment revenue refunding bonds.
Proceeds from WEDA's original bond issue were used to fund the re-development of the US 36 and 
Westminster Boulevard area located within the District. Per the Mandalay Urban Renewal Plan, the 
bond proceeds were to assist with the demolition of existing structures and reconstruction of the 
public infrastructure, assist with relocation and develop parcels to facilitate redevelopment. The 
original bond issue in the URA was for $38.52 million and was refinanced in 2012 for $28.9 million. At 
the end of 2025, the outstanding balance will be $6.98 million with a maturity date of December 1, 
2028. The District is the exact boundaries of the URA. As such, the District mill levy on any 
valuation above the District's base valuation is allocated to the Mandalay URA for the debt service of 
its tax increment bonds. Incremental property tax revenues received by the URA are considered 
pledged revenues for the bond issue.
As part of the redevelopment process, Staff held discussions with City Council regarding adding a 
general improvement district (GID) as a method to finance some costs without an added burden on 
the tax increment financing area. It was determined that a general improvement district provided the

ability to raise property taxes in this area to facilitate additional public improvements while ensuring 
that the area remained competitive with respect to tax levies. The project's developer, Red 
Development, agreed to the formation of the District.
In consideration of WEDA constructing public improvements for the benefit of the District's property 
owners and the furtherance of WEDA's redevelopment goals, an intergovernmental cooperation 
agreement (ICA) between WEDA and the District was approved. The ICA set forth the obligation of 
the District to pay certain revenues to WEDA to assist in the payment of debt service on the bonds. 
As part of the budget process each year, the District's budget includes an amount to be paid to 
WEDA to assist with the repayment of this debt. This payment to WEDA is included in the budget as 
a transfer to WEDA. As of December 31, 2025, the cumulative amount the District has paid WEDA is 
$1,268,377.

Expenditures

compensate for collecting the District's property taxes, the county retains a 1.5% fee which is 
estimated to be $700.
The District does not employ their own professional staff. In an effort to contain costs, the District 
utilizes the expertise of City staff to provide various necessary administrative and record keeping 
services. These services include but are not limited to legal (provided no conflict of interest exists 
between the City and the District), accounting, management, and clerical, as well as the use of the 
City's financial management system (FMS). The District benefits by having the City provide these 
services, as the services would be costlier if outsourced. Additionally, as a blended component unit of 
the City, the District's financial activities are audited as part of the City's annual financial audit by the 
City's external auditors. In consideration of the City providing such services, the District entered into 
an intergovernmental service agreement (IGSA) to formalize the services provided and the annual 
payment due to the City for such services. The amount allocated to pay the City for such services is 
adjusted annually based on an analysis of the various components required to manage the day-to-
day operations of the District. These components include salary and benefits for City staff, 
maintenance costs for the City's FMS, and independent financial audit fees. Based on this 
analysis, Staff is recommending the fee for 2027 increase to $23,600, which is reflective of the 
increasing costs to administer the District.
