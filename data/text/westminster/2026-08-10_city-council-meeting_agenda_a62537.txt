E. Resolution No. 20 Re: Adopting Zoning Interpretation Regarding Prohibition of Data 
Center Land Use 
Hott moved, seconded by Barajas, to adopt Resolution No. 20 with an amendment to 
explicitly ban data centers within the City of Westminster.    
 
Council discussed whether additional action beyond Resolution No. 20 should be 
considered to permanently prohibit standalone data centers through the Westminster 
Municipal Code or the upcoming Unified Development Code update. Discussion 
included the potential impacts of data centers on water resources, future 
Comprehensive Plan amendments, facilities in adjacent jurisdictions, and the 
distinction between standalone commercial data centers and accessory data 
processing facilities. Staff confirmed that standalone data centers are not currently a 
permitted use under the City's zoning regulations and advised that any future 
authorization would require proactive action by City Council.   
 
Following this discussion Hott withdrew the original motion. 
 
Johnson moved, seconded by Barajas, to adopt Resolution No. 20 affirming that data 
centers are not a permitted use in the City of Westminster and supporting the opinion 
of Staff regarding the prohibition of data centers within the City. 
 
The motion passed on a (7-0) roll call vote. 
 
In response to the discussion, Carmelia requested a poll to schedule a future Study 
Session to consider a more formal and permanent prohibition of standalone data 
centers.  
 
By unanimous consent, Council directed Staff to schedule the Study Session.

the Westminster Municipal Code to Provide for Traffic Regulation Through the Use of 
Automated Vehicle Identifications Systems (Speed/Traffic Enforcement Cameras)  
Nurmela moved, seconded by Johnson, to pass Councillor’s Bill No. 28 on second 
reading, adding Section 20 to Chapter 1 of Title X to the Westminster Municipal Code

to provide for traffic regulation through the use of automated vehicle identifications 
systems conditioned on the use of funds going toward traffic safety. 
 
Johnson expressed support for the ordinance but requested additional discussion 
regarding the provision allowing disclosure of AVI data for "other law enforcement 
purposes." Johnson sought clarification from the City Attorney regarding the scope of 
that provision and the safeguards protecting collected data. 
 
Assistant City Attorney Mat Munch explained that the ordinance mirrors state law, that 
AVI systems would only capture data when a qualifying traffic violation occurs, that the 
data is exempt from disclosure under the Colorado Open Records Act, and that the 
"other law enforcement purposes" provision was intended to provide flexibility for 
limited investigative purposes while maintaining the ordinance's privacy protections.  
 
Ezeadi stated that the ordinance did not provide sufficient privacy protections and 
expressed concern that allowing data to be used for other law enforcement purposes 
could lead to future expansion beyond the ordinance's intended purpose. Ezeadi  
reiterated support for a future Study Session to consider additional restrictions on 
automated license plate reader functionality. 
 
Barajas discussed balancing community requests for additional traffic enforcement 
with concerns regarding privacy and questioned whether further policy discussion 
should occur before implementing an AVI program. 
 
Ireland asked questions regarding enforcement thresholds, warning notices, camera 
locations, and the use of mobile versus stationary cameras. Assistant City Attorney 
Munch explained that minor speeding violations would receive warnings before fines 
were assessed and reviewed the ordinance's limitations on camera placement and 
operation. 
 
The motion failed on a (2-5) roll call vote.  Barajas, Ezeadi, Hott, Ireland, and Nurmela 
voted no.

Subject: Presentation from Adams 12 Five Star Schools on Supporting Thriving Schools in 
a Period of Declining Enrollment
Prepared By: Lindsey Kimball, CEcD, EDFP; Community Services Director
Recommended City Council Action:
Receive a presentation from Adams 12 Five Star Schools District staff on Supporting Thriving 
Schools in a Period of Declining Enrollment.
Summary Statement:
The leadership of Adams 12 Five Star Schools District (Adams 12) requested the opportunity 
to present to City Council on declining enrollment, district-specific trends and their impacts, 
and the District’s proposed path forward to ensure thriving and fiscally responsible school 
environments through consolidations. 
Low enrollment leads to fewer resources, reduced staff, and programs, which means reduced 
electives, limited academic support, fewer clubs and sports, and less access to before- and 
after-school childcare. Thriving schools need enough students to offer strong programs and full 
staffing. 
To maintain high-quality learning environments, the District is planning school consolidations to 
combine students into fewer buildings, improve access to programs, ensure stable staffing, 
use resources efficiently, and provide stronger academic and extracurricular opportunities.

The District has developed a multi-year timeline for evaluating data, engaging the community, 
refining options, making decisions, and implementing school consolidations by the 2027–2028 
school year, following Board-approved guidelines to minimize disruption and support families.
Fiscal Impact:
$0 in expenditures
Source of Funds:
Not applicable
Policy Issue(s):
Does City Council wish to receive a presentation from Adams 12 staff and provide comments?
Alternative(s):
City Council could choose not to receive a presentation from Adams 12 staff and withhold comment. 
This is not recommended because this presentation is at the request of City Council and will provide 
important information regarding the future of facilities in the community. 
Background Information:
Adams 12 is facing significant and ongoing declines in student enrollment, caused by factors like 
lower birth rates, less migration, an aging population, high housing costs, and fewer families moving 
in. Because fewer students are enrolling, many schools have more space than they need, with 
utilization as low as 60 percent in middle schools and 69 percent in elementary schools, leaving 
thousands of empty seats. 
The City's Strategic Plan priority of Connected and Inclusive Community is supported by the public 
discussion of the future of facilities that have a deep history in the community and are of great interest 
to many residents.

The Westminster Sustainability Center opened on April 22, 2024, and experiences an average of over

center has diverted over 747 tons of single stream materials from the landfill and recycled almost 
300,000 pounds of electronic waste. 
SustainAbility Recycling operates the Westminster Sustainability Center and partners with PPCH, an 
agency that provides job coaching and supervision to individuals who have intellectual and 
developmental disabilities (IDD). PPCH is a provider agency that receives Medicaid Waiver funding 
for providing on-the-job supports and has historically used that funding to pay the wages of the 
individuals with IDD (“Crew Staff”), as well as the wages for the Direct Support Staff, who provide job 
coaching and supervision. On average, PPCH pays $20,000 per month for Direct Support Staff 
wages and benefits and $16,800 per month for Crew Staff (IDD) wages.  
A change to Medicaid Waiver rules and regulations, effective September 1, 2026, will prohibit 
employers from using Medicaid Waiver funding to pay Crew Staff wages. The Westminster 
Sustainability Center requires eight Crew Staff per day to manage operations and employs 19 part-
time Crew Staff altogether. These staff members are responsible for setting up the site in the 
morning, greeting customers and instructing them where to unload, providing assistance and 
education on materials, operating equipment such as the compactor, balers and forklift, and cleaning 
the site at the end of the day.

anticipating a new funding source that will offset a significant portion of the Westminster Sustainability 
Center expenses in 2026 and beyond. House Bill 22-1355 created the Colorado EPR program, a 
statewide recycling initiative that shifts the cost and management of recycling from residents and local 
governments to the producers that create and sell the materials. The goal of EPR is to create free, 
convenient, statewide recycling access and build a modern recycling system. The EPR program is 
administered by CAA, the nonprofit producer responsibility organization selected by CDPHE. 
The City is eligible to receive reimbursement of up to 100% of the cost of community drop-off sites, 
beginning in 2026. This includes the cost of staffing. Staff submitted an application for reimbursement 
prior to the deadline of April 13, 2026, and is currently awaiting further details. EPR funding is 
reimbursement based, so the City will pay for Crew Staff wages upfront and be reimbursed by CAA 
on a to-be-determined basis, likely quarterly or annually. Since the City has not yet received EPR 
funding, Staff will use repurposed Outcome and Task funding to sustain operations at the 
Westminster Sustainability Center and avoid service disruptions in 2026. Staff will continue to monitor 
the timing of EPR funding and evaluate alternative funding options through the 2027 budget process. 
Staff is requesting approval from City Council to amend the existing service agreement with 
SustainAbility Recycling and allow the City to use funding from the EPR program to cover the wages 
of the Crew Staff at the Westminster Sustainability Center.

Subject: Authorization of a Bid Waiver for Sports Officiating Services with Pros Plus LTD
Prepared By: Tim Lachermeier, Recreation Services Manager
Aric Otzelberger, Parks, Recreation and Libraries Director
Recommended City Council Action:
Based on the recommendation of the City Manager, find that the public interest will be best served by 
authorizing a bid waiver with Pros Plus LTD for sports officiating services in an amount not to exceed 
$175,000 for a one-year contract, with two one-year contract renewals subject to annual 
appropriations, for a cumulative total of up to $525,000 for the three-year period. 
Summary Statement:
Westminster Municipal Code requires authorization by City Council for bid waivers.  
The vendor will provide officiating services for the City of Westminster Adult Softball, Senior 
Softball, Adult Basketball, Youth Basketball, and on-call officiating services for Adult Volleyball 
Leagues. 
There are limited vendors that provide this service in the Denver Metro Area.  
Staff engaged in a formal bid process in 2025 and an award was presented to Millennium 
Health and Fitness.

Pros Plus LTD is a local company and has a history of successfully providing these services to

Policy Issue(s):
Should City Council approve a bid waiver with Pros Plus LTD for sports officiating services? 
Alternative(s):
1. City Council could choose not to approve the agreement as recommended and direct Staff to 
conduct a new competitive solicitation. This alternative is not recommended because the City 
recently completed a formal competitive procurement that resulted in the award of a contract 
that was subsequently terminated. Conducting another solicitation at this time is unlikely to 
produce a materially different outcome, given the limited competition experienced in this 
specialized market, and the extended procurement timeline would create a significant risk of 
disrupting recreation programming and local sports leagues.
2. City Council could choose not to approve the agreement as recommended and no further 
sports officiating services would be pursued. This alternative is not recommended because it 
would require canceling all adult and youth sports league programs effective mid-August 2026.
Background Information:
The Recreation Services Division operates three adult softball leagues, two adult basketball leagues, 
one adult volleyball league, and two youth afterschool leagues, totaling more than 2,150 games each 
year. All of these programs require consistent, qualified sports officials to safely operate.
In 2025, the Recreation Services Team conducted a formal competitive bid process for Sports 
Officiating Services. As a result of that process, a contract was awarded to Millennium Health and 
Fitness in January 2026. However, in April 2026, the City terminated the contract due to the vendor’s 
inability to meet essential performance expectations.

At the time of termination, the City was already several weeks into the spring adult softball season 
and preparing for summer leagues beginning in May. To prevent any interruption to officiating 
services for ongoing recreation leagues, Recreation Services partnered with Procurement to quickly 
draft and execute a short-term contract with Pros Plus Ltd. The temporary bridge contract was 
authorized for an amount not to exceed $50,000.
Staff anticipated that the $50,000 threshold would not be sufficient to fund officiating services for the 
remainder of 2026, however a competitive bid process would disrupt officiating coverage for active 
recreation programs. Maintaining consistent officiating is essential to program quality, participant 
safety, and customer satisfaction. Therefore, Staff is seeking City Council approval for a bid waiver  
with Pros Plus Ltd to complete the remainder of the 2026 recreation programs.
Pros Plus LTD (formerly P.R.O.S.) has provided Sports Officiating Services to the City of Westminster 
for more than 20 years. During multiple formal bid processes over the years, Pros Plus has 
consistently been the lowest-cost provider and is also a local vendor with established capacity and 
familiarity with City programs.
During the 2025 bid cycle, Pros Plus LTD did not submit a proposal due to a clerical error, resulting in 
the award to Millennium Health and Fitness. When Millennium was unable to fulfill contractual 
requirements, Pros Plus responded immediately and assumed officiating responsibilities with very 
short notice, ensuring no interruption to program delivery. Their long-standing service history, proven 
reliability, local availability, and ability to step in promptly during an unplanned transition demonstrate 
that Pros Plus LTD is qualified to meet the City’s immediate and ongoing officiating needs.
The City has competitively solicited sports officiating services on multiple occasions in accordance 
with the Westminster Municipal Code. Historically, Pros Plus LTD has been the successful proposer 
and has demonstrated the ability to reliably recruit, manage, and retain qualified officials necessary to 
support the City’s recreation programs.
Following the termination of the 2025 contract, Staff evaluated available procurement options and 
determined that conducting another formal competitive solicitation at this time is unlikely to produce a 
materially different outcome. Previous solicitations have demonstrated limited competition within this 
specialized market, and conducting another solicitation would require a significant investment of City 
and vendor resources while creating a substantial risk of disrupting recreation programming.
The Procurement Manager has determined that approval of this bid waiver is in the City’s best 
interest because it provides continuity of critical recreation services, minimizes operational risk, and 
ensures uninterrupted service to the public. Based on the City’s procurement history, current market 
conditions, and the successful performance history of Pros Plus LTD, a multi-year agreement 
provides stability while Staff continues evaluating long-term strategies to encourage greater vendor 
participation and competition in future procurements.
Utilizing sports officiating services supports the City’s Strategic Priorities of Opportunity to Thrive and 
Healthy and Safe by providing a variety of recreational and wellness opportunities to Westminster 
community members.
