SUBJECT: A RESOLUTION ADOPTING A FEE SCHEDULE FOR THE SOUTHERN

SUMMARY: 
This proposed Resolution adopts the fee schedule attached as Exhibit A for licenses, 
registrations, applications, and plan review services performed by the Southern 
Colorado Building Department (SCBD) within the City of Pueblo. Section 4-1-15(a) of 
the Pueblo Municipal Code directs that Department fees be established by resolution of 
the City Council. The fee schedule reflects the categories the SCBD Building 
Commission recommended by its Resolution No. 2026-09, adopted July 29, 2026, with 
two categories excluded and noted in the Background section. 
 
This Resolution is presented concurrently with the paired Ordinance amending Sec. 4-
1-24 of the Pueblo Municipal Code to create the Mechanical Contractor-B, Mechanical 
Contractor-C (Residential), and Mechanical Contractor-D classifications. Second 
reading and final passage of the paired Ordinance is scheduled for the October 26, 
2026 Council meeting, concurrent with introduction and adoption of this Resolution, so 
that no fee becomes operative ahead of the classification that authorizes it. 
PREVIOUS COUNCIL ACTION: 
None. Since the effective date of the Intergovernmental Agreement for the Operation of 
the Southern Colorado Building Department on January 1, 2026, no fee schedule has 
been adopted by the City Council under Section 9 of that Agreement. 
BACKGROUND: 
The Intergovernmental Agreement for the Operation of the Southern Colorado Building 
Department (the IGA), effective January 1, 2026, provides at Section 9 that all license, 
building permit, plan review, and inspection fees for activities within each member entity 
shall be established as provided by ordinance or resolution of the respective member 
Page 20 of 467
entity. Section 23 of the IGA provides that the Building Commission recommends a 
schedule of fees, and the member entities, after considering the recommendation, 
adopt the schedule. 
 
Section 4-1-15(a) of the Pueblo Municipal Code, as amended by Ordinance No. 11082, 
provides that the fees and charges for licenses, examinations, permits, inspections, 
reinspections and all other services or activities performed by the Department, the 
Building Official, and the Boards shall be established by the resolution of the City 
Council. That section is the direct authority for adopting the attached fee schedule by 
resolution. 
 
The SCBD Building Commission adopted its Resolution No. 2026-09 on July 29, 2026, 
recommending the fee schedule reflected in Exhibit A. Exhibit A is structured in three 
sections. Section 1 establishes the annual license and registration fees under PMC 
Sec. 4-1-15(a). Section 2 establishes a $95.00 administrative fee under PMC Sec. 4-1-
15(b)(2) for each application for a license or registration listed in Section 1, and a 
$95.00 administrative fee under PMC Sec. 4-1-15(a) for each manufactured home 
setter application, which is a Department service that does not involve the issuance of a 
license. Section 3 establishes a $35.00 plan review re-submittal fee under PMC Sec. 4-
1-15(a) for revisions submitted after the second revision. 
 
Exhibit A does not restate the annual renewal cycle or the renewal fee multipliers 
codified at PMC Sec. 4-1-15(b)(1), which by their own terms apply to the fees 
established in Section 1 of Exhibit A. 
 
Two categories on the Building Commission's recommended schedule are excluded 
from Section 1 of Exhibit A: Water Connected Appliance and Journeyman Refrigeration 
License. Neither category corresponds to a license classification in Title IV, Chapter 1, 
of the Pueblo Municipal Code. Both have been referred to the Southern Colorado 
Building Department's General Counsel for reconciliation. 
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS: 
The Resolution establishes the fees the Southern Colorado Building Department 
collects from applicants for licenses, registrations, applications, and plan review 
services within the City of Pueblo. Pursuant to IGA Section 23, the Department is 
intended to be self-supporting through fees. No City general fund revenue or 
expenditure results from adoption of this Resolution.

The Southern Colorado Building Commission recommended adoption of the fee 
schedule by its Resolution No. 2026-09, adopted July 29, 2026.

SUMMARY: 
Attached is a Resolution addressing two Black Hills Colorado Electric, LLC proceedings

the company’s proposed electric rate increase in Proceeding No. 26AL-0232E, which 
seeks $26,663,479 in additional annual base rate revenue and a 14.7 percent 
residential increase before a temporary offset. The Resolution also opposes the 
additional ratepayer cost impacts of the company’s 2026 Electric Resource Plan and 
Clean Energy Plan in Proceeding No. 26A-0333E, which the company’s own analysis

PREVIOUS COUNCIL ACTION: 
City Council has previously opposed rate increases requested by Black Hills, including 
by resolution adopted July 22, 2024 concerning the company’s prior rate review.  
BACKGROUND: 
Black Hills Colorado Electric, LLC ("Black Hills") provides retail electric service to more 
than 102,000 customers in southern Colorado, including the City of Pueblo, its 
residents, and its businesses, as well as Cañon City, Rocky Ford, and surrounding 
communities. Two Black Hills proceedings are now pending before the Colorado Public 
Utilities Commission (the "Commission"). 
Page 36 of 467
 
In Proceeding No. 26AL-0232E, filed June 12, 2026, Black Hills seeks $26,663,479 in 
additional base rate revenue each year. For a residential customer using 600 kilowatt-
hours per month, the application would raise the average monthly bill from $124.18 to 
$142.48, an increase of 14.7 percent. Black Hills proposes a temporary credit over two 
years that reduces the advertised increase to 8.8 percent, but that credit returns money 
the company previously collected from its ratepayers under its renewable energy and 
clean energy programs, and the full 14.7 percent base rate increase remains once the 
credit expires. 
 
In Proceeding No. 26A-0333E, filed September 11, 2026, Black Hills seeks approval of 
its 2026 Electric Resource Plan and Clean Energy Plan and its 2027 through 2030 
Renewable Energy Standard Compliance Plan. The application proposes acquisition of 
approximately 424 megawatts of new generation and storage and an increase in the 
Renewable Energy Standard Adjustment surcharge from 1.5 percent to 2.0 percent. 
The company’s own bill-impact analysis, filed as Hearing Exhibit 107, Attachment NHL-
1, projects that the plan would raise the average residential monthly bill at 600 kilowatt-
hours by approximately 7.9 percent between 2026 and 2030, from $119.90 to $129.38, 
with further increases after 2030, recovered primarily through the Electric Commodity 
Adjustment as new resources come into service. 
 
Taken together, the proposed rate increase and the projected cost impacts of the 
resource plan would substantially increase the cost of electric service for the City’s 
residents and businesses over the coming years.  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS: 
If the Commission approves the rate increase, or the cost impacts of the resource plan 
reach customer rates, the City will pay higher electric rates for its own facilities and 
operations, and the City’s residents and businesses will pay higher rates for electric 
service. The precise additional cost to the City has not been quantified. Adoption of the 
Resolution itself carries no cost to the City.

Subrecipient may request termination of this Agreement by sending notice to the State, or to the

Award. The terms and conditions of the Federal Award flow down to the Award unless the terms 
and conditions of the Federal Award specifically indicate otherwise.

institution of proceedings under any bankruptcy, insolvency, reorganization or similar law, by or

109-105, C.R.S., at any time during the term of this Agreement, then such debarment or 
suspension shall constitute a breach.
