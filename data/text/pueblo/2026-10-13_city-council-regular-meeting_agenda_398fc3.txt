SUMMARY: 
Attached is a Resolution awarding a Marketing Campaign Contract to Staples 
Marketing, LLC d/b/a AFFIRM. for Project No. PT2402 Pueblo Transit Vanpool Pilot 
Program to develop and execute a comprehensive marketing campaign aimed at 
promoting the Vanpool program. The objective of this campaign is to increase 
awareness, participation, and ridership of the Vanpool service among residents and 
businesses in the Pueblo area. 
PREVIOUS COUNCIL ACTION: 
Ordinance No. 10646, accepted the Grant for this project and budgeted and 
appropriated the funds into Project PT2402. 
BACKGROUND: 
Pueblo Transit has piloted a Vanpool program to create an additional transportation 
option for employers/employees who struggle finding modes of transportation. The 
Vanpool program is run under direct coordination with Commute Enterprise to provide 
late models vehicles used by employees to commute to and from work. The Vanpool 
Pilot project is meant to aid those who work shift hours outside of Pueblo Transit’s 
hours of operation and outside of the fixed route service areas. The project is aimed at 
providing efficient workforce transportation primarily benefiting economically 
disadvantaged individuals. The marketing campaign shall include a strategic marketing 
plan, developing promotional materials, media buys including advertisement and 
implementing various marketing activities to achieve these goals. The City's/Pueblo 
Page 21 of 546
Transit's goal is to increase ridership within the program and offer a subsidy through an 
MMOF funded grant to support the growth of Pueblo and its Citizens. 
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS: 
The cost of the marketing project was set at $50,000 and there is no match required as 
this is included in the budget for the Vanpool Pilot Program project.

Pueblo Transit Board of Directors approve this Resolution.

3.6  PROJECT APPROACH
Approach and Methodology to Accomplish the Scope of Service 
AFFIRM approaches every TDM assignment through a disciplined sequence: understand the audience before 
writing a word, build the plan around what research says will move them, create work that earns attention, 
place it where the budget goes furthest, and measure honestly enough that the next flight is smarter than the 
last. The subsections below map that methodology directly onto Scope of Service elements A through G. 
A. Market Research and Analysis Method. We begin with what Pueblo Transit already knows. In the kickoff
we will request existing rider and applicant data, vanpool roster and waitlist history, prior campaign results,
employer contact lists, and any CDOT or regional commuter survey data. We then augment it with syndicated
research; Scarborough Research, which profiles audiences by age, income, geography, and media consumption,
an
d with U.S. Census LEHD/LODES origin-destination commuting data to map where Pueblo County residents
actually work and which corridors carry enough long-distance commuters to sustain a viable van.
Demographics, commuting patterns, and transportation preferences. Our TDM research consistently 
identifies the receptive commuter as an employed adult 25–54, slightly more likely to be male, with a college or 
postgraduate degree, and, importantly, with a long commute. Receptiveness to ridesharing rises with commute 
length; people who spend more time in the car are measurably more open to alternatives. For Pueblo that 
points the campaign at residents commuting to employment centers within and beyond Pueblo County, and at 
major employers whose workforces cluster geographically and by shift. 
Barriers to vanpool participation. We will validate locally, but the barriers are 
well documented across our TDM work and we will build the strategy to answer 
each one directly: loss of schedule flexibility (answered by the 
Guaranteed/Emergency Ride Home benefit, which our GWRideConnect and 
CIRTA work shows is one of the most persuasive single messages available); not 
knowing anyone else on the route (answered by ridematching and by employer-
hosted formation events); unclear cost and how it compares to driving alone 
(answered with a concrete monthly cost comparison rather than a vague 
savings claim); uncertainty about vehicle quality (answered by showing the 
recent-model vans and SUVs); and simple unawareness that the program exists. 
We will confirm and rank these barriers through a short intercept survey of 
current riders and a structured conversation with three to five major area 
employers during Phase 1. 
Competitive positioning. We will analyze the realistic alternatives available to 
the Pueblo commuter, such as driving alone, informal carpooling, existing 
Pueblo Transit fixed-route service, regional and interregional bus service such 
as CDOT's Bustang, and remote work, and then position vanpool where it wins:

B. Strategic Marketing Plan
The Strategic Marketing Plan converts research into a decision document. It will state measurable objectives 
tied to the program outcomes Pueblo Transit cares about; awareness lift, inquiries, applications, filled seats,

E. Advertising and Media Buying
All media planning and buying is performed in-house by AFFIRM's media team under Media Director Christina
Galke and Associate Media Director Katie Kadlcek. Some agencies farm media out to third parties or list
principals on an account and delegate to a junior team. At AFFIRM there is no junior team, and there is no third-
party markup between Pueblo Transit's budget and the media.
Research and plan development. We set reach and frequency goals as a function of budget, message 
complexity, competitive environment, and universe size. We are media-neutral: every tactic is worth evaluating, 
but the only tactics worth buying are the ones research shows will reach this audience efficiently. In comparable 
TDM markets, Scarborough consistently identifies local radio, online, and local television as the most-used 
tactics by the commuter target. Television is rarely affordable at this budget level; radio, streaming audio, digital 
display, paid social, and search consistently are, and digital and streaming provide the corridor-level geographic 
targeting that vanpool recruitment demands. 
Anticipated tactical mix for Pueblo, subject to Phase 1 research: geo-targeted paid social to commuter 
corridors and employer workplaces; streaming audio, which delivered a 94.76% completion rate on 
GWRideConnect's vanpool effort; Google Search to capture active intent; digital display and video retargeting; 
local broadcast radio in flights; and selective out-of-home along the highest-volume commute corridors. We will 
recommend a flighted structure rather than continuous low-weight presence, so the campaign achieves real 
frequency during its in-market periods. 
Negotiation and added value. Public sector accounts require specialized negotiating skill in achieving both 
value, good pricing on inventory, and added value in the form of bonus spots, PSAs, and promotions. We 
negotiate all tactics simultaneously to maximize leverage, and we routinely secure thousands of dollars in 
bonus placement that we pass directly to the client. On a recent nine-week GWRideConnect radio flight we 
delivered 768 spots against 632 ordered, resulting in 21% bonus weight. On a $50,000 campaign, that kind of 
negotiated value is not a nice-to-have; it is a material share of total delivery. 
Execution and monitoring. AFFIRM uses MediaForce media buying software and subscribes to Nielsen Audio 
and Television ratings. Pueblo Transit will receive a detailed media plan specifying every tactic, unit count, cost 
per point or impression, budget allocation, and value-added component. Our job does not stop at placement: 
we monitor schedule performance, and any deviation is made good through credits or additional spots. Our

G. Performance Measurement and Reporting
We will establish KPIs jointly with Pueblo Transit during Phase 1 so that everyone agrees in advance what 
success looks like. We recommend a tiered structure: awareness measures (impressions, reach, frequency, 
video and audio completion rates, share of search), engagement measures (clicks, CTR, landing page sessions, 
time on page, social engagement rate), and conversion and program outcomes (program inquiries, completed 
applications, matched riders, seats filled, new vanpools formed, and cost per application). 
Tracking. We will implement UTM tagging on every placement, conversion tracking on the Vanpool inquiry and 
application paths, and call tracking where the program receives phone inquiries, so that outcomes can be 
attributed to channel rather than assumed. Baselines are captured before launch; without a baseline, a lift 
claim is not a measurement. 
Reporting. Pueblo Transit will receive a monthly performance report throughout the campaign covering 
delivery against plan, channel-level performance, spend to date against budget, and — most importantly — 
what we are changing next month as a result. Mid-campaign we will conduct a formal optimization review and 
reallocate budget toward what is working. Within 14 calendar days of campaign completion, we will deliver a 
comprehensive final report covering total reach, engagement, ridership and application impact, cost efficiency 
by channel, lessons learned, and specific recommendations for sustaining growth after the campaign period. All 
reports are delivered electronically; print copies are provided as needed. 
Deliverable:  KPI framework and measurement plan, tracking implementation, monthly performance reports 
throughout the campaign, mid-campaign optimization review, and a comprehensive final campaign report 
within 14 calendar days of campaign completion.
