Action Item #1 - Review of Executive Summary and Public Comment 
The Chair clarified that public comment would not be taken during Committee 
reflection on the written Executive Summary or, typically, when reviewing written 
comments. Committee members discussed balancing public input with the 
Committee’s available time. 
Action Item #2 - Continuation of line-by-line discussion and editing of the 
Draft Ordinance 
The Committee began with  Section 9 (Appeals) and supported requiring written 
appeals to be filed with the Town Clerk, either in person or electronically, within 
15 days of the action being appealed. The Board would hear appeals as soon as 
possible, but no later than 45 days after the written appeal is received. The 
Committee favored omitting language regarding judicial review and discussed 
limitations on the scope of appealable matters. 
The Committee discussed Section 10 (Annual Review and Saturation Threshold). 
Discussion here included annual reporting requirements, written complaints, and 
the importance of local data. 
The main topic of discussion focused on the percentage of registered whole-home, 
non-owner-occupied short-term rentals (relative to overall housing stock) that 
would trigger a pause in new registrations and other possible actions. The proposed 
Common Ground ordinance lists that number at 8%. The Committee’s current 
working assumption is that the current percentage of whole-home, non-owner-
occupied short-term rentals in Paonia is around 3%. No final decision was reached 
on the numerical percentage. The Committee will continue discussion on this 
important topic. The Committee did agree that the threshold finding would be

Town, such as estimating administrative costs and measuring housing impacts.

Executive Summary 
Short-Term Rental (STR) Survey Responses 
This executive summary provides an overview of feedback received following the October 
20 STR Committee meeting. 
Preservation of Housing Stock 
Several comments emphasized the importance of maintaining housing availability for 
permanent residents and long-term renters. Respondents expressed concern that STRs may 
reduce access to housing for members of the local workforce and long-term community 
residents. 
Neighborhood Compatibility 
Concerns were raised regarding the placement of STRs within residential neighborhoods, 
including potential impacts related to parking, noise, safety, and the overall residential 
character of neighborhoods. 
Regulatory and Tax Equity 
Survey participants supported regulatory measures that would ensure STRs contribute 
similarly to traditional lodging establishments through lodging taxes and other 
requirements. Some respondents also suggested occupancy limits or owner-occupancy 
requirements. 
Market Conditions 
One respondent noted that current market trends may already be reducing STR activity, 
citing properties returning to the for-sale market and questioning the long-term viability of 
STRs given seasonal tourism patterns in Paonia. 
Conclusion 
While the number of survey responses was limited, the feedback generally supports policy 
approaches that balance tourism accommodations with the protection of long-term housing 
opportunities and neighborhood livability. Respondents favored measures that promote 
fairness between STRs and traditional lodging businesses while ensuring that residential 
housing remains available to local residents.

Suggested approach 
At each meeting, I suggest that we focus first on policy decisions and 
then on wording. Once the committee agrees on what the ordinance should 
do, the language can be refined to reflect those decisions. 
It might also be helpful, before each meeting, to circulate only the sections 
that will be discussed and ask committee members to consider three 
questions: 
1. What do you like? 
2. What concerns you? 
3. What specific change would you propose? 
 
I think this could help us come prepared and keep the meetings focused 
and productive. A guiding principle I think could be useful throughout the 
process is: Before adding a provision, identify the specific problem it is 
intended to address and consider whether the provision is a reasonable 
and necessary way to address that problem. 
 
The goal is to have a clear, fair, proportional, and workable ordinance that 
fits Paonia.

 Town of Paonia, Short-Term Rental Ordinance 
WORKING DRAFT 
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia recognizes that short-term rentals contribute to 
tourism, local businesses, agritourism, and community activities, and can allow local 
homeowners to supplement and diversify their income when renting all or a portion of 
their homes;  
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia recognizes that long-term residents, whether 
homeowners or long-term renters, are the foundation of our sustainable, thriving 
community, economy, and schools; 
WHEREAS, the Board of Trustees recognizes that there are many types of short-term 
rentals, and that non-owner-occupied whole-home short-term rentals may impact the 
availability of housing.” 
 
WHEREAS, the Board of Trustees seeks to establish a clear, fair, and proportional 
framework for the registration and regulation of short-term rentals that is appropriate to 
Paonia’s size, character, and sustainability as a small town; and 
 
Unresolved: WHEREAS, the purposes of this Ordinance are to promote public safety, 
address nuisances, provide accountability, ensure applicable tax compliance, establish 
an administratively workable registration process, continue to monitor how short-term 
rentals are affecting residents of the Town of Paonia, and adaptively manage the total 
stock of non-owner occupied, whole home short-term rentals;  
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE 
TOWN OF PAONIA, COLORADO: 
SECTION 1. DEFINITIONS 
For the purposes of this Article, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth 
below: 
A. “Short-Term Rental” (STR) means the rental of a dwelling unit, or any 
portion thereof, for a period of fewer than thirty (30) consecutive days, in 
exchange for compensation. This term includes the rental of an entire dwelling 
unit or individual rooms within a dwelling unit. This term does not include hotels, 
motels, and lodging facilities (including room houses and bed-and-breakfasts) 
licensed under other provisions of the Paonia Municipal Code, nor does it include 
the renting of rooms on a monthly or longer basis as permitted under §16-11-20 
(Home Occupations) and §16-11-30 (Renting of Rooms).

2 
B. FROM THE REPEALED ORDINANCE: “Single Bedroom STR” means a privately-
owned bedroom within a Primary Residence, or within the Core Commercial District (C-
1) that is rented for the purpose of lodging for any period less than thirty (30) 
consecutive days, where other facilities may be shared with the owner of the dwelling. 
C. “Primary Residence A privately-owned residential dwelling or property at which the 
resident resides for at least 183 days per year. 
 
D. "Dwelling Unit" means a single-family home, an accessory dwelling unit, a 
detached accessory building with sleeping accommodations, or any other residential 
structure lawfully permitted for habitation under the Paonia Municipal Code. 
E. "Operator" means the property owner, or a person or entity authorized by the 
property owner, who manages and operates a short-term rental. 
F. "Local Contact Person" means a person located within thirty (30) miles of the short-
term rental property who is available twenty-four (24) hours per day to respond to 
complaints, emergencies, or inquiries related to the operation of the short-term rental. 
G. "Registration" means the approval granted by the Town of Paonia authorizing the 
operation of a short-term rental in accordance with the requirements of this Article. 
SECTION 2. REGISTRATION REQUIRED 
A. No person shall operate, advertise, or offer a short-term rental within the Town of 
Paonia without first obtaining and maintaining a valid Short-Term Rental Registration 
from the Town. 
B. A Registration is valid for a period of three (3) years from the date of issuance and 
must be renewed for each subsequent three-year period. 
C. A Registration is non-transferable. If ownership of the property changes, the new 
owner must apply for a new Registration prior to operating a short-term rental. 
D. Existing short-term rental operators shall have until December 31, _____ to submit a 
Registration application. 
SECTION 3. APPLICATION AND FEES 
A. Applications for a Short-Term Rental Registration shall be submitted to the Town 
Administrator or designee on forms prescribed by the Town. The application shall 
include: 
1. Property address. 
2. Applicant name and relation to the property (owner, authorized manager, etc.).

3. Mailing address, telephone number(s), and email address of the applicant. 
4. Name, address, and 24-hour telephone number of the Local Contact Person (if 
the operator does not reside on the property). 
5. Name and contact information of the property owner, if different from the 
applicant. 
6. Number of bedrooms to be rented and advertised maximum occupancy. 
7. Whether the rental will be located in a single-family home, accessory dwelling 
unit, or detached accessory building. 
8. Whether the rental will be managed on-site by owner, on-site by manager, off-
site by owner, or off-site by manager. 
9. Anticipated availability (year-round, seasonal, or occasional). 
10. Proof of property ownership or written authorization from the property owner. 
11. Self-certification affidavit affirming compliance with the safety and operating 
standards set forth in Section 5.

B. The Registration fee shall be set for whole home, non-owner-occupied short-term 
rentals by resolution of the Board of Trustees and shall be limited to the reasonable 
costs of administering this Article.  
C. The Town Administrator or designee shall approve or deny an application within thirty 
(30) days of receipt of a complete application. If denied, the applicant shall receive 
written notice stating the reasons for denial. 
SECTION 4. TAX COMPLIANCE 
A. All short-term rental operators shall collect and remit all applicable taxes required by 
the Paonia Municipal Code or Colorado law. 
B. Operators using third-party booking platforms that collect and remit taxes on behalf 
of the operator shall maintain documentation of such arrangements and provide it to the 
Town upon request. 
  
SECTION 5. SAFETY AND OPERATING STANDARDS 
By signature of the Registration application, the owner or operator affirms compliance 
with the following standards, which shall be maintained at all times the short-term rental 
is in operation: 
A. Life Safety

1. All sleeping rooms have operable emergency escape windows that meet the 
applicable building code. 
2. Each bedroom to be rented has a functioning smoke detector, with a smoke 
detector also in an adjacent hallway. 
3. Each bedroom is located on a floor equipped with a functioning carbon monoxide 
alarm if the dwelling unit has a carbon monoxide source (gas appliance, 
fireplace, attached garage, or similar). 
4. An operable fire extinguisher has been placed in proximity to the kitchen. 
5.  Each bedroom shall have ingress and egress paths of travel to exit doors from 
each bedroom. 
B. Occupancy and Wastewater 
6. Maximum occupancy shall not exceed two (2) persons per bedroom plus two (2) 
additional persons. 
7. Properties served by the Town's municipal sewer system shall comply with all 
applicable sewer use regulations. 
C. Property and Neighborhood Standards 
8. Guest vehicles should be parked in a safe and lawful manner consistent with existing 
Town parking regulations. When available, off-street parking on the property may be 
used. However, on-street parking in front of or near the property shall be permitted 
where it is otherwise allowed under Town parking regulations. 
9. Trash and recycling facilities shall be sufficient to address guest impacts and shall be 
properly maintained. Using a Paonia Board of Trustees approved information source or 
sources, guests need to be made aware of best practices and the Town’s ordinance 
related to keeping bears out of trash cans and other garbage.  
10. The house number shall be clearly visible from the street. 
11. If a sign is used to advertise the short-term rental, the sign shall comply with any 
applicable sign regulations in the Paonia Municipal Code. 
12. Quiet hours shall be observed between 10:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. 
13. Sidewalks and pathways shall be kept clear of snow and ice consistent with 
applicable Town requirements. 
D. Guest Information and Contact

14. Guests shall be provided with the 24-hour contact telephone number of the Local 
Contact Person.  
15. Emergency contact information, including the Paonia Police Department non-
emergency number and 911, shall be posted in a visible location inside the rental unit. 
16. The approved Short-Term Rental Registration shall be posted in a location visible to 
visiting guests. 
E. Liability Insurance 
17. Liability insurance is required.   
SECTION 6. LISTING AND ADVERTISING REQUIREMENTS 
A. All advertisements and online listings for a short-term rental within the Town of 
Paonia shall include the Town-issued Registration number. 
B. No person shall advertise or list a short-term rental that does not hold a current, valid 
Registration. 
 SECTION 7. NEIGHBOR NOTIFICATION 
A. Upon request, the operator shall provide neighbors with the name and 24-hour 
contact telephone number of the Local Contact Person. 
SECTION 8. ENFORCEMENT AND COMPLAINTS 
The Local Contact Person is required to respond to calls for assistance within 30 
minutes. If unavailable within that time frame, a back-up person who is available within 
30 minutes must be designated. 
A. Complaint-Based Enforcement 
Enforcement of this Article shall be primarily complaint-based. The Town shall maintain 
a record of all written complaints received concerning a registered short-term rental. 
B. Complaint Process 
1. Any person may file a written complaint with the Town Administrator regarding a 
violation of this Article.  
2. The Town Administrator or designee shall notify the operator and the Local 
Contact Person of the complaint as soon as possible and no later than five (5) 
business days.  
3. The operator shall have fifteen (15) business days from notification to respond in 
writing and, if applicable, to remedy the violation.

C. Escalating Penalties 
1. First documented violation: Written warning. 
2. Second documented violation within a twelve-month period: Fine of $150.00. 
3. Third documented violation within a twelve-month period: Fine of $300.00 and 
suspension of Registration for up to ninety (90) days.  
4. Fourth or subsequent documented violation within a twelve-month period, or 
failure to remedy a prior violation: Revocation of Registration. 
D. Revocation 
1. If a Registration is revoked, a new Registration shall not be issued for the 
operator and property in question for a period of at least one (1) year from the 
date of revocation. 
2. The operator shall cease all short-term rental activity immediately upon receipt of 
a notice to cease and desist. This point may need finessing. 
E. Operating Without Registration 
Any person who operates a short-term rental without a valid Registration shall be 
subject to a fine of up to $300.00 per occurrence. Each day of operation without a 
Registration shall constitute a separate occurrence. 
SECTION 9. APPEALS 
A. Any applicant or operator aggrieved by a determination, interpretation, or decision of 
the Town Administrator under this Article may appeal such action to the Board of 
Trustees. 
B. The appeal shall be in writing, shall state the basis for the appeal, and shall be filed 
(in person or electronically) with the Town Clerk within fifteen (15) days of the action 
being appealed. 
C. Appeals shall be limited to determining whether the Town Administrator has misread, 
misinterpreted, or misapplied a provision of this Article — not for relief from a standard 
or requirement itself. 
D. The Board of Trustees shall hear the appeal at a regular or special meeting as soon 
as possible but no later than forty-five (45) days after the written appeal is received. 
E. The decision of the Board of Trustees shall be final. 
SECTION 10. TWELVE-MONTH REVIEW AND SATURATION THRESHOLD 
A. Annual Review

No later than twelve (12) months after the effective date of this Ordinance, and annually 
thereafter, the Town Administrator shall present to the Board of Trustees a written 
report evaluating the following: 
1. The total number of registered short-term rentals and their share of total housing 
units. 
2. The total number and nature of written complaints received by the Town.  
3. Any recommendations for amendments to this Article, supported by documented 
local data. 
B. Saturation Review Threshold 
1. If the total number of registered whole-home, non-owner-occupied short-term 
rentals equals eight percent (8%) of total housing units within the Town, the 
Town Administrator shall report to the Board of Trustees this finding as soon as 
possible, but no more than 30 days after the threshold is met.  
2. The saturation review threshold established in this subsection is a trigger to 
pause new registrations and determine next steps. 
3. Any new Registration application that is complete and pending at the time the 
threshold finding is reported shall not be processed while the Board of Trustees 
is reviewing the issue.
