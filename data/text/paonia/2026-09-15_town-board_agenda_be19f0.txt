I. Rules of Procedure  
Section 1.  Schedule of Meetings.  Regular Board of Trustees meetings shall be held on the 
second and fourth Tuesdays of each month, except on legal holidays, or as re-scheduled or 
amended and posted on the agenda prior to the scheduled meeting. 
 
Section 2.  Officiating Officer.  The meetings of the Board of Trustees shall be conducted by the 
Mayor or, in the Mayor’s absence, the Mayor Pro-Tem.  The Town Clerk or a designee of the 
Board shall record the minutes of the meetings. 
Section 3.  Time of Meetings.  Regular meetings of the Board of Trustees shall begin at 6:30 
p.m. or as scheduled and posted on the agenda. Board Members shall be called to order by the 
Mayor.  The meetings shall open with the presiding officer leading the Board in the Pledge of 
Allegiance.  The Town Clerk shall then proceed to call the roll, note the absences and announce 
whether a quorum is present.  Regular Meetings are scheduled for three hours, and shall be 
adjourned at 9:30 p.m., unless a majority of the Board votes in the affirmative to extend the 
meeting, by a specific amount of time.  
Section 4. Schedule of Business.  If a quorum is present, the Board of Trustees shall proceed 
with the business before it, which shall be conducted in the following manner.  Note that all 
provided times are estimated:  
     (a) Roll Call - (5 minutes) 
    (b) Approval of Agenda - (5 minutes) 
    (c) Announcements (5 minutes) 
    (d) Recognition of Visitors and Guests (10 minutes) 
    (e) Consent Agenda including Approval of Prior Meeting Minutes (10 
minutes) 
    (f) Mayor’s Report (10 minutes) 
    (g) Staff Reports: (15 minutes) 
        (1) Town Administrator’s Report 
        (2) Public Works Reports  
        (3) Police Report 
        (4) Treasurer Report 
                  
    (h) Unfinished Business (45 minutes) 
    (i) New Business (45 minutes) 
    (j) Disbursements (15 minutes) 
    (k) Committee Reports (15 minutes) 
    (l) Adjournment 
* This schedule of business is subject to change and amendment.  
Section 5. Priority and Order of Business. Questions relative to the priority of business 
and order shall be decided by the Mayor without debate, subject in all cases to an 
appeal to the Board of Trustees.  
 
Section 6.  Conduct of Board Members.   Town Board Members shall treat other Board 
Members and the public in a civil and polite manner and shall comply with the 
Standards of Conduct for Elected Officials of the Town.  Board Members shall address 
Town Staff and the Mayor by his/her title, other Board Members by the title of Trustee or 
the appropriate honorific (i.e.: Mr., Mrs. or Ms.), and members of the public by the 
Page 2 of 103
appropriate honorific.  Subject to the Mayor’s discretion, Board Members shall be limited 
to speaking two times when debating an item on the agenda.  Making a motion, asking 
a question or making a suggestion are not counted as speaking in a debate.  
 
Section 7. Presentations to the Board.  Items on the agenda presented by individuals, 
businesses or other organizations shall be given up to 5 minutes to make a 
presentation.  On certain issues, presenters may be given more time, as determined by 
the Mayor and Town Staff.  After the presentation, Trustees shall be given the 
opportunity to ask questions. 
 
Section 8. Public Comment.  After discussion of an agenda item by the Board of 
Trustees has concluded, the Mayor shall open the floor for comment from members of 
the public, who shall be allowed the opportunity to comment or ask questions on the 
agenda item.  Each member of the public wishing to address the Town Board shall be 
recognized by the presiding officer before speaking.  Members of the public shall speak 
from the podium, stating their name, the address of their residence and any group they 
are representing prior to making comment or asking a question.  Comments shall be 
directed to the Mayor or presiding officer, not to an individual Trustee or Town 
employee.  Comments or questions should be confined to the agenda item or issue(s) 
under discussion.  The speaker should offer factual information and refrain from 
obscene language and personal attacks. 
 
Section 9.  Unacceptable Behavior. Disruptive behavior shall result in expulsion from 
the meeting.   
 
Section 10.  Posting of Rules of Procedure for Paonia Board of Trustees Meetings. 
These rules of procedure shall be provided in the Town Hall meeting room for each 
Board of Trustees meeting so that all attendees know how the meeting will be 
conducted. 
 
II. Consent Agenda 
 
Section 1. Use of Consent Agenda. The Mayor, working with Town Staff, shall place 
items on the Consent Agenda. By using a Consent Agenda, the Board has consented to 
the consideration of certain items as a group under one motion. Should a Consent 
Agenda be used at a meeting, an appropriate amount of discussion time will be allowed 
to review any item upon request.  
Section 2. General Guidelines. Items for consent are those which usually do not require 
discussion or explanation prior to action by the Board, are non-controversial and/or 
similar in content, or are those items which have already been discussed or explained 
and do not require further discussion or explanation. Such agenda items may include 
ministerial tasks such as, but not limited to, approval of previous meeting minutes, 
approval of staff reports, addressing routine correspondence, approval of liquor licenses 
renewals and approval or extension of other Town licenses. Minor changes in the 
minutes such as non-material Scribner errors may be made without removing the 
minutes from the Consent Agenda.  Should any Trustee feel there is a material error in 
the minutes, they should request the minutes be removed from the Consent Agenda for 
Board discussion. 
Section 3. Removal of Item from Consent Agenda. One or more items may be removed 
from the Consent Agenda by a timely request of any Trustee. A request is timely if 
made prior to the vote on the Consent Agenda. The request does not require a second 
Page 3 of 103
or a vote by the Board. An item removed from the Consent Agenda will then be 
discussed and acted on separately either immediately following the consideration of the 
Consent Agenda or placed later on the agenda, at the discretion of the Board.  
 
III.  Executive Session 
 
Section 1.  An executive session may only be called at a regular or special Board 
meeting where official action may be taken by the Board, not at a work session of the 
Board.  To convene an executive session, the Board shall announce to the public in the 
open meeting the topic to be discussed in the executive session, including specific 
citation to the statute authorizing the Board to meet in an executive session and 
identifying the particular matter to be discussed “in as much detail as possible without 
compromising the purpose for which the executive session is authorized.” In the event 
the Board plans to discuss more than one of the authorized topics in the executive 
session, each should be announced, cited and described. Following the announcement 
of the intent to convene an executive session, a motion must then be made and 
seconded.  In order to go into executive session, there must be the affirmative vote of 
two thirds (2/3) of Members of the Board. 
 
Section 2.  During executive session, minutes or notes of the deliberations should not 
be taken. Since meeting minutes are subject to inspection under the Colorado Open 
Records Act, the keeping of minutes would defeat the private nature of executive 
session. In addition, the deliberations carried out during executive session should not be 
discussed outside of that session or with individuals not participating in the session. 
 The contexts of an executive session are to remain confidential unless a majority of the 
Trustees vote to disclose the contents of the executive session. 
 
Section 3.  Once the deliberations have taken place in executive session, the Board 
should reconvene in regular session to take any formal action decided upon during the 
executive session.  If you have questions regarding the wording of the motion or 
whether any other information should be disclosed on the record, it is essential for you 
to consult with the Town Attorney on these matters. 
 
 
IV. Subject to Amendment 
 
Section 1. Deviations.  The Board may deviate from the procedures set forth in this 
Resolution, if, in its sole discretion, such deviation is necessary under the 
circumstances. 
Section 2.  Amendment.   The Board may amend these Rules of Procedures Policy from 
time to time.

1. New Road Base Area Next to the Stage in the Park 
When Christine Patterson asked the Board about this project during the 
last regular meeting, I was very much aware of the project, but not 
aware it had already been completed.  I did not respond because of the 
policy to not respond to public comments.  I was the one who made the 
decision to lay the gravel area beside the stage in the park. 
 
While talking to those who manage events in the Park, they were 
consistently opposed to the ban we proposed regarding parking on the 
grass next to the stage.  A few of them claimed they have been using 
that area to unload and load sound equipment, instruments, etc… for 
over 20 years and they do not understand why we want to eliminate that 
ability.  While that was probably okay before the new grass was planted, 
the reason our staff wanted to eliminate parking in that location is that 
vehicles can and do kill grass.  We talked about this situation during a 
couple recent department heads meetings. We agreed that because we 
have a large vehicle/equipment gate just to the west of the stage it is 
obvious the original plan was to allow musicians and their support 
people to unload and load in that area.  We also agreed that laying a 
Page 5 of 103
 
2 
gravel pad there would provide a much better surface than the grass.  
The placement of road base gravel which contains a small percentage of 
clay, forms a more impermeable and solid surface than just gravel.  It 
also defines the area we are willing to have people parking for 
unloading/loading. We have also discussed that next year we will have 
the time to plan a more permanent surface like concrete.  We removed 
the grass and dug down sufficiently to lay enough road base to create a 
good surface because we wanted to be ready to serve the musicians who 
will use the stage for Mountain Harvest Festival.  This was truly a small 
project because the only cost was the purchase of some road base. 
2) Raw Water Monitoring Project 
We received a project schedule from the contractor on September 11.  
Initially, they will be mobilizing equipment, tools, materials, and supplies 
to Site 1 & 2.  I am enclosing a copy of the timeline for your information. 
3) Western Loop Project 
We met with RESPEC Engineers on Friday, Sept 11 to discuss the 
proposed changes that will move the main into existing easements and 
right of way where possible.  By doing this, it will reduce the number of 
easements needed for the project.  Once we have agreement on this, we 
will be able to finalize plans acquire the easements necessary and begin 
the process of rebidding the project.  We tried to identify the easements 
we need and whether we need permanent or construction easements.  
We did not make as much progress as I hoped we would.  Because of the 
issues with putting the line in right of way, our staff started talking about 
laying the new line in the existing easements.

RE: 
Rejected Citizen Initiative Petitions and Options for the Board 
Before a petition can be printed or circulated, a petition must be reviewed and approved for form. Under 
C.R.S. § 31-11-106(1), the Clerk has five business days to approve or reject it, and must check that it only 
includes what’s required by statute. It can also be rejected if it doesn’t propose municipal legislation, per 
Article V, Section 1(9) of the Colorado Constitution. 
A petition can get turned down at the form review stage for a few reasons: it doesn’t meet the form and 
content requirements in § 31-11-106 (missing warning language or not naming the required 
representatives), format doesn’t match what the statute requires, extra material that isn’t supposed to be 
there, more than one subject (single-subject rule), or it’s not actually legislative — meaning it’s 
administrative in nature, or otherwise doesn’t qualify as proper municipal legislation. Before the Clerk 
makes a final call to accept or reject it, that decision gets discussed with the Town Attorney. 
Once a petition is rejected, it can’t be printed or circulated and any signatures collected on it wouldn’t be 
valid. In practice, if proponents want to move forward, they revise the petition using the rejection reasons 
provided in the letter and resubmit it, which starts the five-business-day clock over. They can resubmit as 
many times as needed, but each version must meet the requirements on its own. 
When a petition is rejected, the Clerk sends the petitioners a letter explaining why. Once that letter’s gone 
out, the Clerk puts together everything related to that petition: the rejection letter, the current version 
petition, and any petition related correspondence with the petitioners and sends it to the Town Board. 
Before a rejected petition lands with the Board, that petition’s process is done. But the proposed 
ordinance doesn’t just go away and what happens next depends on the Trustee’s. 
If the petition had gone all the way through — meaning it had been approved for form, collected enough 
valid signatures and met every requirement — the Clerk would bring it to the Board with two options: 
adopt it, or send it to the voters. The only way it wouldn’t take effect is if voters said no or the petitioner 
withdrew their petition. 
These petitions were rejected for form, either the single-subject problem or the legislative-versus-
administrative issue. The Board actually has more room to work with a rejected petition. Trustees can put 
the ordinance on an agenda, discuss it, and hear public comment to see if it’s something the community 
Page 31 of 103
actually wants; send it to the ballot after review by the Town Attorney; create a new ordinance or simply 
not act on it. 
There are other avenues for the community to take, before the petition process, to get something in front 
of the Board. They can ask during public comment to have an item added to the agenda or they can fill 
out a request to be on the agenda on the Town website. That gives them a chance to make their case 
directly to the Board instead of going through the petition route.

AN INITIATED ORDINANCE OF THE PEOPLE OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA, COLORADO,

PROVIDING FOR ENFORCEMENT
RECITALS
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia is a statutory municipality organized under C. R. S.§ 31- 1- 203; and
WHEREAS, C. R. S.§§ 31-15-101 and 31-15-401 grant statutory municipalities police- power
authority to protect the health, safety, and welfare of its citizens; and
WHEREAS, residents of Paonia have a fundamental right to move freely through their own town
without being surveilled, recorded, tracked, or identified by their government; and
WHEREAS, the right of the People to determine the scope of government surveillance in their
community is a fundamental policy choice reserved to the electorate; and
WHEREAS, government surveillance of public spacesintrudes upon the privacy of every resident
who walks to the post office, visits a neighbor, attends a church, or simply goes about daily life, and
such pervasive monitoring is incompatible with the character of a small, self-governing community;
and
WHEREAS, persistent video monitoring of public spaces also creates a chilling effect on the
exercise of First Amendment rights, including speech, assembly, and the right to petition the
government; and
WHEREAS, in a community of approximately 1,500 residents, government surveillance cameras
enable identification and monitoring of substantially all residents, eliminating the practical
anonymity that has historically characterized life in a small town;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE PEOPLE OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA,
COLORADO:
Section 1. Title.
This initiated ordinance shall be known and cited as the " Surveillance Camera Prohibition."
Section 2. Definitions.
For purposesof this ordinance:
a) " Surveillance camera" means any device capable of capturing, recording, storing, or transmitting
visual images of personsor activities in public spaces,whether operatedin real time or by recording, and
whether analog or digital.
b) " Public property" means any real property owned, leased, or controlled by the Town, including streets,
sidewalks, parks, buildings, facilities, and rights-of-way.
c) " Town" means the Town of Paonia and all its departments, agencies, officials, employees, and
contractors acting on behalf of the Town.
Section 3. Prohibition.
a) No installation. The Town shall not install, operate, or maintain surveillance cameras on public
property.
b) No third- party circumvention. The Town shall not contract with or authorize any third party to install,
operate, or maintain surveillance camerason public property on the Town's behalf.
c) Existing cameras.Any surveillance camerainstalled on public property asof the effective date of this
ordinance that does not fall within the exceptions in Section 4 is in violation of this ordinance.
Section 4. Exceptions.
The following are not prohibited by this ordinance:
a) Body-worn camerason law enforcement officers, governed by separatepolicy.

b) Warrant-authorized camerasusedtemporarily for a specific criminal investigation pursuantto a court
order, which shall be removed upon expiration of the order.
c) Public meeting camerasused solely for live- streaming or recording open public meetings to promote
transparency,provided such camerasare not usedfor any other purpose.
d) Camerasmonitoring unmannedcritical infrastructure facilities that arenot accessibleto the general
public, provided such camerasare directed solely at the facility and do not capture imagesof public
streets, sidewalks, or other areasaccessibleto the general public.
Section 5. Contracts Void.
Any contract, agreement, or expenditure of public funds that violates this ordinance is void and
unenforceable.
Section 6. Enforcement.
a) Any registeredelector of the Town may seekinjunctive relief in Delta County District Court to
enforce this ordinance.
b) A prevailing plaintiff shall be entitled to reasonableattorney feesand costs.

This ordinance may only be amendedor repealedby vote of the registered electors of the Town.
Section 8. Severability.
If any provision of this ordinance is held invalid by a court of competentjurisdiction, the remaining
provisions shall continue in full force and effect.
Section 9. Effective Date.
This ordinance takes effect thirty( 30) days after certification of election results.

Text Of Proposed Initiative
ORDINANCE NO. 2026-
AN INITIATED ORDINANCE OF THE PEOPLE OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA, COLORADO,
ESTABLISHING A MORATORIUM ON AUTONOMOUS ROBOTS IN PUBLIC SPACES,
PROTECTING PUBLIC SAFETY AND PRIVACY, AND PROVIDING FOR ENFORCEMENT
AND REVIEW
RECITALS
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia is a statutory municipality organized under C. R. S. § 31- 1- 203; and
WHEREAS, C. R. S. §§ 31- 15- 101 and 31- 15-401 grant statutory municipalities police- power
authority to protect the health, safety, and welfare of its citizens; and
WHEREAS, residents of Paonia have a fundamental right to move through their own town without
being surveilled, recorded, or tracked by autonomous machines they did not consent to and cannot
opt out of; and
WHEREAS, autonomous robots equipped with cameras, sensors, and wireless connectivity
operating on public sidewalksand streetsconstitutemobile surveillanceplatforms capableof
recording the movements, associations, and daily routines of every person they encounter; and
WHEREAS, in a community of approximately 1,500 residents, such technology eliminates the
practical anonymity and personalprivacy that havehistorically characterizedlife in a small,
self- governing town; and
WHEREAS, in June 2025, autonomous camera- equipped robots manufactured by Daxbot, Inc.
were deployed on Paonia streets and sidewalks without advance public notice through established
channels,without a dedicatedpublic hearing on the technology,andwithout opportunity for citizen
input on the deployment; and
WHEREAS, the robots were equipped with cameras, GPS, laser sensors, and cellular connectivity,
and the data collection capabilities, data storage and retention practices, and third- party data
sharing arrangements were not disclosed to the public before, during, or after deployment; and
WHEREAS, the robot manufacturer, Daxbot, Inc., produces both ADA assessmentrobots and
autonomous security patrol robots on a substantially similar hardware platform, and this dual- use
nature of the technology was not disclosed to the public; and
WHEREAS, no formal safety assessmentwas conducted or published prior to operating
autonomous machines on public sidewalks shared with pedestrians, children, elderly residents, and
persons with disabilities; and
WHEREAS, Colorado has no regulatory framework governing autonomous robots on public
sidewalks, as the only proposed legislation( SB 20- 092, 2020) was postponed indefinitely and never
became law; and
WHEREAS, the People of Paonia have the right to require a public process before data- collecting
autonomous technology is deployed in their community;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE PEOPLE OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA,
COLORADO:
Section 1. Title.
This initiated ordinance shall be known and cited as the " Autonomous Robot Moratorium."
Section 2. Definitions.
For purposes of this ordinance:
a) " Autonomous robot" meansany self-propelled mechanical device that operatesin a public space
without continuous direct human control and that is capableof collecting, recording, or transmitting data
about persons or their activities. This includes ground-based, aerial, and aquatic devices. It does not
includepersonalconsumerdevicessuchasremote-controlledtoysor hobbyequipmentthatlackdata
collection capabilities directed at third parties.
Petition Section 1, Page 2
Page 58 of 103
b) " Public space" means any area accessible to the general public, including streets, sidewalks, parks,
trails, alleys, and public rights-of-way.
c) " Continuous direct human control" meansreal-time operation by a human operator who directs all
movement and functions of the device throughout its operation, asdistinguished from pre-programmed or
algorithmic navigation.
Section 3. Moratorium.
a) Prohibition. During the moratorium period, no person or entity shall operatean autonomousrobot in
any public spacewithin the Town limits.
b) No fees or permits. This ordinance does not establish, require, or authorize any fees, licenses, or
permits.
c) Exception. Robots deployed by a public safety agency in active responseto a specific emergency,
under continuous direct human control by a trained operator, are exempt from this prohibition.
Section 4. Federal Compliance.
Nothing in this ordinance shall be construedto prohibit or impede the Town's compliance with the
Americans with Disabilities Act or any other federal or statelaw. The Town may fulfill any such
obligation using methods that do not require autonomousrobots.
Section 5. Moratorium Period.
a) The moratorium established by this ordinance expires on December 31, 2028.
b) Prior to expiration, the Town is encouragedto develop a regulatory framework for autonomousrobots
through a public process that includes public notice, public hearings, and opportunity for citizen
comment.
Section 6. Enforcement.
a) Violations of this ordinance constitute a civil infraction.
b) Any registered elector of the Town may report a violation to the Town.
c) Any registered elector of the Town may seekinjunctive relief in Delta County District Court to
enforce this ordinance. A prevailing plaintiff shall be entitled to reasonableattorney fees and costs.
Section 7. Amendment and Repeal.
This ordinance may only be amendedor repealedby vote of the registered electors of the Town.
Section 8. Severability.
If any provision of this ordinance is held invalid by a court of competentjurisdiction, the remaining
provisions shall continue in full force and effect.
Section 9. Effective Date.
This ordinance takes effect thirty( 30) days after certification of election results.
