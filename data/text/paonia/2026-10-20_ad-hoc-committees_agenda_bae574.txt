Attendees: Scott Brown (facilitator), Greg Sund, Sheree Fischer, Jaylene

Approval of Agenda 
Motion: Sheree moved to approve the agenda as written. Second by

Action Item #1 - Review of Previous Meeting Summary and Public 
Comment 
Committee members had no comments on the Meeting Summary. 
Regarding written public comments received, Committee member Staley 
expressed frustration over comments that continue to try to undermine the 
Committee’s work. It was noted by the facilitator, that going back to the 
Governmental Affairs and Public Safety (GAPS) report from Aug. 6, 2012, 
and consistent through the Housing Needs Assessment process, overall 
community support for regulating STRs has been demonstrated in public 
surveys and community engagement processes. So while the most recent 
comments continue to reflect a range of opinions, the Committee is on solid 
ground in its efforts to come up with reasonable regulations. 
The Committee discussed whether “vacation rental” would be a better term 
than “short-term rentals” with no agreement on making the change.  
Public comment was not taken during this portion of the meeting since this 
part of the meeting is for Committee members to reflect on what’s in writing 
and comment as they feel moved before moving on to other business.  
Action Item #2 - Review of the Ordinance Living Doc and decisions 
made at the last meeting. 
The Committee started with Section 3 (B) and worked through to Section 5, 
affirming and modifying earlier decisions. This included affirming revisions

to registration fees, including removing the $75 fee; distinguishing whole-
home, non-owner-occupied rentals from owner-occupied rentals; and 
applicable fire and building-code compliance. Public input was included in 
discussions. 
  
Action Item #3 - Continue the item-by-item discussion of the proposed 
Common Ground Ordinance with emphasis on language to retain, 
revise, remove, or add. 
The Committee worked through to Section 9. Topics of discussion included 
additional property standards: ingress and egress, onsite wastewater 
language, occupancy limits, trash and recycling, quiet hours, snow and ice 
removal, and guest information. 
The local-contact requirement was discussed, with the 30-minute response 
time removed. 
The committee supported requiring liability insurance and discussed how 
proof and certification would work. 
Notification and Enforcement (Sections 7 and 8) 
Mandatory Town notification to nearby property owners was viewed as 
costly and was replaced with an operator-provided contact process upon 
request. 
Complaint-based enforcement procedures, response periods, escalating 
penalties, registration revocation, and operation without registration were 
reviewed. 
Neighbor notification shifted away from mandatory town mailing toward an 
operator-provided contact process upon request. 
Public input was included in discussions.

Suggested approach 
At each meeting, I suggest that we focus first on policy decisions and 
then on wording. Once the committee agrees on what the ordinance should 
do, the language can be refined to reflect those decisions. 
It might also be helpful, before each meeting, to circulate only the sections 
that will be discussed and ask committee members to consider three 
questions: 
1. What do you like? 
2. What concerns you? 
3. What specific change would you propose? 
 
I think this could help us come prepared and keep the meetings focused 
and productive. A guiding principle I think could be useful throughout the 
process is: Before adding a provision, identify the specific problem it is 
intended to address and consider whether the provision is a reasonable 
and necessary way to address that problem. 
 
The goal is to have a clear, fair, proportional, and workable ordinance that 
fits Paonia. 
 
Thank you for considering this. 
 
Deborah
