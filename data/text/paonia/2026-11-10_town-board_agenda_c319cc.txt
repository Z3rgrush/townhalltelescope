Re: Agenda Memo for 11/10/2026 Regular Meeting 
F) 1)  
a) Updated Parking Lot 
During the last Work Session, the Board added Appointment of an Implementation Committee 
to the Parking Lot, I planned to add that issue to this agenda, but in talking about it with Mayor 
Fischer, we agreed it would be best to start this discussion during a Work Session, so we are 
planning to move it to the next Work Session agenda. 
b) Policy on the Use of Cameras Currently on Town Property 
I understand that when the former cameras set up around Town were ordered removed by the 
Board of Trustees, they approved the use of a different Internet-based camera system that is 
less secure.  The cameras approved were installed in some locations, but the one in the Office 
at Town Hall has never been plugged in because it looks directly at the area where the public 
and staff discuss utility and other payments.  Because it is an Internet-based system with limited 
security, Town Clerk Vetter does not feel comfortable with operating that camera.  Since the 
camera system was approved, no policy regarding their use was approved.  Staff is asking the 
Board to set policy to ensure the system is being used as the Board intends.

1) Carl E. Berg Offer to Purchase on the Former Twin Lakes Property 
As has been reported several times in recent months, the Berg Foundation is interested in 
purchasing the former Twin Lakes property to construct and establish a Dark Skies outdoor 
facility.  In recent discussions with staff, it was suggested that adding a Visitor Center to this 
property would be a plus.  Andy Thliveris said the Berg Foundation hired an appraisal firm to 
price the property.  Their offer is based on that appraisal.  He also has stated this is an offer that 
should be considered the start of communication between the Town and the Berg Foundation. 
Before for Trustees can consider this offer, they must go through a process described in State 
law regarding the sale of public property.  I typed up a short process based on State law that I 
ask the Trustees to work through in making important decisions about the property, such as 
whether it is currently a governmental use.    In discussion with Andy Thliveris, I mentioned the 
use of a small portion of the property as a yard waste collection site.  He said former Public 
Works Director Nicki Poulos assured him that use is temporary until a better site can be found.  
It is possible the Berg Foundation can help us identify that permanent location.  In addition, 
during recent discussions with staff, it was suggested the Twin Lakes site would serve as a 
good location of a Visitors Center. I visited with Andy Thliveris about this idea and he thought it 
would be a good idea.  I am not sure a project like this should be a Berg Foundation or joint 
venture project, but we can discuss this during this agenda item.  No decision is requested at 
this time 
2) Howe/Rauch Family Town Pathway License 
Public Works Director Ericka Donaldson and I met with Lyn Howe in recent days about her 
willingness to grant the Town an access to a portion of their property that serves as a trail from 
Apple Valley Park that allows hikers/bikers to park at Apple Valley Park and walk/bike a short 
distance to access the Jumbo Mountain Trails.  She originally talked about granting the Town a 
permanent easement, but something changed recently because now she is talking about 
granting the Town a short-term license that will come up for renewal in the near future.  While I 
would prefer to negotiate a permanent easement, I think it is okay to start with the proposed 
license with hopes of negotiating a permanent license at a future time.  I recommend approval. 
Suggested Motions:   
I move to accept a license from the Howe/Rauch Family for public access across their land to 
provide access to the Jumbo Mountain Trails from Apple Valley Park. 
I move to reject the license proposal from the Howe/Rauch Family and direct staff to continue 
negotiating a permanent easement across their property. 
I move to reject the offer of a license agreement from the Howe/Rauch Family.

4) Resolution 24-26 Declaring an Opening on the Board of Trustees 
With Trustee Czech’s resignation from the Board of Trustee’s late last week, the Board needs to 
either advertise the opening on the Board of Trustees for appointment in the near future or 
declare a special election to fill the position.  Because history regarding openings is to fill open 
positions by appointment, this is what Resolution 24-26 proposes.  It is important to note that 
filling this position by election could be expensive. 
Suggested Motions: 
I move to approve Resolution 24-26 which acknowledges an opening on the Board of Trustees 
and authorizes publication of the opening on the Board with the intention of a Board 
appointment in the near future to fill the position. 
I move to declare and set a special election to fill the opening on the Board of Trustees by 
election. 
5) Colorado Parks and Wildlife (CPW) Conservation Easement 
Last year, the Town learned that CPW purchased a conservation easement on the Roeber 
Property in the past that includes the land the Town purchased from the Roeber’s a few years 
ago on which we placed the temporary water storage tank.  At the time, the Town probably 
should have compensated CPW for loss of acreage from their conservation easement, but I 
don’t think the Town knew about the easement.  Our attorney, Clay Buchner claims that Town 
does not owe CPW anything for taking over a small portion of their conservation easement 
because the Town is able to show we were using the property in question many years ago when 
it constructed a backwash tank on the property in the 1940’s or 1950’s.  The challenge is the 
remains of that structure was buried when it was no longer needed and knowledge of it was not 
commonly known.  Because CPW purchased the conservation easement, including the property 
in question, the easiest solution to this issue is to compensate CPW for the conservation 
easement they have lost because if the issue had been known when we purchased it, we would 
likely have paid for the loss of conservation easement acreage.  The letter from CPA requesting 
reimbursement of the loss of conservation easement acreage and staff time required to 
research this issue is enclosed. 
Suggested Motions:

easement acreage resulting from the Town’s purchase of land on which to set a new water tank. 
6) Storm Sewer/Sanitary Sewer Repair Project 
We recently learned that it was likely a residential sanitary sewer line was connected to our 
storm sewer system.  We sought quotes from local contractors to dig down to the line to verify 
whether our understanding was correct and if so, disconnect the line from the storm sewer and 
connect it to the sanitary sewer system.  However, when we dug down to the line and confirmed 
that our concern was correct, we learned the storm sewer line is almost totally plugged with 
roots and now the storm sewer main must be dug up and replaced to re-establish flow.  
Because we found this connection of a sanitary service to the storm sewer system, we are 
concerned there may be other connections on the alley in question.  Hence this project has 
grown, and we need Board approval to continue.  We proposed contracting with the contractor 
who started the work on this repair.  His amended estimate of cost is enclosed. If the Board 
disagrees with this proposal, we will have to fill the excavation back in without fixing the issues 
we have identified. 
Suggested Motions: 
I move to approve the amended estimate from Roop Construction to continue work on the alley 
in question to upgrade the storm sewer line from 8” diameter to 12” diameter thereby removing 
the portion of the line that is compromised with roots and correcting any additional residential 
connections to the storm sewer along the alley in question. 
I move that all work on the storm sewer in the alley be discontinued and the excavation be filled 
back in.

Real Estate Sales Guidelines 
1. According to CRS 31-15-715, the Board of Trustees must first determine whether 
real estate considered for sale is waterworks, ditches, gasworks, geothermal 
systems, solar systems, electric light works, or other public utilities, public 
buildings, real property used or held for park purposes, or any other real property 
used or held for any governmental purpose. 
If the answer is yes, before such sale is made, the question of the sale and the terms 
and consideration thereof shall be submitted to the voters at a regular or special 
election and approved in the manner provided for authorization of bonded 
indebtedness by section 31-15-302 (1)(d) which refers to a majority of the registered 
electors voting upon the question vote in favor of creating such debt. 
If the answer is no, go to #2. 
2. The Town may sell said real estate by ordinance on terms and conditions 
determined by the governing body at a regular or special meeting. 
3. Colorado law does not require that real estate be sold to the highest bidder, be 
publicly advertised, or appraised before sale. Exceptions:  
 
• The municipality’s own ordinances may have requirements different from 
State Law. 
• Conditions of restrictions contained in the deed by which the municipality 
acquired the property may affect how it is sold. 
• Grants or funding used to acquire it may affect how it can be sold. 
• Park/open space restrictions 
• Constitutional limitations concerning governmental expenditures or gifts; or  
• Other statutes applicable to the particular property. 
4. Conveyance. CRS 38-30-41 describes that the Town Board can authorize 
conveyance of municipal real estate and provides mechanisms for execution of the 
deed.  The Mayor and clerk can execute conveyance where authorized by ordinance 
or a vote of the people.

A RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA, COLORADO, 
DECLARING A VACANCY ON THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia (the 'Town") is a statutory town in Delta County, Colorado; 
WHEREAS, with respect to a vacancy on the Board of Trustees, C.R.S. § 31-4-303 provides 
in part: 
“The board of trustees has the power, by appointment, to fill all vacancies in the board or 
any other office, and the person so appointed shall hold his office until the next regular 
election and until his successor is elected and has complied with section 31-4-401. The 
board also has the power to fill a vacancy in the board or in any other elective office of the 
town by ordering an election to fill the vacancy until the next regular election and until a 
successor has been elected and has complied with section 31-4-401. If a vacancy in the 
board or in such other elective office is not filled by appointment or an election is not 
ordered within sixty days after the vacancy occurs. the board shall order an election, 
subject to the municipal election code, to be held as soon as practicable to fill the vacancy 
until the next regular election and until a successor has been elected and has complied with 
section 31-4-401....” 
(emphasis added): 
 
WHEREAS, Section 2-2-10(e) of the Town of Paonia Municipal Code (the "Code") provides, 
in part, that "The Board of Trustees shall have power, by appointment, to fill all vacancies 
in the Board of Trustees or in any other elected office, and the person so appointed shall 
hold office until the next regular election and until his or her successor is elected and 
qualified"; 
WHEREAS, on October 31, 2026, Walter Czech resigned their Trustee seat  for personal 
reasons; 
WHEREAS, accordingly, the Board of Trustees wishes to declare a vacancy on the Board of 
Trustees, as set forth herein. 
NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF 
PAONIA, COLORADO:
