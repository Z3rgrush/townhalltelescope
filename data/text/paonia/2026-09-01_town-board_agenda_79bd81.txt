I. Rules of Procedure  
Section 1.  Schedule of Meetings.  Regular Board of Trustees meetings shall be held on the 
second and fourth Tuesdays of each month, except on legal holidays, or as re-scheduled or 
amended and posted on the agenda prior to the scheduled meeting. 
 
Section 2.  Officiating Officer.  The meetings of the Board of Trustees shall be conducted by the 
Mayor or, in the Mayor’s absence, the Mayor Pro-Tem.  The Town Clerk or a designee of the 
Board shall record the minutes of the meetings. 
Section 3.  Time of Meetings.  Regular meetings of the Board of Trustees shall begin at 6:30 
p.m. or as scheduled and posted on the agenda. Board Members shall be called to order by the 
Mayor.  The meetings shall open with the presiding officer leading the Board in the Pledge of 
Allegiance.  The Town Clerk shall then proceed to call the roll, note the absences and 
announce whether a quorum is present.  Regular Meetings are scheduled for three hours, and 
shall be adjourned at 9:30 p.m., unless a majority of the Board votes in the affirmative to extend 
the meeting, by a specific amount of time.  
Section 4. Schedule of Business.  If a quorum is present, the Board of Trustees shall proceed 
with the business before it, which shall be conducted in the following manner.  Note that all 
provided times are estimated:  
     (a) Roll Call - (5 minutes) 
    (b) Approval of Agenda - (5 minutes) 
    (c) Announcements (5 minutes) 
    (d) Recognition of Visitors and Guests (10 minutes) 
    (e) Consent Agenda including Approval of Prior Meeting Minutes (10 
minutes) 
    (f) Mayor’s Report (10 minutes) 
    (g) Staff Reports: (15 minutes) 
        (1) Town Administrator’s Report 
        (2) Public Works Reports  
        (3) Police Report 
        (4) Treasurer Report 
                  
    (h) Unfinished Business (45 minutes) 
    (i) New Business (45 minutes) 
    (j) Disbursements (15 minutes) 
    (k) Committee Reports (15 minutes) 
    (l) Adjournment 
* This schedule of business is subject to change and amendment.  
Section 5. Priority and Order of Business. Questions relative to the priority of business 
and order shall be decided by the Mayor without debate, subject in all cases to an 
appeal to the Board of Trustees.  
 
Section 6.  Conduct of Board Members.   Town Board Members shall treat other Board 
Members and the public in a civil and polite manner and shall comply with the 
Standards of Conduct for Elected Officials of the Town.  Board Members shall address 
Town Staff and the Mayor by his/her title, other Board Members by the title of Trustee 
or the appropriate honorific (i.e.: Mr., Mrs. or Ms.), and members of the public by the 
appropriate honorific.  Subject to the Mayor’s discretion, Board Members shall be 
limited to speaking two times when debating an item on the agenda.  Making a motion, 
Page 2 of 43
asking a question or making a suggestion are not counted as speaking in a debate.  
 
Section 7. Presentations to the Board.  Items on the agenda presented by individuals, 
businesses or other organizations shall be given up to 5 minutes to make a 
presentation.  On certain issues, presenters may be given more time, as determined by 
the Mayor and Town Staff.  After the presentation, Trustees shall be given the 
opportunity to ask questions. 
 
Section 8. Public Comment.  After discussion of an agenda item by the Board of 
Trustees has concluded, the Mayor shall open the floor for comment from members of 
the public, who shall be allowed the opportunity to comment or ask questions on the 
agenda item.  Each member of the public wishing to address the Town Board shall be 
recognized by the presiding officer before speaking.  Members of the public shall speak 
from the podium, stating their name, the address of their residence and any group they 
are representing prior to making comment or asking a question.  Comments shall be 
directed to the Mayor or presiding officer, not to an individual Trustee or Town 
employee.  Comments or questions should be confined to the agenda item or issue(s) 
under discussion.  The speaker should offer factual information and refrain from 
obscene language and personal attacks. 
 
Section 9.  Unacceptable Behavior. Disruptive behavior shall result in expulsion from 
the meeting.   
 
Section 10.  Posting of Rules of Procedure for Paonia Board of Trustees Meetings. 
These rules of procedure shall be provided in the Town Hall meeting room for each 
Board of Trustees meeting so that all attendees know how the meeting will be 
conducted. 
 
II. Consent Agenda 
 
Section 1. Use of Consent Agenda. The Mayor, working with Town Staff, shall place 
items on the Consent Agenda. By using a Consent Agenda, the Board has consented 
to the consideration of certain items as a group under one motion. Should a Consent 
Agenda be used at a meeting, an appropriate amount of discussion time will be allowed 
to review any item upon request.  
Section 2. General Guidelines. Items for consent are those which usually do not require 
discussion or explanation prior to action by the Board, are non-controversial and/or 
similar in content, or are those items which have already been discussed or explained 
and do not require further discussion or explanation. Such agenda items may include 
ministerial tasks such as, but not limited to, approval of previous meeting minutes, 
approval of staff reports, addressing routine correspondence, approval of liquor 
licenses renewals and approval or extension of other Town licenses. Minor changes in 
the minutes such as non-material Scribner errors may be made without removing the 
minutes from the Consent Agenda.  Should any Trustee feel there is a material error in 
the minutes, they should request the minutes be removed from the Consent Agenda for 
Board discussion. 
Section 3. Removal of Item from Consent Agenda. One or more items may be removed 
from the Consent Agenda by a timely request of any Trustee. A request is timely if 
made prior to the vote on the Consent Agenda. The request does not require a second 
or a vote by the Board. An item removed from the Consent Agenda will then be 
discussed and acted on separately either immediately following the consideration of the 
Page 3 of 43
Consent Agenda or placed later on the agenda, at the discretion of the Board.  
 
III.  Executive Session 
 
Section 1.  An executive session may only be called at a regular or special Board 
meeting where official action may be taken by the Board, not at a work session of the 
Board.  To convene an executive session, the Board shall announce to the public in the 
open meeting the topic to be discussed in the executive session, including specific 
citation to the statute authorizing the Board to meet in an executive session and 
identifying the particular matter to be discussed “in as much detail as possible without 
compromising the purpose for which the executive session is authorized.” In the event 
the Board plans to discuss more than one of the authorized topics in the executive 
session, each should be announced, cited and described. Following the announcement 
of the intent to convene an executive session, a motion must then be made and 
seconded.  In order to go into executive session, there must be the affirmative vote of 
two thirds (2/3) of Members of the Board. 
 
Section 2.  During executive session, minutes or notes of the deliberations should not 
be taken. Since meeting minutes are subject to inspection under the Colorado Open 
Records Act, the keeping of minutes would defeat the private nature of executive 
session. In addition, the deliberations carried out during executive session should not 
be discussed outside of that session or with individuals not participating in the 
session.  The contexts of an executive session are to remain confidential unless a 
majority of the Trustees vote to disclose the contents of the executive session. 
 
Section 3.  Once the deliberations have taken place in executive session, the Board 
should reconvene in regular session to take any formal action decided upon during the 
executive session.  If you have questions regarding the wording of the motion or 
whether any other information should be disclosed on the record, it is essential for you 
to consult with the Town Attorney on these matters. 
 
 
IV. Subject to Amendment 
 
Section 1. Deviations.  The Board may deviate from the procedures set forth in this 
Resolution, if, in its sole discretion, such deviation is necessary under the 
circumstances. 
Section 2.  Amendment.   The Board may amend these Rules of Procedures Policy 
from time to time.

1) Solar Panel Presentation 
In order to do the roof replacement project, we must first have the 
solar panels on the roof removed.  The company that installed them 
originally, Empower, is presenting two potential ways of addressing 
our need.  Firstly, they can remove them and replace them after the 
roof is replaced or they can remove them and upgrade them to new 
panels that are considerably more efficient and may actually meet 
the power needs of Town Hall.

We have invited Leslie Klusmire to address the Trustees to provide a 
little history about the recodification process and the work she needs 
to do to finish the project.  She also plans to promote a future 
workshop for the Board during which she will seek considerable 
input from the Trustees about updates you want in the Town Code.  
We learned recently the DOLA grant the Town received to do the 
recodification expires on 1/31/2027.

3) Ordinances 
We have a list of ordinances we ask the Board to discuss and provide 
input before they are presented during a regular meeting. 
a) Estate Zoning Change 
As presented during the last regular meeting, the Planning 
Commission reviewed Table 16-2 during its last meeting.  In 
reviewing this table staff realized there is likely a typo on the 
table because although it provides guidance for the first home in 
an Estate Zone, to provides no guidance for the 2nd, 3rd, 4th 
houses, etc…  The Planning Commission recommends keeping 
the spacing for houses in this zone at three acres, but since the 
Planning commission meeting the staff has been discussing this 
issue and in consideration of the new comprehensive plan which 
promotes greater density, we recommend changing the 
increment for each added house of 1 ½ acres. 
b) Sidewalk Business Use 
During the last regular meeting, Trustee Brown reported that he 
did a little research and found that in a typical year, the Town 
collects about $2,500 in permit fees for setting items on the 
sidewalk.  Because of the rather small amount of revenue the 
program produces, he recommended eliminating the fee.  I typed 
up an ordinance the does this.  It is very simple because it just 
repeals the subsection of code that describes the permit fee. 
c) Sanitation and Wildlife 
As reported during the last regular meeting, I have been working 
with Trustees Ostrander and Foster developing a new ordinance 
to address ways to discourage bears from getting into garbage 
receptacles.  This ordinance is written to provide guidance on 
making receptacles more resistant and provides a fine structure 
for people who refuse to comply.  The goal is to frustrate the 
bears enough that they seek food out of town.  It is important to 
note that the goal is to make receptacles more resistant to bears.  
The goal is not make them bear proof because they are very 
strong animals that will figure out a way to break into receptacles 
if they truly want to.  However, when the bear resistant program 
has been adopted in other towns, it has been successful.

Agenda Item:  Consideration of changes to Estate Zoning, Table 16-2, Ordinance 
2026-01 
Submitted By:  Daoine Bachran, Community Development Director 
Date:  8-25-2026 
Background:  
  
The Estate Residential zoning tables have an inconsistency that makes 
applying the rules to building requests difficult.  
In Table 16-2, there is a section that needs additional information or 
to be struck . Currently, for each dwelling that is permitted by right, 
there is a minimum lot area  listed except for E-1 zoning, which has 3 
acres required for 1 dwelling, but no listed acreage for 2 dwellings. 
The Planning Commission’s discussion about the issue focused on two 
major points. One was the purpose of the Estate Residential District, 
including in comparison to the 2025 Comprehensive Plan’s  Urban 
Farm. The two zoning districts match well and seem to have similar 
goals of providing an open space for farming and limited livestock 
ranching. 
The second point focused on how much acreage per residence should 
be required for the district. The Commission decided on 3 acres per 
residence and recommended filling in the blank  after 2 
residences with 6 acres.  There was discussion about whether the 
number of houses should go up by 3 acres (i.e. 1 house = 3 acres min., 
2 houses = 6 acres min.), or in another increment as other residential 
zones do. Having lower acreage per residence was also considered – 
mainly 1.5 or 2 acres per dwelling.  
One commissioner pointed to §16-2-20’s definition of Estate 
Residential District  that includes language that strongly suggests 3 
acres per house: 
“It is the intent of this District to provide for orderly development of 
single-family residential areas on three- to ten-acre lots.”

The description of the Estate Residential District also provides a lot 
size range that is not compatible with scaling the number of dwellings 
allowed by 3, so the Board might consider changing the largest lot size 
to be a number divisible by 3 (9 or 12). 
Budget:   No cost or budget items are associated with this item. 
Recommendation: After discussion, staff has changed their initial, neutral 
recommendation that was presented to the Planning Commission. 
Because the 2025 Comprehensive Plan focuses keenly on increasing 
dwelling density, staff recommends considering a lower density 
requirement per lot and per house – 1.5 or 2 acres – and changing 
the lot requirements listed in §16-2-20 to read, “It is the intent of this 
District to provide for orderly development of single-family 
residential areas on three one and a half [or two]- to ten nine-acre 
lots,” or “two to ten acre lots.” 
This would still require lots of approximately 65,300 ft2 to 87,100 ft2, 
providing room for some farming or farm animals, ample room for a 
septic system per house if needed, and still exceed the other least-
dense zoning district’s single residence land requirement more than 
tenfold (R-1 at 6,000 ft2).

§16-2-20 to provide for [X] acres per dwelling, with a minimum lot

removing the “2” from dwelling units permitted by right for the E-1

AN ORDINANCE ENACTING SOLID WASTE DISPOSAL RULES FOR RESIDENTS AND 
BUSINESSES TO ENSURE SOLID WASTE RECEPTACLES ARE WILDLIFE RESISTANT 
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia has been experiencing bears entering Town during the 
summer of 2026 due primarily to the extreme drought that has reduced the availability of 
their typical food in the mountains, and 
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia sees the need to be proactive in addressing solid waste 
rules and practices to discourage bears and other wildlife from entering Town for easy 
meals from solid waste dumpsters and polycarts, and 
WHEREAS, the Town of Paonia has provided free solutions to secure cans and other 
affordable solutions exist which can be taken by residents by residents according to 
Colorado Parks and Wildlife, 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA, 
COLORADO, TO CREATE Section 13-6-65 AS FOLLOWS: 
Section 1. 
Section 13-6-65 (1) Wildlife Resistant Solid Waste Container definition 
Wildlife Resistant Solid Waste Container means, at a minimum , a heavy duty polycart, that 
has locks installed by the Town or the resident under Town guidance, a releasable strapping 
system, or a wildlife resistant polycart provided by the Town or a fully enclosed metal 
container (dumpster) with an attached, lockable metal lid. 
Section 13-6-65 (2) Non-dumpster Container Standards 
The use of polycarts as solid waste containers is hereby mandated for all residential 
premises.  Polycarts shall not exceed ninety-five (95) gallons in capacity.  Polycarts when 
used as wildlife resistant solid waste containers must meet the requirements of Section 
13-6-65 (1) and shall be maintained by the resident in clean condition by the owner or the 
occupant  of the premises served.  Polycarts shall have the street address and unit number 
if applicable of each entity using the polycart permanently affixed to the container in legible 
white letters two inches in height.  The Town Administrator or his/her designee may grant an 
exemption to this standard in extenuating circumstances. 
Section 13-6-65 (3) Use of polycarts 
Polycarts cannot be put out for collection before 6am during the morning of scheduled 
pickup and must be removed by 7pm that same day.

If a polycart is stored in an enclosed area or a garage between collections, there is no need 
to secure them. 
As long as a polycart is empty, there is no need to lock them. 
Overfilling a polycart so the cover is unable to close is a violation.  
Section 13-6-65 (4) Bear Resistant Application Deadline 
All polycarts in Paonia must be secured while they last with free locks provided by the 
Town, customer purchased bear resistant straps, or a new bear resistant polycart provided 
by the Town no later than July 1, 2027. Personally purchased and installed strapping must 
be approved by the Town Administrator or his/her designee. 
Section 13-6-65 (5) Bear Resistant Dumpsters Deadline 
The Town will upgrade all existing dumpsters in Town or install bear resistant straps or 
upgrade them to bear resistant dumpsters no later than January 1, 2029.

Owners or occupants of premises who fail to comply with this code will be subject to the

First Offense: Warning 
Second Offense in One Season: $200 
Third Offense in One Season: Statutory limit

Sec. 10-6-50. Unlawful possession of tobacco/nicotine products by persons under the age of 
twenty-one. 
(a) No minor person under the age of twenty-one years shall possess any tobacco/nicotine product within the

Sec. 10-6-70. Juvenile loitering.

avenue, highway, road, sidewalk, curb, gutter, parking lot, alley, vacant lot, park, playground, yard, building, 
place of amusement or eating place, whether public or private, without the consent or permission of the

for a designated period of time and purpose within a specified area.  
(b) It shall be unlawful for the parent, guardian or other adult person having the care and custody of a juvenile 
under the age of eighteen (18) years to knowingly permit or allow such juvenile to loiter at the places and 
within the time prohibited by Subsection (a) above. The term knowingly includes knowledge which a parent 
or guardian should be reasonably expected to have concerning the whereabouts of a juvenile in that parent 
or guardian's custody. It shall be no defense that a parent, guardian or other person having care and custody 
of the juvenile was indifferent to the activities, conduct or whereabouts of such juvenile.
