Mayor Underwood explained the public comment process, including the available methods for submitting

Claire Noble, an Edwards resident, addressed Council in person regarding her candidacy for Eagle County 
Coroner in the November 3, 2026, election. 
Nicholas Shirley, an Avon resident, addressed Council in person regarding pedestrian safety at the Avon 
Road crosswalks near the intersection with Highway 6. He initially stated that his father had been struck by 
a vehicle and died, then clarified that the statement was a joke intended to draw attention to a recent 
incident in which a motorist nearly struck his father while he was using the south crosswalk on Avon Road, 
outside Avon Town boundaries. He attributed the safety concern to the absence of flashing pedestrian 
beacons at the Avon Road intersections near Highway 6, noting that similar beacons were installed along 
Highway 6 near Avon Road. He stated that he had discussed the matter with a Town engineer to better 
understand why beacons had not been installed at those crosswalks. He requested the installation of 
flashing pedestrian beacons on both sides of Avon Road near Highway 6, and encouraged the Town to 
coordinate with Vail Resorts and Eagle County regarding the south intersection, which is located in 
unincorporated Eagle County.

Kathy Brendza, an Eagle County resident, addressed Council in person regarding her candidacy for Eagle 
County Commissioner in the November 3, 2026, election. She encouraged interested community members 
to contact her if they wished to display a campaign sign on private property. 
Tim McMahon, an Avon resident, addressed Council in person regarding the Town's automated license 
plate reader (ALPR) program. He read into the record a list of communities that he stated had ended 
contracts with Flock Safety, as well as other communities that had discontinued ALPR programs. He noted 
that this was the fifth Council meeting at which he had provided comments on the issue and stated that a

Margaret Sartwelle, an Avon resident, addressed Council in person regarding the cottonwood groves that 
could be affected by future development of Lot B (The Summit). She asked whether the trees are located 
on private property and encouraged Council to take steps to protect them. 
Maria DeSimone, an Avon resident, addressed Council virtually regarding the Town's automated license 
plate reader (ALPR) program. She expressed opposition to the use of Flock cameras and requested 
answers to questions before Council's October 6, 2026 discussion of the program, including questions 
regarding the status of the Town's contract, installation of an additional camera, and the authority under 
which the program is operating. Ms. DeSimone also questioned what additional information was needed 
for Council to take action on the program, referenced a petition she stated had received approximately 
1,900 signatures, expressed concerns regarding Council's responsiveness to public feedback, and urged 
Council to discontinue the Flock camera program. 
Ian Grask, an Avon resident, addressed Council virtually regarding the Town's automated license plate 
reader (ALPR) program. He expressed opposition to the use of Flock cameras and urged Council to 
discontinue the program. Mr. Grask stated that he shared the concerns previously raised by other 
community members regarding the cameras. He also referenced a proposed Colorado West electric rail 
project and asked whether service could potentially be extended to Avon. 
Sara Bahr, an Avon resident, addressed Council in person regarding the Town's automated license plate 
reader (ALPR) program. She expressed opposition to the use of Flock and Axon cameras, citing privacy 
concerns and stating that she believes the technology has been misused in other jurisdictions. Ms. Bahr 
referenced an incident involving a law enforcement officer's misuse of ALPR camera technology elsewhere 
to track an ex -girlfriend, and urged Council to discontinue the ALPR program, stating that she believes the 
use of such systems is inappropriate and creates security concerns for members of the public. 
No additional public comment was received, either in person or virtually. 
Following Public Comment, Mayor Underwood asked Town Manager Eric Heil to respond to questions 
raised regarding the cottonwood trees within Lot B (The Summit). Mr. Heil stated that the trees are expected 
to be removed as part of future redevelopment activities and associated site excavation. He noted that, 
while the trees are valued, their removal is anticipated as part of the project.

6.7. ORDINANCE 26.10, First Reading: Enacting Avon Municipal Code Section 9.08.110 Deceptive 
Business Practices (Town Attorney Nina Williams) 
Video Start Time: 01:14:55 
Town Manager Eric Heil and Town Attorney Nina Williams presented first reading of Ordinance No. 
26-10, which would enact Section 9.08.110 of the Avon Municipal Code to establish the offense of 
deceptive business practices. Town Attorney Williams explained that the proposed ordinance would 
prohibit specified deceptive or misleading business practices and provide additional consumer 
protections for residents, visitors, businesses, and property owners. She also stated that related 
amendments to the Town's private parking lot licensing regulations would identify violations of the 
proposed section as grounds for license revocation. 
In response to Council questions, Town Attorney Williams explained the practices prohibited by the 
ordinance, applicable penalties, treatment of repeat offenses, and application of the ordinance to 
entities and organizations. 
Mayor Underwood opened the public hearing. No public comment was received in person or virtually. 
Mayor Underwood commented that it was unfortunate the ordinance arose from issues involving 
private parking regulations but expressed satisfaction that the matter had concluded. 
Councilor Thuon moved to approve Ordinance No. 26-10 on first reading and set a public hearing 
for second reading on October 6, 2026. Mayor Pro Tern Carroll seconded the motion, and it passed 
unanimously by a 4-0 vote.
