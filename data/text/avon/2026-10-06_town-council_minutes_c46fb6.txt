6.1. RESOLUTION 26-15: Support Eagle County School District Ballot Issue 5B (Chief Administrative 
Officer Ineke de Jong) 
Video Start Time: 00:15:05 
Chief Administrative Officer Ineke de Jong presented Resolution No, 26-15 expressing support for 
Eagle County School District Ballot Issue 5B, a proposed mill levy override to provide additional 
funding for teacher and staff retention, academic programs, mental health services, and educational 
opportunities. She was joined by Wendy Rimel, Lilia Sage DeMuth, and School Superintendent Dr. 
Casey Summers, who provided additional information regarding the ballot measure. During public 
comment, Avon Elementary School Principal Harrison; Gloria Conlon, President of the Eagle County 
School Board; Jennifer Lockman, a local parent of a Battle Mountain student and teacher; Tessa 
Kirchner, an Eagle County resident; Tim McMahon, Avon resident; and Gwen Chamberlin, an Eagle 
County teacher, spoke in support of the measure or regarding the importance of teacher 
compensation, school funding, and educational needs. Greg Cooper, an Avon resident, spoke 
against supporting the ballot issue and the associated tax increase while expressing support for 
increased teacher compensation. No additional public comment was received. Following Council 
discussion, Councilor Hyatt moved to approve Resolution No. 26-15. Councilor Stanley seconded 
the motion, which passed unanimously, 7-0.

6.4. WORK SESSION: Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) Cameras Comprehensive Overview 
(Chief of Police Greg Daly and Deputy Chief of Police Coby Cosper) 
Video Start Time: 00:53:50 
Chief of Police Greg Daly and Deputy Chief Coby Cosper presented a comprehensive overview of 
the Town's Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) program. Staff reviewed the history of the 
program, operational practices, documented investigative uses, privacy and data -sharing 
considerations, legal and legislative developments, policy options, and potential safeguards for 
Council consideration. Beatriz Campo, a representative of Flock Safety, provided additional 
information regarding system capabilities, privacy controls, cybersecurity measures, contractual 
protections, and operational practices. Staff also distinguished ALPR cameras from automated 
speed -enforcement cameras and reviewed camera locations, data retention and sharing, 
investigative uses, costs, and options for the future use and governance of ALPR technology in Avon. 
Mayor Underwood opened the floor to public comment. Tim McMahon (Avon), Scott Tashman (Eagle 
County), Nicholas Shirley (Avon), Tyler Slater (Eagle County), Maria DeSimone (Avon), Ellen Friedle 
(Eagle County), Charles Balocca (Eagle County), Nancy Tashman (Avon), Seth Johnson (Eagle 
County), Ryan Amos (Avon), Jacob Walters (Avon), Charlie Sherwood (Avon), Donna Arenschield 
(Avon), Jennifer Lance (Eagle County), Kelli Rohrig (Avon), Jason Lance (Eagle County), Gwen 
Chamberlin (Vail), Paula Canepa (Minturn), Ian Grask (Avon, virtual), Rick Sudekum (Avon, virtual), 
and Blair Kjorvestad (Avon, virtual) spoke against continued use of ALPR cameras in Avon. No other 
public comment was received. 
Council discussed the investigative benefits of ALPR technology and concerns regarding privacy, 
data security, data sharing, artificial intelligence, federal agency access, warrants, false alerts, and 
the Town's ability to control or restrict data. Councilors Stanley, Hyatt, Hardy, and Brooks supported 
discontinuing the ALPR program, while Councilor Thuon, Mayor Pro Tern Carroll, and Mayor 
Underwood supported retaining the technology with additional safeguards or restrictions. By a 4-3 
majority, Council directed staff to discontinue the Flock ALPR program and proceed with 
decommissioning and removal of the cameras. 
Council recessed at 8:53 p.m. and reconvened at 9:00 p.m. Councilor Hardy did not rejoin the meeting 
following the recess.

6.6. PUBLIC HEARING: ORDINANCE 26.10, Second Reading: Enacting Avon Municipal Code 
Section 9.08.110 Deceptive Business Practices (Town Manager Eric Heil) 
Video Start Time: 04:03:10 
Town Manager Eric Heil presented Ordinance No, 26-10 on second reading, which would create a 
new offense of deceptive business practices within the Avon Municipal Code. Staff explained that 
the ordinance is intended to provide additional protections for residents, visitors, businesses, and 
property owners by prohibiting deceptive, misleading, or fraudulent business conduct within the 
Town. Mayor Underwood opened the public hearing, and no public comment was received, either in 
person or virtually. Councilor Thuon moved to approve Ordinance No. 26-10 on second reading. 
Councilor Hyatt seconded the motion, which passed unanimously by those present, 6-0. 
6.7. ORDINANCE 26-12, First Reading: Amending Avon Municipal Code Chapter 5.14 — Private Parking 
Lots (Community Development Director Matt Pielsticker) 
Video Start Time: 04:05:35 
Community Development Director Matt Pielsticker presented Ordinance No. 26-12 on first reading, 
amending Chapter 5,14 of the Avon Municipal Code governing private parking lots. Staff reviewed 
proposed revisions addressing applicability, signage requirements, operating standards, notice 
requirements, and enforcement provisions intended to improve consumer protections and provide 
clearer operational standards for private parking lot operators within Avon. Mayor Underwood 
opened the floor to public comment, and Ian Grask, an Avon resident, spoke in favor. No other public 
comment was received. Council discussed the proposed regulations and amendments concerning 
parking registration, vehicle stacking, the definition of "patron," references to tenants, mechanical 
devices, and organization of the applicable provisions. Councilor Thuon moved to approve 
Ordinance No. 26-12 on first reading as amended and to schedule second reading for November 10, 
2026. Councilor Brooks seconded the motion, which passed unanimously by those present, 6-0. 
6.8. ACTION: Notice of Award Recreation Center Aquatics Renovations - Construction 
Management/General Contractor (Senior Engineer Keith Ferrell) 
Video Start Time: 04:14:00 
Senior Engineer Keith Ferrell presented a recommendation to award preconstruction Construction 
Manager/General Contractor services for the Avon Recreation Center Aquatics Renovation Project 
to Catamount Pinkard LLC. Staff reviewed the project history, proposed renovation scope, CMGC 
delivery method, anticipated schedule, expenditures and budget status, and next steps toward final 
design and development of a future guaranteed maximum price proposal. Council discussed the 
proposed project schedule, contractor selection, contractual performance provisions, project costs, 
and potential pricing of an expansion to the lap pool, Mayor Underwood opened the floor to public 
comment, and Ian Grask, Avon resident, spoke in favor of the proposed award. No other public 
comment was received. Councilor Stanley moved to approve the Notice of Award for Preconstruction 
Services for the Recreation Center Aquatics Renovation Project to Catamount Pinkard LLC, subject 
to approval by the Town Attorney's Office. Councilor Hyatt seconded the motion, which passed 
unanimously by those present, 6-0.
