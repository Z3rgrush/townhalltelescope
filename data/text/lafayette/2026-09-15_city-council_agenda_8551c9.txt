J. Potential Moratoria on Data Centers and Advanced Air Mobility Land Uses 
Data Centers 
Data centers store and process large amounts of digital information, often requiring significant 
amounts of electricity, water, land, and cooling infrastructure. As more data centers are 
proposed, communities are considering their impacts on utilities, natural resources, land use, 
public services, and community character. Key concerns include electric grid capacity, water 
use, backup power, traffic, noise, heat, air emissions, and the overall intensity of these facilities. 
Erin Poe presented context for data centers in Colorado, the benefits of a moratorium, and 
Planning Commission and staff recommendations. 
Council asked whether the Sustainability and Resilience Advisory Board (SRAB) had discussed 
data centers, about the maximum acceptable length for a moratorium, if the City had been 
approached for data center development, if the City currently had structures or businesses that 
qualify as data centers, and about definitions of a data center. 
Advanced Air Mobility (AAM) 
AAM is an emerging form of transportation that uses new aircraft, including electric vertical 
take-off and landing (eVTOL) vehicles, to move people and goods within urban and regional 
environments. These aircraft include air taxis, small passenger vehicles, and delivery drones. As 
AAM expands, states and municipalities are planning for dedicated launch and landing sites, 
known as “vertiports,” which may be located at existing airports and heliports or developed as 
entirely new stand-alone facilities.
