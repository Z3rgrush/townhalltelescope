LaMont Does Park and Great Outdoors Water Park 
LaMont Does Park is a community park that provides space for many leisure activities and organized 
sports and is home to the Great Outdoors Water Park. The park underwent major improvements following 
voter approval of a $5.52 million bond in 2014, largely to construct the Great Outdoors Water Park which 
features several amenities including the original lap pool, and a new lazy river, splash play zone for infants 
and toddlers, and a 21-foot double flume slide.  
The aquatics portion of LaMont Does Park includes 3.34 acres on the northwest corner of the 13.36-acre 
property. The existing LaMont Does pool is a six-lane lap pool which opened in 1975. No improvements 
have been made to the lap pool since its initial construction. Now more than 50 years old, it requires 
significant repairs and will ultimately require a full replacement.  
Project Expenses 
2026 Revised Budget $207,350 (including a fence installation and shade structure) 
2027 Recommended Budget $115,000 (bathhouse flooring and concrete assessment and repair) 
Future Anticipated Expenses 
Various long-term capital needs over the next 5+ years have been 
identified, including but not limited to:  
• Lap pool replacement ($2.8M) 
• Permanent shade structures ($150K) 
• North bathhouse upgrades ($300K) 
• Park irrigation replacement ($250K) 
• Ballfield lighting conversion ($200K) 
• Playground replacement ($1.1M) 
• Pump and boiler replacement ($300K) 
 
All costs are estimates and subject to change.

Service Center 
In November of 2025, Lafayette voters approved general obligation debt to fund the renovation and 
expansion of the Service Center as part of a larger package with the renovation/expansion of the Bob L. 
Burger Recreation Center and construction of a new Civic Center in Old Town. Since its passage, the City 
has engaged a design-build team led by Buildings by Design (“BBD”) to refine the program from initial 
concepts presented during the exploration effort last year. This program refinement process is nearly 
complete, and the conceptual design includes a new fleet facility south of the current building and 
renovation of much of the existing Service Center to accommodate improved and expanded office space, 
training space, and the relocation of the Facilities and Open Space teams to the building.  
The design development package is anticipated to be complete late fall/early winter, and construction is 
anticipated to begin late spring/early summer of 2027.  
Project Expenses 
2026 Revised Budget $1M (design and project management services, reimbursable with bond proceeds) 
2027 Recommended Budget $6M (maximum combined debt service payment with the Recreation Center project)* 
Future Anticipated Expenses Annual debt service payments for 20 years and ongoing facility operations and maintenance costs 
Total Project Cost $10M ($6M of bond proceeds and $4M of service expansion fees)  
*The City anticipates issuing $74 million in bonds related to the capital bond package for design and construction of the three new or 
improved facilities at Bob L. Burger Recreation Center, Service Center, and Civic Center. This capital bond package and bond issuance was 
approved by voters at the November 4, 2025, coordinated election, and the first debt service payment is anticipated to occur in 2027. The 
City is anticipating issuing the bonds in two tranches; first tranche of bonds is anticipated to occur in October 2026 in the amount of $40 
million, and the second tranche is anticipated to be issued sometime in 2027 for $34 million. The full proceeds and expenses related to the 
capital bond projects are not included above or in the current fund financials but will be included once the issuances are final.

Water Treatment Plant, Distribution, and Storage 
All domestic water for the City is currently treated at the Baseline Water Treatment Plant. The current 
facility has inadequate capacity to reliably meet current peak demands or to serve projected future 
demands. Reliance on a single treatment facility also makes the City’s water supply vulnerable in the event 
of equipment failure, natural disasters, power outages, and other significant events. A site selection 
process is underway to identify a location for a second water treatment plant, and significant investment in 
the existing facility continues so that the City can reliably meet current demands and provide peak capacity 
and resiliency once a new facility can be constructed. The Water Utility capital improvement plan also 
includes significant investment in projects to improve the reliable yield of the City’s water rights portfolio 
and to replace pipe systems that have reached the end of their useful life. Major water capital projects 
often take decades to design and construct and typically rely on debt financing to allow payment over 20 
years. The annual budget reflects projected expenses for the coming year, but the overall capital 
improvements plan relies on a long-term financial model that considers projected revenues and 
expenditures over a 20+ year planning horizon.

West Baseline Road Multimodal Safety Improvements 
This project will implement recommendations of the 2026 Baseline Road Safety Audit. It will include 
modifications to Baseline Road west of CO42/95th Street including reducing the roadway section from 
four through lanes down to two through lanes. Infrastructure improvements will include restriping the 
roadway, ADA upgrades at the intersections, and modifications to the signal at CO42/95th Street. 
The Baseline Road Safety Audit was prepared in response to Council direction following two serious injury 
crashes involving crosswalks equipped with Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacons in this road segment.  
The crashes occurred after the study period for the Vision Zero Action plan and were not included in that 
analysis or prioritization.   
Project Expenses 
2026 Revised Budget None  
2027 Recommended Budget $325,000 
Future Anticipated Expenses 
The project will have an ongoing impact on the traffic supplies 
operating budget due to the added roadway striping and the 
implementation of green paint to improve the visibility of bicycle lanes.

Bob L. Burger Recreation Center 
In November of 2025, Lafayette voters approved general obligation debt to invest in the renovation and 
expansion of the Bob L. Burger Recreation Center (“BBRC”). Since its passage, the City has been actively 
completing the design process in anticipation of construction beginning late spring/early summer of 2027. 
Current design highlights are listed below; please note that sizes referenced are subject to change based 
on final design refinement. 
• Expanding the aquatics facilities (from 13.2k square feet to 18.9k square feet, a 42% increase) to 
add a new eight-lane lap pool; reconstructing the lazy river, children’s amenities, and spa; and 
modernizing the entire equipment and mechanical spaces to improve long-term repairability; 
• Increasing fitness space (from 11.5k square feet to 15.5k square feet, a 35% increase) by 
repurposing existing underutilized space and relocating locker rooms (see below); 
• Relocating and expanding locker rooms (from 3.5k square feet to 4.6k square feet, a 31% 
increase) to make them accessible from the entry to improve building circulation and access to the 
aquatics amenities; and 
• Increasing flexible program space (from 2k square feet to 6.8k square feet, a 245% increase) and 
constructing a building addition that will also support expanded older adult and other community 
programming.  
The City is completing procurement for the Construction Manager/General Contractor team to have them 
onboard as design is completed and advise on the constructability of the final design package.

Future Civic Center 
The current City Hall building was constructed in 1985. Since that time, the City has increased services 
and programming, and the building does not meet modern ADA or sustainability standards, requiring 
substantial capital upgrades. In November of 2025, Lafayette voters approved general obligation debt to 
fund the construction of a new Civic Center as part of a larger package with the Bob Burger Recreation 
Center renovation/expansion and Service Center renovation/expansion. Since its passage, the City has 
dedicated efforts to the Recreation Center and Service Center projects first, but staff anticipate beginning 
design on the Civic Center in 2027.  
The City owns the site for a new facility in Old Town and has completed a conceptual design for this new 
facility. The new facility totals 32K sf and provides adequate space to accommodate anticipated staffing 
growth, improved community meeting spaces, relocated Municipal Court (from the Police Department), 
and a more energy efficient building. The building would house City Council and Municipal Court Chambers 
and the following departments: Administration, City Clerk, Communications, Economic Development, 
Finance, Human Resources, Information Technology, Municipal Court, Planning and Building, Public Works 
(Administration and Engineering), and Sustainability.   
Project Expenses 
2026 Revised Budget None 
2027 Recommended Budget $3,075,000 (design and project management) 
Future Anticipated Expenses Annual debt service payments for 20 years and ongoing facility operations and maintenance costs
