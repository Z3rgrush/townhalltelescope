Executive Summary 
City Council members have recently received public comment and constituent inquiries 
regarding oil and gas facilities located within or near residential neighborhoods and schools, 
including questions about low-producing wells, emissions, and the City's ability to require 
closure of such facilities. Elizabeth Paranhos, the City of Lafayette’s special legal counsel for 
oil and gas law, will provide City Council with an overview of the City's authority to regulate oil 
and gas facilities and operations within Lafayette, including the scope and limitations of 
local regulatory authority under Colorado law. The presentation will summarize the City's 
existing oil and gas regulations, address regulation of low-producing and inactive wells, 
discuss recent and forthcoming state regulatory developments, and explain how Lafayette 
has historically participated in state rulemakings affecting oil and gas development and air 
quality.  

2 
 
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Environmental Stewardship 
Background Information 
Colorado law grants local governments broad authority to regulate the surface impacts of oil 
and gas development through local land use regulatory authority. However, state law also 
establishes limitations on local authority and reserves certain aspects of oil and gas 
regulation to state agencies, including the Energy and Carbon Management Commission 
(“ECMC”). 
In 2021, Lafayette adopted comprehensive oil and gas regulations governing the siting, 
construction, operation, inspection, monitoring, abandonment, decommissioning, and 
reclamation of oil and gas facilities within the city limits. These regulations include 
requirements related to permitting, operator registration, inspections, financial assurances, 
and enforcement. In several areas, Lafayette's regulations provide protections that exceed 
minimum state requirements. 
Recent public comments and regional discussions have focused on low-producing and 
inactive wells located near residential areas and schools. Council members have expressed 
interest in understanding the City’s authority regarding such facilities and what options may 
be available if concerns arise regarding public health, safety, welfare, environmental 
impacts, or air quality. 
During the workshop, special oil and gas counsel Elizabeth Paranhos will provide an 
overview of: 
• The City’s authority to regulate oil and gas facilities and operations, including the 
relationship between state and local regulatory authority; 
• Lafayette's existing oil and gas regulations and areas where the City's standards are 
more protective than state requirements; 
• The City’s authority and limitations regarding low-producing and inactive wells, 
including the legal process for seeking closure or plugging of wells through the ECMC; 
• Updates on recent and anticipated state legislation, rulemakings, and regulatory 
developments that may affect Lafayette, including emerging issues and regional 
efforts related to oil and gas facilities and air quality; and 
3 
 
• The City's historical participation in ECMC and Air Quality Control Commission (AQCC) 
rulemaking processes and how local government participation can influence state 
regulatory outcomes. 
This workshop is informational in nature. No formal Council action is requested. Council will 
have an opportunity to ask questions and discuss topics that may warrant future policy 
consideration or additional analysis. 
Next Steps 
No formal action is currently anticipated as a result of this workshop. Depending on Council 
interest, future actions could include additional legal analysis of specific regulatory tools 
available to local governments, participation in state rulemaking proceedings, engagement 
with regional partners on oil and gas and air quality issues, or further discussion regarding 
low-producing and inactive wells. 
Recommendation 
This agenda item is informational only; no Council action is requested at this time.

Executive Summary 
This presentation serves as an annual update regarding the City’s Winter Mobility Plan. The 
Winter Mobility Plan was developed in 2020 and is intended to coordinate cross-
departmental and interagency efforts to support community mobility and public safety 
during and after winter storm events. A copy of the plan is included as Attachment A. The 
updated plan will be published on the City’s website later this month. 
City Council Strategic Outcome (most applicable): Mobile and Connected 
Background Information 
The City’s Winter Mobility Plan was developed in 2020 to replace the Public Work’s Snow 
and Ice Control Plan and better integrate cross departmental efforts. The plan is presented 
to City Council each winter and is used to guide operations through the snow season.

PURPOSE 
The City of Lafayette Winter Mobility Plan is intended to coordinate cross-departmental and 
interagency efforts to support community mobility and public safety during and after winter 
storm events.  
 
WINTER MOBILITY ROLES 
CITY DEPARTMENTS 
Administration 
The City Manager oversees interdepartmental coordination and is responsible for decisions 
related to closure of City facilities.  
 
Communications 
The Communications Department is responsible for providing information to the community, 
staff, and the media related to facility closures, reduced hours, and cancellations. The 
Department follows the Severe Weather Communication Plan.  
 
Public Works  
The Public Works Department is responsible for management of snow and ice on public streets, 
including on-street bike lanes. Priority is given to designated “Primary” and “Secondary” routes 
with contract resources available for limited deployment to residential areas during designated 
winter emergencies.  
  
Community Services 
The Community Services Department is responsible for management of snow and ice at City 
facilities, sidewalks adjacent to designated City facilities, hard surface sidewalks in parks and at 
designated trailheads and trail facilities.  
 
Police Department 
The Police Department monitors road conditions on a 24/7 basis and requests maintenance 
resources as needed to address potentially hazardous conditions. The Police Chief makes 
recommendations to the City Manager on any facility closures. The Police Chief may implement 
“Accident Alert” when conditions are so severe that Police Officers are unable to respond to the 
volume of motor vehicle crashes. Accident Alert information is posted on the City website.

Planning & Building 
The Planning & Building Department is responsible for ensuring that property owners comply 
with City codes related to clearing of sidewalks. Staff conduct public outreach, respond to areas 
of concern, and may issue municipal court summonses for violations of City ordinance.  
 
Fire Department 
The Fire Department monitors road conditions and requests maintenance support as necessary 
to ensure emergency access to homes and businesses.  
 
Human Resources 
The Risk Manager supports programs and training related to winter safety and is responsible for 
investigation of any related accidents or injuries.  
 
PARTNER AGENCIES 
Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) 
CDOT is responsible for winter maintenance of state highways within the City of Lafayette. 
These include State Highway 287, State Highway 42, and State Highway 7. On highways with 
annual average daily traffic of 1,000 or greater, snow removal operations will occur throughout 
the storm (24 hours) until normal driving conditions exist. 
 
On highways with annual average daily traffic of less than 1,000, snow removal operations will 
be limited to 14-hours, from 5am to 7pm (exceptions may be granted for school bus routes, 
highway segments with high accident rates or hospital/emergency routes). While roads with 
fewer vehicles may have limited hours of snow removal during a typical snow event, CDOT will 
plow the roadway if the storm is such that it may cause the road to become closed and 
impassable. CDOT will also work with emergency service providers when emergency response is 
necessary. 
 
Boulder County Transportation Department 
The Boulder County Transportation Department is responsible for winter maintenance of 
county roads located outside of City limits. 
 
Boulder County Dispatch 
Boulder County Dispatch receives after-hours requests for service and disseminates information 
to City departments as appropriate.

SNOW AND ICE REMOVAL 
PUBLIC STREETS 
Snow and ice on public streets are managed by the Public Works Department primarily using 
truck mounted snowplows and application of de-icing material.  
Prioritization 
The City has approximately 293 lane miles of roadway. City streets are designated into three 
categories to guide prioritization of resources:  
Primary Routes 
Primary routes generally include arterial streets and other roadways that provide access 
to hospitals, police and fire stations, and schools. 
Secondary Routes  
Secondary routes generally include roadways designated as collector streets. These 
include primary routes into neighborhoods and commercial areas. Resources are 
deployed on secondary routes after all primary routes have been cleared.  
Other City Streets including residential 
Streets not designated as primary or secondary routes are cleared only as necessary to 
maintain emergency access. Accumulated snow depths greater than 10” can impact 
emergency vehicle clearance and may result in deployment of on-call heavy equipment 
contractors to do limited clearing.

Procedure 
The Public Works Transportation Operations and Maintenance Manager is responsible for 
monitoring weather predictions and conditions and determining the appropriate deployment of 
Public Works resources. Responsibility may be delegated to the Transportation Operations & 
Maintenance Supervisor as necessary. 
Due to the limited number of snow events per year, the city relies on temporary work 
reassignments and shift schedules to conduct snow and ice removal. Staff are required to be 
available to respond to the Public Works Services Center as soon as possible. A tiered approach 
is used to minimize impacts to regular operations and emergency response capabilities.  
Level 1 – During a Level 1 deployment, winter maintenance is primarily conducted by staff 
assigned to the street maintenance workgroup. Staff work regular shifts with overtime as 
needed. Fleet maintenance staff provide support for vehicle configuration, repairs, and loading 
of de-icing materials. Other Public Works operations, including meter reading and utility 
maintenance operations may be reduced due to snow reassignments, but generally continue. 
During a Level 1 event, 4 trucks equipped with snowplows and/or de-icing material are 
deployed and focus on the three designated Primary/Secondary routes.  
Level 2 – During a Level 2 deployment, staff is split into A/B/C crews to provide 24-coverage 
through 10-hour shifts. A mechanic is assigned to each shift to support repairs and to load de-
icing material. Utilities Maintenance staff suspend most operations and maintenance activities 
and are assigned a route/shift. A Level 2 event continues to rely on 4 trucks covering the three 
designated primary and secondary routes, but with 24-hour coverage. The fourth truck will 
focus on larger primary corridors initially.  
Level 3 – During a Level 3 deployment, staff is split into A/B crews to provide 24-hour coverage 
through 12-hour shifts. Other non-emergency work is temporarily suspended. This can only be 
sustained for a limited time period but allows for the deployment of one additional large truck 
and a medium duty truck for a total of six. Services are provided for designated primary and 
secondary routes. 
Attachment A - Winter Mobility Plan
Level 4 – Winter Emergency – In the event that snow accumulation on streets not designated as 
Primary/Secondary reaches a depth, or is anticipated to reach a depth, that would preclude 
access by emergency vehicles such as ambulances, fire trucks, or four-wheel drive police 
vehicles, the City may deploy emergency on-call heavy equipment contractors. Such a 
deployment involves using equipment such as backhoes, front-end loaders, and graders to clear 
a single lane in the center of residential streets. The equipment is not fitted to apply de-icing 
materials. A determination to deploy emergency contractors is made by the City Manager in 
consultation with the Police Chief and Public Works Director. The decision considers current 
and anticipated road conditions, variability of snow depths across the City, and the weather 
forecast.  
During periods of staffing shortages, the deployment shifts and number of trucks running may 
alter depending on the forecasted duration and severity of the inclement weather event. This 
could look something like running only A/B crews on 10 or 12 hr. rotations to ensure that 
service levels remain as consistent as possible. 
Additional Post Storm Clean Up 
As staff transition back to normal duties, further monitoring will occur. Other areas that have 
problematic icing issues or on street bike lanes may receive additional post storm clean up. 
These will occur as needed and vary with each storm event. Varying equipment will be used 
based upon the situation.

PROCEDURE 
Snow removal procedures are established to provide a consistent level of snow removal for City 
facilities. Exceptions to procedures may occur on a storm-to-storm basis based on conditions 
and availability of resources. 
• Snowplows will be used when snow accumulations are more than 1” or if they would be 
beneficial in removing snow. 
• During snow events of less than 1”, snow shoveling will be done on main entrances and 
sidewalks connecting family and accessible parking to the main entrance of the Police 
Station, Recreation Center, Senior Center, Library, City Hall, Golf Course Clubhouse, and 
Sister Carmen Community Center.  
• The facilities listed above as well as Festival Plaza are required to provide the Parks 
division with schedules of any events taking place that would require snow removal at 
least 48 hours in advance and as soon as possible in the event of any reschedules. Any 
last-minute notifications will be prioritized on a time and personnel basis as well as a 
storm-to-storm basis.

• City employees are asked to park close to each other in parking lots to allow for efficient 
plowing. Snow removal will not be done between individual parking spaces due to the 
risk of vehicle damage.  
• Ice melt is provided for application by staff at some facilities. Staff are encouraged to 
use it only as needed, spread lightly and evenly over any icy or slick surfaces. Excessive 
amounts of ice melt can cause damage to landscape and cement surfaces as well as be 
tracked into buildings, causing mess and damage to floors and carpets. Facilities that run

SIDEWALKS

Snow removal assistance for seniors is available through the City’s Snow Angel volunteer 
program coordinated through Community Services.  
Residents are encouraged to remove snow as soon as possible to avoid it being packed down to 
ice. Public sidewalks maintained by HOAs are still subject to the 24-hour requirement and are

DELAYED OPENINGS, CLOSURES, AND CANCELLATIONS 
The City provides essential services to the community, and facility delayed openings or closures 
are only considered when weather makes for unsafe commutes or operations. In the case of 
extreme weather events, the City Manager may take actions to reduce operating hours, cancel 
public events and meetings, or temporarily close some or all City facilities. Decisions consider 
the safety of the traveling public, the ability of staff to safely travel to/from work, and the 
impacts of additional travelers on public safety and snow removal operations.  
When weather conditions warrant consideration of a delayed opening or facility closures, the 
City Manager, Police Chief, Public Works Director, and Communications Director will confer by 
approximately 5am. When sufficient information may be available to consider a delay or 
closure the prior evening, coordination will occur with the intent of providing public and staff 
notification by 10pm. If the City Manager determines that delayed opening or closure is 
appropriate, the Communications Department will notify media outlets and provide 
information directly to the public via the website and social media. Staff are notified via the 
Alert Media system. In the event of anticipated severe weather impacting the afternoon rush 
hour or evening, the City Manager may elect to close facilities early and/or cancel evening 
meetings and events. As with other closures, the Communications Director provides 
information to the public via media outlets, the website, and City social media platforms.  
Police Department, Fire Stations, Services Center, Water Treatment, and Water Reclamation 
Essential employees at these facilities will maintain normal work schedules during all weather 
conditions. Shift schedules and staffing may be adjusted by the department director, as 
needed. Non-essential employees at these locations may observe delayed opening or closures 
in line with Other City Facilities as outlined below.  
Recreation Center 
The Recreation Center generally opens to the public at 5am M-F. The City Manager may elect to 
open the facility with reduced staffing or delay opening pending a determination on any

ACCIDENT ALERT 
The Police Department may declare an Accident Alert when conditions (usually poor weather or 
other community emergency) are so severe that Police Officers are unable to respond to 
volume of motor vehicle crashes. Some cities may be on Accident Alert while the City of 
Lafayette may not, depending on how the conditions are affecting a specific area. Accident 
Alert may be declared by the on-duty supervisor or member of command staff.  
While Accident Alert is in effect, Police Officers will only respond if the following circumstances 
exist:  
1. Damaged vehicles are causing the street to be closed 
2. The crash involves a fatality or injury requiring medical attention 
3. Impaired driving (alcohol or drugs) is involved 
4. Any of the involved drivers fails to remain on scene (hit and run) 
5. Any of the involved drivers does not have a valid driver's license 
6. Involved with on-duty City personnel or there is damage to public property

CONCLUSION 
This document represents the City’s normal operational plan for winter weather events. Each 
winter storm event is unique, and operations may vary from this plan based on weather 
conditions, resource availability, other emergencies, and unanticipated conditions. This plan 
will be reviewed annually and periodically updated to reflect changes in priorities, 
infrastructure, resources, and approach.
