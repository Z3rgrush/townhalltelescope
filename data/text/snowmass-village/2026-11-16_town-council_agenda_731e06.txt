they come before the Council for final action. A Single Public Hearing will be opened for all items on 
the Consent Agenda. These items will be approved by a single motion of the Council. The Mayor 
will ask if there is anyone present who has objection to such procedure as to certain items. 
Members of the Council may also ask that an item be removed from the consent section and fully 
discussed. All items not removed from the consent section will then be approved. A member of the 
Council may vote no on specific items without asking that they be removed from the consent 
section for full discussion. Any item that is removed from the consent agenda will be placed on the 
regular agenda.

6. Public Hearings - Quasi-Judicial Hearings 
Public Hearings are the formal opportunity for the Town Council to LISTEN to the public regarding 
the issue at hand. For land use hearings the Council is required to act in a quasi-judicial capacity. 
When acting as a quasi-judicial body, the Council is acting in much the same capacity as a judge 
would act in a court of law. Under these circumstances, the judicial or quasi-judicial must limit its 
consideration to matters which are placed into evidence and are part of the public record. The 
Council must base their decision on the law and the evidence presented at the hearing.

Consideration and Possible Approval of Resolution No. 25, 2026 approving an IGA to

Presented by: 
Jeff Conklin, Town Attorney 
 
 
 
Background: 
Since 2017, TOSV along with Aspen School District, Aspen Valley Health, Pitkin 
County, City of Aspen, and the Town of Basalt (collective, the “Participating Members”) 
have been involved in one or more collective funding agreements to strategically 
strengthen the behavioral health services.    
 
The Participating Members wish to enter an updated Intergovernmental Agreement for 
the purpose of providing ongoing behavioral health services and personnel to support 
the implementation of the 2026 Pitkin County Behavioral Health Strategic Plan (the 
“Behavioral Health IGA”).  The Behavioral Health IGA will replace agreements amongst 
some or all of the Participating Members known as agreements known as Vital Mental 
Health Services, Detox / Withdrawal Management, and PACT (Co-Responder)  MOU. 
 
The 2026 Pitkin County Behavior Health Strategic Plan calls for sustaining the provision 
of withdrawal management services and the Pitkin Area Co-Responder Team (PACT) 
Program, effectively streamlining three separate multi-agency agreements for 
behavioral health services.   
 
This IGA will maintain a similar level of service as has been provided in recent years.   
 
 
 
Financial Impact: 
Pitkin County will act as the fiscal agent under Behavioral Health IGA providing payment 
to vendors.  TOSV’s funding for 2027 is equal to $196,860.  This amount has already 
been approved in the approved budget. 
 
Applicability to Council Goals & Objectives: 
N/A

RECITALS 
 
A. The Town of Snowmass Village, Colorado (“TOSV” or the “Town”) is a home-rule 
municipality organized under Article XX of the Colorado Constitution and with the 
authority of the Town of Snowmass Village Home Rule Charter (the “Charter”). 
 
B. Article XIV, Section 18(2)(a) of the Colorado Constitution and C.R.S. § 29-1-203 
authorize and encourage governments to cooperate and contract with each other 
to provide functions and services lawfully authorized to each. 
 
C. Since 2017, TOSV along with Aspen School District, Aspen Valley Health, Pitkin 
County, City of Aspen, and the Town of Basalt (collective, the “Participating 
Members”) have been involved in one or more collective funding agreements to 
strategically strengthen the behavioral health services.    
 
D. The Participating Members wish to enter an updated Intergovernmental 
Agreement for the purpose of purchasing ongoing behavioral health services and 
personnel to support the implementation of the 2026 Pitkin County Behavioral 
Health Strategic Plan (the “Behavioral Health IGA”), as set forth in Exhibit A. 
 
E. The Behavioral Health IGA will replace agreements amongst some or all of the 
Participating Members known as agreements known as Vital Mental Health 
Services, Detox and Withdrawal Management, and the PACT MOU.

provision of withdrawal management services and the Pitkin Area Co-Responder 
Team (PACT) Program, effectively streamlining three separate multi-agency 
agreements for behavioral health services. 
 
G. Pitkin County will act as the fiscal agent under Behavioral Health IGA providing 
payment to vendors, and TOSV’s funding for 2027 is equal to $196,860. 
 
H. The Town Council wishes to approve the IGA and perform its obligations 
thereunder and finds and determines that doing so is in the interest of the Town.

For  
The Purchase of Ongoing Behavioral Health Services and Personnel to Support the 
Implementation of the Pitkin County Behavioral Health Strategic Plan 
 
 THIS INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT (the “Agreement”) is made this 
______ day of ____________________, 2026 by and between the Board of County 
Commissioners of Pitkin County, Colorado, whose address is 530 East Main Street, Suite 
302 Aspen, Colorado 81611 ("the County”), Aspen School District (“ASD”), Aspen Valley 
Health (“AVH”), City of Aspen (“COA”), Town of Basalt (“TOB”), and Town of 
Snowmass Village (“TOSV”) hereinafter severally referred to by name or collectively as 
the “Participating Members”. 
 
RECITALS 
 
WHEREAS, This Agreement is entered into pursuant to, inter alia, C.R.S. §§ 29-1-201, 
et seq., and Article XIV, Section 18 of the Colorado Constitution. 
 
WHEREAS, Mental health and substance use (“Behavioral Health”) are continually 
identified as a top health concern in Pitkin County and the 2026 Pitkin County Community 
Health Assessment identified access to health care, mental health care, and substance use 
as the top 3 concerns in Pitkin County. Additionally the 2026 Pitkin County Community 
Survey found that ratings for the availability of affordable healthcare falls below national 
averages, with only 32% of residents rating the availability of affordable, quality healthcare 
as excellent or good, highlighting ongoing challenges that affect the overall health, well-
being and livability of the community, which encompasses not only the physical 
environment and quality of life, but also the health and well-being of residents, making 
access to affordable, timely and comprehensive behavioral health services essential to a 
healthy and thriving community; and 
 
WHEREAS there are many resources along the continuum of care (prevention, 
intervention, treatment, aftercare) available in the community, yet there are numerous 
barriers to utilizing behavioral health services, including limited providers, long waiting 
lists, navigating and understanding the mental health system, language barriers, lack of 
coordinated care, and affordability of care, exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic and 
the subsequent cost of living increases, making access to affordable care and services a 
growing challenge; and 
 
WHEREAS, Local Governments and Agencies within Pitkin County engaged in a focused 
strategic planning process in 2017, 2021 and again in 2025 with the purpose of building a 
more coordinated and sustainable behavioral health system that can provide needed 
services for those who live, work and play in Pitkin County. Since 2017, the Pitkin County, 
Aspen School District, Aspen Valley Hospital, City of Aspen, Town of Basalt, and the 
Town of Snowmass Village have been involved one or more collective funding agreements 
to strategically strengthen the system of care and improve service delivery of behavioral 
health services that include behavioral health system coordination, integrated behavioral 
health care, school-based mental health, case management, withdrawal management, and 
the Pitkin Area Co-Responder Team (PACT) Program; and

WHEREAS, Participating Members agreed to collectively pool their funding in 2027 to 
support Behavioral Health services as outlined under “Agreement” Heading of Section 2 
and a governance structure as outlined in the 2026 Pitkin County Behavioral Health 
Strategic Plan and sustain the provision of withdrawal management services and the PACT 
Program, effectively streamlining three separate multi-agency agreements for behavioral 
health services; and 
 
WHEREAS, Participating Members have agreed to enter the contract with the current 
Vendor, with use of individual funding levels identified under “Agreement” Heading of 
Section 3 “Funding Allocation”, Subsection B “Cash Funding”; and 
 
WHEREAS, Participating Members do hereby collectively determine and declare that 
this Intergovernmental Agreement is necessary, proper and convenient for the continued 
fostering and preservation of the public health, safety and wellbeing. 
 
AGREEMENT 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE, for and in consideration of the mutual promises and 
agreements of the parties and other good and valuable consideration, the adequacy and 
sufficiency of which is hereby acknowledged, the parties agree as follows: 
 
1. Effective ______________, except as expressly provided herein, this Agreement 
shall replace and supersede all prior agreements of any kind between all or any of 
the Participating Members and any or all other Participating Members hereto, to 
the extent and for the limited purpose as such other agreements may be related to 
the provision of funding collaborative and integrated behavioral health and 
substance use prevention, intervention, treatment, case management, withdrawal 
management and PACT programs. Specifically this Agreement replaces the 
agreements known as Vital Mental Health Services,  Detox and Withdrawal 
Management and the PACT MOU.    
 
2. Effective _____________, Pitkin County will be the fiscal agent providing 
payment to vendors as approved in the 2027 Behavioral Health budget in the 
amount of $1,179,147 in 2027 for integrated behavioral health care, school-based 
mental health, case management, withdrawal management, and the Pitkin Area 
Co-Responder (PACT) Program per the contract and approved in the County's 
2027 budget. Once invoiced by Pitkin County in Quarter one, Participating 
Members agree to pay in full. 
 
3. Funding Allocation. The Participating Members agree to provide funding in 
return for behavioral health system coordination, integrated behavioral health 
care, school-based mental health, case management, withdrawal management, and 
the Pitkin Area Co-Responder (PACT) Program as follows. By August each year, 
Pitkin County will work with the Behavioral Health Advisory Committee to 
propose a budget that must be approved by the Participating Members for the 
following year. Once the Participating Members have approved the budget in a

Funders Meeting, the Advisory Committee will be updated regularly on service 
implementation and is the body that will be consulted if changes in services are 
necessary throughout the year. The share for each entity will be determined as 
follows: 
 
a. In-Kind Contributions: 
 
i. Aspen School District shall provide, at no cost, space on the school 
campus for 3 school-based clinicians serving the school-based mental

Funders budget equal to $324,476 for the provision of Behavioral Health 
services provided by vendors, as well as the personnel approved to lead 
the implementation of the Behavioral Health Strategic Plan. 
 
iii. City of Aspen agrees to pay 23% of the 2027 Behavioral Health 
Funders budget equal to $393,391 for the provision of Behavioral Health 
services provided by vendors, as well as the personnel approved to lead 
the implementation of the Behavioral Health Strategic Plan. 
 
iv. Pitkin County agrees to pay 34% of the 2027 Behavioral Health 
Funders budget equal to $579,059 for the provision of Behavioral Health

Funders budget equal to $75,360 for the provision of Behavioral Health

Health Funders budget equal to $196,860 for the provision of Behavioral

lead the implementation of the Behavioral Health Strategic Plan.

Background: 
March 16, 2026, Town Council and the Planning Commission reviewed and commented 
on a Pre-Sketch application presentation by Elevated Snowmass, LLC (the “Applicant”) 
to redevelop the existing Viewline Resort (consisting of the Viewline Hotel, Snowmass 
Conference Center, and Wildwood Lodge) with a proposed 167 traditional lodge keys, 
61 fractional units, conference center with meeting rooms, commercial and retail space, 
125 on-site workforce housing units, and subgrade parking and centralized deliveries 
facilities. The proposed redevelopment requires a Major PUD Amendment and 
Rezoning (the “Application”). July 20, 2026 the Application advanced to Sketch Plan 
with a joint presentation to Town Council and the Planning Commission. 
 
Due to the size and scale of the Application, Staff and the Applicant agreed to present 
the Application materials in segments to the Planning Commission to allow for thorough 
review and discussion. The public hearing was opened August 5, 2026 with a site visit 
to review the elements of the proposal in relation to existing conditions. The public 
hearing was continued across the following dates –August 19 (lodge aspect), 
September 9 (workforce housing aspect), September 23 (conference center & 
commercial aspects), and October 7 (draft resolution). At the October 7th meeting the 
Planning Commission adopted Resolution No. 4, Series 2026 with conditions, thereby 
recommending the Sketch Plan for approval at Town Council. 
The Application began its review at Town Council with an introduction to the project and 
subsequent site visit at the November 9th meeting of the Town Council. 
In accordance with Section 16A-5-320 of the Land Use Code, the Sketch Plan review is 
an opportunity for the Town and the Applicant to engage in an exploratory discussion of 
a development proposal.  
Due to the size and scale of the Application, Staff and the Applicant have agreed to 
Page 22 of 199
presentation of the Application materials in segments to allow for thorough review and 
discussion.  
The proposed review schedule, subject to change, is as follows: 
• November 9, 2026 Site Visit (completed) 
• November 16, 2026 Project Overview 
• December 7, 2026 Review of Proposed Lodging, Commercial, and 
Conference Center  
• December 21, 2026 Review of Workforce Housing  
• January 4, 2027 Consideration of draft Resolution 
 
This is a tentative review schedule, which may be modified as directed by the Town 
Council. 
 
As part of the Sketch Plan application, the Applicant is requesting to 1) amend the 
existing Silvertree PUD approvals, and 2) rezone the entirety of the subject site to 
Mixed-Use-2 (MU-2) zoning which allows for all proposed uses in the Application. The 
existing PUD approvals exceed the MU-2 zone district dimensional standards, 
consequently the Applicant is seeking variations from dimensional standards of the MU-
2 zone district for specific areas of the Application including height variations, maximum 
FAR variation, parking requirements, and setback requirements. The Application would 
additionally expand the existing Silvertree PUD to encompass the entire site, including 
the conference center and Wildwood parcels. In consideration of the requested 
variations, the Applicant has proposed community benefit aspects in the form of 57,131 
SF of proposed on-site workforce housing units and a centralized delivery facility.  
The overall proposed redevelopment replaces similar existing uses on the property, 
including replacement of lodging, commercial, conference center/event space, and 
residential uses, and uses accessory to the primary uses listed above. The proposed 
uses, mass and scale, are generally consistent with the goals and intent of the 
Comprehensive Plan, as conditionally approved in the adopted Planning Commission 
Resolution No. 4, Series of 2026. Certain aspects of the application elicited greater 
Planning Commission discussion. Those aspects, reflected in the adopted resolution 
conditions, include:  
• Acceptance of the general uses proposed, based on the requirement of detailed 
architectural plans appropriate of a Preliminary Plan application, providing further 
evaluation of the architecture and its relationship to adjacent uses.  
• General acceptance of the centralized delivery facility concept, with concern 
given to the architectural and engineering details, and the facility’s relationship to 
Page 23 of 199
the streetscape along Elbert Lane.  
• Further evaluation of the unit mix and architectural plans of the Workforce 
Housing, particularly Building B, with an exploration of architectural massing and 
articulation in the Preliminary Plan application that considers reduced impacts to 
adjacent properties including the Aspenwood Condominums. 
• Emphasis on safe, attractive and connected pedestrian access from adjacent 
properties, the numbered lots, and the Benedict Trail throughout the site as an 
important community character attribute of the site plan. 
• Emphasis on alternative modes of transportation to supplement the proposed 
subgrade parking as well as to generally support a reduction in the minimum 
parking requirements per Town Code. 
 
Further analysis and discussion of these and general application details will be brought 
forward through the review process through Staff and Applicant presentations. 
Town Council will review the Sketch Plan, and provide direction to the Applicant related 
to use, compatibility, mass and scale. Resolution No. 4, Series of 2026 (Att. A) details 
the Sketch Plan level conditions of approval established at Planning Commission. Staff 
would emphasize that conditions of approval at Sketch Plan provide direction for the 
Applicant in consideration of a future Preliminary Plan application. Approval at Sketch 
Plan provides conceptual direction for applicants based conceptual design, usage, 
mass and scale, and does not constitute a land use entitlement. 
 
 
Financial Impact: 
The Sketch Plan does not obligate the Town to approve future development(s) and 
therefore, there are no financial impacts associated with the land use process. A 
financial impact analysis is required as a part of the Preliminary Plan application.

Section One: General Findings. The Planning Commission hereby generally finds that: 
1. The Applicant submitted the Application for Sketch Plan Review and Rezoning of the 
Project in accordance with the provisions of the Municipal Code. The application 
provided the Minimum Contents required pursuant to Section 16A -5-320(c) of the 
Code and included written and graphic materials in sufficient detail to deem the 
Application complete. 
2. The Sketch Plan  and Rezoning proposes land uses for the subject site which are 
consistent with the intent of the Snowmass Village 2018 Comprehensive Plan (the 
“Comprehensive Plan”) pursuant to Section 16A-5-320 of the Code.  
3. The Applicant is requesting a variance from the height restrictions associated with the 
proposed underlying MU -2 zone from 38 feet to 77 feet which pursuant to Section 
16A-5-310(c)(7)(a) requires a super majority vote (¾) by Town Council. 
4. The Applicant is request ing a variance from the maximum allowed FAR restrictions 
associated the proposed underlying MU-2 zone from 1.7 FAR to 2.53 FAR. 
5. The Applicant is requesting a variance from the required parking requirements , 
decreasing the required minimum to 252 spaces. 
6. The Applicant is requesting a variance from the required setbacks to zero setbacks in 
certain areas of the proposal. 
7. For consideration of the said variation requests (height, FAR, parking, setbacks), the 
Applicant suggests 57,131sf of  workforce housing and a centralized delivery facility  
which may be evaluated pursuant to the Community Purpose criteria in Municipal 
Code Section 16A-5-300(c)(6).  
8. The subject area is identified on the Comprehensive Plan’s Environmental Sensitiviety 
Map as being within the Brush Creek Impact Area. 
9. The Sketch Plan identifies land proposed for development or redevelopment that are 
not located within the Wildlife Sensitive Areas identified in the Comprehensive Plan. 
10. To the extent necessary for and pertinent to a Sketch Plan level of evaluation and 
review, the Application is able to sufficiently answer the questions identified in Section 
16A-5-300(c), General Restrictions, of the Development Code, subject to satisfying 
the applicable conditions contained within Section Three of this resolution. 
11. To the extent necessary for and pertinent to a Sketch Plan level of evaluation and 
review, the Application is generally consistent with the provisions of Section 16A -5-
310, Review Standards, of the Development Code, subject to satisfying the applicable 
conditions contained within Section Three of this resolution.

Section Three: Conditions. The approval granted in Section Two above shall be subject

General 
1. Except as may need to be amended, the Preliminary Plan application shall be 
generally consistent with the provisions of this resolution, and with the 
representations made by the Applicant during the Sketch Plan PUD Review. 
 
2. The Applicant shall demonstrate consistency with the rezoning criteria concerning 
the proposed “Mixed-Use-2" zone district. 
 
3. The architectural design of all proposed buildings shall be further articulated as part 
of the Preliminary Plan application, detailing mass, scale, exterior features, 
materials, fenestration and the like. 
 
4. The Applicant shall consider adjustments to the proposed site plan including 
alternative locations, massing, configurations, and orientation of development to 
minimize the visual impact of development along Fall Lane. 
5. The Applicant shall consider architectural articulation of the slopeside buildings, and 
their relationship to the ski slope. 
 
6. A construction management plan considering conceptual access, staging, material 
storage, fugitive dust, equipment storage, traffic, parking, noise, and erosion control 
shall be submitted with the Preliminary Plan application.  A phased construction 
schedule should be discussed as well. 
 
 
Parking and Traffic 
7. A detailed parking management plan shall be submitted with any subsequent 
Preliminary Plan application, illustrating the proposed mix and allocation of parking 
stalls to associated uses within the development. 
 
8. The residents of the proposed workforce housing shall not be eligible to obtain

resident parking permits for the Town’s public parking lots pursuant to Section 8 
Article II of the Municipal Code. 
 
9. To support reduced vehicular parking, the Applicant shall consider alternative modes 
of travel.  Alternative modes may include bikeshare, carshare, employee shuttles, 
and any other transportation demand management methods as appropriate. A 
reduction in required parking standards may only be considered with the provision of 
substantial alternative transportation. Additional bicycle parking and alternative 
modes of transport are highly encouraged to reduce vehicular parking. 
 
10. The reduction in municipal code parking standards is appropriate only if substantial 
alternative modes of travel (mentioned in the previous condition) are implemented.   
 
11. Any subsequent Preliminary Plan application shall include a Traffic Impact Study for 
the vicinity.  Emphasis should be given to the intersections of Carriage Way and 
Elbert Lane, and Carriage Way and Fall Lane. 
 
 
Site Circulation 
12. Pedestrian connections through and to the lodge between the mall, ski slope, and Carriage 
Way will need to demonstrate intentional design elements (i.e. pavement and/or sidewalk 
treatments, lighting, landscaping, etc.) through submittal of a landscape plan.  Design shall 
contribute to a protected and clearly defined pedestrian environment, through landscape 
design treatments and wayfinding. A plan showing which trees are to be protected, 
removed, and replaced shall be included. 
 
13. The Applicant shall consider a potential trail connection from the end of the Mall at Fanny 
Hill running the hill on the subject property and not interfering with the concert venue. The

14. The Applicant shall consider bicycle infrastructure to improve multi-modal access to and 
through the site.  Incorporating bicycle channels (wheel gutters) on all stairways

cyclists to carry their bikes. 
 
15. Accessibility and accessible routes throughout the site and to all structures shall be 
detailed in any subsequent Preliminary Plan application. 
 
16. ADA access to the ski slope has historically been accommodated via Fall Lane and shall 
be considered in any subsequent Preliminary Plan application.

To: Michelle Bonfils

Date: 7/31/26 
Re: Ritz-Carlton Sketch Plan PUD 
  
With the knowledge that this is a Sketch Plan PUD meeting the transportation 
department’s comments remain broad in detail. That said, there are many special 
concerns for this project from a transportation perspective to be considered.  
• Site Circulation 
o Elbert Lane Traffic Surveys 
 The Transportation Department has conducted a manual traffic 
survey on Elbert Lane under current conditions (7/9/26-7/15/26) 
which was utilized as basis for comments below. The Public Works 
Department is also conducting a traffic survey at the intersection of 
Elbert Lane and Carriage Way which will capture traffic on both 
roads. Collected data will be compiled in a separate report for 
planning department review.  
o Pedestrian 
 Current pedestrian usage of Elbert Lane peaks at over 1000 people 
per day during the summer at the intersection of Elbert Lane and 
Upper Carriage Way, far more than any other user group. This use 
does not capture those entering the property from Fall Lane, further 
east on Elbert Lane, or between Fall and Elbert. 
 The current pedestrian flow highlights the importance of the Elbert 
Lane Right of Way as a connection to the current Viewline Resort, 
as well as to the Snowmass Mall and Ski Area. The proposed 
sidewalks along the entrance to the Lodge, and pedestrian walking 
area, are both desirable infrastructure upgrades. Sidewalks should 
extend along the road the length of Elbert Lane to adequately 
protect the highest volume users of this road. If a sidewalk cannot 
be adequately sized on both sides of the road, an appropriate 
pedestrian crossing should be considered.

 Ensure than adequate ADA access exists along created walkways, 
or that alternative ADA pathways are identified.  
o Valet/Hotel Guests 
 Current conditions require Valet and Hotel Self-Parking to utilize the 
numbered lots. While exact numbers for current valet created trips 
are difficult to ascertain from a traffic survey, it is clear that both 
valet and self-park guests contribute heavily to Elbert Lane Traffic. 
Overall bi-directional personal vehicular traffic peaks at nearly 400 
vehicles per day and Viewline shuttles at over 120 vehicles per day. 
 Valet service often consists of a guest vehicle arriving, a valet 
driven hotel vehicle escorting guest vehicle to the numbered lots, 
and then that valet vehicle returning. Currently each arrival can 
amount to as many as 4 trips along Elbert Lane and Carriage Way 
(and 4 more for departure). Valet being kept on site as shown in the 
Sketch Plan should be a significant improvement to circulation both 
internal and external to the property.  
 If Self-Parking guests are to utilize the numbered parking lots, it will 
be important for the Ritz-Carlton to continue to sell guest parking 
permits for these guests as they do now. The Town does not have 
the capacity to manage payment for self-parking guests. 
o Skier pick-up/drop-off 
 The current Elbert Lane Right of Way allows for skier pick-up and 
drop-off for all lodges and the public, and ADA elevator access. 
While it is difficult to estimate how many private or unmarked 
vehicles are utilizing Elbert Lane for this purpose, during the 
Summer 2026 traffic survey period over 30 marked vehicles from 
other lodges were observed in a day, a number that is expected to 
be much higher during Winter ski season. Finding a location, 
whether it be at the redesigned entrance to the Ritz-Carlton or 
along the existing Elbert Lane Right of Way, to accommodate these 
trips will be important moving forward with the PUD. If this location 
for pick-up and drop-off is removed, it will likely fall to inadequately 
designed infrastructure in other areas of the Town to accommodate 
the use. 
 While not a current use, it would be best to plan for Snowmass 
Village Transit vehicles to be able to utilize any future pick-up/drop-
off sites.  
o Retail/Spa/Restaurant Access 
 The trip generation study from Kimley-Horn discussed high turnover 
restaurant and strip retail trips. There should be some evaluation on 
how these trips affect site circulation. For example, would these 
guests be expected to valet, self-park onsite, or self-park offsite?

Each of these would have a different impact on site circulation, 
whether it be pedestrian or vehicular. Guest parking passes are not

o Biking 
 The Sketch Plan mentions 61 bike spaces. It should be clarified

the current station could serve the new users as is, and if not, 
whether adding to that location rather than adding a new one is a 
better use of resources.

5. 30% group business important and supported goal 
a. 30% is what we understand the current mix to be at Viewline. Keeping it in this range is ideal for

Group business represents an important component of the operating strategy for the resort. The 
intent is to establish a stabilized group business mix of approximately 30% of overall occupied 
room nights, creating year-round demand, supporting shoulder-season occupancy, and generating 
significant food and beverage, wellness, recreation, and retail spending throughout the Town of 
Snowmass Village. This target is generally consistent with the property's historic role as a

b. What is the group ceiling anticipated to be in strategy? (ceiling is the highest number of rooms

While detailed revenue management strategies will not be finalized until the year preceding 
opening, the intent is to maintain a balanced business mix that supports both transient and group 
demand. Group allocations will vary by season and demand period. However, the resort is not 
envisioned as a convention-focused property where group demand dominates occupancy.  
c. What is the ideal peak room night group anticipated to be for the hotel?  
The proposed conference center and resort program are intended to support a wide spectrum of 
significant groups, ranging from executive retreats and board meetings to larger corporate and 
social programs. The group ceiling will be demand-based, with the conference facilities are being 
designed to accommodate significant group activity. Final operating assumptions will be 
determined closer to opening.

To: Michelle Bonfils, Community Development Director    
From: Kevin Rayes, Housing Director  
Date: August 26, 2026   
Re: Sketch Plan PUD Ritz Carlton (Viewline) Land Use Application – Workforce Housing  
Dear Michelle:  
Thank you for the opportunity to weigh in on Sketch Plan PUD Ritz Carlton (Viewline) land use 
application – specifically the workforce housing component of the project.  
Project Summary:  
The application proposes up to 125 onsite deed-restricted workforce housing units. All studio 
units are contemplated; however, the applicant is open to adjusting unit sizes and configurations. 
Roughly half of the proposed units would be reserved for employees of the Ritz based in 
Snowmass, with the remaining half reserved for employees based at the St. Regis in Aspen. The 
application proposes 0.5 parking spaces per unit. The proposed parking ratio falls below the 
code’s minimum studio parking requirement of 1 off-street space per studio. The application also 
seeks a heigh variance of up to four feet to accommodate building sections of up to 48 feet in 
height (the land use code allows a maximum height of 44 ft.).  
Housing Department Comments:  
Unit Mix

applicant’s thoughtful approach to providing a

will be servicing the new development. As the

help increase overall density, they are generally

limited space provided by a studio. The highest turnover of units we experience in our inventory

occurs within studio apartments. A community benefit to the town is for the applicant to develop

Considerations to Maximize Livability   
Beyond unit type, the Housing Department recommends the applicant consider providing some 
combination of the following amenities to improve livability:  
• Kitchen quality, including the layout, size and types of appliances provided can make a big 
difference for unit quality. The Housing Department would like to see the following 
improvements provided in each unit:  
o Minimum 30-inch-wide oven, 4-burner stovetop,  
o Dishwasher  
o Minimum 20 cubic foot refrigerator with freezer 
o Minimum 24 sq. ft. of counter space and a minimum of 15 ft. cubic ft. of cabinet 
space   
• Washer dryer hookups 
• Provide adequate storage. This means a closet in each bedroom and a closet in the 
common area (i.e., living room). Additional storage outside of each unit is recommended.  
• Site porosity – Considering the relatively large number of units proposed, ensure there is 
sufficient “elbow room” on the site so tenants don’t feel crammed.  
o Provide usable private outdoor spaces such as porches, upper floor decks, etc. 
These spaces should be at least six ft. deep and 50 sq. ft. in area  
o Provide shared outdoor gathering areas  
Lease Restrictions  
To ensure the proposed housing units align with the Town of Snowmass workforce housing 
program, units should be leased on a long-term basis, meaning leases should be no shorter than 
one year. No short-term rentals are allowed. Units should be rented at rates consistent with Town 
of Snowmass housing. Generally, this means rental rates should align with those set forth at 
Mountain View II. For context, in 2026, a studio rents for $825/month, a one-bedroom rents at 
$1,180/month and a two-bedroom at $1,730/month.  
As stated throughout this memo, the Housing Department believes providing up to 125 onsite 
workforce housing units to be a notable community benefit. With that said, half of the units are 
set aside for employees based in Aspen. Pursuant to Housing regulations, residents who work 
outside of Snowmass Village – whether in Aspen or unincorporated Pitkin County – do not qualify 
for workforce housing in Snowmass. (Once a Snowmass-based employee is in Snowmass housing, 
they can work anywhere in Pitkin County but cannot get into the housing program unless they 
work in Snowmass at the time of qualifying.) It is understandable that the applicant seeks first 
priority/discretion to lease units to Ritz employees. Setting aside half the units for St. Regis 
employees is a bigger ask. The Housing Department can support the concept, with the condition 
that if any of the 125 units are unfilled for 45 days or more, the Housing Department has the next 
priority to fill said units with a qualified applicant from the housing waitlist. Additional

Parking  
As previously mentioned, the application proposes 0.5 off-street parking spaces per unit, which 
falls below the code minimum of one off-street space per unit. Considering the project’s central 
location in the village along with its proximity to transit, the feasibility of reducing parking is 
reasonable, subject to the conditions set forth by the Transportation Department (see 
Transportation Department Memo Dated 7/31/26). To complement Transportation’s comments, 
the Housing Department supports adding WeCycle stations to the site. Housing also proposes 
increasing the number of bike spaces from 61 to 125 so there is at least one bike space per unit. 
Increasing bicycle parking could be considered additional storage for units so that folks do not 
need to keep bikes in their units. Ideally the bike storage would be covered/enclosed and have 
access to electrical outlets. Many of our residents use e-bikes so providing a secure location 
where the bikes can be charged would be a major benefit. Lastly, the Housing Department 
supports including a car share service to the site.

Fractionals – Restrictions on those units: 
1. How far in the future will these be in the pool for hotel rooms? What is the booking window we can anticipate?

The fractional residences will be professionally managed by The Ritz-Carlton, and owners who elect to participate 
in the rental program will make their available inventor y accessible through the resort's reservation and revenue 
management systems. While participation is voluntary and owner  usage will influence availability, the intent is to 
make participating inventory available for advance sale in a manner consistent with hotel operations. 
 
2. Are there guarantees of how many will be in the rental pool at any given time?  
Participation in the rental program is voluntary. As such, it is not possible to guarantee how many fractional units 
will participate in the rental pool at any given time, as that decision rests with each individual owner following the 
purchase of their unit. Any developer-owned units, including unsold inventory, would be included in the rental pool. 
In addition, there are strong financial incentives for owners to participate, as rental income helps offset ownership 
costs, including operating expenses, association dues, and other carrying costs. 
 
3. Are there restrictions around how many days a unit can be occupied by owner vs in the rental pool?  
The fractional residences are proposed as 1/8 ownership interests, which naturally limits the amount of annual 
owner occupancy associated with each ownership interest. Owners may elect to participate in the rental program 
during periods when they are not using their allocated time.

5. 30% group business important and supported goal 
a. 30% is what we understand the current mix to be at Viewline. Keeping it in this range is ideal for 
our mix in town as well as overflow and larger group opportunity.  
Group business represents an important component of the operating strategy for the resort. The 
intent is to establish a stabilized group business mix of approximately 30% of overall occupied 
room nights, creating year-round demand, supporting shoulder-season occupancy, and generating 
significant food and beverage, wellness, recreation, and retail spending throughout the Town of 
Snowmass Village. This target is generally consistent with the property's historic role as a 
conference and group destination while positioning the resort to compete in the luxury group 
business market. 
b. What is the group ceiling anticipated to be in strategy? (ceiling is the highest number of rooms 
they’d commit to group at any given time) 
While detailed revenue management strategies will not be finalized until the year preceding 
opening, the intent is to maintain a balanced business mix that supports both transient and group 
demand. Group allocations will vary by season and demand period. However, the resort is not 
envisioned as a convention-focused property where group demand dominates occupancy.  
c. What is the ideal peak room night group anticipated to be for the hotel?  
The proposed conference center and resort program are intended to support a wide spectrum of 
significant groups, ranging from executive retreats and board meetings to larger corporate and 
social programs. The group ceiling will be demand-based, with the conference facilities are being 
designed to accommodate significant group activity. Final operating assumptions will be 
determined closer to opening. 
d. What will the consideration for group buyouts look like?  
The resort anticipates retaining the ability to consider full-resort and partial-resort buyouts, subject 
to seasonality, demand and residential ownership occupancy at the time of booking. Similar to 
other destination resorts, buyout opportunities would be evaluated on a case-by-case basis and 
priced to reflect the displacement of transient demand and the overall value of the program. 
Additional details regarding fractional ownership will be provided at Preliminary Plan.

The anticipated booking strategy is consistent with other destination resorts. Transient reservations are expected 
to be managed through a rolling 365-day booking window. For Group business, the resort will maintain the 
flexibility necessary to pursue programs that may book several years into the future, including destination 
meetings, corporate retreats, association programs, weddings, and other special events. 
 
3. Want to know true competitive sets for conference/meeting spaces – who are we going up against? Are there 
target markets we would like to see at Ritz/Snowmass that we intend to steal from competing destinations?  
The proposed facilities are intended to compete with luxury mountain-destination meeting venues throughout 
Colorado and the western United States. This includes existing Aspen-area resorts, Snowmass lodging 
properties, and other luxury mountain destinations that compete for corporate retreats, executive meetings, 
incentive programs, association meetings, and destination weddings.  
The objective is to position the Town of Snowmass Village and The Ritz-Carlton Snowmass to capture a greater 
share of luxury group business currently selecting competing mountain destinations. Competitive advantages 
include: 
 Ritz-Carlton service platform: Global sales reach and brand recognition among luxury meeting 
planners. 
 Purpose-built luxury group facilities: Purpose-built luxury group facilities are a key differentiator. The 
conference center is designed around two dedicated ballrooms, ten breakout rooms, generous pre-
function space, and direct access to outdoor event venues. This configuration provides planners with 
greater flexibility to host concurrent programs, separate general sessions from meals and receptions, 
accommodate multiple groups simultaneously, and incorporate indoor-outdoor experiences that are 
increasingly sought after in the meetings and incentive travel market.  
 Four mountains under one destination: Snowmass, Aspen Mountain, Aspen Highlands, and 
Buttermilk provide a broader range of skiing experiences than most single-resort destinations. 
 Aspen Snowmass brand recognition: Few mountain destinations have comparable global 
recognition and aspirational appeal.

4. What will make this a draw against other Ritz properties?  
The Ritz-Carlton brand is designed to reflect the culture, landscape, and character of each destination it serves. 
At the Town of Snowmass Village, that expression will be rooted in a ski-in/ski-out mountain environment, 
connection to the surrounding alpine landscape, integration with the Village, outdoor recreation, family 
experiences, and access to the broader Aspen Snowmass community. The intent is not to replicate another Ritz-
Carlton property, but rather to create a resort experience that is distinctly reflective of the Town of Snowmass 
Village.
