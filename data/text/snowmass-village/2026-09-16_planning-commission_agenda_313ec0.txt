Dear Michelle:  
Thank you for the opportunity to weigh in on Sketch Plan PUD Ritz Carlton (Viewline) land use 
application – specifically the workforce housing component of the project.  
Project Summary:  
The application proposes up to 125  onsite deed-restricted workforce housing units. All studio 
units are contemplated; however, the applicant is open to adjusting unit sizes and configurations. 
Roughly half of the proposed units would be reserved for employees of the Ritz based in 
Snowmass, with the remaining half reserved for employees based at the St. Regis in Aspen. The 
application proposes 0.5 parking spaces per unit. The proposed parking ratio falls below the 
code’s minimum studio parking requirement of 1 off-street space per studio. The application also 
seeks a heigh variance of up to four feet  to accommodate building sections of up to 48 feet in 
height (the land use code allows a maximum height of 44 ft.).  
Housing Department Comments:  
Unit Mix  
The Housing Department appreciates the 
applicant’s thoughtful approach to providing a 
robust amount of housing for the employees that 
will be servicing the new development. As the 
applicant team continues to refine the project, it 
might be helpful to consider the types of units 
that are in highest demand within the Town’s 
workforce housing program. As shown in Figure 
1, one-bedroom units are in highest demand. 
Most applicants who requested a studio also 
requested a one bedroom , so some are double 
counted between the two-unit types. (Both unit 
types are often selected to increase chances of 
getting a unit sooner .) Two-bedroom units are 
also in high demand as folks with children or 
elderly parents seek extra space . While studios 
help increase overall density, they are generally 
less desirable  for tenants. Many folks live i n 
studio units request transfers to one -bedrooms on a regular basis as they quickly outgrow the 
limited space provided by a studio. The highest turnover of units we experience in our inventory

occurs within studio apartments. A community benefit to the town is for the applicant to develop 
housing that residents enjoy and stay in for many years. Housing intended for seasonal or part-
time employees is not ideal and falls short of meeting the intent of the Town’s workforce housing 
program. For this reason, the Housing Department recommends minimizing the number of studio 
units provided and instead focus on one-bedroom and two-bedroom units.  
Considerations to Maximize Livability   
Beyond unit type, the Housing Department recommends the applicant consider providing some 
combination of the following amenities to improve livability:  
• Kitchen quality, including the layout, size and types of appliances provided can make a big 
difference for unit quality. The Housing Department would like to see the following 
improvements provided in each unit:  
o Minimum 30-inch-wide oven, 4-burner stovetop,  
o Dishwasher  
o Minimum 20 cubic foot refrigerator with freezer 
o Minimum 24 sq. ft. of counter space and a minimum of 15 ft. cubic ft. of cabinet 
space   
• Washer dryer hookups 
• Provide adequate storage. This means a closet in each bedroom and a closet in the 
common area (i.e., living room). Additional storage outside of each unit is recommended.  
• Site porosity – Considering the relatively large number of units proposed, ensure there is 
sufficient “elbow room” on the site so tenants don’t feel crammed.  
o Provide usable private outdoor spaces such as porches, upper floor decks, etc. 
These spaces should be at least six ft. deep and 50 sq. ft. in area  
o Provide shared outdoor gathering areas  
Lease Restrictions  
To ensure the proposed housing units align with the Town of Snowmass workforce housing 
program, units should be leased on a long-term basis, meaning leases should be no shorter than 
one year. No short-term rentals are allowed. Units should be rented at rates consistent with Town 
of Snowmass housing . Generally, this means rental rates should align with those set forth at 
Mountain View II. For context, in 2026, a studio rents for $825/month, a one-bedroom rents at 
$1,180/month and a two-bedroom at $1,730/month.  
As stated throughout this memo, the Housing Department believes providing up to 125 onsite 
workforce housing units to be a notable community benefit. With that said, half of the units are 
set aside for employees based in Aspen. Pursuant to Housing regulations, residents who work 
outside of Snowmass Village – whether in Aspen or unincorporated Pitkin County – do not qualify 
for workforce housing in Snowmass. (Once a Snowmass-based employee is in Snowmass housing, 
they can work anywhere in Pitkin County but cannot get into the housing program unless they 
work in Snowmass at the time of qualifying.)  It is understandable that the applicant seeks first 
priority/discretion to lease units to Ritz employees.  Setting aside half the units for St. Regis 
employees is a bigger ask. The Housing Department can support the concept, with the condition 
that if any of the 125 units are unfilled for 45 days or more, the Housing Department has the next 
priority to  fill said units with a qualified applicant from the housing waitlist. Additional

concept will be memorialized and enforced.  
Parking  
As previously mentioned, the application proposes 0.5 off -street parking spaces per unit, which 
falls below the code minimum of one off-street space per unit. Considering the project’s central 
location in the village  along with its proximity to transit, the feasibility of reducing parking is 
reasonable, subject to the conditions set forth by the Transportation Department  (see 
Transportation Department Memo Dated 7/31/26). To complement Transportation’s comments, 
the Housing Department supports adding WeCycle stations to the site.  Housing also propose s 
increasing the number of bike spaces from 61 to 125 so there is at least one bike space per unit. 
Increasing bicycle parking could be considered additional storage for units so that folks do not 
need to keep bikes in their unit s. Ideally the bike storage would be covered /enclosed and have 
access to electrical outlets. Many of our residents use e -bikes so providing a secure location 
where the bikes can be charged would be a major benefit. Lastly, the Housing Department 
supports including a car share service to the site.

Dear Michelle and Members of the Planning Commission , 
This letter provides some follow up information regarding the Workforce Housing portion of the proposed Elevated 
Returns Rit z Carlton Snowmass Sketch Plan proposal . We look forward to continuing the conversation on this topic in 
our meeting with you on September 16, 2026.  
The Wildwood Lodge has served as a lodge for many years, but has become functionally obsolete as a hotel. Dating 
back to previous ownership, units rented to overnight visitors were not used. Instead, visitors who had reserved a 
Wildwood room would check in at the Viewline and stay at the Viewline. This is indicative  of the real infrastructure 
issues with the building and a key driver for the redevelopment.  
When Elevated Returns purchased the Viewline and Wildwood properties , the Wildwood had not been in operation as 
a hotel because of the infrastructure issues. At the same time, Wildwood units have helped to bridge the gap for hotel 
employees who need housing to work in Snowmass and in Aspen . This type of use pre -dates the current ownership, 
and has continued today. Under current ownership, rather than leaving the hotel vacant, a mix of employees  from the 
Viewline, St. Regis Aspen, and neighboring businesses  are able to use the  hotel rooms through leases that range from 4 
to 12 months. This provides an important temporary benefit to the town's workforce by using an underutilized asset 
prior to redevelopment , and is one that we want to make permeant through the redevelopment.  
The employees staying at the hotel primarily walk and take transit to their jobs. This winter, only 10 people staying at the  
Wildwood had a personal vehicle. Everyone else used transit, biked, or walked.  
We are committed to providing significant workforce housing as part of the redevelopment to address the needs of 
the hotel as well as the community overall. For this reason, we have proposed over 57,000 square feet of workforce 
housing space in a building that steps up the hill with grade. These buildings include 3 to 4 stories of workforce housing 
for the development and community. We understand that this level of workforce housing also presents tradeoffs for 
the Commission and neighbors, and we look forwa rd to hearing how the Commission would balance those tradeoffs.
