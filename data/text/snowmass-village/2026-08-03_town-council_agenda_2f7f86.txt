These are items where all conditions or requirements have been agreed to or met prior to the time 
they come before the Council for final action. A Single Public Hearing will be opened for all items 
on the Consent Agenda. These items will be approved by a single motion of the Council. The 
Mayor will ask if there is anyone present who has objection to such procedure as to certain items. 
Members of the Council may also ask that an item be removed from the consent section and fully 
discussed. All items not removed from the consent section will then be approved. A member of the 
Council may vote no on specific items without asking that they be removed from the consent 
section for full discussion. Any item that is removed from the consent agenda will be placed on the 
regular agenda.

6. Public Hearings - Quasi-Judicial Hearings 
Public Hearings are the formal opportunity for the Town Council to LISTEN to the public regarding 
the issue at hand. For land use hearings the Council is required to act in a quasi-judicial capacity. 
When acting as a quasi-judicial body, the Council is acting in much the same capacity as a judge 
would act in a court of law. Under these circumstances, the judicial or quasi-judicial must limit its 
consideration to matters which are placed into evidence and are part of the public record. The 
Council must base their decision on the law and the evidence presented at the hearing.

Agenda Item: 
An Update from the City of Glenwood Springs on their South Bridge Project 
 
Presented by: 
Steve Boyd 
City Manager 
  
 
Background: 
The City of Glenwood Springs has long been working to build an additional access to 
State Highway 82 on the south side of the City.  The City completed design of the 
project and is now looking for regional support from other municipalities in order to help 
receive final appovals from CDOT and to complete this project.  The City of Glenwood 
Springs is asking Snowmass Village to execute the attached letter of support for this 
project.  Glenwood Spring City Manager Steve Boyd, Finance Director Yvette Gusad, 
and City Engineer Ryan Gordon will attend the meeting and answer questions regarding 
this proposed enhancement.   
 
  
 
Financial Impact: 
There is no direct fiscal impact to the Town of Snowmass Village for supporting this 
project. 
 
Applicability to Council Goals & Objectives: 
While this project is not specifically detailed in the Council Goal Statement, a regional 
approach to traffic managment has long been embraced by TOSV. 
 
Council Options: 
1) Direct Staff to fnalize the letter of support to be executed by the Mayor. 
2) Choose not to exectute the letter of support. 
 
Staff Recommendation: 
It is the recommendation of Town Staff that the Council, by motion;  
     Authorize the Mayor to execute a letter of support for the Glenwood Springs South 
Bridge Project

RE: Town of Snowmass Village supports the Glenwood Springs South Bridge

Dear Mayor Dehm, 
  
On behalf of the Snowmass Village Town Council, I am writing to express our strong support for 
the City of Glenwood Springs' construction of the South Bridge project. The City of Glenwood’s 
strong movement toward a new transportation and emergency egress for not only South Glenwood 
residents, but also the 4-Mile Road corridor, is prudent planning in the ever-changing environment 
that the valley lives and works in. 
  
Because the Town of Snowmass Village is up valley from Glenwood Springs, we have both a 
substantial work force that travels through Glenwood Springs in their daily commute and a large 
number of tourists from other parts of the state that will be positively impacted by this project.  In 
addition to the bridge providing a much-needed secondary egress for emergency purposes, adding 
capacity to Hwy 82 through the bottleneck of travel during rush hours will reduce commute time, 
improve the quality of life of local workers and provide a more reliable level-of-service for 
residents and visitors.  
 
For all of these reasons, Snowmass Village is supportive of the Glenwood Springs South Bridge 
Project and the substantial safety improvements made on Highway 82. 
  
Sincerely,

Agenda Item: 
Update on the "New Skittles" Design 
 
Presented by: 
Sam Guarino, Transportation Director 
  
 
Background: 
The replacement of the Sky Cab (Skittles) Gondola continues to move forward and is 
scheduled to occur next summer. Staff has been working closely with Aspen Skiing 
Company staff to develop the design for the new gondola terminals, cars, and 
surrounding areas. The new gondola will be of a high speed detachable design, 
significantly increasing the capacity over the current lift and alleviating peak rider loads. 
The new design also looks at improving rider/guest experience by beautifying both the 
upper and lower loading zones.  
 
Construction duration is still being finalized but is expected to occur during a significant 
portion of next summer (2027). Once a construction schedule is developed, the 
Transportation Department will react by designing a service to ensure those needing to 
travel between the Snowmass Mall and Base Village are accommodated and the 
Benedict Trail will be activated for those willing and able to utilize it. 
 
Financial Impact: 
This is an update on project progress. However, all expenses related to the project are 
being split evenly between the Town and the Skiing Company. 
 
Applicability to Council Goals & Objectives: 
Replacement of the Skittles Gondola is mentioned in the 2025 Council Goals and 
Objectives Statement both broadly and specifically. 
 
Strategic Initiatives for Snowmass Village: 
• Manage parking and transit to encourage efficient, effective, and sustainable mobility 
choices. 
• Continue to improve the multi-modal connections between Base Village, the Mall, and 
the Center.

Agenda Item: 
A Discussion Regarding a Proposed Personal Property Fire Mitigation Program 
 
Presented by: 
Elyse Hottel, Sustainability Coordinator 
Greg LeBlanc, Assistant Town Manager 
  
 
Background: 
For 2026, the Town secured roughly $1M toward wildfire mitigation efforts between our 
annual Town commitment and a Natural Disaster Mitigation Enterprise (NDME) grant, 
received through a collaboration with local nonprofit Roaring Fork Valley Wildfire 
Collaborative. Thus far, funding has been used to contract project management and 
HOA outreach with the Wildfire Collaborative and advanced wildfire modeling in 
conjunction with Vanderbilt University. Advanced wildfire modeling has allowed us to 
understand how fire will travel through the valley, and where vegetation to vegetation, 
vegetation to building, and building to building transfer poses the biggest threat. That 
information has been used to prioritize mitigation work on Town and HOA-owned 
common areas in Snowmass Village this year, which has begun this week. Per the 
direction of the Town Council, staff is  now looking to  develop and provide a community 
assistance program to help homeowners take steps to mitigate wildfire on personal 
property. 
 
2026 PILOT PROGRAM RECOMMENDATION: 
 
PURPOSE 
While Town resources are currently being directed toward mitigation efforts on high-
priority Town-owned parcels and HOA common areas,  the need for mitigation on private 
property is still recognized.  To this end. a pilot program is proposed for interested property 
owners that endeavor to undertake mitigation efforts on their own property.  A similar 
program has proven successful in Eagle County.  This proposal is molded after that 
program.   
The proposed 2026 pilot program, expected to be administered by the Wildfire 
Collaborative and summarized below, offers an incentive and pathway for homeowners to 
complete mitigation work in the high-impact 0-5 ft perimeter “Zone 0” around their homes, 
while at the same time answering homeowner questions: Where do I start? Who should I 
call? What work matters most? How do I know whether I am doing the right thing? This 
Page 28 of 45
program is intended to answer those questions and reduce barriers to action this year. 
OVERVIEW 
With only a few months left of the season and wildfire home assessments with Roaring 
Fork Fire and Rescue (RFFR) booked out, this pilot program is designed to be simple, 
straightforward and prescriptive. 
 
Step 1: Read the Program Rules and Conditions (Attachent A: Snowmass Pilot Program 
Rules & Conditions) and The First Five Feet checklist (Attachment B: Snowmass Village 
Pilot Program Checklist), adapted from the Insurance Institute for Business & Home 
Safety (IBHS) Wildfire Prepared HomeTM program, to understand the requirements of 
the program and tasks to be completed. 
 
Step 2: Per The First Five Feet checklist (Attachment B: Snowmass Village Pilot Program 
Checklist), clear combustible materials from Zone 0, the first five feet immediately 
surrounding the home, AND screen vents with ⅛” mesh to prevent embers from 
entering the house. This work can be completed by the homeowner, property manager, 
or a contractor. 
 
Step 3: Complete and submit the Homeowner Self Certification form online stating that the 
checklist is complete to the best of their knowledge and schedule a verification with 
the program administrator. The program administrator will then come to the home at 
the scheduled time to verify that ALL items on the checklist have been completed.  
 
Step 4: Upon verification, eligible homeowners will receive a reimbursement of up to 
$1,000 per home, while funding is available, as a way to recognize and support 
meaningful wildfire mitigation work on their property. 
 
A secondary appropriation of funds for HOAs, multi-family units, neighborhood groups, 
and/or groups of adjacent homeowners to explore larger, more strategic projects that align 
with Town wildfire priorities will be considered separately on a case-by-case basis up to 
$10,000 with a required 50% match per request. These projects must provide broader 
community benefit by addressing high risk shared landscapes, common areas, 
neighborhood edges, or other priority locations where coordinated mitigation can reduce 
wildfire risk at a large scale.  
 
Financial Impact: 
Staff is suggesting $30,000 in homeowner rebate funding for the 2026 pilot program, to 
be revisited next year, from the Wildfire Mitigation line of the 2026 Amended Budget. 
Natural Disaster Mitigation Enterprise (NDME) Grant funds cannot be used toward the 
rebate program, as those dollars are expressly for contracted mitigation work.

The First Five FeetZone 0 Wildfire Rebate — Checklist & Self-Certification
TOWN OF SNOWMASS VILLAGE × WILDFIRE COLLABORATIVE ROARING FORK VALLEY
During large wildfire events, homes may have to withstand ember storms and wildfire conditions
when firefighting resources are stretched thin or overwhelmed. Preparing the home and
surrounding property ahead of time can significantly reduce the risk of ignition.
This checklist is adapted from the IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home™ program and focuses on the
vulnerabilities that research shows most often lead to home ignition. It's written so you can
understand it and act on it yourself — no formal assessment from the fire department needed.
Get $1,000 for meeting this checklist
Do it yourself, or hand this page to a contractor — it's the whole scope. Sign and certify you've
completed the checklist, and email or text it to us. We confirm on-site, and you get your check.
Available to 30 homes, for new mitigation work only. Applications reviewed on a rolling basis. Apply
now at (website link). Program rules and conditions apply.
Who's eligible
Your property must be within the Town of Snowmass Village boundaries.
You must be the homeowner, property manager, or an authorized representative of
the property.
You must be willing and able to complete the checklist before winter.
This rebate is for new action. If your home already meets this checklist — for example, a
new build constructed to these standards — it isn't eligible; this program rewards
homeowners who take on the work now.
Town of Snowmass Village · Zone 0 Wildfire Rebate rfvwildfire.org
Page 31 of 45
What you'll do
Clear the ground within five feet of your home, trim back overhanging branches, and screen
your vents. That's the whole checklist, detailed below.
Where it applies
On your property, this checklist applies to the first five feet surrounding your home and
attached features (decks, patios, stairs) — measured outward from your exterior walls in
every direction, including underneath. Vent screening applies to every exterior vent on the
house: roof, attic, eave/soffit, foundation, and crawlspace.
When to complete it
Finish by December 31, 2026, while the ground is still free of snow so program staff can
confirm your work on-site.
Why the first five feet?
Most structure ignitions during a wildfire start when wind-blown embers — which can travel
over a mile ahead of the fire front — land in combustible material near the house: mulch
beds, woodpiles, firewood stacks, clogged gutters, and unscreened attic vents are some of
the most common ignition pathways.
A 2021 IBHS study looked at more than 71,000 homes in the path of a wildfire and found that
homes with a clear five-foot space around them were nearly twice as likely to survive as
homes with plants and clutter right up against the walls.
Removing combustible material from the first five feet and screening your vents eliminates
the fuel embers need to ignite. It's not the only step in protecting your home, but it's the
highest-leverage one — and the best place to start.

Do it yourself, or hire a contractor — either way, this checklist is the whole scope of work.

There is no dead combustible material (mulch, woodpiles, firewood, pine

Not sure? If it's combustible and within the first five feet, the safest choice is always to remove it.

Regularly remove all dead or dying vegetation, including grass, weeds, plants, shrubs, trees,

Groundcover:
Remove all combustible groundcover (wood or rubber mulch, pine needles, artificial turf,

Use only noncombustible materials — gravel, pavers, river rock, decomposed granite,
or steppingstones
Exceptions:
Shade screens that fully retract into noncombustible housing are permitted
Movable items (grills, trash bins, doormats, hoses, pet beds, toys, potted plants) are
allowed but must be moved indoors or at least 30 feet away during Red Flag Warnings or

Permanent items allowed: HVAC units, pool equipment, solar battery enclosures,

Why it matters: a 2021 IBHS study looked at more than 71,000 homes in the path of a wildfire and
found that homes with a clear five-foot space around them were nearly twice as likely to survive as
homes with plants and clutter right up against the walls.
2 Clear combustible vegetation
There is no highly combustible vegetation (junipers, spruce, evergreens,
resinous trees, scrub oak, or trees) or overhanging tree limbs within five feet of
the structure.
Not sure? If it's combustible and within the first five feet, the safest choice is always to remove it.
Full details, exceptions & rules
No junipers, spruce, evergreens, or other resinous trees within five feet of the house
No scrub oak within five feet of the house
No overhanging tree limbs within five feet of the house or over the roofline
Why it matters: IBHS research finds that resinous, needled evergreens act like little torches — their
resin ignites easily and burns hot enough to carry flame directly into siding, regardless of how
healthy or well-watered the plant is. Overhanging branches act as ladder fuel, carrying an ember-
driven ground fire up into a canopy or dropping burning material onto a roof.
A fully noncombustible first five feet is the ideal — and IBHS recommends removing other, lower-
risk vegetation there too. This program doesn't require that level of clearance, in recognition of the
cost and aesthetics involved. Clearing the high-risk items above is still the single best first step you
can take.

3 Screen your vents
All exterior vents (roof, attic, eave, foundation, and crawlspace) are covered with
1/8-inch corrosion-resistant metal mesh.
Full details, exceptions & rules
All vents must meet one of the following:
Install flame- and ember-resistant vents with proper sealing around them
Cover existing vents with 1/8-inch (3.18 mm) corrosion-resistant metal mesh (mesh rolls
are available at most hardware stores or online retailers)
Note: plumbing vents are excluded.
How to measure vent mesh:
Measure the clear opening between the metal mesh — openings must be 1/8 inch (3 mm)
or smaller to comply
Quick check: if a standard pencil tip or golf tee shaft easily passes through the
opening, the mesh is probably too large (1/8 inch is approximately the thickness of two
stacked pennies)
Applies to the following vent types:
Roof vents (ridge, off-ridge, through-roof)
Attic gable vents (on walls of home)
Eave/soffit vents (under roof overhangs)
Under-home vents (foundation or crawl space)
Dryer & exhaust vents (e.g., dryer, central vacuum):
Must have functional louvers or a flap to reduce ember entry and keep clear of lint
and debris
Do not install metal mesh on dryer vents — this can trap lint and create a fire hazard
Why it matters: a cleared yard doesn't help if embers can still blow straight into your attic or under-
floor space and ignite it from the inside — an open vent is one of the last remaining paths in even on
an otherwise hardened home.
