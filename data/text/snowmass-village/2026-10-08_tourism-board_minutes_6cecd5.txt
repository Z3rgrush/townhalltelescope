Brand Revolt — Brand Strategy Presentation & Board Member Roundtable for Feedback – 
Led by Camden Bernatz  
The board considered the brand's central audiences, year-round qualities, relationship to 
Aspen, and the tone and personality that should distinguish Snowmass.   
  
The Multi-Generational Family Experience  
Board members describe multi-generational families as defining the Snowmass audience 
and emphasized making the destination welcoming for every member of the family — the 
grandparents, the children, the grandchildren.   
  
Discussion:   
Deric Gunshor says the brand and future planning should account for grandparents and 
great-grandchildren, including activities that appeal to children while ensuring the older 
members of the family also feel comfortable and welcomed. He described the legacy of 
family memories as a key part of the experience.   
  
Mary Blankenau agrees that multi-generational families are central to what makes 
Snowmass so special.  
  
Wendy Harris says visitors who come to Snowmass as children often return with their own 
families to repeat familiar activities, visit the same places, and follow familiar itineraries.  
  
Tim McMahon describes families in which one person plans a trip for multiple generations. 
Highlighted parents' confidence in ski school and the ability for children to be independent 
within a safe Snowmass environment. Tim notes that family members may choose 
different activities throughout the day but still all be close enough to regroup easily at the 
end of an afternoon of mountain activities.  
  
Camden Bernatz asks whether families generally spend their time together or treat 
Snowmass as a base camp where members can "split and conquer."   
  
Wendy Harris says families may head out in the morning and not regroup until the end of 
the day due to the many avenues we have for each family member and their age range/ski 
ability.   
  
Discussion also noted Snowmass's advantages in family lodging on the mountain and 
direct access to outdoor activities in both winter and summer.   
  
Snowmass Core Values and Guest Experience  
Virginia McNellis follows up to ask board members, "which values or qualities guests might 
notice if they were missing"   
  
Discussion:  
Wendy Harris identifies familiar people and faces, a warm and welcoming atmosphere, an 
easy-going experience, consistent expectations year after year, and making things look 
"easy."  
  
Deric Gunshor notes that visitors have expectations for different levels of service. That 
legacy offerings can remain while facilities and experiences are modernized, citing 
Ullrhof's continued historical burger service, alongside upgraded building experiences.   
  
Deric Gunshor also discusses the perception that Snowmass is difficult to reach (travel 
wise) or too expensive. He said the time and effort of getting here is truly rewarded by the 
amazing experiences we offer year after year. It was also noted that the perception of 
Aspen's expensive atmosphere is not necessarily reflected in our comp set.   
  
Year-round brand qualities of Snowmass  
Camden Bernatz touches base on which aspects of Snowmass carry across summer and 
winter, and whether the destination's audiences and identity differ by season.   
  
Jami Downs says there are many events and activities throughout the year. "There's always 
something to do here."  
  
John Kenny and Mary Blankenau highlight Snowmass's immediate access to nature and 
the mountain experiences.  
  
Deric describes access to outdoor activities at many ability levels, from a family walk on 
Ditch Trail to climbing Snowmass Peak.  
  
Mary Blankenau notes that early January sees more international visitors and reiterates 
that the year-round safety we provide in Snowmass for families is a positive attribute that 
international families consider as well.  
  
Jami Down mentions that shoulder seasons also tend to draw the family crowds and that 
financial profiles seem to be similar in both winter and summer.  
  
Virginia McNellis discusses summer events that are not exclusively family-oriented, 
including running races, and beer tastings. That late summer, after children are back in 
school, is also a great period for younger adults without families to come and visit.  
  
Camden Bernatz distinguishes marketing strategy — which may change by audience, 
season, and call-to-action from brand strategy, which should provide a consistent 
Snowmass voice and feel no matter time of year or audience. Board members agree that 
"fun" is a good way to describe Snowmass year-round.  
  
Snowmass's relationship to Aspen and independent identity  
Board members were asked to discuss the value and limits of associating Snowmass with 
Aspen. And Virginia McNellis proposes a question about how one might describe 
Snowmass in a sentence if someone had never heard of it.   
  
Discussion:  
John Kenny describes Snowmass as a resort community with a low-key, outdoor-focused 
character, in contrast to Aspen's more visible social scene. He said visitors value access to 
Aspen without staying in the middle of it, and described Snowmass as a destination people 
discover.  
  
Virginia McNellis notes that the Aspen-Snowmass relationship can be a strength, 
describing Aspen as "the scene" and Snowmass as "the scenery".  
  
Wendy Harris identifies Snowmass as an uncrowded experience, short or non-existent 
lines, outdoor access, and free options such as summer parking.   
  
Deric Gunshor emphasizes the mountain experience and access to the outdoors for all 
and a mountain adventure portal  
  
Mary Blankenau describes Snowmass as a true mountain resort town vs a city structure 
where all visitors can participate   
  
Tim McMahon is in agreement with Mary, noting how different we are just situationally on 
the mountain with our lodging options hugging the mountainside that we play on.  
  
Joanna Mallory describes Snowmass as a place to relax and get away from the demands of 
daily life  
  
Jami Downs describes Snowmass's arrival experience up Brush Creek road as "magical" 
and that every day we are amazed by the place we call home  
  
John Kenny mentions that he does not see a need to separate Snowmass entirely from 
Aspen, pointing to the strong association between the two that's been around for so long.  
  
Travel access challenges are mentioned, noting that it's worth the effort to get here as the 
reward is every outdoor adventure at your fingertips on arrival, for every family member.   
  
Camden Bernatz ends the discussion by noting the brand direction, focusing less on the 
activities themselves — such as skiing, biking, hiking, which are not necessarily unique to 
Snowmass — and more on the tone and personality with which Snowmass presents those 
experiences. The goal is for the way Snowmass communicates to feel recognizable on its 
own.   
  
  
Business Outlook and Tourism Pacing  
Julia Theisen provided updates on occupancy, rates, revenue and booking pace. The 
September report showed occupancy was below initial expectations, while the rate and 
RevPAR had increased significantly. Winter bookings were described as soft with a low 
snow year hangover affecting performance. Sales and Marketing are doing everything they 
can to support our short-term book window trends. Mary Blankenau mentions some of the 
wait seems to be around what the snow will do and for the November elections to be over.

ASE Transportation and Airport Updates  
Discussion of the proposed transportation service between Eagle Airport and Brush Creek 
Park and Ride which will be provided by Wildpath Transportation. Scheduling and subsidy 
details remain under discussion.   
  
Discussion:  
Deric Gunshor said service is expected to start mid-winter and that rates were expected to 
be an affordable option. RFTA is putting operating budget dollars to support the Wildpath 
option. Wildpath would have 15 new vehicles in total and departure/arrival windows would 
be coordinated with flights and general transportation needs throughout the day.   
  
Julia Theisen notes that the preliminary amount was $50,000 per partner (Fly Aspen 
Snowmass) as a one-time supplemental subsidy, along with funding from RFTA and EOTC. 
Wildpath looking to continue operations after ASE closure without future subsidies. 
Subsidy would help launch the service and reduce prices for the start of the service only.  
  
Deric Gunshor mentions a direct DIA service was in the works ("Landline") but logistics and 
ability to implement it might stall out.   
  
Discussion on the many transportation options at different service and price levels, such 
as the 2x/day Bustang out of Glenwood Springs, Blazing Adventure transportation options, 
and other private company options available to the public beyond the planned Wildpath 
scheduled service.   
  
John Kenny mentioned the WSJ article (article attached to board packet -- 5. A. Other 
Matters Arising) and felt the story was well done and asked if there'd been any feedback 
received on the Tourism Team's side of things. Julia Theisen mentions that there has been 
little direct feedback and describes the response overall as neutral.
