4. Public Hearings - Quasi-Judicial Hearings 
Public Hearings are the formal opportunity for the Town Council to LISTEN to the public regarding 
the issue at hand. For land use hearings the Council is required to act in a quasi-judicial capacity. 
When acting as a quasi-judicial body, the Council is acting in much the same capacity as a judge 
would act in a court of law. Under these circumstances, the judicial or quasi-judicial must limit its 
consideration to matters which are placed into evidence and are part of the public record. The 
Council must base their decision on the law and the evidence presented at the hearing.

Background: 
The Application is available here: Ritz Carlton (Viewline Redevelopment) Sketch Plan 
PUD Application  
 
 
March 16, 2026, Town Council and the Planning Commission reviewed and commented 
on a Pre-Sketch application presentation by Elevated Snowmass, LLC (the “Applicant”) 
to redevelop the existing Viewline Resort (consisting of the Viewline Hotel, Snowmass 
Conference Center, and Wildwood Lodge) with a proposed 167 traditional lodge keys, 
61 fractional units, conference center with meeting rooms, commercial and retail space, 
125 on-site workforce housing units, and subgrade parking and centralized deliveries 
facilities. The proposed redevelopment requires a Major PUD Amendment and 
Rezoning (the “Application”). July 20, 2026 the Application advanced to Sketch Plan 
with a joint presentation to Town Council and the Planning Commission. 
 
Due to the size and scale of the Application, Staff and the Applicant agreed to present 
the Application materials in segments to the Planning Commission to allow for thorough 
review and discussion. The public hearing was opened August 5, 2026 with a site visit 
to review the elements of the proposal in relation to existing conditions. The public 
hearing was continued across the following dates –August 19 (lodge aspect), 
September 9 (workforce housing aspect), September 23 (conference center & 
commercial aspects), and October 7 (draft resolution).   At the October 7th meeting the 
Planning Commission adopted Resolution No. 4, Series 2026 with conditions, thereby 
recommending the Sketch Plan for approval at Town Council. 
In accordance with Section 16A-5-320 of the Land Use Code, the Sketch Plan review is 
an opportunity for the Town and the Applicant to engage in an exploratory discussion of 
a development proposal. Due to the size and scale of the Application, Staff and the 
Applicant have agreed to presentation of the Application materials in segments to allow 
Page 2 of 86
for thorough review and discussion. The proposed review schedule, subject to change, 
is as follows: 
• November 9, 2026 Site Visit  
• November 16, 2026 Project Overview 
• December 7, 2026 Review of Proposed Lodging, Commercial, and 
Conference Center  
• December 21, 2026 Review of Workforce Housing  
• January 4, 2027 Consideration of draft Resolution 
 
This is a tentative review schedule, which may be modified as directed by the Town 
Council. 
As part of the Sketch Plan application, the Applicant is requesting to rezone the entirety 
of the subject site to Mixed-Use-2 (MU-2) zoning which allows for all proposed uses in 
the Application. The Applicant is seeking variations from dimensional standards of the 
MU-2 zone district for specific areas of the Application including height variations, 
maximum FAR variation, parking requirements, and setback requirements. The 
Application would additionally expand the existing Silvertree PUD to encompass the 
entire site, including the conference center and Wildwood parcels. In consideration of 
the requested variations, the Applicant has proposed community benefit aspects in the 
form of 57,131 SF of proposed on-site workforce housing units and a centralized 
delivery facility.  
The overall proposed redevelopment replaces similar existing uses on the property, 
including replacement of lodging, commercial, conference center/event space, and 
residential uses, and uses accessory to the primary uses listed above. The proposed 
uses, mass and scale, are generally consistent with the goals and intent of the 
Comprehensive Plan, as conditionally approved in the adopted Planning Commission 
Resolution No. 4, Series of 2026. Certain aspects of the application elicited greater 
Planning Commission discussion. Those aspects, reflected in the adopted resolution 
conditions, include:  
         *  Acceptance of the general uses proposed, based on the requirement of detailed 
architectural plans appropriate of a Preliminary Plan application, providing further 
evaluation of the architecture and its relationship to adjacent uses.  
 
• General acceptance of the centralized delivery facility concept, with concern 
given to the architectural and engineering details, and the facility’s relationship to 
the streetscape along Elbert Lane.  
 
Page 3 of 86
 
• Further evaluation of the unit mix and architectural plans of the Workforce 
Housing, particularly Building B, with an exploration of architectural massing and 
articulation in the Preliminary Plan application that considers reduced impacts to 
adjacent properties including the Aspenwood Condominums. 
 
 
• Emphasis on safe, attractive and connected pedestrian access from adjacent 
properties, the numbered lots, and the Benedict Trail throughout the site as an 
important community character attribute of the site plan. 
 
 
• Emphasis on alternative modes of transportation to supplement the proposed 
subgrade parking as well as to generally support a reduction in the minimum 
parking requirements per Town Code. 
 
Further analysis and discussion of these and general application details will be brought 
forward through the review process through Staff and Applicant presentations. 
Town Council will review the Sketch Plan, and provide direction to the Applicant related 
to use, compatibility, mass and scale. Resolution No. 4, Series of 2026 (Att. A) details 
the Sketch Plan level conditions of approval established at Planning Commission. Staff 
would emphasize that conditions of approval at Sketch Plan provide direction for the 
Applicant in consideration of a future Preliminary Plan application. Approval at Sketch 
Plan provides conceptual direction for applicants based conceptual design, usage, 
mass and scale, and does not constitute a land use entitlement. 
 
 
Financial Impact: 
The Sketch Plan does not obligate the Town to approve future development(s) and 
therefore, there are no financial impacts associated with the land use process. A 
financial impact analysis is required as a part of the Preliminary Plan application.

Section One: General Findings. The Planning Commission hereby generally finds that: 
1. The Applicant submitted the Application for Sketch Plan Review and Rezoning of the 
Project in accordance with the provisions of the Municipal Code. The application 
provided the Minimum Contents required pursuant to Section 16A -5-320(c) of the 
Code and included written and graphic materials in sufficient detail to deem the 
Application complete. 
2. The Sketch Plan and Rezoning proposes land uses for the subject site which are 
consistent with the intent of the Snowmass Village 2018 Comprehensive Plan (the 
“Comprehensive Plan”) pursuant to Section 16A-5-320 of the Code.  
3. The Applicant is requesting a variance from the height restrictions associated with the 
proposed underlying MU -2 zone from 38 feet to 77 feet which pursuant to Section 
16A-5-310(c)(7)(a) requires a super majority vote (¾) by Town Council. 
4. The Applicant is request ing a variance from the maximum allowed FAR restrictions 
associated the proposed underlying MU-2 zone from 1.7 FAR to 2.53 FAR. 
5. The Applicant is requesting a variance from the required parking requirements , 
decreasing the required minimum to 252 spaces. 
6. The Applicant is requesting a variance from the required setbacks to zero setbacks in 
certain areas of the proposal. 
7. For consideration of the said variation requests (height, FAR, parking, setbacks), the 
Applicant suggests 57,131sf of  workforce housing and a centralized delivery facility  
which may be evaluated pursuant to the Community Purpose criteria in Municipal 
Code Section 16A-5-300(c)(6).  
8. The subject area is identified on the Comprehensive Plan’s Environmental Sensitiviety 
Map as being within the Brush Creek Impact Area. 
9. The Sketch Plan identifies land proposed for development or redevelopment that are 
not located within the Wildlife Sensitive Areas identified in the Comprehensive Plan. 
10. To the extent necessary for and pertinent to a Sketch Plan level of evaluation and 
review, the Application is able to sufficiently answer the questions identified in Section 
16A-5-300(c), General Restrictions, of the Development Code, subject to satisfying 
the applicable conditions contained within Section Three of this resolution. 
11. To the extent necessary for and pertinent to a Sketch Plan level of evaluation and 
review, the Application is generally consistent with the provisions of Section 16A -5-
310, Review Standards, of the Development Code, subject to satisfying the applicable 
conditions contained within Section Three of this resolution.

Parking and Traffic

stalls to associated uses within the development.

resident parking permits for the Town’s public parking lots pursuant to Section 8

9. To support reduced vehicular parking, the Applicant shall consider alternative modes 
of travel.  Alternative modes may include bikeshare, carshare, employee shuttles, 
and any other transportation demand management methods as appropriate. A 
reduction in required parking standards may only be considered with the provision of 
substantial alternative transportation. Additional bicycle parking and alternative

TRANSPORTATION DEPARTMENT MEMO 
To: Michelle Bonfils  
From: Sam Guarino 
CC: Kevin Kiernan 
Date: 7/31/26 
Re: Ritz-Carlton Sketch Plan PUD 
  
With the knowledge that this is a Sketch Plan PUD meeting the transportation 
department’s comments remain broad in detail. That said, there are many special 
concerns for this project from a transportation perspective to be considered.  
• Site Circulation 
o Elbert Lane Traffic Surveys 
 The Transportation Department has conducted a manual traffic 
survey on Elbert Lane under current conditions (7/9/26-7/15/26) 
which was utilized as basis for comments below. The Public Works 
Department is also conducting a traffic survey at the intersection of 
Elbert Lane and Carriage Way which will capture traffic on both 
roads. Collected data will be compiled in a separate report for 
planning department review.  
o Pedestrian 
 Current pedestrian usage of Elbert Lane peaks at over 1000 people 
per day during the summer at the intersection of Elbert Lane and 
Upper Carriage Way, far more than any other user group. This use 
does not capture those entering the property from Fall Lane, further 
east on Elbert Lane, or between Fall and Elbert. 
 The current pedestrian flow highlights the importance of the Elbert 
Lane Right of Way as a connection to the current Viewline Resort, 
as well as to the Snowmass Mall and Ski Area. The proposed 
sidewalks along the entrance to the Lodge, and pedestrian walking 
area, are both desirable infrastructure upgrades. Sidewalks should 
extend along the road the length of Elbert Lane to adequately 
protect the highest volume users of this road. If a sidewalk cannot 
be adequately sized on both sides of the road, an appropriate 
pedestrian crossing should be considered.

 Ensure than adequate ADA access exists along created walkways, 
or that alternative ADA pathways are identified.  
o Valet/Hotel Guests 
 Current conditions require Valet and Hotel Self-Parking to utilize the 
numbered lots. While exact numbers for current valet created trips 
are difficult to ascertain from a traffic survey, it is clear that both 
valet and self-park guests contribute heavily to Elbert Lane Traffic. 
Overall bi-directional personal vehicular traffic peaks at nearly 400 
vehicles per day and Viewline shuttles at over 120 vehicles per day. 
 Valet service often consists of a guest vehicle arriving, a valet 
driven hotel vehicle escorting guest vehicle to the numbered lots, 
and then that valet vehicle returning. Currently each arrival can 
amount to as many as 4 trips along Elbert Lane and Carriage Way 
(and 4 more for departure). Valet being kept on site as shown in the 
Sketch Plan should be a significant improvement to circulation both 
internal and external to the property.  
 If Self-Parking guests are to utilize the numbered parking lots, it will 
be important for the Ritz-Carlton to continue to sell guest parking 
permits for these guests as they do now. The Town does not have 
the capacity to manage payment for self-parking guests. 
o Skier pick-up/drop-off 
 The current Elbert Lane Right of Way allows for skier pick-up and 
drop-off for all lodges and the public, and ADA elevator access. 
While it is difficult to estimate how many private or unmarked 
vehicles are utilizing Elbert Lane for this purpose, during the 
Summer 2026 traffic survey period over 30 marked vehicles from 
other lodges were observed in a day, a number that is expected to 
be much higher during Winter ski season. Finding a location, 
whether it be at the redesigned entrance to the Ritz-Carlton or 
along the existing Elbert Lane Right of Way, to accommodate these 
trips will be important moving forward with the PUD. If this location 
for pick-up and drop-off is removed, it will likely fall to inadequately 
designed infrastructure in other areas of the Town to accommodate 
the use. 
 While not a current use, it would be best to plan for Snowmass 
Village Transit vehicles to be able to utilize any future pick-up/drop-
off sites.  
o Retail/Spa/Restaurant Access 
 The trip generation study from Kimley-Horn discussed high turnover 
restaurant and strip retail trips. There should be some evaluation on 
how these trips affect site circulation. For example, would these

Group business represents an important component of the operating strategy for the resort. The

While detailed revenue management strategies will not be finalized until the year preceding

The proposed conference center and resort program are intended to support a wide spectrum of

SUBJECT: Ritz-Carlton Redevelopment Proposal 
 
Recommendation 
 
We respectfully urge the Planning Commission not to advance or further consider the Ritz-
Carlton proposal as currently proposed and revised, due to its inadequate parking 
capacity.  To advance the current proposal would be to ﬂaunt the town code, which calls for 
approximately twice the on-site parking capacity for housing of that size and nature. 
 
A Trend Toward Inadequate Parking 
 
Over the years, the Town has repeatedly and signiﬁcantly lowered its parking space 
requirements for workforce housing, from code-required 1.5 parking spaces per bedroom, 
down to 1.5 spaces per housing unit, and then down to 1.3 spaces per unit in the current 
plan for the Town Hall Draw site’s 79 units. 
 
In addition, one of the designs being shown for the contemplated Mall Transit Center has 
the Center and roadway displacing the 56 parking spaces in Lot Number 6.  This would 
eliminate perhaps the busiest of all the Numbered Lots.  And the current design for 
renovation of the Snowmass Town Center –  although on hold –  contains a 40% increase in 
commercial and retail space, with virtually no increase in parking spaces.  More positively, 
recognizing the near universality of workforce vehicle ownership, the SHOP project is 
planning 36 parking spaces for its 30 units and 36 adult residents.  But the Daly Lane 
proposal currently before Town Council includes a mere 8 on-site parking spaces for 42 
residences - - far below code and original Town projections. 
 
Work Proximity Does Not Eliminate Vehicles 
 
Proximity of workforce residents to jobs and to public transportation does not eliminate the 
need or desire of residents to have a vehicle, even if they do not drive to work.  Beyond the 
need to meet personal living needs of all kinds in a non-urban environment, our mountain 
Town and destination resort Valley contains a broad spectrum of sports, entertainment, 
shopping, healthcare, and other activities – all reachable by private vehicle.  As a result, 
Page 44 of 86
 2 
people will own vehicles and use them other than for work.  It is not a matter of improving 
or adding to the transit system.  Even if all residents of the Snowmass LLC’s project were 
to commute to work without using their cars, it is safe to assume that virtually all the 
units would require residential parking.  The important factor here is not the driving, 
but whether and where the housing occupants will own and park their vehicles.  
 
It would be useful to know from a fact base for current Town workforce residential parking: 
- How many vehicles are owned by workforce residents, and in how many units? 
- How many residents in workforce units own no vehicles? 
 
Assignment of Numbered Lots Not the Answer 
 
As the Town has grown and Town-owned land has ﬁlled in over the decades with new 
hospitality buildings, residences of all kinds, infrastructure, and amenities, the numbered 
lots have become an increasingly critical and well-used part of the Carriage Way artery, 
especially proximate to the Mall and the slopes.  They are accessible all year to residents 
and others at nominal cost.  Vehicle access is open and convenient, and permits and 
parking times are enforced.  The lots occasionally prove inadequate for popular Town 
events and top powder days, when they ﬁll up immediately. 
 
Dependance on assigned spaces in the Numbered Lots for new residential housing – 
including workforce housing and the Ritz-Carlton and Daly Lane projects – should not be 
allowed, as it would be counter to the long history and increasing importance of providing 
temporary community parking for all Town residents, visitors, and employees, as they move 
about the Town. 
 
Making an exception for the Ritz-Carlton project would risk setting an unwanted precedent 
for usurping the Numbered Lots that serve the broad community. 
 
Summary 
 
The Town coded requirement for on-site workforce housing was clear and available to the 
developer prior to his acquiring the property last year.  Whether the Commission and Town 
Council intend to apply, change, or ignore current provisions in the Code for 
restricted/workforce parking, we would ask that the currently proposed Ritz-Carlton project 
be denied or postponed until and unless appropriate on-site parking is included.

That omission is significant. In responding to §16A-4-340's direction concerning sight lines as 
viewed from neighboring developments, the application discusses the views the proposed 
buildings themselves will capture toward the ski areas and down valley. That does not answer 
the corresponding question of how the proposed buildings will be experienced from the 
neighboring development. 
Nor is the proposal simply rebuilding VVildwood in its existing configuration. Staff notes that 
Workforce Housing Buildings A and B are oriented parallel to Elbert and Fall Lanes, while the 
existing VVildwood buildings are oriented perpendicular to those streets. Building heights, 
setbacks and the distribution of mass also change. Staff has separately identified issues 
concerning proposed five-foot setbacks, the need for at least ten feet in certain locations, and 
an apparent Building B encroachment requiring further review.

This change in orientation makes §16A-4-340(c)(2)(b) particularly important. From 
Aspenwood and Fall Lane, the current drawings raise a legitimate question whether Buildings A 
and B, with their longer frontages running parallel to the lane, create the "wall" or "row" effect 
the guideline specifically directs projects to avoid. The applicant may believe that setbacks, 
stepping, roof forms and architectural articulation sufficiently break up that mass. But without a 
proposed-condition visualization from Aspenwood, the Commission cannot directly evaluate 
whether those measures actually accomplish that objective from the neighboring property most 
affected. 
Exhibit A illustrates why a comparison of site -wide maximum building heights alone does 
not adequately describe this change from Aspenwood's perspective. Based on 
approximate field measurements taken from Fall Lane street level, the peak of the existing 
Wildwood  roofline directly across from Aspenwood Building K rises approximately 25 feet 
above the street, with other portions of the roofline along Fall Lane approximately 20 feet 
above street level. Additionally, the VVildwood's pool area provides significant visual relief. 
We recognize that these field measurements are not the Town's Code-defined method of 
calculating building height, and we do not present them as such. They instead document the 
existing physical relationship among Aspenwood, Fall Lane and the Wildwood roofline. A 
comparison between an approximately 45-foot existing maximum somewhere on the Wildwood 
site and a proposed 48-foot maximum therefore does not, by itself, demonstrate what the 
proposed buildings will look or feel like from Aspenwood. 
We also recognize an important fact favorable to the proposal: the proposed workforce-housing 
buildings contain less finished floor area than the existing Wildwood buildings. But less floor 
area does not necessarily mean less visual mass or less impact on an immediately 
adjacent property. Height, orientation, configuration, building length, placement, grade and 
separation all affect that relationship, which is precisely why the Code evaluates more than 
square footage. 
Before the Commission recommends the requested exceptional height relief, we 
respectfully ask that the applicant provide accurate proposed -condition visualizations 
from representative Aspenwood viewpoints, including viewpoints corresponding as 
closely as practicable to those shown in Exhibit A. We also request cross-sections through 
Aspenwood, Fall Lane and the proposed buildings showing existing and proposed grades, 
building heights, setbacks and sight lines. If staff considers a three-dimensional massing 
comparison, story poles or another accepted method more appropriate, that could accomplish 
the same purpose. 
This is not a request for Preliminary Plan-level architectural detail. It is a request for enough 
information to evaluate the fundamental height, massing and placement now before the 
Commission.

Redevelopment is encouraged, but compatibility and community character 
remain part of the review 
The 2018 Comprehensive Plan clearly supports revitalization of West Village, and Aspenwood 
does not dispute that direction. But the Plan's language is balanced: 
"The scale of any re-development should complement the existing scale but could 
be increased in size/height in limited strategic locations." 
The Plan also calls for an "intimate village character" in West Village and explains elsewhere 
that scale involves the relationship of buildings not only to occupants and pedestrians, but also 
to "the other buildings around them." 
Recent Town consideration of potential workforce housing on the Upper Numbered Lots 
provides additional relevant planning context. In that process, Town Council specifically 
identified "community/neighborhood character" and "view planes/massing" among the 
matters future development should address. Those same considerations are directly relevant to 
the placement and form of the workforce-housing buildings proposed here. 
The historical record reinforces, rather than controls, this analysis. Earlier Snowmass plans 
repeatedly emphasized human scale, mountain character and accommodating redevelopment 
while protecting community character. When the Town approved the 2012 Wildwood renovation, 
it found the proposal acceptable in part because overall mass and scale remained the same and 
adverse impacts on surrounding properties were insubstantial; the approval also stated that 
future development improvements at Wildwood would be reviewed under the Town's PUD 
procedures and standards. 
We do not suggest that the 2012 approval or older planning documents legally freeze Wildwood 
in its existing configuration. They do demonstrate that evaluating Wildwood in relation to its 
surroundings is neither a new concept nor an objection created in response to this application. 
These issues should be addressed at Sketch Plan, not after the basic development form 
is established 
We understand that the Sketch Plan is conceptual and that many architectural details 
appropriately belong at the Preliminary Plan. But staff's September 2 memorandum explains 
that Sketch Plan materials should show building mass, scale and height conceptually in 
relation to the surrounding area, and the Commission has already requested additional 
exhibits to better understand those issues. 
The relationship between the Wildwood replacement buildings and Aspenwood is not a 
minor architectural detail. Building location, orientation, maximum height, setbacks and 
overall massing are fundamental components of the development concept. If those relationships 
are not adequately understood until the Preliminary Plan, the Commission will have provided 
Page 49 of 86
Sketch Plan direction without seeing the proposed concept from the neighboring residential 
property most directly affected by it. 
We therefore respectfully ask the Planning Commission, before recommending the requested 
height and dimensional relief, to: 
1. Require accurate existing -and-proposed visualizations from representative 
Aspenwood viewpoints, including viewpoints corresponding to Exhibit A, and 
cross -sections sufficient to evaluate height, massing, setbacks, grade and sight 
lines; 
2. Evaluate Workforce Housing Buildings A and B specifically for compatibility with 
Aspenwood and surrounding development in height, mass, scale, orientation and 
configuration, including whether their siting and articulation avoid the "wall" or 
"row" effect identified in §16A-4-340(c)(2)(b); 
3. Determine whether the proposed height increases and building placement are 
appropriate in these particular locations in light of the Comprehensive Plan's 
direction that redevelopment complement existing scale and that increased height 
occur in limited strategic locations; and 
4. Provide clear direction for the Preliminary Plan regarding setbacks, building 
placement, pedestrian relationships and mitigation of adverse impacts on existing 
neighboring development. 
Aspenwood is not asking the Commission to prevent Wldwood's redevelopment or preserve the 
property unchanged. We want to see a successful project that improves West Village for 
residents, employees and visitors. 
But discretionary height and dimensional relief should follow, not precede, a record 
sufficient to demonstrate that the resulting buildings satisfy the Town's standards for 
their relationship to neighboring properties. The present record, without meaningful 
proposed -condition analysis from Aspenwood, does not yet provide that information. 
We believe the additional analysis requested above is reasonable, achievable and necessary 
before the Commission makes those findings.
