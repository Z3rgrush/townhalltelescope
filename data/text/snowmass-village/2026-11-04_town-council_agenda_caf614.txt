 7.A. Ordinance No. 15, 2026 1st Reading - Cavern Springs Budget Appropriation 
Housing Director Kevin Reyes explained that the ordinance appropriates a $1 million 
Town contribution toward the residents' purchase of the Cavern Springs Mobile Home 
Park in unincorporated Garfield County, near Glenwood Springs. The park has 98 
homes and about 300 residents. The asking price is $24 million, and residents and 
donors have raised about $13 million. The remaining balance would be financed by 
lender Thistle Rock, with closing expected in late October. West Mountain Regional 
Housing Coalition will manage the deed restriction ensuring affordability and full-time 
occupancy. 
 
Town Attorney Jeff Conklin stated that the restrictive covenant has been improved over 
the prior mobile home park covenants with clearer enforcement mechanisms. The Town 
would be the fourth-priority lender in the lending stack. An intergovernmental agreement 
(IGA) and the form of the covenant will come before Council at second reading. Council 
members expressed appreciation for the work.  
 
No public comment. 
 
Council Member Gustafson motioned to approve Ordinance No. 15, 2026 on first 
reading. Council Member Marolt seconded. All in favor. Council Member Fridstein 
absent. 
 
Second reading is scheduled for October 5, 2026, on the consent agenda.

 6.A. Ordinance No. 14, 2026  - 2nd Reading - regarding SHOP PUD 
Community Development Director Michelle Bonfils Thibeault presented the second 
reading of Ordinance No. 14, 2026. The ordinance is a major amendment to the final 
PUD for Divide Lot 44 to permit 20 units of workforce housing.  
 
Staff reviewed the following changes since first reading: 
●    The annual contribution to the Town's on-demand transit service was reduced to 
$42,000 (draft condition 33A). 
●    The ordinance states that approval is not precedent for unrelated PUD amendments 
(miscellaneous condition F). 
●    Aspen One would have employee priority for units, with vacancy limited while it 
actively recruits. 
 
Town Attorney Jeff Conklin recommended a development agreement condition allowing 
a reduction in, or substitute for, the letter of credit. Any change would require approval 
by the Town Attorney and Town Manager, and then by Council by resolution. The letters 
of credit would remain in place until substantial completion unless Council approves 
otherwise. Council Member Fridstein had earlier worried about releasing security too 
aggressively. After the applicant clarified that no release is built into the current default, 
Council accepted the flexibility. 
 
Heather Henry, for the applicant, formally supported the Divide HOA's request to extend 
the vacancy period from 90 to 180 days before units go to the next priority pool. She 
described it as a "backup to a backup" given high demand. Bill Kyriagis, counsel for the 
Divide, said Ski Co is better positioned than other tenants to manage noise from its own 
employees. The longer period also supports the priority preferences negotiated between 
the parties. Council Member Fridstein said he was comfortable with the change after 
hearing this. 
 
Public comment:  
Mr. Kyriagis confirmed that the Divide HOA and Ski Co had reached a compromise and 
executed a restrictive covenant, and that the HOA now supports approval. He asked 
that the updated landscape plan with additional screening trees be included in the final 
documents. He also asked that construction hours under the construction management 
plan align with those the Divide applies to its own lots.  
John Nolan, a neighbor about 25 yards from the site, asked that the Pitkin County 
objective noise standard be a condition of approval. 
 
Mr. Conklin and Town Manager Clint Kinney advised against a single-property 
condition. They explained that the Town's noise ordinance uses an officer-discretion 
Page 15 of 205
reasonableness standard informed by the Colorado Noise Abatement Act. A land use 
condition would also be enforced through the planning department rather than by 
immediate citation. Ms. Henry said Ski Co was agnostic because it had already agreed 
to the standard in its covenant. After discussion, Council consensus was not to add the 
condition. Council agreed to schedule a future agenda item on a town-wide review of 
the noise ordinance, ideally before construction begins. 
 
Motion to approve Ordinance No. 14, 2026 on second reading, including (1) an

Section 6.7 of the Town Charter states:  
"The Town Manager, with the approval of the Council, shall appoint or remove a Town 
Treasurer. The Town Treasurer shall be responsible for the receipt and disbursement of all 
funds of the Town and such other duties and responsibilities as may from time to time be 
established by the Council by ordinance." 
 
The Town Charter goes on to state: 
"Neither the Council, its members, the Mayor, nor any Council committee shall dictate the 
appointment of any person to office by the Town Manager except as otherwise provided in 
this Charter, or in any way interfere with the Town Manager to prevent him from exercising 
his judgment in the appointment or employment of employees in the administrative service. 
Except for the purpose of inquiry, the Council, its members, the Mayor and any Council 
committee shall deal with the administrative service solely through the Town Manager." 
 
The term Town Treasurer is an antiquated term and is not used by TOSV any longer. 
 No job description exists for the position and no one has that title.  The responsibilities 
outlined in the Charter, and, of course, many other financial responsibilities, fall to the 
Town Finance Director.  As such, and in an attempt to comply with this Charter 
provision, the Town Council is being asked to approve the appointment of Devin 
Billingsley to the Finance Director position. Billingsley was selected after an extensive 
recruitment and hiring process, including skills and proficiency tests and multiple 
interview panels.  His first day will be November 9, 2026.

Director, Marianne Rakowski has decided to retire later this year.  This appointment will 
allow Billingsley and Rakowski to overlap for a few weeks prior to Rakowski's departure.
