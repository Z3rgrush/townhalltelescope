5. IMPACT Accelerator Grant Update 
The IMPACT Accelerator Grant was awarded by the Colorado Energy Office to a cohort

of ten communities with the City of Aspen holding the grant and releasing the funds to 
the participants through an IGA.  There are two main parts to the grant. 
1.  Code Alignment and Update portion of the grant for the ten communities will have a

2. Rebate funds will be accessible once the code update portion is underway, hopefully

multifamily & affordable housing funding. 
6. Items for Discussion 
 6.A. 08-03-26 Work Session Results 
Recap of the Wildfire Mitigation Rebate Program item for discussion from the Town 
Council meeting on 08-03-26. 
This pilot program for 2026 focuses on rebates for the 0-5 ft zone from a permanent 
building structure with $30,000 allocated by the town for $1000 rebates for individual 
homeowners.  Since Roaring Fork Fire Rescue doesn't have the capacity to perform fire 
risk assessments the program will be based on self verification and checklists of tasks 
to be completed.  The rebate forms will be available online by the end of August and 
RFV Wildfire Collaborative will administer the program.  Homeowners who are awarded 
a rebate will likely be eligible to apply for up to three rebates if /when the program has 
future allocations.

9. Non-Agenda Items 
Discussion on the spraying of trails and lack of signage about spraying in Snowmass 
was introduced by resident Jill Carnavale.  She was at the April 2026 EAB meeting that 
discussed the noxious weed program in Carbondale and would like to know what the 
town's spraying strategy is going forward.  Not having seen any signage this season, Jill 
is concerned and would like to know where and when the town has sprayed and if there 
will be further spraying. The use of spraying by other entities is also troubling. 
Greg clarified some issues: The town hasn't sprayed for weeks, but property 
Page 3 of 4
owners/land custodians where trails go through do often spray including the Ski 
Company, the Snowmass Club golf course and HOA's for example.  The state's 
preemption on regulating spraying by private entities is still in effect, so there is no 
regulatory mechanism to restrict spraying by them.  As for town spraying, there is 
always signage, the work is done by a licensed distributor applicator, and it's only within 
a certain distance of a trail. 
In order to address this issue as a board, a key factor is leadership - to research and 
determine a process forward.  Morgan and Jill will meet to discuss options and Greg will 
consult with Andy Worline, head of Parks, Rec and Trails and get back to the board with 
answers to questions about the Town's spraying schedule this season. 
 
Darrell offered to reach out to the Executive Director of the Aspen Community 
Foundation to explore the possibility of developing a program to assist Snowmass 
households that may have difficulty making larger investments in their homes, such as 
those required for wildfire mitigation or CORE projects. The concept could involve using 
Donor Advised Funds to provide loans secured by the equity homeowners have in their 
properties.
