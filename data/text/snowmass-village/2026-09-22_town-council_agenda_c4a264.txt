 3.A. An Update from the City of Glenwood Springs on their South Bridge Project 
     Representatives from the City of Glenwood Springs — including City Manager Steve 
Boyd, City Engineer Ryan Gordon, Finance Director Yvette Gustad, and Public Works 
Page 5 of 84
Director Matt Langhorst — presented an update on the Southbridge Project. The 
project's primary goals are to create a secondary egress route from southern Glenwood 
Springs for fire evacuation and to address longstanding safety deficiencies on Highway 
82 near the Buffalo Valley area. Additional project benefits include a new 10-foot-wide 
concrete trail connecting to the Rio Grande Trail and projected reduction of 
approximately 3,000 vehicles per day from the Highway 82 corridor. 
 
     Since 2023, the City undertook a significant redesign that eliminated a costly tunnel 
beneath the airport runway, replaced a specialized concrete segmental bridge design 
with a standard steel girder bridge, and removed extensive retaining walls. These 
changes are estimated to have reduced costs by more than $30 million in today's 
dollars. The redesigned alignment incorporates extensive traffic calming measures, 
including a 25 mph curve and new roundabouts, consistent with the project's stated 
purpose of fire evacuation rather than serving as a general bypass. 
 
     The project is currently at 100% design. A critical $49.6 million federal Rural Surface 
Transportation Program grant must be obligated by the end of September 2026, after 
which a bid date of approximately October 2026 is anticipated, with construction 
beginning in early 2027 and a two-year construction period projected. The total 
estimated project cost is $77.5 million, against $72.5 million in secured funding from 
federal grants, congressional earmarks, CDOT safety funds, and RAFTA contributions, 
leaving a current shortfall of approximately $5 million. 
 
     The presenters noted two active headwinds: a regulatory dispute with Garfield 
County over whether the project is subject to a 10-41 or Location and Extent (L&E) 
review process, and philosophical opposition from the Aspen Valley Land Trust (AVLT) 
regarding condemnation of a small corner of a conservation easement. Inflationary risk 
at bid time was also acknowledged as a concern. 
 
     The delegation requested a letter of support from the Town Council to assist with 
stakeholder relations, including with Garfield County and AVLT. No financial contribution 
was requested at this time, though a modest symbolic ask from regional partners may 
be forthcoming pending bid results. 
 
     Council Member Fridstein moved to authorize the Mayor to execute a letter of 
support for the Southbridge Project on behalf of the Town of Snowmass Village. Council 
Member Marolt seconded the motion. All in favor.

 8.A. Update on the "New Skittles" Design 
     SkiCo’s Mak Keeling and Transportation Director Sam Guarino presented a design 
update on the replacement gondola, known locally as the "Skittles." The replacement lift 
will follow the same alignment as the existing gondola, with the number of towers on 
Fanny Hill reduced to improve the skiing experience. Capacity will increase from 510 to 
1,000 persons per hour, and the new six-person detachable gondola cabins will feature 
expanded glazing, interior ski/equipment retention bars to replace scratched plexiglass, 
and ADA-accessible bench configurations. 
 
     At the upper (mall) terminal, the project proposes a permanent ADA-accessible ramp 
to the mall, a snow-melted electric walkway, improved staircase, and a more finished 
landscaped area, replacing the existing temporary metal ramp and gravel surface. The 
platform elevation at the upper terminal will be approximately nine inches higher than at 
present due to reuse of the existing structural foundation. At the lower (Base Village) 
terminal, modifications are minimal and the platform elevation will remain unchanged. 
The project is on schedule for construction beginning August 2027, with a targeted 
opening by Thanksgiving 2027. During the construction period, transit and the Benedict 
Trail will be activated to provide alternative connections between Base Village and the 
mall. A communications plan was confirmed as a priority to inform visitors and residents 
of the gondola's temporary closure. 
 
     Regarding cabin branding, Aspen Skiing Company's internal marketing team is 
developing color scheme and logo options, with recommendations expected by mid-
September. The replacement gondola will continue the multi-color "Skittles" motif with 
12 continuous cabins. The disposition of the existing cabins — which are owned by 
General Improvement District No. 1 — was noted as a matter to be resolved; potential 
interest from other ski areas was acknowledged, and staff and Keeling agreed to solicit 
offers and report back. 
  
 8.B. A Discussion Regarding a Proposed Personal Property Fire Mitigation 
Program 
Page 8 of 84
     Assistant Town Manager Greg LeBlanc, Emergency Management/Wildfire 
Coordinator Elyse Hottel, and Nick Sparling (Director of Community Resilience, Roaring 
Fork Valley Wildfire Collaborative) presented a proposed pilot rebate program to 
incentivize individual homeowners to undertake wildfire mitigation on their private 
properties. 
 
     The pilot is designed as a simplified alternative to the existing valley-wide program 
administered by the Wildfire Collaborative, which normally requires a formal fire 
department assessment prior to reimbursement. Because Roaring Fork Fire Rescue is 
currently overwhelmed with assessment demand, the pilot would instead utilize a 
prescriptive self-certification checklist focused on the two most scientifically impactful 
mitigations: clearing all combustible material within the first five feet of the home (Zone 
0), and screening vents. A homeowner who completes the checklist, with photo 
documentation verified by Collaborative staff, would receive a $1,000 rebate. The 
program is proposed to be funded at $30,000 from the Town's existing wildfire mitigation 
budget line, accommodating approximately 30 households in the 2026 season. 
 
     Council discussion focused on equity concerns — noting that homes requiring 
extensive and costly tree removal may be unable to qualify, particularly among long-
term local residents — as well as the importance of community awareness as an 
outcome in itself. Council Member Fridstein emphasized that the program's primary 
value is motivating action and building community awareness, and that the Town cannot 
be expected to subsidize the full mitigation cost for all properties. Council broadly 
supported the pilot moving forward this season, with a more comprehensive program to 
be developed for 2027 with fire district involvement. 
 
     EAB member Darrell Smith recommended two amendments to the checklist criteria: 
(1) that the checklist require removal of all woody shrubs (not merely recommend it), 
consistent with standards adopted by communities such as Vail; and (2) that wood 
fencing abutting structures be explicitly prohibited within the five-foot zone, as it can act 
as an ignition fuse (citing lessons from the Marshall Fire in Louisville, Colorado). Council 
Member Fridstein suggested incorporating these two amendments. The question of 
wood decks was raised, but it was agreed they would not be added as a disqualifying 
criterion for the pilot, though the associated fire risk would be included in public 
communications. Staff confirmed that the program would proceed with these 
modifications and that outreach to HOAs was already underway on a separate track. 
Staff was directed to implement signage requirements immediately and to explore grant 
funding for program expansion in future budget cycles.

 3.A. Roaring Fork Transportation Coalition Update 
     Representatives of the Roaring Fork Transportation Coalition for the 21st Century — 
George Newman (former Pitkin County Commissioner and former RAFTA Board Chair), 
Michael Miracle (Coalition Steering Committee, Aspen Sustainability Department), and 
Hannah Berman (Aspen One Sustainability Team, Mayor Pro Tem of Basalt) — 
Page 12 of 84
presented the Coalition's findings report on valley-wide transportation and congestion 
challenges. 
 
     The Coalition, comprising 28 community members from Aspen to Glenwood Springs 
including current and former elected officials, business owners, and commuters, was 
formed in response to growing development and the absence of a holistic, valley-wide 
approach to transit and congestion. The group's guiding principle is to make travel 
through the valley faster, easier, safer, and more affordable, while doing no harm to 
RAFTA. 
 
     A key data point driving the plan is that a contractor stationed at Castle Creek Bridge 
recorded an average of 34,500 empty vehicle seats entering Aspen each morning — 
equivalent to 650 fifty-passenger buses — representing a significant opportunity to 
reduce single-occupancy vehicle traffic without major infrastructure investment. 
 
     The Coalition identified two primary demand-management tools: a High Occupancy 
Tolled (HOT) Lane, which would allow HOV-3+ vehicles, buses, and van pools to travel 
free while single-occupancy vehicles could pay a toll for access; and a Peak Hour 
Congestion Reduction Fee, applied dynamically to all low-occupancy vehicles during 
peak periods, with HOV-3+ vehicles, buses, and van pools exempt. Both tools would be 
dynamically priced to protect RAFTA bus service. All revenue generated would flow 
back into transit improvements, including enhanced bus service, micro-transit, and 
rideshare infrastructure — a feature the Coalition described as distinguishing this plan 
from similar programs elsewhere, such as New York City's congestion pricing scheme. 
 
     Council discussion reflected a general shift in preference toward congestion pricing 
over the HOT lane. Council Member DeAngelo and Council Member Gustafson both 
expressed concern that the HOT lane functioned as a "VIP lane," allowing wealthier 
commuters to purchase faster access, which conflicted with the plan's stated equity 
goals. Council Member Fridstein raised the importance of ensuring the plan extends 
meaningful benefits down-valley to communities such as Glenwood Springs, noting that 
congestion there is equally severe. The presenters acknowledged that while the plan is 
geographically focused on the upper valley — likely beginning at Brush Creek — ride-
sharing and carpooling components have the potential to benefit the full length of the 
Roaring Fork Valley. 
 
     The Coalition emphasized that any pricing mechanism would only be introduced 
after foundational "carrot" measures are in place, including a valley-wide rideshare app, 
school drop-off reduction strategies developed in partnership with the school district, 
dedicated RAFTA bus lanes, traffic signal prioritization, and expanded micro-transit. The 
plan is intended to be flexible, incremental, and recalibrate-able without large sunk 
infrastructure costs. Next steps include reconvening the full Coalition group, 
incorporating feedback from this and other presentations, and initiating modeling — 
some of which will occur in conjunction with the ongoing Entrance to Aspen 
Reevaluation process.

management (the Coalition's focus), highway improvements (Jacobs Engineering's 
scope), and a fixed guideway mass transit component — which she contended should 
be an aerial gondola system. She cited FAA and Pitkin County approval of space 
reservations for a multimodal connection at the airport as meaningful milestones toward 
that goal, and raised emergency evacuation capacity as a critical public safety rationale 
for pursuing high-capacity transit infrastructure.

 9.B. A Review and Discussion regarding an updated Employee Housing Mitigation 
Study 
Community Development Director Bonfils Thibeault introduced a presentation by 
consultants Economic and Planning Systems (EPS) and RRC Associates. Andrew 
Knudtsen (EPS), David Becher (RRC), and Karyln Russell-Carlson (EPS) walked 
Council through findings of a comprehensive nexus study to update the Town's housing 
mitigation framework, prompted by the adoption of Ordinance No. 1 of 2026, which 
increased mitigation to 100%. 
 
     The study updates employee generation rates for residential and commercial 
development, simplifies the existing category structure (reducing residential from three 
categories to two, and commercial from eleven to two), and adds—for the first time in 
any Colorado jurisdiction—a mitigation requirement for high-value residential remodels. 
Key proposed inputs include: an employment generation rate of 0.1946 workers per 
thousand square feet for residential (up significantly from the 2008 study), 0.62 workers 
per hotel unit, and 3.0 workers per thousand square feet for all other commercial. These 
increases reflect both updated labor data and the inclusion of construction-phase 
employment, which was excluded from the 2008 methodology. 
 
     For computing the fee in lieu, EPS recommended a unit size of 750 square feet at 
Page 20 of 84
1.75 employees per unit and a construction cost of $945 per square foot (inclusive of 
land), yielding an affordability gap fee of approximately $638 per square foot of required 
mitigation. At 100% mitigation, a hypothetical 5,000 square foot single-family home 
would generate approximately 417 square feet of mitigation obligation (~$266,000 fee); 
a 2,500 square foot commercial project would generate approximately 1,900 square feet 
(~$2M+ fee); and a 100-room hotel would generate fees in the tens of millions. 
 
     Council discussion centered on several key policy questions. Members broadly 
supported retaining the 100% mitigation rate established by Ordinance No. 1 of 2026, 
noting that Snowmass Village is at near build-out and that peer communities (Aspen at 
65%, Telluride at 40%) mitigate at lower commercial rates without necessarily reflecting 
comparable market pressures. Council also expressed consensus in favor of eliminating 
the existing 115% redevelopment credit, which was viewed as an outdated economic 
incentive no longer necessary given current development demand. Regarding 
residential remodels, Council expressed general support for the concept but called for 
careful calibration of the trigger threshold—likely set by permit valuation—so as not to 
burden mid-range homeowners. Staff confirmed the current proposal would apply only 
to high-value permits, with the threshold remaining a policy decision for Council. 
 
     Council directed staff to return with a draft ordinance incorporating the updated 
generation rates, simplified category structure, and remodel provisions. Council 
requested that future materials include: a comparison from the Town's housing authority 
on actual employees-per-unit and average unit size data; additional analysis on hotel 
mitigation relative to peers; and a rubric distinguishing remodels from scrape-and-
replace new construction. The working assumptions of 750 square feet per unit, 1.75 
employees per unit, and $945 per square foot were provisionally endorsed as starting 
points for ordinance drafting.

Resolution No. 20, 2026 adopting the IGA with Pitkin County regarding Massage Facility

Presented by: 
Jeff Conklin, Town Attorney 
  
 
Background: 
House Bill 24-1371, codified in C.R.S. § 30-15-401.4, updated the state’s policy that 
seeks to prevent the operation of illicit massage businesses. The law establishes a 
statewide policy of ensuring every current and prospective operator, owner, and 
employee of a massage facility submits to a background check. Every local government 
in the state that has a massage facility within its boundaries must establish a local 
process to ensure background checks are conducted as a condition to continued or 
future employment in such facilities. 
 
 A “background check” is defined as a fingerprint-based criminal history record check 
and must be conducted for any “employee” of a massage facility, which includes all 
individuals employed at the facility and all independent contractors hired to conduct 
routine operations of the massage facility. Exempt from the background check 
requirements are licensed massage therapists and independent contractors performing 
routine maintenance or janitorial services.  
 
In addition to providing a local process for background checks, local governments may 
establish business licensure requirements to regulate massage facilities and attempt to 
further deter illicit massage business practices by prohibiting certain human trafficking-
related offenses; however, this is optional and not required to be included with the 
establishment of a background check process.  
 
The local background check process must be established by ordinance or resolution 
and conform to federal and state law requirements for conducting background checks. 
The statute requires a county and a municipality within the county to consult with one 
another when developing a background check process and allows a municipality to 
choose to have a county’s resolution or ordinance adopted pursuant to the statute to 
apply to massage facilities operating within the jurisdictional boundaries of the 
municipality in lieu of adopting its own ordinance or resolution. 
 
Pitkin County drafted an ordinance establishing a background check process that 
Page 31 of 84
complies with state and federal requirements. The ordinance does not establish 
business licensure requirements. After consulting with Pitkin County, Town staff 
believes it is in the best interests of the Town to choose to have the County’s ordinance 
apply within the jurisdictional boundaries of the Town.  
 
The proposed Resolution would approve an IGA between Pitkin County, the Town, the 
City of Aspen, and the Town of Basalt to allow Pitkin County to enforce the background 
check requirements within the municipalities that have opted for the County ordinance 
to apply within their jurisdictions. Staff recommends adopting the Resolution to approve 
the IGA.

Background:

regulations and to improve the ease of use of the Village Shuttle’s Drug and Alcohol 
policy we have adopted a new template for our policy that will both bring the policy into

responsibility for adopting any new policies or amending existing policies.

1.  Purpose of Policy 
This policy complies with 49 CFR Part 655, as amended and 49 CFR Part 40, as amended. Copies of 
Parts 655 and 40 are available in the drug and alcohol program manager’s office and can be found on the 
internet at the Federal Transit Administration (FTA) Drug and Alcohol Program website 
http://transit-safety.fta.dot.gov/DrugAndAlcohol/.  
All covered employees are required to submit to drug and alcohol tests as a condition of employment in 
accordance with 49 CFR Part 655. 
Portions of this policy are not FTA-mandated but reflect Town of Snowmass Village’s policy. These 
additional provisions are identified by bold text.  
In addition, DOT has published 49 CFR Part 32, implementing the Drug-Free Workplace Act of 1988, 
which requires the establishment of drug-free workplace policies and the reporting of certain drug-related 
offenses to the FTA.  
All Town of Snowmass Village employees are subject to the provisions of the Drug-Free Workplace 
Act of 1988. 
The unlawful manufacture, distribution, dispensation, possession or use of a controlled substance is 
prohibited in the covered workplace. An employee who is convicted of any criminal drug statute for 
a violation occurring in the workplace shall notify the Town of Snowmass Village management in 
writing no later than five days after such conviction. 
Employees must abide by the terms of this policy as a condition of employment.  
2.  Covered Employees 
This policy applies to every person, including an applicant or transferee, who performs or will perform a 
“safety-sensitive function” as defined in Part 655, section 655.4.  
You are a covered employee if you perform any of the following: 
• Operating a revenue service vehicle, in or out of revenue service 
• Operating a non-revenue vehicle requiring a commercial driver’s license  
• Controlling movement or dispatch of a revenue service vehicle 
• Maintaining (including repairs, overhaul and rebuilding) of a revenue service vehicle or 
equipment used in revenue service 
• Carrying a firearm for security purposes 
See Attachment A for a list of covered positions by job title.

3.  Prohibited Behavior  
Use of illegal drugs is prohibited at all times.  Prohibited drugs include: 
• marijuana 
• cocaine 
• phencyclidine (PCP) 
• opioids 
• amphetamines 
All covered employees are prohibited from performing or continuing to perform safety-sensitive functions 
while having an alcohol concentration of 0.04 or greater.  
All covered employees are prohibited from consuming alcohol while performing safety-sensitive job 
functions or while on-call to perform safety-sensitive job functions. If an on-call employee has consumed 
alcohol, they must acknowledge the use of alcohol at the time that they are called to report for duty. If the 
on-call employee claims the ability to perform his or her safety-sensitive function, he or she must take an 
alcohol test with a result of less than 0.02 prior to performance. 
All covered employees are prohibited from consuming alcohol within four (4) hours prior to the 
performance of safety-sensitive job functions.  
All covered employees required to take a post-accident test are prohibited from consuming alcohol for 
eight (8) hours following involvement in an accident or until he or she submits to the post-accident drug 
and alcohol test, whichever occurs first.  
4.  Consequences for Violations 
Following a positive drug or alcohol (BAC at or above 0.04) test result or test refusal, the employee will 
be immediately removed from safety-sensitive duty and provided with contact information for Substance 
Abuse Professionals (SAPs).  
Following a BAC of 0.02 or greater, but less than 0.04, the employee will be immediately removed from 
safety-sensitive duties until the start of their next regularly scheduled duty period (but for not less than 
eight hours) unless a retest results in the employee’s alcohol concentration being less than 0.02.  
Treatment/Discipline 
Per Town of Snowmass Village policy, any employee who tests positive for drugs or alcohol (BAC at or 
above 0.04) or refuses to test will be subject to the following:

5. Circumstances for Testing 
Pre-Employment Testing 
A negative pre-employment drug test result is required before an employee can first perform safety-
sensitive functions. If a pre-employment test is cancelled, the individual will be required to undergo 
another test and successfully pass with a verified negative result before performing safety-sensitive 
functions.

If a covered employee has not performed a safety-sensitive function for 90 or more consecutive calendar 
days and has not been in the random testing pool during that time, the employee must take and pass a pre-
employment test before he or she can return to a safety-sensitive function.  
 
A covered employee or applicant who has previously failed or refused a DOT drug and/or alcohol test 
must provide proof of having successfully completed a referral, evaluation, and treatment plan meeting 
DOT requirements.  
 
Reasonable Suspicion Testing 
All covered employees shall be subject to a drug and/or alcohol test when Town of Snowmass Village has 
reasonable suspicion to believe that the covered employee has used a prohibited drug and/or engaged in 
alcohol misuse. A reasonable suspicion referral for testing will be made by a trained supervisor or other 
trained company official on the basis of specific, contemporaneous, articulable observations concerning 
the appearance, behavior, speech, or body odors of the covered employee.  
 
Covered employees may be subject to reasonable suspicion drug testing any time while on duty. Covered 
employees may be subject to reasonable suspicion alcohol testing while the employee is performing 
safety-sensitive functions, just before the employee is to perform safety-sensitive functions, or just after 
the employee has ceased performing such functions. 
 
Post-Accident Testing 
Covered employees shall be subject to post-accident drug and alcohol testing under the following 
circumstances:  
 
Fatal Accidents 
As soon as it is practicable following an accident involving the loss of a human life, drug and 
alcohol tests will be conducted on each surviving covered employee operating the public 
transportation vehicle at the time of the accident. In addition, any other covered employee whose 
performance could have contributed to the accident, as determined by Town of Snowmass Village 
using the best information available at the time of the decision, will be tested.  
 
Non-fatal Accidents  
As soon as practicable following an accident not involving the loss of a human life, drug and 
alcohol tests will be conducted on each covered employee operating the public transportation 
vehicle at the time of the accident if at least one of the following conditions is met: 
(1) The accident results in injuries requiring immediate medical treatment away from the 
scene, unless the covered employee can be completely discounted as a contributing 
factor to the accident 
(2) One or more vehicles incurs disabling damage and must be towed away from the 
scene, unless the covered employee can be completely discounted as a contributing 
factor to the accident

In addition, any other covered employee whose performance could have contributed to the 
accident, as determined by Town of Snowmass Village using the best information available at the 
time of the decision, will be tested. 
 
A covered employee subject to post-accident testing must remain readily available, or it is considered a 
refusal to test. Nothing in this section shall be construed to require the delay of necessary medical 
attention for the injured following an accident or to prohibit a covered employee from leaving the 
scene of an accident for the period necessary to obtain assistance in responding to the accident or to 
obtain necessary emergency medical care. 
 
Random Testing 
Random drug and alcohol tests are unannounced and unpredictable, and the dates for administering 
random tests are spread reasonably throughout the calendar year. Random testing will be conducted at all 
times of the day when safety-sensitive functions are performed.  
 
Testing rates will meet or exceed the minimum annual percentage rate set each year by the FTA 
administrator. The current year testing rates can be viewed online at 
www.transportation.gov/odapc/random-testing-rates.  
 
The selection of employees for random drug and alcohol testing will be made by a scientifically valid 
method, such as a random number table or a computer-based random number generator. Under the 
selection process used, each covered employee will have an equal chance of being tested each time 
selections are made.  
 
A covered employee may only be randomly tested for alcohol misuse while the employee is performing 
safety-sensitive functions, just before the employee is to perform safety-sensitive functions, or just after 
the employee has ceased performing such functions. A covered employee may be randomly tested for 
prohibited drug use anytime while on duty.  
 
Each covered employee who is notified of selection for random drug or random alcohol testing must 
immediately proceed to the designated testing site.  
 
Return to Duty Testing 
Any employee who is allowed to return to safety-sensitive duty after failing or refusing to submit to a 
DOT drug and/or alcohol test must first be evaluated by a substance abuse professional (SAP), complete a 
SAP-required program of education and/or treatment, and provide a negative return-to-duty drug test 
result and/or an alcohol test result of less than 0.02. Any return-to-duty drug testing will be directly 
observed.  All tests will be conducted in accordance with 49 CFR Part 40, Subpart O.  
 
Follow-up Testing 
Employees returning to safety-sensitive duty following a return-to-duty test will be required to undergo 
unannounced follow-up alcohol and/or drug testing for a period of one (1) to five (5) years, as directed by

the SAP. The duration of testing will be extended to account for any subsequent leaves of absence, as

6.  Testing Procedures 
All FTA drug and alcohol testing will be conducted in accordance with 49 CFR Part 40, as amended.  
 
Dilute Urine Specimen 
If there is a negative dilute test result, Town of Snowmass Village will conduct one additional retest. 
The result of the second test will be the test of record. Dilute negative results with a creatinine level 
greater than or equal to 2 mg/dL but less than or equal to 5 mg/dL require an immediate recollection 
under direct observation (see 49 CFR Part 40, section 40.67).  
 
Split Specimen Test 
Any covered employee who questions the results of a required drug test may request that the split sample 
be tested. The split sample test must be conducted at a second HHS-certified laboratory. The test must be 
conducted on the split sample that was provided by the employee at the same time as the primary sample. 
The method of collecting, storing, and testing the split sample will be consistent with the procedures set 
forth in 49 CFR Part 40, as amended. The employee's request for a split sample test must be made to the 
Medical Review Officer within 72 hours of notice of the original sample verified test result. Requests 
after 72 hours will only be accepted at the discretion of the MRO if the delay was due to documentable 
facts that were beyond the control of the employee.  
 
Town of Snowmass Village will ensure that the cost for the split specimen analysis is covered in order for 
a timely analysis of the sample, however Town of Snowmass will seek reimbursement for the split 
sample test from the employee if it is confirmed non-negative.

As a covered employee, you have refused to test if you:

(1) Fail to appear for any test (except a pre-employment test) within a reasonable time, as determined by

(2) Fail to remain at the testing site until the testing process is complete.  An employee who leaves the

(4) In the case of a directly observed or monitored urine drug collection, fail to permit monitoring or

(13) Fail to remain readily available following an accident.

As a covered employee, if you refuse to take a drug and/or alcohol test, you incur the same consequences 
as testing positive and will be immediately removed from performing safety-sensitive functions and 
provided with contact information for SAPs. 
 
8.  Voluntary Self-Referral 
Any employee who has a drug and/or alcohol abuse problem and has not been notified of the 
requirement to submit to reasonable suspicion, random or post-accident testing or has not refused a 
drug or alcohol test may voluntarily refer her or himself to the Town of Snowmass Village 
management or Drug and Alcohol Program Manager, who will refer the individual to a substance 
abuse counselor for evaluation and treatment.  
 
The substance abuse counselor will evaluate the employee and make a specific recommendation 
regarding the appropriate treatment.  Employees are encouraged to voluntarily seek professional 
substance abuse assistance before any substance use or dependence affects job performance.

Any safety-sensitive employee who admits to a drug and/or alcohol problem will immediately be 
removed from his/her safety-sensitive function and will not be allowed to perform such function 
until successful completion of a prescribed rehabilitation program. 
 
In the instance of a self-referral or a management referral, disciplinary action against the employee 
shall include:  
 
 Mandatory referral for an assessment by an employer approved counseling professional for 
assessment, formulation of a treatment plan, and execution of a return-to-work agreement.  
Failure to execute or remain compliant with the return-to-work agreement shall result 
in termination of Town of Snowmass Village employment. Compliance with the return-to-work 
agreement means that the employee has submitted to a drug/alcohol test immediately prior to 
returning to work; the result of that test is negative; the employee is cooperating with his/her 
recommended treatment program; and, the employee has agreed to periodic unannounced 
follow-up testing as described in this policy; however, all follow-up testing performed as part of 
a return-to-work agreement required under this policy is under the sole authority of Town of 
Snowmass Village and will be performed using non-DOT testing forms.  
 
Refusal to submit to a periodic unannounced follow-up drug/alcohol test shall be considered a 
direct act of insubordination and shall result in termination. All tests conducted as part of the 
return-to-work agreement will be conducted under company authority and will be performed using 
non-DOT testing forms.  
A self-referral or management referral to the employer's counseling professional that was not 
precipitated by a positive test result does not constitute a violation of the Federal regulations and 
will not be considered as a positive test result in relation to the progressive discipline defined in this 
policy.  
Periodic unannounced follow-up drug/alcohol testing conducted as a result of a self-referral or 
management referral which results in a verified positive shall be considered a positive test result in 
relation to the progressive discipline defined in this policy.  
A Voluntary Referral does not shield an employee from disciplinary action or guarantee 
employment with Town of Snowmass Village.  
A Voluntary Referral does not shield an employee from the requirement to comply with drug 
and alcohol testing. 
 
9.  Prescription Drug Use 
The appropriate use of legally prescribed drugs and non-prescription medications is not prohibited. 
However, the use of any substance which carries a warning label that indicates that mental 
functioning, motor skills, or judgment may be adversely affected must be reported to the Town of 
Snowmass Village management. Medical advice should be sought, as appropriate, while taking such 
medication and before performing safety-sensitive duties.
