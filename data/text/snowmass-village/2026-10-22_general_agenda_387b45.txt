WELCOME AND INTRODUCTIONS 
Meeting Chair Mayor Rachael Richards called the meeting of the Elected Officials 
Transportation Committee (EOTC) to order at 4:05 p.m., followed by a roll call for attendance. It 
was noted that there were technical difficulties with the internet, so it was not known if members 
who were not previously confirmed as absent were attempting to join the meeting. 
 
Pitkin County Public Works Director Brian Pettet was asked to come up for tribute for all his 
contributions over the years, as he is retiring and this is the last EOTC meeting of his career. RR 
gave him a bag of “City of Aspen Schwag”, and noted that Brian gave 20 years of service to the 
EOTC, in 34 total years at the County.

of the retreat. Mr. Mahoney introduced the purpose of retreat is to discuss current issues and new 
ideas for transportation solutions.  
 
Following is a summary and highlights of discussions during the retreat. Please see link above 
for a recording of the complete transcript.  
 
EOTC HISTORY AND KEY ACCOMPLISHMENTS 
A brief history was given of the EOTC which was established in 1993, upon voter approve of a 
½ cent Transportation Sales and Use Tax. Eventually the Comprehensive Valley Transportation 
Plan was adopted to help guide the EOTC’s work. Mr. Mahoney noted the packet includes nearly 
three pages of history. Commissioner Clapper asked what had happened to the airport park & 
ride built in 1998. Mayor Richards said it was transformed into rental car parking space. She also

noted significance of EOTC helping establish RFTA’s BRT, which led to the single greatest drop 
in travel over the Castle Creek Bridge.  
 
EOTC FUNDING  
RM introduced EOTC funding formula and current statutory language governing what funding 
can be used for and stated that recent discussion with Legal staff pointed to there not being a 
great deal of flexibility. Asked for comments. Mayor Richards recommended EOTC should send 
a formal memo to Pitkin County Legal staff with questions about specific transportation 
solutions and if they qualify. Mayor Richards is concerned that EOTC adheres to original 
enabling state legislation. Members had questions about what types of projects EOTC had 
funded in the past, and what the current budget was. RM showed the current EOTC 2026 budget. 
Councilmember Marolt agreed clarification is needed for what the EOTC can fund including 
new initiatives, and expressed interest in expanding the range of possible projects. Mayor 
Richards expressed concern that a new ballot attempting to expand the rules would result in 
voters doing away with the ½ cent. 
 
RM presented questions about geographic reach of EOTC and Commissioner Clapper 
commented that is may be time for a more formal strategic planning effort, perhaps in the fall. 
Members discussed the issue of keeping the ETOC’s efforts to the upper valley or expanding 
down valley. Mayor Richards commented that the 81% of the transit tax already goes to valley 
wide efforts through RFTA system funding. Councilmember Fridstein is in favor of keeping the 
focus on mass transit; in the Roaring Fork Valley. Councilmembers Rose and Doyle and 
Commissioner had questions about funding current efforts, plus the geographic range. 
Councilmember Benedetti cautioned about expanding EOTC funding beyond current scope, and 
also to consider ideas from the Transportation Coalition for the 21st Century. Councilmember 
Gustafson supports a strategic planning effort. To complete the question of what EOTC funding 
can or cannot be used for, Commissioner Clapper suggested each jurisdiction submit a lit of 
project ideas for review by Legal staff. 
 
Mr. Mahoney introduced a list of project ideas compiled by transportation staff for the three 
ETOC member agencies. Snowmass Transportation Director Sam Guarino brought up the 
question of geographic boundaries and also the idea of broadening use of funds, for example 
Transportation Coalition ideas. Members discussed needing legal opinion about specific 
proposed projects. Ms. DuPriest asked for the three agencies to forward lists of potential projects 
for legal opinion and to combine those with ideas already generated by staff.  
 
GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE

flexible operation including a small subcommittee that could meet between regular meetings of 
the entire 15-member group.  He gave the example of needing budget increase for Buttermilk 
and having time constraints to meet a bid schedule for beginning construction in 2026. In this 
situation, getting ETOC direction through a small subcommittee that meets more frequently than 
the full ETOC would help keep projects on schedule. 
 
Members discussed different ways the subcommittee could function including number of 
members and how often the meetings might occur. It was suggested that a subcommittee should 
meet frequently, possibly once per month, but that was rejected as too often. Mr. Mahoney asked

RE:     2026 Work Plan Updates & Additional Items 
 
Following are updates on 2026 projects. Some projects are also included in the 2027 proposed 
workplan and budget as they are being carried forward to next year. 
 
Truscott to Owl Creek Trail  
Issue Statement: Funding opportunity; grant with local match 
This project is an element of the Near Term Transit Improvement Program begun in 2021. 
Design has been completed to 90% level; the final 10% will be completed in coordination with 
the Truscott Tunnel improvements being managed by the City of Aspen. The City’s Open Space 
and Trails staff have applied for a Safe Roads and Streets for All grant to fund construction. The 
EOTC member agencies all signed a letter of support for the grant. Award notifications are 
anticipated in late 2026 or early 2027. If awarded, the City will have 12 months to finalize and 
execute the formal grant agreement. This period is intended to allow local governments time to 
confirm partner agency contributions and formally budget the funds. The EOTC may consider 
funding all or part of the local match; this can be considered during the budget and workplan 
meeting on October 22, 2026, or at meeting in early 2027. 
 
Here is a summary of the project cost estimates: 
• Total project cost estimate: $7,024,816 
• Federal request amount: $5,268,621 
• Non-federal match amount: $1,756,195 (25% match) 
 
 
Buttermilk Pedestrian Crossing Construction 
Issue statement: Consider budget amendment to cover construction bid. 
As discussed at the EOTC retreat on April 22, 2026, bids for the construction came in 
substantially higher than the original estimate approved by the EOTC 2026 budget, so the 
decision was made to rebid the project later in the year for scheduled construction in 2027. The 
reason for the gap between estimate and bids is simply that the internal staff estimate was low, at 
$342,000. One cause for the gap was we did not anticipate costs for extensive lighting to better 
illuminate the crossing for Buttermilk employees and others accessing the up- and down-valley 
bus stops in the dark. Other reasons could be general construction costs in the valley increasing 
in the time period between budget approval and design completion. When the design consultant 
completed their Engineer’s Estimate of Probably Costs (EPOC), the revised estimate was 
$550,000. Two construction firms submitted bids in April 2026, at $751,481 and $927,867.  The 
goal now is to rebid the project in fall of 2026 after the EOTC can meet and approve additional 
funds, to ensure we get a construction firm under contract before the end of the year and 
committed for the work in Spring 2027. Note that it is important to have a commitment before 
Page 12 of 22
 
potential bidders schedule other construction jobs such as hotels and hospitality businesses 
expected to occur in the valley with the closure of the Airport in 2027. 
 
Brush Creek Park & Ride Parking Enforcement 
Issue Statement: Implement parking enforcement at reduced cost 
Overnight parking/camping prohibition is scheduled to begin in November. Staff will coordinate 
with Healthy and Human Services on the question of accommodating unhoused individuals on a 
trial basis, as outlined in the Brush Creek Monitoring & Management Plan. In addition, a 
communication plan will be put in place to inform the public of the change, with ample warnings 
ahead of time for when enforcement will begin. Metropolis, Pitkin County’s contractor handling 
paid parking at the Airport and Buttermilk have submitted a proposal for $49,233 for one year, 
considerably lower than the estimate which was based on a more ambitious strategy to utilize 
technology to track usage of the lot. In the past several months, there have been fewer parking 
violations at Brush Creek due to more regular patrols by Pitkin County Sheriff’s Deputies, but as 
they do not have the capacity for daily/nightly patrols additional enforcement is needed. 
 
Transportation Project Funding Eligibility 
Issue Statement: Legal opinion needed on what transportation projects and initiatives may 
be funded with the Transit Sales and Use Tax. At the April 23, 2026, retreat staff presented a 
list of transportation projects and initiatives aimed at congestion reduction and transportation 
demand management in the upper Roaring Fork Valley. EOTC members requested the list be 
evaluated by Pitkin County legal staff for funding eligibility. Staff from City of Aspen, Town of 
Snowmass Village and Pitkin County submitted a list of project ideas to the Pitkin County legal 
department; subsequently the list was discussed with attorneys from the City of Aspen and Town 
of Snowmass Village. Legal staff from Pitkin County will be available at the meeting to address 
questions. 
 
EOTC Subcommittee Formation 
Issue Statement: Legal issue with subcommittee formation plus logistics. Since the idea was 
explored at the April 23, 2026, mini retreat, staff has dealt with any legal and procedural issues 
with establishing a subcommittee of the EOTC that would meet regularly in between full EOTC 
meetings. It has been determined that there is no need to update the ETOC Intergovernmental 
Agreement to establish a subcommittee, however, the meetings will have to be noticed and 
provide an opportunity for public comment. Staff will work with the three agencies to set up a 
subcommittee meeting in January 2027.  
 
EOTC Member Agency and Partner Updates  
Staff from the City of Aspen, Pitkin County, Town of Snowmass Village and RFTA will provide 
brief updates on current projects.

AABC multimodal Improvements Design -- $200,000 
In late 2024 EOTC approved funding for a plan to improve pedestrian and bicycle safety and 
access throughout the Aspen Airport Business Center. The planning project was triggered by the 
beginning of the City of Aspen's Lumberyard housing project and was an opportunity to analyze 
pedestrian and bicyclist access to transit for not only the residents of the Lumberyard but also 
residents, business owners and customers of the AABC. The project, conducted in 2025 – 2026, 
included extensive public outreach and analysis of the AABC's transportation network, which 
historically had not included a complete multimodal safety and access analysis. The plan was 
completed in late summer 2026 and is set to proceed to the design phase. The $200,000 being 
requested will supplement funding committed by Pitkin County.

Memo to EOTC 
To: Elected Officials Transportation Committee (EOTC) 
From: Fly Aspen Snowmass (Aspen One, ACRA, Snowmass Tourism)  
Date: October 1, 2026 
Re: Request for Funds to Support Scheduled Ground Transportation Shuttle to EGE in 2027 
On May 11, 2026, Fly Aspen Snowmass (FAS) sent out a Request for Quotes (RFQ) for 
Passenger Van or Motorcoach Transportation scheduled service between the Aspen, Eagle and 
Denver airports. The 2027 airport closure was the impetus for the distribution of this RFQ; 
however, FAS has been working to fill the transportation void in the valley since the departure of 
Colorado Mountain Express (now Epic Mountain Express) in 2020. In 2022 there was a glimmer 
of hope with Roaring Fork Express, which ended up being a failed attempt due to poor customer 
service, inadequate booking platforms and logistical challenges. 
Throughout the process of trying to secure transportation options for the valley, FAS has become 
well-versed in the limits and complications of the business model associated with this request; 
the committee is also acutely aware of the challenges that the 2027 Airport Closure will have on 
access for both visitors and locals to the valley.   
While solutions were sought for ground transportation links to and from both Denver 
International Airport (DEN) along with the Eagle County Regional Airport (EGE), the line to 
EGE was determined to be a priority because of the significant capacity increases added by both 
American and United Airlines during ASE’s 2027 closure. 
Subsidy Request 
While Fly Aspen Snowmass and the ASE Ground Transportation Subcommittee are still vetting 
the final proposals, it has become clear that the desired level of scheduled trips at price points 
that we feel would be acceptable will require a total subsidy of up to $600,000. 
We would like to formally request a $300,000 contribution towards this fund from the EOTC, 
which would be matched by contributions from RFTA, Aspen One, ACRA and the Town of 
Snowmass Village. 
Alignment with Community Values and the Pitkin County Comprehensive Plan 
The closure will redirect a large share of travelers to EGE, DEN and GJT. Today, nearly all 
ground travel to and from those airports is by private vehicle or rental car, and without a 
compelling alternative the closure will add more of them to Highway 82, where traffic has 
already grown significantly over the past two decades.  
Page 19 of 22
 
 
FAS does not believe the closure should be solved by adding rental cars and single-occupancy 
vehicles to the valley’s roads. That approach would run counter to the values at the center of 
Pitkin County Vision 2050 and the new Comprehensive Plan: bold climate action, a balanced 
economy, rural preservation, and quality of life. As the Plan recognizes, the community wants 
less reliance on fossil fuels, but also “less traffic and congestion.” 
A scheduled, shared shuttle to EGE directly advances the Plan’s key themes. The Plan calls for 
reducing single-occupancy vehicle travel, offering multimodal options at and around the airport, 
and working regionally through partners like the EOTC and RFTA. It also acknowledges that 
widening roads is not a realistic answer in this landscape, which makes shared trips one of the 
few practical tools for managing congestion and meeting the County’s long-term climate goals. 
To ensure this investment delivers on those commitments, the RFQ requires the selected operator 
to coordinate schedules and connections with RFTA so the shuttle complements regional transit, 
and to provide monthly reporting on ridership, on-time performance and customer satisfaction. 
This will allow FAS and the EOTC to track whether the service is taking cars off the road. 
Conclusion 
Solving the transportation and connectivity needs at ASE clearly demands a multi-pronged 
approach. Scheduled ground shuttles to and from EGE during the 2027 closure will be a key 
component that is in line with community values for both environmental and congestion relief 
efforts.  We believe this service will serve our residents and visitors both during the 2027 closure 
while setting the stage for sustainable multi-modal transportation options worthy of our world-
class community well into the future. 
Thank you for your consideration.
