Safe & Resilient Community 
 
• Public Works staff recently completed the installation of Rectangular Rapid 
Flashing Beacons ( RRFB) flashers at all three Town roundabouts. According to 
national studies, drivers stopping for pedestrian and bicycle crossing compliance 
jumps from 22% to 90% with these types of installations. The final installation cost 
was $73,000 versus $290,000 for the previously planned alternative. 
• The Town issued an RFP for the PFAS clean up on the existing Public Works 
property, a requirement of the Agilent land swap agreement. Remediation should 
be completed within 90 days. 
• Safety is always a team priority in Public Works. With the forthcoming activation of 
the Municipal Service campus, Town staff are taking advantage of the new large 
conference and meeting space in Building A to schedule events. Specifically, 
hosting regionally sponsored safety and operations training programs that will 
benefit our team as well as other municipalities in the area. By hosting,  the Town 
can offer expanded training opportunities to our team at a reduced cost and give 
more team members the opportunity to attend.

Summary Statement: 
 
Staff is proposing bi-annual Land Use Code amendments to several sections of the 
Code in response to recent changes to Colorado State Law, changes to community 
needs and preferences, and for corrections and clarifications.  
 
Detail of Issue/Request: 
 
The Land Use Code received a major overhaul over the last few years with the newly 
adopted Code going into effect on June 1, 2026. The overhaul included major shifts in 
development standards, taking into account the goals of the Comprehensive Plan; 1. 
Provide economic vitality for residents and local businesses, 2. Provide quality places to 
live, and 3. Provide neighborhoods and districts with distinct character. In 2026, Town 
staff presented to the Board of Trustees the intention to amend the Land Use Code on 
a bi-annual basis to make clarifications, correct technical errors and adjust the 
provisions when community and development needs shift. This code amendment 
package includes proposed amendments to the following sections of the Town of 
Frederick Land Use Code: 
 
Article 2 - Zoning 
- Modifying the definition of multiple uses 
- Creating a use for marijuana retail sales and prohibiting it in all zoning districts 
- Making Oil and Gas Operations a Conditional Use in LI-Flex and HI-Flex and 
prohibited in P-Public 
- Making Recreation Center (Indoor/Outdoor) a Conditional Use in R-E, R-1, R-2 and R-
3. 
- Modifying the permitted animals list in A and AOS 
- Clarifying setbacks and screening for Vehicle Service and Repair Light 
- Adding Accessory Dwelling Unit provisions for larger homes in the A, AOS and RE 
zone districts 
- Clarifying language for residential and non-residential accessory structures 
- Clarifying language for Home Occupations and commercial vehicles 
- Clarifying language in the Animal Unit Table 
- Clarifying language in the Storage and Shipping Containers table 
 
Article 3 - Density and Dimensional Standards 
- Adding accessory dwelling unit language in the General Table Notes under the 
Residential Density and Dimensional Standards table 
- Clarifying setbacks for accessory buildings in non-residential zone districts 
 
Page 17 of 61
Article 4 - Design Principles and Development Standards  
- Clarifying and removing language for service and off-street loading areas 
- Clarifying language for ground mounted mechanical equipment 
- Clarifying language in the Off-Street Parking Requirements table 
 
Article 5 - Sign Standards 
- Clarifying language in the Authority section, specifically, when permits are required 
- Clarifying sign prohibition language 
- Clarifying language for Home Occupation wall and window sign allowance and size 
- Clarifying window sign location and size allowance provisions 
- Clarifying provisions for Permanent Small Signs 
- Clarifying and removing language under Temporary Signs 
 
Article 6 - Approval Process, Vesting of Property Rights, and Enforcement 
- Adding Oil and Gas Conditional Use as a Major Application type (Already exists as a 
process. Clarifying it's an actual application type) 
- Adding Right-of-Way Tree Lawn Replacement as an Administrative Application type 
- Clarifying standards for mineral interest owners 
- Removing one criterion from the Subdivision Amendment list of criteria 
- Adjusting Administrative Citations' location within the article 
 
Article 7 - Annexations 
- Clarifying annexation review procedures  
 
Article 9 - Oil and Gas Drilling and Production 
- Clarifying the zone districts where Oil and Gas is permitted  
 
Article 13 - Definitions 
- Adding a definition for Heavy commercial vehicle 
- Updating the definition of Industrial, Light 
- Clarifying the definition for Outdoor Storage Facility 
- Adding a definition for Short-term rental 
 
Review Criteria:  
Section 16-6-70.H.3. For the purpose of establishing and maintaining sound, stable, and 
desirable development within the Town, the text of this Code shall not be amended 
except: 
1. To correct a manifest error in the text of this Code; 
Staff Response: Several of the proposed amendments are indeed errors. These 
corrections will continue to be flagged as they are discovered and amended with each bi-
annual Code update. This is a very typical scenario after a large code update.  
2. To provide for changes in administrative practices as may be necessary to 
accommodate changing needs of the community and the Town staff; 
Page 18 of 61
Staff Response: Some of the proposed amendments are a result of changing community 
and staff needs. Staff aims to provide great customer service, which includes a more 
streamlined set of rules and regulations. 
3. To accommodate innovations in land use and development practices that were not 
contemplated at the adoption of this Code; or 
Staff Response: Several of these proposed amendments are in response to specific land 
use development projects. Specific elements of these projects prompted staff to look at 
the newly adopted rules and regulations and propose adjustments. The proposed 
changes are believed to promote a smarter approach to development that will benefit 
future applications.  
4. To further the implementation of the goals and objectives of the Comprehensive Plan.  
Staff Response: The overarching goal of the Comprehensive Plan is to plan for the best 
Frederick possible. The Land Use Code is the direct implementation of that long-term 
vision. Every proposed amendment is aimed towards providing clarity and predictability 
for our residents and the development community. This translates into bringing the goals 
and objectives of the Comprehensive Plan to fruition through the built environment.  
Planning Commission 
 
The Planning Commission held a Public Hearing on October 1, 2026. They voted 5 Yes 
and 0 No to recommend approval of the Code amendments to the Board of Trustees. 
During the hearing, the Planning Commission went through several of the proposed 
amendments and discussed intentions with staff, alternative language, and future 
potential amendments.  
 
The PC directed staff to make small edits before the BOT hearing: 
- Spell out the correct number (one thousand five-hundred) within the ADU section. 
- Add clarifying language within the ordinance for two tables (Add the zone districts that 
apply to the tables). 
- Remove "maximum" from Section 7.2.C 
 
The Planning Commission identified Home Occupations as a potential future topic of 
discussion. Two attendees from the public joined the meeting but did not provide any 
public comment.  
 
Notifications and Public Comment 
 
The item was published in the Longmont Times Call at least 15 days prior to the public 
hearing. Staff has received no public comment on the proposed amendments.

Community Impact 
 
Making bi-annual amendments to improve the Land Use Code is a new approach for 
Town Staff. The goal is to keep an updated Code to help balance the pressures of 
development and protect our residents. These bi-annual updates will help Town staff 
address changing conditions or new issues that were never contemplated before. A 
living and evolving document is the best approach to keep Frederick modern, while 
maintaining and protecting what makes Frederick unique.

Proposal: Adding Accessory Dwelling Unit provisions for larger homes in the A, AOS and RE
