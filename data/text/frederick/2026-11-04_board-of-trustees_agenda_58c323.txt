Summary Statement: 
 
With Board of Trustee approval, the Town of Frederick is entering into an agreement 
with the St. Vrain Education Foundation to manage the Town’s High School Scholarship 
program, ensuring professional administration, transparent selection, and continued 
support for local students pursuing higher education. 
 
Detail of Issue/Request: 
 
This is a request for the Board to authorize an agreement with the St. Vrain Education 
Foundation to administer the Town of Frederick High School Scholarship program—
handling application intake, eligibility verification, impartial selection, fund 
custodianship, and award disbursement. 
 
Legal Comments: 
 
The Town Attorney's Office has reviewed all documents and found them satisfactory. 
 
Alternatives/Options 
 
The alternative option would be for Town staff to manage the scholarship program. The 
proposed partnership with St. Vrain Education Foundation was selected instead due to 
their greater efficiency and capacity, as well as their ability to track and support student 
progress throughout the application process. 
 
Financial Considerations 
 
This agreement itself will not have a financial impact. The scholarships will be paid from 
the oil and gas royalty fund consistent with previous awards. 
 
Staff Recommendation 
 
Staff recommends partnering with St. Vrain Education Foundation to leverage their 
streamlined online application platform and extensive experience managing a portfolio 
of more than forty scholarships. 
 
Community Impact 
 
Partnering with St. Vrain Education Foundation ensures all eligible students have equal 
access to a wide range of scholarships through a standardized, transparent application 
process.

Summary Statement: 
 
The 2027 Proposed Budget First Public Hearing presentation provides a 
comprehensive overview of the Town of Frederick’s financial plan, bringing together the 
information reviewed during the three previous budget work sessions. The proposed 
budget includes $47.85 million in revenues and $68.94 million in expenditures, with 
planned use of accumulated fund balances primarily associated with capital 
investments. The presentation reviews major revenue sources, operating expenditures, 
capital improvement requests, and the staff recommendation of 5.5 new positions. It 
also examines projected fund balances, reserve levels, long-term financial 
sustainability, and proposed changes to the Town’s fee schedule. The hearing provides 
an opportunity for public comment and Board feedback before the final budget hearing 
and adoption scheduled for December 2, 2026. 
 
Detail of Issue/Request: 
 
The purpose of the November 4, 2026, public hearing is to present the Town of 
Frederick’s proposed 2027 budget for public review and Board consideration. This 
presentation brings together the information discussed during the three previous budget 
work sessions, providing a comprehensive overview of the Town’s financial position, 
anticipated revenues, proposed expenditures, capital investments, staffing needs, fund 
balances, and recommended fee adjustments. The hearing provides an opportunity for 
the Board and community to review the proposed financial plan and identify any 
necessary changes before final budget adoption on December 2, 2026. 
Page 21 of 151
The proposed 2027 budget includes approximately $47.85 million in revenues and other 
sources and $68.94 million in expenditures and other uses across all Town funds. The 
difference primarily reflects planned capital investments and the use of resources 
accumulated in prior years. The presentation examines these expenditures by fund and 
category, including $22.54 million in capital outlay, $22.41 million in personnel and 
benefits, and $19.68 million in services and operations. It also outlines the capital 
improvement requests reviewed during the budget process and the recommended 
addition of 5.5 positions to support school safety, victim services, parks maintenance, 
and golf operations. 
An important component of the presentation is the Town's long-term financial 
sustainability. Staff will review projected ending fund balances for the Town's major 
operating funds and evaluate those balances against existing reserve benchmarks and 
potential future reserve strategies. This includes examining the use of accumulated 
resources, distinguishing restricted revenues from unrestricted resources, and 
considering the financial capacity available for future infrastructure investments and 
operational commitments. The presentation also reviews proposed 2027 fee schedule 
adjustments intended to improve cost recovery and better align fees with the services 
provided. Any formal changes to reserve policies or approval of the fee schedule would 
be addressed through separate Board action. 
The proposed budget directly impacts the community by establishing how the Town will 
allocate financial resources to maintain essential municipal services, support public 
safety, invest in infrastructure, and accommodate continued growth. Through the 
budget process, the Town seeks to balance current service expectations with long-term 
financial responsibilities, ensuring that investments made today do not unnecessarily 
compromise the Town's ability to provide services in the future. 
The first public hearing is an opportunity for transparency, public participation, and 
continued Board discussion. No final budget adoption is requested at this meeting. Staff 
will incorporate applicable feedback, finalize the necessary budget documents, and 
present the final proposed budget for consideration at the second public hearing on 
December 2, 2026. 
 
Legal Comments: 
 
The 2027 budget process is governed by the Colorado Local Government Budget Law 
(C.R.S. §§ 29-1-101 through 29-1-115), which establishes requirements for public 
notice, budget availability, public hearings, and formal adoption of the annual budget 
and appropriations. Sections 29-1-106 through 29-1-108 specifically address public 
participation and the Board's responsibility to review and adopt the budget. The Town 
must also comply with the Colorado Taxpayer's Bill of Rights (TABOR), Article X,

Summary Statement: 
 
The Board of Trustees is being asked to convey three easements, one permanent utility 
easement and two temporary construction easements, to the St. Vrain Sanitation 
District to facilitate their "Idaho Creek Line 2 Sanitary Sewer Extension" project on 
Town owned parcels. 
 
Detail of Issue/Request: 
 
The St. Vrain Sanitation District (District) approached the Town during the spring of 
2025 with a preliminary request to consider the placement of sanitary sewer on Town 
owned parcels. Town Staff agreed to consider the request, pending the engineering 
design process and ultimately, dedication of easements by our Board of Trustees. This 
action would dedicate those easements.  
 
The project includes the addition of sanitary sewer utilities from approximately 
Aggregate Blvd and CR 20.5, south to Aggregate Blvd and Godding Hollow Prky. The 
immediate need is to satisfy District service requirements for the new St. Vrain Valley 
School District high school site, but the utility line will ultimately facilitate other potential 
growth areas in the vicinity over time.  
 
There are two separate actions on this Ordinance. The first action authorizes the 
conveyance of the properties identified in the legal descriptions of Exhibit A, B and C, 
by means of a permanent utility easement over 3105 Rinn and temporary construction 
easements over 3100 Rinn, Outlot E and 3105 Rinn - this action includes a condition of 
approval that the permanent easement shall not be executed on behalf of the Town 
until such time as the Town issues a right-of-way permit and/or a grading permit, both of 
which are administrative processes. The second action authorizes the Town to accept 
the offer of compensation dated June 4, 2026 for 3105 Rinn in the amount of $79,270 
and one dated June 25, 2026 for 3100 Rinn, Outlot E in the amount of $29,200.  
 
Additionally, Town Staff are requesting direction on the placement of revenues from the 
offers of compensation. Without any specific direction, the funds would be placed in the 
General Fund and appropriated through the normal budgeting process without any 
notation. Staff is recommending the funds be placed in the Parks and Open Space 
Fund, which would restrict their usage to the limitations in Frederick Municipal Code for 
that fund, and that Staff come back with a budget amendment in 2027 or an 
appropriation request for 2028, with improvements on or near the impacted sites, after 
both construction of the project is completed and discussions can be held with the 
nearby residents.

This action includes the authorization to receive approximately $108,000 in revenue,

Staff Recommendation

Community Impact 
 
This utility installation will impact the community in two ways. First, the construction of 
the utility will be impactful and disruptive. Town leaders have met with District leaders 
and talked through the project to minimize these impacts to the extent practical. District 
leaders are prepared to engage the local community throughout the project, to ensure 
information is being relayed and residents are heard, and that impacts are minimized. 
Second, once the project is complete, additional development may be progressed in the 
immediate area, as sanitary sewer was a major missing utility in this area. This could 
indirectly, positively impact the community by building additional revenues for streets, 
stormwater infrastructure and local parks, among other community priorities.
