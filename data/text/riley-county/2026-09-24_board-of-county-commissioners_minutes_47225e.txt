Commission Comments 
 
Ford's Comments: 
  
Monday post BOCC I met with Riley County Extension Director Daniel Skucius to discuss 
several items including the recent Riley County Fair in July, current discussions on CiCo Park 
and a possible new fairground, the status of 4-H programming overall, and general thoughts 
and direction coming from both the fair and rodeo boards. Very informative conversation and 
hopefully we can get some more depth on the dialogs moving forward. 
  
Tuesday and Wednesday I spent at work filling out my usual schedule, staying plenty busy, 
which is great this time of year. 
  
Tuesday afternoon I attended the September Board of Directors meeting of Pawnee Mental 
Health. Started with the usual Finance Oversight Committee meeting to get caught up on the 
budget and finances though August. During the regular meeting and after the call to order we 
approved the minutes from the July 28th meeting had a couple of new introductions, and then 
we heard from Will Baldwin and a new IT roadmap for the organization, followed by a Chief 
Financial Officers Report from Lindsay Miller and a Finance Oversight Committee report 
detailing on both the July and August finances followed by a Chief Executive Officer report 
from Mike Rezkalla and then a marketing and development report and the meeting ended with 
several executive sessions on personnel matters of non-elected personnel. We plan on meeting 
again in October.  
  
Also Tuesday I attended the retirement ceremony for Ron Wilson up at the Blue Hills Room. 
Great turnout from the community as a whole as he retires from the Huck Boyd Institute and 
various other roles at Kansas State University over the last 35 years. He plans on still staying 
active in other community endeavors and I wish him and his family all the best in the future. 
  
Tuesday evening I attended the September Board of Directors Meeting of the Manhattan Area 
Technical College. I attended as a member of the foundation and with many different things 
going on it was important we all tried to meet together as a group. Heard a presentation from a 
grant and funding group McAlister & Quinn out of Washington, DC and got caught up on all 
the early year activities and data, got a sneak peek at the new MATC dashboard, and was 
updated on a handful of potential policy changes and upgrades as well. Lots of good dialog 
and questions and some bold thoughts and ideas to help keep the college moving forward. 
  
Speaking of MATC I was also invited to join staff and other community partners including 
Shultz Construction and Mead Lumber at the new Skills Lab as part of new updates for the

Electrical Technicians Program there at the college. This is a one-of-a-kind addition made 
possible by the above listed community partners that will help shape and educate a new wave 
of local electrical technicians and professional for the future. Appreciated the opportunity to 
see this for myself as I was not able to attend last week’s official open house and ribbon

Lastly wanted to make everyone aware of this webinar that took place yesterday from the 
National Association on a new program they are rolling out called Public Promise Insurance. It 
is something they have been working on for a while and will provide a whole suite of 
Supplemental Benefits for counties at the start of the new year including dental, vision, life, 
short-term disability, long term care, worksite coverage and even pet insurance and possible 
attraction and retention tools for employees. This webinar was in coalition with MetLife and 
Trustmark, and they covered the specific benefits and how the program turnkey design makes 
both implementation and ongoing management simple for HR and other benefit teams.  
  
Also spent time some this week continuing to catch up on email and various other 
correspondence that has come up along the way. 
  
  
McKinley's Comments: 
  
Monday was the Law Board meeting. There was a group there to ask that we not approve the 
BIDEN Grant. Director Peete was not there because he had car trouble and was stuck at home 
in Topeka. Discussions included some new IT equipment, Aggieville street closing, and 
Community National Night Out on September 29th. There was also a discussion clarifying 
what is expected from the search committee. 
  
Tuesday evening I attended the City of Manhattan work session. There was a long discussion 
on applying to have Sunset Cemetery added to the Historical Register. I was there to comment 
on the latest presentation on the latest Edge District project. They are wanting a 20 year tax 
abatement. I am not for it and pointed out that they will not pay property tax but still will 
receive city and county services. There will be a public hearing in October or November.  
  
  
Focke's Comments: 
  
Monday afternoon, the Law Board met at Manhattan City Hall. There were a number of people 
who wanted to speak about the BIDEN grant proposal. The board agreed to allocate 30 
minutes for hearing the issues, with each person to have 3 minutes. After that we had the 
business meeting with one executive session at the end.  
  
Tuesday I got my yearly COVID shot and had a minor reaction for several days. I spent some 
time in my office going through the various packing bins that I hadn’t unpacked from our 
move. I still have several to go through.  
  
Wednesday I met with a constituent who had some issues and questions concerning the Law 
Board. This week has been full of a lot of phone calls and e-mails, and I’m ready for a change 
of pace tomorrow at the Northeast Kansas County Officials meeting.
