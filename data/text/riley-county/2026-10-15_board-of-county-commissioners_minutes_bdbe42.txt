Ford’s Comments: 
 
The end of last week was rather quiet with not a lot going on. Got caught up on a couple of 
county related phone calls and other correspondence and worked to get packed and ready for 
the AUSA Conference in Washington, D.C.  
 
Saturday I watched the K-State game at Powercat Grill with a couple of friends who were in 
town. What an ugly and disappointing game throughout with the Wildcats losing to Houston 
35-19 dropping to 0-2 to start conference play. 
 
Sunday morning, I woke up early and left Manhattan at 5:50 a.m. in route to Washington, D.C. 
for the annual AUSA Conference. I got into Washington, D.C. around 1:00 p.m. local time 
into a complete monsoon but got to the hotel and couldn’t get checked in till 4 p.m. so I went 
and had lunch and met up with part the delegation, got my badge and information for the 
event, and chatted and planned for the upcoming week. Also, on Sunday I watched from my 
phone as my Jaguars won on a last second field goal in dramatic fashion to beat the 
Philadelphia Eagles 19-17 to win their 4th game of the year. 
 
Monday morning started day one of the AUSA Conference. Started off with an opening 
ceremony at the Walter E. Washington Convention Center. First we heard from General 
Robert B. Brown, U.S. Army Ret., the President and CEO of AUSA gave an update and 
introduction of the acting Secretary of the Army Adam Telle. He went through everything the 
U.S. Army is working on and planning currently, future plans for recruitment, technology and 
communication, and went through some new training and development plan in the works as 
well. It was a comprehensive and informative update. We also stuck around for several 
personal, family, and nationwide chapter awards, and got an update from the Army Ten-Miler 
that took place in that same monsoon on Saturday including the Fort Riley team (who finished 
5th out of 35 in their category) and teams from both K-State and Wichita State as well.  
 
Monday afternoon we headed over to Clydes of Gallery Place for our annual Chapter 
Luncheon. It included members from Manhattan/Riley County, Junction City/Geary County, 
Fort Riley, and Kansas State as well. We first heard from the Riley County-Fort Riley Chapter

President Rich Jankovich. He spoke about the importance of the relationship with Fort Riley 
and introduced the guest speaker Command Sargent Major Rickey G Jackson and discussed 
the importance and readiness of the 1st Infantry Division and his time at Fort Riley. We later 
heard from current Commanding General of the 1st Infantry Division and Fort Riley Major 
General Monté L. Rone and discussed his time over the last two years at Fort Riley as he will 
be moving on to a new assignment and Post October 23rd. 
 
Later Monday afternoon we traveled over to meet with the Governor’s Military Council 
Lobbyist Baker-Donelson at their offices. The team of Cade Groton and Steven Hyjek went 
through a presentation updating us on the fiscal, logistical, infrastructural, and political, that 
the current environment will be playing on Fort Riley and discussed the same impacts going 
through fiscal year 2029. Lots of good dialog and questions followed by the group and as usual 
it was very insightful and educational. 
 
Tuesday morning the Chapter headed up to Capitol Hill for the annual discussion on priorities 
for Fort Riley. This meeting took place in the Armed Services hearing room at the Dirksen 
Senate building and included a contingent from Junction City, State Legislature, Military 
Relations, and several private Military 501c3s as well as myself. We talked to staff from both 
Senator Moran, and Congressman Mann’s offices and was joined by US Congressman Derek 
Schmidt as well. We had a great discussion and went through everything point by point and 
are making plans to follow up.  
 
Later Tuesday I took some time to visit the AUSA Exposition downstairs in the Convention 
Center. A lot of new tools, technology and innovation are being developed and used by the 
United State Army and it’s always cool to check out and see. Late afternoon Tuesday I went 
down to the Lincoln Memorial to see the new Upcroft Museum they have built underneath the 
Memorial and the temporary National Military Monument sponsored by USAA till Veterans 
Day. Tuesday evening, I attended the Riley County-Fort Riley AUSA Social at Clydes Place 
of Gallery. It was great to check up with some people I haven’t seen since last year and I met 
few new people as well.  
 
Wednesday I traveled back from Washington, D.C. got unpacked, settled back in and caught 
up on correspondence, emails, and prepared for the rest of the week moving forward. 
 
 
McKinley’s Comments: 
 
Last Friday was the Pre-Law Board meeting. There doesn't seem to be anything too exciting 
for the meeting. 
 
Monday was the Riley County Employee Day. Our two speakers were Sandra Wiley and 
Trooper Ben. They were both great. It was good to see the years of service awards for long 
term employees. Lunch was a windy adventure at Public Works. The afternoon displays and 
games were fun and interesting. 
 
Tuesday evening was the Fire Department Advisory Board meeting. A good meal with hot 
cobbler and ice cream. They had good conversations about the work they do and it was a little 
spirited at times.

Focke’s Comments: 
 
Friday, October 9th I stopped by the office before the Pre-Law Board meeting.  
 
Saturday I did some household chores before going to the football game. It was a very 
disappointing game.  
 
Sunday I played in the bell choir at church and then stopped to see some friends from 
McDonald that I hadn’t seen in about 20 years. They were visiting their daughter who lives 
here now. It was a great visit. Later, I went over opening remarks for Monday morning at the 
Employee Day. 
 
Monday, October 12th I attended the Riley County Employee Day, starting at the AMC 
Theaters. The first presentation was by Sandra Wiley - “Customer Service – Internal Focus,” 
which was very good. After that, we had “years of service” awards and the REACH Employee 
of the Year Award. The second presentation was by “Trooper Ben”, entitled “Go & Grow”. 
Lunch was held at the Public Works campus, followed by team building activities and 
department information tables and games. I left a little early, as I wasn’t feeling too well – 
which continued Tuesday and Wednesday.  
 
Wednesday afternoon I went over today’s agenda and documents and made some calls 
concerning some of the items. That evening, I went to bell practice and then had an “early to 
bed” night.
