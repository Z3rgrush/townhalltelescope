B. Consideration of Ordinance No. 26-46 (RZ26-0006), requesting 
approval of a rezoning from the BP (Business Park) District to the R-3 
(Low-Density Multifamily) District and a conceptual preliminary site 
development plan for College & Sunnybrook Multifamily on 
approximately 20 acres, located southwest of College Boulevard and S. 
Valley Road. Planning Commission recommends approval 6 to 1.
Planner II, Taylor Vande Velde, presented the request for 333 rental 
units consisting of apartments and townhomes. Council discussed 
traffic, planned road improvements, housing demand, compatibility 
with nearby neighborhoods, building height and the proposed 
transition to adjacent single-family homes. Staff stated that the traffic 
analysis accounted for the Dillons and multifamily developments and 
planned roadway improvements.
The applicant, Curt Peterson, stated that the closest multifamily 
building would be approximately 250 feet from the homes to the 
west, that approximately 75 percent of the existing trees would be 
preserved and that the multifamily buildings would be near the same 
elevation as the homes. Council requested greater review of 
elevations, sightlines, architecture and buffering during the next 
stage of site-plan review.
Council also weighed the need for additional housing against the 
loss of land zoned for business-park uses. 
Councilmember Creighton opposed converting additional potential 
commercial land to residential, citing the City's need to strengthen its 
commercial tax base and plan for future growth in the corridor.

2. Report on a request to annex approximately 70.12 acres for 
Johnson County Aggregates, located southeast of W. Old 56 
Highway and W. 151st Street (ANX26-0004).
City Attorney, Ron Shaver, explained that annexation must 
occur before the City can consider the related rezoning and 
special-use-permit applications, although the actions could be 
scheduled for the same meeting.
City Manager, Susan Sherman, explained that annexation 
would bring the property under City control and allow operating 
conditions to be imposed through the rezoning and 
special-use-permit process. 
Staff said the later permits would address both the existing 
quarry and the proposed expansion, including operating limits, 
dust mitigation and monitoring. Measures discussed included 
enclosing a rock-transfer point, paving haul areas associated 
with the northern quarry and operating a vacuum truck daily; 
roads serving the southern quarry would remain unpaved. 
Mayor Bacon and Councilmember Schoonover requested a 
demonstrated commitment to dust control before the later 
permit decisions, citing conditions experienced by neighboring 
residents. Staff advised that the applications and Planning 
Commission record were expected to return to the Council on 
October 6.
Report accepted.
