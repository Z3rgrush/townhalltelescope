B. Consideration of Resolution No. 26-1075, SU26-0006, requesting 
approval of a special use permit for Community Food and Personal 
Support Services, Nonresidential for Starfish Project Foundation; 
located southwest of W. 135th Street and S. Brougham Drive. Planning 
Commission recommends denial 5 to 3.
Kim Hollingsworth, Planning & Development Manager, presented the 
request and summarized public input, including approximately 215 
letters and noting that the nearest residence is approximately 230 
feet from the site. Charlie Love, Chief Development Engineer, 
presented information regarding site access, circulation, hours of 
operation, and intersection and signal improvements in the area. In 
response to Council questions, staff stated the intersection 
improvements were already underway and were anticipated to be 
completed in the fall. Historical and current traffic counts were also 
discussed, including lower total intersection volumes in 2025 
compared to 2012.
Council discussion focused heavily on whether the request should 
be evaluated as a land use matter under the Unified Development 
Ordinance and Golden Criteria, rather than on generalized concerns 
about homelessness. Councilmembers asked about prior issues 
involving a Goodwill use at another location, and Ron Shaver, City 
Attorney,  explained that Goodwill had previously operated as a 
grandfathered Distance Restricted Business use, but that 
circumstance is not the same as the Starfish application. Staff and 
legal counsel also addressed enforcement, explaining that 
compliance with SUP conditions would be monitored by the City and 
that violations could lead to enforcement action or revocation under 
the UDO.
Police Chief Mike Butaud presented police call data from prior 
Starfish locations. He stated that a relatively small number of calls 
were directly attributable to Starfish operations and that incident 
levels were not unusual when compared with other retail areas. He

also stated there was no evidence of homeless camping on the 
nearby trail and explained that while it is not illegal to be homeless, 
police can respond immediately to trespassing or private-property 
complaints when requested.
Councilmembers discussed differing perspectives on the likely 
impact of the use. Some members expressed concern that 
neighboring homes and businesses could be negatively affected by 
traffic, parking, loitering, or misuse of the site, regardless of the 
applicant’s intent. Other members emphasized that Starfish is not a 
homeless shelter, but rather a service organization providing food, 
clothing, and support to residents in need, and noted testimony that 
meeting basic needs can reduce negative outcomes, including 
some criminal activity. Several members stated the application 
should be judged on the standards applicable to the proposed use 
and whether enforceable conditions could address neighborhood 
concerns.
Laverne Fields, Vice President of the Board of Starfish, spoke about 
the organization’s history and operations. He stated that parking had 
been the main concern at the prior location and that the proposed 
site would provide a safer and more functional setting for donations 
and services. He emphasized that the organization serves seniors, 
families, and others facing financial hardship, and stated Starfish 
was willing to work with the City, police, and neighbors and already 
intended to implement several of the operational measures 
discussed by Council.

C. Consideration of Ordinance No. 26-43 amending Title 9 of the Olathe 
Municipal Code (the Olathe Public Offense Code).
Bob Gallimore, Assistant City Attorney, introduced the ordinance 
and explained that it was partially new and partially intended to 
Page 9 of 13
City Council Meeting Minutes August 18, 2026
address local enforcement of conduct involving interference with 
first responders. 
Council discussion focused on the scope of the proposed offense, 
how it would be applied in practice, and whether adopting a local 
ordinance would provide better local control than relying solely on 
state law. Councilmembers asked what conduct would be prohibited, 
how intent would be established, what degree of interference or 
safety concern would be required, and how the 25-foot separation 
distance would function. Staff explained that enforcement would 
depend on the totality of the circumstances and would require a first 
responder to be engaged in official duties, to issue a verbal 
direction, and for a person to fail to comply while interfering with or 
creating a safety concern for that response activity.
Amanda Stanley, Deputy City Attorney, addressed questions 
regarding constitutional concerns, application of state law, and 
whether future changes in state law could require corresponding 
changes to the City Code. She explained that the City could choose 
whether to adopt certain state-law provisions into the municipal 
code, but that police officers would still be required to enforce 
applicable state law. She also discussed concerns that ordinance 
language in this area must be carefully applied to avoid infringing on 
protected speech or other constitutional rights.
Police Chief Mike Butaud discussed the ordinance from an 
operational and enforcement perspective. He explained that the 
measure was intended to address situations in which first 
responders are trying to manage large or volatile scenes and need 
sufficient space to perform their duties safely. He also explained that 
if the City did not adopt a comparable local ordinance, violations 
would instead be handled under state law, which could require 
arrest, booking into county jail, and additional City boarding costs. By 
adopting the ordinance locally, the City would retain greater 
discretion to issue citations and process cases in municipal court 
rather than defaulting to more restrictive state-law procedures.
Councilmembers also discussed whether the ordinance should have 
Page 10 of 13
City Council Meeting Minutes August 18, 2026
been more clearly identified as related to “halo” legislation, whether 
existing laws already covered the conduct at issue, and whether the 
proposed 25-foot standard was broader than necessary. Some 
members questioned whether the ordinance created ambiguity by 
relying on an officer’s perception of risk and whether it could be 
applied too broadly in situations involving public observation or 
verbal criticism. Others concluded that local adoption would allow 
the City to preserve municipal control over charging and adjudication 
while still addressing safety concerns for first responders.
Councilmember Felter stated that the practical benefit of retaining 
municipal control, including reducing unnecessary overnight jail stays 
and related boarding fees, was a significant factor in support of the 
ordinance. Discussion also included whether enforcement would 
differ on public versus private property, how officers would 
document violations, and how municipal prosecution would differ 
from state prosecution.

During the budget discussion, Clara Bermauer, Budget 
Manager, gave the presentation on Economy-related items. 
Council discussion covered several budget assumptions, 
program areas, infrastructure planning, and partner funding. 
Questions were asked about “regional market price,” and staff 
clarified certain pricing and participation assumptions, 
including that some offerings were more focused on adult 
programs and that the City’s intent was to keep plans 
affordable and accessible to the community. Councilmember 
Bacon asked about the City’s street evaluation process, and 
staff explained that approximately one-third of the City’s 
streets are reviewed each year, with the list of streets to be 
addressed in the following year becoming clearer in January. 
Staff further explained the value of preventative street 
maintenance, noting that each dollar spent on timely 
maintenance can avoid significantly greater reconstruction 
costs later, with an estimated savings of eight dollars in future 
improvements for every dollar invested early.
The discussion also addressed reporting and forecasting 
practices. Councilmember Vakas asked about future road 
project identification and the status of advisory committee 
reporting, and staff indicated that the relevant advisory 
committee meets annually and that a verbal report would be 
provided in late fall. Councilmember Creighton commented 
that a 2.5 percent estimate was a prudent budgeting 
assumption and discussed the delay between economic 
activity and receipt of related City revenues, noting a lag of 
roughly two to three months. Staff emphasized that revenue 
trends are evaluated over the course of the full year rather than 
month-to-month in isolation. Additional discussion addressed 
the use of the OCI (Overall Condition Index) street condition 
measure, with staff explaining that streets with OCI scores 
above 80 are generally not prioritized for maintenance, while 
attention is focused more heavily on streets in the 50 and 60 
range. Staff also noted that Olathe’s OCI compares favorably,

Council also discussed parks and economic development 
funding. A question was raised about the location of a future 
dog park, and staff responded that no location had been 
selected yet. Councilmember Vakas asked about the amount 
provided to the Chamber and what the storefront grant 
program includes. Staff explained that the storefront grant is a 
partnership between the City and the Olathe Chamber and 
applies to eligible buildings in the downtown area, with building
