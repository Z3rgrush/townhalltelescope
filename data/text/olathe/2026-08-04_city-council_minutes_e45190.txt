6. NEW BUSINESS
A. Consideration of Ordinance No. 26-40 (RZ26-0005), requesting 
approval of a rezoning from the AG (Agricultural) District to the PD 
(Planned) District and a preliminary site development plan for 133rd and 
Black Bob Mixed Use on approximately 5 acres, southeast of W. 133rd 
Street and Black Bob Road. Planning Commission recommends 
approval 6 to 0.
Senior Planner, Nathan Jurey, provided a presentation for the 
Council regarding this project.
Council members asked questions about both the design of the 
proposed development and its potential traffic impacts.
Councilmember Vakas asked whether the units would be offered for 
sale or rent, and Mr. Jurey said that had not yet been decided. He 
also asked whether the units would be elevator-accessible, and Mr. 
Jurey said he believed they would be.

Councilmember Deneault focused on traffic along 133rd Street and 
asked when a traffic signal might be warranted. City Engineer, Nate 
Baldwin, said there is no justification for one at this time, but it would 
likely become warranted with future development and said staff 
regularly monitors traffic data at those intersections.
Councilmember Schoonover encouraged the City to consider 
improvements to 133rd Street sooner rather than later, and 
Councilmember Creighton agreed. Mayor Bacon also pointed out 
that the renderings appeared to show the street not lining up, but Mr. 
Baldwin clarified that the street alignment would be correct.
The applicant, Jib Felter, said the project is intended to serve the 
“missing middle” housing market. He explained that he had worked 
with staff to increase density so the units could be sold below 
market value. He also stated that each unit would be designed to 
allow for a potential elevator shaft if desired. Mayor Bacon asked if 
they had used this type of elevator before. Mr. Felter said they had 
not, but they were familiar with the product and pleased to bring it to 
this project.

B. DISCUSSION ITEMS
1. General Overview of the Proposed 2027 Water & Sewer, 
Stormwater, and Solid Waste Budgets, 2027-2031 Capital 
Improvement Plan (CIP), and fees
Clara Bernauer, Budget Manager, presented an overview of 
the water and sewer, stormwater, and solid waste funds. She 
said handouts were provided to the Council and would also be 
posted online for the public.
Mary Jaeger, Infrastructure Director, then provided more 
detailed information on the Capital Improvement Plan and 
anticipated future needs of the City’s water and sewer 
systems. 
Council discussion focused on long-term system planning, 
risks, and cost management. Topics included whether it may 
eventually make sense to bring more waterline rehabilitation 
work in-house, continue partnering with outside organizations, 
or compare City utility operations against other regional 
providers. It was also noted that many of the planned utility 
investments are driven by federal regulatory requirements. 
However, staff indicated that the City pursues grant 
opportunities when available.
Staff said that they are always evaluating water usage and they 
look at models several years into the future to help guide 
where there they anticipate the most need. Additional 
discussion addressed deferred maintenance, within 
wastewater infrastructure, the tracking of water main breaks 
and system capacity for new development. 
Council members asked about the food waste program and 
staff said it is performing well and expanding with grant 
support. They did indicate that curbside service is not under 
consideration as it would not be cost effective at this time.
