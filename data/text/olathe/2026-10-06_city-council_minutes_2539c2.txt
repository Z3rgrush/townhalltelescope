E. Consideration of Resolution No. 26-1083 (SU26-0007), requesting 
approval of a special use permit for Extractive Industries (Rock 
Quarry/Mine) for Johnson County Aggregates Quarry Expansion on 
approximately 108.71 acres, located southeast of W. 151st Street and 
Old 56 Highway. Planning Commission recommends approval 6 to 2.
Senior Planner, Nathan Jurey, presented Items E and F regarding 
the proposed special use permit for expansion of the Johnson 
County Aggregates quarry and amendment of the special use permit 
governing the existing quarry.
Councilmembers discussed the duration of quarry operations and 
the effect of approving or denying the requests. Staff explained that 
significant rock reserves remain at the existing quarry and that 
without approval of the proposed special use permit conditions, 
existing operations could continue without the additional dust-control, 
monitoring and operational standards proposed with these 
applications. Staff stated that the new conditions would be 
enforceable by the City in addition to applicable state and federal 
requirements. 
The applicant, represented by Polsinelli lawyer, Curt Peterson, 
described proposed improvements intended to reduce impacts on 
surrounding properties. Measures discussed included additional 
Page 7 of 12
City Council Meeting Minutes October 6, 2026
containment and water suppression at the crusher, daily street 
sweeping, screening of quarry operations, closure of a roadway to 
truck traffic near residences, advance communication regarding 
blasting and increased public access to monitoring and compliance 
information. The applicant stated that the existing quarry is currently 
in regulatory compliance but that the proposed conditions would 
establish additional mitigation measures. 
Councilmembers discussed blasting and potential impacts to 
neighboring homes. The applicant described the use of pre-blast 
surveys and monitoring records to establish existing conditions and 
evaluate future claims of property damage. The applicant stated that 
blasting records are maintained for each event and that homes within 
the applicable area could be evaluated. Councilmember Felter 
requested that monitoring and compliance information be made 
readily available to residents rather than requiring individual Kansas 
Open Records Act requests. 
Councilmembers also discussed the location of the crusher, future 
underground mining, weather-related operating limitations, berms 
and screening, property-damage claims and preservation of 
right-of-way for future improvements to 151st Street. The applicant 
stated that the crusher would remain centrally located, operations 
could be suspended during unfavorable wind conditions, and the 
company maintains insurance and a claims-resolution process for 
alleged property damage.

Councilmembers returned to air-quality and dust concerns. The

whether dust could adversely affect residents with respiratory 
conditions even if silica levels remained within regulatory standards. 
Staff explained that the City's review considered overall air-quality 
standards and stated that the proposed crusher improvements and 
dust-control measures were expected to improve conditions.Council 
questioned the lack of baseline particulate measurements at the 
property line. 
Councilmembers requested establishment of baseline 
measurements and asked whether the proposed improvements 
could be completed before expansion of quarry operations. Staff 
indicated that an additional stipulation could be added. Council also 
discussed taking measurements near the crusher and making 
monitoring information publicly accessible. 
Councilmembers discussed annual review of the dust-control plan, 
the ability to require additional mitigation measures when necessary, 
periodic testing outside regularly scheduled monitoring periods and 
advance notification of blasting. Staff stated that monitoring results 
could be requested as needed and City Manager, Susan Sherman, 
indicated that information could be made publicly available without 
requiring residents to submit KORA requests. The applicant 
indicated a willingness to cooperate with additional measures when 
warranted. 
Councilmembers compared the proposed conditions with 
requirements applicable to other quarries and discussed whether 
the proposal could establish stronger operating standards. The 
applicant stated that the operation meets and in some instances 
exceeds federal requirements and described the proposed 
conditions as a higher standard for quarry operations in the area. 
Council also discussed paving and dust suppression on internal 
quarry roads. 
Councilmembers acknowledged the concerns and frustration 
expressed by neighboring residents but concluded that the 
proposed conditions offered an opportunity to improve an existing 
situation. Councilmember Creighton stated that he understood the
