2. PUBLIC HEARINGS
A. Public hearing, pursuant to K.S.A. 79-2929 and K.S.A. 79-2988, to hear 
comments regarding the proposed City of Olathe FY 2027 Budget and 
exceeding the revenue neutral rate.
Mayor Bacon opened the public hearing. Budget Analyst, Ashley 
Wooten, presented information regarding the proposed Fiscal Year 
2027 budget and the City’s intent to exceed the revenue neutral rate 
(RNR). Mayor Bacon asked what revenue reduction would result if 
the City maintained the revenue neutral rate. Staff responded that 
maintaining revenue neutrality would require an estimated 
22.496-mill reduction, resulting in an approximate $3 million annual 
loss in property-tax revenue.
Individuals who spoke regarding the budget:
Doug Doerfler
Charlotte Ohara
Kathy Winters
Aaron Haffey
Councilmember Vakas thanked residents who spoke during the 
hearing and acknowledged public concerns regarding taxation, 
particularly for persons on fixed incomes. He noted that the City also 
faces increased operating costs, including recurring double-digit 
increases in health-care costs.
Following public comment, the Council discussed the proposed

budget, anticipated service demands associated with growth, and

Councilmember Essex recognized the difficult circumstances 
described by residents and expressed appreciation for the City’s 
property-tax rebate program. She stated that, while the City has 
taken steps to control expenses, increasing taxes and inflation 
continue to create financial pressure for residents. She indicated her 
intent to vote against exceeding the RNR.
Councilmember Creighton thanked staff for their assistance during 
his first budget process. He cited rising household costs, including 
fuel and insurance, and noted that approximately 800 one- and 
two-family dwelling building permits had been issued between 
January 2025 and July 2026. He stated that continued growth 
increases the City’s need for solid-waste collection, public safety, 
engineering, street maintenance, and emergency-response 
services. He indicated his intent to support exceeding the RNR to 
maintain services and accommodate growth.
Councilmember Schoonover asked staff to explain projected 
General Fund and overall budget increases. Staff explained that the 
FY 2027 budget is developed using current revenue and 
expenditure projections rather than only the prior-year adopted 
budget. Staff further stated revenues are reviewed monthly, sales 
tax is the City’s largest General Fund revenue source, and 
stronger-than-projected revenues may support future capital 
improvement program (CIP) funding.
Councilmember Felter asked whether the projected 2026 revenue 
variance of nearly $10 million would be directed to the CIP. Staff 
clarified that the amount represented the projected 2026 year-end 
position and was being considered as part of longer-term structural 
balance. Staff stated that if sales-tax revenues were flat, future 
reductions could be required in both the CIP and General Fund. 
Staff also noted that the Council’s 30 percent ending-fund-balance 
policy must be maintained and that those reserves are not available 
for direct property-tax relief. Dr. Felter thanked staff for identifying

Councilmember Vakas noted that, based on an average Olathe 
home value of approximately $460,000, the estimated impact 
discussed was approximately $102 per homeowner. He also stated 
that the county owns the Chiefs training facility; City Manager, Susan 
Sherman, stated that the facility will be state-owned.
Councilmember Deneault thanked residents for participating and 
stated that the proposed budget reflected more forward-looking 
planning than in prior years. He requested continued attention to 
potential structural imbalance in future budgets. He also asked about 
the property-tax relief program. Staff reported that the FY 2027 
budget includes $300,000 for the program, compared with 
$250,000 in the current year, and that the program will close when 
the budgeted amount is exhausted. Staff explained that the program 
includes separate veteran, senior, and income-eligible components, 
with a current maximum income-eligible rebate of $750, and is 
administered on a first-come, first-served basis. Staff indicated it 
would consider ways to improve public communication and evaluate 
whether future program administration could better prioritize 
applicants based on need.
Mayor Bacon clarified that Resolution No. 26-1077 does not 
establish the final mill levy for the FY 2027 budget. Rather, it 
authorizes the City to exceed the revenue neutral rate as required by 
Kansas law. The final property-tax rate will be determined when the 
City Council considers adoption of the FY 2027 budget at its 
September 15, 2026 meeting.
The Council’s discussion reflected competing priorities of limiting 
the tax burden on residents, particularly seniors and households 
facing inflationary pressures, while maintaining service levels, 
addressing growth-related demands, preserving required reserves, 
and sustaining the City’s long-term financial and capital plans.
