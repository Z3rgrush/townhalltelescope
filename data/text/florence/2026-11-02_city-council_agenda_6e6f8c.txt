PROCLAMATION 
WHEREAS, the greatest acknowledgement of our freedom is to honor Our armed forces veterans 
who have sacrificed and in many instances paid the ultimate price for our freedom; and  
 
WHEREAS, in 1919, President Wilson proclaimed November 11 as the first commemoration of 
Armistice Day stating "To us in America, the reflections of Armistice Day will be filled with solemn 
pride in the heroism of those who died in the country's service and with gra titude for the victory, both 
because of the thing from which it has freed us and because of the opportunity it has given America to 
show her sympathy with peace and justice in the councils of the nations..." , and  
 
WHEREAS, City of Florence citizens, along with millions of other Americans, can attest to the 
importance of veterans' sacrifices for and contributions to our freedom and security; and  
 
WHEREAS, the City of Florence realizes many veterans who gave so much of themselves came 
home without any recognition and were denied educational and occupational opportunities; and  
 
WHEREAS, both men and women of our armed forces continue to be an inspiration to all of us 
through their demonstration of courage, leadership and commitment in service to our community and 
country, and  
 
WHEREAS, we in the City of Florence always will remember and pay respect to our military 
community, both active and inactive, for their contribution, dedication and commitment to the cause of 
our freedom; and  
 
WHEREAS, the City of Florence would like to specifically recognize the veterans who currently 
serve as City of Florence Employees:  
 
Aaron Jackson, Sergeant, US Army  
Tom Piltingsrud, Lieutenant Colonel, US Army 
Tom Danler, Corporal, US Marines 
Richard Brown. Specialist 4, US Army 
Jacob Goode, Corporal, US Marines, & Specialist, US National Guard 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, LET IT BE PROCLAIMED by the City of Florence City Council, that in 
observance of Veterans' Day, on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, Florence proudly joins the rest of 
our nation to salute and give special honor and recognition to the men and women who served us in 
our armed forces.  
 
In witness whereof, I have hereunto set my hand and caused the Seal of the City of Florence, Colorado 
to be affixed this 2nd day of November 2026.

c) Consider authorizing the release of Certificate of Occupancy restrictions for the Pike View  
Subdivision upon receipt and acceptance of an acceptable performance bond for required 
sidewalk public improvements 
 
Planning Director Ashley Fox stated the Pike View developer was requesting a release of 
restrictions on Certificates of Occupancy for lots within Phase 1 of the development prior to the 
final completion of all required public sidewalk improvements. Under Section IX of the 
Subdivision Improvement Agreement for Pike View, the restriction on the issuance of Certificates 
of Occupancy or temporary Certificates of Occupancy may be released on all or part of the lots in 
a phase upon recommendation of the City Manager and formal determination by the City Council 
upon provision of acceptable collateral. In accordance with Section IX of the governing 
agreement, the required collateral must be an amount not less than 125% of the estimated cost of 
construction for the remaining sidewalk public improvements, which is $73,280. A performance 
bond has been submitted by the developer.  
 
Councilor Stone moved to authorize the release of Certificate of Occupancy restrictions for the 
applicable lots in the Pike View Subdivision Phase 1 pursuant to Section IX of the Subdivision 
Improvement Agreement. 
Seconded by: Councilor Gardner 
6 Ayes 
Motion Passed: 6 – 0

Emergency Action Plan for the Florence Chamber of Commerce:  Holly Jolly Christmas 2025 
Purpose: 
To ensure the safety of participants, vendors, and the public during the annual Christmas  
event while managing Main Street closures and responding effectively to emergencies. 
 
Event Details: 
• Event Name:   Holly Jolly Christmas 2026, Florence, CO 
• Location: Main Street, Florence 
• Dates/Times: On November 28,  8 am -   12:30 pm.    December 5, December 12 and 
December 19,2026.     
• Organizer: Florence Chamber of Commerce 
• Contact: Joen Elliott, Florence Chamber of Commerce President   -  303-880-8146 
 
I. Pre-Event Planning 
1. Coordination with Local Authorities: 
o Notify the Florence Police Department, Fire Department, and EMS about the 
event details. 
o Obtain necessary permits for street closures and event activities. 
2. Main Street Closure Plan: 
o Closure Time: Close Main Street to vehicular traffic at least 2 hours before 
the event starts and maintain street closure until 2 hours after event ends. 
o Barricades: City of Florence maintenance will set up sturdy barricades at 
designated intersections to restrict access. Specific placement will block 
traffic on Main Street (HWY 115) from 300 Main Street (Maple)  to 100 East Main 
Street   (Pikes’s Peak ). 
o Signage: Signs will be posted including “detour” signs and event information 
at key points. 
o Access Points: Designated entry points for emergency vehicles will be at 
Florence Fire Department Station 1, 300 Main Street, Florence, CO 81226. 
 
3. Vendor Setup: 
o Assign specific booth locations to vendors. 
o Provide vendors with safety guidelines and emergency contact numbers. 
4. Communication Plan: 
o Establish a communication hub for event staff and volunteers. 
o Distribute a contact list of key personnel, including event coordinators, 
security, and first responders. 
o Equip staff with radios or cell phones for real-time communication. 
5. First Aid and Emergency Stations: 
o Set up first aid stations at Florence Fire Department Station 1 
o Ensure first aid kits and AEDs (Automated External Defibrillators) are 
available. 
6. Training: 
o Conduct safety briefings for staff and volunteers. 
o Provide training on emergency procedures, including evacuation and first aid. 
 
II. Emergency Procedures 
1. Medical Emergencies: 
o Notify the nearest first aid station or call 911. 
o Event staff should clear the area and provide assistance as needed. 
o Document the incident for post-event reporting. 
2. Fire or Hazardous Incidents: 
o Evacuate the immediate area. 
o Contact the Fire Department at [Insert Number]. 
o Use fire extinguishers if safe to do so. 
o Alert vendors and attendees through public address systems. 
o 
 
3. Weather Emergencies: 
o Monitor weather forecasts throughout the event. 
o In case of severe weather (e.g., thunderstorms, high winds): 
▪ Direct attendees to designated shelters or safe indoor locations. 
▪ Pause or cancel the event if necessary. 
 
4. Lost Child or Missing Person: 
o Report to the communication hub immediately. 
o Broadcast a description of the individual over the PA system. 
o Deploy event staff to search the area. 
5. Traffic or Security Incidents: 
o Notify the Florence Police Department for any security concerns or traffic 
violations. 
o Maintain barricades and enforce road closures. 
o Document any incidents for follow-up. 
 
III. Post-Event Actions 
1. Street Reopening: 
o Ensure all vendors have vacated the street. 
o Remove barricades and signage once cleared by the Police Department. 
2. Debrief: 
o Hold a post-event meeting with staff and volunteers to review successes and 
challenges. 
o Compile an incident report if applicable. 
3. Cleanup: 
o Organize a cleanup crew to restore Main Street to its original condition. 
o Dispose of waste properly and recycle where possible. 
 
 
 
 
4. Feedback: 
o Collect feedback from attendees, vendors, and staff to improve future 
events.

11-2-2026 
Water Department Report 
 
The Water Department continues routine treatment-plant and distribution system operations to 
provide safe, reliable water service while advancing key projects and initiatives that support the 
City's water infrastructure. This report highlights significant activities, major project updates, and 
other noteworthy accomplishments from the past month, along with any important issues requiring 
attention or follow-up. 
 
Water Department Projects & Major Initiatives     
 
 Fremont County Airport Storm Drainage Project 
 The Water Department has completed the first of four planned water line relocations. The 
first relocation was located at the intersection of Shoop and Decker. Work included 
installing an isolation valve and lowering the existing water main approximately four feet to 
accommodate the proposed storm drainage culvert being installed by Fremont County. 
 During planning for the second water line relocation, the Water Department identified that 
the affected section of water main was part of a loop within the airport water distribution 
system. After evaluating the existing infrastructure and available alternatives, the we 
determined that a full relocation could be avoided by cutting and capping one end of the 
existing line and installing a blow-off assembly on the opposite end. This approach provides 
a practical operational solution while avoiding unnecessary construction and generating 
significant cost savings for the City. 
 Excavation has begun for the third water line relocation. The Department anticipates 
completing both the alternative work for the second relocation and the third relocation 
during the week of November 2nd. The fourth and final relocation remains to be completed. 
 
 Risk & Resilience Assessment / Emergency Response Plan Updates 
 The updated Risk and Resilience Assessment has been completed, and certification has 
been submitted to the United States Environmental Protection Agency. 
 We are continuing to work with HR Green to update the Emergency Response Plan for 
submission to the EPA by December 31, 2026. 
 
 East Florence Master Meter Replacement Project 
 The City issued the Request for Proposals (RFP) for the East Florence Master Meter 
Replacement Project on October 1st, 2026. A mandatory pre-proposal meeting was held on 
October 12th, with four contractors in attendance. Proposals were due October 15th.  
 Rhoades Construction was selected for the project with a proposal of $48,275.15 which is 
significantly lower than the previously estimated project cost of $77,000. 
 The City issued the Notice of Award to Rhoades Construction on October 19th, followed by 
the Notice to Proceed on October 27th. 
 The contractor anticipates beginning the initial project requirements during the week of 
November 2nd. 
 
 System-Wide Radio & Communications Upgrade 
 The City has completed the Request for Proposals process for the system-wide radio and 
communications upgrade and has awarded the project to Talos Technologies. The contract 
has been executed, and the Notice to Proceed has been issued. 
 Talos Technologies is scheduled to be on-site during the week of November 2, 2026, to 
begin implementation. The project is anticipated to take approximately one week to 
complete. 
 
Significant Events & Unplanned Activities 
 
 Water Leak Repair – West Third Street (Highway 115) 
 The Water Department identified a water leak on West Third Street, which is also designated 
as State Highway 115. After confirming the leak, the we promptly initiated the process of 
obtaining the necessary Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) permit because the 
affected infrastructure was located within the CDOT right-of-way. 
 On October 28, 2026, the Department temporarily closed a small section of Highway 115 
and established a traffic detour around the work area to safely investigate and repair the 
leak. 
 We were able to locate the leak without cutting into or excavating the highway asphalt, 
which allowed us to avoiding additional roadway restoration work. 
 The water distribution crew successfully repaired the leak, and the highway was reopened 
at approximately 2:30 p.m. 
 North Raw Water Pump Station – VFD Cabinet Cooling Unit  
 The air conditioning unit inside the VFD cabinet at the North Raw Water Pump Station failed 
approximately one year ago. The unit is critical for cooling the variable frequency drives 
(VFDs) that operate our vertical turbine pumps and preventing damage to this expensive 
equipment. 
 Assistant Superintendent Aaron Wood worked with multiple vendors to locate replacement 
parts or a suitable replacement unit. After extensive research, it was determined that the 
original unit and its replacement parts were no longer in production and that there is no 
available direct replacement unit.  
 Aaron obtained multiple quotes for a comparable replacement unit, with two vendors 
quoting over $11,000. After seeking an additional option, he obtained a quote for $6,720 for 
the same replacement unit. 
 Cost savings: Through his diligence and persistence, Aaron secured a replacement option 
that will save the City more than $5,000 compared to the higher quotes.
