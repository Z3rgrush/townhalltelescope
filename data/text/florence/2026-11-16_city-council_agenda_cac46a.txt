STAFF REPORT:   LORI COBLER, CITY MANAGER 
 
Agenda Item: Public Hearing - Ordinance No. 11 – 2026, an ordinance authorizing the 
termination of a 1964 Trust Agreement and conveyance of real property located 
at 100 East Front Street  
 
Department: Administration/Legal 
 
Background/Description: 
 
The City of Florence has owned Lots 7 and 8, Block 4, Colorado Coal and Iron Company’s Addition, 
which are associated with the Florence Pioneer Museum and Research Center, since 1964.  In June 1964, 
the property was conveyed to the City by the Fraternal Order of Eagles. Shortly thereafter, the City 
entered into an agreement with the Pioneer Day Board to allow the Board to use and occupy the building 
for the purpose of establishing and operating a museum for as long as the museum exists. The City 
Council formally accepted the agreement at its July 6, 1964, regular meeting. 
 
Lot 8 was vacant when it was conveyed to the City.  In 1976, the Museum, through fundraising efforts 
and a grant from the State Centennial Commission, constructed an additional building on Lot 8 that was 
connected to the original building on Lot 7. The two structures have continued to serve as the home of the 
Florence Pioneer Museum and Research Center. 
 
Staff Comments:  
 
The City of Florence holds record legal title to the property where the Florence Pioneer Museum and 
Research Center is located at (100 East Front Street).  The property has been continuously operated as a 
public museum for more than 60 years and has long-standing relationship with the City.  Transferring 
simple fee ownership to the Museum terminates the historical 1964 trust and places one hundred percent 
of the ongoing responsibility for the property with the non-profit entity that operates it. 
 
The proposed Mutual Termination of 1964 Trust Agreement and Release and Quitclaim Deed would 
accomplish this by terminating the 1964 agreement, releasing the Museum's equitable interest in the 
property, conveying bare legal title.  
 
As part of the proposed agreement, the Museum expressly  assumes full legal and financial responsibility 
for all ongoing and future maintenance, insurance, utilities, structural repairs, and capital improvements 
associated with the property, relieving the City's General Fund of recurring financial exposure .  The City 
would also relinquish any reversionary rights or other restrictions associated with the original 1964 trust 
arrangement.

MUTUAL TERMINATION OF 1964 TRUST AND RELEASE 
THIS MUTUAL TERMINATION OF 1964 TRUST AGREEMENT AND RELEASE (this "Agreement") 
is made and entered into this ____ day of __________________, 2026, by and between the CITY OF 
FLORENCE, COLORADO , a municipal corporation ("City"), and FLORENCE PIONEER 
MUSEUM AND RESEARCH CENTER, a Colorado non-profit corporation, legal successor-in-interest 
to the Pioneer Day Board ("Museum") 
RECITALS 
A.  Pursuant to a Warranty Deed and Contract dated July 6, 1964 (the "1964 Contract"), the City accepted 
record legal title to Lots 7 and 8, Block 4, Colorado Coal and Iron Company's Addition to the Town (now 
City) of Florence, Colorado (the "Property"), to hold "in trust" for the benefit of the Pioneer Day Board.  
B.  The Museum is the legal corporate successor-in-interest to the Pioneer Day Board.  
C.  The City and the Museum now desire to formally extinguish and terminate the 1964 trust relationship, 
release all equitable trust interests, and authorize the City to convey pure fee simple title to the Museum 
via a separate Quitclaim Deed without ongoing municipal restrictions, warranties, reservations, or 
reversionary interests.  
AGREEMENT  
1. Termination of 1964 Trust:  
The City and the Museum hereby mutually terminate, extinguish, and cancel the 1964 Contract 
and any trust arrangement, fiduciary obligations, or equitable restrictions created thereunder.  
 
2. Release of Equitable Interest:  
The Museum hereby remises, releases, and quitclaims unto the City all of its right, title, interest, 
claim, and equitable trust estate in and to the Property under the 1964 Contract, merging full 
legal and equitable title into the City.  
 
3. Legal Consideration:  
As consideration for this Agreement and the subsequent conveyance of the Property, the Museum 
expressly assumes 100% of all ongoing, future, and historical maintenance, insurance, utility, 
structural, and capital repair liabilities for the Property, relieving the City of recurring municipal 
financial obligations.  
 
4. Waiver of Reversionary Rights:  
The City expressly disclaims and waives any reversionary rights, rights of entry, powers of 
termination, or operational restrictions upon the Property.

Agenda Item: Consider the transfer of ownership and management of the City of Florence 
July Fourth Festival 
 
Department: Administration  
 
 
Staff Recommendation: 
The City of Florence has historically sponsored and managed the annual Fourth of July Festival and 
related activities. The America's Parade Committee has expressed its willingness and capability to 
assume responsibility for planning, organizing, operating, fundraising, and managing the event. City 
Council is being asked to authorize the transfer of ownership and operational responsibility for the 
festival to the America's Parade Committee. 
 
Background: 
The Fourth of July Festival is a longstanding community event that celebrates Independence Day 
and attracts residents and visitors to Florence. The event has traditionally required significant City 
staff time and municipal resources for planning, coordination, and administration. 
 
The America's Parade Committee is a local nonprofit/community organization whose mission 
includes organizing community celebrations and preserving Florence's patriotic traditions. The 
Committee has requested that responsibility for the annual Fourth of July Festival be transferred 
from the City to the Committee beginning with the next festival cycle. 
 
The proposed transfer would place responsibility for event planning, programming, sponsorships, 
fundraising, vendor management, entertainment, volunteer coordination, and overall festival 
operations with the America's Parade Committee. Any future City support would be provided only 
as approved by City Council through separate agreements, permits, or budget appropriations. 
 
Financial Impact: 
Transfer of the festival is expected to reduce direct City expenditures and staff involvement 
associated with annual event management. Any future City contributions, in- kind services, or 
support will require separate Council approval and budget authorization. 
 
Attachments: 
None. 
 
Suggested Motion: 
Motion to authorize the transfer of ownership and management of the City of Florence Fourth of 
July Festival to the America's Parade Committee and authorizing the City Manager to execute any 
necessary documents to accomplish the transfer.
