MISSION STATEMENT 
Our mission is to provide a high quality of life for the citizens of 
Federal Heights, while preserving a close-knit atmosphere, through 
responsible and effective stewardship of all resources, prudent 
economic development, enhancement of our current services, and 
participation in public, private, and regional partnerships. 
VISION STATEMENT 
The City of Federal Heights: 
- is comprised of distinct neighborhoods that are safe, secure, and         
have convenient access to services and amenities; 
- has a sustainable and growing economic base that fosters diverse    
employment and business opportunities; 
- offers a community-focused system of recreational, educational, 
and cultural activities for people of all ages and income levels; 
- capitalized upon its strategic location within the Denver metro         
area and adapts to a changing environment; and 
- proudly enjoys a City government that is responsive and                  
responsible to the needs of its citizens.

CALL TO ORDER 
The October 6, 2026, Regular City Council Meeting of the City of Federal Heights was called to 
order by Mayor Linda S. Montoya at 6:01 p.m. 
PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE 
Those in attendance recited the pledge of allegiance. 
ROLL CALL 
Council Members in attendance were Mayor Linda S. Montoya, Mayor Pro Tem Celeste Arner, 
Sonia Jensen, Shari DeVries, Kevin Dougherty, and Mike Vallero. Also present were City 
Manager Jacquie Halburnt, City Attorney Bill Hayashi, and City Clerk Patti Lowell.  It was 
announced that a quorum was in attendance.   
AGENDA REVIEW 
No changes were made to the agenda. 
APPROVAL OF MINUTES  
Motion was made by Council Member DeVries, seconded by Council Member Dougherty to 
approve the minutes of the Regular Meeting of September 15, 2026, and the Study Session of 
September 15, 2026.  The motion passed unanimously. 
APPROVAL OF ACCOUNTS PAYABLE – SEPTEMBER 16 – OCTOBER 6, 2026 
Motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Vallero to approve 
the September 16 – October 6, 2026, Accounts Payable in the amounts of $196,083.96 in the 
General Fund, $501,004.66 in the Utility Fund, $2,264.92 in the Drainage Utility Enterprise, 
$470,407.23 in Road Improvement, $557,549.54 in Capital Improvements, and $440,314.30 in 
Payroll for a total of $2,167.624.61.  The motion passed unanimously. 
PROCLAMATION – OCTOBER 7 REMEMBRANCE 
Mayor Montoya read the proclamation designating October 7th as a day of remembrance and 
community resilience. 
PUBLIC COMMENT 
Danyel Vail commented on the importance of transparency, consistency, and accountability in 
City government for Federal Heights residents, employees, and first responders’ families. 
Moses Chavez informed the City Council of his interest in holding a holiday market in the Water 
World parking lot. 
Art Dubus warned City Council that their voting following tonight’s upcoming Executive 
Session was going to have a long-lasting effect on the men and women on the City’s emergency 
services. 
Sharon Richardson emphasized the importance of continuity of government in conducting City 
business.  She stated that, during her time on the City Council, an administrator had been 
removed from the position, which she said caused significant harm to the City and is something 
that she regrets.  She commented that attracting qualified managers can be difficult.  She also 
stated that information provided to the City Council within the previous two weeks did not, in 
her opinion, justify dismissal of the City Manager.

City government.

approximately 95% of City employees want a change in City leadership. 
Mary Tunberg stated that, in her opinion, Federal Heights has a high crime rate and that 
residents need accountability from City management. 
Jacquelyn Thomas stated that she believes that the former Chiefs had been excellent chiefs.  She 
added that the City deserves quality leadership that earns the trust of first responders and 
residents. 
Lisa Lopez told City Council that when the people they represent are expressing concerns about 
an issue, Council must be willing to make difficult decisions. She stated that when she considers 
an individual to be a “poison” to her team or division, she feels obligated to take action to stop it 
from spreading, despite the time and personal effort required. 
Emanuel Aguilar, representing Way Church, stated that Lisa Lopez is his wife and that the 
church serves the community by providing worship services and distributing food.

EXECUTIVE SESSION –  
Motion was made by Council Member Vallero, seconded by Council Member DeVries to go 
into an executive session pursuant to C.R.S. Sec. 24-6-402(4)(f)(I) for a personnel matter 
regarding the City Manager.  The motion passed unanimously. 
The meeting was recessed at 6:50 p.m. and was reconvened at 8:20 p.m. 
DISCUSSION AND DECISION REGARDING THE CITY MANAGER’S 
EMPLOYMENT  
Motion was made by Council Member Vallero, seconded by Council Member Dougherty to 
terminate the City Manager’s employment for no cause.  The motion failed by a vote of 2-4. 
PUBLIC COMMENT 
Ken Murphy criticized the City Council’s handling of the matter of the City Manager’s 
employment and stated that he had been dealing with the situation for approximately one year. 
Art Dubus told the City Council that he doesn’t think that they are supporting the firefighters, 
paramedics, and police officers.   
Jim Fenimore called the vote a tragedy, adding criticism of the City Attorney and the City 
Manager. 
Danyel Vail said that she was disappointed in the City Council’s decision, adding that her sister 
and the people she considers to be her family as well as countless other Police Department and 
Fire Department families will continue dealing with their instability. 
Sharon Richardson said that there are many residents of the City who support stability and want 
to move forward to solve City problems in a timely fashion. 
Jim Engle, managing partner of Hinkle & Company, talked about his company, which had 
submitted a bid to provide audit services to the City. 
A resident expressed concern that the City’s records do not identify the property lines for his 
home and stated that he was required to obtain a survey when rebuilding his fence because the 
Permit Department could not determine the property lines.  He asked the City Council to address 
how the issue of identifying property lines could be resolved. 
Jacquelyn Thomas urged the community and first responders to stand together and rebuild trust.   
QUESTIONS AND COMMENTS AND COMMITTEE UPDATES FROM MUNICIPAL 
OFFICIALS 
Council Member Jensen explained her vote against termination of the City Manager, stating that 
she had spoken with department directors to understand their perspectives.  She noted that an 
outside investigation, initiated in response to a complaint by a City employee, had taken 
approximately six months and resulted in findings that supported the termination of the Fire 
Chief and the Police Chief.  She stated that the City has dedicated first responders who had 
appeared before the City Council to discuss their needs and concerns.  She added that she 
believed that there had been a coordinated effort by one of the individuals who was terminated 
to influence the narrative presented to her, which is why she sought input directly from 
department directors. 
Council Member DeVries explained that she had voted in favor of the City Manager’s 
termination because she believes the City needs to rebuild trust with residents, City staff, and the 
Minutes of the City Council Meeting 
October 6, 2026 
Police and Fire departments, and that the current process is not working.  She stated that she 
would like to move forward from this point. 
Council Member Dougherty explained that he voted against termination of the City Manager’s 
employment because he had heard accusations but had not seen evidence to substantiate them. 
Council Member Vallero explained that he had voted against termination because he had 
thoroughly reviewed the matter.  He stated that decisions of this nature are not as easy or clear-
cut as some may believe.  He said that the City Charter clearly delineates responsibilities, with 
personnel matters being the responsibility of the City Manager, while the City Council provides 
oversight but should not cross that line to interfere with the City Manager’s responsibilities.  He 
added that he believes in the process, does not want to compromise the core integrity of the City, 
and believes the decision was appropriate under the circumstances. 
Mayor Pro Tem Arner stated that the decision was difficult and that she has always admired the 
firefighters and police officers for the work they perform, recognizing that their jobs are 
stressful.  She stated that this matter came before the City Council because. in her opinion, some 
Council members had acted outside the scope of their responsibilities.  She said that she had 
heard statements that City staff were overwhelmed and over-worked, but she did not believe the 
City should be placed in a financially unsustainable position in response to concerns expressed 
publicly or through the media.  She added that there is significant work to be done in the City 
beginning immediately and that the City needs to move forward. 
Mayor Montoya stated that this issue has been a significant part of her life for the past 11 
months and that some individuals have gone so far as to blame her for the termination of the 
Police Chief and Fire Chief.  She stated that she has been trying to keep City residents, police 
officers, and other first responders safe.  She added that the Police Department is short-handed 
by six officers, and that she does not believe that crime has decreased. 
ADJOURNMENT 
The regular City Council meeting was adjourned at 9:02 p.m.

CALL TO ORDER 
The October 6, 2026, Study Session of the City Council of the City of Federal Heights was called 
to order by Mayor Linda S. Montoya at 9:02 p.m. 
ROLL CALL 
Council Members in attendance were Mayor Linda S. Montoya, Mayor Pro Tem Celeste Arner, 
Shari DeVries, Kevin Dougherty, Sonia Jensen, and Mike Vallero.  Also present were City 
Manager Jacquie Halburnt, City Attorney Bill Hayashi and City Clerk Patti Lowell.   
WARD II CITY COUNCIL VACANCY INTERVIEWS 
City Council interviewed the two candidates, Kathryn Heistand and Randy Longstrom, for the 
vacant Ward II Council position. 
CONSIDERATION OF CDR – AUDIT SERVICES (Finance Director Tim Weitzman) 
Director Weitzman reviewed the two responses received for the recent RFP for audit services.  
John Cutler & Associates proposed $13,500 per year for five years, for a total cost of $67,500, 
while Hinkle & Company proposed a five-year total of $178,200.  Council Member Jensen 
asked for clarification regarding the recent peer review failure of Cutler & Associates.  Director 
Weitzman responded that the firm represented that it has implemented corrective procedures to 
address the identified issues and expects to receive a positive rating when the next review is 
completed in 2027.  Director Weitzman recommended retaining Cutler & Associates for the 
2026 audit and repeating the bid process for 2027.  Council consensus was to move this item to 
the next meeting agenda and include Director Weitzman’s recommendation. 
CONSIDERATION OF CDR – FLEET FUEL SUPPLIER                                            
(Acting Fire Chief Mike Patterson) 
Acting Fire Chief Patterson stated that three bids had been obtained through Sourcewell, a 
cooperative purchasing and procurement organization used by public agencies to streamline the 
procurement process.  Staff communicated with three companies and recommended switching 
from the City’s current vendor to TACenergy LLC for the purchase and delivery of fleet fuel.  
Council consensus was to move this item to the next meeting agenda. 
CONSIDERATION OF CDR – PD CODE ENFORCEMENT VAN REPLACEMENT 
AND UPFIT (Acting Fire Chief Mike Patterson) 
Acting Fire Chief Patterson stated that the current vehicle used by the PD Code Enforcement 
unit is a 2005 Ford Econoline van that has exceeded its projected service life and has become 
increasingly costly to maintain and repair.  He stated that staff had obtained one bid, $86,890, for 
the purchase and upfit of a Chevrolet 2500 HD 4WD truck with a SWAB animal transport body 
to replace the existing van.  The vehicle was budgeted for replacement, and funds have been 
allocated for the purchase.  Acting Chief Patterson added that the City has been purchasing 
Police Department vehicles through John Elway Chevrolet under the current State of Colorado 
award contract.  Council consensus was to move this item to the next meeting agenda. 
QUESTIONS AND COMMENTS AND COMMITTEE UPDATES FROM MUNICIPAL 
OFFICIALS 
Manager Halburnt stated that she appreciates her position as City Manager and is committed to 
doing a good job for the City. 
Minutes of the Study Session 
October 6, 2026 
Council Member Vallero requested an update on the recently completed administrative audit.  
Manager Halburnt responded that the City had received a draft which would be reviewed by the 
end of the week. 
Council Member Arner asked the Mayor to poll the Council regarding a discussion at the next 
study session about increasing the frequency of City Council meetings to four times per month.   
Council consensus was to add this item to the next study session agenda.   
Council Member Arner asked the Mayor to poll the Council regarding a discussion at the next 
study session about a City public safety tax.  Council consensus was to add this item to the next 
study session agenda. 
ADJOURNMENT 
The Study Session was adjourned at 9:55 p.m.

STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS 
Recommend approval of using TACenergy LLC as our new supplier of fleet fuel for City-owned 
and operated vehicles and equipment. 
SUMMARY OF THE BASIS FOR APPROVAL/DISAPPROVAL BIDS 
The recommendation for approval of moving to this new vendor is  outlined below. 
The City of Federal Heights has been purchasing bulk quantities of unleaded and diesel fuel for 
delivery to our bulk fuel storage tank located behind the fleet repair facility.  By contracting with 
an authorized vendor, we are able to purchase fuel at a guaranteed bulk daily Oil Price 
Information Service (OPIS) rate and have it delivered to our single on-site storage tank, thus 
allowing local refueling of vehicles and equipment at the lowest cost possible and without the 
need for the use of credit cards or fuel specific fleet cards. 
In seeking a new vendor, staff has secured three bids from Sourcewell, a cooperative purchasing 
and procurement site utilized by public agencies to exercise fiscal responsibility while 
streamlining the procurement process.  These bids are in the form of Sourcewell awarded 
contracts for the purchase and delivery of fuel products by local vendors.  Staff communicated 
with the designated representatives for each of the three companies to discuss our specific needs 
and to request pricing based upon the awarded contracts and our specific estimated annual 
quantities utilized and delivery requirements.  The three companies are Saratoga Rack Marketing 
LLC (Tartan Oil), Mansfield Oil Company of Gainesville, Inc., and TACenergy, LLC, dba 
TACenergy. 
The three companies each responded to our requests, however, Saratoga Rack (Tartan Oil) 
advised us that they would not be able to fulfill our needs as they only deal with entities that can 
receive larger quantity deliveries via a full-size fuel tanker rather than the smaller tank wagon 
delivery vehicle that would be required to deliver to our site.  Both other companies could 
service us through local vendors that utilize tank wagon delivery vehicles. 
Each of the two remaining companies provided us with quotes based upon the Denver daily 
OPIS rack fuel rate for a given date, from local fuel delivery vendors including their delivery 
surcharge and any additional delivery fees.  It should be noted that our current vendor as well as 
the other two proposed vendors, all by contract, use the same guaranteed Denver daily OPIS rack 
fuel rate for the actual day that the fuel is loaded into the truck for delivery.  The difference  in 
pricing only varies by any delivery surcharges and additional delivery fees.  Staff determined the 
historical use of both unleaded gasoline and red dyed diesel fuel at approximately 26,000 gallons 
of unleaded and 6,000 gallons of diesel per year.  Based upon these estimated quantities, there 
was no appreciable difference between the quotes received from either company, and was 
consistent with the pricing from our current vendor. 
Staff also inquired about the specific local suppliers utilized by each company, fuel ordering 
process, company contacts and local-based representatives for the parent company.  TACenergy 
utilizes two well-known local fuel delivery vendors who regularly serve the Denver metro area 
and have a local company representative based in the Denver area.  This individual toured our 
site to ensure they could successfully meet our needs and committed to assisting with any 
transition.  Mansfield Oil did provide names of local vendors of which staff were not familiar 
with, and they currently do not have a Denver based or other local area company representative.  
Our current fuel vendor does not have a local representative, just a contact number for their 
corporate dispatch center located out of state. 
In final summary, the Council-approved 2026 budget for fuel is $131,100.00 and based on recent 
historical fuel price increases, staff is proposing increasing the 2027 budget to $161,100.00 to 
accurately reflect what we could reasonably expect to spend on fuel and delivery under this 
contract. 
ALTERNATIVES 
An alternative to selecting a new vendor would be to continue using our current vendor, of which 
we are not satisfied with our current and past delivery performance.

Background 
In 2021, the City entered into an intergovernmental agreement with the other cities in Adams County and 
the Adams County Sheriff’s Office to provide municipal court video services. The agreement has been 
updated annually since then. 
Video Court Operations 
Federal Heights defendants housed at the Adams County Detention Facility are made available by Adams 
County staff for video court appearances on Wednesdays from 1:30 p.m. to 2:30 p.m. Adams County 
provides the required staffing and technology support, allowing defendants to appear remotely rather than 
being transported for municipal court proceedings. 
The court typically hears an average of about six in-custody defendants per session. 
Type of Hearings 
Most appearances are advisements or bond hearings that are scheduled for future court dates. In some 
cases, the City’s prosecuting attorney may extend a plea offer. The defense attorney reviews the offer 
with the defendant, provides legal advice, and, if the defendant agrees, the plea is accepted by the court. 
City Council Consideration 
The attached IGA continues the agreement for another year and establishes a fee of $8,000 for 2027, 
which is unchanged from 2026.  The IGA will be presented to City Council for adoption by resolution at 
the next regular meeting.

BACKGROUND & DISCUSSION 
The City of Federal Heights and Hyland Hills Park and Recreation District mutually own land that requires re-platting into 
new legal lots and tracts. The area in question is the Water World Property, Camenisch Park, Carstens Park, Messina 
Park and the Federal Heights Municipal Campus.  This area was all once part of the historic Camenisch Dairy that was 
owned by the descendants of the original Camenisch family who settled this area.  As such, the City is proposing that this 
new subdivision be named the “Camenisch Dairy Subdivision” to reflect that history. A map of the proposed subdivision 
concept has been attached to this memo for council review.  
To accomplish this, the project entails surveying the area, preparing a Preliminary Plat and Final Plat in compliance with 
City Municipal Code standards, vacating historic easements that are no longer needed, and dedicating any necessary 
new easements.   Hyland Hills has retained CWC Consulting Group (a firm utilized for past projects) to perform the 
necessary surveying and document preparation services. Because the subdivision project provides a mutual benefit to 
both entities, the proposed IGA establishes that the City and Hyland Hills will equally split the total project fees in an 
amount not to exceed $30,000.       
FINANCIAL IMPACT 
The total cost for surveying and document preparation is not to exceed $30,000. The City’s financial obligation under the 
IGA will be an equal split of the total actual costs (up to $15,000), to be accommodated within budgeted funds.    
RECOMMENDATION 
Staff recommends the approval of a resolution authorizing an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) between the City of 
Federal Heights and the Hyland Hills Park and Recreation District for shared survey, platting, and document preparation 
costs associated with the mutually owned Camenisch Dairy Subdivision project.

MEMO:         JOINT HAZARD MITIGATION PLAN INTER-GOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT (IGA)

BACKGROUND 
The attached, proposed Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) between the Cities of Thornton, Federal Heights, and 
Northglenn will enable the cities to develop, draft, and adopt an updated 2028 Local Natural Hazard Mitigation Plan 
cooperatively. The Cities previously collaborated on the 2017 and 2023 plans, and with the current plan nearing the end 
of its five-year approval period, a renewed agreement is necessary to maintain compliance with standards set by the 
Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the State of Colorado.    
TERMS OF AGREEMENT 
The terms of this agreement follow the structure of the terms of the two previous, successful cooperative plan projects. 
Under the terms of the IGA, Thornton will act as the lead agency responsible for issuing the Request for Proposals, 
contracting with and paying the consultant, managing the planning process, and collecting financial contributions from 
the partner cities. A selection committee comprising representatives from all three municipalities will evaluate proposals 
and recommend a consultant for award. Federal Heights and Northglenn agree to support the project by assigning staff, 
coordinating schedules, providing necessary background information and meeting spaces, and paying invoices within 30 
days.    
The total cost of the project is anticipated not to exceed $114,000, with expenses shared roughly in proportion to each 
city's geographic area, including future growth areas:    
*City of Thornton (61.19 sq. mi. / 87%): $99,180    
*City of Northglenn (7.36 sq. mi. / 10%): $11,400    
*City of Federal Heights (1.78 sq. mi. / 3%): $3,420   
The IGA will remain in effect until the updated Plan is formally approved by FEMA, the State of Colorado, and each of the 
participating cities. The IGA also includes standard provisions for dispute resolution, change orders, and the removal of a 
participant for cause following a 30-day cure period. The City Staff recommend approval of the IGA with no changes.
