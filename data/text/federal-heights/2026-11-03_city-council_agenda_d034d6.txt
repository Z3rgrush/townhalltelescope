MISSION STATEMENT 
Our mission is to provide a high quality of life for the citizens of 
Federal Heights, while preserving a close-knit atmosphere, through 
responsible and effective stewardship of all resources, prudent 
economic development, enhancement of our current services, and 
participation in public, private, and regional partnerships. 
VISION STATEMENT 
The City of Federal Heights: 
- is comprised of distinct neighborhoods that are safe, secure, and         
have convenient access to services and amenities; 
- has a sustainable and growing economic base that fosters diverse    
employment and business opportunities; 
- offers a community-focused system of recreational, educational, 
and cultural activities for people of all ages and income levels; 
- capitalized upon its strategic location within the Denver metro         
area and adapts to a changing environment; and 
- proudly enjoys a City government that is responsive and                  
responsible to the needs of its citizens.

CALL TO ORDER 
The October 20, 2026, Study Session of the City Council of the City of Federal Heights was 
called to order by Mayor Linda S. Montoya at 6:29 p.m. 
ROLL CALL 
Council Members in attendance were Mayor Linda S. Montoya, Mayor Pro Tem Celeste Arner, 
Shari DeVries, Kevin Dougherty, and Mike Vallero.  Council Member Sonia Jensen was absent.  
Also present were City Manager Jacquie Halburnt, City Attorney Bill Hayashi and City Clerk 
Patti Lowell.   
ANNUAL EVALUATION (Municipal Court Judge Amanda Bailhache) 
Judge Bailhache reported that the Municipal Court had recently implemented touchless payments, 
which had been requested, and was working smoothly.  She discussed a 2025 Colorado Supreme 
Court decision limiting the maximum penalties for municipal violations and establishing that 
sentences imposed by municipal courts cannot exceed the maximum sentence permitted under 
state law for a comparable state misdemeanor or petty offense.  She reported that 187 in-custody 
hearings had been conducted via Zoom with Adams County so far in 2026.  She also reviewed 
her judicial leadership and professional development, along with her goals for 2026 and 2027.  
She requested a 3% cost of living increase, which would be the first since 2022.  Council 
consensus was to approve a 3% cost of living increase for Municipal Court Judge Amanda 
Bailhache, effective January 2027. 
CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. 26-XX – A RESOLUTION O F THE CITY 
COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FEDERAL HEIGHTS, COLORADO, ADOPTING 
EXHIBIT A, THE 2027 FEE SCHEDULE FOR THE INTERGOVERNMENTAL 
AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITIES OF BRIGHTON, COMMERCE CITY, 
FEDERAL HEIGHTS, NORTHGLENN, THORNTON, AND WESTMINSTER AND 
THE ADAMS COUNTY SHERIFF’S OFFICE FOR THE PROVISION OF MUNICIPAL 
COURT VIDEO SERVICES (City Clerk Patti Lowell) 
City Clerk Lowell reported that since 2021 the City has participated in an intergovernmental 
agreement with other Adams County municipalities and the Adams County Sheriff’s Office to 
provide municipal court video services, which allows defendants housed at the Adams County 
Detention Facility to appear remotely for municipal court proceedings.  The 2027 fee will 
remain $8,000, the same amount as in 2026.  Council consensus was to move this item to the 
next meeting agenda. 
CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. 26-XX – A RESOLUTION O F THE CITY 
COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FEDERAL HEIGHTS, COLORADO, APPROVING AN 
INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY OF FEDERAL 
HEIGHTS AND HYLAND HILLS PARK AND RECREATION DISTRICT FOR 
SURVEYING SERVICES (Community Development Director Tim Williams) 
Director Williams explained that the City and Hyland Hills Park and Recreation District jointly 
own property that must be re-platted into new legal lots and tracts.  The project includes 
surveying the property and preparing preliminary and final plats in accordance with the City’s 
Municipal Code.  Hyland Hills has retained a consulting firm to provide the necessary surveying 
and document preparation services.  Because the subdivision project benefits both entities, the 
Minutes of the Study Session 
October 20, 2026 
proposed agreement provides for the City and Hyland Hills to share the project costs equally, 
with the total fees not to exceed $30,000.  Mayor Montoya asked whether the City would incur 
any additional costs.  Director Williams explained that additional costs would be incurred to 
contract for preparation of a quitclaim deed to facilitate the property transfer.  Council 
consensus was to move this item to the next meeting agenda. 
CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. 26-XX – A RESOLUTION O F THE CITY 
COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FEDERAL HEIGHTS, COLORADO, APPROVING AN 
INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT FOR A LOCAL NATURAL HAZARD 
MITIGATION PLAN BETWEEN THE CITY OF FEDERAL HEIGHTS, THE CITY OF 
THORNTON, AND THE CITY OF NORTHGLENN                                             
(Community Development Director Tim Williams) 
Director Williams said that the proposed Intergovernmental Agreement between the Cities of 
Thornton, Northglenn, and Federal Heights would allow the cities to cooperatively develop, 
draft, and adopt an updated 2028 Local Natural Hazard Mitigation Plan.  He said that the cities 
had previously collaborated on the 2017 and the 2023 plans, and that the current plan is nearing 
the end of its five-year approval period.  A renewed agreement is necessary to maintain 
compliance with standards established by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) 
and the State of Colorado.  The total project cost is estimated not to exceed $114,000, with 
expenses shared proportionally based on each City’s geographic area and future growth areas.  
Federal Heights’ share, based on approximately three percent of the geographic area, is $3,420.  
Council Member Dougherty asked how often the plan is used.  Director Williams responded that 
it is rarely used.  Manager Halburnt added that the plan must be in place in the event of an 
emergency requiring FEMA funding.  Council consensus was to move this item to the next 
meeting agenda. 
CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. 26-XX – A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY 
COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FEDERAL HEIGHTS, COLORADO, ESTABLISHING A 
MONTHLY EMERGENCY TELEPHONE CHARGE FOR 2027                              
(Finance Director Tim Weitzman) 
Director Weitzman explained that state law allows governing bodies that impose an emergency 
telephone charge to establish new rates effective February 1 or June 1 and requires at least 60 
days’ notice to service suppliers before the new rates take effect.  He said that the Public Utilities 
Commission recently increased the current charge from $2.17 to $2.26 to account for inflation.  
The resolution establishes the City’s E911 charge at $2.26, effective February 1.  Council 
consensus was to move this item to the next meeting agenda. 
CONSIDERATION OF CDR FOR 2027 MOTOROLA VESTA NXT PROJECT        
(Acting Chief of Police Karl Ballard) 
Police Commander Jason Schlenker and Communications Center Manager Chris Lyman said 
that the system would replace the current dispatch system, which is eight years old.  Richard 
Steidel of Motorola explained that the 2027 Motorola VESTA NXT project would upgrade the 
dispatch center’s emergency call-handling system to support Next Generation 9-1-1 operations.  
He explained that the system offers a streamlined interface, integrated mapping and location 
information as well as improved access to caller data to help telecommunicators process 
emergency calls more efficiently.  He added that enhanced transcription, translation, and other 
features are designed to reduce the workload on call takers and help ensure that critical 
information is communicated quickly to dispatchers and first responders.  Council Member 
Dougherty asked whether residents could text 9-1-1, and Steidel confirmed that the system 
Minutes of the Study Session 
October 20, 2026 
supports texting 9-1-1.  Mayor Pro Tem Arner asked whether a non-emergency call could be 
upgraded to an emergency call, and Steidel confirmed that it could be.  Council Member Vallero 
asked about the interpreter feature.  Communications Center Manager Lyman noted that the City 
currently has two bilingual employees.  Steidel explained that the software’s interpreting feature 
provides rapid interpretation in multiple languages.  Council consensus was to move this item to 
the next meeting agenda. 
DISCUSSION OF 2027 DRAFT BUDGET CHANGES (Finance Director Tim Weitzman) 
Finance Director Weitzman reviewed revisions to the draft 2027 budget, including updated 2026 
estimates, potential budget reductions, and preliminary revenue estimates from a proposed sales 
and admission tax and marijuana tax increases.  Council Member Vallero noted a $2.6 million 
budget shortfall and asked about the City’s reserves.  Manager Halburnt reported that the City 
has approximately $20 million in reserves and said that staff is seeking Council direction on 
sustainable spending levels.  Mayor Montoya asked about CIRSA insurance costs.  Director 
Weitzman said that the 2027 budget includes $450,000 for insurance, an increase of $103,500 
over the 2026 budget.  Manager Halburnt said that CIRSA has attributed statewide rate increases 
to rising Police Department claims.  Council consensus was to move this item to the next study 
session agenda. 
DISCUSSION OF PUBLIC SAFETY TAX (City Manager Jacquie Halburnt) 
Manager Halburnt said that the matter was brought before City Council at the previous meeting 
by Mayor Pro Tem Arner, with Council consensus to discuss options.  She said that she had 
provided Council with the requested information and expressed concern about depleting the 
General Fund reserves, adding that the discussion was an opportunity to explore ways to 
replenish those funds.  Mayor Pro Tem Arner said that City Council needs to ensure that staff 
has the resources necessary to perform their duties and maintain community safety, while 
recognizing that Federal Heights is a small community with a limited budget.  She added that 
she thinks that reductions need to be made to the proposed 2027 draft budget.  Council Member 
Vallero requested an itemized list of potential budget cuts that could reduce the $2.6 million 
shortfall to a more manageable level.  Council Member Dougherty stated that he does not 
support a tax increase.  Mayor Montoya asked whether a sales tax increase would require voter 
approval, and Manager Halburnt confirmed that it would.  Council consensus was to continue 
the discussion at the next study session, including options for moving closer to a balanced 
budget. 
DISCUSSION OF INCREASING FREQUENCY OF CITY COUNCIL MEETINGS    
(City Manager Jacquie Halburnt) 
Manager Halburnt said that Mayor Pro Tem Arner had raised the matter at the previous City 
Council meeting, and Council had agreed to discuss it.  Mayor Pro Tem Arner said that she 
believes that additional study sessions will be necessary for City Council to adequately address 
the budget challenges.  Council consensus was to schedule an additional study session for 
November 10.  
QUESTIONS AND COMMENTS AND COMMITTEE UPDATES FROM MUNICIPAL 
OFFICIALS 
Council Member Vallero said that he doesn’t think that an annual retreat is sufficient to discuss 
City priorities and expectations throughout the year and he recommended quarterly workshops 
where the City could meet with the City Manager to make sure that she and the Council are 
aligned with goals, priorities, expectations, and communication.  Council consensus was to 
discuss the possibility of quarterly workshops to replace the annual City Council retreat.

INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITIES OF THORNTON, 
FEDERAL HEIGHTS, AND NORTHGLENN FOR THE DRAFTING, FUNDING, AND 
ADOPTION OF A LOCAL NATURAL HAZARD MITIGATION PLAN 
This Intergovernmental Agreement is entered into this __ day of _______, 2026 by and 
between the City of Thornton located at 9500 Civic Center Drive, Thornton, CO 80229 
("Thornton"), the City of Federal Heights, located at 2380 W. 90th Ave. Federal Heights, CO 
80260 ("Federal Heights"), and the City of Northglenn located at 11701 Community Center 
Drive, Northglenn, CO 80233 ("Northglenn"), or collectively the ("Cities"). 
WITNESSETH 
WHEREAS, the Cities have previously collaborated to develop and adopt the 2017 and 
2023 Thornton, Federal Heights and Northglenn Hazard Mitigation Plans; and as the 2023 Plan is 
approaching the end of its five-year approval period, the Cities wish to enter into an 
Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) to work cooperatively toward the development, drafting, 
and adoption of an updated Local Hazard Mitigation Plan (the "Plan"); and 
WHEREAS, the Cities will be allocating costs for development, drafting and adoption of 
an updated Plan based on the geographic area of the respective Cities; and  
WHEREAS, the Cities agree that Thornton will be the lead agency in coordinating the 
efforts associated with managing and developing the Plan in accordance with the terms and 
conditions of this IGA; and  
WHEREAS, Thornton will invoice Northglenn and Federal Heights for their 
proportionate shares of the costs; and 
NOW THEREFORE in consideration of the terms and conditions contained herein, the 
Cities hereto agree as follows: 
I. PROJECT MANAGEMENT 
A. Project Management. Thornton shall manage the development, drafting, and adoption 
of the updated Local Hazard Mitigation Plan ( the “Project”). Northglenn and Federal Heights 
shall provide to Thornton or to the consultant all information related to the Project upon request. 
The Project shall be conducted, and the Plan shall be drafted in accordance with the specifications 
provided by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the State of Colorado, and 
in the best interests of the Cities. 
1.  Thornton Obligations. The Cities agree that Thornton will be the lead agency 
in coordinating the efforts associated with managing and developing the Plan, 
which will include the following responsibilities: 
a.  Drafting and issuing a request for proposals (RFP) 
b.  Contracting with a consultant 
c.  Paying the consultant 
d.  Collecting contributions from Northglenn and Federal Heights 
e.  Managing the consultant and the planning process; and 
2.  Northglenn and Federal Heights agree to meet the following obligations: 
a.  Cooperate with Thornton in regard to scheduling meetings 
b.  Assign appropriate staff members to participate in meetings 
c.  Provide all appropriate and available background information in 
support of the Plan 
d.  Provide meeting space as necessary for meetings 
e.  Pay Thornton invoices within 30 days 
f.  Work diligently, in good faith, and in a timely manner toward the 
completion of the Plan. 
B. Project Completion. The Cities agree and acknowledge that it is essential to each city 
that the entire Plan be completed, which shall include approval by FEMA, the State of Colorado, 
and each city. No city shall have the right to stop or significantly delay the development, drafting, 
or approval of the Plan without the written consent of the other Cities, except in the event of an 
emergency. 
C. Contract Documents.  Upon completion of the scope of work, Thornton shall develop 
the contract documents for the Plan. The Cities acknowledge that within the Plan there are areas 
under the jurisdiction of each of the Cities. The Cities agree that the Plan shall be drafted in 
accordance with the standards and specifications provided by FEMA and the State of Colorado, 
and in the best interest of each of the Cities. 
The contract documents shall include a Scope of Work for each of four categories: 
1. Work to be completed for the Cities, 
2. Work to be completed for Thornton, 
3. Work to be completed for Northglenn, and 
4. Work to be completed for Federal Heights. 
Upon finalization of the contract documents, Thornton shall issue a Request for Proposals (RFP). 
Following receipt of the consultant proposals in response to the RFP, Thornton shall assemble a 
team of individuals, including representatives of both Northglenn and Federal Heights, to serve 
on a selection committee. The selection committee will evaluate the proposals based on the 
criteria indicated in the RFP and recommend a consultant proposal for award. Thornton shall 
award, execute and administer the contract for the Plan in accordance with Thornton's policies 
and procedures. Northglenn and Federal Heights shall be specifically listed as beneficiaries of all 
warranties and bonds provided for in the contract documents. 
D. Project Funding and Invoices. The cost of the Plan is anticipated to not exceed 
$114,000. Thornton will bill Northglenn and Federal Heights for their respective shares, with 
Northglenn's and Federal Heights' share due to Thornton within 30 days. 
E. Proportionality of Costs. The Cities will be responsible for paying approximately 
$114,000 in costs, to be shared roughly in proportion to geographic area of the respective Cities 
(including future growth area and Northglenn’s Section 36) which is as follows: 
City Geographic Area  
(sq. mi.) 
Percent Amount

F. Additional Services. Each city may choose to contract for additional services with the 
consultant at their own expense independent of the other two cities. 
G. Project Manager and Staff Coordinators. Thornton shall designate a project manager 
who shall coordinate the Project on behalf of the Cities. Northglenn and Federal Heights shall 
each designate a Staff Coordinator who shall serve as the primary point of contact for both the 
project manager as well as the consultant. 
H. Change Orders. The project manager and staff coordinators shall meet in person or 
electronically and communicate via telephone conference, video conference, and electronic mail 
as necessary to review all Project change orders. Pursuant to the Thornton purchasing manual, the 
Thornton City Manager may approve change orders up to $10,000 for work done exclusively for 
Thornton and paid for by Thornton, with no impact on the financial obligations of the other two 
cities. 
The Parties agree that Northglenn and Federal Heights have the right to challenge or dispute any 
change order that solely affects work provided to their respective cities. The project manager 
shall use all best efforts to resolve the disputed change order to avoid any delay in the work. In 
the event the project manager and staff coordinators are unable to resolve the disputed change 
order, the issue will be resolved in accordance with the dispute resolution provisions provided in 
Paragraph II. C. herein. 
II. GENERAL TERMS

This is an item that is in the proposed budget for 2027. The vendor has given the City a lead time 
of six months from the time of the contract signing until full implementation of the new system 
is completed. Also, as an incentive if we sign the contract prior to 2027, Motorola will credit the

critical failure we have been advised parts could be hard to find.

by Motorola. There are no other authorized dealers for this system. There are many other 911 
call taking systems on the market from other companies, but this is the only system that will 
seamlessly interface with our current CAD/RMS (Computer Aided Dispatch/Record 
Management System) used by the Police Department, which is Motorola Flex.
