Executive Summary: 
The President of the Rifle Community Foundation recently resigned. To help maintain the group during the 
interim period, City of Rifle staff has been supporting by reviewing financial information and preparing packets 
and meeting agendas. At their last meeting, the Community Foundation voted to restructure the entity so that all 
administrative functions are under the City of Rifle. Essentially, the Community Foundation would stop being a 
separate 501(c)(3) and would instead be a board under the City.  
 
Staff is supportive of this change for a number of reasons including:  
 
1. The City of Rifle is the only entity that provides ongoing financial contributions to the organization. The City 
Manager previously reached-out to New Castle, Silt, and Parachute to see if they were interested in also 
contributing. None of the organizations wanted to contribute funding.  
2. City Staff has the capacity and ability to support another board. The City's Senior Administrative Specialist 
currently supports PRAB and could also take the administrative role for this new board.  
3. If the organization was overseen by the City, the Finance Director would be responsible for direct oversight 
of the finances.  
4. The administrative side of operating a 501(c)(3), would no longer be required and the board could focus

purely on grant funding decisions. 
 
The foundation also requested to host their page on the City website. It has been published with updated 
information that reflects the current board and grant process. Click here to view the webpage.

Purpose of this document. This is a request for a budget estimate, not a final statement of work or 
commitment to fund. BIC should price the base scope, identify assumptions and recommended 
adjustments. The final scope, performance measures, compensation, and terms will be negotiated 
after the City reviews the estimate. 
 
1. Program Objective 
The City is providing a scope of work to the Grand Junction BIC to create and operate a Rifle Branch in 
2027 through a dedicated BIC employee. The branch would extend BIC's established hub model into 
Rifle, drawing on BIC's existing staff, systems, curriculum, advisors, facilities, tools, and regional network. 
The City's two primary objectives are: 
Downtown vitality: generate more viable downtown businesses by developing startup concepts and 
helping existing retailers, restaurants, services, and other businesses stabilize, grow, transition 
ownership, and remain in Rifle. 
Better jobs closer to home: develop small-scale manufacturers and product businesses that can 
move from garages, homes, or side businesses into sustainable companies with local 
production, investment, and employment. 
Services should begin without being contingent on acquisition, construction, or operation of a 
permanent incubator facility. An agreement with the Rifle Regional Economic Development Corporation 
(RREDC) may be established to house Rifle’s BIC employee. Future downtown, maker, kitchen, or light-
industrial space may be evaluated separately as demand becomes clear.

Dear Zach, 
Thank you for the preliminary scope of services for the 2027 Rifle Branch. It's a 
thoughtful document, and it reflects the conversations we've had about what Rifle 
actually needs, so pricing it was straightforward. Below is our full-year 2027 
estimate for the base scope, with one-time startup costs shown separately from 
recurring annual costs as you requested. I've also attached the detailed 
worksheet so Council can see how every number is built. 
We've priced this to the scope exactly as written: one full-time employee 
dedicated to Rifle, employed, housed, trained, supervised, and insured by the 
BIC, serving as the local relationship manager and front door to everything we 
do. That structure matters, because it keeps all of the employer, HR, 
confidentiality, and liability exposure with us rather than the City, which is one of 
the real advantages of delivering this through a nonprofit partner. 
2027 Budget Estimate 
Fully-loaded personnel (1.0 FTE) $86,839 
Recurring program operations $28,500 
Administration / indirect (10%) $11,534 
Recurring annual total $126,873 
One-time startup (2027 only) $10,500 
Total 2027 (recurring + startup) $137,373 
The personnel figure reflects a competitive base salary for an advisor who can counsel 
manufacturers and established business owners, fully loaded at our actual benefits rate of 20% 
plus required payroll taxes and a small market cushion. It also includes travel between Grand 
Junction and Rifle.  
What the estimate assumes 
A few assumptions are worth surfacing now, because they shape the number and a 
couple of them are savings the City is providing. 
Page 11 of 84
 
 
 
The workspace. We've assumed, per your scope, that the City provides a furnished, 
internet-enabled, publicly accessible workspace in or near downtown Rifle at no charge 
to the BIC. That assumption keeps rent out of our budget. If that changes, we'd need to 
add it back. 
Facility and tools are not in this number. The scope correctly defers any light-
industrial build-out, equipment purchase, and facility operation until space is actually 
acquired and demand is demonstrated. So in 2027 we're pricing the person and the 
program, not a building. The manufacturing work this year is preparing the cost and tools 
schedules and referring Rifle makers to our Grand Junction facility until there's space in 
Rifle. When you're ready to pursue a facility, we'll price that separately. 
We stretch Grand Junction into Rifle. Because we're already operating an hour away, 
the Rifle employee pulls on our existing specialists, advisors, curriculum, systems, and 
funding expertise rather than rebuilding any of it. The allocated cost of that support is in 
the operations line, and it's a fraction of what standing up those capabilities from scratch 
would run. 
Intensive grant work is separate. Per your section 3.5, maintaining a funding pipeline 
and preparing a couple of concepts is included, but intensive grant development, 
engineering, design, or feasibility work would be scoped and priced on its own. 
What the one-time startup covers 
The $10,500 in one-time startup is the cost of standing the branch up during the 
January-February mobilize phase, before it opens to clients on March 1. It isn't a 
recurring expense, so it falls away after 2027. It covers recruiting and onboarding the 
Rifle employee, the technology that person needs to work (laptop, phone, and software 
setup, since the City is providing the furnished workspace itself), the Rifle branch identity 
and launch materials so the branch has its own public presence from day one, and the 
upfront development of the baseline, work plan, and partner mapping the scope calls for 
before launch. In short, it's what turns a funded position into an operating branch. 
A few shared assumptions 
So, we're building on the same foundation, I want to name three assumptions behind this 
estimate. None of these are concerns, they're just the things worth agreeing on up front 
so we measure success fairly as we go. 
The first-year workload figures are planning targets, not guarantees. Your scope 
describes them the same way, and I appreciate that. A single person building a brand-

We're genuinely excited about this. Rifle is doing the right thing by building something

RE-2 School District Update on Ballot Questions – 4A and 4B 
RE-2 School District Communication Director Theresa Hamilton  and Superintendent Kirk 
Banghart presented information regarding ballot measures 4A and 4B. Garfield RE -2 School 
District serves 4,700 students from Pre-K through 12 th grade across ten (10) schools located in 
Rifle, Silt, and New Castle communities. They currently are the largest provider for early education 
in Garfield County. The RE-2 school district currently employs 850 staff members; 338 teachers, 
140 paraprofessionals, 124 cooks, custodians, bus drivers, maintenance/grounds, 64 Pre-K staff, 
and 27 special service providers. RE -2 recently achieved its highest ever  performance ranking, 
outperforming the Garfield RE-1 Roaring Fork School District growth by 2.2%.  Kirk gave credit 
for this success to the 2023 RE-2 Strategic Plan implementation, the hard work from district staff 
and students, and their ability to prioritize district leadership in recruitment and retention efforts. 
RE-2 has seen reductions of $740,00 0.00 in their central office budget and $200,000.00 in non -
salary budgets. RE -2 currently sits in the lowest 30 of 179 school districts in Colorado for 
administration salary costs, with only 1.1% of their general fund going to central office staff.  
Teaching salaries in RE-2 are the lowest among neighboring districts with starting salaries sitting 
at $44,700.00 annually for new teachers, making recruitment and retention efforts difficult. It is 
the hope of the district that the mill levy override proposed in the 4A ballot measure will allow for 
an increase of 6% to salaries in order to retain staff in our community. Kirk explained that the state 
finance formula is portioned differently for all districts; RE -2 currently receives 70% of their 
funding from the state. Ballot Question 4B proposes extending existing bond funding for capital 
improvements without increasing the current tax rates. Proposed projects include career and

Background 
HB24-1152 Accessory Dwelling Units was passed on May 2024 and effective June 30, 
2025. Colorado House Bill 24-1152 mandates that local governments in urban areas 
allow Accessory Dwelling Units on land zoned for single-family homes. The law 
removes restrictions like extra parking requirements and prohibits restrictive HOA rules, 
aiming to increase affordable housing and support multigenerational living. This bill 
does not directly affect the City of Rifle as we are not within a Metropolitan Planning 
Area, Rifle has long been supportive of ADU’s. Compliance with HB24-1152 allows the 
City to obtain additional DOLA funding as well as releases awarded funds from 
Colorado Energy Office for pre-approved ADU Plans. 
On April 28, 2026 the Planning and Zoning Commission reviewed and recommended 
staff’s recommendations to come into compliance with HB24-1152. The City Council 
heard and reviewed the proposed text amendment on May 20, 2026 with a second 
reading of Ordinance 13 Series of 2026 on June 3, 2026. Since adoption staff has had 
comments back from DOLA that the code will require further revisions to meet State 
requirements to become a Supportive ADU Jurisdiction. Specifically, they find that the 
required rear setback will be an encumbrance to the addition of an ADU on properties 
within City limits. Further they have concerns that the existing language regarding use 
of manufactured housing, compatibility and landscaping are too restrictive and not 
objective. Most recently, DOLA has expressed that Estate Zone District requires to 
allow ADU’s as a use by right. Staff has prepared the following text amendment to 
address their concerns/requirements. 
Proposed Amendment 
The proposed text amendment addresses DOLA’s comments regarding permitted rear 
yard setbacks, allowances for manufactured housing, revisions to Estate Zone District 
and objectivity when applied to ADU’s.
