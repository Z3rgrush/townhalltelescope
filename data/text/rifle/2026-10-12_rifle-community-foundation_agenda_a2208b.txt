FOUNDATION ADMINISTRATION AND ORGANIZATIONAL TRANSITION 
Following the resent resignation of former Board President Kasey Nipsel, the board discussed simplifying 
operations and returning the board’s primary focus to reviewing grant applications and awarding funds. 
Patrick Waller outlined a possible structure under which the City would assume financial and 
administrative responsibilities, lightening the load on volunteer board members. This shift in operations 
would still require board members to review grant requests and meet twice each year to conduct the award 
process. Patrick Waller also explained that transitioning the foundation under City administration could 
affect its independent 501(c)(3) status and would require direction from the city’s attorney as well as 
action from City Council.  
WEBSITE AND ELECTRONIC GRANT APPLICATION 
Patrick Waller informed the board that the new website for the foundation had been developed, and a link 
will be sent to board members for their review. Sarah Stubbs presented the electronic, fillable grant 
application that she created using the original paper application as reference. Because outreach for  the 
upcoming grant cycle has already begun, applications will continue to be received through the foundation’s 
existing channels. 
BOARD MEMBERSHIP 
The board reviewed its current membership and identified the need to fill a vacancy that was created by 
Kasey Nipsel’s resignation. Members discussed seeking representation from the Garfield RE-2 School 
District as well as Grand River Health District. Brisa Chavez mentioned she new a possible candidate within 
Garfield RE-2 School District and will be reaching out to them to gauge their interest and invite them to the 
October meeting. Additional candidates may be considered if needed. Patrick Waller explained that if the 
foundation transitions under City administration, future appointments will go before City Council for 
approval.

Brief Summary of Organization's History
Kings and Priests Ministry, dba Discovery Café, was established in 2020 with a mission to provide peer recovery support, connection, and 
practical resources to individuals impacted by substance use, incarceration, homelessness, and other barriers to stability.
Discovery Café oﬃcially opened its doors in 2021 at the Colorado Mountain College Riﬂe Campus, later expanded services to Basalt, and in 2023 
relocated its community-based services to the Riﬂe Branch of the Garﬁeld County Libraries. Our partnership with the library and peer-led 
recovery groups received statewide recognition through the Colorado Association of Libraries Partnership of the Year Award.
As funding opportunities became limited, Discovery Café adapted its service model and moved its primary operations to Glenwood Springs, 
while continuing to serve individuals from Riﬂe and maintaining a signiﬁcant investment in the Riﬂe community.
In 2023, Kings and Priests Ministry purchased its ﬁrst sober living home in Riﬂe. We have since expanded to three sober living homes located in 
Riﬂe, providing structured, supportive housing for individuals in recovery, including those transitioning from incarceration and Community 
Corrections.
Our organization has also expanded its peer recovery and reentry services into Colorado correctional facilities and Community Corrections, 
supporting individuals both before and after release. Executive Director Gabe Cohen serves as Chaplain at the Riﬂe Correctional Center, 
strengthening our ability to establish relationships with individuals during incarceration and provide continuity of support following release.
In December 2025, Kings and Priests Ministry expanded its continuum of care with the opening of the Jesus Saves Homeless Day Shelter in 
Glenwood Springs. Today, our Glenwood Springs location serves as a central access point for services while we continue serving residents of Riﬂe 
and surrounding communities through peer recovery, reentry, sober living, and homeless services.
Number of people served annually in Riﬂe Community demographic area?
Discovery Café served an average of approximately 100 unique individuals annually from the Riﬂe community demographic area through peer
recovery, reentry, sober living, correctional, and supportive services. Currently many services are delivered through our Glenwood Springs
location. However, our organization continues to serve Riﬂe residents and operates three sober living homes physically located in Riﬂe.
Please describe current programs, activities, and accomplishments:
Kings and Priests Ministry currently provides a continuum of peer recovery, reentry, sober living, and homeless services to individuals 
throughout Garﬁeld County, including the Riﬂe community.
Our Discovery Café Peer Recovery Program provides peer-led recovery groups, individual recovery coaching, mentorship, and connections to 
community resources. Individuals from Riﬂe have access to these services as well as our reentry and recovery support.
We operate three sober living homes located in Riﬂe, providing structured, substance-free housing for individuals committed to recovery. 
Residents receive peer support and assistance connecting to employment, transportation, healthcare, recovery resources, and other services 
that promote long-term stability and independence.
Our reentry services extend into Colorado correctional facilities and Community Corrections, allowing relationships to begin before release and 
continue after individuals return to their communities. Executive Director Gabe Cohen serves as Chaplain at Riﬂe Correctional Center, providing 
an important connection between our organization and individuals preparing for reentry.
Our accomplishments include helping individuals transition from incarceration into sober living, obtain employment, driver's licenses and 
transportation, successfully complete parole, become sober living house managers, obtain Recovery Coach certiﬁcations, and ultimately become 
Recovery Coaches who now help others.

Brief description of request:
In 2022 and 2023 Kings and Priests Ministry, dba Discovery Café, received $25,000 (year 1) and $20,000 (year 2) Dependency, Prevention and 
Recovery grants to support recovery-focused services for individuals in the Riﬂe community. Funding supported Discovery Café’s eﬀorts to 
provide peer recovery support, recovery groups, individual connection and mentorship, and access to resources for individuals impacted by 
substance use. The funding helped sustain our community-based approach to dependency prevention and recovery while strengthening 
connections and support for individuals working toward long-term recovery and stability.
Measurements of success from the past 3 years:
Between 2023 and 2025, Kings and Priests Ministry, dba Discovery Café, served 626 unique individuals through our recovery, reentry, sober 
living, and supportive services.
During this three-year period, measurable growth and outcomes included:
• Expanded from one sober living home purchased in Riﬂe in 2023 to three sober living homes currently operating in Riﬂe, increasing 
access to safe, structured, substance-free housing.
• Provided ongoing peer recovery groups, individual recovery coaching, and reentry support to individuals aﬀected by substance use and 
incarceration.
• Expanded services within Colorado correctional facilities and Community Corrections, allowing us to begin building relationships before 
release and continue supporting individuals after reentry.
• Helped individuals transition from incarceration into stable sober housing, employment, and greater independence.
• Supported participants in obtaining driver's licenses, reliable transportation, employment, Recovery Coach certiﬁcations, and leadership 
positions within our sober living homes.
• Seen individuals successfully complete parole and transition toward independent living, including participants who ﬁrst connected with 
Discovery Café while incarcerated and later became Recovery Coaches helping others in their own recovery and reentry journeys.
• Received the Colorado Association of Libraries Partnership of the Year Award in recognition of our collaborative peer recovery work with 
the Garﬁeld County Libraries.
• Expanded our continuum of care in 2025 with the opening of the Jesus Saves Homeless Day Shelter, creating another point of connection 
for individuals experiencing homelessness, substance use, and housing instability.
We measure success not only by the number of individuals served, but by meaningful changes in recovery stability, housing, employment, 
successful reentry, connection to community resources, and increased self-suﬃciency. Our growth from one to three sober living homes in 
Riﬂe, combined with 626 unique individuals served from 2023–2025, demonstrates both the continued need for these services and our 
organization's ability to respond to that need.
