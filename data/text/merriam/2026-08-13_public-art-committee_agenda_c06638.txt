Semifinalist Process 
The committee discussed the process and timeline for the next phase of the selection 
process. 
Questions and discussion included: 
 Project Location: The general project area has been identified. Artists will be 
provided flexibility in developing their concepts, with any proposed modifications 
to the existing landscape discussed during the design review process. 
 Artist Stipend: Semi-finalists will receive a $2,000 stipend to develop their 
proposals. 
 Site Information: While artists will be encouraged to visit the project site, staff will 
coordinate with the Police Department to obtain drone footage of the Butterfly 
Garden area to provide additional site context. 
 Proposal Timeline: The committee agreed artists should have approximately six 
to eight weeks to develop their proposals. To accommodate this schedule, the 
September committee meeting will be moved to September 21. 
 Artist Notification: Semi-finalists will be notified through the CaFE system and 
invited to participate in the final round. The remaining applicants will not be 
notified until the selected artists have formally accepted their invitations. Staff 
will coordinate with the former committee liaison to ensure the notification 
process is consistent with the procedures used during the 2025 project. 
  
August Committee Meeting Discussion 
The committee discussed whether to cancel the August meeting while waiting for artist 
proposals. Members generally supported holding the meeting to continue planning for 
future public art initiatives. 
Potential discussion topics include: 
 Identifying future public art locations throughout the community. 
 Exploring opportunities within the South Park/Brown Park area following the 
recent placement of Philadelphia Missionary Baptist Church on the National 
Register of Historic Places. 
 Working collaboratively with church leadership and inviting a representative from 
the church to participate in future discussions. 
 Requesting assistance from the City's Economic Development staff in 
coordinating introductions and facilitating collaboration. 
 Revisiting the Public Art Master Plan to evaluate priority locations in light of 
changes to the community since the plan's adoption.

FROM:  Anna Slocum, Director of Parks and Recreation 
SUBJECT: Streamway Trail Art Project – Semifinalist Update 
DATE:   August 13, 2026 
 
HIGHLIGHTS 
This memo provides an update to the Public Art Committee on the Streamway Trail Art Project, including the status of 
the semifinalists, direction provided for the development of site-specific proposals, upcoming project milestones, and 
additional information that has been shared with the artists. 
The semifinalists for the Streamway Trail Art Project were notified of their invitation on Friday, July 24, 2026, via an 
email through CaFE. All artists accepted the invitation to proceed and have completed their W9 and invoicing 
requirements to receive their stipend. 
Site-Specific Proposal Direction 
Artists were provided with the following directions to develop their site-specific proposals: 
• Incorporate a meaningful connection to Merriam and consider ways the artwork can be personalized to the 
community. 
• Review the Streamway Trail Master Plan and take its goals and recommendations into consideration when 
developing the proposal. 
• Design for Merriam's seasonal weather conditions, which can range from below 0°F to above 100°F. 
• Include a visual rendering that communicates the proposed artwork and its relationship to the site. 
• Provide an introduction and big-picture overview of the concept, including how the artwork will enhance the 
visitor experience. 
• Include detailed images and descriptions of the major elements of the proposed design. 
• Provide a refined project budget and timeline. 
• Identify long-term maintenance and care requirements. 
 
 
 
 
Site Information and Questions Answered to Date 
Additional information has been provided to the artists as questions have been received regarding the site: 
• The proposed area is relatively secluded and has limited monitoring. Artists have been advised that locating the 
artwork where it remains visible from the Public Works service road would provide a greater level of daily 
visibility. 
• There are no existing utilities in the immediate area. Access to utilities would be challenging, if not impossible, 
and proposals should be developed with this limitation in mind. 
• The site borders Turkey Creek and is located within the floodplain. These conditions will need to be considered 
in the design, materials, construction, and long-term durability of the artwork. 
• Artists were provided access to six site photographs and a 360-degree drone video to assist with understanding 
the site and surrounding conditions. 
• Because the site is a large area that is used by the public for a variety of recreational activities, staff identified a 
specific section for artists to consider for the artwork, see photo below. This approach is intended to preserve 
the larger field area for continued recreational use. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

 
 
Upcoming Artist Engagement and Review 
The semifinalists will have an opportunity to meet with the Public Art Committee during a Q&A session on August 13. 
This will allow the artists to ask questions and receive clarification as they continue developing their proposals. 
The current project schedule is:

November 13, 2026 Notice of selection / contract negotiation

For the September presentation, each artist will be assigned a 30-minute meeting with the committee. Artists will have 
approximately 15–20 minutes to present their proposal, followed by 10–15 minutes for questions and discussion with

There will be a time for public input on the three designs after the proposal on September 21. Notice to proceed will be 
given on Friday, November 13, 2026.
