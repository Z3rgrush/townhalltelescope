 DOWNTOWN MASTER PLAN UPDATE 
  Shockey Consulting will brief the Planning Commission and City Council on 
work completed since the July 13 Council update. The presentation covers five 
items: results of the statistically valid community survey conducted in July, 
which drew 471 responses from residents and business owners; feedback from 
Public Workshop #2 on Sept. 1, attended by about 60 people; an overview of 
five potential funding sources for downtown improvements; the three vision 
frameworks released Aug. 8 — Refine, Grow and Transform — including what 
each includes, what all three share, and the flood pathway and order-of-
magnitude cost associated with each; and the path forward. 
  
All three scales of change are presented for discussion. No selection is 
requested at this meeting. The Steering Committee records a vision preference 
on Sept. 29, the online vision framework survey closes Oct. 4, and the 
Planning Commission and City Council each receive the final draft plan in 
November. The scale of change ultimately selected will be carried into the 
Comprehensive Plan 2040 amendment in 2027. 
  
No action is requested. Discussion and questions only.

PAYING  FOR IT
Five potential main funding sources
This is an overview of the tools. No funding strategy is recommended, and no source is attached to Vision A, B or C. That 
work is underway.
1 County stormwater cost share (SMAC) The largest single outside contribution available. Pays only for flood risk reduction — not land, buildings or streetscape. Percentage and schedule are 
still being confirmed in writing.
2 County road cost share (CARS) Pays 50% of construction and inspection on eligible arterial and collector projects; the City pays design and right of way. Merriam Drive from 55th Street to Antioch is already funded at $7.58 million, with $5.2 million from 
county, state and federal sources.
3 A dedicated annual amount in the 
capital improvement program
The current five-year program carries downtown revitalization at $300,000 
a year, alongside $12.5 million for Upper Turkey Creek drainage and $14.4 
million of Merriam Drive work held for this plan to direct.
4 Dedicated sales tax The quarter-cent sales tax that built the community center expires Dec. 31, 2027. A separate new quarter-cent for downtown could go to voters and, if 
approved, begin Jan. 1, 2028 — roughly $2 million a year.
5 Private investment and development -
based tools
Tax increment financing, community improvement and transportation 
development districts, industrial revenue bonds, and tax credit programs. 
Most depend on a specific project or on new value being created first.
Source: Investing in Downtown weekly read, Sept. 9, 2026. The City has held a AAA bond rating since 2018.

WHAT PEOPLE WROTE IN THEIR OWN WORDS
171 written responses to “Why does this vision appeal to you? What matters most?” Five themes recurred most often.
1
A place to go, not drive through
Strongest among Vision B and C
The most common reason given for change of any scale. Respondents describe wanting walkable streets, 
evening and weekend activity, restaurants and shops, and somewhere to bring family. Nearby downtowns are 
named repeatedly as the comparison.
2
Protecting existing business owners
Strongest among Vision A
Long-tenured owners and their customers describe redevelopment as a threat to livelihoods. Several 
respondents identify themselves as downtown business owners. Concern about how property is acquired 
appears throughout.
3
Small-town character and building stock
Strongest among Vision A
Respondents connect Merriam's appeal to its older buildings, quiet, and scale, and worry the result would look 
like any other suburb. Some counter that the current building stock has little to preserve.
4
Flood risk as the thing to settle first
Across all three visions
Flooding is framed as the precondition for investment — reduce the risk and private money follows. A minority 
argue for giving Turkey Creek room instead of building walls.
5
Skepticism about more apartments
Across all three visions
Housing draws the sharpest split. Some see residents as necessary to support shops; others say Merriam has 
approved enough apartments already and question whether they are affordable.
Themes are a qualitative reading of the written comments, not a count. Full comment text is available on request. 4
