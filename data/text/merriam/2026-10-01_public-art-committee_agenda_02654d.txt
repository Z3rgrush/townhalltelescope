FLOODED BY WONDER
Dimensions: 18′ H × 6′ W × 6′ D
Materials: Painted structural steel, fiberglass, acrylic glass, UV-stable pigments, and dichroic film
Concept Overview
FLOODED BY WONDER is a monumental public sculpture celebrating the resilience of Merriam—a community defined by how it 
transformed historical floodwaters into thriving civic landscapes. Rising 18 feet above the trail, an expressive hand emerges from the 
earth to offer a gentle perch for the Regal Fritillary Butterfly (Speyeria idalia), a vanishing emblem of the Kansas tallgrass prairie. 
Once widespread across North America, the Regal Fritillary now finds its primary surviving stronghold in the tallgrass prairies of 
Kansas. By re-establishing moist floodplain corridors and native meadows, Merriam’s trails offer vital waystations for prairie 
pollinators. The striping on the hand signifies the constant rising and lowering water table and the colors represent the different flora 
species these butterflies are in a symbiotic relationship with. FLOODED BY WONDER invites viewers into a shared dialogue of 
healing, wonder, and ecological coexistence.

https://prairieecologist.com/2011/12/14/regal-fritillary-butterflies-in-the-platte-river-prairies-2011/
Historical & Ecological Context
Merriam’s civic identity is intrinsically linked to Turkey Creek. Devastating historical floods—most notably in 1977, 1993, 
and the historic crest of October 4, 1998—submerged neighborhoods and altered the town’s footprint. Rather than turning 
its back on the water, the City of Merriam re-envisioned its floodplain. Through deliberate flood mitigation and reclamation, 
vulnerable ground was transformed into community treasures like Chatlain Park and the Turkey Creek Streamway Trail. 
Today, this meandering greenway acts as an essential ecological buffer and wildlife corridor. The same bottomland 
hydrology and silt deposits that once threatened infrastructure nourish native flora, including the wild violets (Viola sororia 
and Viola pedata) that serve as the sole larval food source for the Regal Fritillary butterfly. 


Visual & Symbolic Narrative
The sculpture's base—a welcoming, stylized hand—is wrapped in stratified horizontal bands of color. These stripes carry 
a dual narrative:
● The Hydrologic Lineage: Reflecting the historic flood stages and watermarks of Turkey Creek, documenting past 
inundation transformed into current life.
● Botanical Vibrancy: Echoing the soft violets, warm corals, and riparian greens of the bottomland prairie flora that 
sustain local wildlife.
Perched atop the index finger, well above reach, rests the Regal Fritillary. Crafted with luminous color shifting dichroic 
elements, its wings catch changing daylight, shifting in hue just as the prairie shifts with the seasons. 


Fabrication & Durability
Engineered specifically for public parks and active greenbelts, the installation balances delicate optical effects with 
robust, low-maintenance design:
● Ground-Level Interaction: The lower hand structure is fabricated from heavy-gauge structural steel and durable 
fiberglass coated in solid, impact-resistant industrial paints, allowing park visitors to approach, touch, and 
photograph the piece without risk of delicate component wear.
● Elevated Kinetic Wonder: Intricate, luminous components including dichroic film and UV resistant acrylics are 
elevated over 12 feet in the air, protecting the butterfly form from vandalism while maximizing its silhouette 
against the Kansas sky.

● Paint finish: Golden Artists paint/Satin Base, custom paint applied as required, UV-stable pigments
● Clear-coat: PPG Deltron NXT DCU2021(Urethane automotive clear coat)
● Internal structure (armature/plinth): stainless steel or mild steel with a corrosion resistant coating (tbc)
● Foundation/pad-Concrete with steel reinforcing (by others)
Artwork fabrication methods:
● Steelwork/armatures: Cut/weld/dress steel tubes/plates. Coated with a corrosion resistant finish if mild-steel. 
● Fiberglass component: EPS-foam blocks are 5-axis machine cut to form the sculpt/shape. The foam is hollowed out to accept 
the metal armature/frame which supports it. The foam parts are then bonded together over the frame, to form the finished 
shape, with all joints made good.
● A Polyurea hard coat is applied over the foam to create the hard sculpted surface, this is then re-worked by hand to enhance 
and retain the sculptures surface details. 
● A primer then paint coat is then applied to the work
● Finally a clear top coat is applied to protect the paint finish (extends warranty, UV protection)

Care & Maintenance recommendations:
● Unless vandalised, it is not anticipated that the structure (steel, foam/hard-coat/paint) will need maintenance over its anticipated 
lifetime, however the clear top coat should be regularly reapplied (see below). Regular visual inspections of the Artwork should 
be made to ensure integrity. If vandalised, the figure can be reworked/painted by a suitable professional. 
● The finished surface can be occasionally lightly cleaned using a damp (water/mild soap solution/any type of normal car cleaner) 
lint free/microfiber cloth. When cleaning, light actions only should be used. Treat it like a car when cleaning, but avoid 
high-pressure washing. A data sheet detailing maintenance instructions. DO NOT POWER-WASH THE ARTWORK.
Long term surface treatments:
● Every 10 years it is advised to HVLP (High Volume Low Pressure) spray the sculpture with clear coat (PPG Deltron NXT). To be 
carried out by industry professionals. The clear coat paint is Urethane sign and automotive grade paint (it has a similar lifespan 
to car paint). This will need to be carried out according to the clear coat manufactures instructions.

8. All joints are made good and painted.

10. Once safely secured, all packaging can be carefully removed.
11. The site is then cleaned and returned to its original state
