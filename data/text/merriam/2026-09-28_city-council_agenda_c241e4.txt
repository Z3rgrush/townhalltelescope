 1. Proclamation - National Co-Responder Week   
   
Mayor Pape read the Proclamation declaring September 13-19 as National Co-
Responder Week and presented the Proclamation to Co-Responder Supervisor Jen 
Melby. 
  
Ms. Melby provided some statistics for 2025. There were 877 Police report referrals to 
co-responders; there were 318 cases where the co-responder was on scene to a police 
call. Additionally the co-responders had 600 follow-up calls, as well as 177 trauma 
follow-ups for after incident calls.While most of the follow-ups with folks are after a 
police call involving those individuals, the trauma follow-ups could be for people that 
witnessed an accident or some other type of traumatic event and were not necessarily 
involved.  
  
She estimates that approximately 30% of all co-responder calls resulted in diverting 
emergency room visits. The co-responders do an assessment on a individual and are 
able to establish a safety plan for them. This is the same service that they would 
receive at the emergency room. Having the co-responder provide these services free of 
charge also eliminates further stress on these individuals as they don't have incur 
hospital expenses while having a mental health crisis.

 2. Acceptance of the updated Emergency Operations Plan   
   
Captain Jerimiah Waters provided the background for this item. The City of Merriam 
maintains an Emergency Operations Plan (EOP).  This manual provides direction on 
the personnel, duties, and operational guidance in mitigating emergency situations.  
This year, all departments reviewed the EOP and made changes based on changes in 
personnel and other minor interoperation changes.  
 
The elected officials are also important participants in our Emergency Operations.  The 
following is a summary of your responsibilities: 
City Council's Role in Emergency Management Establish policy and priorities: 
 Accept the city's Emergency Operations Plan (EOP) and related emergency-
management policies. 
 Establish community priorities for preparedness, response, recovery, and 
mitigation. 
 Ensure emergency management is treated as a citywide responsibility. 
 Adopt ordinances necessary to support emergency operations. 
Provide funding and resources: 
 Authorize emergency expenditures when circumstances exceed normal 
departmental resources. 
 Support applications for state and federal grants when appropriate. 
Support continuity of government: 
The Council should ensure the city can continue its governmental functions during a 
disaster, including: 
 Establishing succession plans. 
 Ensuring alternate meeting locations and communication methods. 
 Maintaining essential financial and administrative functions. 
 Understanding how council meetings and governmental actions will occur if City 
Hall is unavailable. 
 Declare or authorize emergency actions when required: 
The Council may have a role in: 
 Declaring or extending a local state of emergency. 
 Approving emergency ordinances or resolutions. 
 Authorizing extraordinary expenditures. 
 Approving emergency contracts or purchases when required. 
 Requesting state or federal assistance. 
Provide oversight—not incident command: 
 This is an important distinction; During an active incident, Council members

Project Background/Description: 
Since the opening of the original Merriam Community Center (MCC), the City has had an 
agreement with Johnson County through the Office of the Area Agency on Aging to offer 
services to seniors in Northeast Johnson County.  
  
The City of Merriam and Johnson County currently have a Facility Use Agreement for the use 
of a multi-purpose room and catering kitchen at the MCC. The agreement permits the county 
to administer a Senior Citizens congregate meal program and Meals on Wheels program. 
Reimbursement for the use of the space is $125/month.  
  
Johnson County Nutrition Program is requesting renewal of the program from October 1, 2026 
through September 30, 2027; renewable for an additional year pending release of federal 
funds.  
  
The City Attorney has reviewed the agreement as to form.

Project Background/Description: 
At the Sept. 14 City Council meeting, Council accepted the City's updated Emergency 
Operations Plan (EOP). During the discussion, Council requested additional guidance outlining 
the Governing Body's responsibilities during an emergency, including expectations for 
availability, communication, and participation in emergency meetings. 
  
In response, staff developed Council Policy 15.0 Emergency Operations for inclusion in the 
Governing Body Rules & Procedures. The language clarifies the Governing Body's policy and 
oversight role, expectations for availability and communication, emergency meeting 
procedures, and the distinction between Governing Body responsibilities and operational 
incident command. 
  
Staff also developed a City Council Emergency Operations Quick Reference Guide that 
summarizes roles and provides practical guidance for Councilmembers during an emergency, 
including staying available, receiving official information, responding to resident concerns, and 
preparing for Governing Body action. If accepted, this document will be added as an 
addendum to the Emergency Operations Plan. 
  
Both documents are attached for Council review.  
City Council Goals and Objectives: 
2.0 Provide Exceptional Service Delivery 
   
Recommended Motion: 
1. Staff recommends the City Council adopt Council Policy 15.0 Emergency Operations. 
2. Staff recommends the City Council accept the City Council Emergency Operations Quick 
Reference Guide to be added into the Emergency Operations Plan.

CITY COUNCIL EMERGENCY OPERATIONS • QUICK REFERENCE
CITY COUNCIL EMERGENCY OPERATIONS
A Quick Reference Guide
WHO DOES WHAT?
MAYOR — Elected Official Liaison
•Serves as the primary elected-official liaison during a major emergency.
•Works with the City Administrator/Manager and emergency officials.
•Helps keep the Governing Body informed.
•Performs duties assigned by law and City policy.
•Does not direct field operations unless specifically authorized by law.
CITY COUNCIL — Policy & Governance
•Makes policy decisions required during an emergency.
•Acts on emergency appropriations, contracts, ordinances, and resolutions when required.
•Takes action required by law concerning emergency declarations.
•Supports continuity of government.
•Does not direct individual emergency personnel or field operations.

Project Background/Description: 
Staff recently discovered that there are changes forthcoming in regard to how the National 
Certification Board for Therapeutic Massage & Bodywork (NCBTMB) assigns and approves 
schools that qualify for education standards in the field of Massage Therapy. Previously the 
NCBTMB provided a list of assigned schools that met those education qualifications and were 
Board approved educational institutions. Massage Therapy professionals who completed the 
educational requirements of those approved schools were allowed to take the National 
Certification Examination for Therapeutic Massage and Bodywork, administered by the 
NCBTMB.  
  
The National Certification Examination for Therapeutic Massage and Bodywork exam tests the 
knowledge and skills of Massage Therapists. Passage of the exam results in a Massage 
Therapist earning the the distinction of being nationally certified to preform massage and 
bodywork. This National Certification is recognized by all states that regulate Massage 
Therapy Licensing as a qualifying factor to receive a state Massage Therapy License.  Since 
the State of Kansas does not license Massage Therapists each local jurisdiction must provide 
their own rules and regulations for Massage Therapy Licensing.  
  
The NCBTMB is no longer providing the oversight on qualifying these educational institutions 
and is transferring that oversight to the Commission on Massage Therapy Accreditation 
(COMTA). Complete transfer will occur by 2031 which creates a large gap in oversite of 
qualified educational institutions.    
  
Current City regulations do not require Massage Therapist to be Nationally Certified. 
Applicants for a Merriam Massage Therapist License must submit a number of documents, 
including educational transcripts for review. Those transcripts must be from an educational 
institution approved by the NCBTMB. City staff must verify that transcripts submitted are from 
an NCBTMB approved school. With the NCBTMB no longer providing the list, this creates a 
situation where verification is difficult to complete.  
  
Due to the fact that the Massage Therapy industry has known human trafficking and illicit 
massage business networks, there is also a lot of fraudulent documentation of educational 
transcripts. AI has made detecting these fraudulent documents more difficult, and can take a 
lot of staff time to verify these documents and educational institutions.  
  
As a solution to City staff vetting out the educational requirements, the proposed Code 
changes will require all Massage Therapist applicants to either be Nationally Certified by 
passing the national certification exam or the Massage & Bodywork Licensing Exam (MBLEx). 
The MBLEx is also a national  entry-level licensing exam created and administered by the 
Federation of State Massage Therapy Board (FSMTB). The FSMTMB and follows a similar 
process as the NCBTMB when reviewing applicants who want to sit for the MBLEx. They verify 
the educational transcripts an other required documents to ensure those applicants sitting for 
the exam have adequate education and training by approved educational institutions.  
  
The following outlines the proposed changes to Chapter 14, Article IV: 
  
14-111 Definitions- Re-defined Approved /Assigned Schools as the NCBTMB is no longer 
providing the oversight on qualifying these educational institutions and is transferring that 
oversight to the Commission on Massage Therapy Accreditation (COMTA). Complete transfer 
will occur by 2031 which creates a large gap in oversite of qualified educational institutions.    
  
MBLEx- The MBLEx is one of two exams that test competence of massage therapists. 
Applicants must have passed this exam or the National exam to qualify for a Merriam 
Massage Therapy License.  
  
14-142-Establishment Issuance or Denial of License - Denial of a license prohibits the 
same applicant to re-apply for a one-year period. Lenexa has similar regulations. 
  
14-145- Suspension or Revocation of Massage Therapy Establishment License. Current 
regulations require a public hearing before the Police Chief  and is a different process from a 
regular Occupational License. It is recommended that Suspension or Revocation of a Massage 
Establishment should follow the same process as any other business license. A hearing before 
the Police Chief when it is likely that police action taken due to a violation of the Code was the 
cause of the revocation/suspension seems like an unfair process. Having the hearing before 
the City Council provides opportunity for the City and Licensee to provide evidence/information 
related to the case, with the City Council making the determination to suspend or revoke.  
Appeals go through District Court. 
  
14-177 Therapist Application - Changes to Therapist Applications require all Massage 
Therapist to have either passed the National Exam or MBLEx. Passage of either of these 
exams measures the therapists knowledge and skills and verifies that the applicant has 
completed the required number of education and training hours from an approved/assigned 
educational institution. Verification of education is completed by the the NCBTMB or  FSMTB. 
  
14-179 Therapist License Issuance or Denial - Same as denial for Establishment Licenses. 
Lenexa has similar regulations. 
  
14-180 Therapist Education and Training requirements - Requires all Massage Therapists 
to meet the new educational requirement for having passed the NCBTMB National exam or 
MBLEx. 
  
14-180(c) Continuing Education requirements- this section deleted as the NCBTMB and 
FSTMB have required continuing education requirements to maintain a therapist status.  
  
14-181 Therapist Revocation or Suspension of License - Adds additional violations that 
could cause a suspension or revocation. Current Code requires hearing before Police Chief. 
This is a similar process as the Establishment License which seems unfair. This section allows 
for review by the City Administrator with appeals going to the City Council. 
  
14-184 Therapist License Applicability to existing Massage providers - This section 
allows currently licenses Massage Therapists who do not meet the education requirements 
one year to get in compliance with the new regulations, This provision is allowed for Massage 
Therapists who have been licensed with the City for at least two years. Therapists who have 
been licensed for at least five consecutive years may continue to renew their licenses without 
the new educational requirements as long as there are no lapses in active licensure.   
  
If approved, notice of the changes will be sent to all currently licensed massage therapy 
providers prior to licensing renewal which occurs in January. 
  
The proposed ordinance has be reviewed by legal counsel.

Background/Information: 
As part of the Downtown Merriam Master Plan process, Shockey Consulting engaged ETC 
Institute to conduct a community survey to better understand resident and business priorities 
related to Downtown. ETC mailed survey packets to Merriam residents and business owners in 
July 2026 and followed up to encourage participation. Respondents could return the survey by 
mail or complete it online. A total of 471 surveys were completed. Here's what we heard: 
 Direction for Downtown: 78% of residents support or strongly support moving in a 
different direction for Downtown, with more dining and local shops, more walkable 
streets, new public spaces, and some added housing. Ten percent oppose or strongly 
oppose that direction. 
 What residents want: When asked to select the three things they would most like to see 
Downtown, the leading responses were more restaurants and cafes (62%), more local 
shops and retail (48%), evening venues (34%), and Turkey Creek greenway and trails 
(32%). Preserved historic character was selected by 23%. 
 Flooding: Approximately nine in ten residents said protecting people and property from 
flooding and avoiding worsening impacts upstream or downstream are important. When 
asked about the level of flood protection, 71% preferred a moderate option that 
balances cost and benefit. 
 Timing of improvements: 44% said the City should pursue flood control and Downtown 
improvements at the same time, 38% said flood control should come first, and 19% said 
the City should move ahead with improvements on safe land and decide flood control 
later. 
 Funding: 52% said Downtown improvements should be funded mostly through sales 
tax, while 36% preferred a balanced mix of sales and property tax. Eleven percent said 
no taxes should fund Downtown improvements. Separately, 81% support or strongly 
support dedicating a 1/4-cent sales tax to Downtown improvements. 
 Private investment: 77% support or strongly support the City using partnerships or tax 
incentives to attract private investment Downtown. 
 Business perspective: 67% of Merriam business owners/operators support or strongly 
support the general direction described for Downtown. Business respondents placed 
particular importance on respect for existing business locations and property rights, 
safety, parking, and reduced flood risk. 
  
The survey is one component of the broader Downtown Master Plan engagement process. It 
does not select a preferred Master Plan vision or direct a specific policy outcome. Rather, it 
provides the Steering Committee and City Council with community input on the priorities and 
tradeoffs residents and Merriam business owners/operators consider important as the Master 
Plan is refined. 
 
One distinction is important when reviewing the business results: the business survey 
represents Merriam business owners/operators citywide. Twenty-two percent of business 
respondents were located within the Downtown planning area, 22% were along Merriam Drive 
outside the planning area, and 56% were elsewhere in Merriam.

Executive Summary 
Overview 
ETC Institute administered a Downtown Master Plan Survey to Merriam residents and businesses in July of 
2026. The results from the survey will help the City understand the priorities and preferences of residents and 
business owners/operators as the City considers improvements to the area while facing challenges related to 
flooding, access, redevelopment, and how future improvement could be funded. 
Methodology 
ETC Institute mailed a survey packet to residents and business owners in the City of Merriam. Each survey 
packet contained a cover letter, a copy of the survey, and a postage‐paid return envelope. After the surveys 
were mailed, ETC Institute followed up with respondents to encourage participation. If respondents did not 
want to return the survey by mail, they were given the option to complete it online.  A total of 471  
respondents completed the survey.

• Charts showing the overall results of the survey (Section 1)

• Survey instrument (Section 3)
