The Arapahoe County Board of Commissioners typically holds weekly study sessions on Monday and 
Tuesday. Study sessions (except for executive sessions) are open to the public and agendas are available 
online at arapahoe.legistar.com. Meetings marked with an asterisk (*) can be attended virtually via 
arapahoe.legistar.com while non-asteriked (*) sessions are open to in-person attendance only. The 
members of the Board of County Commissioners may choose to attend study sessions virtually. 
The Board of County Commissioners may go into executive session during or at the conclusion of a 
study session or administrative meeting as necessary to receive legal advice or discuss other 
confidential matters, and if they do so, the public will be excluded from that portion of the meeting. The 
Board may alter the times of the meetings throughout the day, as well as cancel or reschedule noticed 
meetings. Contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or kdavis2@arapahoegov.com with 
questions about the agenda.
Arapahoe County is committed to making its public meetings accessible to persons with disabilities. If 
you need special accommodations, please contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or Relay 
Colorado 711 at least 3 days in advance to make arrangements.

Study Session
Meeting Minute Summaries October 6, 2026
The purpose of this study session was to provide the Board with an analysis of the 2026 state ballot 
measures as well as an overview of local government initiatives being considered by voters.
A PowerPoint presentation was presented, a copy of which has been retained for the record.
The agenda was established for state and local measures on the November 2026 ballot. Staff presented 
the State Ballot Measures. 
Staff summarized Proposition NN which would allow the state to keep some or all revenue above the 
TABOR cap equal to the amount spent on public education. Discussion was held regarding the 
distribution of funds, and it was mentioned that the senior homestead exemption would still be 
protected.  Commissioners asked how the funding would be distributed to the different districts. 
Commissioner asked for clarification of TABOR and the anticipated tabor refund. 
Commissioner asked about the support that the proposition is receiving. 
Staff summarized Proposition 132 which would increase and make adjustments to penalties for 
possession and distribution of fentanyl. The anticipated impacts of the increased penalties were 
reviewed.
Commissioner inquired about distribution of fentanyl and the position of the local partners. 
Staff summarized Proposition 133 which would increase the penalty for and expand the definition of 
child sex trafficking. The increased costs and workloads were reviewed. 
Staff summarized Proposition 134 which would require all elementary through collegiate school-based 
athletic teams and sports be designated male- or female-only based on biological sex and require schools 
and school districts to adopt policies on sex eligibility.
Staff summarized Proposition 135 which would prohibit surgeries on minors to alter their biological sex 
characteristics in response to their perception of sex or gender and the use of state or federal funds or 
insurance coverage as payment.
Staff summarized Proposition 136 which would set a cap on the state income tax rate for individual and 
corporate taxpayers at the current 4.4%. Discussion was held as to the current tax policy and TABOR.
Staff summarized Proposition 137 which would require a portion of sales tax revenue from sporting 
goods sales to be directed toward conservation, wildfire risk, and outdoor recreation initiatives. The 
current proposition does not include the senior homestead exemption. Clarification was made as to the 
revenue and distribution of the funds.

Staff summarized Amendment 82 which would establish a constitutional right for consumers to 
purchase natural gas for cooking or heating, and businesses to sell natural gas for use in homes and 
businesses.
Staff summarized Amendment 83 which would establish the constitutional right to fish and hunt certain 
wildlife and a policy that states fishing and hunting are the preferred means of managing Colorado’s fish 
and wildlife populations. Commissioner asked if the amendment lists specific species. 
Staff summarized Amendment 84 which would require voters to present the last four digits of a valid 
government issued form of identification in addition to their signature for their mail-in ballots to be 
counted in federal and statewide elections.
Staff summarized Amendment 85 which would require ballot titles to be written in plain language at no 
more than an eighth grade reading level and prohibit laws that require specific language in ballot 
initiative titles that interfere with the plain language requirement.
Staff summarized Amendment 86 which would require the Colorado Independent Congressional 
Redistricting Commission and the Colorado Supreme Court to review and approve any proposed 
congressional redistricting outside of the redistricting year and prohibit off-cycle changes that 
intentionally favor any political party or unnecessarily divide communities.
Staff summarized Amendment 87 which would remove the constitutional requirement for a flat income 
tax rate, replace Colorado’s flat income tax with a graduated rate structure, dictate allowable uses for 
new revenue, and exempt new revenue from TABOR. Staff discussed the impact of the proposed taxes.
Staff presented Municipal, School District, and Special District Ballot Measures. The different 
municipalities and districts were discussed in reference to their ballot measures. 
Presentation concluded without a vote. Commissioner Campbell absent and excused.
