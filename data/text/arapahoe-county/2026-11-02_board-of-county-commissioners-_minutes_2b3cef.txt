The Arapahoe County Board of Commissioners typically holds weekly study sessions on Monday and 
Tuesday. Study sessions (except for executive sessions) are open to the public and agendas are available 
online at arapahoe.legistar.com. Meetings marked with an asterisk (*) can be attended virtually via 
arapahoe.legistar.com while non-asteriked (*) sessions are open to in-person attendance only. The 
members of the Board of County Commissioners may choose to attend study sessions virtually. 
The Board of County Commissioners may go into executive session during or at the conclusion of a 
study session or administrative meeting as necessary to receive legal advice or discuss other 
confidential matters, and if they do so, the public will be excluded from that portion of the meeting. The 
Board may alter the times of the meetings throughout the day, as well as cancel or reschedule noticed 
meetings. Contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or kdavis2@arapahoegov.com with 
questions about the agenda.
Arapahoe County is committed to making its public meetings accessible to persons with disabilities. If 
you need special accommodations, please contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or Relay 
Colorado 711 at least 3 days in advance to make arrangements.

1:30 PM *Recommended Next Steps for Energy Performance Contract No. 3
Board Summary Report

The purpose of this study session was to present the proposed audit results and contract options, review 
the financial and operational evaluations conducted by the Facilities and Fleet Management Department, 
and provide staff’s recommendation on next steps for the county’s third energy performance contract.
A PowerPoint presentation was presented, a copy of which has been retained for the record.
Staff presented the purpose of the meeting and staff recommendations.
Background of the Energy Performance Contracts (EPC) was reviewed. A graph was displayed showing 
how an EPC works.
Arapahoe County History of EPC’s was presented. EPC #1 was in 2006, and the EPC #2 was in 2014. 
Both identified project areas. The current EPC #3 was reviewed. The contract evaluated areas Arapahoe 
Couty buildings for water and energy efficiency. The EPC#3 identified on-site solar as an opportunity to 
accelerate because of sunsetting federal tax credits.
A list of measures and their highlights was presented. The payback of the project summaries was listed. 
Staff attempted to remain near a 15-year payback. 
Commissioner asked about solar payback.
Staff reviewed the areas of refinement. The Willdan Phase 2 Proposal projects were summarized. 
Executive takeaways from the 15-year financial structure were listed. A chart showing the cashflow was 
displayed.
Staff does not recommend moving forward with Phase 2 as presented as the analysis does not 
demonstrate adequate return on investment or energy savings.
Phase 1 solar feasibility refinement was discussed for the different proposed buildings. 
Commissioner asked about the different options for solar install. 
A chart was displayed reflection the saving after 30 years.

The purpose of this study session was the CSU Extension staff presented an informational review of 
2025-26 programmatic highlights as a recap of existing Extension programs in Arapahoe County. The 
Extension Regional Director provided an overview of the 2026-27 Community Needs Assessment 
process and findings to date and will seek the board’s feedback/reactions to those findings as well as 
guidance with community conversations and planning.
A PowerPoint presentation was presented, a copy of which has been retained for the record.
Background of the CSU Extension was presented. A map of the service area for the Extension Program 
was displayed. The partnership collaborates with youth programs and partnerships. The community 
accomplishments were reviewed. Collaborations and the 2027 plans were presented. 
The 4-H Youth Development was presented. 4-H club programming is for ages 5-18 and offers a variety 
of project areas & events. Key projects and accomplishments were reviewed. 
The Family and Consumer Science was presented. The goal is to make informed choices and increase 
livelihood, health, and enhance wellbeing. 
CSU Horticulture was presented. Staff assist the community with gardening assistance. Some of the 
major topics are droughts and pests. The programs within the horticulture program were reviewed. 
Commissioner asked for clarification regarding the tour and how it was marketed.
Commissioner commented of the interest of the community in horticulture.
The 2026 Community Needs Assessment was presented. The assessment process was reviewed. Phase 1 
was a CSU Extension Opinion Survey which did not provide as much data as anticipated. A Front 
Range survey was conducted and the county and results were discussed. The Key findings of the survey 
were listed.

2:30 PM *1A Resident Advisory Committee Review and Discussion of the 2027 Recommended Budget 
in Alignment with Ballot Measure 1A
Board Summary Report
Presentation
Attachments:
The purpose of this study session was to provide the Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) with an 
overview of the 1A Resident Advisory Committee’s (RAC) review of the 2027 proposed budget as it 
pertains to 1A revenues and whether the proposed budget aligns with the intent and purpose of Ballot 
Measure 1A, approved by voters in 2024.
A PowerPoint presentation was presented, a copy of which has been retained for the record.
Commissioner Fields left at 2:58 p.m.
The background of the Committee and the purpose was reviewed. 
The meeting summaries were presented. The advisory committee meets quarterly and reviewed annual 
work plan, budget recap and strategic outlook, transportation and road maintenance capital plan, and 
2027 recommended budget presentation.
The themes and observations of the advisory committee were presented. The Committee confirmed 
alignment of 2027 budget investments with established 1A priorities. Committee members want to have 
improved communication with the departments and address federal financial impacts. 
Commissioner thanked the Committee for their work and reiterated the impact of the requested 
discussions sounding the federal impacts. Commissioner also wanted to ensure the Committee felt 
supported.
