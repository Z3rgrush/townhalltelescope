The Arapahoe County Board of Commissioners typically holds weekly study sessions on Monday and 
Tuesday. Study sessions (except for executive sessions) are open to the public and agendas are available

members of the Board of County Commissioners may choose to attend study sessions virtually.

meetings. Contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or kdavis2@arapahoegov.com with 
questions about the agenda.
Arapahoe County is committed to making its public meetings accessible to persons with disabilities. If 
you need special accommodations, please contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or Relay 
Colorado 711 at least 3 days in advance to make arrangements.

Board Summary ReportAttachments:
The purpose of this study session was the Community Development and Housing Stability Service 
(CDHSS) staff requested direction from the Board of County Commissioners regarding the 
reassignment of the County’s 2026 Private Activity Bond allocation due to a change in the financing 
status of a project previously prioritized to receive $3,000,000 of the County’s 2026 PAB allocation and 
subsequently approved by the Board of County Commissioners on March 31, 2026.
A background of the request was presented. 
Staff are requesting and recommending reallocating the available $3 million in County PAB authority to 
the Reserve at South Creek which would increase the County’s total PAB for the Reserve at South 
Creek to $ 7,005,092. This increase would allow the project to move forward with its financing plan 
while ensuring that the county’s available PAB allocation is fully utilized and would advance affordable 
housing development priorities. 
Discussion was held regarding why the project was selected over others. 
5-0 in favor of request.

1:00 PM *Intergovernmental Agreement between El Paso County Sheriff’s Office and Arapahoe County 
Sheriff’s Office Regarding Incinerator Use and Restrictions
Board Summary Report
Draft Agreement
Attachments:
The purpose of this study session was the Sheriff’s Office sought to enter into an intergovernmental 
agreement (IGA) with the El Paso County Sheriff’s Office for the purpose of using their Incinerator for 
drug and firearm destruction. The purpose of this IGA is to have a backup plan in place due to 
incinerator options becoming more difficult to sustain and find throughout the state. The Sheriff’s 
Office requested the Board of County Commissioners to approve the IGA and authorize the Sheriff to 
sign the IGA.
A background of the request was presented. 
Sheriff’s office staff are requesting to enter into an IGA with El Paso County. The IGA would create a 
backup plan to be in place due to incinerator options becoming more difficult to sustain and find 
throughout the state. 
The IGA would allow the Sheriff’s Office to continue to destroy former items of evidentiary value with 
minimal costs incurred to the county. Staff provided the current cost per incendiary cycle which is $350.
Commissioner confirmed that the fiscal impact will be absorbed by the current budget. Commissioner 
asked how frequently the Pueblo free incendiary location is utilized. 
Staff clarified if the Sheriff would be authorized to sign the IGA. 
5-0 in favor of the request.
1:15 PM *Drop-in: Evidence.com Revised Contract; Need for In-House Programmers
Board Summary Report
Evidence.com Quote
Attachments:
The purpose of this study session was for the Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) to consider 
approving a necessary contract modification to the 18th Judicial District Attorney's Office 
Evidence.com contract from the "Justice" to "Justice+" tier. This critical upgrade will deploy advanced 
features allowing our staff to immediately identify key moments within the nearly 200 hours of video 
evidence received each workday, ensuring effective case evaluation and preparation for motions 
hearings and jury trials. Additionally, this request serves to introduce the immediate need for dedicated 
technological development/programming assistance - specifically evaluating a future Senior Solutions 
Architect role- to rapidly identify and implement modern workflow solutions as our office continues to 
be inundated with digital evidence and other technological issues.
A background of the request was presented.

The purpose of this study session was, since the adoption of the Transit Oriented Communities bill 
(HB24-1313), staff have been creating proposed rezoning maps throughout the unincorporated county to 
provide zoning that will allow the amount of density required by the State in our Housing Opportunity 
Goal (HOG). At the direction of the Board of County Commissioners (BOCC), staff have focused on 
three key areas for the rezoning: the Federal Boulevard transit area; the Dry Creek Light Rail Station 
transit area (Inverness neighborhood); and the Four Square Mile neighborhood transit area. Community 
outreach meetings have been held, seeking feedback on the proposed rezoning maps. In this study 
session, staff shared feedback from the community as well as seeking guidance on next steps.
A PowerPoint presentation was presented, a copy of which has been retained for the record. 
A background of HB24-1313 was reviewed. 
Staff shared the Unincorporated Arapahoe County Housing Opportunity Goal ( HOG). The HOG 
requirement is 24,463, current zoned capacity is 5,287, making the housing unit shortfall 19,176.
The new zoning districts Residential Multifamily (R-MF) and Mixed Use (MU) were listed showing the 
maximum building height and minimum density for each of the zoning districts.
The Board’s direction from previous study sessions were shared. The three focus areas that were 
selected were Federal Boulevard Enclave, Four Square Mile Area, and the Dry Creek Light Rail Station. 
A map of the Federal Boulevard Enclave was presented along with the existing zoning and proposed 
zoning of the site. The proposed zoning includes 73 parcels rezoned to R-MF which calculates to 
approximately 650 dwelling units, and 39 parcels rezoned to MU which calculates to approximately 566 
dwelling units. 
Staff shared what the density would look like with R-MF 13-35 dwelling units per acre, and MU 35 
dwelling units/acre. Images of the complexes were displayed.
Survey results, housing preferences, and primary concerns from the open houses surrounding the 
Federal Boulevard Enclave were discussed.
