The Arapahoe County Board of Commissioners typically holds weekly study sessions on Monday and 
Tuesday. Study sessions (except for executive sessions) are open to the public and agendas are available 
online at arapahoe.legistar.com. Meetings marked with an asterisk (*) can be attended virtually via 
arapahoe.legistar.com while non-asteriked (*) sessions are open to in-person attendance only. The 
members of the Board of County Commissioners may choose to attend study sessions virtually. 
The Board of County Commissioners may go into executive session during or at the conclusion of a 
study session or administrative meeting as necessary to receive legal advice or discuss other 
confidential matters, and if they do so, the public will be excluded from that portion of the meeting. The 
Board may alter the times of the meetings throughout the day, as well as cancel or reschedule noticed 
meetings. Contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or kdavis2@arapahoegov.com with 
questions about the agenda.
Arapahoe County is committed to making its public meetings accessible to persons with disabilities. If 
you need special accommodations, please contact the Commissioners’ Office at 303 795 4630 or Relay 
Colorado 711 at least 3 days in advance to make arrangements.

1:30 PM *Data Center Environmental & Regulatory Follow-up Discussion for Potential Land 
Development Code Updates
Board Summary Report
Data Center Policy Matrix
Air Quality Permitting Fact Sheet CDPHE
Staff Presentation
Attachments:
The purpose of this study session was staff was seeking direction from the Board of County 
Commissioners (BOCC) concerning potential updates to the Land Development Code (LDC) related to 
data center regulations.
A PowerPoint presentation was presented, a copy of which has been retained for the record.
Staff presented the background and purpose to the follow-up discussion.
The current existing county code was presented. The current Land Development Code does not define 
“data center”. 
Staff reviewed impacts that were previously discussed. Noise concerns were discussed, and staff 
mentioned mitigation factors. Air quality concerns were previously raised, and data center developments 
are subject to Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment. Energy consumption concerns 
were addressed. The BOCC may consider implementing use-specific standards for data centers. Staff 
discussed water usage as it relates to data centers and different options for the BOCC.
Staff reviewed what other cities and counties are doing to regulate data centers. The first option was 
temporary moratoriums. Other counties have strict allowances such as micro or accessory only data 
centers. Allowances have also been utilized in other counties. Examples of these allowances were listed. 
The next steps for staff were discussed. Staff presented three options for the BOCC for their preferred 
approach. 
Commissioner asked about noise levels overtime and protections to not harm neighborhoods.
Commissioner asked regarding the regulatory possibilities for the different levels of data centers. 
Commissioner inquired as to utilizing metrics used by other counties to regulate data centers, and other 
possibilities to promote energy saving. 
Discussion was held surrounding the preferred options and areas of focus such as economic impact and 
zoning. Commissioners advised staff of possible regulations and categorization for the future study

2:30 PM *Court Operations Update
Board Summary ReportAttachments:
The purpose of this study session was the Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) heard an update on 
court operations for the 18th Judicial District.
Staff provided an update and asked about their Capital Improvement Plan.
Chief Judge provided highlights of the BOCC’s impact to the County Justice System. First was the 
increase in District Court Judges. This has improved Domestic cases and improved time and caseloads . 
Second was the opportunity to open a new civil division. The impact has been reflected on the 
community that is served.
The court also received Magistrate Judge FTEs from the state to provide services for mental health and 
commitment cases. Competency docket will also be increased dues to the increased Magistrate. 
Upcoming and recent events were also reviewed. 
Commissioner mentioned legislative work and the possible increase to County Court cases.
Commissioner commented on the positivity from the presentation.
Presentation concluded without a vote. Commissioner Baker and Commissioner Campbell appeared 
virtually.
