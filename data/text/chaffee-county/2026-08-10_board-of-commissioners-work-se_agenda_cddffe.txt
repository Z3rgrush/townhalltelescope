Youth Prevention and Wellness:  
• The GVP grant has been completed. Although the project had a relatively short implementation period, it 
provided a valuable opportunity to build relationships with diverse community partners. One of the primary 
challenges was that local firearm retailers were already being approached by multiple initiatives, resulting in 
outreach fatigue and making engagement more difficult. Additionally, the grant required very specific 
messaging around safe firearm storage that did not always align with the perspectives and realities of local 
firearm owners, limiting opportunities for meaningful dialogue. Despite these challenges, the partnership 
established with 4-H is a positive outcome that will hopefully continue beyond the grant period, creating 
opportunities to support youth through mental health promotion and prevention programming. 
• Helped in 3 substance related RJ referrals this month. This is helping create a strong partnership between FYI, 
FCRJ, and CCPH and getting youth connected to resources. 
• Youth mental health curriculum for court-mandated referrals is ready to go. Have 3 referrals and will be running 
the 6 session class starting in late July. This program is an important resource because it addresses the 
underlying causes of many youth health challenges before they escalate into crises. By providing youth with 
coping skills, supportive peer connections, and trusted adult relationships, the class promotes long-term mental 
health, resilience, and healthy decision-making while filling a critical gap in prevention services available to local 
youth. 
• Class description “Making Supportive Choices (Youth Mind and Body Lab) is a six-session, evidence-informed 
youth wellness program that equips young people ages 12–18 with practical skills to manage stress, regulate 
emotions, build healthy relationships, strengthen resilience, and make positive decisions. Using interactive 
activities grounded in Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), Social Emotional 
Learning (SEL), trauma-informed care, and adventure therapy, the program helps youth develop protective 
factors that reduce the risk of substance use, delinquency, and other high-risk behaviors.” 
• Cross Country- Held a team breakfast and had great conversations about healthy eating and 
proper fueling. Attending 1-2 practices a week and been trying to run with all of the girls (rotating between the 
faster girls and the ones newer to running) and it’s been hugely successful. 
• Chronic Disease Assessment- We received the local health kids data! The assessment was completed and 
submitted. We picked Alcohol Use-Awareness and Substance Use Supports and Sugary drinks as our strategies.

PURPOSE/BACKGROUND
The purpose of this agenda item is to consider a resolution to approve the City of Durango 2022 and 
2021 Private Activity Bond State Allocation to the Colorado Housing and Finance Authority (CHFA) to 
support the financing of the Best Western Durango Inn & Suites motel conversion project for affordable 
housing, located at 21382 U.S. Highway 160 W, Durango, Colorado.
PABs are tax-exempt bonds that can be issued for specific purposes, including the provision of low-
income housing and economic development. The PAB allocation authorizes an issuance of bonds via 
CHFA for private sector projects that meet a public purpose as defined by the Internal Revenue Code. 
This bonding capacity can be used for manufacturing industrial development bonds, new construction or 
acquisition and/or rehabilitation of housing for low-to-moderate-income people, single-family mortgage 
revenue bonds, mortgage credit certificates (MCC), qualified redevelopment bonds for acquiring 
property in blighted areas, exempt facility bonds (such as hazardous waste facilities, solid waste 
facilities, or water & sewer facilities) or qualified 501(c)(3) bonds for use by non-profit hospitals and 
private universities.  It is the proceeds from the placement of the PABs that enable low interest, tax 
exempt debt to help support projects. The total allocation of PABs provided to local governments are 
adjusted annually based on population and a state allocation formula. 
The PAB Program allows the City to carry forward its annual allocation for up to 3 years of accumulated 
PABs for future use; assign its allocation to another entity or use; or allow the allocation to revert back 
to the State for general projects in Colorado. 
In 2020, the City assigned its first ever PAB allocation to assist with the refinance and rehabilitation of 
the Tamarin Square Senior Housing complex, a 68 unit affordable housing project in Durango. In 2021, 
the City did not receive any local requests for use of its allocation and approved Resolution 21-52 to 
carry forward the City’s 2021 PAB Allocation for future affordable housing projects within the City of 
Durango. 
On August 17, 2021 the Council approved a contract to acquire the 71-room Best Western Durango Inn 
and Suites hotel property in Durango for purposes of soliciting and partnering with a private developer 
to rehabilitate and own the property as long-term affordable rental or transitional housing. The Council 
determined use of the City’s 2021 PAB Allocation of $1,059,415 by assignment to CHFA may be 
necessary in future support of the motel conversion, or other eligible projects.
