Oral Health Program:  
• We have been spending a lot of time researching dental services for the Veterans Service Resource Fair.  We 
contacted numerous dental clinics to see if they are accepting any of the new dental insurance plans being 
offered through the Department of Veteran Affairs, calling multiple offices in the region (20 offices so far) and 
have only confirmed 1 in Salida and 2 in Canon City. This is a nationwide effort to get Veterans dental care in 
rural areas. Many Veterans are unable to receive covered dental services at the VA unless the care is due to 
direct effect of combat. This leaves many of our Veterans with poor dental health and this has been identified as 
a top health need. We are working with Ashley from our VSO to put together a presentation on the high

Maternal Mental Health: Our IMPACT BH grant work began on August 1. This $80,000 grant will support two projects - 
This Isn't What I Expected in Salida and BV, as well as our home visit program called Nourish. The goal of this grant is to 
increase screening and referral for maternal behavioral health, which includes screening for depression/anxiety, 
substance use, social needs and intimate partner violence. We hope to develop a really thorough pathway for all 
perinatal families who need additional support, and to make sure all providers know the resources available locally and 
in the state. This grant is in collaboration with Salida Health Center and includes a data collection component to 
understand screening and referral practices for perinatal families.

Youth Prevention and Wellness:  
• Youth mental health curriculum for court-mandated referrals is ready to go. Have 4 referrals and will be running 
the 6 session class starting in mid-August. It is now open to the public. This program is an important resource 
because it addresses the underlying causes of many youth health challenges before they escalate into crises. By 
providing youth with coping skills, supportive peer connections, and trusted adult relationships, the class 
promotes long-term mental health, resilience, and healthy decision-making while filling a critical gap in 
prevention services available to local 
youth. 
• Trusted Adult Night trainings to be held late September in both Salida and BV. 
• We will be partnering with Salida School District on their big read book club, likely reading The Addiction 
Inoculation.  A little bit about the book: The Addiction Inoculation is a comprehensive resource parents and 
educators can use to prevent substance abuse in children. Based on research in child welfare, psychology, 
substance abuse, and developmental neuroscience, this essential guide provides evidence-based strategies and 
practical tools adults need to understand, support, and educate resilient, addiction-resistant children. The 
guidelines are age-appropriate and actionable—from navigating a child’s risk for addiction, to interpreting signs 
of early abuse, to advice for broaching difficult conversations with children.

 -  CPNR’s Weed Management Division (WMD) has spent approximately 90% of their summer 
 hours in the ﬁeld. The Weed Management Program manages all the County Roads in both 
 Chaﬀee and Lake Counties (per IGA), as well as U.S. Forest Service lands, CDOT 
 rights-of-way, and private lands. In addition to ﬁeld work, WMD has re-established IGAs 
 with Lake County, the City of Salida, and Fremont County. The Fremont County IGA allows 
 the WMD to receive funding to treat noxious weeds on BLM lands. 
 -  In 2026, WMD used a cost-share program through a Noxious Weed Fund Grant to oﬀset 
 50% of the cost for private land treatment with landowners. So far, WMD has treated 150 
 acres of private property through this program. This is a vital part of our overall weed 
 management work as it helps get WMD treatments in the ﬁeld and stops the spread onto 
 neighboring properties. 
 -  WMD spent roughly 3 weeks in Lake County treating their County Roads; 139 miles in 
 total. Lake County has a list A species, Orange Hawkweed, on Forest Service land. WMD 
 also manages Forest Service lands in Chaﬀee County, and this year Spotted Knapweed 
 and Hoary Alyssum (watch list species) were the main targets. We managed roughly 10 
 acres of FS land in Chaﬀee County. 
 -  WMD has treated 337 miles of Chaﬀee County Roads so far. We’ve also released 
 Bio-Control for Spotted Knapweed and are hoping to release some Puncturevine weevils 
 this fall. We also have more CDOT treatments planned this fall on highways 291, 285, and 
 50. 
 -  WMD has been managing the America the Beautiful grant through summer of 2025. This 
 grant is speciﬁcally for ﬁre mitigation, and we utilized this funding to help suppress 
 cheatgrass. We’ve proactively treated our County Roads with a pre-emergent called 
 Rejuvra to help stop the next 3 seasons of growth. A third-party contractor does most of 
 this spraying, and collectively we have treated 326 miles of County Roads across 600 
 acres. 
 -  Summer 2026 brought a number of equipment malfunctions. The new UTV was in the 
 shop for 2.5 months, but the work was covered under warranty and the Division was able 
 to complete treatment with a loaner UTV from the dealer. 
 -  The WMD programs also help manage and review revegetation on new developments 
 within the county. WMD has been deployed to a handful of developments to verify 
 ongoing weed management eﬀorts or assist developers in creating a management plan 
 for their noxious weeds. 
 -  Weed Board reestablished with biannual meetings (spring and fall).. 
 -  This winter will be focused on data collection and mapping. We utilize the points we 
 plotted this season to show where new target populations may occur and see which 
 historic areas may be weed free. All our grant reporting and grant renewals will be done

To:   Department Director and Board of County Commissioners 
From:  Brandon Wolff 
Subject:   STR Licensing  
 
 
 
Summary: 
 
Of the 310 STR Licenses available in the county: 
 
• Approximately 286 active and licensed units.  
• Approximately 13 applications in process, 5 of which are novel applications  
• All initial application units were inspected by staff. 
• When we began physical inspections in 2023, approximately 50 units were referred to the 
Building Safety Department for open building permits or unpermitted construction. In 2025 
there were 6. In 2026 we had about 15. Most of the issues that have gone back to the Building 
Department are on initial applications, but we have had some properties with unpermitted 
modifications since their last inspection. This illustrates the non-static nature of the properties, 
and the continued importance of “boots on the ground” inspections. Staff continues to work 
with applicants to rectify impediments to license issuance. 
 
• The same software, “Munirevs”, is still being used for STR monitoring and licensure, but has 
been purchased by a competitor and there has been some amalgamation. The company is 
now known as “Neumo.” Aside from typical growing pains and some subsequently repaired 
software conflicts with our current payment processing agent, the applicant experience has not 
changed. 
 
• Staff would like to revisit the possibility of adopting the powers granted under CO HB 23-1287 
to remove the ads of properties which do not have a valid STR license. CO HB 23-1287 allows 
 
Page 42 of 70
Page 2 of 2 
 
Chaffee County to contact the ad hosts (AirBnB, VRBO, etc) directly, in order to have them 
remove advertisements. This would ensure a greater percentage of compliance and reduce 
county resources used on enforcement action. 
 
• Exploitation of common spaces being utilized to maximize guest capacity is on the rise and 
becoming problematic. Properties which have a septic capacity greater than the room sizes 
allow for, often use a sleeper sofa to make up the difference. However, this often creates 
safety issues and potentially put the property at odds with adopted CDPHE standards and 
building code. Staff would be amenable to more defined parameters on bedrooms and 
sleeping space as they pertain to STR Licensing, for the sake of consistency, general 
occupant safety, and the prevention of situations which encourage violations.

 Revision Date:  April 12, 2022  , 
 September __, 2026 
 1. Compliance with  Law  State Statutes  and County Policy 
 1.1. All expenditures shall be made in compliance with  federal and state law  the 
 Colorado Revised  Statutes, as amended,  and adhere to all County policies  and 
 procedures  issued relative to the purchase of goods and the expenditures of 
 approved budgets. 
 1.2.  Purchases  or  contracts  made  contrary  to  the  policies  will  not  be  binding  upon  the 
 County and the person making the purchase may be held personally  liable. 
 1.3.  Intergovernmental agreements (IGAs) and memorandums of understanding 
 (MOUs) are written agreements binding the County and require BOCC approval 
 after legal review unless speciﬁcally delegated in this Policy. 
 1.4 Grant applications and agreements are written documents that bind the County, 
 with or without ﬁnancial obligations, and must be approved by the BOCC after 
 legal review. 
 1.5  Failure to comply with the terms of this Code may result in disciplinary  action up 
 to and including termination of employment or legal action. 
 1.  6  4  . Any provisions in contracts agreeing to be governed by laws other than  those of 
 Colorado or federal law shall be void and without eﬀect. 
 2. Purchasing and Contracting Authority 
 2.1. All purchases in excess of $15,000 and contracts obligating the County for an amount in 
 excess of $15,000 require the approval of the Board of County  Commissioners 
 (B  O  o  CC) at a publicly-noticed meeting.  Unless delegated below, all such contracts shall 
 be signed by the Chair of the BOCC.
