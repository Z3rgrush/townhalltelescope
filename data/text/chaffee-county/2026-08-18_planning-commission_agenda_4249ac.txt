STAFF REPORT 
DATE: 8/11/2026 
PLANNER: Nick Gomer 
APPLICANT(S): Jacob Labrie 
ADDRESS/PARCEL: 15099 County Road 350 (“The Property”) 
REQUEST: To reduce the standard applicable resource setbacks to as little as one foot for 
installation of a deck onto an existing structure. 
STAFF RECCOMENDATION: Staff recommends approval of the request based on the below 
analysis and findings. 
 
PROJECT DESCRIPTION:  
Location: 15099 County Road 350 – West of Buena Vista 
Current Zoning: Residential Suburban (RS) 
Level of Review Required: Planning Commission Hearing and Resolution 
Background and Context: Sited on the subject property are two existing dwellings and two 
existing accessory buildings. Also located on the subject property are ponds and wetland areas, 
which have been delineated to be smaller than a tenth of an acre. Per Table 3.6.2.1 of the 
Chaffee County Land Use Code (“CCLUC”), lakes require a one hundred foot resource setback 
and wetlands smaller than a third of an acre in size receive a fifty foot resource setback.  Sec. 
3.6.2.2 of the CCLUC provides an “alternative compliance” process by which resource setbacks 
can be reduced. If they are to be reduced to within 50’ of the resource, hearing and decision by 
the Planning Commission is required. 
 
In this instance, the Applicant is requesting a setback reduction for a new outdoor uncovered 
deck, connected to the principal dwelling, that at it’s closest would be one foot from the edge 
of the pond. The new deck would be approximately two hundred and eighty eight square feet 
in size. Currently, both existing dwelling units are located within fifty feet of the resources and 
the secondary dwelling has an existing deck that is approximately the same distance from the 
pond as the new deck is proposed to be.

1. Red Mountain Environmental Engineering Report Key Excerpts and Findings (See Exhibit E) 
A. Overview and site information (page 1) 
Masterpiece Outdoor requested that Red Mountain Environmental, LLC (RME) delineate all 
potential wetlands and Waters of the U.S. (WoUS), and Waters of the State (WoS) around the 
existing home at 15099 County Road 350 (parcel 327107100192) for the potential remodel of

Based on the wetland delineation, the homesite redevelopment project would have no direct 
impact to wetlands or other Waters of the U.S., or waters of the State, and no permitting from 
the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) or the Colorado Department of Public Health and 
Environment (CDPHE) would be needed. 
 
The conduit for the powerline from the solar array to the house would cross USACE-regulated 
wetlands, and would have temporary impacts to those wetlands, requiring a non-notifying 
Nationwide Permit 57 – Electrical Utility Line and Telecommunications Activities. 
 
B. Direct Impacts (page 15) 
The project would result in approximately 288 sqft of new impacts within County-prescribed lake 
buffers. As the new decks and infrastructure (such as the helical piers) are not within any wetlands 
or waters, the project would have no direct impact on aquatic resources, and there would be no 
loss of wetlands or open waters. The proposed development areas would not impact or remove 
any riparian vegetation (Sec. 3.6.5.2 Disturbance of Riparian Vegetation, Shorelines, Riverbanks, 
and Streambanks of the LUC). There would be no dumping into waterbodies (Sec. 3.6.5.3 
Dumping). 
 
As most of the new decks and home components would be constructed on helical piers, there 
would be no loss or impingement on fluvial systems or floodplain functionality. 
 
The electrical conduit would result in 7.2 sqft of temporary wetland impacts, and the disturbed 
areas would be reseeded and revegetated. 
 
C. Indirect Impacts (page 15) 
With the appropriate use of stormwater best management practices (BMPs), such as silt 
fence or wattles, there should be no discharge of sediment into wetlands or surface waters, 
and there should be no diminished function of wetland or surface water values. 
 
Even with the use of BMPs, there is a risk or discharge of sediment into wetlands or waters. 
Given the small size of the project and use of BMPs, these potential discharges are 
anticipated to be extremely minor in size and scope. Wetland plant species in this area are 
Page 22 of 67
dominated by relatively common and resilient species such as beaked sedge, which have 
deep and extensive root systems, and can easily grow through minor amounts of sediments. 
The ponds are relatively large and deep, and minor accidental discharges of sediment into 
these waters would not be anticipated to result in any measurable or meaningful loss in water 
holding capacity or negative impact to water quality. Minor sediments accidentally discharged 
from construction would quickly settle out, and would 
have not meaningful impact.  
 
Given the small 288 sqft expansion of the decks near the wetland fringe, it is highly unlikely that 
the decks would result in increased shading of wetlands, and there should be no decrease in 
wetland function due to shading. In the area of the proposed eastern patio, the wetland fringe is 
discontinuous given the steep excavated banks of the pond, and along most of the proposed patio 
area there is no wetland fringe; the open waters of the pond transition directly to upland lawns. 
Based on these existing conditions, the indirect impacts of the new decks is not likely result in any 
decreased wetland function. 
 
The incursion into the resource buffers would not result in water quality degradation, stream 
bank erosion, a meaningful reduction in riparian or wetland habitat, degradation of aquatic 
habitat, destruction of floodplain functionality, or the endangerment of life or property due to 
fluvial hazards. 
 
D. Mitigation Plan for Restoration of Resource Setback Areas (page 16) 
The proposed remodel of the house would result in approximately 288 square feet of new impacts 
within 100-foot resource setback areas for lakes (100-foot setback from the OHWM of a lake). The 
area proposed for the new development is dominated by mowed lawns, with a couple of planted 
ornamental aspen and spruce trees. There is no effective riparian habitat in this area, and the 
wetland fringe is very minor, and is mostly absent given the steep banks of the excavated pond. 
Based on the near monoculture of bluegrass, the ecological value of the proposed impacted area 
is very low. 
 
As presented in the County LUC, (Sec. 3.6.2.4 Restoration of Resource Setback Areas), the 
applicant is proposing to offset and mitigate the potential indirect impacts of disturbances 
within a resource setback; the applicant is proposing to restore approximately 0.13 acres 
(6,200 sqft) of riparian vegetation along Cottonwood Creek. This area is currently seeing 
consistent mowing to create landscaped lawn features. 
 
The restoration plan would include the cessation of mowing, and would allow native grasses, 
forbs and shrubs to naturally grow and establish within the 0.13-acre restoration area. This 
would be an approximately 300 percent larger area than the newly developed area 
(restoration would be at approximately 2:1 ratio to impacts).  See Exhibit  
 
The restoration plan would also include the removal of the deck adjacent to the caretaker unit 
(which is currently shading potential wetland areas) and allowing that area to be reestablished 
naturally. This could result in around 50 sqft of additional wetland development. 
 
Given the poor-quality habitats that would be impacted immediately adjacent to the 
existing home by the new development, the restoration of wetlands and riparian areas 
abutting and near Cottonwood Creek would have much more meaningful and effective 
ecological benefits. With the mitigation ratio of 3:1, the restoration area would be

B. Net-Positive Engineering & Safety 
A. Geotechnical Stability: The use of helical piers anchors the structure deep into 
competent soil layers. This "pins" the bank, preventing the erosion or "sloughing" common

traditional footings cannot offer.  The project will remove 13 large pre-existing caissons in

B. Structural Safety: The existing deck is at the end of its useful life. The new steel-framed, 
composite system meets code for the 2021 IBC/IRC standards and eliminates the 
liability of structural failure. 
C. Hydraulic Integration: The deck is a semi-pervious system. Slotted composite boards 
allow vertical water flow, ensuring the area beneath the deck continues to function as part 
of the overall water detention and infiltration plan with less wrought and debris from 
deteriorating building material. 
C. Maintenance and Access Continuity 
A. The expansion does not impede the ability to maintain the water detention functionality. 
Primary maintenance access is 390' away via a gated access road on the northeast of the 
property. The topography immediately in front of the deck is already unsuitable for heavy 
machinery; therefore, no functional access is lost. 
B. Unobstructed Shoreline: 86% of the detention pond perimeter remains open. 
Conclusion 
This application represents a low-impact, technically sound upgrade that resolves a legacy safety issue 
while providing a net ecological benefit to Chaffee County for minimal exception. Given the lack of 
federal/state objections and the project’s adherence to the 2025 Code, we appreciate the Planning 
Committee’s consideration on approving the residential project.
