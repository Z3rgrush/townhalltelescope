Overall position: Through September 30, 2026, DHS is projecting approximately $11.03 million in expenditures and $10.98 
million in revenue, resulting in a $44,695 deficit. This is approximately $72,000 below the Q2 projected result, primarily because 
expected revenue declined more than projected spending. Ending fund balance is projected at $666,684, well below the

Page 1 — Expenditures

$657,000 below 2025. 
• State and Federal Program Administration remains close to budget overall. Projected staffing and administrative costs of 
$3.67 million match the budget. This is about $230,000 less than the previous year and shows that we are being fiscally 
responsible in the areas that we can control. 
• Benefit and direct-service expenditures remain above budget. Projected costs of $6.09 million are $237,000 above budget but 
$51,000 below previous projections. Food Assistance, TANF, and Child Care are the primary increases above budget, partly 
offset by lower Child Welfare placement, LEAP, and Old Age Pension costs. Overall, benefit and assistance expenditures seem 
to be leveling off which is what we were hoping for.

weren't held whole are not reimbursed in July/August and this is why the amount is less than originally budget.

over expenditures minus a small match amount. 
 
Page 3 — Taxes and Fund Balance 
• Approximately $1.13 million of County funding is needed to support DHS programs. The projected uses are $583,356 for 
State and federal program administration, $326,798 for benefit-related costs, $68,675 for FYI programs, and $146,866 for 
County-payback after credits. 
• County taxes and other funding are projected at approximately $1.08 million.  The $44K difference between the funding 
needed and the actual amount available would be covered by fund balance. 
• Local match and reimbursement remain continuing risks. Changes in benefit activity, State reimbursement, and grant 
availability affect the County share of program costs. Budget planning needs to allow for delayed or lower reimbursements 
while maintaining required services. 
 
Page 4 — Summary and Highlights  
• Expenses are projected $657,425 below 2025. DHS continues to control operating costs and adjust grant-funded programming 
to available resources. The current expenditure forecast is also $113,000 lower than the Q2 projection. 
• Revenue is projected $65,316 above 2025. The modest increase in revenue, together with lower expenditures, improves the 
annual result. 
• The projected annual result improves by $722,741 compared with 2025. The prior-year deficit of $767,436 compares with a

1. The season is early, but the curve looks normal. Hospitalizations are rising at about the rate we’d expect.

Northern Hemisphere will see.

• Medicaid: The Governor proposes capping General Fund spending growth to 4.3%, compared with the 15% 
increase projected without additional action. Medicaid would still receive an additional $820 million in total

• Education: The proposal increases K-12 total program funding by $212.1 million, bringing average per-pupil

measure and a cash fund transfer of tobacco education grants.  
• Department reductions: Net operating reductions include $11.7 million for Human Services, $3.6 million for 
Public Health and Environment, and $1.9 million for Early Childhood. The proposal includes a 1% personnel 
services reduction, reduced pension amortization payments, and repeal of the Business Personal Property Tax 
Exemption Reimbursement. The governor also included his preference to privatize Pinnacol Assurance in order 
to maintain the state's reserves. 
Several items stand out for local public health:

 -  SB24-174 (Housing Planning) requires the County to adopt a strategic growth element and 
 a water supply element into our Comprehensive Plans by the “end of 2026.” 
 -  Strategic Growth Element 
 -  A strategic growth element integrates elements of the master plan to 
 discourage sprawl and promote the development or redevelopment of 
 vacant and underutilized parcels in urban areas to address the 
 demonstrated housing needs of the county or region and mitigate the 
 need for extension of infrastructure and public services to develop natural 
 and agricultural lands for residential uses. 
 -  Steps: 
 -  Describe a community’s existing and potential policies to promote 
 strategic growth; 
 -  Analyze vacant and underutilized land near transit or job centers 
 for potential inﬁll or redevelopment; and 
 -  Analyze undeveloped land that is under consideration for future 
 development, including its infrastructure needs, service needs, and 
 long-term ﬁscal impacts.
