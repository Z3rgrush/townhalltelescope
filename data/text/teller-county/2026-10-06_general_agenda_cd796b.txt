Work sessions conducted by the Board of County Commissioners are informal discussions among Commissioners, 
discussions between the Commissioners and County staff and with persons who are invited to attend to provide information 
to the Commissioners on specific topics.  Work sessions are not public hearings.  Members of the public may attend work 
sessions to listen to the discussions; however, public comment will not be taken. The Board does not take any formal actions 
at work sessions. The scheduling of work sessions is subject to change because the Board may move a work session to an 
earlier or later time during the day to accommodate changes in the Board’s schedule.

Teller County Board of Health July 7, 2026 
Meeting Minutes 
Date: July 7, 2026 
Time: 9:36 a.m. – 10:50 a.m. 
I. Call to Order 
Commissioner Bob Campbell called the meeting to order at 9:36 a.m. 
Present: Commissioners Bob Campbell, Dan Williams, and Erik Stone (via video); Dr. Laura 
Boschert, Medical Officer, Michelle Wolff, Director of Public Health, Austin May, Deputy Director of 
Public Health, Mackenzie Marsolini, Administrative Assistant IV, Ross Herzog, County 
Administrator, Mary Higgins, PH Nurse III, Ivy Morris, Director HR and Deputy County Administrator, 
and members of the public: Carl Anderson and Greg Liverman. 
II. Approval of Previous Minutes 
The Board reviewed the previous meeting minutes. No corrections were requested. 
Motion: Commissioner Bob Campbell moved to approve the minutes. 
Second: Commissioner Erik Stone. 
Vote: Approved unanimously. 
III. Fiscal Report 
Mrs. Marsolini presented the fiscal report. Commissioner Dan Williams asked about potential 
financial impacts to Teller County, and Commissioner Erik Stone asked whether staff anticipated 
additional financial holdovers. None were identified and the board discussed the importance of 
continuing to monitor the department’s financial position.  
Motion: Commissioner Dan Williams moved to accept the financial report. 
Second: Commissioner Erik Stone. 
Vote: Approved unanimously. 
IV. Community Health Assessment & Planning (CHAPS) 
Page 3 of 9
Mrs. Wolff reported an increase in suicides among males ages 45–65 and discussed the September 
19 Suicide Awareness Symposium at Columbine Elementary. 
Commissioner Williams asked whether outreach would include students and teachers, noting a 
need for greater focus on youth mental health. Mrs. Wolff reported that Public Health has met with 
school representatives and learned that their grant funding has slowed progresd. 
Commissioner Williams offered Board assistance, including a letter to the School Board. Ms. Wolff 
agreed that a letter could be helpful. Mary Higgins noted that additional local data is needed for 
many grant applications. 
Ms. Wolff also provided updates on the Rural Health Transformation Grant and ROAC. Teller 
County's role in the rural grant will include support for the non-emergent oxygen program and 
additional community data collection. ROAC has completed its strategic plan and continues 
reviewing substance-use data and reporting priorities. 
V. Communicable Disease 
Mrs. Higgins reported that communicable disease trends were generally favorable, with decreases 
in animal bites, to include respiratory illness. Tuberculosis gateway activity has increased and 
remains time-consuming with limited State reimbursement. 
Commissioner Williams asked about STDs; Ms. Higgins reported nothing unusual. Commissioner 
Bob Campbell asked about Ebola monitoring in Teller County. Ms. Higgins explained that 
individuals are federally assigned for 21-day monitoring and that the process is a significant 
workload. 
VI. Tick Surveillance 
Ms. Higgins reported that Colorado has received funding for increased tick surveillance. Teller 
County has conducted three surveillance outings and found one tick. 
Commissioner Williams asked about public information on tick prevention. Ms. Wolff will follow up 
regarding website resources. Dr. Boschert clarified that Lyme disease is not endemic in Colorado. 
Commissioner Williams recommended additional prevention information for hunters and outdoor 
recreation. 
VII. Environmental Health 
Mr. May reported that temporary food inspections and summer camp inspections were proceeding 
without significant issues. 
The Board discussed the recently passed Tamale Act, including concerns regarding outbreak 
response and reimbursement. Commissioner Williams emphasized the importance of maintaining 
data.

The Board also discussed childcare facilities, Special Use Permits, and facilities that may have lost 
State childcare licenses. Staff were directed to review facilities and determine whether their 
current operations remain consistent with applicable licenses and permits. 
VIII. OWTS Regulation Update 
Mr. May reported that the updated OWTS regulations will become effective July 24, 2026. Staff are 
preparing FAQs because some recent public information was inaccurate. 
Commissioner Williams recommended discussing the regulations at the upcoming hearing and 
providing information to the press, city managers, and realtors. Mr. May reported that OWTS 
practitioners have been supportive of the Transfer of Title program. 
Staff are also working with Human Resources on a new Environmental Health position to address 
increased workload. 
IX. Clinical and Preventative Services 
Presenter: Michelle Wolff 
Immunization activity is expected to increase as the school year approaches. The State 
immunization van may participate in the COMOM event. 
Commissioner Williams offered assistance with School District outreach regarding immunization 
rates. 
The reproductive health chart audit had no findings. Staff anticipate recruiting a replacement for 
Christina before her departure later this year. 
X. WIC 
WIC enrollment totaled 264 participants from January through May. Staff continue working to fill an 
open position. 
Ivy reported that Human Resources is reviewing the salary band and recruitment strategy. 
Commissioner Campbell asked about grant funding and whether an on-the-job training option 
could be used. Staff confirmed that training is available and that recruitment has been challenging 
countywide. 
XI. Vital Statistics and CFPS 
Vital Statistics operations were reported as normal. Commissioner Williams asked about 343 
death certificates; Ms. Marsolini explained that the number was primarily related to the local 
funeral home. 
CFPS funding was received, with previous funding supporting gun locks, medication  lock boxes, 
and bike helmets. Helmets were donated to TCSO for shop with a hero to provide them to those in 
need. Staff will continue outreach through community events.

XII. Emergency Preparedness 
Mrs. Wolff and Mrs. Higgins reported on disaster recovery activities, including Crisis Track, damage 
assessment, debris management, and asbestos concerns. 
Mrs. Wolff discussed reflective address signs to improve emergency response. Signs are estimated 
at approximately $20–$25 each, however, grant funding allowed for purchase and the signs will be 
given out during the county fair with the 4-H community on Senior day,  
Staff also reported on the oxygen program and the purchase of five emergency oxygen 
concentrators with backup batteries, totaling approximately $17,000 using PHEP funding 
Wildfire-related prescription access was discussed. Public Health issued a HAN message to 
providers regarding prescription access challenges. Dr. Boschert noted that pharmacies may 
provide emergency prescription fills. 
Commissioner Stone suggested using fire district events to promote emergency preparedness and 
bug-out bags. Ms. Wolff agreed to look into upcoming events, these will also be provided during the 
county fair 
XIII. Additional Childcare Discussion 
Commissioner Williams requested that Planning and Public Health review childcare facilities 
affected by State funding or licensing changes, including their SUPs, food service, and current 
authorized uses. 
XIV. Public Comment 
Greg Liverman asked about emergency registration for seniors so first responders know which 
residents may need assistance. Mrs. Wolff explained that the EARP system, in coordination with 
the Sheriff's Office and Senior Coalition, provides this service.  
Carl Anderson reported seeing his first tick in approximately eight years on his Divide property and 
offered his property for surveillance. Mr. Liverman also offered his property. Mr. Liverman 
expressed concern about prairie dogs, ticks, and potential plague transmission. 
XV. Adjournment 
There was no further discussion and the meeting was adjourned at 10:50 a.m.
