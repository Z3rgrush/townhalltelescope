Miller reported that the Moffat Counry Planning {a Zoning Commission met on Wednesday, September 2, 
2026, to review and discuss the proposed temporary moratorium resolutions on data center development. 
The Planning Commission is generally opposed to imposing a moratorium because members believe it could 
send the wrong message to potential businesses and investors interested in Moffat County. The Commission 
also recognizes that a lengthy moratorium could have broader economic and development impacts throughout 
the Counry, including limiting private landowners' abiliry to consider these rypes of projects and exercise their 
property rights. 
However, the Commission realizes the importance of providing legal clariry while the Counry develops 
appropriate definitions, Iand-use classifications, review procedures, and regulations for data centers. 
To minimize potential legal uncertainry associated by refusing to accept or process an application for a use that 
is not currently addressed in the Moffat County ZonrngResolution, the Commission agreed that a six-month 
moratorium would be sufficient. 
The Commission does not suppom a moratorium extending beyond six months and emphasized that the 
necessary work shouldbe completed within that period. If the BCC were to approve the six-month option, 
that would put the end point at March 2027. 
Bohrer commented that a moratorium can be rescinded as easily as put into place. He leans more towards a 
year moratorium and would like to table any action on that until the Ocrober l3th BCC meeting and hold some 
public comment meetings prior to that in connection with this process. 
Broom welcomed the chance to get more educated about data centers and while, originally, he had thought a 
longer moratorium would be the best, if the Commission can complete all their work in the six-month time 
period, all the better. He also encouraged the citizens to attend the public meetings. 
Villard observed that when the power plant and the coal mines were originally coming into Moffat County, 
there were the same rype of concerns and opposition voiced by the residents. Those entities rurned out to 
provide economic development/opporruniry and communiry partnerships. It is the dury of the BCC to do their 
due diligence and research to understand what gets brought into our communiry. A moratorium would be 
short sighted; education and regulation are the better path. Villard did agree with tabling the moratorium 
resolution. 
There was some discussion about what the public meetings process would involve. 
Bohrer moved to table the discussion regarding possible adoption of a Data Center moratorium until the 
October 13 BCC meeting, with public meetings in the interim. Broom seconded the motion. Motion carried. 3-0.
