Public Lands Update 
Chris Canaly, SLV Ecosystem Council, provided an update on the work the SLV Ecosystem Council has 
done. Over the last year, they have written over 100 letters involving 12 different public agencies 
advocating for changes in public land management. She said people are really concerned about 
changes, specifically about oil and gas, and specifically feel that oil companies should be paying 
royalties.  
There is a proposed oil and gas rule coming up and a deadline of August 24 to make comments. The 
proposal removes opportunities for the public, including hunters, anglers, ranchers, and local 
residents, to weigh in when land use conflicts arise in their own backyards. The actions taken by this 
administration thus far have disregarded what Westerners want. Chris Canaly believes they're going 
even further by reducing opportunities for people to express concerns about activities taking place on 
lands in their own backyard. She thinks the administration is proposing to get rid of a long-standing 
requirement that private landowners must be notified anytime oil and gas companies want to lease on 
their lands. There are over 57 million acres of these lands, where the federal government owns the oil 
and gas resources. In the San Luis Valley there are parcels between South Fork and Del Norte that went 
up for lease in 2008, and if that were to happen again, the Counties might not have a voice in that.

Emergency Management Animal Response 
Larry Brown said that he was recently called in to assist with the animal sheltering and rescue at the 
Aspen Acres fire in Pueblo.  Part of his job is to work with emergency managers to put together an 
animal response plan in the case of an emergency or disaster. Larry Brown showed pictures and 
examples of the setup of the shelters that were set up for the animals at the Pueblo fair grounds. One 
of the possible tools that could be implemented would be an emergency response trailer. It would be 
an enclosed trailer that would be completely stocked and supplied with anything necessary in the 
event of an emergency that would need this type of response.  All of the counties could share the use 
of the trailer. But, there would need to be discussion about how the trailer could be funded. The trailer 
would probably need to be at least 14 feet and it might cost under $4,000 or $5,000 to supply.  
Larry Brown was asked to include a separate line item in his funding request for the trailer if he was 
interested in requesting funds from the Commissioners. Or, the County Emergency Managers could 
possibly make the funding requests.

GIS Updates 
Craig Barraclough provided an update to the Commissioners. SLVCOG secured a state workforce grant 
and administered it for the region, paying for GIS mapping training for local government staff across 
the San Lis Valley. The funds were basically leftover COVID funds. Eight agencies and 14 staff were able 
to participate over a two-year span. One person in Rio Grande County was able to attend a national 
conference with all expenses paid. A couple of people took advantage of ESRI training. The total value 
of that was around $49,000.  
The Costilla County Emergency Manager was able to use the GIS training on the fire evacuations this 
summer so that the public could see it which was great timing. Craig Barraclough was able to assist 
with the railroad La Veta Pass project showing derailments and the project size which was part of the 
application. That was a great example of how the COG works together with other groups.  
The COG also applied for a grant through the Colorado State Internet Portal Authority and were 
awarded a grant to put up a mapping portal and hub. Counties and municipalities have been building 
geographic data though not all have had the same resources. A more regional approach let everyone 
work together for a shared benefit. This initiative will add a shared layer on top of local efforts, it will 
not replace the work any county is already doing. Craig Barraclough said that there will be policies and 
planning that will need to be developed.  
 
Proposed Legislation Requiring Revegetation 
Liza Marron, Saguache County, said that they are very much at the beginning of this legislative idea, 
and hoping they can gain stakeholders. She sent out two pieces of information, one is an issue brief 
that was presented to CCI, and the other is legislation already passed, HB 26-1340, which was to 
mandate revegetation or dry farming on formerly irrigated ag land, only for Water Division Two. At 
that time, it was asked it the legislation could be expanded to Water Division Three, but it wasn’t 
appropriate.  
There is a state statute requiring management of noxious weeds and generation of significant dust 
during high wind events. These conditions negatively impact neighboring ag operations, public health, 
wildlife habitat, and roadway safety. Dust storms originating from these properties have reduced 
visibility on county and state highways, and have contributed to multiple vehicle accidents. The 
proposal seeks to establish revegetation requirements that stabilize soils, reduce noxious weed 
infestations and improve environmental conditions while protecting public safety. It was asked why 
Water Division Three didn't want to be a part of it, and Craig Cotton and Nathan Coombs responded 
that it was too prescriptive. Saguache County wanted to share this information and hope to gain 
support. 
 
USDA Rural Development  
John Ready with the USDA Rural Development provided some updates and overview of their mission 
areas including affordable housing, business assistance, and community development. The affordable 
housing program helps very low and low-income individuals with low-interest loans and grants for 
housing development. The business assistance program provided guaranteed loans to banks for higher-
risk loans and direct loans to non-profits for revolving loan funds. They have worked with local banks 
such as Alamosa State Bank and First Southwest Bank on these loans. The community development 
program focuses on brick and mortar projects, water and wastewater infrastructure, and other 
community development initiatives.
