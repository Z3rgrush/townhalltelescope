Hayden Town Council Meeting Minutes 
October 1, 2026 
 
 
Call to Order/Roll Call 
 
Mayor Pro Tem Gann called the regular meeting of the Hayden Town Council to order at 6:00 p.m.    
Mayor Pro Tem Gann Offered a moment of Silence  
Mayor Pro Tem Gann Led the Pledge of Allegiance 
 
Mayor Pro Tem Gann, Councilmembers Carlson, Bell (Zoom), Haight (Zoom), Hayden, and Hicks were present. 
Mayor Banks was Absent. Also, present were Town Manager Mathew Mendisco, Chief Scott Scurlock, Finance 
Director Andrea Salazar, Intern Nathan Dettelbach, and Town Clerk Barbara Binetti.  
 
Councilmember and Staff Reports and Updates 
Councilmember Haight reported from the EDC that the Business Pitch is under way and will be held November 
17th. There are 12 candidates in the running for the competition. She said that the EDC commissioners would like 
to do a presentation to the Council on more initiatives in early 2027. 
Councilmember Carlson reported from the Arts Council that they had done the 2027 planning calendar early 
since Sarah Stinson would be out for the next few months. She also said that the Arts Council would be putting 
out a community programming survey. 
 
Study Session – There was no study session. 
 
Proclamations/Presentations – There were none.  
 
Public Comment –  There was none. 
 
Consent Agenda Items -  
1. Consideration of minutes for the Regular Meeting of September 17, 2026      
2. Consideration to Review and Approve payments dated 09/24/2026 in the amount of $146,581.50 
 
Mayor Pro Tem Gann moved to approve all consent items, seconded by Councilmember Hicks. A roll 
call vote was held. The motion passed unanimously.  
 
Old Business –   
The Council reviewed and considered  approval of Ordinance 752 - AN ORDINANCE ADOPTING A NEW 
SECTION 4.12.060 OF TITLE 4 OF THE HAYDEN MUNICIPAL CODE TO ESTABLISH A LEASH LAW FOR 
ANIMALS WITHIN THE TOWN AND MAKING CONFORMING AMENDMENTS TO SECTION 14.20.040 IN 
CONNECTION THEREWITH. Chief Scurlock presented the Ordinance at the last meeting. There were no 
further questions. A second reading was held and a Public Hearing opened at 6:06 p.m. There being no 
public comment, the Public Hearing closed at 6:07 p.m. 
Mayor Pro Tem Gann moved to approve Ordinance 752, seconded by Councilmember Hicks. A roll call vote 
was held and the motion passed unanimously. 
          
 
35
New Business. 
Council reviewed and considered for approval of RESOLUTION NO. 2026-14 - A Resolution Endorsing 
2026 Ballot Measure 6A, A Mill Levy Override for the West Routt Fire Protection District, Ballot Measure 
4B, Authorizing the Annexation of Hayden RE-1 School District into the Colorado Mountain College 
District, Ballot Measure 7A, A Half-Percent Sales Tax to Fund the Yampa Valley Regional Transportation 
Authority, and Ballot Measure 4A, A Mill Levy Override for the Hayden RE -1 School District. 
Mayor Pro Tem Gann read the resolution in its entirety.  
Councilmember Haight objected to the resolution saying that the language surrounding the RTA was 
making speculative claims that are not supported by any studies presented to Council. She proposed to 
remove the RTA language from the resolution. After discussion, Councilmember Haight  moved to 
amend the resolution, removing the RTA language. Councilmember Hayden seconded the motion.  
A roll call vote was held and the amended Resolution 2026-14 was approved unanimously. 
 
The Council reviewed and considered for approval RESOLUTION NO. 2026-15 A RESOLUTION OF THE 
TOWN OF HAYDEN, COLORADO ADOPTING AN EMERGENCY FEE SCHEDULE CHANGE WTH APEX 
WASTE SOLUTIONS. 
Apex Wast Solutions presented to the Council at the October 1, 2026, meeting. Councilmember Carlson 
noted that there were several typos on the appendix and asked to be corrected.  
Mayor Pro Tem Gann moved, seconded by Councilmember Bell, to approve Resolution 2026-15. A roll call 
vote was held. The motion passed unanimously.  
 
Staff and Councilmember Reports and Updates –  
Mathew reported Nathan Dettlebach will be covering for Sarah Stinson while she is on leave for the Arts and 
Culture department. He reminded Council that his (Mathew’s) review would be at the next meeting.  
He also reported that public forums have been held for the Fire Dstrct and School Districts ballot initiatives. They 
have been well attended and there has been good feedback. Mayor Pro Tem Gann said he would like for the 
Town to facilitate more forums.  
The citizen committee for the data center moratorium study will be announced shortly.  Council asked that Staff 
to draft letters to State and Federal Representatives on bills concerning data centers and related legislation, to 
be presented at the next meeting for Council review.  
Staff will facilitate the first CDOT/Union Pacific site visit for rail station planning at the end of October and there 
will also be a call for a volunteer citizen committee for the planning. 
 
Executive Session – There was none. 
 
Adjournment: - Mayor Pro Tem Gann adjourned the meeting at 6:53 p.m.

RESOLUTION 2026 – 16 
 
Endorsing the Referral of Ballot Measure 7A, A Half-Percent Sales Tax to Fund the Yampa 
Valley Regional Transportation Authority, to Voters for Approval 
 
Recitals 
 
Whereas, the voters of Routt County approved the creation of the Vampa Valley Regional 
Transportation Authority (RTA) in November, 2025; and 
 
Whereas, the Yampa Valley RTA was established with the goals of providing safe, reliable mass 
transportation options for the people of the Yampa Valley, improving overall traffic safety, and 
improving transportation access to its workforce; and  
 
Whereas, the RTA, in order to secure a permanent long-term funding source, has proposed a 
half-cent sales tax to help fund the organization, which, if approved by the voters, will be 
imposed upon non-essential items, specifically exempting groceries, food for home consumption, 
gasoline, diesel, prescription drugs, medical equipment, and fuel for home heat and power, 
within RTA boundaries in addition to other taxes currently imposed; and 
 
Whereas, the Council believes that endorsing the referral of the measure to the voters to let the 
voters “decide” on whether to approve the increase in sales tax is in the best interest of the 
community and the valley.  
 
Now, therefore be it resolved by the Town Council of the Town of Hayden, Colorado that: 
 
Section 1. The Town of Hayden endorses the referral of the Yampa Valley Regional 
Transportation Authority sales tax measure for the possible establishment of a 0.5% increase in 
sales tax on non-essential items (with exemptions on groceries, food for home consumption, 
gasoline, diesel, prescription drugs, medical equipment, and fuel for home heat and power) to 
fund the Yampa Valley RTA to the voters on the November 2026 ballot.  
 
Section 2. This Resolution shall be in full force and effective immediately upon its adoption. 
 
Passed, approved, and resolved this 15th Day of October, 2026.

About Western Resilience Center 
 
Western Resilience Center, formerly Yampa Valley Sustainability Council, is a 501(c)(3) 
nonprofit organization based in Steamboat Springs and serving Northwest Colorado. 
Established in 2009, WRC serves as a regional leader, resource, and catalyst for 
advancing practical, science-based solutions that reduce greenhouse gas emissions 
and strengthen the resilience of Northwest Coloradoʼs communities, ecosystems, and 
economy. 
 
Western Resilience Center works with local and regional governments, businesses, 
utilities, community organizations, state agencies, and residents to translate climate and 
resilience goals into coordinated action. The organization provides planning, facilitation, 
project management, technical assistance, community engagement, data analysis, and 
implementation support across interconnected areas including clean energy and building 
decarbonization, transportation, waste reduction and circularity, water conservation, 
watershed and forest resilience, and community preparedness. 
 
A core strength of Western Resilience Center is its ability to convene and manage 
complex, cross-jurisdictional initiatives that require sustained coordination among 
government, technical, nonprofit, business, and community partners. WRC has served 
as the project management entity for the Routt County Climate Action Plan Collaborative 
since its establishment, supporting participating jurisdictions and working groups in 
advancing the goals and strategies of the 2021 Routt County Climate Action Plan. 
 
As Northwest Colorado experiences increasingly interconnected climate, economic, and 
community pressures – including rising temperatures, changing snowpack and runoff, 
drought and water-supply variability, wildfire and forest impacts, regional growth, and an 
evolving energy economy – Western Resilience Center is increasingly focused on 
integrating decarbonization and climate adaptation into a comprehensive approach to 
regional resilience. Through this work, Western Resilience Center helps communities 
move from planning to implementation by building partnerships, aligning resources and 
policy, engaging residents and stakeholders, and creating the sustained capacity 
necessary to achieve measurable regional outcomes.
