Spruce Summit Resiliency LLC, an affiliate of Jupiter Power LLC (“Jupiter”), is proposing 
to construct and operate the Spruce Summit Energy Storage Project (the “Project”), a 400-
megawatt (MW) utility-scale battery energy storage system (“BESS”) within the Town of Hayden, 
Colorado (“Town”) on Parcel #940131001, an approximately 73-acre lot (the “Project Site”).  The 
Project would connect to the existing 138- kV Xcel Mt. Harris Substation via  a generation  
transmission line (“gen-tie line”) in unincorporated Routt County, Colorado ( the “County”). The 
Project would provide additional capacity to the electrical grid to assist with serving load during 
periods of peak demand by charging when demand is low and discharging when demand is high. 
Batteries are extremely fast acting, capable of responding within fractions of a second to stabilize 
voltages and support a very high- quality and reliable electric grid. In addition to supporting 
reliability and power quality, these batteries also accom modate the future integration of 
intermittent renewable energy sources, such as wind and solar. This will assist the  State of 
Colorado in meeting its energy procurement needs.

boundaries and jurisdiction, Hayden annexed the Project Site and two other nearby parcels into 
Hayden’s municipal boundaries and at such time, designated the Project Site “AC: Auto-Oriented

Zone Change – Review Criteria 
 
a. Correction of an error in an ordinance establishing the zoning for a specific property (if 
applicable).  The requested zone change corrects and clarifies the zoning status of the Project Site. 
Hayden had annexed t he Project Site with an “AC: Auto -Oriented Commercial Zone District” 
designation, but that zoning category is no longer recognized or contemplated in the current 
Hayden Development Code. The current map and code framework therefore create an ambiguity 
between the annexation -era designation and the zoning districts now available under the 
Development Code. The requested zone change resolves that ambiguity by placing the Project Site 
into a recognized district that better reflects the Project’s utility -scale energy infrastructure 
function and Hayden staff’s current direction that the Project be reviewed as an industrial use. 
b. Evidence of substantial compliance with the purposes of the Development Code. 
The requested zone change substantially complies with the purposes of the Development Code. 
The Project will place a utility -scale battery energy storage system on a parcel within Hayden’s 
municipal boundaries, where land use, infrastructure, access, publi c services, and operational 
impacts can be reviewed and conditioned through Hayden’s development review process. The 
Project promotes public health, safety, and welfare through modern fire protection, monitoring, 
access, stormwater, screening, lighting, and decommissioning measures , pollution-free provision 
of electricity and supports efficient public infrastructure by clustering a  grid-supporting use near 
existing energy infrastructure. 
The Project also supports the Development Code’s stated goals of implementing the Master Plan, 
protecting environmental quality, avoiding inefficient land use, providing orderly land 
development, and encouraging sustainable and resilient development design.  
c. Consistency with the  Master Plan.   The 2020 Hayden Master Plan sets out Hayden’s policy 
framework and growth strategy, placing significant emphasis on infrastructure, economic 
resiliency, and education. The Project is highly consistent with each of these goals. Hayden’s 
economy has historically relied on coal mining and power generation. With the impending closures 
of the Twenty Mile Coal Mine and Hayden Station threatening a substantial loss of local tax 
revenue, the Project offers a direct and timely solution. By repurposing existing transmission assets 
at Hayden Station, the Project not only bridges this fiscal gap but also preserves Hayden’s legacy 
as a regional energy hub. The Project also aligns directly with the Master Plan's infrastructure 
goals by supporting this transition to alternative energy and strengthening grid resiliency and 
reliability for Hayden and the region. Lastly, the Project will bolster local schools by providing 
significant tax revenue without a corresponding strain on public re sources. Crucially, the Project 
supports each of these Master Plan tenets while being sited on a property adjacent to similar uses 
(airport, gravel pit, Hayden Station) and  with a future non-residential development priority of 
“Industrial/Business/Light Industrial”.1

Summary 
Spruce Summit Resiliency LLC, on behalf of Jupiter Power LLC (Jupiter), is proposing to construct and operate the 
Spruce Summit Energy Storage Project (Project), a 400-megawatt (MW) scale battery energy storage system (BESS) 
within the Town of Hayden , Colorado (Town).  The Project would connect to the existing 138 -kV Xcel Mt. Harris 
Substation via a generation transmission line (gen-tie line) in unincorporated Routt County, Colorado (County). The 
Project would provide additional capacity to the electrical grid to assist with serving load during periods of peak 
demand by charging when demand is low and discharging when demand is high. Batteries are extremely fast-acting, 
capable of responding within fractions of a second to stabilize voltages and support a very high-quality and reliable 
electric grid. In addition to supporting reliability and power quality, these batteries also accommodate the future 
integration of intermittent renewable energy sources, such as wind and solar. This will assist the State of Colorado 
in meeting its energy procurement needs.  
This Project Description has been prepared to provide an overview of the facilities that are proposed to be 
constructed and operated as part of the Project. While all Project components are included in this Project 
Description, the BESS, collector substation, and beginning of the gen-tie line are located within Town jurisdiction, 
while only the gen-tie line is located within County jurisdiction. The Project site plan has been provided under 
separate cover.

1.2 Project Objectives 
Colorado’s electric grid is a complex system providing reliable power to Colorado residents. Per the goals 
established in Colorado’s Greenhouse Gas Emission Reduction Measures Act, the state relies increasingly on 
renewable sources of energy, such as solar, wind, geothermal, hydroelectricity, and biomass. Grid energy storage 
technologies provide  multiple services, such as energy management, backup power, load leveling, frequency 
regulation, voltage support, and grid stabilization. A host of energy storage facilities is necessary to provide these 
services to the grid effectively. As noted by the U.S. Department of Energy, “energy storage can reduce the need for 
major new transmission grid construction upgrades as well as augment the performance of exi sting transmission 
and distribution assets.” Furthermore, “energy storage will also play a significant role in emergency preparedness 
and increasing overall grid resilience” (USDOE 2013). 
Battery energy storage systems charge when there is an abundance of power available, and discharge the energy 
back to the grid when demand for power becomes high. The technology is advancing rapidly as a grid resource and 
will support system load -balancing when solar and wind resources are intermittently available. Power from solar 
and wind are often produced at different times of the day, which may not align with peak demand. Energy storage 
can be used to fill in the gaps when those resources may not be generating when power is needed.  
Due to Colorado’s clean energy goals and increasing energy deman ds in the region, Colorado utilities are actively 
soliciting battery energy storage systems like Spruce Summit to serve as load balancing  systems on their electric 
grids. In fact, the Colorado Public Utility Commission issued a decision on April 22, 2026 including the Project 
(referred to as Bid 118, a 400MW x 4-hour BESS) in the approved portfolio of resources in the PSCo 2025 Near-
Term Procurement RFP. A battery energy storage system at the Xcel Mt. Harris Substation would help to improve 
the overall reliability and resiliency of the Yampa Valley regional power grid. 
Construction of the Project would accomplish the following: 
▪ Enhance grid reliability in the region by providing a new and incremental capacity resource that can be 
utilized by Xcel to minimize reliance on the transmission system during periods of peak demand or 
transmission contingencies

3.1.2 Battery Energy Storage System Enclosures and Controller 
The BESS enclosure consists of cells, modules, and racks. Cells are the smallest component of the BESS enclosure 
and look like motorcycle batteries. These cells are lined up and inserted into modules, which are used to hold rows 
of cells and provide battery monitoring capabilities. Each module is then inserted into a rack, and multiple racks

make up the BESS enclosure. The enclosure structure s are hermetically sealed and temperature controlled via

The Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) includes two distinct levels of controls: the Battery Management System 
(BMS) and the Energy Management System (EMS). The BMS is responsible for monitoring and protecting individual 
battery modules, monitoring voltage, temperature, state of charge, and health to ensure safe operation at the cell 
and rack level. Separately, the EMS coordinates the operation of the full system, including inverters, the medium-
voltage system, and the connection to the electrical grid. It provides real-time control logic to optimize performance, 
ensure response to grid signals (e.g., frequency or voltage events), and implement site-wide shutdown protocols if 
needed. Together, these layered controls ensure both operational performance and safety, allowing the system to 
autonomously respond to grid emergencies while protecting critical components. 
3.1.3 Power Conversion System 
The Power Conversion System (PCS) would consist of an inverter, a medium -voltage transformer, protection 
equipment, direct current (DC) and alternating current (AC) fuses and circuit breakers, harmonic filters, equipment 
terminals, and a connection cabling system. The PCS may consist of standalone units connected to multiple BESS 
enclosures, or they can be integrated into the BESS enclosures themselves. The PCS convert s electrical energy 
from AC to DC when the energy is transferred from the grid to the battery enclosure, and from DC to AC when the 
energy is transferred from the battery enclosure to the grid. The energy conversion between AC and DC is achieved 
using a bi-directional inverter that connects the DC battery enclosure to the AC electrical grid. The PCS units also 
include a transformer that steps up and down  the voltage to maintain 34.5 kV AC at the PCS  inverter level to 
increase the overall efficiency of the BESS. Approximately 160 PCSs are currently estimated, but the final number 
will be determined during detailed design.

Temporary construction facilities would be necessary for construction of the gen-tie line and would include concrete 
pads at each new structure location and pull sites at each angle structure for stringing of the gen-tie line. Material 
storage for the gen-tie line would occur at the staging and laydown areas associated with the BESS component of 
the Project.  
3.5 Perimeter Fencing, Signage, Landscaping, Lighting, 
and Access 
The perimeter of the Project would be enclosed by an approximately 7-foot-tall chain-link security fence with 1-foot-
tall, barbed wire and light brown colored panels. Landscaping on the Project site would be augmented as needed to 
meet screening and Town of Hayden Code requirements. Low-elevation security lighting infrastructure would be 
installed throughout the Project site. The Project would be accessed via US 40, with primary access via County Road 
51, and secondary access from County Road 27. The Project site would include access from County Road 51 and 
County Road 51A. The gen-tie line ROW would be accessed from County Road 51 and County Road 51B.

3.7 Traffic 
Access to the Project site would be off County Road 51, County Road 51A and County Road 51B. Secondary access 
from County Road 27 may also be used. During project operations the site will require low-frequency visits from a 
small number of members from the operations and maintenance team in light utility trucks for routine maintenance 
checks. During construction, delivery of material and supplies would reach the site through on-road truck delivery. 
The majority of truck deliveries would be for battery enclosures, as well as any aggregate material that may be 
required for site preparation. The heaviest delivery loads to the site would consist of rock truck deliveries, concrete 
trucks, and the high voltage step-up transformers. Typically, the rock would be delivered in “bottom dump trucks” 
or “transfer trucks” with six axles. Low-bed transport trucks would transport construction equipment to the site as 
needed. The size of  the low-bed trucks (axles for  weight distribution) would depend on the equipment/materials 
transported. Construction traffic would be managed with a construction traffic control plan.

Zone Change – Review Criteria 
 
a. Correction of an error in an ordinance establishing the zoning for a specific property (if 
applicable).  The requested zone change corrects and clarifies the zoning status of the Project Site. 
Hayden had annexed t he Project Site with an “AC: Auto -Oriented Commercial Zone District” 
designation, but that zoning category is no longer recognized or contemplated in the current 
Hayden Development Code. The current map and code framework therefore create an ambiguity 
between the annexation -era designation and the zoning districts now available under the 
Development Code. The requested zone change resolves that ambiguity by placing the Project Site 
into a recognized district that better reflects the Project’s utility -scale energy infrastructure 
function and Hayden staff’s current direction that the Project be reviewed as an industrial use. 
b. Evidence of substantial compliance with the purposes of the Development Code. 
The requested zone change substantially complies with the purposes of the Development Code. 
The Project will place a utility -scale battery energy storage system on a parcel within Hayden’s 
municipal boundaries, where land use, infrastructure, access, publi c services, and operational 
impacts can be reviewed and conditioned through Hayden’s development review process. The 
Project promotes public health, safety, and welfare through modern fire protection, monitoring, 
access, stormwater, screening, lighting, and decommissioning measures , pollution-free provision 
of electricity and supports efficient public infrastructure by clustering a  grid-supporting use near 
existing energy infrastructure. 
The Project also supports the Development Code’s stated goals of implementing the Master Plan, 
protecting environmental quality, avoiding inefficient land use, providing orderly land 
development, and encouraging sustainable and resilient development design.  
c. Consistency with the  Master Plan.   The 2020 Hayden Master Plan sets out Hayden’s policy 
framework and growth strategy, placing significant emphasis on infrastructure, economic 
resiliency, and education. The Project is highly consistent with each of these goals. Hayden’s 
economy has historically relied on coal mining and power generation. With the impending closures 
of the Twenty Mile Coal Mine and Hayden Station threatening a substantial loss of local tax 
revenue, the Project offers a direct and timely solution. By repurposing existing transmission assets 
at Hayden Station, the Project not only bridges this fiscal gap but also preserves Hayden’s legacy 
as a regional energy hub. The Project also aligns directly with the Master Plan's infrastructure 
goals by supporting this transition to alternative energy and strengthening grid resiliency and 
reliability for Hayden and the region. Lastly, the Project will bolster local schools by providing 
significant tax revenue without a corresponding strain on public re sources. Crucially, the Project 
supports each of these Master Plan tenets while being sited on a property adjacent to similar uses 
(airport, gravel pit, Hayden Station) and  with a future non-residential development priority of 
“Industrial/Business/Light Industrial”.1
