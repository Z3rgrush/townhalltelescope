Below are the Procedural Rules of the Committee 
The governing body encourages citizen participation in local governance processes. To that end, and in 
compliance with the Kansas Open meetings Act (KSA 45-215), you are invited to participate in this 
meeting.The following rules have been established to facilitate the transaction of business during the 
meeting. Please take a moment to review these rules before the meeting begins. 
 
A. Audience Decorum. Members of the audience shall not engage in disorderly or boisterous conduct, 
including but not limited to; the utterance of loud, obnoxious, threatening, or abusive language; clapping; 
Page 1 of 17
cheering; whistling; stomping; or any other acts that disrupt, impede, or otherwise render the orderly 
conduct of the Committee of the Whole meeting unfeasible. Any member(s) of the audience engaging in 
such conduct shall, at the discretion of the City Council President (Chair) or a majority of the Council 
Members, be declared out of order and shall be subject to reprimand and/or removal from that meeting. 
Please turn all cellular telephones and other noise-making devices off or to "silent mode" before the 
meeting begins. 
 
B. Public Comment Request to Speak Form. The request form's purpose is to have a record for the 
City Clerk. Members of the public may address the Committee of the Whole during Public Comments 
and/or before consideration of any agenda item; however, no person shall address the Committee of the 
Whole without first being recognized by the Chair or Committee Chair. Any person wishing to speak at 
the beginning of an agenda topic, shall first complete a Request to Speak form and submit this form to 
the City Clerk before discussion begins on that topic. 
 
C. Purpose. The purpose of addressing the Committee of the Whole is to communicate formally with the 
governing body with a question or comment regarding matters that are on the Committee's agenda. 
 
D. Speaker Decorum. Each person addressing the Committee of the Whole, shall do so in an orderly, 
respectful, dignified manner and shall not engage in conduct or language that disturbs, or otherwise 
impedes the orderly conduct of the committee meeting. Any person, who so disrupts the meeting shall, at 
the discretion of the City Council President (Chair) or a majority of the Council Members, be declared out 
of order and shall be subject to reprimand and/or be subject to removal from that meeting. 
 
E. Time Limit. In the interest of fairness to other persons wishing to speak and to other individuals or 
groups having business before the Committee of the Whole, each speaker shall limit comments to two 
minutes per agenda item. If a large number of people wish to speak, this time may be shortened by the 
Chair so that the number of persons wishing to speak may be accommodated within the time available. 
 
F. Speak Only Once Per Agenda Item. Second opportunities for the public to speak on the same issue 
will not be permitted unless mandated by state or local law. No speaker will be allowed to yield part or all 
of his/her time to another, and no speaker will be credited with time requested but not used by another. 
 
G. Addressing the Committee of the Whole. Comment and testimony are to be directed to the Chair. 
Dialogue between and inquiries from citizens and individual Committee Members, members of staff, or 
the seated audience is not permitted. Only one speaker shall have the floor at one time. Before 
addressing Committee speakers shall state their full name, address and/or resident/non-resident group 
affiliation, if any, before delivering any remarks. 
 
H. Agendas and minutes can be accessed at www.roelandpark.org or by contacting the City Clerk

reunión. Por favor, silencie todos los dispositivos móviles y otros dispositivos que emitan ruido antes de 
que comience la reunión.

1. Pawnee Complete Street Analysis and Sidewalk Location Options  
 
Janelle Clayton from Lamp Rynearson reviewed the results of her study and what the Pawnee Street 
project would encompass. 
 
Greg Van Patten, also from Lamp Rynearson, reviewed the design considerations for Pawnee Drive for 
the sidewalks, curb replacement options, expected costs, as well as potential design conflicts. 
 
After receiving the reports from Lamp Rynearson, the City Council gave a thumbs up to proceed.  
 
Mayor Poppa asked for a reminder as to what is driving this project.  City Manager Moody said the 
pavement condition index for this street is a failed condition.  It is not a good candidate for either UBAS 
or a mill and overlay, so they are looking at a reconstruction.   
 
CMBR Hage asked if there is any way to navigate around the ash tree versus its removal.  Mr. Van 
Patten said they did look at both sides of the street for curb placement or leaving that portion of the 
sidewalk and the right-of-way would not be centered.  City Manager Moody said it is not likely they 
would shift the entire corridor west around the tree.  They have also contacted the property owner to 
let them know of the potential loss of the tree.   
 
CMBR Dickens said he is loath to take out a tree if that is the only tree in the yard.  City Manager 
Moody said there are two other mature trees in the yard.   
 
CMBR Faidley said she thinks that any ash tree is on borrowed time.  She also commented on the 
buffer zone and that is safer when adding a sidewalk and would be a huge factor even adding a 
sidewalk, so people do not have to walk in the street.   
 
CMBR Lero said he has received e-mail complaints about the condition of the street and curbs, but 
some are not thrilled about the addition of a sidewalk and did not think this was a problem that 
Page 5 of 17

needed to be addressed.  As this area is near a school, it is a place where sidewalks are important and 
the City wants to address that.  The time to do that is when they are redoing the street.  Having the 
green space between the curb and sidewalk does make it more comfortable to walk, and he would 
hate to remove a tree if it is in good condition. 
 
City Manager Moody said with all street projects they have to make a choice to keep things in balance.  
Putting the sidewalk on the east side provides the benefit of few pedestrian crossings.  And he asked if 
that benefit is of greater value than that of the tree.  The tree is infested now but is still healthy.  He

There was consensus to support a back of curb option for the sidewalk.

2. Review Results of On-Street Parking Survey - Get Direction on Next Steps from Council  
 
City Manager Moody said the survey is strongly conclusive in support for prohibiting parking on 
narrower streets.   
 
CMBR Hage said in reading a lot of the narrative responses, several comments were targeted at people 
living on the narrower streets.  She asked if they would have a neighborhood meeting to discuss this 
policy.  City Manager Moody said that would be prudent as they develop a policy to have a community 
forum or a neighborhood meeting prior to implementing a policy and is a good step for them to take.   
 
CMBR Faidley said it struck her that the first priority was safety of pedestrians, children, and cyclists at 
89 percent.  If they are to take that seriously, then ideally, they could not allow any on-street parking 
as it would then make the visibility of those road users more prominent.  She said she would support 
parking on one side of the street but there are dangers associated with on-street parking.  She said 
that the on-street parking option could be restricted more for the older portions of town and those 
with smaller driveways.

CMBR Lero said that most of their narrow streets do not have sidewalks and the residents have 
concerns about those streets.  He said they should look at the percentage of neighbors who are in 
favor of on-street parking.  They also need to consider the ramifications of where people are going to 
go and park if they do not have the driveway space and on-street parking is prohibited. 
 
Mayor Poppa said they can revisit at any time any policy they implement.  It could also be done on a 
street to street basis and would depend on multiple factors.  He would like staff to look at a policy 
using a similar process as their traffic calming policy.  City Manager Moody said that would be good for

Body members to come up with a framework. 
 
CMBR Dickens said he was in support restricting parking City-wide on narrow streets. 
 
CMBR Faidley said she would like to see making a sidewalk an option on those streets, which would be 
the majority of streets in the older parts of the City.  She asked them to consider whether there is a 
correlation between sidewalks and on-street parking. 
 
CMBR Hage said that a task force is a good idea.   
 
City Manager Moody said he would put a draft policy together to bring to the Governing Body and at 
this time there was agreement there would be no task force.

II.   DISCUSSION ITEMS    
1. Roe Blvd and 52nd Terrace Enhanced Stop Sign Visibility Analysis  
 
Public Works Director Ward-Bopp provided an aerial view of the intersection of Roe Boulevard and 
52nd Terrace, noting how the width of the road widens quite a bit in a short distance on the north side 
of the intersection.  Staff recommendation is to remove the current stop sign in the grass and place it 
on the roadway adding a stop bar and markings to make it clearer where vehicles need to stop.  The 
goal is to prevent vehicle and pedestrian conflicts.  The City would like to make improvements in the 
fall at a cost of less than $3,000.  Also, when it is time to redo this street in the future, they would fix 
the geometry of the intersection.   
 
Mayor Poppa asked for clarification on the construction and realignment of the street curb.  Public 
Works Director Ward-Bopp said it would be done when it is the correct time to do the construction on 
the street.  
 
CMBR Faidley agreed that the recommended changes will improve safety.  She asked them to take it a 
step further with the installation of a crosswalk.  She said this intersection is the dividing line of 
residential to the south and the businesses on the north, and a crosswalk might add more safety.   
 
Public Works Director Ward-Bopp said a crosswalk was his initial recommendation.  The City Traffic 
Engineer stated that if they took that approach, they would need to put in a crosswalk for the entire 
eastern side of Roe Boulevard. 
 
City Manager Moody added that where there are traffic signals there are crosswalks.   
 
CMBR Faidley said they are still dealing with the complexity of removing the traffic light.  She said the 
Bike Ped plan is scheduled for review in 2027 and would like to have this area looked at again as this is 
too wide of an opening for a street that busy.

Public Works Director Ward-Bopp said there is an existing challenge for pedestrians to go from east to 
west.  He said he is excited to work with the consultants if this item is funded so they can move 
forward with the review.   
 
CMBR Lero has also heard from a constituent about a desire to have a crosswalk.  He personally has 
walked that stretch quite a bit and noted vehicles are moving fast through the intersection and not 
looking for pedestrians.   
 
Public Works Director Ward-Bopp said there is nothing that would prevent them from doing a 
crosswalk as well.  
 
CMBR Hage asked what the downside is of adding a crosswalk.  Public Works Director Ward-Bopp said 
the initial cost is one thing and ongoing maintenance is another.  If they put the crosswalk here on a 
non-signalized intersection, then potentially it is setting a precedent that they are put throughout the 
City and would then have an impact cost-wise.   
 
City Manager Moody said adding this one crosswalk would have a small impact but would be a greater 
expense if implemented City-wide.   
 
CMBR Hage supported measures that make it safer to cross an intersection.  She said that Roe is a 
public thoroughfare and so is 52nd Terrace as these are routes to all the schools in the City.  She would 
like to see them be more proactive instead of waiting for something to happen.  She also mentioned 
the increase in e-bikes and speed of traffic.   
 
Public Works Director Ward-Bopp said they currently install crosswalks tactically on multi-purpose 
paths and intersections.  If it is an arterial route, they would want a crosswalk.  The thought is that by 
having crosswalks everywhere, they lose their effectiveness to the driver, which is a potential 
downside.   
 
CMBR Morales asked if they could draft guidelines for where to put crosswalks and what streets would 
merit a crosswalk.  He said that many might be too comfortable with a plain crosswalk, but they could 
add lights as well.   
 
City Manager Moody said they look to their traffic engineer, Janelle Clayton, who in turn looks at 
resources available to her like the MUTCD which is a standard.  Sometimes the answer isn’t always 
clear.  Ms. Clayton’s recommendation on this is not for lack of a standard, but she looks at pedestrian 
patterns, and most crosswalks are placed in areas going to a specific destination.  He said they can 
make an argument that this is approaching a commercial area and would warrant it.  They are not 
experts, but they try to provide enough insight and information to help the Governing Body make the 
best decision they can.   
 
CMBR Lero said they don’t want to set a precedent for the City but did say the speeds on Roe are 
unique to traffic in general in the City and they should take that into consideration.   
 
Police Chief Honas said if this recommendation is approved, his department will be monitoring the 
intersection heavily to make sure what has been put in place is working.  He has personally monitored 
the intersection and will have his officers keeping an eye on this after the project is complete.  If they 
see an issue, they will bring it to the City’s attention.

Title: Direction on Buena Vista Traffic Calming Plan Design (5 min) 
Item Type: Presentation 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff is seeking direction on a design modification for the elevated pedestrian crossing 
at 50th and Buena Vista.  
 
Details: 
Construction of the raised pedestrian crossing on Buena Vista at 50th Street is not 
possible due to the slope of Buena Vista being too great. The raised crossing could be 
shifted north, but it would not align with the sidewalks along 50th Street.  The raised 
crosswalk also creates a skew in the ped crossing on the east side of the intersection, 
which also impacts the site triangle for those stopping on westbound 50th Street at 
Buena Vista. The traffic engineers recommend installing a speed hump with cushions to 
the south of 50th Street as an alternative. This will slow the traffic northbound as it 
approaches 50th Street, which is heading downhill. The southbound traffic is climbing 
the hill as it approaches 50th Street. This is the principal reason for recommending 
placing a speed cushion on the south vs the north side of the intersection. 
 
The attached presentation includes a plan showing the distance between the stop signs 
and the traffic-calming elements as proposed. 
 
This is a relatively minor modification, but because the design of the traffic-calming has 
involved significant neighbor engagement, we want to get the Council's direction.
