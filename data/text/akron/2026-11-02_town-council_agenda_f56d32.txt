6.a Greg Etl- Department of Local Affairs  
Greg Etl of the Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) discussed the Town's open contracts, particularly Energy 
Impact grant no. 9540 funding the town manager position. He explained that the grant's work description 
requires the Town to provide a plan for maintaining the position when the grant ends. The grant is written to 
expire in June, and funding shifts over three years from 75 percent grant, to 50/50, to 25 percent grant. He said 
4
the requirement is about four years old and was created because some communities eliminated the position once 
grant funding ran out. 
Mr. Etl said the Town should not simply move the duties onto an existing employe e who already has another 
job. An internal candidate who is willing to complete the necessary training could potentially be acceptable, and 
DOLA would decide after conferring with colleagues. He said the Town is not required to use a recruiting firm, 
though there are benefits to doing so, and that average tenure for a town manager is about 2.5 years.  
Mayor Bowin said the Council needed more time to review the contract and would address the succession plan 
as quickly as possible. Trustee Braden Brent asked for DOLA's expectations of an appropriate plan and about 
consultant requirements. 
6.b Water Pollution Control Revolving Fund (WPCRF) public hearing for 
the sanitary sewer collection system replacement project- Dannah 
Koeniger, GMS  
Mayor Bowin closed the water hearing and opened the wastewater hearing. The GMS representative presented 
background on the collection system. It was originally constructed in the 1930s and is largely vitrified clay pipe. 
The treatment facility is a three-cell aerated lagoon with wetlands, serving about 950 EQRs. A three-year 
system-wide video inspection found root intrusion, breaks, and joint separations that cause significant 
infiltration. It also found a cast iron line under the railroad with severe tuberculation and negative slo pe. 
Priority 1 is the replacement and rerouting of the deep, diagonal trunk line, which has negative -grade segments, 
including a new crossing under the railroad. The abandoned pipe would be sealed and left in place. Priority 2 
addresses the worst remaining segments identified in the video inspection. A DOLA CDBG grant of roughly 
$700,000 was reported as already awarded. Additional funding would come from principal forgiveness and a 
loan through the CDPHE WPCRF. The presenter estimated a needed average month ly sewer bill of about $55, a 
$13 increase. In the worst-case schedule, loan approval would come in March, loan execution in June, and 
advertising at the end of summer 2027. Environmental impacts were described as short -term construction 
impacts, with best management practices to be used. 
Mr. Etl added that DOLA's unwritten CDBG maximum is about $600,000 and that the timing of the next CDBG 
cycle was uncertain. The hearing was closed. 
6.c Drinking Water Revolving Fund (DWRF) public hearing for the water 
system improvements project- Dannah Koeniger, GMS  
Mayor Bowin opened the public hearing. The GMS representative reviewed the water system, which has been in 
service since 1915. It has about 10 active supply wells, disinfection-only treatment, one storage tank, and a 
closed, pump-fed distribution system of cast iron, PVC, and asbestos cement pipe. Arsenic, nitrate, uranium, and 
gross alpha are at or near maximum contaminant levels in various wells, and the Town has historically blended 
well water to meet standards. 
Priority 1 is an anion exchange treatment plant that can treat all or part of the flow. Priority 2 includes:  
● recoating the existing storage tank, which was built in the 1990s; 
● a new secondary storage tank; 
● replacement of well master meters; 
● a portable generator for backup well power; and 
● pump station energy-efficiency upgrades. 
The needed rate increase was estimated at $13 to $18 per month. The schedule calls for a loan application by 
January 5, funding commitment in March, loan execution in summer, and construction beginning late the 
following year. The presenter said no significant environmental impacts were identified.  
In discussion, the presenter said a welded steel tank can last 50 to 70 years, and that the tank is structurally 
sound but needs recoating. Mr. Etl advised the Town to apply for the full loan amount, which can be reduced if 
grants are received but cannot be increased later. He said DOLA expects applicants to have rates at or above the 
statewide average for similar communities, currently $60.61 for water and $44.65 for sewer, and that the Town's 
rates are too low today. He also said DOLA expects to hold only one grant cycle next year and that roughly 
$500,000 is likely the maximum award. Mayor Pro Tem Jefferson said the Town has taken do uble-digit 
5
increases in recent years and that budget discussions would begin within a couple of weeks. The hearing was 
closed.

6.e State of Colorado Free Recycling Program-Dennis Wise  
Dennis Wise of Recyclops presented on Colorado's new Extended Producer Responsibility program, under 
which manufacturers fund curbside recycling for all residents. Recyclops has been appointed to serve the region 
and uses independent contract drivers with their own vehicles. The program is free to the Town and to residents, 
and the company is paid only by the state. Service was hoped to begin October 1, though the state had not yet 
finalized materials or the designated material recovery facility. 
Mr. Wise described the process: 
● Residents would first receive printed bags and later carts, and would set recyclables out for pickup. 
Cardboard would be placed beside the bags. 
● Collections would be broken into quadrants and held on alternating weeks.  
● Recyclables would be aggregated in leased enclosed space, such as storage units, before being hauled 
to a state-designated facility. 
● Service is residential only. 
● Residents can opt out by phone or website, and contaminated bags would not be collected.  
The company asked the Town to help with outreach through its website, social media, and utility billing. It also 
asked for the Town's collection routes, a street address list, and a map. 
Attorney Richardson stated he saw no statutory need for a franchise agreement, since the company would not 
use Town rights-of-way, though an operational coordination agreement could be considered. Trustee Br ent 
raised concerns about odor and zoning for aggregation sites and asked about alley pickup. Mr. Wise said 
industrial zoning would likely be needed and that alleys are acceptable if kept separate from trash. Mayor Pro 
Tem Jefferson offered temporary use of Town shop property while the company finds longer-term space.

8.d Discussion Regarding The Proposed Agreement With the 
Washington County Housing Authority  
Attorney Richardson explained that the draft MOU would let the Housing Authority receive municipal water, 
and possibly sewer, before annexation, with terms for future annexation. The Town's policy requires property to 
be within the Town to receive municipal utilities, and the Housing Authority's grant timeline does not allow 
annexation before year-end. The Housing Authority representative said the group wanted an answer on eight 
water taps and had not seen the draft until recently. She said they needed a sewer cost estimate and a layout of 
the homes before moving forward. 
Council discussion covered the following points: 
● Fire hydrants: The Council agreed that hydrant placement should reference the fire code rather than 
specify details. 
● Zoning: Trustee Brent said no existing Town zone fits the project without sewer. Mayor Pro Tem 
Jefferson said that with sewer service it would fit the Class B residential zone. 
● Septic: The Housing Authority said it had been told three homes could share one septic system. Mayor 
Pro Tem Jefferson objected that shared septic would create problems if the homes were later divided 
and sold. 
● Sewer connection: A manhole south of the railroad is roughly a quarter mile from the planned homes. 
The earlier $200,000 estimate assumed a 25-foot depth, while the MOU requires 4 feet of cover. 
Trustee Brent said a bid should be obtained before decisions are made. Mayor Pro Tem Je fferson asked 
that connection to Town sewer be required, to be reconsidered if costs prove prohibitive.  
● Cost relief: Trustee Brent said he favored selling taps at cost and waiving building permit fees. Several 
members agreed to include this in the MOU. The Housing Authority also asked that the MOU address 
a planned eight-home expansion and the proposed credit for the Town's investment. 
No formal action was taken. Attorney Richardson will revise the MOU to provide that sewer and water taps are 
sold at cost and that any permit fees are waived, and will send it to the Housing Authority the next morning. 
Trustee Brent will work with the Housing Authority on a sewer bid and the manhole location. The Town's 
zoning information will be sent to the Housing Authority, and communication will go through Attorney 
Richardson with Mayor Bowin copied.

9.a Lindsie Grass-Special Animal Permit  
Ms. Grass had requested a variance to keep a fourth dog, a service animal. She reported that neighbors rai sed no 
objections. She stated that the dog is trained to assist with PTSD and dissociative episodes, and cited C.R.S. 24 -
34-803(5), which exempts owners of service animals from local licensing fees. She said she had been charged a 
license fee for three years. 
Attorney Richardson said the Town code does not exclude service animals from the limit on the number of dogs 
and that he was not aware of a state statute that does. He also advised that the Town must follow the statute 
regardless of the code on fees. Trustee Brent urged caution about potential liability. Mayor Bowin recommended 
granting the variance and waiving the fee now and bringing the code into compliance later.  
Motion to approve Ms. Grass's special animal permit for a fourth dog and waive the spec ial permit fee 
was made by Trustee Jefferson and seconded by Terry Alexander. The motion carried 6 -0. 
Trustee Jefferson clarified that the motion covered only the permit fee for these four dogs. The annual 
registration fee for the service animal would be addressed through a code amendment. Attorney Richardson 
agreed to research whether service animals may be counted toward the limit and what proof the Town may 
request, and to prepare a memo and ordinance for the October meeting.

10.a Acting Town Manager Report 08/26  
Senior Center. The Acting Town Manager reported that the Senior Center board dissolved for lack of members 
and that the Town has taken over operations. The Senior Center's part-time employee, whose salary is paid by 
the Town through a mill levy, asked the Town to pay rent for the Town-owned building. Utilities and other bills 
are still being paid from the dissolved nonprofit's accounts. Attorney Richardson said the documents he 
reviewed were only dissolution filings and meeting minutes, and that a formal assignment of assets to the Town 
would be needed. He said Town employees should not use the nonprofit's accounts. Council members raised 
concerns about the nonprofit's EIN and tax filings and agreed the utilities should be moved to the Town. 
Cemetery Fence. Two quotes were received: $32,903 for a five-foot fence and $39,984 for a six-foot powder-
coated iron fence. The Council indicated support for the six-foot fence, to be funded through the trust. 
Accelerator Grant. The state allows the Town to reduce the required policy work to one or two measures, and 
work on the energy-efficiency portion is on hold until an RFP is completed. The options were waste measure 1 
(waste collection routes), transportation measure 1 (active transportation infrastructure), and land use measure 1 
(accessory dwelling units and attached homes). Staff said waste measure 1 was listed first because it was the 
most easily accomplished with current staffing. Mayor Bowin said it could be implemented most quickly and 
would most affect the budget. Mayor Pro Tem Jefferson said he preferred that ADUs be part of a larger zoning 
effort. Trustee Brent said he favored land use measure 1 and questioned the value of the waste m easure, but 
would accept the Council's decision. No formal vote was taken. 
Town Manager Recruitment. Mayor Pro Tem Jefferson asked to contact the recruiting firm used previously, 
noting a recruitment could take 90 days or longer. Mayor Bowin said he wanted to review the DOLA contract 
first and noted that a new Town Clerk would relieve some workload. Staff said they were stretched thin. Trustee 
Ron said he did not see a need to proceed now. Trustee Brent said failing to act could damage the Town's 
standing with DOLA and that the Town should be planning ahead, citing roughly $4.5 million in grants brought 
in over 2.5 years. Mayor Bowin, who previously served as combined clerk -treasurer and administrator, said that 
role handled grant writing and reporting without added staff, and he questioned whether the Town can afford all 
it wishes to do. Mayor Pro Tem Jefferson said that Akron is behind on policy and infrastructure and needs more 
staff capacity. The Council agreed to place the topic on the agenda of a special meeting, expected in about two 
weeks, so members could first review the grant contract.

AIRPORT DEVELOPMENT AND ADVISORY COMMITTEE  
MEETING OCTOBER 28, 2026 
2:00 P.M. TOWN HALL 
 
 
I. MEETING DETAILS 
A meeting of the Airport Development and Advisory Committee (ADAC) was 
held October 28, 2026 at 2:00 p.m. at Town Hall. 
 
II. ATTENDEES 
Present: Annette Bowin,  Linda Hayes, John Horn,  Ron Kraich, Kim Melani, 
Braden Piel, Brandy Ward and  John Wright. Braden Brent arrived mid-
meeting. 
 
III. APPROVAL OF PREVIOUS MINUTES 
The meeting was called to order by Linda Hayes.  The minutes from the 
September 14, 2026 meeting had been read by all members.  A motion was 
made by Annette Bowin to approve the minutes as presented.   Ron Kraich 
seconded the motion and all votes were in favor. 
 
IV. UPDATE ON TERMINAL PROJECT 
Braden Piel reported that TCC were planning to finish the utility hookup this 
week, have more concrete work done next week and begin framing the 
following week. 
Financially, the project has had two bills presented from TCC on Earth 
Engineering.  They have been paid and we are awaiting reimbursement from 
CDOT.  We have both CDOT and DOLA funds available.   
 
V. UPDATE ON 2027 RUNWAY PROJECT 
Nothing new since last communication. 
VI. AIRPORT MANAGER/FBO 
Sale of old Fuel Trucks. Invitation to bid for old fuel trucks closes today at 
5:00 P.M.  One interested party came to look at trucks.  Trucks were started, 
batteries charged, etc.   The party has made a bid. 
 
Courtesy Car. The old courtesy car was discussed and it was decided that 
due to its age and condition, the Town board will be asked to retire the 
vehicle. 
 
19
Snow Plow.  It has been noted  previously that the new snow plow would  be 
parked between Hangar 15 and 16 during the winter months  and be granted 
access to battery charging from Hangar 15.   It is too big to fit in any available 
buildings, and needs to be accessible to runway. Piel would like a long-term 
CIP to include a building on the airport to house the plow and other airport 
equipment. 
 
NECO Flight School update. A caller from NECO requested they be allowed 
to place a trailer on the airport property in which to hold ground school 
classes, etc.   He wanted to bring the trailer that weekend.  Hayes advised 
him we would need to return to the ADAC a completed application before a 
decision could be made.   The application has not been returned to date.  
 
Budget.  Hayes requested to be included in the budget process for the airport 
so that potential long and short-term expenses can be budgeted.  There will 
need to be some fuel farm maintenance, lighting replaced, and ground 
maintenance.  Airport roads/streets would be another expense to consider, 
especially now that the terminal will have access from Road 42.   
 
Approach to Hangars.  A discussion was held regarding the responsibility of 
tenant verus Town to pay for the approach to hangars.  The current 
understanding is that tenants would have to pay for the approach within their 
lot lease, the Town would develop the area up to the lot. 
This led to another question about where the approach could intersect with 
the parallel taxiway.   Hayes had contacted Mike Beckhoff, PE with LJA,  
airport engineers for CPRA, and he had said there is no FAA requirement 
about placement. It would be permissible for a tenant to build an approach all 
the way to the taxiway if it was economically feasible. 
 
Economic Development.  Hayes asked if there could be some money 
budgeted for economic development. The county no longer has an employee 
devoted to economic development.  Several members of the committee were 
unaware of the Town’s and ADAC’s previous attempts to promote the airport.  
Horn advised of how Akron was able to promote CPRA at the NBAA 
convention for several years by volunteering to set up booths and earning 
Akron’s share of the state booth.   The NREE was discussed as one way the 
airport was promoted for nine years.  There have been numerous other 
attempts and some of these were discussed, to inform the current Board.  
Hayes feels there should be some money committed to economic 
development and would like to see the ADAC be involved.  Wright noted that 
the community has expressed a desire for economic development. As the 
community’s largest capital asset, the airport is a logical focal point for 
economic growth. 
 
        
VII. NWS  TEAGUE WILLIAMS AND DAVID PASCHAL _ PHONE CALL 
20
There is weather equipment housed in the current terminal building.  This is 
an FAA site maintained by the NWS.  
 
A. NWS would like to know what the plans are for the old terminal after the 
new terminal is built and occupied. The committee told them the plan is to 
abandon the old building.  With this information NWS said they would 
need to start a request to move equipment.  They need GPS coordinates, 
and a survey for elevations so pressure sensors are correct. They will 
need to get engineers involved, cabling, and antennas.   It will probably 
take a year.   
B. There was no plan for the weather service equipment to be placed in the 
new terminal.  The equipment is in a case roughly the size of a large 
refrigerator.  The committee asked if the equipment needs to be climate 
controlled and were told yes.  Hayes will send drawings of the new 
terminal to them.  Possible places to locate the equipment were 
discussed. 
C. They would need to move the Visual Display unit (VDU) to the new 
terminal.  It is real-time weather reporting.  Most airports have a VDU and 
since we are the only one in this area with the service, they would like us 
to have it. There is a possibility of putting a real time display on a TV in the 
new lobby.  Committee advised them we will not be getting fiber optic to 
the new terminal.   
D. Williams and Paschal asked Hayes to send an email for the purpose of  
starting a relocation request and a point-to-point service request  to 
bou.eltechs@noaa.gov.  Include them and any one else who has need to 
be involved.  It will require high level FAA and NWS approval. 
 
VIII. ADAC DISCUSSION 
The composition and membership size of ADAC were discussed. Several 
participants expressed concern that a nine-member committee was too large. 
After considering the arguments in favor of the existing size, it was decided to 
retain the committee’s nine-member composition. 
 
The Airport Development Committee was formed by resolution dated June 6, 
2011 and a second resolution was adopted in 2014 to add ‘Advisory’ to the 
name, amend the purpose, and state the composition of the nine member 
committee.  Since the signed resolutions cannot be found, some members of 
the committee felt it would be advisable to affirm the resolutions so they are 
on record.  After discussion, a motion was made by John Horn to affirm the 
resolution of 2014, if the signed resolution cannot be found by the next Town 
21
Council meeting date.  Motion was seconded by Annette Bowin and all votes 
were in favor. 
 
IX. ADJOURNMENT 
There being no further business, Annette Bowin moved to adjourn the 
meeting.  The motion was seconded by John Horn  Meeting was adjourned at 
4:10 P.M.
