budget.  These changes included: 
• Adjustments for the Federico//Duerst Agreement

sales tax distribution for August was $241,513.09 which is well above the budgeted average of 
$215,416.67.  The July Cash Flow report shows expenses are slightly below budget while revenues 
are slightly above budget.

Definition
of Fund Accounting Terms
Net Assets 
–
Total Assets minus Total Liabilities. Net Assets fall into one of two categories:
1.
With Donor Restriction
–
funds that may be spent only as restricted by the donor.
2.
Without Donor Restrictions
–
funds available for general operations. These may be further 
separated as follows:
a.
Net Investment in Fixed Assets
-
total fixed assets minus accumulated depreciation 
minus any loans related to fixed assets.
b.
Board Designated 
-
although not donor restricted, these amounts have been segregated 
by the board (or finance committee, elders, etc.) for special purposes.
c.
Prior Years’ Net Balance 
-
cumulative net activity (revenue minus expenses) from all 
prior years.
d.
Current Year Net Balance 
-
current year net activity (detailed on the Statement of 
Activities).

August 2026 Site Director Report 
 
Fall programming/Marketing 
The Fairway newsletter has hit mailboxes for fall, and I have given Cindy all of the information for the 
website and email blasts. I gave all programs/events to the new KHS marketing coordinator, and they 
will advertise our events state-wide.  I have also created 4 fliers, and all are available in the lobby of the 
East building. All our registration-based programs are live on the Fairway Parks and Rec page and most 
already have registered participants. I have also created facebook events for all programs and have 
posts scheduled for the next month.  Lastly, The Civil War Roundtable has also requested our event 
info and will start advertising to their membership.   
 
Trespassers Beware 
We are gearing up for the traveling exhibit, and I believe this will be great exposure and publicity for 
the site throughout the fall.  Chief Judith was our first fall speaker on Saturday, Aug 22 and although 
the KCK extended their location dates, it is a good preview to the exhibit and topic and people will have 
all fall to return to view the Fort. Attendance was great and the program was well received. I was 
contacted by KCTV prior, and with her approval, they attended the event and interviewed her after.  Ed 
and Cindy have been given the exhibit contact and it is set it to be delivered on Friday, August 28.  
Brice, Neysa (exhibit creator) and myself met on Tues, Aug 25 to finalize logistics before delivery.  The 
Fort is set to be on site until November 14 and the trees are delivered the following week.  
 
I was contacted by the marketing coordinator of PBS and they want to partner with us this fall, as a 
documentary on the Conley Sisters will be released in November.  They may have a presence near the 
Fort during Fall Festival, and we are currently working on some ideas for collaboration.  
 
Chief Judith and I have scheduled a special private reception and exhibit viewing for Tuesday, 
September 22 at 5:30. Invitations will go out to board members, docents, regular volunteers, SIMHS, 
Fairway elected officials and donors.   
 
West Building 
No news from KHS regarding the progress of the building survey and when projects will begin, but we 
are nearly one year into the 3 year grant.   
 
North Building Windows  
All south windows have been removed, repaired and restored.  The north side, second floor were all 
removed at the same time and boards were placed in their spaces while the windows were taken off 
site for repair.  A lift was a on-site for a week but has been returned.  The first floor, north windows will 
start the week of August 24.  This project was much needed and looks great.   
 
Fibers Instructor 
Our new instructor was approved by the Fairway City Council at the August meeting and 2 workshops 
and 1 class is currently live in our reservation system.  They will be using the northwest classroom on 
the second floor of the North building for all fibers classes, and I am currently purchasing from a supply 
list for items to be available to registrants.   
 
 
Internships 
Audrey and Alex have both completed their summer internships, and Reagan (Mid-America Nazarene) 
wants to continue this fall.  All of her summer work was for John and the library, and this fall we will be 
working on creating some QR codes around the site for visitors to get more information during their 
visit on the history, the land, the people and the importance of this site.   
 
 Docent Training   
The Docent training will take place on Friday, September 18 at 10am.  It will be open to previous 
docents, as well as those interested in the program.  It is being advertised within JCYM membership, 
and we will have email blasts and social media to get the word out to the community.  We will focus on 
Trespasser’s Beware, as we will add the exhibit into fall fieldtrips, as well as Fall Festival.  We will also 
present information on the Indian Removal Act, which ties into the forced relocation of the Wyandot 
into this area.   
 
Interpretative Sites Coalition  
The Mission hosted the quarterly meeting for 26 different sites around the city.  I am an officer for the  
organization, and it was great to have colleagues on site to share all of our growth and activity.   
 
Fieldtrips  
We are up to 9 booked field trips for 2026-2027 and we are only a week or two into teachers being 
back into their classrooms.  I have had 3 other schools reach out for information and date options, and 
I am confident the number will continue to grow.  
 
JCYM 
This month I received the funds raised from the Jump on the Trail event that I plan and implement with 
JCYM in June.  We raised $5700 (!) this year, earmarked for education at the Mission.  It is because of 
the funds from Jump on the Trail 2025 that we are able to pay for Fort Conley, as it covers 50% of the 
rental fee, and their annual grant is covering another $1000.  
They will kick off their new membership year in September and Ed and I have been invited to their 
Annual Banquet on the 22nd.  I am currently working on the slide program to present to their 
membership on the Mission and the impact of JCYM on the site.  The calendar for the site has been 
finalized for their aspects through June 2027 and they have added a new docent chair.   
 
Cemetery Program 
I am working with SIMSH to create a fun, interesting first-person program in the cemetery and 
registration is live for Saturday, October 24. Eight of us will present biographical information of 
“ourselves” and then have a discussion with John the moderator about our roles at the Mission.  Thank 
you to Jane for participating! We will be using clothing from the education room and are currently 
working on our scripts.

POLICE COMMITTEE, CHIEF THURLO 
 
Monthly Report 
 
Chief Thurlo referred to the report in the packet, subject to questions.  Last month 
there were 40 arrests and 417 citations issued.  The Department is aggressively 
enforcing traffic laws.  He outlined several incidents that occurred last month.  He 
also discussed scams and fraud that are being committed by individuals utilizing 
everything in their power to fool people, including municipalities, jails, police and 
sheriff’s departments.  He asked that residents be extra vigilant with emails and 
instead of just clicking the button to submit payment, he asked that residents 
actually talk to the individuals seeking payment before transferring funds to 
confirm it is legitimate.

NEW BUSINESS 
 
A. Consider Ordinance #1855 – An Ordinance Relating to the Regulation of Traffic 
Within the Corporate Limits of the City of Fairway, Kansas; Amending and 
Repealing Existing Section 10-50 of the Code of Ordinances, City of Fairway. 
 
City Administrator Nogelmeier explained this Ordinance is nearly identical to the 
Ordinance approved last month, except that there was an error in the times when 
early education dismisses.  The times have been corrected to 10:30 A.M. to 11:30 
A.M.   
 
Mayor Hepperly asked for discussion from the Council.  Hearing none, she asked 
for comments from the public.  Hearing none, she asked for a motion.  
 
Councilman Williams moved that the Council approve Ordinance #1855 – An 
Ordinance Relating to the Regulation of Traffic Within the Corporate Limits of 
the City of Fairway, Kansas; Amending and Repealing Existing Section 10-50 of 
the Code of Ordinances, City of Fairway.  Councilwoman Buszek seconded the 
motion and the motion carried unanimously.  
 
B. Consider Planning Commission Recommendation Regarding Sign Deviations at 
4210 Shawnee Mission Parkway and 4100 Shawnee Mission Parkway. 
 
City Administrator Nogelmeier explained that the Planning Commission is 
recommending approval of the sign deviations requested for the Fieldston.  The 
signs include waving banners, one large banner on the West side of the building, 
and a 4 x 8-foot billboard sign that will be installed by the Starbucks building 
(which will not affect line of sight).  The signage will be allowed for 18 months.   
 
Mayor Hepperly asked if there were questions for staff.  Hearing none, she asked 
for public comment on this item.  Hearing one, she asked for a motion.    
 
Councilman Story moved that the Council approve Planning Commission 
recommendation regarding sign deviations at 4210 Shawnee Mission Parkway 
and 4100 Shawnee Mission Parkway.  Councilwoman Buszek seconded the 
motion and the motion carried unanimously.

Councilwoman Buszek explained that the City has been trying to reduce the 30

understands that Kansas Statutes allow the City Council to decrease the maximum

PUBLIC COMMENT 
 
Mayor Hepperly asked if there were comments from the public.   
 
John Hale, 6018 Catalina, asked how long the 4 mills levy increase will last and 
whether there is anything restricting the timeframe for the increase.    
 
Mayor Hepperly responded that the mill levy increase has not yet been approved 
by the Council.  She does not know if it is possible to include a time frame for the 
mill levy increase, but she would check into that possibility.  
 
Mr. Hale is concerned that the City will continue to approve increases every year 
because of the adoption of the revenue neutral rate. Every year since the revenue 
neutral rate was passed, the City has decided to spend more than the revenue 
neutral rate.  Last year it was 9 percent.  This year it is about 20 or 25 percent 
over the revenue neutral rate.  He asked if the City did any long-range planning, 
why it did not see the increase coming, and whether there is a plan for it not to 
happen again.
