ATCT determined that returning the facility to the redeveloped rooftop would create a worse

The proposed monopine incorporates simulated bark t reatment, evergreen branch

With regards to why the telecommunication equipment  has to be higher than what the pre-
existing equipment on the structure was, the application materials state: 
 
The proposed 75-foot monopine replaces ATCT's existing antennas that were mounted on 
the building being removed as part of the property redevelopment. Although the former 
antennas were not located on a 75-foot tower, the b uilding itself provided substantial 
elevation above the surrounding ground. The former installation therefore benefited from 
the combined height of the building and the rooftop  antenna mounts. The proposed 
monopine begins at ground level and must independen tly replace both the elevation 
previously provided by the building and the elevation of the rooftop antenna installation. 
The proposed monopine base will be located approximately five feet below the architectural 
grade of the redeveloped property. This grade diffe rence effectively reduces the 
operational elevation of the proposed antennas rela tive to the new building and adjacent 
portions of the site. A portion of the requested to wer height is therefore necessary to 
compensate for the lower tower-base elevation. ATCT's RF engineer has determined that 
the proposed structure provides an approximately 60 - to 70-foot mounting envelope for 
ATCT's antennas, allowing the antennas to extend sl ightly above the surrounding tree 
canopy and reducing obstruction from trees, buildings, and terrain. 
 
The 75-foot dimension describes the overall height of the monopine concealment structure, 
not the height of every ATCT antenna. The upper str ucture is necessary to create the 
tapered crown, branching, and foliage coverage needed to conceal the antennas and make 
the facility resemble a mature evergreen tree. Reducing  the overall height without lowering 
the antennas would compress the upper branching and  concealment area and make the 
antennas more visible. Reducing both the structure and antenna height would place the 
antennas closer to the tree canopy and other obstructions, reducing coverage and service 
reliability. 
 
Antenna height affects wireless performance because  radio frequency signals are 
weakened, scattered, or blocked by terrain, buildin gs, trees, and other physical 
obstructions. Positioning the antennas slightly above the nearby tree canopy improves the 
signal path between the facility and wireless users. Locating antennas within or below the 
tree canopy would increase signal obstruction and c ould reduce the geographic area 
reliably served by the facility. 
 
Returning the antennas to the new building would not eliminate the need for elevation. To 
provide comparable service from the new building, t he antennas and associated 
concealment elements would need to extend above the new roofline. This would increase 
Page 13 of 79
9 
 
the apparent height and mass of the building and cr eate rooftop additions that would look 
out of place and would not conform to the intended architectural design of the 
redevelopment. The proposed monopine separates the telecommunications use from the 
new building and provides the necessary antenna ele vation within a structure designed 
specifically for concealment. 
 
The proposed height also preserves a lower mounting envelope, generally in the 50- to 60-
foot range, that may accommodate future colocation.  Providing this reserve space 
promotes infrastructure sharing and may reduce the need for another freestanding tower 
in the area. The RF engineer has also indicated that the freestanding configuration reduces 
potential RF-safety concerns on the new rooftop for  workers and other users if another 
provider is located there. 
 
The proposed 75-foot height is therefore justified because it: 
1. Replaces the combined elevation previously provi ded by the former building and 
rooftop mounts 
2. Compensates for the tower base being approximate ly five feet below 
architectural grade 
3. Positions ATCT's antennas within the 60- to 70-f oot mounting envelope 
identified by ATCT's RF engineer 
4. Allows the antennas to extend slightly above the  surrounding tree canopy 
5. Provides sufficient monopine crown, branching, a nd foliage above and around 
the antennas for effective concealment 
6. Avoids visually incompatible telecommunications extensions above the new 
building's roofline; and 
7. Preserves a lower mounting envelope for potentia l future colocation, thereby

1. Supplemental Height Justification 
Why the Proposed Facility Must Be 75 Feet When the Previous Rooftop Facility Was Lower 
Applicant Response: 
The proposed 75-foot monopine replaces AT&T's existing antennas that were mounted on the building being 
removed as part of the property redevelopment. Although the former antennas were not located on a 75-foot 
tower, the building itself provided substantial elevation above the surrounding ground. The former installation 
therefore benefited from the combined height of the building and the rooftop antenna mounts. The proposed 
monopine begins at ground level and must independently replace both the elevation previously provided by 
the building and the elevation of the rooftop antenna installation. 
The proposed monopine base will be located approximately five feet below the architectural grade of the 
redeveloped property. This grade difference effectively reduces the operational elevation of the proposed 
antennas relative to the new building and adjacent portions of the site. A portion of the requested tower height 
is therefore necessary to compensate for the lower tower-base elevation. AT&T's RF engineer has determined 
that the proposed structure provides an approximately 60- to 70-foot mounting envelope for AT&T's antennas, 
allowing the antennas to extend slightly above the surrounding tree canopy and reducing obstruction from 
trees, buildings, and terrain.  
The 75-foot dimension describes the overall height of the monopine concealment structure, not the height of 
every AT&T antenna. The upper structure is necessary to create the tapered crown, branching, and foliage 
coverage needed to conceal the antennas and make the facility resemble a mature evergreen tree. Reducing 
Page 28 of 79
AT&T Frisco NSB | Supplemental Application Information 
Supplement to July 2026 Special Use Permit Narrative 
the overall height without lowering the antennas would compress the upper branching and concealment area 
and make the antennas more visible. Reducing both the structure and antenna height would place the 
antennas closer to the tree canopy and other obstructions, reducing coverage and service reliability. 
Antenna height affects wireless performance because radio frequency signals are weakened, scattered, or 
blocked by terrain, buildings, trees, and other physical obstructions. Positioning the antennas slightly above 
the nearby tree canopy improves the signal path between the facility and wireless users. Locating antennas 
within or below the tree canopy would increase signal obstruction and could reduce the geographic area 
reliably served by the facility. 
Returning the antennas to the new building would not eliminate the need for elevation. To provide comparable 
service from the new building, the antennas and associated concealment elements would need to extend 
above the new roofline. This would increase the apparent height and mass of the building and create rooftop 
additions that would look out of place and would not conform to the intended architectural design of the 
redevelopment. The proposed monopine separates the telecommunications use from the new building and 
provides the necessary antenna elevation within a structure designed specifically for concealment. 
The proposed height also preserves a lower mounting envelope, generally in the 50- to 60-foot range, that may 
accommodate future colocation. Providing this reserve space promotes infrastructure sharing and may 
reduce the need for another freestanding tower in the area. The RF engineer has also indicated that the 
freestanding configuration reduces potential RF-safety concerns on the new rooftop for workers and other 
users if another provider is located there.  
The proposed 75-foot height is therefore justified because it: 
1. Replaces the combined elevation previously provided by the former building and rooftop mounts 
2. Compensates for the tower base being approximately five feet below architectural grade 
3. Positions AT&T's antennas within the 60- to 70-foot mounting envelope identified by AT&T's RF engineer 
4. Allows the antennas to extend slightly above the surrounding tree canopy 
5. Provides sufficient monopine crown, branching, and foliage above and around the antennas for effective 
concealment 
6. Avoids visually incompatible telecommunications extensions above the new building's roofline; and 
7. Preserves a lower mounting envelope for potential future colocation, thereby reducing the potential need 
for another tower 
Accordingly, the requested 75-foot monopine should not be viewed as an unexplained increase over the 
former rooftop facility. It is a ground-based replacement that must recreate the effective antenna elevation 
previously supplied by the building while also accommodating the structural and concealment components of 
a stealth monopine.

C (5)(g) Height, Bulk, and Zoning District Regulations 
Response: 
The proposed 75-foot height is the minimum height necessary to satisfy AT&T's RF coverage and capacity 
objectives at this replacement site. The monopine design reduces apparent bulk and visual contrast compared 
to a conventional exposed tower and avoids increasing the height and visual mass of the redeveloped building 
through rooftop extensions. Any zoning dimensional standard requiring interpretation or relief will be addressed 
through the Special Use Permit and associated plan review process. 
Height Justification for Proposed 75-Foot Monopine 
Response: 
Antenna height directly affects wireless service because radio frequency signals perform best when antennas 
are elevated above surrounding obstructions. Increased antenna elevation allows signals to propagate over 
terrain, buildings, vegetation, and other physical features. As antenna height is reduced, signals encounter 
more obstruction, resulting in reduced coverage area, weaker in-building service, increased shadowing, 
reduced network capacity, and increased potential for dropped calls or interrupted data sessions. 
AT&T evaluated lower antenna elevations and determined that heights below the proposed 75-foot monopine 
would not adequately satisfy the service objectives for the intended coverage area. Reducing the facility height 
Page 38 of 79
AT&T Frisco NSB | 75-foot Monopine Telecommunications Facility 
Special Use Permit Narrative | Town of Frisco UDC Section 180-5.2.11 
would place the antennas closer to existing obstructions and terrain features, resulting in diminished signal 
propagation and reduced service reliability. 
Multiple lower-height facilities would be required to provide comparable service performance if the proposed 
height were substantially reduced. Such an approach would result in additional telecommunications sites, 
equipment compounds, utility connections, access needs, and cumulative visual impacts. A single 75-foot 
monopine allows AT&T to maintain service from the established site area while minimizing the number of 
facilities required in the Town. 
The requested height also avoids the need to extend the redeveloped building's roofline to accommodate 
rooftop telecommunications equipment. A roof-mounted alternative trying to achieve similar antenna elevation 
would require visible rooftop extensions and screening that would make the building look visually out of place. 
The proposed monopine provides the necessary antenna elevation in a structure intentionally designed for 
concealment and visual compatibility. 
C (6) Co-Location 
C (6)(a) Demonstration That Co-Location Is Not Feasible 
Response: 
AT&T previously maintained a telecommunications facility on the property prior to redevelopment. The 
proposed monopine is not intended to establish service in a new area unrelated to AT&T's existing network; 
rather, it is intended to provide a permanent replacement solution for a location that has historically supported 
AT&T wireless service. 
Because AT&T had an existing telecommunications lease and an established rooftop facility at the property, the 
principal site evaluation question was how to replace the removed rooftop installation in a manner that 
maintains service while respecting the redevelopment. The analysis therefore focused on the redevelopment 
property and the feasibility of returning to the new building rather than treating the project as a new geographic 
search for an unrelated tower site. 
Reinstallation on the redeveloped building was evaluated and determined visually unsuitable because it would 
require rooftop antennas, concealment elements, screening walls, extensions, or similar appurtenances above 
the designed roofline. Those modifications would increase the apparent height and mass of the building, alter 
the intended architectural design, and make the building appear visually out of place. 
Relocating the facility to an existing tower or other structure elsewhere would not provide a like-for-like 
replacement for the established site and lease area and could fail to replicate the coverage contribution of the 
existing rooftop facility. It could also require additional facilities to address resulting coverage deficiencies. For 
this replacement project, the proposed monopine on or associated with the redevelopment property is the most 
appropriate and least intrusive means of maintaining service while avoiding visually incompatible rooftop 
modifications. 
Accordingly, AT&T submits that co-location on another existing structure is not a feasible or appropriate 
substitute for the proposed replacement facility, and that construction of the proposed monopine represents 
the best permanent replacement solution for the telecommunications facility being removed from the building.

characteristics of the monopine design.

GEOTECHNICAL ENGINEER/TESTING AGENCY.
