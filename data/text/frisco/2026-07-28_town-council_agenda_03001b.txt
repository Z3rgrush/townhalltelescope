FROM: LESLIE EDWARDS, FINANCE DIRECTOR 
RE:  MID-YEAR FINANCIAL & BUDGET UPDATE 
DATE: JULY 28, 2026 
 
 
  
Summary & Background: 
The purpose of this work session is to set the stage for the 2027 budget process by 
reviewing current 2026 financial conditions, discussing the broader economic outlook, 
and confirming Town Council’s priorities before Staff prepares the proposed 2027 
Budget. The overall message is one of cautious optimism. Frisco remains financially 
strong, but 2027 budget development will require careful planning because revenue 
growth is slowing, inflationary pressures remain, and some economically sensitive 
revenues are showing signs of stagnation. 
 
The 2026 budget year has been impacted by a difficult winter season, including low 
snow conditions, reduced visitation, and lower sales activity. Through May sales tax 
returns, overall sales activity was down approximately 7% compared to the prior year, 
and combined Town sales tax and County sales tax distributions were approximately 
$210,000 below budget year-to-date. We are also experiencing challenges at the Frisco 
Bay Marina related to low water and reduced visitation at that location. 
 
As of June 30, 2026, General Fund revenues were $242,507 below year-to-date 
budget, while expenditures were approximately $2.16 million below year-to-date budget. 
While this expenditure savings is helpful, some savings may be related to timing and 
may not represent ongoing budget capacity. 
 
Analysis: 
The work session will focus on the major factors that will shape the 2027 Budget. These 
include sales tax trends, inflation, consumer sentiment, construction activity, and tourism 
trends.  
 
Staff’s current forecast assumes Town sales tax will be down 2% in 2026, increase 1% in 
2027 over 2025, and increase 2% in 2028. Total overall General Fund revenue is currently

The presentation will also discuss economic challenges and opportunities facing Frisco.

FROM: NICO CRUZ, SUSTAINABILITY COORDINATOR 
RE:  WORK SESSION: WASTE REDUCTION STRATEGIES OVERVIEW 
DATE:        JULY 28, 2026 
 
  
Summary & Background: 
The purpose of this work session is to review the Town’s waste reduction strategies 
including, Pay As You Throw (PAYT), Universal Recycling, and other related programs.  
Since 2021, the Town has implemented sustainability efforts aimed at achieving net-zero 
greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. Key initiatives include the Climate Action and 
Resiliency Plan (CARP), waste reduction ordinances with regards to single -use plastics, 
disposable bags, and waste/recycling requirements . Additionally , the Town has  
implemented pilot programs such as the upcoming Main Street restaurant compost 
program and the Retrocycle Colorado Cup. 
Town Council adopted the PAYT and Universal Recycling requirements in 2022 after a 
2021 work session, stakeholder process, and public forums. PAYT implementation began 
in 2023. Universal Recycling was phased in during 2024 and reached its full compliance 
deadline in June 2025. In 2026, staff are implementing the glass recycling code update 
and preparing for statewide EPR implementation. 
All of this work supports Summit County’s goal of a 40% diversion rate by 2035. In 2025, 
the overall diversion rate was 23% and the municipal solid waste diversion rate was 32%. 
The Town therefore continues to make progress but remains below the regional target. 
At the work session, staff anticipates Council receiving a comprehensive understanding 
of the various waste reduction strategies that are outlined in Town Code along with 
understanding the customer recycling variance and waiver process. 
Staff specifically request Council feedback on the following: 
1. Is the waiver and variance process functioning as intended, including the current 
timelines? 
2. Are there compliance barriers staff should address differently, including barriers 
Council members have heard from constituents, and would additional incentives 
or technical assistance help?  
3. Are there additional waste reduction strategies Council would like staff to explore?

Waste Reduction Strategies 
 
 
Analysis: 
The programs outlined below can be found within the Town Code, Chapter 124, Article III 
Waste Reduction and Recycling. 
The Town’s waste reduction framework has three parts:  
1. Residential Pay As You Throw 
2. Commercial and Multifamily Universal Recycling  
3. Implementation and support through grants, education, technical assistance, 
waivers and variances, and Town-led infrastructure.  
 
Residential Pay As You Throw 
PAYT uses a waste utility model for residential units in which smaller trash containers 
cost less, and recycling access is included with service. This creates a financial incentive 
for residential customers to reduce landfill waste.  The smaller the bin utilized, the lower 
the bill for the consumer. 
Universal Recycling and Pay as You Throw provide environmental, operational, and 
community benefits, including: 
• Reduced litter and wildlife conflicts; 
• Improved recycling and compost diversion rates; 
• Enhanced downtown cleanliness and aesthetics; 
• Increased public education regarding waste diversion; and 
• Support for local sustainability and climate goals. 
 
For 2026, reports from three Frisco haulers indicate that approximately 96% of residential 
accounts had active recycling service, compared with 99% in 2025 and 93% in 2024. The 
decrease in 2026 may be explained by the 4% of reported accounts that declined 
recycling service. These figures are useful for identifying trends but do not represent an 
audited compliance or diversion rate. 
 
Container data as of April 30, 2026, show that Frisco has the largest share of small trash 
containers and the lowest share of large containers among the jurisdictions compared. 
This suggests the PAYT policy is working as intended by encouraging residents to choose 
smaller trash containers and reduce the amount of waste they set out for disposal. 
However, container size alone does not measure actual diversion.

Commercial and Multifamily Universal Recycling

Universal Recycling applies to commercial properties, multifamily homeowners 
associations, and mixed -use developments. It requires all commercial businesses and 
multi-family properties (such as HOAs) to provide on -site recycling collection services in 
a service capacity that must equal 50% of the property’s trash. Within this program, single-
stream recycling, food scraps, and glass all apply towards the 50% recycling minimum.  
Confusion and difficulty with this program have arisen with some property owners and 
managers having difficulty meeting the requirement of recycling/trash enclosures be ing 
provided within a fully screened, 4-walled, roofed enclosure which is through Chapter 180 
of the Town Code and not within the Chapter 124 which regulates Universal Recycling.  
The requirements for recycling enclosure design, and request for relief to code language 
as stated in Town Code, 180 -6.17 are brought directly to the Planning staff and there is 
an allowance for a retrofitting incentive for existing developments which may be allowed 
through administrative approval by the Director. 
The most common barriers are limited space, enclosure capacity, cost, timing, and HOA 
coordination. To assist local businesses and homeowners in meeting this requirement, 
the Town offers financial assistance to cover structural improvements, new bins, and 
signage for an amount up to $10,000 per application.  
As of July 2026, four properties have applied for funding assistance. Two projects have 
been completed, and the two remaining awarded projects are moving forward, with one 
preparing to solicit bids and the other awaiting permit issuance before beginning 
construction. 
Program milestones required 50% of commercial customers to have compliant recycling 
collection by June 2024 and 100% to have compliant containers and service by June 
2025. Hauler reporting indicates 63% commercial recycling access in 2026. Commercial 
access remains the clearest implementation gap and should be the primary focus for 
compliance, assistance and follow-up. 
Reported residential recycling access in Frisco was 93% in 2024, 99% in 2025, and 96% 
in 2026. Reported commercial recycling access was 57% in 2024, 78% in 2025, and 63% 
in 2026. Because reporting methods may vary by hauler and year, these figures are best 
used to identify general trends and potential gaps rather than as audited compliance 
rates.

complement PAYT and Universal Recycling by reducing waste at the source.  
Sustainability staff also continue to provide outreach and guidance to local businesses to 
support understanding of the requirements and improve compliance.

operations by allowing glass in single-stream recycling and removing outdated separate-
collection requirements. Summit County Resource Allocation Park staff and licensed

Waivers and Variances: Waivers and variances provide flexibility when full compliance is 
infeasible or additional time is needed. A variance may be applied for to temporarily 
modify specific recycling requirements. For example, a multi-family property may receive 
a variance to reduce the recycling container volume to 25% of trash capacity if they 
average over 70% short -term rentals. A waiver temporarily exempts a property or 
business from specific recycling requirements when compliance is not currently feasible. 
A waiver or variance is applied for through an online form on the Town’s website and 
applicants are to provide supporting documentation justifying the need for relief such as 
a lack of waste generation or unique property constraints. The application is the n 
reviewed by staff to determine if a variance or waiver is warranted.  
A variance and waiver are both permitted through Town Code for a maximum period of 
two years. If relief is still needed, a new request must be submitted which cannot exceed 
an additional six months.  
Recent cases show that the waiver and variance process is serving as a temporary tool 
to address site constraints, financial hardship, and coordination challenges while 
properties work toward compliance. Approved requests include milestone requirements, 
such as submitting site evaluations, project plans, or design updates, to demonstrate 
progress before the relief period ends. Seven properties have received temporary relief 
(Crossroads Townhomes Association, Peak One Condos, Ore House HOA, Teller 
Townhomes, Royal Glen Homeowners Association, Windwood Condominium 
Association, and Rocky Mountain Bible Church), generally through December 31, 2026. 
At that time, each property must either achieve compliance or request the one -time six-
month extension allowed by Town Code. 
Waivers for Snowshoe Motel and Eclectic Bar & Grill were denied after staff identified 
workable paths to on-site recycling.  
Staff is seeking guidance from Town Council specifically if they think that the timeline in 
the current Code language should be extended for compliance, or if two years plus a six-
month extension was adequate for properties to budget and plan ahead for required 
improvements to their waste and recycling disposal.  Staff would like to avoid creating 
“permanent waivers” for recycling service since they are intended to be temporary.

Extended Producer Responsibility : Colorado’s EPR program , through House Bill 22 -
1355, shifts funding responsibility for covered packaging and paper products to 
producers. In January 2026  mandatory registration and responsibility dues officially 
began for producers.  
The program is intended to expand recycling access, improve education, and reduce 
landfill disposal. Staff plans to enter into an MOU with High Country Conservation Center 
for EPR education and outreach. Other Summit County jurisdictions plan to do the same 
so public messaging is consistent across the county. Licensed haulers will coordinate 
with Circular Action Alliance Colorado. Staff can return with a more detailed presentation 
at a future meeting if Council would like additional information. 
Reuse: Retrocycle’s Colorado Cup program has launched in Frisco at Mountain Dweller 
Coffee Roasters and Rocky Mountain Coffee Roasters. The borrow-and-return drinkware 
program provides a visible local example of source reduction. 
Town Facilities and Infrastructure: Staff is reviewing Town-owned and managed facilities 
for consistency with Chapter 124, including paired bins, recycling access, signage, single-
use plastics, and event practices. Current efforts include the Main Street bin replacement 
project, a restaurant compost pilot, and construction and demolition diversion tracking for 
the 101 W. Main Street project. 
A shared enclosure for Hotel Frisco, Eclectic Bar & Grill is anticipated and the Town has 
a signed letter of intent and a target development year of 2027. Staff is also evaluating 
an enclosure at the Historic Park. These projects address a recurring barrier: limited 
space for wildlife-resistant trash and recycling infrastructure. 
As of July 2026, four Town -owned or managed locations —Public Works, the Museum, 
the Visitor Information Center/Community Center, and the Marina —need new or 
upgraded enclosures to fully meet the Universal Recycling or enclosure screening 
requirements in Tow n Code. Based on preliminary estimates of approximately $80,000 
per enclosure, bringing these facilities into compliance could require approximately 
$320,000 in capital investment. Staff will continue to confirm the scope, prioritize projects, 
refine cost estimates, and recommend funding through the 5-year Capital Plan.

Plan include expanding compost access, promoting sustainable consumption, evaluating
