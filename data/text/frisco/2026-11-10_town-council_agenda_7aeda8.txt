STAFF REPORT 
TO: MAYOR & TOWN COUNCIL 
FROM: JOSH SOUTHWORTH, PUBLIC WORKS DIRECTOR 
RE: I-70 Exit #203 AC Marriott/CDOT Real Property Transition 
Discussion 
DATE: NOVEMBER 10, 2026 
 
Summary & Background: 
The Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) has submitted three requests from 
AC Frisco for Town Council consideration. The requests are intended to facilitate a 
settlement related to parking modifications required on the AC Frisco property as part of 
the I-70 Exit 203 project. They concern snow storage, landscaping, and confirmation of 
access across an area where the Town would receive an easement. 
Staff is presenting the requests for Council discussion and direction without a 
recommendation. Council direction would guide further coordination with CDOT, AC 
Frisco, and the Town Attorney regarding whether to pursue the requests and what terms 
would need to be addressed. 
Analysis: 
CDOT describes the following requests from AC Frisco in connection with the proposed 
settlement. 
1. Proposed snow storage easement 
AC Frisco requests a snow storage easement over property to be conveyed to the Town 
as part of the project. The requested area consists of 20REV-EX1a (2,741 square feet) 
and RW-102REVa (21 square feet), totaling 2,762 square feet. These parcels would be 
conveyed separately from 20REV-EX1b, which consists of the street/right-of-way 
(ROW). CDOT indicates that this approach reflects prior coordination with Chris 
McGinnis concerning the Town's preference to hold the snow storage area as Town 
property separate from the ROW. 
The area is at the northwest corner of the project, northeast of the Alpine Inn property 
and northwest of the AC Frisco property. Proposed drainage improvements and a storm 
inlet are within the requested easement area. CDOT indicates that the layout leaves an 
approximately 10-foot buffer for a future sidewalk or path along the street. AC Frisco is 
aware that snow would need to be hauled or transported to the area and cannot be 
plowed across the roadway. The proposed approach in this area has been removed 
from the project. 
Page 4 of 265
Page 2 
2. Proposed landscape and snow storage easement 
AC Frisco requests a landscape and snow storage easement over property to be 
conveyed to the Town as part of the project and a small portion of existing Town ROW. 
The requested area consists of PE-1 (117 square feet) and PE-2 (15 square feet), 
totaling 132 square feet. The area is along the north side of Lusher Court, east of AC 
Frisco's existing primary entrance. The purpose is to avoid a gap in landscaping 
between the proposed back of sidewalk and the AC Frisco property line. PE-2 is within 
the existing Lusher Court ROW. 
3. Requested confirmation of access at PE-109 
AC Frisco requests formal confirmation that the Town will not block the proposed new 
access to its property at the west perimeter of PE-109. PE-109 would be granted to the 
Town for snow removal, snow storage, utilities, and lighting. The access exhibit shows 
that the proposed access overlaps the easement area. The request therefore concerns 
how the Town's use of PE-109 would coexist with access to the AC Frisco property; the 
wording and scope of any Town confirmation remain to be developed. 
Considerations for Council 
Council consideration involves the compatibility of these requests with Town snow 
operations, drainage maintenance, and future sidewalk or path improvements. For PE-
109, a central issue is how an access assurance would affect the Town's ability to use 
and maintain its easement. Further agreement discussions could address maintenance 
and restoration responsibilities, costs, liability, and the Town's ability to access or 
relocate improvements when public needs change. The attached exhibits depict the 
proposed locations and areas; they do not establish these agreement terms. 
Council may provide direction on all or individual requests and identify any conditions or 
additional information needed before considering final documents. 
Financial Impact: 
No appropriation is requested with this item. CDOT's proposal does not identify 
compensation for the requested rights or a complete allocation of costs for snow 
hauling, landscaping, maintenance, restoration, or agreement preparation and 
recording. Any financial impact to the Town would depend on the terms developed 
following Council direction. 
Alignment with Strategic Plan: 
This discussion relates to the Strategic Plan objectives of providing quality core services 
and supporting a thriving economy. Council consideration involves accommodating 
project-related impacts to a local business while evaluating the Town's ability to maintain 
public infrastructure, conduct winter operations, and preserve space for future 
pedestrian connections. 
Sustainability: 
Environmental considerations include snowmelt drainage, access to the proposed storm 
inlet, and the maintenance of landscaping next to the sidewalk. The snow storage 
request would require hauling or transporting snow to the site. The proposed buffer also 
preserves space for a future sidewalk or path. Operational and maintenance terms 
would influence these effects.

2026 
DATE: NOVEMBER 10, 2026 
 
  
Overview:  
 
September is the busiest wedding month, with peak fall colors serving as the primary 
draw. This year was no exception. The Adventure Park hosted 40 rentals, with 21 of those 
being at the Ceremony Overlook site, 11 at the Day Lodge, 4 at Slopeside Hall, and 4 
park bookings.  Nearly every Saturday and Sunday in September  was filled with a 
reception booking, and on September 12, staff hosted two receptions at the same time.  
 
Due to the low water levels, T-Dock, the paddle docks, and the Summit County Sherriff’s 
dock were successfully moved out toward the main body of the reservoir, and a 
specialized anchor system was installed to secure them. 
In September Programs staff ran after school skateboarding and after school mountain 
biking, in addition to after school Fun Club. Staff also hosted Fall Fest and Run the 
Rockies Road Half Marathon and 10k.   
 
The report below details each of the different divisions (Adventure Park, Marina, Special 
Events, and Recreation Programs) within the Department.  Participation numbers (as 
appropriate) and financials are all presented for September 2026. 
Adventure Park:  
For the purpose of this report, the Adventure Park consists of all aspects of the Peninsula 
Recreation Area (PRA) including the tubing hill, ski and ride hill, terrain park, field and 
facility reservations, concessionaire operations, the bike park, the skatepark, disc golf, 
trails and the Nordic Center.  Finances are broken down into 1160 accounts (tubing hill, 
ski and ride hill, terrain park, and rentals) and 1170 accounts (Nordic and Trails).

STAFF REPORT 
 
TO:  MAYOR & TOWN COUNCIL 
FROM: THAD RENAUD, TOWN ATTORNEY 
RE:  Consideration and Possible Approval of Colorado Municipality 
Opioid Settlement Participation & Release Form Approval - 
Albertsons 
DATE: NOVEMBER 10, 2026 
 
 
  
Summary & Background: 
In 2021, the Town, along with many other Colorado municipalities, entered into the 
attached Colorado Opioids Settlement Memorandum of Understanding (the “MOU”) in 
order to maximize the funds available to the state and local governments in Colorado 
under the settlement of national class action litigation against certain manufacturers of 
opioid drugs. Under the MOU, all of the participating local governments released any 
claims that they may have had against those manufacturers and all of those settlement 
funds are to be used for purposes of mitigating the harms that have been caused by 
those drugs.  Also under the MOU, most of the settlement funds (60%) go to regional 
spending authorities in which the local governments within the region participate in 
decision-making, with an additional 20% going directly to the participating local 
governments, and the remaining 20% going to the state. 
 
In 2025, a similar opioids class action was settled with Purdue Pharma and the Town 
agreed at that time that it would release any individual legal claims that the Town may 
have had against Purdue, and that the settlement funds received from that settlement 
would be subject to distribution under the MOU. 
 
The latest settlement by the state of Colorado in the opioids class action is with 
Albertsons.  As with the previous settlements, the state and local governments can 
maximize the funds they receive by maximizing the number of local governments who 
release individual legal claims against Albertsons. Accordingly, the state has asked that 
the Town consider and approve the attached Subdivision Participation and Release 
Form.  Like the prior releases, all funds received as result of this settlement will be 
distributed pursuant to the MOU.

Because the Town’s damages are uncertain and the cost of pursuing an individual claim 
against Albertsons would be prohibitively expensive, the Town has little to lose and, along 
with the state and other Colorado local governments, much to gain by maximizing the 
number of governmental entities in the state that release claims. 
 
Financial Impact: 
The amount of money that the Town will receive under this settlement has not been 
estimated by the state and will depend on the number of local governments that ultimately 
participate.  The attached spreadsheet provided by the state shows how previous 
settlement funds have been distributed under the MOU. 
 
Staff Recommendation: 
On that basis, it is my recommendation that the Council approve the attached Subdivision 
Participation Release Form and authorize the Town Mayor and Town Clerk to execute 
and deliver the form to the State of Colorado.
