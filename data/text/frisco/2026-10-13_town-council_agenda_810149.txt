Summary & Background:  
 
The Town of Frisco began regulating short -term rental properties in May of 2019. Any 
accommodation unit available for lease for a term of less than thirty consecutive days is 
required to obtain a short -term rental license. The short -term rental regulations  were 
designed to protect public health, safety, and welfare, preserve residential character, 
ensure adequate lodging options for tourists, ensure adequate housing options for 
residents, and balance the demands of a tourist-driven economy with the housing needs 
of the local community. Further information on the STR program can be found on the 
Town website regarding Short Term Rentals in Frisco.  
In 2022, the Town established an STR cap of 25% of residential units  within the Town 
boundaries, which at that time totaled 3,600 units. With 3,600 units, a total of 900 STR 
licenses were available, or 25% of residential units. This 3,600 number included all rental 
and ownership residential units including deed restricted housing units. As new units have 
been built, the overall number of STR licenses has increased. There are currently 924 
STR licenses within the Town of Frisco. 
Staff was recently asked to review the STR cap code language, questioning if deed 
restricted units should be included in the overall total of residential units in Town when 
used to calculate the number of STR licenses permitted within the Town. According to 
Town Code, 110-38, all residential housing units , regardless of vacant, rented, owner 
occupied, or deed restricted, are counted towards the total number of residential housing 
units. When an income restricted rental housing development, such as the Scout, comes 
online in 2026 with fifty-four (54) rental units, this in effect will increase the number of STR 
licenses available in 2027 by fourteen (14).

In 2022, the Town Council held four discussions on Short -Term Rental Overview and 
Strategies to discuss implementing the license cap. The approved language is as stated 
in Town Code, 110-38:  
Except as otherwise set forth in this Section, at no time shall the Town issue 
a license that would cause the total number of licenses issued pursuant to 
this Article to exceed the number that is equal to 25 percent of the total 
number of residential housing units within Town boundaries. For purposes of 
this Section, the Town determines that as of October 11, 2022, and until 
January 3, 2023, the total number of residential housing units within the Town 
boundaries is 3,600. On the first business day of each calendar year, the 
Town's Community Development Director shall determine and certify in 
writing to the Town Clerk the then -current number of residential 
housing units within the Town boundaries. Such determination shall be 
made by the addition (if any) and the subtraction (if any) of residential 
housing units completed or demolished during the immediately prior 
calendar year from the number initially established herein, as modified 
from year to year in accordance with this Section. Such number shall 
remain the total number of residential housing units for purposes of this 
Section until the next subsequent certification on the first business day of the 
subsequent calendar year.

Analysis:  
 
License Cap 
Conversations in 2022 regarding implementing the current STR license cap were 
established at 900 with insight at the time being that was approximately how many 
licenses Staff and Council thought were reflective of capturing the current demand of 
an STR license.  
Since 2022, there has been a waitlist to obtain an STR license. As of September 24, 
2026 there are 62 properties on the STR waitlist with the top of the list being on the 
waitlist since January 12, 2026.  
Properties are released from the waitlist when a STR license becomes available which 
is through four methods:  
1. A property owner can contact the Town and ask to cancel their STR license. 
a. Whereas this is a possibility, current staff have not had anyone request 
to just void their license.  
2. A property sells. Each month, the Town receives a list of real estate transactions 
and compares each property with the STR license list. If there are properties 
that sold and had a license, Staff ensures taxes are closed out for the account 
holder and that STR license is then voided. 
a. Approximately 2-3 licenses are voided each month due to real estate 
transactions.  
3. Properties do not renew their STR license. Each STR license is required to 
annually renew the license and pay the annual fee prior to expiration. All STR 
licenses expire April 30 th.  Numerous attempts to contact nonresponders are 
taken, but if a property does not fill out their renewal paperwork, Staff will void 
their license.  
a. In 2026, Staff released approximately 40 people from the waitlist to fill 
the number of STR licenses that were not renewed. 
4. New licenses added. Each January, as being discussed in this work session, 
Staff calculates how many new residential units were added in Town the prior 
year with 25% of that number added to the STR license inventory.  
There are currently 924 STR licenses due to:  
• In 2024, fifteen (15) new residential units received a Certificate of Occupancy 
and were added to the overall residential inventory. This led to three (3) new 
STR licenses being available. 900 STR licenses went to 903 STR licenses. 
• In 202 5, eighty-six (86) new residential units received a Certificate of 
Occupancy and were added to the overall residential inventory. This led to 
twenty-one (21) new STR licenses being available. 9 03 STR licenses went to 
924 STR licenses.

Workforce Housing 
Currently, all housing units are included in the total inventory of residential units. This 
includes residential units which have a restrictive housing covenant on them limiting 
the Area Median Income  or limiting the occupant to work for a business operating 
within a specified geographic area.  
Staff notes that if the Council supports exempting an existing residential unit that has a 
housing restrictive covenant recorded on it through the Housing Helps program, it would 
not be classified as a new unit, or a unit demolished, but a new category for  Staff’s 
calculation purposes to provide transparency. Staff would recommend the unit be 
removed from the overall housing residential inventory when determining STR license 
counts. 
Should Council decide not to make modifications to Town Code, Chapter 1 10, then the 
54 recently constructed residential apartments in The Scout will allow fourteen (14)  
additional STR licenses to be available in 2027. Then, after the 52 residential apartments 
in the Bella receive a Certificate of Occupancy in 2027, it will allow thirteen (13) additional 
STR licenses to be available in 2028.

How STRs are Impacting the Local Real Estate Market 
When the STR cap was adopted, one consideration was whether the availability of STR 
licenses was contributing to demand for residential real estate and increasing housing 
prices. Since the STR waitlist was implemented, however, Frisco real estate activity has 
remained strong despite buyers having no assurance that a property will receive an STR 
license.

While REIF collections are influenced by both property values and transaction volume 
and therefore should not be interpreted as a direct measure of home -price appreciation, 
the trend indicates that the value of Frisco real estate transactions has remained strong 
even while new STR licenses have been limited by the waitlist. This suggests that access 
to an STR license is only one of many factors influencing demand and property values in 
Frisco. 
Accordingly, there is limited evidence that limiting STR licenses materially reduce s 
housing prices or improves affordability on its own. Frisco remains a highly desirable 
resort community, and broader market forces, including limited housing supply, second -
home demand, interest rates, and regional economic conditions, continue to influen ce 
real estate values. 
 
Financial Impact:  
Town Council should not assume a proportional revenue increase from expanding the 
cap, or an equivalent revenue loss from limiting its growth. The financial benefit depends 
on additional visitor spending, not simply additional licenses at $250/STR license/year.  
 
Projected 2026 short term rental tax revenue of $22.2 million will generate approximately 
$2.7million in tax revenue at the combined 12.075% rate  (2% Town of Frisco sales tax, 
2.35% lodging tax, 5% STR excise tax, 2% County sales tax, 0.725% SCHA tax) that is 
received by the Town of Frisco. With a cap of 924 licenses, this represents approximately 
$3k annually per unit of license capacity as shown in Figure 3.

While the cap has increased, estimated annual taxes per cap unit have decreased 
approximately 12% between 2023 and 2025. The decline began before the cap increased 
and reflects factors beyond license availability, alongside the Town’s broader flattening of 
revenues across tax sectors. Increasing the supply of STR units does not necessarily 
increase visitor demand or total taxable spending. Additional units may compete for a 
limited amount of rental activity, redistributing bookings among properties rather than 
generating new Town revenue. 
Accordingly, Staff recommends that adding protected workforce housing not automatically 
increase STR capacity elsewhere in Town. The decision should be guided primarily by 
Frisco’s housing and community objectives, rather than an assumed revenue gain from 
additional licenses or an equivalent revenue loss from limiting their growth.

Staff Recommendation:  
Staff recommends the Council direct Staff to draft an Ordinance to modify Town Code, 
Section 110 -38 regarding the limitations on the number of STR licenses issued. 
Specifically, Staff recommends the Council support exempting the following categories 
when reviewing the current number of housing units on an annual basis and determining 
the number of STR licenses:

• New housing units that have a restrictive covenant recorded on them, limiting 
the Area Median Income (AMI) of occupant. 
• New housing units that have a restrictive covenant recorded on them, limiting 
occupancy to working for a business operating within a specified geographic 
area. 
Furthermore, Staff recommends the Council support deducting the properties in the 
scenarios below when reviewing the current number of housing units on an annual basis 
for the purpose of determining the number of STR licenses. 
• Existing housing units in which a new restrictive covenant has been recorded 
on them, limiting the Area Median Income (AMI) of occupant. 
• Existing housing units in which a new restrictive covenant has been recorded 
on them, limiting the Area Median Income (AMI) of occupant to working for a 
business operating within a specified geographic area. 
This approach supports the Town’s stated goal of balancing visitor accommodations with 
housing opportunities for residents and preserving residential community character. 
Those objectives are consistent with the purposes of Frisco’s STR regulations and the  
Town’s goal of increasing resident-occupied housing.

STAFF REPORT 
 
TO:  MAYOR & TOWN COUNCIL 
FROM: LINSEY JOYCE, RECREATION DIRECTOR 
RE:  RECREATION DEPARTMENT MONTHLY REPORT - AUGUST 2026 
DATE: OCTOBER 13, 2026 
 
  
Overview:  
 
In August staff ran three weeks of Frisco Fun Club, and one week each of Adventure 
Camp, H2O Camp, Bike Camp and Skateboard Camp.  Summit School District kicked off 
the 26/27 school year on Friday, August 21, and staff ran the first day of After School Fun 
Club.  Staff continue to run After School Fun Club Monday-Friday at Frisco Elementary 
and can take up to 30 kids in the program.   
 
Adventure Park staff managed 44 facility reservations in August, 16 of those were 
weddings on the Ceremony Site.   The Ceremony Site had a 60% increase in facility 
reservations from 2025 (10 rentals) to 2026 (16 rentals). 
Throughout the month of August, the trails crew had two mini-excavators on the Tin Box 
Trail; staff constructed the trail from both ends, eventually connecting in the middle, with 
the trail anticipated to be fully open in late September.   
Marina staff continued to experience declining water levels: reservoir water levels 
continued to recede rapidly in August.  Due to the flat topography of the lakebed, staff 
faced dock management challenges; minor drops in vertical water levels (just a few 
inches) required moving the docks horizontally by multiple feet to keep them floating and 
operational. 
Staff hosted the final four Concerts in the Park with music from Float Like A Buffalo, Lose 
Hermanos Latin Funk, Hight Five, and Bonfire Dub. 
The report below details each of the different divisions (Adventure Park, Marina, Special 
Events, and Recreation Programs) within the Department.  Participation numbers (as 
appropriate) and financials are all presented for August 2026. 
 
Page 59 of 141
 
Adventure Park:  
For the purpose of this report, the Adventure Park consists of all aspects of the Peninsula 
Recreation Area (PRA) including the tubing hill, ski and ride hill, field and facility 
reservations, concessionaire operations, the bike park, the skatepark, disc golf, trails and 
the Nordic Center.  Finances are broken down into 1160 accounts (tubing hill, ski and ride 
hill, Day Lodge) and 1170 accounts (Nordic and Trails).     
August 2026 highlights for the Adventure Park (1160) included the following: 
Facility Improvements: 
 Staining of the Day Lodge exterior took place over a few weeks in August. The 
Day lodge patio is noticeably improved.
