DATE: SEPTEMBER 22, 2026 
 
 
  
Summary & Background: 
Voters in Summit County will consider the initiative on 2026 ballot to reauthorize, in 
perpetuity, the Strong Future mill levy. This is not a new or increased tax, only a 
continuation of the mill levy that already exists. It is important to note that public staff 
cannot promote a position for the Town government on this mill levy question, but it can 
provide factual information in regards the ballot measure. The group presenting at this 
Work Session and the Resolution of support that you will consider in your regular 
meeting are both within the bounds of promoting this ballot measure. 
 
Analysis: 
The 1A Summit County mill levy question on the 2018 ballot was posed in order to 
address the most pressing needs around the County in the areas of: 
 
• Wildfire Prevention 
• Behavioral Health and Suicide Prevention 
• Early Childhood Programs  
• Recycling and Waste Diversion, and  
• Community Infrastructure Development and Maintenance 
 
These investments have strengthened Summit County by expanding essential services, 
supporting families and improving community safety. 
 
In 2018, voters in Summit County approved the mill levy to be gin being collected in 
January of 2019. Since 2019, ov er $72 million has been invested in the five bulleted 
program areas to benefit the citizens and visitors of Summit County. 
 
Although the current Strong Future mill levy does not sunset until 2028, it was the desire 
of the Summit County Commissioners to place the reauthorization question on the 2026 
ballot to ask the voters of the County for their support this year.

  September 22, 2026

Financial Impact: 
The Strong Future mill levy already exists and is not seeking an increase. The voters are 
only being asked to continue the mill levy. 
 
Alignment with Strategic Plan:  
All program areas of Strong Future have a positive impact in Frisco being able to 
achieve elements of its Strategic Plan. Affordability of workforce and residents is 
supported by the efforts area early childhood education. Inclusion and public safety 
goals are supported by the mental health and suicide prevention efforts. Our local 
economic vitality and public safety is aided by the support of our wildfire mitigation 
efforts in Frisco’s Backyard. Supporting the Town’s climate action work within strong 
core services is aided by the work being done in Frisco to reduce the effects of our 
waste stream.  Last, the building of the Search and Rescue facility helps Frisco achieve 
its public safety goals.

Summary & Background: 
SOS Outreach was founded at Vail Mountain in 1993 when they introduced 15 kids from 
Denver to snowboarding. Or iginally named Snowboard Outr each Society, their mission 
was to bring awareness to the new sport while  also giving back to the community. Now, 
as SOS Outreach, they do more than just skiing and riding and are using outdoor-based 
mentorship to teach life skills to kids and teenagers. 
 
The Town of Frisco (TOF) and SOS Outreach have had a building use and management 
agreement in place since 2008 for the building located at 110 S. 3rd Avenue, Frisco.  The 
agreement is automatically renewed on Ma y 1 of each year, unless terminated in 
advance. in writing. by either party.   
 
In this agreement, the TOF’s responsibilities of 110 S. 3rd Ave. include: 
 Payment of all utilities and services 
 Building maintenance and repair 
 Supply basic furniture for the buildi ng, including tables and chairs   
 
Joe Schleifer, Project & Facilities Manager fo r TOF, and the facilities team, oversee the 
cleaning schedule, building maintenance, trash, and supplies for the building.   
 
In this agreement, SOS Outreach’s responsibilities of 110 S. 3rd Ave. include: 
 General site upkeep and cleaning, ensuri ng the building is properly maintained 
 Use of the building to host SO S programs, events and meetings 
 Oversight and coordination of  all private group rentals 
 Exclusive use of office space

RE: Discretionary Fund Requests for Budget Year 2027 
DATE: September 22, 2026 
 
 
 
Summary & Background: 
 
The Town of Frisco receives many grant requests through a variety of grant avenues. These grant 
avenues include: 
• Community Micro Grant. The Town of Frisco annually considers requests for in- kind 
services and grants of up to $5,000 through the Summit County Common Grant 
Application process —referred to locally as “micro grants.” Eligible applicants must be 
501(c)(3) organizations. In 2026, for the 2027 budget, the Town received 71 applications, 
up from 65 in the previous cycle. 
 
• The Community Impact Grant. In 2024, the Town of Frisco implemented the Frisco 
Community Impact Grant Program. The purpose of this grant is to create a formal, annual 
process for evaluating worthwhile Frisco investments in non- profit projects that support 
the Town’s goals and that can be evaluated within the budget process. These are 
significant grants for non-profits that have programs that will have an exceptional impact 
on the Frisco community and, therefore, require a higher level of support. Non-profits can 
request up to $80,000 through the Frisco Community Impact Grant Program. The Town 
received thirteen (13) applications for these Community Impact Grants, up from seven (7) 
applications the year before. 
 
• Other funding requests. These requests are reviewed annually by Council through the 
budget process and may be funded through the General Fund and the Water Fund. In 
2026, funding was granted to High Country Conservation Center , including Solarize  
Summit ($116,858 from the General Fund  and $35,000 from the Water Fund ) and Blue 
River Watershed Group ($5,000 from the General Fund). Please note that funding for HC3 
is to make progress on the Town’s climate action goals and fund programs and services to 
supplement the Town’s sustainability efforts. 
The Council subcommittee, comprised of Council members Elizabeth Skrzypczak- Adrian and 
Martin Allen, and supported by staff, Anna Jones, Dylan Olchin, and Kristen Kall, reviewed all 
applications and will present funding recommendations to Council during the September 22, 2026, 
work session. These recommendations are included as Attachment 1 to this memo.

Please note, three organizations are also funded directly through the Nicotine Tax Fund. In 2026, 
Building Hope was funded $77,500; the Family & Intercultural Resource Center was funded

Analysis: 
Four criteria were used to rate each of the applications, with each criteria given a score of 1-5. A 
total of 100 points were possible. The four questions were: 
1) Does this non-proﬁt provide fundamental services such as food and shelter? 
2) Does this non-proﬁt directly impact the Town of Frisco and its residents or 
Businesses? 
3) Does this non-proﬁt serve a large portion of the community? 
4) Does this non-proﬁt have strong leadership that drives healthy ﬁscal practices? 
Budget parameters were also considered throughout the process. In 2025, for budget year 2026, 
a total of $89,700 was awarded in cash awards for the micro grants, $43,654 for in-kind services, 
and $80,000 in cash awards for the Community Impact Grant Program. Non-profits can apply for 
both the annual countywide $5,000 micro grants and the Frisco Community Impact Grant. 
The committee started with the following budget parameters for the 2027 process: 
• $80,000 for the micro-grant program, which does not include in-kind services. 
• $80,000 for the Community Impact Grant Program 
 
Additional budget consideration was given to those organizations that receive grant funds through 
the other town avenues. 
 
The proposed funding recommendations for 2027 are included in the “Financial Impact” section 
of this report.

Cash Basis Financial Summary Section
This section provides a high-level snapshot of the Town of Frisco’s financial activity using cash basis 
accounting. Under this method, revenues and expenditures are recorded only when cash is received 
or paid, rather than when they are earned or incurred. This provides a clear, real-time view of available 
funds and the Town’s current financial position.
This summary is different from the year-over-year business activity section earlier in the report. That 
section looks at sales tax based on when sales happen, while this one focuses on when the Town 
actually receives the money—giving a real-time look at how collections are tracking against the budget.
This summary is designed to:
•Track revenue based on actual cash received.
•Support monthly budget monitoring and cash flow analysis.
•Complement economic activity data presented in the earlier sales tax trend comparison section.
Understanding the timing of tax collection versus remittance is key to interpreting the data in this report. 
Key Distinction
•Collected = When the customer pays tax to the business. 
•Remitted = When the business sends that tax to the Town. 
•When: At the time of sale to the customer.
•What Happens: A business charges and collects tax at the point of sale (in-store, online, etc.).
•Example: A $100 purchase with an 8% sales tax results in $8 collected from the customer by the business at 
checkout and held until the tax is remitted to the government.
•When: Typically by the 20th of the following month (depending on the business’ filing frequency).
•What Happens: The business submits the collected tax to the State and to the Town.
•Example: Sales tax collected in January is generally remitted to the Town in February.
