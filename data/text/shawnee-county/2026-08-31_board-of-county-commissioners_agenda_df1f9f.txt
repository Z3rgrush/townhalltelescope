An Analysis and Summary of Local Economic Development 
Incentive Policies Across Thirteen Kansas Cities and Counties
This presentation was prepared for the Board of County Commissioners of 
Shawnee County, Kansas, for consideration and evaluation of the 
County’s Industrial Revenue Bond (IRB) policy. It presents Shawnee 
County’s current policy framework first and then compares it against 
twelve peer Kansas cities and counties, so the Board can identify 
provisions it may wish to retain, amend, adopt, or incorporate from 
other jurisdictions. 
Page 3 of 48
Core Choices Made by Every Jurisdiction
1. Who qualifies? Some jurisdictions limit eligibility to a defined list of 
industries, some extend eligibility to multi-family/affordable housing, while 
others appear to leave it completely to the discretion of the governing body.
2. How much base exemption, and what qualifies a project for more? Nearly all 
jurisdictions set a base exemption amount generally tied to job creation 
and/or capital investment, then layer on bonus exemption percentages for 
items such as wage levels, local resident hiring, targeted industries, and 
development in targeted areas. However, the mechanics (published 
point/percentage matrix versus governing body discretion) differ among the 
jurisdictions. 
3. What is required in return? PILOT (payment in lieu of taxes) requirements, 
annual compliance review, and clawback/rescission triggers are the 
enforcement mechanisms and they vary from very structured to  very 
discretionary.
4. What is the application and approval procedure and fee structure? Application 
fees, cost benefit analysis requirements, annual renewal fees, and committee 
review structures differ among jurisdictions.
Page 4 of 48
Key Decision Points for the BCC
1. Industry eligibility: a defined list of targeted uses with explicitly exclusions, versus a broad/open 
eligibility standard that is largely discretionary. 
2. Base exemption formula: a published, formula driven matrix (transparent, predictable, less 
discretion) versus a discretionary, case-by-case governing body determination (more flexible, less 
predictable). 
3. Bonus criteria: factors (local hiring, wage thresholds, targeted industries, and targeted 
development areas) that might earn additional exemption above the base, and how are those 
bonuses verified and enforced. 
4. PILOT Requirements: whether PILOT are required as a matter of policy or considered case by 
case, and how the PILOT amount is calculated relative to the exempted tax liability.
5. Annual review standards and rescission or reduction triggers: the strength of the compliance 
mechanism — who certifies continued compliance, what triggers a reduction or rescission of the 
exemption, and what the annual renewal fee/process costs both the applicant and the 
jurisdiction.
6. Fee structure: application fees, service fees, cost benefit analysis costs, and annual review fees – 
all levers that impact both program integrity and ease of use and access.

City of Topeka
Base Exemption Amount / Criteria for Exemption Above Base Amount
The exemption amount is structured as a PILOT reduction rather than frontline abatement 
percentage. PILOT is presumed to be 100% of the ad valorem taxes otherwise due unless a 
reduction is calculated under the reduction formula.
 
Contains a reduction formula combining three independently capped factors, which are then 
summed (total capped at 100% reduction).
 
1. Job Quantity Reduction – 1% per new FTE job created, capped at 70% reduction;
2. Job Quality Reduction – the company’s average wage rate for newly created jobs divided 
by the Shawnee County base wage rate (computed annually from US Census Business 
Patterns data), then multiplied by 30% (total capped at 30% reduction);
3. Capital Investment Reduction – 1% per $100,000 of capital investment (total capped at 
80% reduction).
 
Payments in lieu of Taxes
PILOT is mandatory, absent the governing body granted reduction. Regardless of the 
reduction formula, each business must pay PILOT equal to 20% of the tax otherwise due on 
the IRB financed improvement, which is waivable in whole or in part by the governing body 
for extraordinary circumstances, such as the acquisition of vacant buildings, decreased 
market value, or an exceptionally large jobs/investment impact.

Base Exemption Amount / Criteria for Exemption Above Base Amount
There is no express base exemption amount. The percentage of abatement is cumulative based on 
new job creation, capital investment, and additional considerations outlined in the policy.
New Jobs: 5-25 equals 30%; 26-50 equals 40%; greater than 50 equals 50%.
New Capital Investment: 1 million to 5 million equals 40%; 5 million to 10 million equals 50%; 
greater than 10 million equals 60%.
Additional Considerations: Regional growth plan sector (10%); utilizing state, federal, or utility 
incentives (10%); sustainable development/alternative energy use (15%); investing in, or funding, 
entrepreneurship (25%); talent attraction plan (must be documented) (25%); locating in 
redevelopment area (25%); community and workforce development benefits (25%).
For projects where the company is committing to a five year capital investment of at least 
$25,000,000 with annual bond issues, where the nature of the investment is general improvements 
to facilities located on a business campus, those projects will be granted a ten year tax abatement 
for the improvements.
Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILOT)
If receiving a tax abatement may be required to make PILOT equal to the amount of the property 
tax abated. May be required to make PILOT at any time in the event of non-compliance with 
conditions imposed by the economic development incentive agreement, as an alternative to 
complete cancellation of the abatement.

between $1 million and $2 million = 0.5% (up to 10%); between $2 million and $5 million = 0.25% (up to 15%); above $5 million =
