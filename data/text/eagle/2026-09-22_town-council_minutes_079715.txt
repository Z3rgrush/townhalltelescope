1. Minutes 
STAFF REPORTS 
1. Town Manager Update 
Council received a comprehensive review of the Town's current organizational memberships to identify potential 
overlaps in services and benefits. While no decisions were made to discontinue any memberships,· Council 
requested additional information regarding the benefits of membership with the Northwest Colorado Council of 
Governments (NWCCOG). 
Council also expressed interest in exploring opportunities to negotiate additional benefits with the Eagle 
Chamber of Commerce and comparing its membership benefits with those offered by Vail Valley Partnership. 
Lastly, Council requested a comparison of the roles, services, and benefits provided by three climate-focused

organizations: Climate Action Collaborative, Colorado Communities for Climate Action (CC4CA), and the Adam 
Palmer Sustainability Fund. 
BUSINESS ITEMS 
1. Sustainability and Greenhouse Gas Emissions Update 
Town Manager Melissa Daruna and Sustainability Specialist Kira Koppel answered Council questions regarding 
the progress of the Town's sustainability and greenhouse-gas reduction goals. 
Council discussed how carbon sequestration is factored into greenhouse-gas inventories and net-zero targets, as 
well as which sustainability initiatives could have the greatest impact on reducing operational costs for both the 
Town and the broader community . The discussion also covered the energy demands of water and wastewater 
treatment and opportunities to improve data collection on Town vehicle usage. 
2. Parking in the Right-of-Way: Town Code and Enforcement 
Chief Carrie Buhlman and Code Enforcement Officer Ron Delp presented to Council on how the Town's current 
municipal code addresses parking within the right-of-way. They reviewed the definitions of abandoned and 
junked vehicles, regulations related to trailers and campers, and the challenges associated with enforcement . 
Council also discussed encroachments within the right-of-way and explored opportunities to reclaim and 
organize parking within the downtown core. The discussion will continue at the next work session, scheduled for 
October 6. 
COUNCIL DISCUSSION AND FUTURE AGENDA ITEMS 
A council member requested a future discussion identifying where the town uses "forever chemicals" and 
evaluate the trade-off between health concerns and noxious-weed control. 
A council member also proposed exploring a regulated summer van-life program with designated spaces and 
hook-ups, potentially requiring registration, insurance, identification, and employment or a paycheck in 
Eagle. Staff noted that the town currently prohibits camping in vans but could consider modifying the code 
for a limited pilot.
