1. Project: LUDC26-04 - Modifications to Title 4, Section 4.10.050 
File #: LUDC26-04 
Applicant: Town of Eagle 
Location: Town of Eagle 
Staff Contact: Jessica Johnsen, Community Development Director 
Request: For the Planning and Zoning Commission to review and recommend approval of staff proposed 
modifications to the Land Use and Development Code in reference to Title 4; Section 4.10.050. 
 
Staff Presentation 
 
Community Development Director Jessica Johnsen presented proposed amendments to the Town of Eagle Land 
Use and Development Code, specifically Title 4, Section 4.10.050, concerning mixed-use and commercial structure and 
site design standards. Ms. Johnsen explained that the Town Council had identified amendments to the Land Use and 
Development Code as a priority, including revisions to the mixed-use development standards contained in Section 
4.10.050.C.1.b.3. The proposed amendments were intended to address the existing step-back requirements for multi-
story buildings in the Broadway District and implement direction provided by the Town Council. 
 
Ms. Johnsen explained that the existing Code established significant step-back requirements for the third and 
fourth stories of buildings in the Broadway District. Under the existing standards, buildings were generally limited to 
three stories, and a fourth story could be permitted for buildings that include affordable housing in excess of what is 
required by the Town’s Inclusionary Housing requirements. Where a fourth story is permitted, the Code requires both 
the third and fourth stories to step back from street-facing  property lines. The existing provision requires a minimum 
10-foot step back for the third story and a minimum 20-foot step back for the fourth story when development is built to 
the applicable zero-foot setback. Ms. Johnsen noted that these requirements could substantially limit design options for 
multi-story buildings and could create challenges for properties with unique site characteristics. 
 
The proposed amendment would  simplify the step-back framework by applying the step back requirement only 
to the fourth floor. Under the proposed language, buildings would remain limited to a maximum of three stories, and a 
fourth floor could  be added when the building included affordable housing  in excess of what is required by the Town’s 
Inclusionary Housing requirements and as otherwise permitted by the Code. However, rather than requiring both the 
third and fourth floors to step back, only the fourth floor would be required to provide a minimum 10-foot step back 
from the street-facing property lines.  This change would have eliminated the existing requirement for a separate third-
floor step back while retaining a step-back standard for the uppermost floor of a four-story building. 
 
Ms. Johnsen also clarified how the proposed amendment would apply to developments that were not 
constructed at the zero-foot property-line setback. Under the existing Code, such developments were required to 
provide a proportional step back. For example, a building constructed with a five-foot setback would have been required 
to provide a five-foot step back for the third story and a 15-foot step back for the fourth story. Under the proposed 
amendment, the proportional requirement would have applied only to the fourth story. Using the same example, a 
building with a five-foot initial setback would therefore have been required to provide a five-foot step back for the 
fourth story, while the third story would no longer have been subject to a separate step back requirement. 
 
Ms. Johnsen stated that the proposed revisions had been developed in response to explicit policy direction from 
the Town Council. The intent had been to modernize the development code and provide greater flexibility in building 
design while continuing to protect the integrity of the Town’s zoning standards. She emphasized that the revised 
framework had been intended to establish clear, consistent, and predictable standards while allowing additional design 
flexibility for properties with unique characteristics or development constraints. By limiting the stepback requirement to 
the fourth floor, the proposed amendments would provide more options for the design and configuration of multi-story

Ms. Johnsen responded that staff had viewed the proposed amendment as providing structured flexibility while 
continuing to protect the integrity of the Town’s zoning standards. She explained that the change had not been 
considered a significant departure from the existing standards and that staff had believed it continued to meet the 
intent of both the Comprehensive Plan and the Land Use and Development Code. She stated that the amendment would 
have provided additional flexibility for unique properties and could have facilitated additional residential density and 
development, particularly within the Broadway District. Ms. Johnsen emphasized that the proposed standards would 
have remained predictable and would have applied consistently throughout the Broadway District rather than allowing 
varying step-back requirements on a case-by-case basis.

 RE: LUDC Text Amendment – Referrals, Call-ups, and Appeals 
 
 
 
 On September 8, the Town Council directed staff to prepare a text amendment addressing 
some inconsistencies and desired changes in the Town’s Land Use and Development Code (LUDC), 
Eagle Municipal Code Title  4, regarding referrals, call -ups, and appeals , collectively referred to as 
review measures.   
 
Background 
 
The 2023 readoption of the LUD C resulted in a n approach to re view measures that 
emphasized staff and decisionmaker initiative to review a n inferior body ’s decision while leaving 
applicants and the public with little avenue to seek review outside of appeals to the district court.  The 
review measures currently in the LUDC include call-up, referral, and limited appeal.  In August the 
Town Council added an  appeal review to the LUDC ’s variance procedures.   In our review of the 
existing review measures , we found the provisions lacking in proce dural safeguards to ensure due 
process and conflicts with other provisions in the LUDC and accompanying tables.  As such, we have 
deleted and redrafted nearly all of these review measures, and per Council direction have proposed to 
remove call-ups entirely from the LUDC, limit referral only to staff, and adopt an appeal procedure 
for every type of application under the LUDC.   
 
Summary of Proposed Amendments 
 
Removal of Call-Up Procedures 
 
The first major amendment is the deletion in its entire ty of the LUDC’s call-up procedures found in 
LUDC Section 4.17.060.C.5.c & d.  Additionally references to call-up contained in 4.17.060.A.3 and 
4.17.040.D.2.b were deleted.  Currentl y both the Planning Commission and Town Council can  call 
up an applicat ion pending before the lower  decisionmaker – staff and Planning & Zoning (P&Z) 
respectively upon the vote of 3 members of the superior body.  Council directed staff to delet e this 
provision entirely, to be replaced by an appeal procedure, as further described below. 
 
 
Page 10 of 47
 
 
Page 2 
 
Limited Referral Procedures 
 
The second major amendment is the limitation of referral – the ability of an inferior decisionmaker to 
elevate review to a higher body  without making any decision itself  – only to staff referral to P&Z.  
Currently the LUDC provides for referral from administrative review to P&Z and then also to Town 
Council.  While this will require P&Z to make a decision on every application that comes to it, that 
decision will nonetheless be reviewable on appeal as provided below.  
 
Appeal Procedure 
 
A new Section 4.17.070.E  would establish a single appeal framework for decisions made in 
administering or enforcing the LUDC. An appeal could be filed by staff, the applicant, the property 
owner, and adjacent property owners within the applicable notice area who participated in the original 
process. A notice of appeal will need to be filed within 14 days after the challenged decision, and 
upon completeness review an appeal hearing would be scheduled within 21 days.  The appellant will 
bear the burden of proof, and the appeal would be limited to the record from the original proceeding, 
meaning that new evidence, testimony, and revised plans will not be considered.  Filing an appeal 
will stay or pause the challenged decision subject to an emergency exception . The appellate body’s 
review standard will be the arbitrary and capricious  and abuse of discretion standard that is typical 
for review of the  record below. Staff reports for appeals will be limited to identifying the relevant 
facts, procedural history, and challenged decision but would not make a recommendation on the 
merits of the appeal.   This appeal procedure will be available for all levels of land use applications 
but any single application can be appealed only once and after that only to district court.  
 
Table 4.17-1 Amendments 
 
Table 4.17-1 governs the review and decisionmaking process under the LUDC for each application 
type, but had several conflicts internally and with other provisions of the LUDC.  We took a thorough 
review of that table to ensure that the appropriate recommending, decisionmaking, and appellate body 
is identified.  
 
Conforming Amendments 
 
4.17.080.F.4.d and 4.17.120.A contai n appe al provisions that may conflict with the  proposed 
4.17.070.E, and as such are proposed to be deleted since that new provision will govern those types 
of applications.  Finally, the review of a Design Variance by either Planning Commission or Town 
Council provided too much discret ion and so was limited to Planning Commission, which per the 
Table 4.17-1 amendments will be subject to appeal.

data is available from two reports, both completed prior to 2020. the Climate Action Collaborative joined the 
ICLEI Forests & Trees Carbon Accounting Cohort to fold carbon sequestration into the full picture of our county-

that “typical greenhouse gas inventories of forests using similar approaches report uncertainties in the net GHG 
balance that can be as high as ±45%.”)

emissions figures included are now out-of-date, the analysis emphasizes that the greatest 
opportunity for carbon sequestration state-wide is reforesting burn scars (forests and woodlands 
together store the most carbon in Colorado’s lands) and avoiding conversion of wetlands and 
grasslands in Colorado through 2051 (as this would increase how much carbon those lands would 
store).

Raw Water Supply and Stream Flows (Tom Gosiorowski, Public Works Director) 
• Brush Creek Stream Flows: flows have benefited from recent rains. On August 31st the flow below the Love and 
White ditch diversion crossed above 12 cfs, which is the threshold between the Dry Year and Critical Dry Year 
defined in the Brush Creek Watershed Management Plan. I expect this to be short lived, and because of that I 
expect that the creek will stay within the Critical Dry Year condition through October. I have been informed that 
Eagle County will cease irrigation of portions of the Brush Creek Valley Ranch Open Space beginning September 
1st, and this will help to improve stream flows in the lower reach of Brush Creek. 
• Eagle River Flows: Over the past 7 days flows have averaged 62 cfs, which is  40% of the flow for the same period 
in 2025. While this is an improvement from a month ago, the flows are still the lowest ever recorded for this 
period. 
• Treatment Plant Diversions: the 7-day average combined diversion into both treatment plants for the last week 
of August was 2.74 cfs. This is 79% of the diversion for the same period in 2025. Throughout most of Augus we 
were seeing water usage around 35% lower than the same period in 2025. Although usage in the final week of 
August was only down around 20% compared to the same week in 2025, this is mostly a reflection of usage 
falling quickly in late August 2025 due to a period of increased precipitation in that year. This year we have been 
seeing a downward trend in usage since mid-August. Normally we see a larger decrease in usage beginning in 
mid-September as customers begin to shut down and winterize irrigation systems, and irrigation demand usually 
ends completely by mid-October. 
• I expect to maintain our current Stage 3 water use restrictions through the end of the irrigation season.

identifying individuals in the field. Just five days after department-wide training was completed, Eagle Police officers
