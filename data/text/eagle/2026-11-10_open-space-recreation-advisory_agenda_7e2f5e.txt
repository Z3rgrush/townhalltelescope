Welcome! 
 
THANK YOU! Dedicating your personal time on a volunteer citizen committee is 
one of the best ways to give back to your community. The Town Council, 
Planning and Zoning Commission, and Town staff values your time, energy, and 
perspective as we move through a critical project for the Town of Eagle’s future. 
 
INTRODUCTION  
 
The Town of Eagle relies on the active participation of residents volunteering 
their time and effort to sustain the quality of community we enjoy. Committee 
members exemplify the spirit of volunteerism by lending their talents and 
resources to benefit the Town. Appointment to an advisory board or committee in 
Eagle is an honor that brings both responsibility and opportunity. This manual has 
been designed to provide information on the organization of Eagle and the role 
served by committee members. 
 
The following materials provide general information on the citizen committee 
including procedures and policies that apply to their activities.  
 
What is a citizen committee? 
 
A citizen committee is a non-regulatory advisory group to the Town Council, 
Planning and Zoning Commission, and Town staff. The group is appointed by the 
Town Council and is charged with ensuring that community feedback is sought 
out and incorporated in various plans and policies proposed by the Town or its 
consultants.  
 
Citizen Committee Job Description 
 
As a committee member you will review plans and policies and provide feedback 
based on the stated vision/goals of the project, gathered community input, and 
potential outcomes or consequences. The committee and its members advise but 
do not adopt or set policy for the Town. You offer opinions, advice, and provide 
expertise in an advisory capacity. You serve as an ambassador of the Town and 
project of your committee. As an advisory committee member, you have the 
following responsibilities: 
 
• Review current master plans, strategic plans, recent updates, 
P&Z/Council minutes, and other relevant documents shared prior to 
the meeting. 
• Attend 90% of all committee meetings. The number of meetings 
depends on the process, but at a minimum one meeting per quarter.  
• Make staff aware when you are unable to attend a meeting.

• Be available to spend 2 – 4 hours per committee meeting preparing for 
the meeting and reviewing appropriate documents. 
• Respond to communications sent out by staff and consultants if 
applicable. 
• Meet deadlines established by the committee, staff, and the 
consultants. 
• Provide recommendations to the committee grounded in research that 
align with the vision and goals for the community. 
• Provide support to your peers and encourage active participation. 
• If possible, participate in other Town initiatives that help inform your 
committee. 
• If possible, participate in relevant Council/P&Z work sessions to gain 
understanding of any relevant process.   
• Attend topic specific trainings as part of the project.  
 
TOWN AND COMMITTEE EXPECTATIONS 
 
Like most successful relationships, both parties have to stay committed to each 
other. The Town is committed to you for the duration of your appointment or the 
life of the project. Outlined below are commitments we make to the committee 
and commitments we expect from the committee members.   
 
The Town’s commitment to YOU: 
 
• We will listen to and respect your point of view – staff will not assume to 
know or see every aspect of our community and each person’s perspective 
is important. 
 
• We will engage in crucial conversations – not all discussion topics are easy 
or comfortable. Staff will work to facilitate robust dialogue in a neutral 
environment. 
 
• We will do our best to set you up for success – we will provide subject 
matter material for review ahead of key elements to help bring everyone to 
the same base level of knowledge. 
 
• We will be respectful of your time by preparing for each meeting - 
Agendas will be created and distributed ahead of each meeting. Meeting 
minutes will be generated and distributed after each meeting with follow-
up items and action steps as applicable. 
 
Your commitment to the Committee and the Process:

• Show up as a group, leave as a group – engage in crucial conversations, ask 
questions, and challenge assumptions. Ultimately, your committee 
functions as one body. Once a recommendation by the group has been

externally to community members.  
 
• Representation as a committee member – as an individual member of a

Please come to meetings on-time and plan to stay for the entire duration. Staff

Appointments, Resignations, and Terms 
 
Committee members have applied through an application process designed and 
advertised by the Town. Selected members are appointed by the Town Council by 
resolution. If members are found to be in violation of any portion of this 
handbook, Town staff may recommend removal of any members to the Town 
Council for consideration. If any member of the committee feels they can no 
longer fulfill the needs and requirements of the committee as outlined in this 
handbook, a resignation letter shall be submitted to Town staff for consideration 
and recommendation to the Town Council. Town staff may recommend 
replacement committee members if identified as necessary to fulfill the goals of 
the project.

CONFLICT OF INTEREST GUIDELINES 
 
The members of the committee are held to ethical standards. The following 
summary highlights the primary areas of concern addressed by these rules. When 
in doubt, a committee member should ask staff for clarification of any applicable 
regulations. As representatives of the Town of Eagle statutory codes of ethics 
apply even if they are not distinctly called out in Town policy. Refer to CRS 
24.18.101 rev. 2017 for more information.  
 
A committee member should refrain from attempting to influence any decision in 
which he or she has a direct financial or personal conflict of interest.  
 
Generally, a committee member has a financial interest in a decision when the 
decision entails some foreseeable, measurable financial benefit to the individual 
member or relative. 
 
Even if a member does not have a financial interest in a decision or  
recommendation, a personal interest may also create a conflict of interest. The 
test for a personal interest is whether, in the judgment of a reasonable prudent 
person, the committee member would realize or experience some direct or 
substantial benefit or detriment different in kind from that experienced by the 
general public. In applying this test in a business context, committee members 
should check with Town staff before participating in any decision which directly 
and substantially benefits a business in which he or she either has a substantial 
financial interest or is engaged as a consultant or representative.  
 
Or, if a decision of the Town might directly and substantially affect a particular 
business to its economic detriment, and a committee member has a substantial 
financial interest in a competing firm, the member should also exercise caution in 
deciding whether to participate in the decision.  
 
Contracts with the Town 
 
Committee members and their relatives are prohibited from having a financial 
interest in sales to the Town, but only if they exercise, directly or indirectly, any 
decision-making or supervisory authority with regard to the goods and services to 
be sold to the Town.  
 
As to purchases from the Town, such purchases are permitted only when the 
property is offered for sale at an established price and not by bid or auction, and

the purchase must be on the same terms and conditions as would be available to

Disclosure of Confidences 
 
Committee members sometimes receive confidential information from Town 
staff. Such information should not be used for the private benefit of the board or 
commission member or any other individual. Additionally, disclosure of the 
information is prohibited if a reasonable person would consider that the 
disclosure would injure the financial interests of the Town.  
 
Gifts and Favors 
 
The acceptance of honoraria (compensation for speech or participation in public 
events as an official of the Town) is prohibited. The acceptance of any other gift 
or favor from persons doing business with the Town or desiring to do business 
with the Town is also prohibited if the gift or favor might reasonably be construed 
as compensation for an official decision or as something that would tend to impair 
the committee member’s independence of judgment in the performance of their 
official duties.  
 
The following specific items are excluded from the definition of prohibited gifts or 
favors and may be accepted: 
 
• Nonpecuniary awards for public service. 
• Reimbursement for expenditure for attending job-related conferences 
or other meetings. 
• Invitations to social functions or meetings which are not extraordinary 
when viewed in the light of the position held by the board or 
commission member. 
• Perishable or nonpermanent items that are insignificant in value, such 
as meals, lodging, travel, tickets to sporting, recreation, educational or 
cultural events. 
• Salary from outside employment.  
 
Checklist 
 
The following questions are suggested when examining a potential conflict of 
interest or a question of ethical conduct. If the answer to the question is “yes,” 
the committee member should avoid participating in the decision of engaging in 
the proposed transaction:

 Sales to the Town: Will I or my relative receive some foreseeable, 
measurable financial benefit? Am I involved in the procurement or 
supervision? 
 
 Financial interest in a decision: Will I or my relative receive some 
foreseeable, measurable financial benefit?  
 
 Personal interests: Would a reasonably prudent person believe that I or my 
relative will realize or experience some direct and substantial benefit or 
detriment from this decision that is different in kind from that experienced 
by the general public? Will the business that either of us represents 
directly and substantially benefit? Will a competing firm be directly and 
substantially harmed?  
 
 Gifts or favors: Is this gift or favor being offered to me because of a 
decision I have made or am about to make? Would a reasonably prudent 
person think that this gift or favor would impair my independence of 
judgment? (Before deciding to accept a gift, it should fit under one of the 
exemptions in the code.)  
 
 Confidences: Am I about to use or disclose a confidence which, in the 
judgment of a reasonably prudent person, would injure the financial 
interests of the Town? Will I be using it for private gain, either for myself 
or for another person?

Agenda Item:   Seasonal Wildlife Closure Compliance 
Purpose: 
Discuss options to improve compliance with seasonal wildlife closures and identify priorities for staff 
follow-up. 
Background: 
Seasonal wildlife closures protect important habitat during a sensitive time of year. During the October 
discussion of the School House Rock to Kill Bill connector, OSRAC emphasized the need for additional 
education, outreach, enforcement, and social trail closure and restoration. With winter closures 
approaching, staff is seeking committee input on practical ways to improve compliance. 
Options for Discussion: 
 
• Education and outreach: Coordinate reminders through Town communications, neighborhood 
associations, and partner organizations. Explain closure dates, the reasons for closures, and nearby 
areas that remain open. 
• Signs and access points: Review closure signs and boundary markers for visibility and clarity, 
particularly at informal access points. 
• Staff and partner presence: Explore targeted outreach at frequently used access points and 
opportunities for coordinated patrols with appropriate enforcement agencies. 
• Reporting and follow-up: Clarify how residents should report violations and how reports can help 
identify recurring problem areas. 
 
Requested Committee Input: 
 
Which approaches should staff prioritize for the upcoming closure season, and which warrant further 
evaluation? Staff will follow up on feasibility, partner availability, costs, and enforcement authority 
before bringing forward specific commitments.

Agenda Item:   Town-BLM Boundary Fence Removal 
PURPOSE 
Provide an update and seek OSRAC feedback on a phased proposal to remove portions of the Town–
BLM boundary fence south of Eagle Ranch and evaluate whether livestock can be managed without a 
physical boundary fence. Council would consider targeted removal this fall, with remaining removal and 
a potential 2027 grazing trial subject to a separate decision. 
BACKGROUND 
Approximately 11,500 feet of four strand barbed wire separates Town open space from adjacent BLM 
grazing land. The fence is intended to keep cattle off Town property, nearby residential properties, and 
the golf course, while providing a visible jurisdictional boundary. 
Colorado’s “fence out” framework generally places responsibility on landowners to fence livestock out 
of their property. Separately, a maintenance and operations agreement with the Town, Eagle Ranch 
Association, and Metro District assigns the Town responsibility for maintaining this fence. Annual 
inspection and repairs use Open Space operating resources, although those costs have not been tracked 
separately. 
The current grazing permittees, the Gerards, have held the permit for approximately 20 years. BLM 
identifies the grazing area as the E Hardscrabble Allotment (CO08502), encompassing 7,995 acres, with 
an authorization of 552 animal unit months (AUMs), 400 cattle, and grazing dates of May 15 through 
June 25. The permit expires in March 2032. These authorizations apply to the full allotment, not solely 
the parcel adjoining Town open space. The Gerards have begun using GPS collars to establish virtual 
livestock boundaries, with financial support from the Eagle County Conservation District (ECCD). The 
current permit does not require collar use. 
Staff originally pursued replacement with wildlife friendly, high tension fencing. The Eagle Ranch Wildlife 
Protection Committee approved a motion recommending the Eagle Ranch Association to fund 
installation, with Town support for removal through volunteers, youth corps, and staff time. A funding 
agreement was drafted but has not advanced as the project scope is reconsidered. 
The permittees’ experience with virtual fencing, a short grazing season, and ongoing maintenance costs 
prompted staff to evaluate whether replacement is necessary. The boundary crosses elk migration and 
winter habitat. Staff has documented wildlife trails and hair caught on fence barbs. The Gerards also 
reported six elk deaths from fence entanglement across their grazing areas this season, although those 
incidents were not all along this boundary. 
Based on this information, staff is exploring a trial grazing period without a physical boundary fence to 
evaluate whether virtual fencing can effectively manage cattle while improving wildlife movement. The 
trial would proceed only after public outreach, secured replacement funding, with monitoring and 
response procedures in place to protect neighboring properties and guide a long-term decision. 
 
 
Remains of an elk discovered after becoming entangled in a fence near Town-owned open space (July 2026). 
 
BENEFITS AND RISK CONSIDERATIONS 
Removing wire would provide the greatest benefit to wildlife movement. If a physical barrier is 
necessary, CPW standard wildlife friendly fencing would be the preferred alternative. Evaluating virtual 
fencing before constructing a replacement could conserve partner funding and reduce future Town 
maintenance obligations. The existing H-braces, corners, and post spacing cannot support the proposed 
high-tension design, so removal of the existing wire would happen either way. 
Neither physical nor virtual fencing is completely failsafe. Cattle could enter residential properties, Town 
land, or the golf course. Fence removal may also affect remedies for livestock damage under Colorado 
law. Staff would work with partners to clarify responsibilities for cattle retrieval, damage, and associated 
costs. Homeowners would receive reporting contacts and instructions before cattle return. 

Annual collar fees and uncertain future ECCD support could affect continued use. The Gerards could 
request Town assistance, or a future permittee could choose not to use collars. Any subsidy request 
would require separate consideration. The Town can advocate for virtual fencing requirements, but 
BLM retains permitting authority. 
Selected posts would remain with boundary signs, and existing seasonal closure gates would remain. 
Trail crossings and boundary signs would continue to communicate leash requirements, seasonal 
closures, hunting restrictions, and motor vehicle regulations. 
FUNDING AND STAFF IMPACT 
The Town would contract with Jones Cattle Co., with the Association reimbursing approved removal 
through wildlife protection funding. Staff is aiming to fund $20,000 worth of removal this fall of high-
priority areas identified through frequent game sightings, fur stuck in barbs, and ease of vehicle access.  
Full removal at the quoted rate would cost approximately $58,000, leaving approximately $38,000 after 
the proposed fall phase. Staff is pursuing a Youth Corps Association Grant to support future removal. 
Before removal, an agreement with the Association would secure removal reimbursement and reserve 
replacement funding if the trial does not proceed or replacement fencing is needed following the trial. 
Coordination, signage, outreach, and monitoring would occur within existing staff responsibilities and 
resources, with no additional Town funding requested. Potential future costs, including collar subsidies, 
livestock response, or property damage, remain uncertain. 
MONITORING AND COMMITTEE DISCUSSION 
Staff will work with the Gerards and ECCD to monitor cattle movement during summer 2027. An 
authorized trial would track cattle crossings, response times, damage, corrective actions, and safety 
concerns. Results would inform whether to continue without a physical fence, extend the evaluation, or 
install wildlife friendly fencing. 
Staff seeks OSRAC feedback on the phased approach, homeowner outreach, and criteria for evaluating a 
potential trial. The proposal supports wildlife stewardship, responsible infrastructure investment, and 
collaboration with community and public land partners. 
PROPOSED APPROACH 
Staff recommends proceeding cautiously through three phases: 
• Fall 2026: Remove selected wire sections where field observations indicate immediate wildlife
benefits. Work would target completion before the December 1 seasonal closure, weather permitting.
• Winter 2026–2027: Coordinate homeowner notice and input through the HOA, and develop livestock
response procedures, monitoring criteria, and funding protections with project partners.
• Spring 2027: Return for separate consideration of remaining removal and a grazing trial following 
public notice and outreach. If a trial does not proceed, adequate containment would need to be 
established before cattle return, including fence restoration where necessary. 
The Eagle Ranch Association and Metropolitan District have provided written approval to pursue a trial 
period. A formal amendment to the maintenance MOU could follow as the parties determine the 
appropriate long-term arrangement. 
One grazing season may not capture the full range of conditions, so trial results would inform whether 
to continue without a physical fence, extend the evaluation, or install wildlife friendly fencing. 
SUGGESTED ACTION 
Staff requests OSRAC feedback and a recommendation supporting development of a proposed trial 
grazing period without a physical boundary fence. The recommendation would guide further planning 
and inform any future request for approval. 
Suggested motion: 
“I move to recommend that staff develop a proposed trial grazing period without a physical boundary 
fence, including public outreach, monitoring, and secured funding for fence replacement if needed.”

Agenda Item:   OSRAC Membership and Recruitment 
PURPOSE 
Discuss upcoming term expirations, member interest in reappointment, and the proposed recruitment 
and applicant review process. 
BACKGROUND 
OSRAC currently has one vacant seat, and three members’ terms expire in December 2026, resulting in 
four seats to be filled. 
PROPOSED PROCESS 
Staff will continue to use the existing recruitment process: 
• Confirm application requirements for returning members and establish a recruitment deadline. 
• Advertise the openings through Town communications and partner organizations. 
• Direct applicants to the Town’s Commission & Committee Application Form, which collects 
information about eligibility, experience, organizational involvement, and interest in serving. 
• Provide new applications to OSRAC for review using an evaluation rubric. 
• Forward applications and Committee recommendations to Town Council for appointment 
consideration, with returning member applications reviewed directly by Council. 
Members with expiring terms are encouraged to notify staff of their interest in continuing. All members 
are encouraged to help share recruitment information and identify prospective applicants. 
SUGGESTED ACTION 
Provide feedback on the proposed recruitment and review process and discuss member interest in 
reappointment. No formal action is requested.
