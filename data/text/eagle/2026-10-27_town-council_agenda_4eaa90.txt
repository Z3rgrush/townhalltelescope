BUSINESS ITEMS  
 
1. Resolution 61, Series 2026, "A Resolution of the Town Council of the Town of Eagle, Colorado, Supporting Eagle County School District Ballot Issue 5B on the November 3, 2026 Ballot.  
  
 
Tessa Kirchner presented the item and requested Council’s support for Ballot Issue 5B. She provided an overview 
of statewide K-12 school funding, enrollment trends, staffing and teacher compensation, and local funding levels 
in Eagle County compared with surrounding school districts. She expressed that passage of 5B would bring Eagle 
County School funding to levels comparable with those of neighboring districts. 
 
Council discussed whether to take formal positions on ballot initiatives and agreed to revisit the topic during 
future work sessions to establish an approach for responding to similar requests. A motion to deny the resolution 
failed, and a subsequent motion to support the resolution passed.  
 
Public Comment:  
• Megan Orvis, 60 Mayor Street- Asked Council to support the resolution so Eagle County will not continue 
to lose teachers and force them to leave the area.  
• Matt Hood, 50 Beecher Street- Urged Council to vote in favor of the resolution, arguing that a strong 
show of support goes a long way.  
• Nicole Dewell, Eby Creek Mesa- Asked Council to support the resolution, expressing that many teachers 
are retiring and new teachers cannot afford the cost of living. 
• Michael Agostinho, 17 Adams Way Rd- Asked Council to support 5B, telling them that his school is losing 
many veteran teachers and the turnover rate of new teachers is high. 
• Rachel Glowacki, 29 Thresher Court- Expressed support for Ballot Issue 5B, emphasizing its potential to 
improve teacher compensation and retention and support local schools.  
• Lucila Tvarkunas- Voiced her support for 5B, citing the need to improve teacher recruitment and 
retention, expand student mental health services, and preserve programs such as physical education and 
music.  
• Matt Jones, 801 Brush Creek Terrace- Asked Council to support 5B, describing challenges caused by 
budget reductions, including shortages of instructional materials, persistent reading and math gaps, and 
difficulty recruiting qualified teachers due to uncompetitive salaries. 
• Laura Daly- Asked Council to support 5B, describing how shrinking school budgets have led to larger class 
sizes, reduced prep time, fewer enrichment programs, limited counseling support, and teacher stress, 
while pay freezes have affected teacher families. 
• Valerie Gross, 39 Greenhorn- She emphasized that supporting the resolution is important to families with 
children in school and to the wider community.  
• Aurora Sprincz- Argued that supporting schools is about strengthening the entire community, preparing 
future professionals, and ensuring long-term prosperity, not just supporting teachers. She urged the 
community to value education and its commitment to children, warning that failing to support schools 
would leave educators feeling unappreciated and diminish the community’s future. 
• Mary Anne Stavney- She urged the town to support school funding and that visible town support could 
influence voters to help address these challenges. 
• DJ McClary, 150 Palmer Loop- Asked Council to support 5B, arguing that investing in education benefits 
the entire community. 
• Natia Luck, 333 Hilltop St- She supports 5B and encouraged Council and community members to visit 
schools, volunteer, and witness the challenges firsthand. 
• Jeanine Kenney, 384 Hilltop- She urged the town to support measure 5B, emphasizing that visible 
community support for schools can influence voters.

2. 215 Capitol St. Property Assessment Update - Wold Architecture & Engineering 
  
 
Representatives from Wold Architecture and Engineering presented their findings on the preliminary condition of 
structures on the 215 Capitol St/St. Mary's property, staff facility needs, and cost analysis for two renovation 
options. After discussion on the presented options, Council directed the team to put together a lower-cost bridge 
solution rather than a full permanent renovation concept.  
 
 
3. 2026 Strategic Plan Quarterly Update 
  
 
Town Manager Melissa Daruna gave Council the first quarterly update on the strategic plan. Staff identified 
several high-priority projects not yet reflected in the 2027 strategic plan, including planning for the 215 Capitol 
property, Nogel Park renovations, River Park enhancements, the North Broadway corridor, and Grand Avenue 
beautification. Council was asked to decide in January what existing priorities may be delayed to make room for 
these projects. 
 
4. Public Hearing: 2026 / 2027 Budget 
  
 
Finance Director Rachel Tand presented the current 2026-2027 budget, highlighting changes made since the last 
revisions and providing an overview of each fund. Currently, every fund is balanced, operating revenues and 
expenditures are aligned, and fund-balance and reserve policies are satisfied without projected ongoing 
operating deficits. Council did not identify any new projects that needed funding.  
 
Public Comment 
• Matt Hood, 50 Beecher St- Advocated to keep funding for the bike park to at least cover maintenance 
costs. Allowing the bike park to deteriorate could cost more later. 
• Lachie Thomas- He thanked Council for making a site visit to the river park during a previous work session 
 
5. Phase 3 Investment Grade Audit Information - Distributed Solar 
  
 
Sustainability Specialist Kira Koppel and representatives from ESG provided an update to Council on the phase 3 
solar investment grade audit and options for moving forward. Council discussed the pros and cons of continuing 
with ESG or bringing the project in-house, whether solar arrays would actually increase the Town's resiliency 
Page 8 of 159
  5 | P a g e  
 
against power outages, and whether the Town should invest in solar while tax credits are available  
 
Council reached consensus to remove the 2027 solar allocations from the Town Hall and water funds rather than 
proceed solely to capture expiring tax credits. Staff will complete or close out the current analysis as appropriate, 
continue studying battery storage, return in January with a broader net-zero and decarbonization discussion, and 
recommend alternative uses for funds previously assigned to Town Hall solar.  
 
6. Holman Capital Corporation: Draft Property Acquisition Proposal 
  
Mayor Woods stepped out of the meeting.  
 
Finance Director Rachel Tand and Tim Hoffman with Holman Capital presented financing options for acquiring the 
215 Capitol Street/St. Mary’s property. After discussion, Council supported staff’s recommended financing 
approach and authorized the Town Manager to proceed.   
 
  
COUNCIL DISCUSSION AND FUTURE AGENDA ITEMS  
 
Council member Woodworth Foral proposed exploring 10 hours of enforcement per week during the December–April 
trail-closure period, either through a seasonal ranger, an agreement with the county, or additional code-enforcement 
capacity. At least four council members supported staff to investigate options and return with costs and capacity 
implications. The proposal was intended to proceed only if Open Space Manager Alex Smiley determined the task is 
feasible without affecting other priorities.  
 
Council debated further whether and how the Town should take positions on ballot measures and legislative proposals, 
distinguishing voter-decided ballot initiatives from legislation, and agreed to move the discussion to a future work 
session.

This document provides a year-to-date (YTD) budget to actual revenue analysis, including narrative comments for 
significant budget to actual and year-over-year (YOY) variances. A “significant variance” is defined as a variance 
greater than $15,000 and 10% over the prior year. However, if Council would like further detail on items below this 
threshold, staff will gladly provide it. 
 
Please review the Budget to Actual Report alongside this document. For comparative purposes, the report 
includes YTD actuals through September 30 for both 2025 and 2026 as well as the revised approved 2026 budget. 
The column showing unearned or unexpended amounts and percentages compares YTD collections or spending to 
the revised approved budget. 
 
Townwide Interest Accounts – To avoid redundancy, this comment applies to interest accounts across all funds. 
The majority of the Town’s funds are held in COLOTRUST. At the end of Q3 2026, the Townwide balance in 
COLOTRUST was approximately $65.1 million, compared to approximately $62.3 million at the end of Q3 2025. 
Interest rates have declined steadily over that same period, from 4.3% to the current rate of 3.9%. Interest 
earnings for individual funds vary based on both the interest rate and the specific fund balance throughout the 
quarter, which varies based on timing of operating and capital expenditures.

The General Fund has collected 77% of its budgeted revenues YTD, with revenue currently exceeding 
expenditures by $1,704,573. Excluding planned transfers to the Capital Improvements Fund, Open Space Fund,

Disposable Bag Fee Fund – The Disposable Bag Fee Fund was established in 2024 to manage fees collected under 
the Colorado Plastic Pollution Reduction Act (HB 21-1162). Under this legislation, retailers retain $0.04 per bag, 
and the remaining $0.06 is remitted to the Town. No revenue variances meet the threshold for comment.  
 
Exterior Energy Offset Program (EEOP) Fund – The EEOP Fund was established in 2024 following the Town’s 2023 
adoption of the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code. This fund tracks fee-in-lieu revenues collected from 
residential, commercial, and governmental projects that use exterior energy sources emitting greenhouse gases 
without onsite renewable energy offsets, as calculated by the EEOP tool. These revenues are expected to be one-
time and irregular, as property owners have the option to install qualifying energy offsets in lieu of paying the fee. 
No revenue variances meet the threshold for comment.

Reminder: Change to Reporting
Previously, we reported sales tax received  in the GL for the period indicated as well as sales tax earned 
by filing period, in the same quarterly report. Going forward, we have reworked the report to only show 
sales tax received  in the GL for the period indicated. 
The Finance team would like to thank Mark Herota for leading this project and spending many 
hours reworking the report, including pulling years of past data!
Why did we make this change?
• Eliminate confusion from reporting two different data sets and increase community understanding of our sales tax
reven
ue.
• Previously, we could not report sector or location data for the sales tax received in our GL, which made it difficult to
compa
re YOY tax received by source.
• Sales tax earned by filing period pulled in data from all prior periods that were paid late, so the numbers changed in
each 
report and were not always useful to analyze trends.
• Because we receive funds two months delayed from the filing period, we couldn’t complete this report until the
seco
nd month after the quarter end. We will now be able to complete this report the first month after the quarter
end, coinciding with our other quarterly reports.

To:  Mayor and Town Council  
From:  Melissa Daruna, Town Manager 
  Rachel Tand, Finance Director 
Date:   October 27, 2026  
Re: Public Hearing: 2026 / 2027 Operating & Capital Budget 
 
REQUEST: Staff requests that Town Council review the 2026 Supplemental Budget and proposed 2027 Budget, in 
conjunction with the staff memo below and the staff memos from the 9/15 Work Session, the 9/29 Work Session, 
and the 10/21 Public Hearing and provide feedback. 
 
INTRODUCTION: The annual budget process requires significant time and dedication and serves as a blueprint for 
Town operations to communicate Town Council and staff priorities. As stewards of public resources, it is essential 
to uphold our fiscal responsibility to our citizens and taxpayers by managing funds effectively and spending them 
in the community's best interest. The decisions presented in this document will require thoughtful consideration 
from Town Council, as outlined in the analysis below. 
 
This Public Hearing is our fourth opportunity to look at the Town’s future projected operating and capital revenues 
and expenditures. The analysis provides an overview of our budget approach, year-over-year changes and changes 
from the prior Public Hearing, the proposed 2027 Fee Schedule, and next steps . In addition, Staff have prepared 
a five-year projection for our capital project funds to assist with your analysis; please see the 10.27.26_Town of 
Eagle 2027 Fund Projections document. Staff will adjust the budget based on feedback received at this meeting  
prior to our final scheduled Public Hearing on November 10. 
 
ANALYSIS:  
 
Budget Approach  
 
Our goal is to present a balanced budget to Council, with projected operating revenues equaling or exceeding 
projected operating expenditures. Our 2027 draft budget shows a  General Fund surplus of $ 46,537, including 
transfers. In this budget cycle, we have transitioned from a conservative approach to a moderately conservative 
approach, based on feedback from staff and Town Council during the 2026 budget cycle. In the past,  our 
conservative approach resulted in actual annual revenues exceeding original budget by an average of $1.5M and 
actual annual expenditures falling short of original budget by an average of $1.1M. These variances allowed us to 
make significant transfers to the Capital Improvements Fund (CIF) but made it challenging for staff and Council to 
accurately forecast budget availability for critical investments such as personnel and contractual needs. 
 
Our prior conservative approach included calculating costs based on 100% employment of all positions throughout 
the year, which rarely occurs. Vacancies directly impact salaries and related benefits but also the ability of staff to 
complete projects with dedicated funding. New for 2027, we are including Assumed Vacancy Savings of $350,000, 
and we have also adjusted our health insurance budget to reflect actual percentages of employees likely to utilize 
our insurance. In addition, we have carefully evaluated individual department budgets to ensure we are only 
including projects that are more likely than not to occur, given current staffing and priorities.

As we transition to a less conservative budget approach, it’s important for Council to understand that accessing 
more budget dollars  now will result in less availability to make transfers to the CIF and other funds. We are 
recommending a 1X transfer to CIF of $5,600,000 in 2026 to bring the General Fund unassigned fund balance back 
into policy compliance  at 25% of total expenditures, excluding transfers. Per Council direction, $1.5M of that 
transfer will be allocated for Grand Avenue. Going forward into 2027 and beyond, we will likely not have as much 
budget surplus available to transfer.  
 
For 2027, based on preliminary revenue projections, we provided each department or f und with a 2% operating 
increase, excluding personnel costs and mandatory increased contracts discussed below. It’s important to note 
that because we implemented 5% budget reductions in 2025 and 0% budget increases in 2026, we have not yet 
returned operating budgets to their base 2024 budget levels.

development and housing, communications, code enforcement, and engineering. In addition, inflation and 
intense local competition for employees have increased the Town's personnel expenses.

Other Personnel Budget Increases 
 
• Pay Plan: The Town completed a Compensation Study Market Analysis  with Graves HR Consulting in 
September 2026. Based on this analysis, our Pay Plan grades are changing for 2027  to better align with 
the regional and statewide market. The average percentage wage increase to staff is approximately 7%, 
with all employees receiving a minimum 4% increase.  We are pleased to be able to fund the entire 
recommended Pay Plan within this 2027 Budget draft.  
 
• Health Benefits: The Town currently offers staff a PPO plan and a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). 
We received notice from CEBT of a 16% increase for both plans due to claim activity in the prior 12-
month period. The Town has historically budgeted health insurance very conservatively, resulting in 
budget savings of over $164K in 2025. As part of our new moderately conservative budget approach, 
we have been able to absorb some of this increase and keep the staff increase manageable. In addition, 
we are continuing to proactively invest in the HDHP to encourage employees to consider a more 
affordable insurance option. Claim activity directly drives our premiums, and we will continue to see 
large year-over-year premium increases while our claims remain high. 
 
For 2027, we have budgeted to keep the plan deductibles and out of pocket maximums the same as in 
2026 and will also continue to fund employee HSA accounts. As in 2026, single employees will receive 
$1,000 in their HSA account in January, and employees with at least one other person on their health 
insurance plan will receive $1,500. With the lower premiums and this contribution to the HSA, it is more 
cost effective for every single employee to be on the HDHP instead of the PPO plan. New employees 
are only eligible to join the HDHP.

We have chosen to eliminate the Health Reimbursement Account (HRA) from our benefit portfolio 
effective January 1, 2027. Under this plan, employees were eligible to be reimbursed directly by the 
Town for health insurance claim costs. While this is a benefit to employees who experience significant 
health claims, we would like to focus our limited resources on benefits that can equitably impact all 
employees versus only employees who have health claims. The benefit usage is low (only six employees 
have accessed the benefit to date in 2026), but the dollar impact is quite large, with the Town 
reimbursing $20K to date in 2026 for those six employees. 
 
Finally, we have streamlined the wellness discount program to create a more efficient administration 
process and more flexible employee benefit. Previously, employees were eligible to receive a 
$25/month discount ($300 total) on their insurance premium, requiring the Town to manage two 
different sets of rates for each type of insurance plan. We have modified the process to award a 1X 
wellness bonus of $400 in February, creating a much simpler administration process and allowing 
employees the flexibility to use the discount however they wish. 
 
• Retirement Benefits: Employee and Town retirement contributions are unchanged at 7% each for 2027.
