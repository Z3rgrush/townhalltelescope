From:   Alex Smiley, Open Space and Trails Manager  
Date:  September 29, 2026 
Agenda Item:  2027 Bike Park Funding  
REQUEST:  
Staff requests that the Town Council review the historical maintenance responsibilities and financial 
contributions associated with the Eagle Bike Park and provide direction regarding the appropriate level 
of Town funding for 2027 operations and maintenance. 
BACKGROUND:  
The Eagle Bike Park opened in fall 2023 through a partnership between the Town of Eagle and the Vail 
Valley Mountain Trails Alliance (VVMTA). The Town owns the property, while VVMTA operates and 
manages the Bike Park under a Revocable License Agreement. 
The 2023 License Agreement assigns VVMTA responsibility for operation and maintenance of the facility. 
Specifically, the agreement states that VVMTA is solely responsible for maintaining Bike Park features, 
trails, and amenities; conducting regular inspections; addressing hazards; maintaining irrigation 
infrastructure; controlling weeds and vegetation; and performing other routine maintenance activities. 
The agreement does not establish a specific annual Town contribution or a formal cost-sharing 
percentage between the Town and VVMTA 
In practice, the partnership has evolved to include direct Town funding for Bike Park maintenance. Prior 
to 2026, the Town historically provided approximately $10,000 annually to VVMTA for maintenance, 
safety inspections, volunteer coordination, and seasonal management, with this funding coming from 
the Open Space Preservation Fund. In 2025, VVMTA reported supplementing the Town contribution 
with approximately $3,100 from organizational reserves, 248 volunteer hours, and donated materials 
and equipment. 
For 2026, Town Council approved a total maintenance budget of $44,500 and shifted Bike Park 
maintenance funding to the Sales Tax Capital Improvement Fund (STCIF). Staff recommended the 
change because the Bike Park functions as a developed recreation amenity with specialized operational 
and maintenance needs, making STCIF a more appropriate long-term funding source than the Open 
Space Preservation Fund. VVMTA has submitted a 2027 maintenance budget totaling the same amount, 
$44,500.

ANALYSIS:  
Council requested additional information regarding the respective financial contributions of the Town 
and VVMTA and whether the original partnership contemplated VVMTA covering all Bike Park 
maintenance costs. 
Staff's review of the available agreements found that the 2023 License Agreement assigns maintenance 
responsibility to VVMTA, but does not contain an annual funding commitment from the Town, a 
required VVMTA cash contribution, or an established cost-sharing formula. Accordingly, the Town's 
financial participation in annual maintenance has developed through subsequent budgeting decisions. 
At the same time, VVMTA has provided substantial financial and non-financial support to the Bike Park. 
VVMTA reports that during initial construction the Town contributed approximately $190,000, while 
VVMTA contributed $35,000 directly and raised an additional approximately $150,000 through grants 
and donations.

VVMTA reports 888 volunteer hours at the Bike Park between 2024 and 2026. Using Independent 
Sector’s current estimated value of volunteer time in Colorado of $39.99 per hour, these volunteer 
contributions represent approximately $35,500 in estimated in-kind value. VVMTA also provides use of 
its mini-excavator at no charge and leverages community relationships to secure discounted materials, 
equipment, and services. 
The proposed 2027 budget maintains the same $44,500 total funding level approved for 2026. The 
largest costs are specialized professional jump maintenance, including $13,500 for spring opening work 
and $10,000 for professional maintenance visits during the summer. Together, these services account 
for $23,500, or more than half of the proposed annual budget. Other costs include ongoing seasonal 
maintenance, landscape maintenance, Trails & Stewardship Crew support, tools, signage, materials, and 
administration.

COMMUNITY INPUT:  
The Eagle Bike Park serves youth programs, local riders of all skill levels, and regional mountain bikers 
and functions as a free public recreation amenity. VVMTA's volunteer participation also demonstrates 
significant community investment in the facility. 
VVMTA reports 888 volunteer hours at the Bike Park between 2024 and 2026, in addition to donated 
equipment access, materials, fundraising, and organizational support. 
BUDGET/ STAFF IMPACT:  
VVMTA's proposed 2027 Eagle Bike Park Management and Maintenance Budget totals $44,500. Staff 
proposes funding this amount through the Sales Tax Capital Improvement Fund (STCIF). 
The proposed budget includes: 
• $13,500 – Spring opening professional jump maintenance 
• $2,000 – Spring skills-area maintenance 
• $4,700 – Ongoing summer maintenance 
• $10,000 – Professional jump maintenance, June through September 
• $5,000 – Landscape maintenance 
• $1,500 – Trails & Stewardship Crew 
• $500 – Tools 
• $500 – Signage 
• $800 – Miscellaneous supplies and materials 
• $6,000 – Administrative and indirect costs 
No specific VVMTA cash contribution is identified within the proposed 2027 budget. VVMTA's 
contribution would primarily consist of volunteer labor, equipment access, partnership coordination, 
and administration of the maintenance program. 
STRATEGIC PLAN ALIGNMENT / STANDARDS ACHIEVED:  
Continued maintenance of the Eagle Bike Park supports the Town's goals related to high-quality outdoor 
recreation facilities, community access, and partnerships with local recreation organizations. 
The Eagle Open Space and Trails Plan specifically recommends continued collaboration with VVMTA and 
other trail organizations on maintenance, development of annual maintenance plans identifying 
responsible parties and costs, and Town support for trail organizations through funding, supplies, staff 
time, and coordination. 
RECOMMENDED ACTION OR PROPOSED MOTION:  
Staff recommends that Town Council provide direction on how staff should fund Eagle Bike Park 
maintenance moving forward.

Additional Details
1. Spring Opening Jump Maintenance – $13,500
Winter freeze–thaw cycles, snow runoff, and wet conditions cause significant damage to jump faces, landings,
and berms. Reopening the bike park requires professional machinery and skilled builders to rebuild, reshape,
and compact features to safe standards. VVMTA intends to contract with Jump Doctors, a professional bike park
construction and maintenance contractor, to perform this work. This professional work early on sets the park up
for success and makes it easier for smaller crews and/or volunteers to maintain the park throughout the year.
2. Spring Opening Skills Area Maintenance – $2,000
The skills area experiences similar winter damage but requires lighter-duty reconstruction. This includes
repairing rollers, drops, skinnies, and beginner-level features to ensure the area is safe for youth riders and new
users before peak season begins.
3. Ongoing Summer Maintenance – $4,700
This funds a part-time seasonal employee dedicated to routine weekly tasks, including refacing jumps, soil
patching, compaction, trash removal, vegetation trimming, and irrigation adjustments. This baseline labor is
essential to prevent rapid deterioration under heavy summer use.Page 201 of 218
4. Professional Jump Maintenance (June–Sept) – $10,000
Monthly visits from a professional jump builder ensure that advanced features meet modern safety and
performance standards. High-level jump lines deteriorate quickly, and small shape changes can create unsafe
conditions. This contractor work is specialized, technical, and critical for risk mitigation.
Page 31 of 109
5. Landscape Maintenance – $5,000
Includes weed treatment, tree and shrub care, irrigation system servicing, and vegetation management. Healthy
vegetative cover reduces erosion, dust, and surface degradation and keeps the facility visually and
environmentally compatible with surrounding open space.
6. Administration & Management – $6,000
Covers administrative overhead, scheduling, volunteer coordination, contractor management, and
communication with the Town. This ensures consistent, professional delivery of all maintenance activities
throughout the year.
7. Tools – $500
Replacement of broken tools and purchase of new shovels, tampers, rakes, hoses, and other equipment needed
for weekly maintenance.
8. Signage – $500
Covers replacing or updating damaged signs.
9. Miscellaneous Supplies & Materials – $800
Covers any supplies or materials such as hoses, irrigation parts, fence repairs, etc
10. Admin & Indirect – $6,000
Covers management level oversight of the bike park along with a proportionate amount of VVMTA's general operating expenses

BACKGROUND: 
The Town has historically contracted with VVMTA to provide maintenance, safety inspections, volunteer 
coordination, and seasonal management of the Eagle Bike Park at a rate of $10,000/year. 
 
Over the past several seasons, VVMTA has consistently communicated that this level of funding is insufficient to 
maintain features, surfaces, infrastructure, and user safety standards expected at a destination-level bike park. 
In 2025, VVMTA again requested an increase to support an enhanced level of service. 
 
On November 21, 2025, VVMTA submitted its annual invoice for the contracted $10,000. In accompanying 
correspondence, VVMTA’s Executive Director noted that their crews had to spend approximately $3,100 of 
VVMTA’s own reserve funds to complete the required maintenance this year, in addition to 248 volunteer hours 
and donated materials such as equipment and picnic tables. These contributions represent substantial value the 
Town did not directly pay for. 
 
To achieve a consistently high level of functionality, safety, and rider experience, VVMTA developed a proposed

properly maintain the facility at an elevated standard.

ANALYSIS: 
The Eagle Bike Park is a high-use recreational facility that attracts a wide range of riders, from youth and 
beginner users to advanced riders training on dirt jumps and technical features. As regional use has increased, 
the park requires more consistent and specialized maintenance to ensure safety, durability, and a high-quality 
user experience. 
 
Maintaing a high-level bike park requires: 
• Routine inspections of high risk features 
• Frequent reshaping and compaction of soils to maintain safety  
• Facility upkeep, including signage, fencing, trash management, landscaping, and weed control 
• Volunteer coordination 
 
The proposed increase to $44,500 reflects the actual cost needed to deliver these services at a professional 
standard.  
 
1. Spring Opening Jump Maintenance – $15,000 
Winter freeze–thaw cycles, snow runoff, and wet conditions cause significant damage to jump faces, landings, 
and berms. Reopening the bike park requires professional machinery and skilled builders to rebuild, reshape, 
and compact features to safe standards. VVMTA intends to contract with Jump Doctors, a professional bike park 
construction and maintenance contractor, to perform this work. This professional work early on sets the park up 
for success and makes it easier for smaller crews and/or volunteers to maintain the park throughout the year.  
 
2. Spring Opening Skills Area Maintenance – $3,500 
The skills area experiences similar winter damage but requires lighter-duty reconstruction. This includes 
repairing rollers, drops, skinnies, and beginner-level features to ensure the area is safe for youth riders and new 
users before peak season begins. 
 
3. Ongoing Summer Maintenance – $4,700 
This funds a part-time seasonal employee dedicated to routine weekly tasks, including refacing jumps, soil 
patching, compaction, trash removal, vegetation trimming, and irrigation adjustments. This baseline labor is 
essential to prevent rapid deterioration under heavy summer use.

4. Professional Jump Maintenance (June–Sept) – $12,000 
Monthly visits from a professional jump builder ensure that advanced features meet modern safety and 
performance standards. High-level jump lines deteriorate quickly, and small shape changes can create unsafe 
conditions. This contractor work is specialized, technical, and critical for risk mitigation. 
 
5. Landscape Maintenance – $3,500 
Includes weed treatment, tree and shrub care, irrigation system servicing, and vegetation management. Healthy 
vegetative cover reduces erosion, dust, and surface degradation and keeps the facility visually and 
environmentally compatible with surrounding open space.

Covers administrative overhead, scheduling, volunteer coordination, contractor management, and 
communication with the Town. This ensures consistent, professional delivery of all maintenance activities 
throughout the year.

for weekly maintenance.  
 
8. Signage – $500 
Covers replacing or updating damaged signs. 
 
9. Miscellaneous Supplies & Materials – $800 
Any small but necessary expenses. 
 
Combined, these line items reflect the true operational needs of a modern, high-use bike park. While certain 
elements of the Eagle Bike Park could be considered compatible with some allowable uses of the Open Space 
Preservation Fund, the facility as a whole functions as a built, engineered recreation amenity with specialized 
construction, maintenance, and safety requirements that are substantially different in scale, frequency, and 
technical complexity than the Town’s natural-surface trail system. 
 
The Open Space Preservation Fund was primarily established to support land preservation, habitat protection, 
wetlands, and low-impact trail and access facilities. Funding the ongoing operations of a high-intensity bike park 
at this level would represent a disproportionate use of the fund, reducing the Town’s capacity to invest in 
restoration, wildlife management, wetland projects, and natural-surface trail maintenance and construction 
across the broader open space system. Funding bike park operations at this level through the Open Space 
Preservation Fund alone would consume approximately 10% of the department’s annual operating budget, 
limiting the Town’s ability to meet other core open space priorities. 
 
While some limited open space support may be defensible, Staff believes the STCIF is the more appropriate and 
sustainable long-term funding mechanism for the Eagle Bike Park. The Eagle Bike Park is currently the only 
recreational park amenity funded by the Open Space Fund. Moving this expense to the STCIF along with Eagle 
River Park maintenance, playground improvements, dog park improvements, and other park facility 
improvements will improve comparabiltiy and give us a complete picture of our annual operating and capital 
recreational park spend.

ANALYSIS:  
 
Budget Approach  
 
Our goal is to present a balanced budget to Council, with projected operating revenues equaling or exceeding 
projected operating expenditures. Our 2027 draft budget shows a  General Fund surplus of $ 41,802, including 
transfers. In this budget cycle, we have transitioned from a conservative approach to a moderately conservative 
approach, based on feedback from staff and Town Council during the 2026 budget cycle. In the past,  our 
conservative approach resulted in actual annual revenues exceeding original budget by an average of $1.5M and 
actual annual expenditures falling short of original budget by an average of $1.1M. These variances allowed us to 
make significant transfers to the Capital Improvements Fund (CIF) but made it challenging for staff and Council to 
accurately forecast budget availability for critical investments such as personnel and contractual needs. 
 
Our prior conservative approach included calculating costs based on 100% employment of all positions throughout 
the year, which rarely occurs. Vacancies directly impact salaries and related benefits but also the ability of staff to 
complete projects with dedicated funding. New for 2027, we are including Assumed Vacancy Savings of $350,000, 
and we have also adjusted our insurance budget to reflect actual percentages of employees likely to utilize our 
insurance. In addition, we have carefully evaluated individual department budgets to ensure we are only including 
projects that are more likely than not to occur, given current staffing and priorities. 
 
As we transition to a less conservative budget approach, it’s important for Council to understand that accessing 
more budget dollars  now will result in less availability to make transfers to the CIF and other funds. We are
