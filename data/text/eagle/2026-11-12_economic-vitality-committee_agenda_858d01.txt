• Motion to approve by Mick, seconded by Jason. Motion carried.  
 
Staff & Other Updates: 
• Downtown Development Authority Expansion: The DDA reviewed alternate-
board-seat recruitment, the proposed inclusion of properties on Block 13 adjacent 
to the district, a potential 2027 rebate program, and opportunities to expand the 
district westward and along Town-owned properties. 
o Alternate Board Seats: The DDA opened applications for two alternate seats 
to diversify board participation. Katie Bristow was identified as an applicant, 
and eligibility was clarified as requiring a business owner, property owner, 
property-owner representative, or resident within the district.  
o DDA District Expansion: The DDA approved a resolution supporting a 
petition to include properties on Block 13 at Capitol Street and Highway 6. 
The properties’ adjacency to the district was viewed as a basis for inclusion, 
which would allow the district to grow and potentially make any future 
development eligible to seek DDA resources. Other potential growth areas 
include Grand Avenue and properties near Eby Creek, Eagle River, Chambers 
Avenue, and other Town-owned parcels.  
o Rebate Program: The DDA is considering another rebate grant program for 
2027, estimated at $40,000.  
• Chamber Programs And Business Support: Michelle reported on the Chamber’s 
upcoming education series and networking events, while members discussed 
Page 4 of 8
expanding communication, promoting member events, and using customer 
experience and advocacy to support business retention and growth. 
o Education Series: The Chamber’s marketing and education series begins 
October 27 at Encore Electric’s training facility and will include seven 90-
minute sessions held bi-weekly through the third week of January. The stated 
price is $20 per session for Eagle Chamber members and $30 for non-
members.  
o Program Content: The series is designed to help business representatives 
implement a marketing program during class, with substantial use of 
artificial intelligence and digital-marketing tools. Marketing materials were 
expected to be distributed by the beginning of the following week to recruit 
participants. 
o Networking Events: Michelle dates and locations for upcoming mixers for 
the remainder of 2026. 
o Member Promotion: The Chamber can promote events for members and 
may share information without requiring the member to produce a formal 
flyer, while non-member events do not receive the same promotion. 
o Business Advocacy: The group discussed the Chamber’s roles in 
membership growth, member support, business retention, and advocacy. 
Members noted that the chamber has unique legal capacity to lobby under 
its tax status, while the chamber has used the term “action” instead of 
“advocacy” because of concerns about political connotations and limited 
understanding of the applicable statutes. 
o Customer Experience: Participants identified customer experience as a 
practical basis for encouraging local spending and business success. After 
the education series concludes in January, the Chamber may consider 
lunch-and-learns or similar programs bringing members and non-members 
together to learn ways to improve customer experience. 
 
Business & Discussion Items: 
• Economic Development Priorities And Action Planning: Nikki guided the 
committee through its economic development plan priorities, including sports 
tourism, business-process improvements, civic branding, and small-business 
advocacy, with members agreeing to focus work between meetings and document 
concrete action items. 
o Action Items: Nikki asked the group to begin ending meetings with 
documented action items and accountability owners. Because November 
and December availability may be limited, the group discussed January as 
the point for more substantial action planning, while still maintaining 
momentum through interim communication. 
o Work Between Meetings: Members acknowledged that meaningful progress 
depends on volunteers and staff completing work between meetings, 
including preparation and follow-up. The committee was encouraged to 
Page 5 of 8
identify not only what it should pursue, but also what it should defer or stop 
doing so that strategic priorities remain manageable. 
• Sports Tourism And Visitor Spending: The group discussed using tournament and 
event data to target sports visitors, prepare local businesses, and increase visitor 
spending in Eagle through coordinated marketing and event intelligence. 
o Sports Tourism Campaign: The joint effort involving the EVC and MEAC is 
developing targeted, forward-facing materials for sports tourism, initially 
centered on the Vail Sportsmanship tournament. The materials are intended 
to direct families and visitors to Eagle restaurants, retailers, and experiential 
businesses that can serve groups and generate sales tax. 
o Tournament Data: Eric agreed to help compile back-end information such 
as attendance, visitor origin, and participant profiles, including data from the 
tournament director. The committee said this information is needed to 
distinguish local participation from visitors from other counties or the Front 
Range and to validate economic-impact claims against sources such as 
Placer AI. 
o Event Coordination: Members identified additional opportunities involving 
soccer, lacrosse, baseball, volleyball, softball, trail running, mountain biking, 
and the Vail Cup and Triple Crown events. The group discussed creating a 
one-stop guide for visitors that could recommend coffee, meals, family 
activities, and other local services before, during, and after events. 
o Business Readiness: Members noted that businesses can lose sales when 
event-related demand exceeds capacity, citing long waits and visitors 
choosing to spend elsewhere. The committee discussed notifying 
businesses in advance, encouraging extended hours, and considering food 
trucks or other supplemental service options when major events create 
concentrated demand. 
o Eagle Town Park Hockey Tournament: The group discussed the post-
Christmas youth and adult hockey tournament organized by Battle Mountain 
Hockey and the opportunity to connect the organizers with the Chamber or 
DDA. The purpose would be to promote the tournament to local businesses 
and explore a nonprofit membership discount or another way to distribute 
event information. 
• Business Process Reform And Permitting: Town staff described ongoing code 
cleanup and implementation of Tyler Technologies, while the committee discussed 
reducing permitting friction, improving completeness reviews, and examining fee 
structures and development incentives. 
o Process Guide: The committee’s earlier work with Com Dev on a guide to 
navigating Town development and permitting processes was effectively 
paused while the technology decision and implementation proceeded.  
o Fees And Incentives: The committee discussed a Strategic Plan goal of 
ensuring that tax and permitting fees do not deter development. Staff 
explained that enterprise-fund fees are difficult to waive or backfill because 
Page 6 of 8
replacement funding would need to come from the General Fund, while 
some impact fees provide more flexibility. Members also identified specific 
fee structures, including fees affecting small restaurants and tenant 
improvements, as areas for further review. 
• Civic Branding Initiative: Patrick, Mick, Melissa, and Nikki agreed to coordinate an 
initial civic-branding discussion, assess whether the Town budget can support 
outside expertise, and consider inviting the book’s author to help launch the project. 
o Author Participation: Members discussed inviting the author of Civic Brand, 
Ryan Short, to Eagle for a one- or two-day kickoff. They noted that this would 
require funding and that the author might offer a municipality-sensitive price, 
but no commitment or cost was finalized. 
o Budget Availability: Nikki said the current budget included approximately 
$10,000 for strategic planning and consultants. The group was advised to 
develop a specific proposal or obtain a quote. 
• Open Rewards Program: The committee questioned whether the proposed Open 
Rewards program would generate incremental economic activity or primarily 
redistribute existing sales-tax revenue, and members favored deferring it while 
prioritizing strategic-plan work. 
o Program Evidence: The program could be intended to change consumer 
behavior and establish repeat local-shopping patterns, but its effectiveness 
remains unverified. 
o Funding Model: The proposal discussed using $20,000 to launch the 
program and potentially returning a portion of sales-tax revenue to fund 
rewards. Members raised concerns that a 20% sales-tax allocation would be 
difficult to sustain and could create an obligation that grows if the program 
becomes popular. 
o Alternative Sponsors: Members suggested that a Merchants’ Association, 
DDA, or Chamber could potentially operate a pooled local gift-card or 
rewards program instead of requiring Town staff to manage it. Such an 
approach could allow points or value earned at one business to be used at 
another while relying on participating businesses to fund the program. 
o Committee Prioritization: Several members said the program should be 
placed on hold or ranked below existing economic-development priorities 
until stronger evidence, business-community support, and implementation 
resources are available. The discussion emphasized growing the overall 
economic base. 
• Place.ai: Cal expressed interest in supporting Joel’s Placer.ai work, and the group 
agreed to provide information and clarify how the platform’s reporting can support 
more targeted economic decisions. 
o Data Interpretation: Joel’s role is intended to extend beyond reading reports: 
the team wants to understand the implied data, outcomes, and limitations of

the platform so the information can inform targeted economic decisions and 
credible event-impact narratives. 
• Meeting Schedule And Continuity: The committee agreed to maintain momentum 
with a November Teams check-in and a December social or earlier-month gathering 
rather than allowing the holiday period to interrupt communication.
