1. Minutes 
   
 
  
STAFF REPORTS   
 
1. Town Manager Update 
  
 
Council received a comprehensive review of the Town’s current organizational memberships to identify potential 
overlaps in services and benefits. While no decisions were made to discontinue any memberships, Council 
requested additional information regarding the benefits of membership with the Northwest Colorado Council of 
Governments (NWCCOG). 
 
Council also expressed interest in exploring opportunities to negotiate additional benefits with the Eagle 
Chamber of Commerce and comparing its membership benefits with those offered by Vail Valley Partnership. 
Lastly, Council requested a comparison of the roles, services, and benefits provided by three climate-focused 
Page 5 of 209
  3 | P a g e  
 
organizations: Climate Action Collaborative, Colorado Communities for Climate Action (CC4CA), and the Adam 
Palmer Sustainability Fund. 
 
  
BUSINESS ITEMS  
 
1. Sustainability and Greenhouse Gas Emissions Update 
  
 
Town Manager Melissa Daruna and Sustainability Specialist Kira Koppel answered Council questions regarding 
the progress of the Town’s sustainability and greenhouse-gas reduction goals. 
 
Council discussed how carbon sequestration is factored into greenhouse-gas inventories and net-zero targets, as 
well as which sustainability initiatives could have the greatest impact on reducing operational costs for both the 
Town and the broader community. The discussion also covered the energy demands of water and wastewater 
treatment and opportunities to improve data collection on Town vehicle usage.  
 
2. Parking in the Right-of-Way: Town Code and Enforcement 
  
 
Chief Carrie Buhlman and Code Enforcement Officer Ron Delp presented to Council on how the Town’s current 
municipal code addresses parking within the right-of-way. They reviewed the definitions of abandoned and 
junked vehicles, regulations related to trailers and campers, and the challenges associated with enforcement. 
 
Council also discussed encroachments within the right-of-way and explored opportunities to reclaim and 
organize parking within the downtown core. The discussion will continue at the next work session, scheduled for 
October 6. 
 
  
COUNCIL DISCUSSION AND FUTURE AGENDA ITEMS  
 
A council member requested a future discussion identifying where the town uses "forever chemicals" and 
evaluate the trade-off between health concerns and noxious-weed control. 
 
A council member also proposed exploring a regulated summer van-life program with designated spaces and 
hook-ups, potentially requiring registration, insurance, identification, and employment or a paycheck in 
Eagle. Staff noted that the town currently prohibits camping in vans but could consider modifying the code 
for a limited pilot.

Approving the 2027 Property Casualty Preliminary Contribution Quotation” 
 
 
REQUEST: Staff is requesting that Town Council review and approve the 2027 Property & Casualty (P/C) 
preliminary contribution quotation from the Colorado Intergovernmental Risk Sharing Agency (CIRSA). 
 
INTRODUCTION: Each year, CIRSA provides the Town with an estimated quotation for P/C insurance coverage, 
which must be accepted by October 1. Staff requested and received a deductible analysis from CIRSA for the 
prior five years of claims with a breakdown by coverage type of potential savings from changing deductibles 
and/or coverage limits. Based on this analysis as well as the premium cost for 2027, Staff did not find that it 
made financial sense to change any of our current deductibles or limits. We did increase our General Liability 
and Public Official Errors & Omissions coverage deductibles in 2026. Staff recommends keeping all 2027 
deductibles and limits consistent with 2026, as shown below. 
 
 
 
ANALYSIS:   
Premiums are determined based on the following factors: 
 
1. Performance of the Town: The Town’s claim and loss experience in the prior year. 
2. Changes in the number or valuation of assets: Assets are insured at replacement value and adjusted for 
inflation annually, with any increases or decreases impacting the premium. 
3. Budget growth: As the Town’s budget continues to grow annually, the premium increases. 
4. Industry trends: Market-wide issues continue to impact rates. CIRSA has cited catastrophic wind and 
hail events, along with significant law enforcement liability claims, as reasons for requiring higher 
minimum deductibles across all member entities, including the Town.

REQUEST: For Town Council to consider approving the proposed resolution allocating the entire balance 
of the Town’s Holy Cross Community Enhancement funds to fund, in part, the Town’s Building 
Electrification Project. 
 
INTRODUCTION: The Town has received preliminary approval from Holy Cross to use the entire available 
balance of Holy Cross Community Enhancement funds for the Building Electrification Project. To date, 
the Town has received $58,587.92 in unallocated funds and will also receive an unknown amount of 
funding for the 2026 fiscal year, accrued in December 2026. Holy Cross has authorized the Town to use 
the total accrued amount as of December 31, 2026 toward the Building Electrification Project.  
 
ANALYSIS: In 2010 the Town approved Ordinance No. 19, series 2010, granting a franchise to Holy Cross 
Energy. Article 11 of the ordinance provides that Holy Cross will dedicate 1% of its annual revenues to 
the Town, in the form of Community Enhancement funds, “designed to make a difference in people’s 
lives and the communities in which they reside”. Community Enhancement funds may be used for the 
following purposes: 
 
1) Beautification projects 
2) Energy conservation projects  
3) Equipment and technology upgrades for schools 
4) Scholarship funds 
5) Acquisition of open space and/or park land and development thereof 
6) Sponsorship of special community events  
7) Undergrounding of overhead electric and other utility lines  
8) Distributed energy resource projects  
9) Electric vehicle charging infrastructure  
10) Participation in the renewable energy purchase program 
11) Other purposes with the express written consent of Holy Cross Energy 
 
The Town is required to approve a resolution and make a formal request to Holy Cross to spend 
Community Enhancement funds. The Town may elect to conduct this process on an annual basis and 
consistently spend the funds or save funding year over year to accumulate funds for a larger project. An 
historical list of approved projects is shown on the following page.

OSRAC met on October 6 and voted 4–2 to recommend that Council support the Town-focused alignment for the School 
House Rock to Kill Bill connector, with increased resources for education, outreach, enforcement, and social trail closure 
and restoration to protect open space, wildlife, and habitat. Discussion emphasized seasonal closure compliance,

generally supported increasing resources for closure compliance, outreach, and stewardship. Staff will work to bring the 
proposed trail alignment forward for Council consideration in the coming months.

the Haymaker Trailhead restroom, where staff have obtained a quote within budget and are moving toward contracting.
