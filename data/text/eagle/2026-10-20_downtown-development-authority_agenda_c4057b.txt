PROJECT INVESTMENT PROGRAM OVERVIEW 
 
WHAT IS THE PROJECT INVESTMENT PROGRAM? 
The DDA Board of Directors considers investments in development projects within the DDA boundary 
which fulfill its Downtown Plan of Development. The DDA offers Project Investment opportunities with a 
program which gives access to Tax Increment Finance (TIF). 
 Proposals are considered on a case-by-case basis and evaluated based on the ability of the 
project to further the goals of the DDA.  
 Funding is at the discretion of the DDA Board: satisfaction of the criteria does not guarantee 
funding will be approved and, if approved, funds must be used on aspects of the project which 
have a direct community benefit and meets the goals/ strategies of the Downtown Plan of 
Development.  
 For its investment, the DDA is able to secure an interest therein. Funding is typically used to 
create façade features, public amenities, life and safety issues and/or improvements in the 
public right-of-way (ROW) that would be infeasible without DDA funding. 
HOW TO APPLY 
A. APPLICANTS MUST MEET PROGRAM REQUIREMENTS 
 Participation is limited to owners of real property within the territorial limits of the DDA, 
their lessees with the consent of the owners, or their authorized representatives. 
 Applications will be submitted to the DDA, City Planning, Building and Community 
Development staff for review and recommendation to the DDA Board. 
 Applications may be approved only by the DDA Board. Any approval will be subject to a 
financial review, project commitment, façade agreement, and grant of easement. 
Approval may require the applicant to reimburse the DDA should the applicant fail to 
comply with the terms of the agreement.  
 
B. APPLICATION TIME PERIOD 
 All applications must be in compliance and submitted by March 1st. The DDA Board will 
review project application at the April DDA Board Meeting on the first Wednesday of 
the month.  
 Project Application Responses or request for more information will come after the April 
DDA Board Meeting 
 Any money granted from the DDA Board will occur in May and June  
 
C. EVALUATION CRITERIA  
Projects will be scored by the Eagle DDA Board utilizing the Scoring Matrix.

1. Purpose 
The Town of Eagle Vacant to Vibrant Program (“Program”) encourages investment in vacant, underutilized, and 
existing commercial spaces and new commercial development by helping offset Town building permit fees 
and Town use tax associated with eligible projects. The Program seeks to reduce barriers to commercial 
investment, activate commercial spaces, improve existing buildings, support businesses and employment, 
and strengthen Eagle’s commercial tax base. 
This SOP combines the Program’s eligibility, evaluation, award, reimbursement, and recordkeeping 
procedures. The Downtown Development Authority (“DDA”) administers the Program with assistance from the 
Town Clerk and appropriate Town staff. 
2. Program Parameters

3. Code Basis and Eligible Locations 
The Program must operate consistently with the Town of Eagle Land Use and Development Code (“LUDC”). 
The LUDC organizes primary uses by zone district and identifies the applicable approval type for each use in 
Table 4.09-1. The Program therefore does not create or expand a land use right; an applicant must obtain all 
land use, zoning, building, and other approvals that the LUDC requires. 
• Eligible projects must comply with the LUDC, occur within the boundaries of the Downtown Development 
Authority, and have a proposed commercial use authorized under LUDC Table 4.09-1 based on the 
pertinent zoning classification. 
• A project does not qualify merely because it lies on a parcel within the DDA boundary. The proposed use 
and project must satisfy the LUDC and all other applicable Town requirements.

• The Program may support new commercial construction and renovation, tenant improvement, or core-
and-shell work in an existing commercial building or commercial space. 
4. Eligible Projects and Applicants 
4.1 Eligible Projects 
• New commercial construction that creates an eligible commercial space or building. 
• Renovation or redevelopment of an existing commercial building or commercial space. 
• Commercial tenant improvements, including improvements needed to occupy or reactivate an existing 
vacant space. 
• Core-and-shell improvements that prepare an existing or new commercial property for occupancy. 
• Projects that bring an older commercial building or space into compliance with current Town 
requirements, including code-related improvements, may qualify. 
• A project may qualify whether the applicant owns the property or leases the space, subject to the 
requirements below. 
4.2 Eligible Applicants 
• A property owner may apply when the owner will undertake and pay for the eligible improvements. 
• A business tenant may apply when the tenant will undertake and pay for the eligible improvements and 
holds, or will obtain, a lease for the premises. 
• A landlord may apply on behalf of a tenant when the landlord will undertake the improvements, obtain the 
applicable permits, and pay the eligible charges. 
• Applicants must remain in good standing with any applicable Town and State of Colorado licensing and/or 
registration requirements. 
• The DDA may request documentation establishing the applicant’s authority and capacity to undertake the 
project and receive the rebate. 
4.3 Lease Term 
A tenant applicant should demonstrate a lease term of at least three years. The DDA may waive or modify this 
requirement when the circumstances of the project support a shorter term and the project otherwise 
advances the Program’s goals. 
5. Application Timing 
Applicants should submit an application before signing a lease or purchase agreement and before starting 
eligible work. The DDA may consider an application submitted after one of those events when the project 
otherwise satisfies the Program requirements and the timing does not undermine the Program’s purpose. 
Applicants may not receive reimbursement for work that began before the DDA issued a conditional award. 
6. Eligible and Ineligible Charges 
6.1 Eligible Charges 
• Town building permit fees associated with the eligible project.

• Town plan review and other building-permit-related charges that the Town assesses as part of the eligible 
construction permit. 
• Town use tax actually paid on construction and building materials for the eligible project under Chapter 
3.05 of the Eagle Municipal Code. 
• Land use application fees, deposits, or consultant deposits unless the DDA expressly identifies them as 
eligible for a particular pilot period. 
• Impact fees, including street improvement, fire protection/public safety, school, park, or similar impact 
fees. 
• Tap tap fees. 
The Program calculates the rebate from the final eligible charges actually paid by the applicant, subject to the 
50% limit and the $5,000 project maximum. 
6.2 Ineligible Charges 
• Water, sewer, meter, utility, or similar fees. 
• County, State, or other governmental taxes or fees. 
• Charges, penalties, or fees resulting from work performed without a required permit. 
• Costs that the applicant did not actually pay. 
• Any charge that the Town cannot lawfully rebate or reimburse from DDA funds. 
7. Application Requirements 
The Town Clerk receives applications and confirms that each application contains, at a minimum: 
• Applicant name and contact information. 
• Property address, parcel information, property owner, and applicant’s relationship to the property. 
• Current and proposed use of the property or commercial space. 
• Applicable zone district and a description of how the proposed use complies with the LUDC. 
• Description and estimated cost of the project, including the anticipated eligible permit fees and use tax. 
• Estimated project schedule. 
• For tenant applicants, a proposed or executed lease and anticipated lease term when available. 
• Evidence of ownership or authority to undertake the project, if requested. 
• Estimated annual Town sales/use tax revenue, if applicable, and any other information the DDA requests 
to evaluate public benefit. 
• Certification that the information submitted is accurate and that the applicant understands the Program’s 
requirements. 
8. Application Review and Award Process 
1. The Town Clerk receives the application, confirms completeness, and requests missing information from 
the applicant. 
2. Community Development/Building staff review the proposed use and project for consistency with the 
LUDC and applicable building requirements. This review does not replace any required land use, zoning, 
building, fire, or other approval. 
3. The Town Clerk coordinates with Finance or the appropriate Town staff to verify the estimated eligible 
charges and confirm the remaining DDA Program balance. 
Town of Eagle – Vacant to Vibrant Program SOP (Draft) 
4. The Town Clerk prepares a project summary and evaluation for the DDA. 
5. The DDA reviews the application and decides whether to issue a conditional award, deny the application, 
or request additional information. 
6. The Town Clerk sends the applicant a written conditional award letter describing the approved rebate 
amount or maximum rebate, eligible charges, required next steps, and reimbursement requirements. 
7. The applicant proceeds with all required leases, purchases, permits, inspections, and construction work 
at the applicant’s risk. A conditional award does not guarantee payment if the applicant fails to satisfy the 
Program requirements. 
8. The DDA may deny an application that does not satisfy the minimum eligibility requirements or does not 
advance the Program’s goals. 
9. Evaluation Criteria 
The DDA shall use the following criteria on a 0-5 scale to evaluate applications. The criteria provide a 
consistent framework but do not create a right to a rebate. The DDA retains discretion to consider the 
particular circumstances and public benefit of each project.

10. Rebate Calculation and Award 
• The maximum rebate equals the lesser of (1) 50% of eligible Town building permit fees and Town use tax 
actually paid for the project or (2) $5,000. 
• The DDA may award less than the maximum based on the project’s public benefit, available Program 
funds, and the evaluation criteria. 
• The DDA may not commit more than the remaining $15,000 pilot allocation unless the DDA or Town 
Council separately authorizes additional funding. 
• If multiple eligible applications compete for the remaining funds, the DDA may reserve funds for a project, 
reduce an award, or deny an application to preserve the Program’s remaining allocation. 
• A conditional award does not obligate the DDA to pay more than the final eligible charges actually paid by 
the applicant.

11. Project Completion and Reimbursement 
9. After the applicant completes the eligible work, the applicant submits a reimbursement request to the 
Town Clerk. 
10. The reimbursement request must include proof that the project received all required final inspections and, 
when applicable, a certificate of occupancy or other final approval. 
11. The applicant must provide documentation showing the final eligible permit fees and Town use tax paid for 
the project. 
12. Town staff verify project completion and final eligible charges. 
13. The Town Clerk calculates the final rebate under Section 10 and prepares the reimbursement 
recommendation for the DDA. 
14. The DDA approves the final reimbursement. 
15. Finance processes the approved reimbursement from the DDA Program funds and issues payment 
according to the Town’s normal payment procedures. 
The Town will not issue a rebate for a project that lacks required final approvals, remains in violation of 
applicable Town requirements, or otherwise fails to satisfy the conditions of the award. 
12. No Clawback; Business Failure 
The Program does not require a clawback solely because a business later closes, relocates, or fails. The DDA 
recognizes that a completed improvement can remain valuable to the property and future tenants even if the 
original business does not succeed. The DDA may require repayment, however, if the applicant obtained the 
award through fraud, material misrepresentation, or failure to satisfy a material condition of the award.

14. Recordkeeping and Reporting 
• The Town Clerk maintains a Program file for each application, including the application, supporting 
materials, staff review, DDA decision, award letter, reimbursement request, final documentation, and 
payment record. 
• The Town Clerk maintains a running balance of the $15,000 pilot allocation and records each conditional 
award and final reimbursement. 
• The DDA should review Program performance at least annually and at the end of the pilot period. 
• Program reporting should include the number of applications received, projects approved, amounts 
awarded and paid, project types, commercial space activated or improved, and other measurable 
economic or community benefits when available. 
15. Administrative Rules 
• The DDA may adopt application forms, checklists, scoring worksheets, and other administrative materials 
consistent with this SOP. 
• The Town Clerk may request additional information reasonably necessary to determine eligibility, 
calculate the rebate, or document completion. 
• The DDA may interpret this SOP when an application presents circumstances not addressed expressly 
here, provided the interpretation remains consistent with applicable law, the LUDC, the DDA budget, and 
the Program’s stated purpose. 
• The DDA may amend administrative procedures during the pilot. Any change that requires legislative 
action or changes the Program’s funding, eligibility, or authority must receive the approval required by law. 
• The Program does not waive, reduce, or modify any land use, zoning, building, fire, health, safety, tax, or 
other regulatory requirement unless a separate Town action expressly authorizes that result.

Vacant to Vibrant Permit Fee and Use Tax Rebate Program – Two-Year Pilot Program 
Internal Policy 
Purpose 
The Vacant to Vibrant Permit Fee and Use Tax Rebate Program (“Program”) encourages the activation of 
vacant commercial or industrial spaces in Lafayette by offering rebates on City building permit fees and 
construction use taxes. The goal is to reduce vacancy, increase investment, support local job creation, and 
enhance sales tax revenue. 
Rebate Details 
• Rebate up to 50% of permit fees and use taxes with a maximum rebate of up to $10,000 per project. 
If a significant public benefit is identified, the City Manager may provide a special approval above 
$10,000 and/or over 50%. 
• Rebate applies to City building permit fees, plan check fees, fire inspection fees, and City use tax on 
construction materials and construction equipment used to make improvements under the building 
permit for an eligible project. Excludes utility fees (including meter and tap fees), stormwater fees, 
county and other taxes, and any enterprise fees.  
• Eligible project types: commercial and industrial tenant improvements, core and shell build outs, and 
new commercial or industrial construction. 
• The rebate will be determined once the project is complete and the Certificate of Occupancy (CO) has 
been obtained, or the permit is finalized and passed all final inspections if no CO is required. 
• Businesses experiencing financial hardship may request special approval for a permit fee waiver at 
the time fees are due. Proof of hardship will be required. Special approval is at the City’s discretion 
and not guaranteed. Use tax deposits are not eligible for special approval and must be paid at the 
time of permit issuance. 
• All rebates are subject to annual appropriations and funding availability. Program funding levels may 
vary from year to year based on the City’s overall financial health and revenue outlook and may be 
adjusted as needed to align with budget priorities and available appropriations. 
• Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis with funds being disbursed on a first-come, first-served 
basis, subject to annual appropriations and funding availability.  
Eligibility Requirements 
To qualify, applicants must meet the following requirements: 
• The suite, building, or parcel of land is currently vacant and zoned for commercial or industrial use 
• Applicants are required to submit an application before signing a lease, purchasing a property, or 
starting any work. In cases where one of these actions has occurred prior to applying, staff may still 
consider the application eligible at their discretion, provided the project aligns with the Program goals 
and the timing demonstrates clear intent to participate. 
• If a landlord is applying to the program, they must be making the improvements on behalf of a 
tenant. This includes pulling the permit, completing the work, and paying all associated permit fees.

• Lease term must be a minimum of three years (if leasing) 
• The business must be new to Lafayette, expanding to a second location, or relocating to a new space 
within the City. 
• Must be in good standing with the State of Colorado and the City of Lafayette. 
Evaluation Criteria 
Each criterion reflects the Program’s priorities, including attracting new businesses, supporting expanding or 
relocating businesses, fostering sales and use tax growth, and maximizing the highest and best use of 
vacant spaces. 
Projects are scored 0-5 points across five criteria for a total of 25 points: Use Type, Sales Tax Generation, 
Vacancy Duration, Highest and Best Use, and Catalytic Impact. 
The following tiers are established to determine the amount of rebate a business may receive depending on 
how many points their project scores:

Tier 1 20 – 25 $10,000+/50%+* 
Tier 2 15 – 19 Up to $10,000 (not to exceed 
50% of fees and use taxes) 
Tier 3 5 – 14 Up to $5,000 (not to exceed 
50% of fees and use taxes) 
*City Manager may provide special approval for rebates over $10,000 or greater than 50% of fees and use taxes if 
significant public benefit is found.

When filling out the rubric, staff will insert which classification the project falls under based on the following: 
Business Use Type: Measures how well the proposed use aligns with the City’s desired industry sectors and 
commercial priorities as set forth in the Comprehensive Plan and supplemental plans. Higher scores are 
given to retail, restaurant, and primary industry uses that activate the area, generate high sales tax revenue, 
or bring high paying jobs to the City. Service-based and entertainment uses that are low-impact or don’t 
generate significant sales tax revenue will score lower. In the rubric, staff will list which category the project 
falls under and can score appropriately based on the classification and specific project. The general 
guidance can be used: 
• Retail: 4-5 points 
• Restaurant: 4-5 points 
 
3 
 
• Service-based: 0-3 points 
• Entertainment: 0-3 points 
• Primary Industry: 4-5 points 
Sales Tax Generation: Evaluates the potential of the project to generate sales or use tax revenue for the City. 
A high classification generates $35,000 or more in sales tax annually; medium classification generates 
$20,000 to $35,000 annually; and a low classification generates less than $20,000 in sales tax annually. 
High sales tax generators will score a 4-5; medium sales tax generators will score a 3-4 depending on where 
they fall in the range; low sales tax generators will score a 0-2 depending on where they fall in the range.  
More specifically staff can follow these guidelines: 
• 5 points: $60,000 or more 
• 4 points: $35,000 - $59,999 
• 3 points: $20,000 - $34,999 
• 2 points: $10,000 - $19,999 
• 1 point: Under $10,000  
• 0 points: No expected revenue 
Vacancy Duration: Assesses how long the property has been vacant for. A short vacancy is classified as less 
than six months; a moderate vacancy is six to twelve months; and a long-term vacancy is a year or more. A 
long-term vacancy will score highest compared to a short-term vacancy.  
• Long-term: 4-5 points 
• Moderate term: 3-4 points 
• Short-term: 0-2 points 
Highest and Best Use: Evaluates whether the proposed business use represents the optimal use for the 
property and makes the best use of a site’s location and size, within existing zoning allowances. Staff 
discretion can be used heavily in the category as it depends on what the site formerly was, what the 
incoming use is and level of investment into the space. High: highest and best use for the proposed location; 
Medium: the proposed location is great for the use, however there could be a better use for the site; Low: the 
proposed location does not fit the use and should consider a different location.  
• High: 4-5 points 
• Medium: 2-3 points 
• Low: 0-1 points 
Catalytic Project: Evaluates the project’s ability to provide a major public benefit, has a transformative and 
activating impact on the site or commercial area, fills a community gap in retail or services, or contributes to 
community vitality (e.g. through job creation, tax base growth, area revitalization, adaptive reuse of a vacant 
space, etc.) Staff discretion can be used heavily in this category depending on the project, the area, and level 
of investment.  
A catalytic project that scores high is expected to significantly transform the proposed space and surrounding 
area by drawing consistent foot traffic, inspiring additional private investment, or filling a major community 
gap. A medium classification provides a moderate impact to the area, enhancing nearby business traffic, or 
improving visibility and activity. A low classification does not have any real positive impact on the area and is 
unlikely to trigger surrounding improvements or increased activity.  
• High: 4-5 points

• Staff may use discretion throughout the rubric and where it does not capture all nuances of a project 
• Projects not meeting minimum criteria or scoring below 5 points may be denied 
Approval Process 
1. Applicant submits a completed application with project details. 
2. Staff reviews the application, scores the project, and issues either a conditional award letter or denial 
letter. 
3. If conditionally approved, the applicant proceeds with signing the lease, or purchasing the property, and 
obtaining the necessary permits. The applicant must email a copy of the executed lease or warranty deed 
once the property has been leased or purchased. 
4. Upon project completion, the applicant submits a reimbursement form with proof that final permit fees 
and use taxes have been paid by the applicant. The final rebate amount is determined based on the tier and 
final permit fees, and will be issued within 30 days. To receive a rebate, the applicant must be the party 
named on the building permit and the applicant (not a third party) must have paid the fees and use taxes 
eligible for rebate.
