MEAC Funding Review:  
Staff briefed the MEAC members on 45 event-funding requests totaling $246,000 
against approximately $50,000 available for discretionary event funding, with members 
expected to evaluate applications using community impact, sales-tax generation, and 
sports-tourism objectives before the November 4 presentations. 
 Available Funding: The committee was told that the overall budget includes 
$103,000, but $50,000 is reserved for Showdown Town and $3,000 is specifically 
reserved for the outdoor ice rink. After those commitments, approximately $50,000 
remains for the 45 applications requesting $246,000. 
 Evaluation Criteria: Staff reminded members to apply the MEAC objectives and 
criteria, with particular attention to events that attract visitors, generate sales-tax 
revenue, support sports tourism, and contribute to the fabric of the community. 
 Funding Tiers: Staff proposed moving away from awards as small as $250 and 
setting $1,000 as the smallest event-funding amount, potentially as a future funding-tier 
structure. They also offered to continue promoting applicants on the town website even 
when they do not receive funding. 
 Questions: A member questioned whether requests such as Brush Creek 
Elementary PTO's Flannel Fest, Cub Scout activities, and other parent- or member-
focused events fit MEAC's mission. Staff explained that Flannel Fest had not historically 
been funded because it primarily serves Brush Creek families rather than bringing 
broader community or visitor impact. 
 Revenue Evidence: A member asked whether sales-tax data exists for events 
such as the Mushroom Festival. Staff said the applicant completed a post-event survey 
that would be included in the packet, but they did not have sales-tax results available 
during the meeting. 
Applicant-Specific Funding Considerations:  
Staff and committee members discussed notable applications, including new events, 
sports-tourism opportunities, prior award history, event-location concerns, and questions 
about whether previously awarded funds were used as proposed. 
 New Events: Staff discussed several new or expanded requests, including Eagle 
Arts' $10,000 request for Second Friday events, Resort Entertainment Group's 
proposed two-day bouncy-house festival at Town Park, Tim Zandy's sidewalk 
photography event, Vail Valley Entertainment's $30,000 Runoff Rodeo, and Vail Valley 
Wellness's new women's summit. 
 Sports Tourism: Staff identified sports-oriented events as especially relevant to 
MEAC's tourism objectives. They described Vail Valley Ride and Eagle Outside as 
having attracted vendors, food, bike demonstrations, and substantial participation, while 
noting that the Colorado High School Cycling League plans one race next year and is 
requesting $5,000, compared with $2,500 per race previously. 
 Runoff Rodeo: The Runoff Rodeo would take place at Eagle River Park in 
partnership with the Eagle River Coalition. Staff said the event could be valuable but 
raised a timing and safety concern that would remain the event organizer's responsibility 
to address. 
 Unused Prior Funds: Staff reported that Eagle Rising, or the DBA, received 
$6,000 for 2026 seasonal micro-events but had not yet conducted the planned activities 
by October. They said the organization is participating in the Christmas on Broadway 
collaboration and that they would need to discuss how the awarded funds may be used, 
because the current activity differs substantially from the original application. 
 Event Location: The Vail Valley Unbound Children's Business Fair requested a 
street closure, reportedly because many children would be moving around the event 
area. Staff believed the event could potentially relocate to Town Park or Brush Creek 
Park and said they would speak with Heather about the possibility; Public Works has 
raised the location question previously. 
Community Program Funding Reallocation:  
The committee clarified that the paused community-program funding primarily 
supported nonprofit operating budgets and that the former funds were redistributed 
across community art, marketing, MEAC's ice-rink allocation, Molly's budget, and 
Council discretionary funds rather than placed into a single new program. 
 Funding Pause: A participant asked whether pausing community-program 
funding was temporary and what would happen to that funding bucket. Council 
Chambers said they hoped the pause would be permanent, although future Councils 
could restore the program. 
 Prior Uses: The former community-program fund primarily supported nonprofit 
operating budgets, including organizations such as Mountain Youth and Mountain Pride, 
along with some miscellaneous efforts such as the Masonic Lodge Christmas Toy Store. 
 Reallocated Amounts: Staff stated that $7,000 was transferred to community art, 
$5,000 to additional marketing initiatives, and $3,000 to MEAC for the outdoor ice rink. 
They also stated that $15,000 went to Molly's budget and $10,000 remained with 
Council for discretionary use. 
 Discretionary Funding: The discretionary amount functions as a catch-all for 
emerging needs or opportunities. Council Chambers gave Walking Mountains' heat-
pump rebate request as an example: after its federal rebate funding was exhausted and 
demand remained high, the organization requested additional town support. 
 Economic Development Option: A participant asked whether future budgets could 
include an economic-development line item for sports tournaments, visiting clubs, or 
other activities expected to generate town revenue. Council Chambers responded that 
related mechanisms already exist through programs administered by entities such as 
EVC and the DDA, often using grants or required town matching funds.
