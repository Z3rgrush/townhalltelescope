5.4 Locally Administered Projects Program (LAPP) FFY2028 Target Modal Mix
Victoria Samayoa, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Recommend the Executive Board approve the FFY 2028 LAPP Program and 
opening of the annual One-Call-for-All for LAPP and UPWP projects.
Staff Report
FFY28 LAPP Program Memorandum
Attachments:
Ms. Samayoa stated that the FFY 2028 Call for Projects cycle starts August 19, 2026, 
and runs through October 30, 2026. She noted that LAPP Program funding is increasing 
to $35 million and provided an overview of the LAPP Guidebook improvements, Blueprint 
for Safety influences on LAPP scoring, considerations of the local match criteria on 
LAPP scoring, rolling stock as eligible projects, target modal investment mix, and next 
steps. She then announced that mandatory applicant training will be held on August 27, 
2026, from 1:00-4:00 p.m.
Bret Martin asked whether any thought has been given to giving non-Wake County 
applicants preference if no one applies for transit funds, since no one in Wake County is 
motivated to apply for that funding because of the nature of the projects that it allows. Mr. 
Rickard responded that it is not a preference in the way Mr. Martin described it. He stated 
that LAPP is set up with a target modal mix. The Selection Committee can take that 
under advisement in December 2026. In the past, TCC has requested that the Selection 
Committee consider certain things. TCC can make that motion in November 2026. The 
Selection Committee makes a recommendation to the TCC and, in January 2027, the 
TCC can make a different recommendation to the Executive Board. The Executive Board 
can also do what they want. The current system is designed to provide the most flexibility 
possible. He added that he is not sure there is an advantage to hard coding those types 
of rules.
Mr. Martin stated that, year after year, there are no transit applications submitted, yet we 
continue to say there is this percentage for it in the modal mix. Mr. Rickard responded 
that there have been conversations with the non-Wake Counties over the last year about 
submitting transit applications and that he believes there will be continued interest in 
those projects going forward. 
Mr. Martin noted that Wake County is not interested. Mr. Rickard responded that it is just 
easier for them to get funding from the Wake Transit Program.
Mr. Martin commented that he is just looking out for the other counties.
Paul Black made a motion to recommend the Executive Board approve the FFY 
2028 LAPP Program and opening of the annual One-Call-for-All for LAPP and 
UPWP projects. Emma Linn seconded the motion. The motion carried 35-0.

5.5 Clayton UZA Federal Funding Temporary Agreement
Taylor Cooleen, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Approve the temporary funding agreement.
Clayton UZA Temporary AgreementAttachments:
Ms. Cooleen provided an overview of the Clayton UZA Federal Funding Temporary 
Agreement, noting that the Johnston County/Town of Clayton local matching funds split 
may change to about 50/50 due to population split.
Bret Martin expressed difficulty reconciling how this arrangement is going to work since 
the City of Raleigh cannot legally be a designated recipient for a small urbanized area. 
The designated recipient for a small UZA, by law, is the State of North Carolina. Also, 
since JCAT is a non-profit, it is not eligible to be a 5307 direct recipient or sub-recipient, 
yet the agreement references JCAT as a sub-grantee. 
Mr. Martin asked how the funds are making it to JCAT. He noted that there are so many 
other arrangements that can be made and asked what the arrangement is here. Alex 
Rickard responded that Staff will look into that before the Executive Board meeting.
Mr. Martin noted that, when he worked at FTA, they had a similar situation in Connecticut 
where funds had to be paid back because it was considered an improper payment. 
Conrad Olmedo commented that the draft agreement was reviewed by the City of 
Raleigh’s and the Town of Clayton’s legal teams and has been vetted. The Town of 
Clayton is not set up to be a sub-recipient right now. We will be working with Johnston 
County, JCATS, the City of Raleigh, and the Town of Archer Lodge. He noted that it is a 
temporary agreement right now and that he has to defer to the legal experts that created 
the ILA as to the legality of the transfer of funds.
Mr. Martin stated that they do not understand the United States Code Title 49 though.
It was asked whether JCATS already receives 5311 and 5307 funds. To which Mr. Martin 
responded that they are eligible for 5311 funds, but non-profits are not eligible to receive 
5307 funds. They can receive 5307 funds if it is a direct project, and they can receive 
5339 funds. 
Vice Chair Kenneth Ritchie stated that Staff will look in this further before things are 
finalized.  
Kevin McCarthy asked how he should advise his Executive Board Members on this and 
what impact it will have on other jurisdictions. Ms. Cooleen responded that there will not 
be much of an impact, but she will look into it before the Executive Board meeting. 
Hopefully, the impact will be better regional transit outside and around that you can 
connect to. 
Mr. Olmedo stated that, in the next census, the Clayton UZA might merge into the UZA 
next to it. This will allow us to launch a transit study and will result in a relationship with 
the Town of Garner, and maybe the City of Raleigh. For example, the BRT Extension 
Study is going to connect from Raleigh to Garner, and Garner down to the Town of 
Clayton. He commented that he sees that as just partnerships with transportation 
planning efforts and that, as far as the distribution of funds goes, since Clayton is now its 
own UZA he does not see that money possibly being diverted from other money pots.

It was noted that this agreement sets up a framework for better future cooperation. 
Mr. Martin commented that the State also gets 5339A money for small UZAs and asked

Mr. Martin further noted that the agreement talks about bus/vehicle revenue miles going
