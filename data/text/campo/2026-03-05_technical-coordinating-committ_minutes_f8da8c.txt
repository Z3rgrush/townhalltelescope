5.  Regular Business
5.1 FAST Study 2.0 Recommendations
Greg Saur, WSP
Requested Action: Receive as information.
Staff Report
FAST 2.0 Highlights
FAST 2.0 Summary
Attachments:
Rachel Gaylord-Miles, WSP, provided an overview of FAST Transit and the development 
of the regional network and six priority corridors, and how those corridors relate to 
employment and commuter rail.
Greg Saur, WSP, provided an overview of the four freeway concepts evaluated: Bus on 
Shoulder System (BOSS), Dedicated Transit Lanes, Dynamic Median Shoulder System 
(DMSS), and Direct Access Ramps. He identified where each was planned for and 
provided illustrations for each concept. He provided an overview of the West Raleigh 
Arterial Concept and the Blue Ridge Road RED Transit Priority Lanes Concept. He noted 
that the dedicated lanes could also be BAT lanes for general purpose traffic to make 
right turns only on. This also offers an opportunity for queue jumps and transit signal 
prioritization. He commented that a rigorous traffic analysis was not part of this Study, 
but that CAMPO will be doing that analysis. The focus of this Study was to see if these 
options can fit and are reasonable, and to develop the concepts so that cost estimates 
can be calculated. He spoke regarding next steps for the Corridor and stated that plans 
for direct access to the airport will be included as part of the next study.
Responding to Neil Perry’s question, Mr. Saur stated that return on investment and cost 
per rider calculations for the various alternatives were not conducted as a part of this 
process, but that the BOSS concept typically wins out. He noted that costs for the 
DMSS concept are generally higher.
Responding to Conrad Olmedo’s inquiry regarding consideration of I-540 being used for 
transit, Mr. Saur stated that some BOSS is being considered on I-540 North, but that 
I-540 was identified as a longer-term transit network opportunity. He noted that there were 
opportunities in the Towns of Wendell and Zebulon too.
Chris Lukasina added that CAMPO conducted a regional BOSS Study in 2020-2021 and 
that the results of that Study are available on the CAMPO website for those interested.
This item was received as information only.

5.2 Regional Transit Technology Plan
Austin Stanion, GoTriangle Staff & Steven Mott, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Approve the Regional Transit Technology Plan as guiding document for future 
technology investment
Staff Report
Regional Technology Plan
Regional Technology Plan-Budget Summary
Regional Technology Plan-Engagement Summary Report
Attachments:
Austin Stanion, GoTriangle, provided an update on the Plan, noting that TPAC approved 
it last month. He spoke regarding the Plan’s intent and timeframe and credited it as a 
regional effort. He listed the six transit focus areas identified: Passenger Real Time & 
Trip Planning, Transit Service Planning Tools, Transit Signal Priority, Regionally 
Integrated Payments, GTFS Standards, and Open Data Portal. He provided details on 
each area, highlighting the Plan recommendations and cost estimates. He then 
summarized the total cost estimates, broken down by chapter, approach, and partner, 
noting that the cost savings over the next five years is estimated to be between 
$11.8-$15.6 million.
Mr. Mott acknowledged all of the partners brought together and noted that the Plan was a 
good synthesis of interests. He spoke regarding the public engagement efforts and the 
decision to initiate an additional 30-day public comment period to adhere to CAMPO 
policy. He stated that no comments had been received as of 10:00 a.m., March 4, 2026, 
and noted that comments can be submitted at www.waketransit.org. 
Mr. Stanion clarified that the actual verbiage of the motion approved by TPAC was, “to 
endorse and recommend governing board approval of the updated Regional Transit 
Technology Plan as a guiding document for future technology investment.” He noted that 
this language reflects the Committee’s intention that the plan should guide transit 
technology investments rather than serve as a stand-alone implementation element.
Responding to Bret Martin’s question regarding the differences between the requested 
action on the Agenda and in the presentation, Mr. Mott clarified that TPAC had a lengthy 
discussion on the recommendation and that the verbiage on the presentation slide is 
what they settled on.
Bret Martin made a motion to recommend Executive Board approval of the 
updated Regional Transit Technology Plan as a guiding document for future 
technology investment. Paul Black seconded the motion. The motion carried 
35-0.
