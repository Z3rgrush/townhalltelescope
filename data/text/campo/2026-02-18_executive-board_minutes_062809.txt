6.3 FFY 2027 LAPP Program
Alex Rickard, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Conduct a public hearing and consider approval of the FFY 2027 LAPP 
Investment Program  
Staff Report
FFY27 LAPP Investment Program
FFY27 LAPP Selection Panel Discussion
Atlantic Ave MUP Questions
FFY27 LAPP Recommended Projects Map
Attachments:
Mr. Rickard provided an overview of the 19 projects submitted for the FFY 2027 LAPP 
Program, the LAPP Program and its target modal mix, the Project Selection Process and 
evaluation philosophy, and the “50% Rule” and options for when it is not met. He stated 
that the Selection Committee recommended funding the top four scoring roadway 
projects (Rolesville at Riley Hill-Realignment, Holly Springs Road-Central, Youngsville 
Bypass East-Cedar Creek Straightening, and Morrisville Parkway Intersection 
Improvements), for a total of $22,426,165. He noted that the January recommendation for 
the bicycle/pedestrian project submittals was to fund the top scoring project (Atlantic 
Avenue Multi-Use Path) and the third top scoring project (Vandora Springs SRTS), as 
there was not enough funding for the second top scoring project (Smith Creek Greenway). 
Upon request from the TCC for Staff to review all projects to ensure that funds could be 
obligated on time, Staff determined that all projects met that requirement. Staff went 
back to the Selection Committee to recommend expanding funding to include the Smith 
Creek Greenway Project. The Selection Committee agreed and is now recommending 
funding of all three top scoring bicycle/pedestrian projects. He noted that no transit 
projects were submitted. He then compared the target versus recommended percent 
modal investment mix and noted that the amount being recommended above the Board’s 
target recommendation is $8,302,900. He stated that funding for those additional 
investments was identified upon further review of anticipated federal funds, which are 
expected to increase as the population increases. We feel comfortable now that a $33 
million investment program can be funded. In Spring 2026 there will be discussion on 
raising the $25 million LAPP Program target, possibly to $30 million. He added that the 
LAPP projects approved here will be incorporated into Amendment #2 to the FY 
2026-2035 Transportation Improvement Program, which is the following Agenda item. He 
noted that there will be mandatory LAPP Project Manager training scheduled for March 
2026 for all funded projects.
Chair Butch Lawter opened the public hearing.
Elizabeth Brunner expressed user-friendliness concerns with the volume of some of 
these documents and the use of acronyms and links to various other documents and 
requested that work from older cycles of public engagement be carried over and 
feedback be re-reviewed to identify comments that may cross-apply to other 
projects/areas.
Upon seeing no further public comments, Chair Lawter closed the public hearing.
Board Member Scott Brame commented that the Town of Youngsville is excited about 
their roadway project, noting that the reduction of traffic congestion is their top priority.
Board Member Scott Brame motioned to approve the FFY 2027 LAPP Investment

7.5 Wake Transit Conference Committee Update
Ben Howell, CAMPO Staff
Requested Action: Receive as information.
Staff Report
CAMPO Notice of Significant Concern
Attachments:
Mr. Howell provided background information on vehicle rental taxes (VRT) and an 
overview of how GoTriangle has historically levied them, how the Wake Transit Plan has 
historically included an assumption for including VRT as a revenue source for the Plan, 
how VRT proceeds were distributed amongst the Wake, Durham, and Orange County 
Transit Plans from FY 2018-FY 2023, the Wake Transit Governance Interlocal 
Agreement (ILA), the Wake Transit Master Participation Agreement (MPA), the 
Significant Concern Process (Section X of ILA), the makeup of the Conference 
Committee, Committee facilitation and staff resource responsibilities, the Committee’s 
decision-making process, GoTriangle’s outside Counsel’s legal opinions, and the 2035 
Wake Transit Plan Financial Model Projections. He provided an update on the 
Conference Committee, highlighting its formation in 2024 to address the following three 
Significant Concerns:
   1.  What “portion” of the VRT revenue collected by GoTriangle will be allocated to 
Wake Transit in Fiscal Year 2025?
   2.  What “portion” of the VRT revenue collected by GoTriangle will be allocated to 
Wake Transit in the ten-year period from Fiscal Year 2026 to Fiscal Year 2035, 
effectively setting capacity assumptions for the next update of the Wake Transit Vision 
Plan?
   3.  The financial outlook for GoTriangle's Authority-controlled Funds, including the 
actual Fiscal Year 2023 financial results, Fiscal Year 2024 Budget, and long-range 
financial projections, which CAMPO understands as the driver for GoTriangle’s plans to 
recapture all VRT Revenues.
Mr. Howell continued by summarizing the Conference Committee’s 2024 meetings, the 
settlement of Significant Concern 1 by agreeing to include $2.528 million in VRT revenue 
for FY 2025 and to revise the Financial Assumptions to show “TBD” for VRT revenue in 
future years, the agreement to go to mediation for Significant Concerns 2 and 3, and the 
2024 mediation session. He provided an overview of the Wake Transit Work Plans and 
the FY 2024, 2025, and 2026 agreements regarding VRT revenue allocation and inclusion 
in the Wake Transit Work Plan. He next summarized the current status of the 
Conference Committee, noting that it has not met since June 2025 as it is waiting for 
additional information. He provided an overview of the next steps, stating that the 
Committee is scheduled to meet on February 23, 2026, March 26, 2026, and May 19, 
2026, and that it will be provided the additional information requested from GoTriangle, 
CAMPO, and Wake Transit, and that the goals of these meetings will be for the 
Conference Committee to provide guidance to TPAC on VRT revenue allocation for the 
FY 2027 Work Plan and for the Conference Committee to discuss and recommend a 
long-term solution for allocation of VRT revenue to the Wake Transit Plan. He noted that 
a Wake Transit Work Plan amendment may be required, depending on these 
discussions.
Chair Butch Lawter noted that CAMPO’s two Conference Committee representatives are 
Vice Chair TJ Cawley and himself. He stated that he prefers maintaining continuity, but 
will listen if another Board Member has a strong desire to serve on the Conference 
Committee.
