6.1 Regional Transit Technology Plan
Austin Stanion, GoTriangle Staff & Steven Mott, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Approve the Regional Transit Technology Plan as guiding document for future 
technology investment
Staff Report
Regional Technology Plan
Regional Technology Plan-Budget Summary
Regional Technology Plan-Engagement Summary Report
Attachments:
Austin Stanion, GoTriangle Staff, provided an update on the Plan, noting that TPAC 
approved it last month. He spoke regarding the Plan’s intent and timeframe and credited 
it as a regional effort. He listed the six transit focus areas identified: Passenger Real 
Time & Trip Planning, Transit Service Planning Tools, Transit Signal Priority, Regionally 
Integrated Payments, GTFS Standards, and Open Data Portal. He provided details on 
each area, highlighting the Plan recommendations and cost estimates. He then 
summarized the total cost estimates, broken down by chapter, approach, and partner, 
noting that the cost savings over the next five years is estimated to be between 
$11.8-$15.6 million.
 
Mr. Mott spoke regarding the public engagement efforts and the decision to initiate an 
additional 30-day public comment period to adhere to CAMPO policy. He stated that no 
public comments had been received. 
Responding to Board Member Stephen Morgan’s question, Mr. Stanion stated that trip 
planning and payments would probably not extend to trains in the next year or two, but 
that it is a goal for the future. We want to add BRT and train services. We are in 
communication with NC by Train to get a code to activate the Umo app benefit for riders 
getting off at RUSS. Board Member Morgan requested that he ensure that any vendors 
chosen are not exclusive and preclude fare payment integration in the future. Mr. Stanion 
responded that he will do so.
Board Member Patrick Hannah motioned to approve the Regional Transit 
Technology Plan as a guiding document for future technology investment. Board 
Member Jacques Gilbert seconded the motion. The motion carried 20-0.

6.2 FAST Study 2.0 Recommendations
Greg Saur, WSP
Requested Action: Receive as information.
Staff Report
FAST 2.0 Highlights
FAST 2.0 Summary
Attachments:
Rachel Gaylord-Miles, WSP, provided an overview of FAST Transit and the development 
of the regional network and six priority corridors, and how those corridors relate to 
employment and commuter rail.
Greg Saur, WSP, provided an overview of the four freeway concepts evaluated: Bus on 
Shoulder System (BOSS), Dedicated Transit Lanes, Dynamic Median Shoulder System 
(DMSS), and Direct Access Ramps. He identified where each was planned for and 
provided illustrations for each concept. He provided an overview of the West Raleigh 
Arterial Concept and the Blue Ridge Road RED Transit Priority Lanes Concept and 
summarized next steps for the Study.
Board Member Patrick Hannah asked if any risk mitigation studies had been conducted 
comparing models utilizing inside versus outside lanes. Mr. Saur responded that there 
are two pieces to consider, bus speed and exposure for buses interacting with traffic. He 
commented that, generally speaking, outside shoulders that intersect with vehicles at 
ramps are the least safe portion of a corridor; however, those collisions are at lower 
speeds and have less severe impacts. There is not a lot of safety data comparing the 
risks between the two models.
Responding to Alex Rickard, Mr. Saur confirmed that the DMSS illustration represents 
the current ROW on I-40, near Trinity Road.
This item was received as information only.
