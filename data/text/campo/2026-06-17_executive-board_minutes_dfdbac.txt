7.2 SPOT 8 Update - Statewide Programming & Regional Impact Target 
Modal Mixes
Daniel Spruill, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Approve the Regional Impact target modal mix
Staff ReportAttachments:
Mr. Spruill provided an overview of the SPOT process, evolution of the SPOT 
prioritization process, SPOT actions and where we are at, STI programming process and 
funding buckets, the different regions and divisions CAMPO competes in, STIP funding 
distributions, revised P8 schedule, draft Statewide programming, project development 
influences and why some projects are included earlier in the process, local input points 
allocation, draft funding availabilities and their likelihood of changes, recommended P8 
modal allocations, non-highway funding competition and how it is allocated, CAMPO’s 
adopted methodology for local input point allocation to maximize funding potential, the 
redline projects list and funding amounts in each area, STI-Region C funding needs 
versus projected 10-year available funding, and the recommended Regional Impact target 
modal mixes.
Responding to Board Member Stephen Morgan’s question, Mr. Spruill stated that CAMPO 
does break some projects up; however, that does not always mean the project will 
receive a higher score. It can also diminish some of the project benefits, especially in the 
Statewide tier. Alex Rickard added that, as projects get farther along in the TIP and 
design process, they might be broken up with schedule changes, but it is too far out to 
see if any of the recommended projects will be broken up. 
Chair Butch Lawter Jr. noted that, for new members, this is a lot of information and that 
he hopes they will better understand with time.
Mr. Rickard commented that the two guiding principles for project selection are how much 
funding is available in each competition and how high a score needs to be to be in the 
running. What it comes down to is what is reasonable and practical, and common sense 
math.
 
Responding to Board Member Russ May’s inquiry into how population is taken into 
consideration when scoring projects, Mr. Rickard stated that traffic volume is the most 
significant metric when it comes to roadway project scoring.
Board Member May expressed his concern with larger communities sucking this money 
up and the smaller communities’ needs remaining unmet. He stated that there has to be 
a better way to do this and help the smaller communities. He hopes CAMPO can help 
them find those ways. He noted that he is not being critical of CAMPO, but that he is 
frustrated with the lack of equity in the process. He spoke regarding efforts he and others 
are undertaking to write a white paper on the issue.
Board Member Deans Eatman stated that the most important thing to note is that the 
North Carolina General Assembly funds transportation and sets the processes and equity 
built into the system.
Responding to Board Member May’s question regarding how smaller communities are 
supposed to compete with two big MPOs in the area, Board Member Eatman stated that 
the conversation regarding the scoring system is ongoing and is actively managed every 
year through the Legislature and NCDOT. He noted that many metrics and a lot of data

Board Member Deans Eatman motioned to approve the Regional Impact target
