5.2 FFY 2027 LAPP Program
Alex Rickard, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Recommend Executive Board approval of the FFY 2027 LAPP Investment 
Program  
Staff Report
FFY27 LAPP Investment Program
FFY27 LAPP Selection Panel Discussion
Atlantic Ave MUP Questions
FFY27 LAPP Recommended Projects Map
Attachments:
Mr. Rickard spoke regarding the previous Staff direction to review the projects to ensure 
that they can be obligated on time and noted that, based on the information submitted 
and the LAPP rules, the projects are scheduled to be obligated on time. He provided a 
recap of the project submittals, noted that the FFY 2027 Target Modal Mix is the same 
as the last six years, and provided a review of the selection process and the 50% Rule. 
He stated that the Selection Committee is recommending funding for the top four scoring 
roadway projects: Rolesville at Riley Hill – realignment, Holly Springs Road – Central, 
Youngsville Bypass East – Cedar Creek Straightening Submittal 2, and Morrisville 
Parkway Intersection Improvements. He noted that the original Board-Adopted Target 
Modal Mix was $25 million; however, it was determined that CAMPO’s LAPP funding was 
going to increase. He commented that Staff does not know the exact amount yet, but 
that they felt comfortable taking the mix up to $27-28 million. Based on the January 8, 
2026, TCC meeting comments, the LAPP Committee was informed that the mix could be 
increased further, to $33 million. This would allow for full funding of the bicycle/pedestrian 
Smith Creek Greenway Phases 2 and 3 Project. The Committee unanimously approved 
the funding of this additional project. He compared the target versus recommended 
percent modal investment mixes. He commented that FFY 2025 programming is 
expected to come before the LAPP Committee for consideration of raising the mix. There 
are no FFY 2028 numbers available yet; however, Staff and the LAPP Committee are 
comfortable that CAMPO can cover the full $33 million recommendation.
Emma Linn made a motion to recommend Executive Board approval of the FFY 
2027 LAPP Investment Program. Bret Martin seconded the motion. The motion 
carried 36-0.

5.8 Congestion Management Process - Status of the System FY 2025 Report
Evan Koff, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Receive as Information
Staff Report
CMP Status of the System
Attachments:
Mr. Koff provided a brief overview of the report and noted that more information will be 
provided at the MPO 101 training. He added that, if there is interest, we can also do a 
more detailed lunch and learn. He provided an overview of how the Status of the System 
is developed and evaluated and the requirement for the Congestion Management Process 
(CMP) to match the MTP. He announced that, in the next couple of months, CAMPO is 
going to try integrating quarterly bottleneck reports. He reported on what has happened 
this year on the CMP Network. He commented that the Raleigh/Cary Urbanized Area has 
the least congestion in the country for areas with populations over one million. He noted 
the major bottleneck locations and related planned projects, primary drivers of 
congestion, total cost of congestion and percent of total delay, congestion’s impact to 
freight on CMP interstates, CAMPO’s Truck Travel Time Reliability Index and target, 
Transit’s impact on congestion, congestion’s impact on public health, and next steps in 
the process. 
Responding to Melanie Rausch’s question, Mr. Koff stated that micromobility was not 
looked at, but that it can be as we move forward with the CMP Update.
Neil Perry spoke regarding an incident where he was caught in a long line of traffic 
caused by a bus. He stated that transit can cause more congestion than it fixes when 
buses are not full, and that bus pullout areas are needed in congested corridors. Mr. Koff 
responded that we are exploring more dedicated right-of-way or pullout areas on those 
corridors. He added that he was pleased that the region is addressing the issue by 
increasing investment in transit, rather than reducing transit. Mr. Perry commented that 
there is no right-of-way or room for pullouts along some arterial roads. He stated that the 
buses are not full, and that they are causing more harm than good.
Melanie Rausch spoke from the perspective of bus drivers and their difficulty in being let 
back in after they pull out. She noted the possibility of technical solutions or other ways 
to prioritize buses getting back in the lane.
Bynum Walter noted that the same is true for single occupancy vehicles on the road and 
that it only takes two people on a bus to make it more efficient than a vehicle.
Mr. Koff noted that there are many factors that impact congestion and the need to look at 
the bigger picture and understand the actual numbers and conditions.
Bret Martin commented that 12 people on a bus is pretty good and that it is 12 possible 
vehicles off the network. He noted the need to think differently about how transit is 
contributing to congestion.
Paul Black stated that there is a perception that cyclists also slow traffic, but that both 
buses and cyclists do not actually slow people down as much as they think.
Chair Tracy Stephenson concluded the discussion, noting that the matter would be a 
good side discussion.

5.9 Cape Fear River Basin Wildlife Crossing Study Update
Caleb Allred, MPO Staff
Requested Action: Receive as information
Staff ReportAttachments:
Mr. Allred provided an update on the Study, beginning with its history, goal, and objective, 
followed by an overview of the schedule, the data being collected, how hotspots will be 
analyzed, the Site Assessment Form, anticipated deliverables, examples of other MPOs’ 
studies and outcomes, and next steps.
Conrad Olmedo stated that there is some interest in Johnson County regarding wildlife 
crossings and noted the potential opportunity for additional studies outside this Basin.
Responding to Mr. Olmedo, Mr. Allred stated that this Study will not be recommending 
treatments as more ecological study is required first. He commented that some general 
treatment options include bridges, fencing, signage, or combinations of these options. 
Responding to Barry Baker, Mr. Allred stated that he was not familiar with any similar 
studies outside of the Triangle West TPO (TWTPO) and French Broad River MPO 
Studies.
Thanh Schado stated that Chatham County requested a Wildlife Crossing Study 
matching TWTPO’s work and noted that one of TWTPO’s recommendations was to add 
small rocks under river bridges for frogs and turtles to cross.
