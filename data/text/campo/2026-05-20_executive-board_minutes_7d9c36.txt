7.1 NCDOT Confident Scope & Cost Estimate (CSCE) 
Brian Wert, PE, NCDOT STIP Central Region Manager 
Requested Action: Receive as information  
Staff Report
CSCE Status - April 28, 2026
Attachments:
Brian Wert, NCDOT,  provided an overview of what the CSCE is and what it applies to, 
expected goals and outcomes, the appropriate time to document CSCE based on project 
type, status updates on projects currently scheduled for delivery, the September 30, 
2027, deadline and how it was selected, and steps taken since the January 2026 
meeting.
Mr. Wert presented a project status update on CAMPO projects in Division 4. He noted 
that U-6208 was listed as TBD as the work has just been completed and is being 
reviewed. 
Mr. Wert presented a project status update on CAMPO projects in Division 5. He noted 
that EB-5718 was listed as TBD due to the uncertainty regarding confluence with the BRT 
project and commented that the City of Raleigh wants to identify things first, which is 
delaying this project. He spoke regarding separate consideration for signal systems 
projects, such as U-6119 and U-6194, due to the speed of changing technologies. 
Responding to Alex Rickard’s question regarding the Apex signal system project status, 
Mr. Wert stated that the Apex project is listed as “Likely to Meet” because the project is 
closer along to delivery.
Mr. Wert presented a project status update on CAMPO projects in Division 8, followed by 
a summary of the overall CAMPO project statuses.
Responding to Board Member Deans Eatman’s question regarding whether the 
September 30, 2027,   deadline is a hard deadline, Mr. Wert stated that there will need to 
be some case by case reviews. In response to Board Member Eatman’s follow up inquiry 
as to what will happen after the deadline, Mr. Wert stated that NCDOT is focusing on 
those projects scheduled for delivery now. Going forward, NCDOT views the CSCE 
milestone as the gatekeeper for moving from that scheduled for PE only status at the 
back end half of the STIP, to the scheduled for delivery front half of the STIP. He 
commented that how this will work out in the next round of prioritization will have to be 
worked out. There will probably be a transition round before a fully implemented round.
Responding to Chair Butch Lawter, Jr., Mr. Wert stated that the CSCE milestone will 
generally be met at the 25% design review plans part of the process for most projects. 
He noted that there will also be a CSCE sign off requirement from the MPOs.
Responding to Chair Lawter’s inquiry regarding what happens if bids on signed off 
projects come in much differently from the estimates, Mr. Wert stated that NCDOT will 
review the bids to determine why they are off. That is the purpose of the CSCE. He added 
that, lately, project estimates and bids are coming more in line with each other. 
Mr. Rickard asked Mr. Wert if it was safe to say that there will always be variation 
between estimated and actual costs and that the CSCE process is meant to avoid 
ballooning projects. Mr. Wert responded that he did not disagree with the intent behind 
that. If we all agree on what the project should encompass before we get to that stage,

10.  Informational Item:  Staff Reports
MPO Report:
Chris Lukasina reported the following:
   •  Yesterday was the North Falls Lake Area Study Open House; we received great 
community input.
   •  The NCAMPO Conference was two weeks ago. CAMPO was well represented, with 
several Staff members presenting on the good work that is being done here in the region. 
   •  Please remember to sign in if you have not already.
   •  At the federal level, the next version of the Transportation Reauthorization has been 
introduced in the House as the Build America 250 Act. The Senate has not introduced 
anything yet, but we may see something in the next couple months. The Act is a large 
document, and we are still reviewing it. Some highlights include:
         o  An overall increase in the amount of federal transportation funding through either 
formula funding or competitive grants available at the local and regional level. 
         o  A 9.5% increase in MPO operations and planning funding, with a 3% year over 
year increase that will enable us to reduce some of the STP funds used to supplement 
our budget. It also expands how those funds can be used in a couple of areas, including 
going beyond feasibility studies and preliminary design work. This will help accelerate us 
into the project development process. 
        o  Enablement of MPOs to become direct recipients of some federal funding, which 
currently flows through NCDOT, to streamline the process.
         o  Ensures direct access to the Federal Financial Management System so we can 
see what is happening with all of our projects and their federal funding statuses. That will 
help with our LAPP program as well. 
         o  An increase to about $9 billion annually to competitive programs like the bridge 
formula program.   
         o  The RAISE and BUILD Grant and Safe Streets for All are also included with 
regular funding.
         o  Addition of a new Surface Transportation Accelerator Grant Program.
         o  Several studies would be required to be completed at the federal level, looking 
at efficiencies and streamlining regulations to accelerate project delivery.
