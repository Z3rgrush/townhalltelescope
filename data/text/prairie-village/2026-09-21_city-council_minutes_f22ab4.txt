Consider amendments to Chapter XI, Article 2 – Conversion Therapy of Minors Prohibited 
 
Mr. Aggen stated that a Colorado law drafted by the state intending to regulate conduct by 
counseling providers was focused on prohibiting what was often referred to as “conversion 
therapy”. A licensed counselor in Colorado, Kaley Chiles, who practiced exclusively 
through talk therapy, challenged Colorado’s conversion therapy law, arguing that the law 
regulated speech, and that it violated her First Amendment rights by restricting certain 
counseling conversations. After a legal challenge and different analysis by a lower court, 
the Supreme Court ultimately reviewed the case and held that the law should be analyzed 
under tests related to first amendment free speech rights, as the Supreme Court 
determined that the underlying law attempted to regulate speech.  
 
The Kansas Attorney General recently sent a letter to the City of Prairie Village and two 
other jurisdictions in the state that had previously enacted conversion therapy bans similar 
to the one in Colorado. The letter shared the Supreme Court’s decision, and demanded 
that the Kansas jurisdictions come into compliance by September 21, 2026. 
 
Since the Chiles decision, the State of Colorado enacted new legislation aiming to reframe 
its restrictions into a viewpoint neutral framework. The legislation seeks to prohibit any 
forced or predetermined therapy outcome, while appearing to permit neutral, client-
directed counseling. Mr. Aggen said that the amended language included in the city’s 
proposed ordinance was also viewpoint neutral, but there could still be further challenges 
based on other theories. 
 
The council committee of the whole previously voted to recommend approval of the draft 
ordinance at its September 8 meeting by a vote of 7-4. 
 
Mr. Nelson made a motion to approve Ordinance 2523, an ordinance amending Chapter 
XI, Article 2 – Conversion Therapy of Minors Prohibited. The motion was seconded by Mr.

Consider approval of agreement and easements related to the Johnson County 
Wastewater improvement project at Porter Park 
 
Mr. Bredehoeft stated that on August 17, 2026, Johnson County Wastewater (JCW) 
representatives presented to city council their future improvement needs at Porter Park. 
Given future demands for the wastewater system, JCW will need to rebuild the pump 
station currently located near the southwest part of the park. The new building will require 
a small five-foot-wide piece of land to be acquired by JCW so that the entire building's 
footprint will be on land owned by JCW. There are other permanent easements needed for 
piping and fencing as well as a temporary construction easement in the area of the south 
baseball field. 
 
Patrick Denning, Assistant Chief Engineer with Johnson County Wastewater, shared a 
presentation with details about the project. He stated that the existing pump station at 
Porter Park had been built in the 1950s, and that during periods of rain, defects caused 
water to leak into the system.  
 
Mr. Denning said the proposed timeline would begin with design work in 2027 and 
construction in 2028 and 2029. The new station would provide additional pumping 
capacity, allow JCW to make future wet-weather improvements, and meet all current 
codes. The proposed building would sit next to the existing station, which would continue 
to function until the new facility became operational, and then be demolished. JCW would 
need to acquire approximately 854 square feet of city property, along with a sewer 
easement of 3,224 square feet and a sanitary sewer pump easement of 2,175 square feet. 
Construction materials would need to be stored on the baseball field on the south side of 
the park during construction. After construction completed, JCW would restore the field. 
 
Mr. O’Toole made a motion to approve the agreement and easements for the Johnson 
County Wastewater improvement project at Porter Park as presented. The motion was 
seconded by Mr. Reddell.  
 
Ms. Lawrence asked for assurances that there would be as little impact as possible to trees 
in the construction area. She also asked how much noise would be generated by the new 
pump station. Mr. Denning stated that there were five trees that could potentially be 
impacted by construction, but that the goal was to avoid affecting as many of them as 
possible. He also said that there had not been any noise complaints about the existing 
pump station, and that he did not expect there to be any once the new station became 
active. 
 
After further discussion, the motion passed 11-0.

Discuss approval of MOU with Johnson County Library 
 
*Mr. Logan recused himself from the discussion and vote due to his firm’s legal 
representation of the Johnson County Library, and left the dais. 
 
Mr. Bredeheoft said that on June 15, 2026, Johnson County Library (JCL) officials 
presented their "test fit" for a potential new library to be constructed on city property 
adjacent to the current city hall building. Following that discussion, it was determined that 
an initial MOU was necessary for the library to continue developing the "test fit". He noted 
that the MOU did not commit the city to the project, but did formalize the city’s commitment 
to advance the studying of this option. The continued study of the new building is expected 
to take about six months, at which point the city and library could enter into a formal 
development agreement which would commit both parties to the project.  
 
Mr. Bredehoeft shared highlights from the MOU:  
 
1. The MOU is non-binding. The development agreement is expected within 180 days.  
 
7 
 
2. Shared parking in the current city-owned parking lot: JCL will be building parking on 
the floor level of their new building but will also need shared parking at existing city-
owned parking lots.  
3. JCL and the city will both pay for maintenance of common facilities like landscaping 
and driveways.  
4. There will not be a separate first responder lane. The existing driveways will function 
like they currently do.  
5. Land purchase: value will be based on appraisals and mutually agreed upon. 
 
If approved, the next steps would be for the library to work with their design firms to develop 
plans showing how the proposed building would fit on the land.  
 
Mr. Sellers made a motion to recommend approval of the MOU with the Johnson County 
Library. The motion was seconded by Mr. Nelson.  
 
Mr. O’Toole asked if it would be beneficial for the city and the library to agree on the value 
of the land on which the new building would sit before proceeding with the MOU. Mr. 
Jordan stated that he felt it would be premature to discuss land value before the council 
had approved the MOU and given staff the authority to negotiate with the library. 
 
Mr. Vallette asked how the appraisal process would work. Mr. Bredehoeft stated that each 
party would get an appraisal, and then negotiate based on the results. 
 
Mr. Jordan added that if approved by the council, the MOU would be considered by the 
library board the following week. 
 
After further discussion, the motion passed 10-0.  
 
*Mr. Logan returned to the dais after the vote was taken.
