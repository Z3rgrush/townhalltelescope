CITY OF PRAIRIE VILLAGE 
Proclamation 
National Co-Responder and Crisis Responder Week      
September 13 – 19, 2026 
 
WHEREAS, Prairie Village, Kansas, has continued to address the rising 
demand for mental health and substance use services; and 
 
WHEREAS, mental health and substance use challenges can lead to 
homelessness, incarceration, and emergency room visits, which are more 
intrusive and costly for communities than earlier interventions and 
connection to behavioral health services; and 
 
WHEREAS, Prairie Village, Kansas, has recognized the need for additional 
resources for individuals experiencing mental health crises and identified 
co-responders and police department training on crisis intervention, as an 
emerging, effective practice; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Johnson County Mental Health Center’s Co-Responder 
Program was established in 2011 to embed mental health clinicians with law 
enforcement agencies to respond collaboratively to individuals in crisis, 
provide the right intervention at the right time to avoid arrests, emergency 
room visits, and repeated 911 calls by connecting to appropriate resources; 
and 
 
WHEREAS, today, the co-responder program has 23 co-responder 
positions, 2 crisis case managers, and 16 partnerships with local law 
enforcement agencies, two of which are embedded with the Prairie Village 
Police Department;  
 
NOW THEREFORE, I, Eric Mikkelson, Mayor of the City of Prairie 
Village, in recognition of their service to our community, the week of 
September 13 th as National Co-Responder and Crisis Responder Week in 
the City of Prairie Village, Kansas.

Whereas, National Hispanic Heritage Month takes place September 15 
through October 15 every year as a time to recognize and celebrate the

and acceptance.

COUNCIL COMMITTEE OF THE WHOLE  
Discuss Johnson County Wastewater Brush Creek pump station improvements at Porter 
Park 
 
Mr. Bredehoeft said that staff had been in discussion with Johnson County Wastewater 
(JCW) related to the proposed project for several years. He noted that JCW would need to 
acquire a small section of right-of-way at Porter Park and easements from the city to 
complete the improvements.  
 
Patrick Denning, Assistant Chief Engineer with Johnson County Wastewater, shared a 
presentation with details about the project. He stated that the existing pump station at 
Porter Park had been built in the 1950s, and that during periods of rain, defects caused 
water to leak into the system. The Environmental Protection Agency had tasked the 
agency with eliminating “wet-weather discharges”. Initially, JCW had proposed the 
installation of a 4.5 million gallon wet-weather underground storage tank at Porter Park to 
address the issue, but after discussion with city staff, opted to proceed with the 
replacement of the pump station to reduce impact to the park and surrounding residences. 
 
Mr. Denning said the proposed timeline would begin with design work in 2027 and 
construction in 2028 and 2029. The new station would provide additional pumping 
capacity, allow JCW to make future wet-weather improvements, and meet all current 
codes. The proposed building would sit next to the existing station, which would continue 
to function until the new facility became operational, and then be demolished. JCW would 
need to acquire approximately 854 square feet of city property, along with a sewer 
easement of 3,224 square feet and a sanitary sewer pump easement of 2,175 square feet. 
Construction materials would need to be stored on the baseball field on the south side of 
the park during construction. After construction completed, JCW would restore the field. 
 
Ms. Selders noted that her house across the street from the park occasionally experienced 
sewage backups, and if the project would amplify these problems. Mr. Denning stated that 
the new pump would not negatively impact surrounding homes. 
 
Mr. Robinson asked if there would be any remuneration to the city for the land that JCW 
wished to acquire. Mr. Denning said that JCW had spoken with staff about remuneration, 
and could further discuss options when the agreement was considered by council. 
 
Ms. Lawrence asked if the community outreach portion of the project would give 
surrounding neighbors the opportunity to provide feedback on the design of the new

Mr. Jordan noted that the pump station replacement was a compromise after staff shared

be donated to JCW without remuneration. 
 
After further discussion, Mr. Sellers moved that the city council end the council committee

Program (CIP) package recommended to the Governing Body by the Parks and 
Recreation Committee. The project includes installation of rubberized, pour-in-place

quote.

surfacing. Playscapes Recreation was the only company to submit a quote for the 
project. Staff has reviewed the quote and determined that the proposed cost is 
consistent with the estimated project cost. Playscapes Recreation has successfully 
completed previous park projects in Prairie Village and has proven to be 
knowledgeable, responsive, and reliable. Staff recommends approval of the quote from 
Playscapes Recreation. 
 
The surfacing is anticipated to be installed this fall, weather permitting. If weather 
conditions delay installation, the project will be completed in spring 2027.

City Council: Tuesday, September 8, 2026 
New Business: Hold a public hearing to receive comments regarding the city 
exceeding the revenue neutral rate, then; 
Consider Resolution 2026-03 approving a tax rate or mill levy rate in excess of the 
revenue neutral rate for the 2027 budget year 
 
Recommendation 
The City Council hold the Revenue Neutral Rate Hearing, and afterward consider 
Resolution 2026-03 adopting a tax rate in excess of the revenue neutral rate for the 
2027 budget year. 
 
Background 
Per State Statute, the Governing Body must hold a public hearing for the adoption of the 
upcoming year’s budget.  Adoption of Senate Bill 13 amended the budget timeline and 
added an additional hearing requirement for cities exceeding the Revenue Neutral Rate. 
The hearings may be conducted at the same meeting.    
 
The revenue neutral rate is the tax rate in mills that will generate the same property tax 
in dollars as the previous tax year using the current tax year’s total assessed valuation. 
In Prairie Village, the 2027 revenue neutral rate would be 16.973 mills. Since the 
recommended budget requires 18.057 mills, a public hearing is required per K.S.A. 79-
2988. Notice of intent to exceed the revenue neutral rate was provided to the County 
Clerk before July 20th, and the notice was published on July 21, 2026. The hearing 
must occur between August 20th and September 20th.  
 
The budget must be certified to the County Clerk by October 1st if exceeding the 
Revenue Neutral Rate.  The preliminary 2027 budget was recommended for approval 
by City Council on June 15, 2026, and Council approved a resolution of intent to exceed 
the Revenue Neutral Rate on July 6, 2026. The attached hearing notice was published 
in The Legal Record on Tuesday, July 21, 2026, in accordance with state statute.   
 
Following the Revenue Neutral Rate and Budget hearings, the Council must approve a 
resolution to exceed the Revenue Neutral Rate prior to adopting the 2027 budget as 
previously recommended. These actions will occur on the September 8 agenda.  
 
The total mill levy for 2027 is 18.057 mills, which is the same as the 2026 levy. 
Additional budget information can be found on the City’s website.

Proposed Flat Mill Levy 
 The budget as previously advanced by Council includes holding the mill 
levy flat at 18.057 mills, after a reduction from 18.326 the prior year. If 
this flat mill levy is adopted, the General Fund would end 2027 slightly 
over a target 25% fund balance (25.5%).
 The county-wide Public Safety Sales tax that provides approximately $760,000 
in annual revenue will expire April 1, 2027. The County has signaled intent to 
renew this tax (or replace it with a similar tax) but will have to get Legislature 
approval to do so. The anticipated loss of revenue for 2027 is $570,000.
 Given the continued bills at the state level that could lead to future property tax 
revenue growth not keeping pace with inflationary costs, as well as the expiration 
of the public safety sales tax, staff is not recommending a mill levy reduction for 
the 2027 budget in order to meet Council budgeting goals and objectives.

Revenue Neutral Rate Overview
10
 During the 2021 Kansas legislative session, the tax lid was removed and 
the legislature enacted SB13 and HB2104, establishing new notice and 
public hearing requirements if the proposed budget will exceed the 
property tax levy’s revenue neutral rate.
 The revenue neutral rate is the tax rate in mills that will generate the 
same property tax in dollars as the previous tax year using the current 
tax year’s total assessed valuation.
 Because the 2027 budget exceeds the Revenue Neutral Rate of 16.973, 
a hearing is required. The hearing can occur on the same night as the 
budget public hearing.
 The Council must consider a resolution to exceed the Revenue Neutral 
Rate prior to adopting the budget.
