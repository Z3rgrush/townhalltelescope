COUNCIL COMMITTEE OF THE WHOLE  
Recommendations for amendments to the E-bike and E-scooter ordinance with adoption 
of the 2026 Standard Traffic Ordinance (STO) and Uniform Public Offense Code (UPOC) 
 
Mr. Robinson stated that after discussion with residents, councilmembers, and Chief 
McCullough, he did not believe the council committee of the whole was prepared to discuss 
the addition of the Unlawful Approach of a First Responder section (also known as the 
“halo law”) to the Uniform Public Offense Code. He asked for a motion to postpone the 
discussion until the next council meeting on October 19. 
 
Mr. Graves made a motion to postpone the discussion of the “halo law” to the October 19 
council meeting. Mr. Nelson seconded the motion. 
 
Mr. Jordan requested that staff have an opportunity to present information about the halo 
law and provide time for discussion at the current meeting. He added that approval of the 
STO and UPOC was typically done under the “new business” portion of the meeting, but 
that it had been placed under the council committee of the whole to provide additional time 
to discuss and work through any potential recommended changes. 
 
After discussion, Mr. Graves withdrew his motion. 
 
Chief McCullough presented proposed amendments to the e-bike and e-scooter ordinance 
first, stating that its July 21, 2025, meeting, the council passed an ordinance regulating the 
use of e-bikes and e-scooters. The ordinance was created to address concerns received 
from the community and police department regarding safe use of e-devices for both riders 
and pedestrians. He noted that the police department had used the ordinance as part of a 
multi-faceted approach of education, public messaging, and enforcement in response to 
community concerns.  
 
Because the e-device market continued to evolve and the number of e-devices users kept 
growing, the Mid-America Regional Council (MARC) convened a working group of metro 
Page 6 of 85
 
5 
 
law enforcement agencies, community groups, medical professionals, and other 
stakeholders to try and develop a model ordinance that would provide consistency across 
jurisdictions. The police department participated in the working group, and with the 
publication of the model ordinance, the department recommended adoption of elements 
of the model ordinance.  
 
Chief McCullough stated that due to the foresight of the city council, the city’s current 
ordinance already included many of the recommendations made by the working group. 
After review, the police department recommended that the council adopt the following 
amendments to the current ordinance from the MARC model policy:  
 
1. Adopt the definition of a micro-mobility device to capture all electric-assisted 
devices in the ordinance, and amend the e-scooter ordinance to a micro-mobility 
device ordinance. 
2. Adopt amendments that clarify that a parent or guardian of a child that knowingly 
allows a child to violate the ordinance can be cited for violations committed by the 
child.  
 
Ms. Lawrence asked if there had been any discussion about changing the fine amount for 
violators. Chief McCullough said there had not been any discussion of fines, and that fine 
amounts were set by judges. 
 
Mr. Nelson made a motion to recommend approval of the amendments to the e-bike and 
e-scooter ordinances. Mr. Agniel seconded the motion, which passed unanimously. 
 
 
Chief McCullough said that the police department and municipal court also recommended 
the adoption of the 2026 Standard Traffic Ordinance (STO) and 2026 Uniform Public 
Offense code (UPOC). The most significant proposed change is the adoption of the new 
crime of Unlawful Approach of a First Responder, more commonly known as the "halo law" 
in the UPOC. The law was passed by the 2026 Kansas Legislature and makes it illegal for 
a person to approach or remain within 25 feet of a first responder while the responder is 
engaged in the performance of their duties, after they have been given a warning by the 
first responder to stay back and that the separation is necessary for the safety of any 
person or property, and is either causing the first responder to be distracted or have an 
increased level of concern of physical harm to any person or damage to property.  
 
The police department and court recommend the adoption of the UPOC and the "halo law" 
as it allows the city to maintain local control of the court process and consequences of a 
conviction. Chief McCullough added that if the city did not adopt the law, it would still need 
to be enforced because it is a state law. For example, if an outside agency working in 
Prairie Village invoked the law, the case would be handled by the district attorney’s office 
rather than the city’s municipal court. 
 
Page 7 of 85
 
6 
 
Mr. Sellers asked for additional details about what local controls would be lost if the council 
did not approve the law. Chief McCullough stated the biggest loss would be to the court 
process and consequences afterward. If approved, violations would be handed by the city 
judge and city prosecutor rather than the district court. 
 
Mr. Vallette asked what would happen if the city adopted the law and a lawsuit was filed. 
Mr. Aggen said that any city law that was challenged would be something the city would 
have to defend. He added that anytime a city law enforcement officer was involved in 
enforcing a state law or federal law, they could be sued for any specific incident. 
 
Mayor Mikkelson stated that he preferred the city retain control to prevent abuses and 
injustices.  
 
Ms. Lawrence asked what impact a local ordinance would have to the city’s police officers. 
Chief McCullough said that the department would be hesitant to invoke the law due to 
concerns of future legal challenges. Instead, officers would likely rely on existing laws 
dictating interference with law enforcement. 
 
Ms. Bartelt made a motion to recommend adoption of the STO and UPOC with the 
Unlawful Approach of a First Responder section excluded. Mr. Graves seconded the 
motion. 
 
Ms. Selders and Mr. O’Toole stated that they preferred that city officers maintain their 
discretion in how the law would be applied rather than handing such control to an outside 
agency. 
 
After further discussion, the motion passed 10-2, with Ms. Selders and Mr. Agniel in 
opposition. 
 
Mr. O’Toole made a motion that the Unlawful Approach of a First Responder section be 
brought to New Business at the October 19 council meeting. Mr. Agniel seconded the 
motion. 
 
Mr. Graves made a motion to postpone the motion and hold the discussion of the Unlawful 
Approach of a First Responder section to the October 19 council committee of the whole 
meeting rather than at New Business. Mr. Sellers seconded the motion. 
 
After discussion, the motion passed 8-4, with Mr. O’Toole, Mr. Nelson, Mr. Agniel and Mr. 
Reddell in opposition. 
 
 
Mr. Vallette moved that the city council end the council committee of the whole portion of 
the meeting. The motion was seconded by Mr. Agniel and passed unanimously.

Background 
The Insurance Committee met on October 1st and voted 5-0 to recommend this proposal to the full 
Council for final consideration. Holmes Murphy team members will be available to answer questions 
during the meeting. 
 
The Holmes Murphy team provided our renewal summary from Blue Cross Blue Shield Kansas 
City for 2027. The City is completing the third  full year with the carrier. A rolling 12 month 
experience rating period, 06/2025-05/2026, was utilized for the report. 
BCBSKC released their Fully Insured Renewal rate to Holmes Murphy at a 4.5% increase. Based 
on input from City Administration and the City’s Insurance Committee, plan changes to the PPO 
Base Plan were introduced to follow benchmarking trends. These plan design changes reflected 
a negotiated rate renewal to -0.5%. 
 
As requested during the 2026 Insur ance Renewal presentation to Ci ty Council on Oct 6, 2025, 
staff had several conversations with Holmes Murphy  to conduct pre-emptive marketing, review 
alternative administration and funding structures, and explore long-term cost containment levers 
for the 2027 plan year and beyond. The Holmes Mu rphy team introduced the BCBSKC Cost-
Plus plan as a viable option for the city to accomplish the pre-established criteria. 
A summary of the differences between our current fully-insured plan and a cost-plus 
arrangement is below: 
 
Fully-Insured Plan Cost-Plus Arrangement 
The City pays a fixed monthly premium 
to the carrier. 
The City pays employees’ actual medical and 
pharmacy claims plus an administrative fee. 
The carrier bears the financial risk: it 
absorbs losses if claims run high and 
keeps savings if claims run low. 
The City bears the financial risk for claims; costs vary 
based on actual use and the City keeps the savings if 
claims run low. 
Costs are entirely fixed (the premium). 
Costs are mixed: fixed (administrative fees, stop-loss 
premiums) and variable (claims minus pharmacy 
rebates and stop loss reimbursements).

The BCBSKC Cost-Plus plan continues to include the wellness incentive program that will fund 
up to $30k for employees to participate in Wellne ss activities, a Blue Select SPIRA option that 
has a specific network of care centers at a lower premium cost, and the Preferred Provider 
Organization (PPO) and Qualified High-Deductible Health Plan (QHDHP) designs.  The Cost-
Plus contract also allows the City to retain the current Max Refund provision, where the City gets 
a refund check if claims are below the set threshold.

current 135.1 Electric-Assisted Scooters ordinance with 135.1 Micromobility Devices as 
the wording is more inclusive to all battery or powered devices excluding E-Bicycles.

Micromobility Device. A compact electric-assisted or powered device commonly 
designed for one person, but not to exceed two people, including such devices as 
electric assisted scooters, motorized skateboards, electric unicycles, or any other 
electric powered mobility device, designed for single-person mobility, either privately-
owned, or part of a shared mobility system; but shall not mean an electric-assisted 
bicycle, a vehicle or device like that which is an all-terrain vehicle, golf cart, motorized 
bicycle, motor-driven cycle, or motorcycle, nor like coasters, roller skates or 
skateboards; and shall not include a motorized wheel chair or power scooter or similar 
device intended as a mobility aid for a person with disability.
