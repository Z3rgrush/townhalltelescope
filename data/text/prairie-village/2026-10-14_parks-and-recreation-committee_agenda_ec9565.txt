Windsor Park  
The committee walked the park trail and reviewed upcoming improvements to ADA accessibility including the widening of 
the path and improved connectivity to the north-west by the senior living facility. The project will be awarded at an 
upcoming City Council meeting with construction to follow over the summer. The age 2-5 playset will be replaced in June, 
and Public Works is in the process of making improvements to the sandbox and nature play area. The committee 
discussed the recent placement of a fence by the adjacent property owner, St. Ann Church. Ms. Buum stated that the city 
had several conversations with church leadership regarding the fence, and while it creates a large change for the 
neighborhood and park users, the fence is on private property. 
 
Bennett Park 
The committee reviewed recent improvements made to Bennett Park, including a new accessible spinner, pour-in-place 
rubber surfacing, a new shelter, and new drinking fountain/bottle filler. There are funds budgeted for 2030 for additional 
play equipment and swings.  
 
Bennett Park is currently a chemical free park, and the monarch butterfly way station previously located in this park is in 
the process of being re-established. Ms. Buum asked the committee for feedback on expanding the number of chemical-
free parks in the community, which is a priority of the environmental committee. The response was generally positive, but 
the committee was cognizant of the high expectations in the community surrounding well-maintained spaces. The 
committee was amenable to a "baby steps" approach, with the possible addition of one more chemical-free park to 
continue to assess how maintenance would be handled.  
 
Franklin Park 
The committee reviewed upcoming projects at Franklin Park, including pour-in-place rubber surfacing. This park 
frequently has flooding issues that should be alleviated with this upgrade, and will improve accessibility. The previously 
approved historical monument denoting the polo fields previously located in this area is underway. Projects are tentatively 
scheduled for the fall.

Prairie Village Pool 
 
General Overview 
The swimming pool program is designed to provide a safe summer outdoor recreation opportunity as well as 
instruction in swimming and water safety.  The pool complex at Harmon Park includes a recreational 
swimming pool, diving pool, slide pool, meter pool, baby pool, adult pool, and a food service facility.  
Members pay an annual fee to purchase a swim card for admission to the pool complex; others can pay at 
the gate for daily admission.

Staffing 
The pool used 76 different lifeguards throughout the year and 25 operations attendants. The base wage for 
starting lifeguards was $15.50/hour. The base wage for a starting operations employee was $13.25/hour.  
The team was led by Suzanne McCullough, Aquatics and Recreation Supervisor, and Daniel Jackson, Pool 
Manager. They were supported by five assistant managers and four headguards. Red Cross training was 
organized and led by pool manager Daniel Jackson. 
 
Recruitment began efforts shortly after the first of the year by reaching out to previous employees and high 
schools. A referral bonus was offered of $50 per referral, up to $150. $1,300 was paid in referral bonuses.  
 
Two incentive bonuses were used to encourage employees to work until the end of the season: 
• Reduced Hour Bonus: To qualify, employees must work an average of 15 hours per week during 
reduced hours. The bonus payment is based on the total hours worked during the entire season. 
$5,250 in bonus wages were paid. 
• Labor Day Bonus: To qualify, employees must work through Labor Day weekend. The bonus payment 
is based on the total hours worked during the final two pay periods of the season. $2,825in bonus 
wages were paid.

City Recreation Programs 
 
Swim 
Membership for the 2026 Prairie Village Piranhas on both the competitive and pre-competitive teams was 
166, down from 194 in 2025. The team was coached this year by Bridget Kelly who was assisted by six part-
time assistant coaches.  
   
Dive 
The 2026 Dive Team had 15 participants, down from 28 participants in 2025, and was taught by head coach 
Riley Strickland and one assistant coach.  
 
Tennis Program 
The 2026 Junior Tennis League was fielded with 38 players, up from 52 players in 2025. The team was 
coached by tennis pro Tracy Cooper. 
 
Youth tennis lessons were offered in partnership with Johnson County Park & Recreation District throughout 
the summer. 12 sessions were held with a total of 110 participants. 
 
Skateboarding 101 
Dan Mapes hosted four Skateboarding 101 sessions for ages 3 to 12, with a total of 25 participants. Each 
session included three lessons where participants were taught skate park etiquette, balance, basic skills, 
and simple tricks.  
 
Swim Lessons 
Seven water safety instructors led 46 classes with 177 participants, up from 39 classes with 134 participants 
in 2025.
