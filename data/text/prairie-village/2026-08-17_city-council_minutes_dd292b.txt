COUNCIL COMMITTEE OF THE WHOLE  
Discuss Johnson County Wastewater Brush Creek pump station improvements at Porter 
Park 
 
Mr. Bredehoeft said that s taff had been in discussion with Johnson County Wastewater 
(JCW) related to the proposed project for several years. He noted that JCW would need to 
acquire a small section of right -of-way at Porter Park  and easements from the city  to 
complete the improvements.  
 
Patrick Denning, Assistant Chief Engineer with Johnson County Wastewater , shared a 
presentation with details about the project. He stated that the existing pump station at 
Porter Park had been built in the 1950s, and that during periods of rain, defects caused 
water to leak into the system. The Environmental Protection Agency h ad tasked the 
agency with eliminating “wet -weather discharges” . I nitially, JCW had proposed the 
installation of a 4.5 million gallon wet-weather underground storage tank at Porter Park to 
address the issue , but after discussion with city staff, opted to proceed  with the 
replacement of the pump station to reduce impact to the park and surrounding residences. 
 
Mr. Denning said the proposed timeline would begin with design work in 2027 and 
construction in 2028 and 2029. The new station would provide additional pumping 
capacity, allow JCW to make future wet -weather improvements,  and meet all current 
codes. The proposed building would sit next to the existing station, which would continue 
to function until the new facility became operational, and then be demolished. JCW would 
need to acquire approximately 854 square feet of city property, along with a sewer 
easement of 3,224 square feet and a sanitary sewer pump easement of 2,175 square feet. 
Construction materials would need to be stored on the baseball field on the south side of 
the park during construction. After construction completed, JCW would restore the field. 
 
Ms. Selders noted that her house across the street from the park occasionally experienced 
sewage backups, and if the project would amplify these problems. Mr. Denning stated that 
the new pump would not negatively impact surrounding homes. 
 
Mr. Robinson asked if there would be any remuneration to the city for the land that JCW 
wished to acquire. Mr. Denning said that JCW had spoken with staff about remuneration, 
and could further discuss options when the agreement was considered by council. 
 
Ms. Lawrence asked if the community outreach portion of the project would give 
surrounding neighbors the opportunity to provide feedback on the design of the new 
 
5 
 
station. Mr. Denning said that proposed designs would be shared with residents to review 
and offer suggestions. 
 
Mr. Jordan noted that the pump station replacement was a compromise after staff shared 
their objection to the underground storage facility. As a result, staff felt that the land could 
be donated to JCW without remuneration. 
 
After further discussion, Mr. Sellers moved that the city council end the council committee 
of the whole portion of the meeting. The motion was seconded by Mr. Agniel and passed 
unanimously.
