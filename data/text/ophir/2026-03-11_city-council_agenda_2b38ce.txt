VARIANCE REQUEST #1 
Reduction of Required Side Yard Setbacks from 15 Feet to 10 Feet 
 
§1104.1 – Special Circumstances 
Special circumstances exist on the Property related to size, area, configuration, and overlay 
constraints that do not generally apply to other properties within the same Zone District. 
• The Property’s surveyed area of 7,844 sq. ft. includes a narrow strip of vacated alley 
right-of-way along the northern boundary. 
• Inclusion of this strip increases the total lot area above the threshold that triggers the 15-
foot side yard setback requirement. 
• The alley strip does not materially expand the functional development footprint and is not 
included within the proposed building area. 
• As a result, a modest increase in technical lot area triggers a substantial increase in 
required setbacks, reducing the practical buildable footprint.  
• An ~6% increase in land area (444 sq ft) results in an ~20% reduction in buildable 
footprint (2,025 sq ft down to 1,650 sq ft) 
Additionally: 
• The Property is subject to a defined avalanche hazard boundary that establishes a fixed 
development footprint. 
• The interaction between the hazard boundary and 15-foot side setbacks further constrains 
the buildable envelope. 
These dimensional and overlay conditions are unique to this property and are not generally 
applicable to similarly situated lots. 
 
§1104.2 – Impacts on Setback Purposes 
The requested setback reduction will not unduly impact: 
Snow Removal and Drainage: 
No change to drainage patterns or snow storage areas is proposed. 
Streetscape: 
Building placement remains consistent with neighborhood alignment and historic Townsite 
spacing. 
Exhibit A 
Separation of Buildings for Fire Protection: 
A 10-foot side setback maintains meaningful separation between structures and remains subject 
to applicable building and fire code requirements. 
Off-Street Parking: 
Parking compliance is unaffected. 
Solar Access and Mountain Views: 
The variance does not increase building height and does not affect view corridors. 
Continuity of Design and Visual Impact: 
The 10-foot setback aligns more closely with historic Townsite development patterns and does 
not intensify visual massing. 
 
§1104.3 – No Material Detriment 
The setback reduction will not constitute a material detriment to public welfare or injury to 
property in the vicinity. 
• The use remains single-family residential. 
• No increase in density is proposed. 
• The avalanche hazard boundary is respected. 
• The variance addresses a dimensional threshold anomaly rather than intensifying 
development. 
 
§1104.4 – Hardship Not Created by Applicant 
The dimensional condition arises from: 
• Municipal abandonment of the alley right-of-way; 
• Application of lot-area-based setback thresholds; 
• Historic Townsite configuration; and 
• Overlay constraints including the established avalanche hazard boundary. 
The Applicant did not create these conditions. 
 
§1104.5 – Permitted Use 
The proposed single-family residential use is permitted in the underlying Residential Zone 
District.

VARIANCE REQUEST #2 
2/12 ROOF PITCH 
The Applicant requests approval to construct a single-family residence with a 2/12 roof pitch 
rather than the required 4/12 minimum roof pitch. 
The requested modification reduces overall ridge height and visual mass while maintaining 
compliance with all other applicable zoning standards. 
 
§1104 STANDARDS FOR REVIEW 
 
§1104.1 – Special Circumstances 
Special circumstances exist on the Property related to overlay constraints and site conditions that 
do not generally apply to other parcels within the same Zone District: 
• The Property contains measurable east-to-west topographic change, which increases the visual 
effect of vertical building elements. 
• The Property is subject to a defined avalanche hazard overlay that establishes a fixed and 
limited development footprint, constraining the location and width of the building envelope. 
• Because the development footprint is fixed and cannot expand beyond the established hazard 
boundary, roof pitch becomes the primary determinant of ridge height and overall vertical mass. 
A 4/12 roof increases ridge elevation across the full building span, while a 2/12 roof 
proportionally reduces that vertical scale without altering the footprint. 
The interaction between the constrained building envelope and site topography makes roof pitch 
materially consequential on this Property in a manner not generally applicable to unconstrained 
parcels within the same Zone District. 
 
§1104.2 – Impacts on Community and Neighborhood Protections 
The proposed 2/12 roof pitch will not unduly impact snow removal, drainage, streetscapes, 
building separation, solar access, or neighborhood character. In several respects, it reduces 
potential impacts. 
Exhibit B 
View Preservation 
• The reduced ridge height preserves the west-facing mountain view corridor of the neighboring 
property to the east.  
• Lower vertical mass reduces visual obstruction. 
Reduced Visual Presence 
• A shallower roof profile lowers the overall building mass. 
• The reduction in ridge elevation minimizes the structure’s prominence within the streetscape. 
Energy and Material Efficiency 
• Reduced roof pitch decreases interior volume relative to a 4/12 roof. 
• Lower volume reduces material use and long-term energy demand. 
The requested modification decreases visual intensity without altering the approved footprint. 
 
§1104.3 – No Material Detriment 
The proposed roof pitch will not constitute a material detriment to public welfare or injury to 
property in the vicinity. 
• The building footprint remains unchanged and within the established hazard boundary. 
• The use remains single-family residential. 
• The reduced ridge height preserves neighboring west-facing views. 
• The roof will be engineered to meet all applicable structural and snow load requirements. 
The requested modification reduces visual and spatial impacts. 
 
§1104.4 – Hardship Not Created by Applicant 
The conditions giving rise to this request stem from existing site topography and the fixed 
development envelope established by the avalanche hazard overlay. The Applicant did not create 
these conditions. 
The variance is sought to moderate vertical mass within the permitted footprint, not to intensify 
development. 
 
§1104.5 – Permitted Use 
The proposed structure remains a permitted single-family residential use within the underlying 
Zone District. 
Exhibit B 
 
 
SUMMARY 
Approval of the 2/12 roof pitch will: 
• Reduce ridge height by approximately 5’8” across the primary span; 
• Preserve the neighboring west-facing view corridor; 
• Reduce overall visual mass; 
• Improve proportionality of the structure within a constrained footprint; 
• Maintain compliance with all other zoning standards. 
The requested modification reduces impact and maintains neighborhood compatibility.
