CALL TO ORDER 
Mayor Weed calls the meeting to order at 11:30 a.m.  
 
NEW BUSINESS  
A. Updates re: Cell Tower Leases at 6400 State Line Road  
 
i.       Existing Leases  
Mayor Weed reports that the City received a certified letter from T-Mobile cancelling its lease in 
the 6400 State Line property tower, which ran through 2044, effective December 31, 2026. He 
states that Ms. Lee later learned the reasons; the tower is too low for T-Mobile's needs, and its 
concrete and brick construction prevents a satisfactory signal. Mayor Weed asks whether there 
are any penalties. Ms. Lee responds that T-Mobile must pay to remove its equipment and owes a 
lump sum payment of approximately six months' rent in addition to lease payments through 
December 31.  
Mayor Weed shares that a few days later the regional T-Mobile office requested a 
meeting. He explains that the cancelled lease was likely negotiated nationally. The regional 
representatives stated that they want to remain in this location. They asked whether the City 
would be willing to construct a monopole of 130 to 150 feet with approximately 3,600 square 
feet at its base for equipment and stated that a new structure would allow them to offer a 
stronger, state-of-the-art signal. Mayor Weed states that the cancellation was unexpected but 
opens options for the church and the tower. 
Councilperson Sullivan confirms that T-Mobile will pay through December 31 and asks 
about the AT&T lease term. Ms. Lee responds that the AT&T lease expires August 14, 2027, and 
that the City must notify AT&T six months prior, by February 14, 2027, whether it will extend, 
cancel, or negotiate a new arrangement. She states that AT&T has also expressed strong interest 
in the location, that the structure was not discussed with AT&T, and that with only about five

Council meetings before the deadline she would like Council to begin the discussion now. 
Councilperson Thorne asks how T-Mobile operates nationally versus regionally and how 
a national decision canceled the contract. Ms. Lee responds that the regional representative was 
surprised the letter had been sent and that her supervisor has notified the national office of the 
continued interest in the location. She states that any new lease would require removal of the 
tower within a set period if it were no longer used, similar to the protections in the small cell 
agreement. Mayor Weed adds that any contract would clearly state its duration and termination 
provisions. 
 
ii.      Possible Options  
Mayor Weed presents three options with option 1 to retain the bell tower and renew the AT&T 
lease. The City would work around the bell tower in its plans for the property and, assuming the 
rate could be renegotiated, retain approximately $3,500 per month from AT&T, while the $4,100 
per month from T-Mobile would end. The City would spend a minimum of approximately 
$250,000 to make the bell tower structurally and aesthetically sound and to build an enclosure 
for equipment at its base if the  church is demolished  . Ms. Lee states that the consultant's 
estimate was approximately $239,000 as a bare minimum. Mayor Weed notes that the tower's 
soundness would not be known until the building is removed, and that carriers share information 
and AT&T may also find the tower inadequate once T-Mobile leaves. 
Mayor Weed shares that option 2 is to demolish the bell tower and work with a partner to 
construct a new tower. A tower company would pay the estimated construction cost and manage 
the relationships with AT&T, T-Mobile, and any other carriers. The City would receive roughly 
$1,500 to $2,000 per month, with the partner retaining the remainder to recover its investment, 
and additional carriers could increase that amount. Construction could take 9 to 18 months and 
require FCC, FAA, and City ARB and BZA approvals. Ms. Lee states that ground lease sizes 
typically range from 2,500 to 3,600 square feet. 
Mayor Weed shares option 3 to terminate the AT&T lease and all cell tower 
commitments. The City could demolish the church and the bell tower and receive no revenue. 
Mayor Weed notes that several residents have reported unsatisfactory cell service. Mayor Weed 
states that reliable cellular service is approaching an essential utility, citing public safety, 911 
calls, noting that an estimated 70 to 80 percent of which are made from cell phones, emergency 
alerts,  remote work and learning, telehealth, and deliveries. Mayor Weed asks Councilmembers 
to share their thoughts on the three options or suggest any additional alternatives. 
Councilperson Sullivan asks whether any carriers are looking at sites on the Missouri 
side. Ms. Lee responds that both carriers consider this an essential location. Mayor Weed reports 
hearing that Verizon is unhappy with its site at 333 W Meyer, that Verizon has an installation 
atop the new apartment complex at 76th Street and State Line Road, and that the State Line 
McDonald's tree tower is currently under some sort of construction. He states that consultants 
repeatedly cite the City's topography and trees as the reason the tower on 63rd Street and 
Mission Road does not serve the lower part of Mission Hills. 
Mayor Weed states that he favors Option 2, that the requested 130 to 150 feet may be an 
opening number, and that he would like a tower that keeps AT&T and T-Mobile and possibly 
attracts one or two more carriers. He states that it is not a revenue opportunity but avoids the 
construction and maintenance costs.  He hopes to have clear photo simulations to share at the 
October 12th City Council meeting. He asks for Council's guidance on whether staff should 
negotiate and whether a tower of that height would be a problem for residents. 
Councilperson Thorne states that she has difficulty visualizing a 130- to 150-foot tower

and a 3,600-square-foot pad. She states that she understands the need for service, but that the 
City works hard to keep the community beautiful and that it should be up to the carriers to find a 
solution. Mayor Weed estimates the existing bell tower at about 80 feet. 
Ms. Lee states that City Hall's first floor is about 2,600 square feet. She explains that City 
code requires a tower to be 200 feet from residential property, which rules out the church 
property, and that a second provision requires a setback equal to the tower's height so staff is 
applying the more restrictive rule. She identifies a potential site in the grove of trees on the City 
Hall property along Tomahawk Road, between the speed limit sign and the first pedestrian 
crossing sign. The City’s arborist, Mr. Carmack, estimated the surrounding trees at about 75 feet 
to the high 80s, with a group of four at about 90 feet, and the trees are in good condition, though 
he recommends an unhealthy Scotch pine should be removed because it is receiving excess 
shade. Councilperson Davidson notes that the area is where residents sit in the shade during 
concerts, and Ms. Lee responds that the site is a little further south from the preferred seating 
area. 
Councilperson Moran states that she supports pursuing Option 2 but needs a clear picture 
of the tower's appearance, placement, and required size before deciding, because beauty and trees 
are very important to the City. She asks whether the equipment could be placed on a roof. Ms. 
Lee responds that this was not studied because of City Hall's size but could be. Mayor Weed 
states that the City could counter with a lower height, such as 110 feet, and a limit of about three 
carriers. 
Councilperson Sullivan states that he favors pursuing Option 2 and that Option 3 puts the 
City at real risk, citing his experience with poor cell service in Colorado and noting that the City 
appears to be in a dead spot. He states that locating the tower on City Hall property gives the 
greatest flexibility for the church property, that a partner would take the financial risk, and that 
the tower must be aesthetically acceptable for years to come. He suggests three carriers on as 
short a pole as possible. 
Councilperson Sullivan asks whether the 200-foot setback applies to property in 
Missouri. Ms. Lee responds that staff believe it does not and that Ms. Krstulic will confirm. 
Councilperson Moran notes that the City sets these rules and could grant a variance, and Ms. Lee 
states that Council could also amend the rule. Councilperson Thorne asks whether newer 
accessory equipment requires less space. Ms. Lee responds that this could be explored, that T-
Mobile needs about 15 by 20 feet within the ground lease, that City code requires utilities to be 
underground, and that the enclosure could be brick in keeping with City Hall and the garage, 
with controlled access. 
Councilperson Davidson states that he is in favor of at least looking at Option 2 for the 
sake of residents' cell service. Councilperson Moran requests an analysis showing what 
disruption would result if the bell tower equipment is removed. Mayor Weed describes the 
proposed location as largely unused space screened by 90-foot trees, away from the soccer field 
and preserving shade for concerts, with a pad of about 60 by 60 feet, a six-foot brick wall, and 
landscaping, and invites Council to walk the site. Councilperson Thorne asks about views from 
homes, and Mayor Weed responds that he believes the trees would hide most of the tower. 
Councilperson Thorne states that she is not convinced a 130-foot tower should be placed 
there and asks why Option 1 was presented. Mayor Weed responds that it remains an option 
while AT&T stays. Councilperson Moran notes that Option 1 would still require the $250,000 
expense. Councilperson Thorne states that she finds it hard to decide before plans for the 6400 
property are settled, noting that keeping the bell tower was shown to residents as part of a design. 
Mayor Weed responds that T-Mobile's departure was not known at that time. Councilperson

Sullivan states that there may be no carrier in the bell tower after next August, and that removing 
it gives the greatest flexibility for the 6400 property. 
Mayor Weed states that staff has spoken with three firms and would like one highly 
recommended firm, which helped open doors at T-Mobile, to negotiate on the City's behalf at no

.  Councilperson Moran asks whether the firm should be competitively selected to ensure the best

Councilperson Sullivan requests information on the height of the trees across the creek. 
He suggests that the equipment enclosure be as small as possible, ideally no larger than City

he is not overly concerned about the 200-foot setback if another location makes more sense 
aesthetically. Councilperson Davidson expresses concern that a large walled structure near the 
open field and concert area will draw objections from residents, recalling the small cell 
discussion, but states that he supports exploring the option.  
 
Mayor Weed states that no vote is being taken and that the item will be placed on the public 
agenda for the October 12th City Council meeting. 
 
ADJOURNMENT  
The meeting was adjourned at 12:36 p.m.
