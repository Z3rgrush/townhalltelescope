#1   Richard & Mary Sloss* 6646 Wenonga Terrace 
The Slosses are proposing to replace the wood fence along the rear and side of their property. 
Continued from the August 11th meeting so that a survey locating the trees and the City Arborist’s review of 
the trees could be provided.  
Summary of Property: 
• Character Area: Neighborhood Estates 
• Location of Common Green Space: Front & Side 
• Any Special Frontages: Corner Lot 
Summary of Project: 
The proposed rear fence will bridge the gap between their neighbor’s rear fence and the Slosses’ side fence.  
The gap was formed when the BZA denied a variance for the neighbor’s fence (installed without a building 
permit) in approximately the same location.  The Slosses propose to inset the fence 3 inches.  
The side fence that currently runs along Tomahawk Road was installed in a location not approved by the 
ARB or the Board of Zoning Appeals. 
Ordinance Compliance: 
The new fences are/will be in violation of a platted setback.  A variance of 30 feet is required.  The fences 
are also in violation of City Code Section 5-135.D.1 which requires that a solid fence extend no further from 
the principal building than 30% of the distance from the street (back of curb) to the point of the principal 
building that is closest to the street, and be no taller than 5 ft.  A variance of 18 ft. is required for the 
location, and a variance of 1 ft. is required for the height. 
Design Guideline Review: 
Section 2.7.3.C on page 109 of the Design Guidelines recommends that fences be located behind platted 
setbacks in conformance with the Mission Hills Zoning ordinances.  This recommendation has not been 
met.

Summary of Project: 
The existing detached garage and some hardscape will be removed (shown in red on sheet LA 102).  A new 
single-car detached garage and studio will be added in the rear yard.  Two private courtyards will be added 
between the detached garage and the studio, and a third courtyard will be added to the northwest side of the 
new studio.  A crushed stone walkway will be added connecting one of the courtyards to the main house 
patio behind the home.   
An existing stone walkway at the rear of the home will be extended along the new reconfigured concrete 
drive.  AC units will be moved from the west side of the home to the front of the home.  
A 3-story addition will be added to the front façade.  The addition will tie into the existing front turret and 
existing front wing screened porch. The turret roof and the roof over the front wing will be removed and 
replaced with new wood roofs. The front addition includes a large 2-story box bay window that sits on a stone 
archway. The window extension will have a relatively flat copper roof. Similarly, there is a dormer at the top 
of the new addition that is entirely clad in copper siding and roofing.  The existing front door, located in the 
turret, will be closed in.  The new front door is located on the east side of the home.  The front wing eave 
height is being raised and the roof reoriented. New pilasters are proposed at the corners of the screened 
porch wing.  A small, copper-clad dormer is proposed in the new roof. 
At the right (north) side of the house, you can see the new gabled end sided with wood planks.  
At the left (south) side of the house, they propose rebuilding the existing side porch.  A new stone wall with 
an archway is proposed in front of the existing garage door. The roof structure will be replaced with a new 
copper roof supported by brackets.  New railings are proposed at the existing porch stairs.  A second- floor 
wall dormer will be removed and replaced with a much smaller copper-clad dormer. 
At the rear of the house, a new large boxed-bay window is proposed opposite the bay window on the front of 
the house. The configuration is similar; however, this side includes doors accessing the existing courtyard 
patio.  A projecting planter separates the first-floor doors from the second-floor windows.  The attic dormer 
will be replaced with a shorter but wider, new dormer that will be clad with copper.  A similar dormer will 
replace an existing dormer at the south rear wing.  The first-floor window in the south wing will be replaced 
with a wider unit.

The proposed detached garage is 1-story and is proposed to cut into the existing landscape. The eave height 
at the single garage door is just under the 10-foot maximum height, and the side eaves extend out further are 
only 7 feet above the garage floor.  With the change in topography, sections of the side eaves are only 2 feet 
or less above grade.  The garage is sided entirely with brick and has a slate roof to match the main house.  
The all-brick detached studio building has two major components.  The first is a tall gabled structure that has 
a Jacobean-style stone façade and parapet at the front (east) side.  This section includes a stone fireplace at 
the rear. The second part of the building is a more contemporary structure with large expanses of glass and 
a green roof.  The stone and brick materials are maintained throughout and tie the structure together.  Two 
private courtyards are created at the detached accessory buildings by using low stone walls. Due to the site 
configuration, the visibility of both accessory buildings is greatly reduced by topography, site walls and 
landscaping.

Design Guideline Review: 
Section 2.3 on pages 64 through 67 of the Design Guidelines provides specific recommendations for the 
Neighborhood Estates character area. 
Subsection A recommends that the main mass of the house should be no more than 50% of the lot width or 
70 ft. whichever is greater. This section goes on to recommend that the depth of the main mass should not 
exceed 40 ft.  With the modifications, the main mass is approximately 30 feet wide and 30 feet deep.  The 
main mass height should be no more than 2 ½ stories and 35 ft.  At 39’-5” tall this recommendation has been 
met, but this is an existing condition that includes a basement level. The main mass should be located 
entirely within the Primary Building Area. 
Also, “With few exceptions, the main mass should be the tallest, widest and most highly visible massing 
element of the home.  The picturesque massing of this house lends itself to rambling configuration.  
Subsection B recommends that the total width of all front wings not exceed 50% of the main mass width.  
The depth should not be greater than the width. The height is limited to 2 stories and should be at least 3 ft. 
shorter than the main mass.  These recommendations have been met.  
Subsection E recommends that detached accessory buildings located in the Secondary Building Area 
should have a height of no more than 2 stories and 24 ft. with a maximum area of 720 sq. ft.  Accessory 
buildings located in the Conditional Building or Primary Landscape Areas are limited to 1 story with 10- ft. 
maximum eave heights with a maximum area of 720 sq. ft. if allowed under Section 2.6.4 (pg. 88).  There 
should be no more than 2 detached accessory buildings per lot.  These recommendations have been met.
