Budget Message 
To the Mayor and Board of Trustees: 
This preliminary 2027 budget is submitted for your review and workshop. It is a planning 
document, not a proposal for adoption — it precedes the public hearings and the statutory 
calendar set out at the end of this book. 
It differs from previous years in one important respect. Prior budgets were built forward from the 
previous year's budget and year-to-date estimates. This one is built from the 2025 audited 
financial statements. Revenue is based on what the Town collected in 2025 rather than what it 
expected to collect, and every comparative figure in this book ties to the audited statements or 
to the adopted 2026 appropriation. Where the two disagree, the audit governs.  
The reason for the change is practical. The Town is converting its financial system from Caselle to 
OpenGov, and reconciliation differences between the general ledger and the audited 
statements are being worked through. Building the budget on audited figures gives the Board a 
base that does not move while that work is completed. 
The headline 
Setting aside grant-funded activity, the General Fund shows recurring revenue of $4,428,043 
against recurring expenditure of $4,946,130 — an operating gap of ($445,487) . Adding the 
Town's share of four new grant contracts brings the General Fund deficit to ($589,184), funded 
from a projected beginning fund balance of $2,939,617. 
That gap is affordable for a year or two, but it is not a position to hold indefinitely. The drivers are 
identifiable and each is defensible on its own: a new Marketing and Communications 
department carrying $636,331, including $371,581 of personnel that did not exist in 2025; the 
$100,000 marketing increase directed for 2027; the OpenGov subscription; $68,000 of local 
match on grant contracts; and roughly $90,000 of insurance increases now confirmed by 
quotation. What they have in common is that they recur. 
What is new in 2027 
Four grant-funded service contracts were executed during 2026 and appear in a Town budget 
for the first time. Together they carry $2,866,982 of grant and reimbursement revenue against 
$2,934,982 of contract cost. They are set out in their own section of this book, with scope, cost 
and expected outcomes for each. 
The Space to Create property went into service on 1 August 2026 and is budgeted for its first full 
operating year, with all nine residential units and the Grand Lake Creative District commercial 
space in place. It is a separate enterprise fund with its own two bank accounts, managed by 
the Grand County Housing Authority under an executed memorandum of understanding.  
The Water Department is budgeted for a replacement service vehicle. The Capital Improvement 
Fund carries a $200,000 paving appropriation plus $150,000 of 2026 road money not spent and 
carried into 2027, giving a $350,000 paving program. 
All three insurance renewals arrived while this budget was being prepared and are reflected in 
it. CIRSA quoted the 2027 property and casualty contribution at $81,431, up 17.7% on the $69,200 
budgeted across four funds in 2026. CIRSA quoted workers’ compensation at $211,767, up 18.5% 
on 2026 — an increase that comes entirely from how payroll was allocated across job 
classifications, not from rates, which CIRSA reports are falling pool-wide. And CEBT added 13.00% 
to every medical plan the Town offers. Together the three add roughly $105,000 to the 2027 
budget, and the workers’ compensation share of that is challengeable — see Matters Requiring 
Board Direction.

• Bag purchases are zeroed for 2027. The $40,000 of inventory on hand covers the year, which 
reduces fund expenditures from $82,179 to $72,679. This is a one-year measure,  and the line

This fund needs a decision 
Bag revenue has collapsed. The 2025 audited actual was $41,058 against a $79,368 budget — a 
48% shortfall confirmed in the auditor's management discussion. Expenses were $80,027. The fund 
lost $38,969 in 2025 and $16,479 in 2024, and only $4,426 of revenue had been recorded by 31 
March 2026. 
A $40,000 correction for unrecorded bag inventory on hand raises funds available at 31 
December 2025 from $129,929 to $169,929 and lets the Town skip bag purchases in 2027 
altogether. Both are welcome, and neither is revenue. The fund still plans to spend $31,621 more 
than it collects in 2027, and once the inventory is consumed the purchase line returns. At this rate 
the fund exhausts its balance in about four years. 
As an enterprise fund this is meant to be self-supporting. A General Fund subsidy would put its 
TABOR enterprise status at risk, since an enterprise must receive less than 10% of annual revenue 
in grants from Colorado state and local governments. The Board should decide in 2027 between 
raising bag prices, restructuring the vendor discount, folding the service into the General Fund as 
a governmental activity, or winding it down. What should not happen is a third consecutive 
budget built on revenue that has not materialized. 
The auditor should also confirm the period and treatment of the $40,000 — if it relates to 2025 or 
earlier it is a prior period adjustment requiring restatement rather than a current -year entry.

What the increase applies to 
Gross wages, overtime and compensatory time buyout, bonuses, part-time and seasonal wages, 
and on-call pay — together with the payroll costs that move with wages: the Social Security 
match at 6.2%, Medicare at 1.45%, unemployment insurance at 0.2%, the FAM LI premium at 
0.45% and the ICMA retirement match at 8%. 
What it does not apply to, and why 
• Health, dental, vision and dependent coverage are premium-driven rather than wage-
driven and are now budgeted at the CEBT January 2027 renewal of +13.00% on every 
medical plan the Town offers - roughly $77,000 across all funds. CEBT attributes the increase 
to a 9% assumed medical trend and a pool loss ratio in the 88–93% band, against a ten-year 
average increase of 6.5%. At 33.2% of wages across all funds, and roughly 42% in 
Administration and Marketing, employer health costs are doing more work in the Town’s 
compensation package than base pay is. 
• Workers’ compensation is now budgeted at the 2027 CIRSA quotation of $211,766.90, up 
18.5% on 2026 and $14,692 above what the draft carried. CIRSA reports pool-wide rates 
FALLING for 2027, so the whole increase comes from how payroll was allocated across j ob 
classifications on the renewal application rather than from pricing. At roughly 10% of wages, 
against a municipal norm of 2% to 5%, this line should be challenged before the contribution 
is finalized on 18 December — see Matters Requiring Board Direction, item 5. 
• Medical benefit allowances and uniform allowances are fixed dollar benefits, not 
percentages of pay. 
• Board of Trustees and Planning Commission compensation are set by ordinance. Changing 
them requires separate Board action. 
One observation on method. A flat across-the-board increase is simple and transparent, but it 
preserves whatever compression already exists in the pay scale rather than correcting it. If the 
Board intends a merit or step component, or different treatment for seasonal staff, that should 
be modeled separately. 
A separate memorandum accompanies this budget setting out how the Town’s wages and 
benefits compare with those of similar Colorado municipalities, and what should be pulled from 
the Colorado Municipal League compensation survey before the Board settles the  2027 figure.

Matters Requiring Board Direction 
The following need a decision or a correction before this budget can be brought forward for 
adoption. They are listed in order of urgency. 
1. The Marina Fund appropriation for 2026 is understated by $16,350   IMMEDIATE 
A subtotal formula in the adopted 2026 budget workbook returns zero where it should return the 
sum of seven Purchased Services lines. The Marina total of $480,877 in the appropriation 
resolution therefore excludes $16,350 that the underlying detail budgets, and the Treasurer's 
monthly statements already report the corrected $497,227. C.R.S. §29-1-110 prohibits spending in 
excess of an appropriation. Staff should confirm 2026 Marina spending against $480,877 and, if it 
will exceed that figure, bring a supplemental appropriation under §29-1-109 before the money is 
spent — a supplemental cannot be adopted retroactively. 
2. The March 2026 fund balance summary understates the Marina Fund by $933,999   
IMMEDIATE 
The Assets and Liabilities Summary presented to the Board reports the Marina fund balance as 
$103,579. The general ledger at the same date reports $1,037,578.50. The other four funds tie 
exactly, and the summary's total of $7,212,046 was footed on the incorrect figure — it should be 
$8,146,045. The schedule should be corrected and reissued, and a standing tie -out to the trial 
balance added before future packets are distributed. 
3. Reconciliation of the general ledger to the audited statements   BEFORE ADOPTION 
Opening General Fund equity components in the ledger total $4,339,153 against an audited 
balance of $3,040,158, and emergency reserves are carried at $117,000 against $186,000 
restricted in the audit. These are the 2027 opening balances. The audit adjustme nts should be 
posted, each fund reconciled, and the opening balances confirmed by the auditor before 
migration to OpenGov. 
4. Is the $200,000 paving appropriation a total or an increase?   BOARD DECISION 
This budget sets the Capital Improvement Fund pavement line at $200,000 as the total. For 
context, 2026 adopted $250,000 and 2025 actual spending was $395,110, so read as a total this 
is a reduction. If the intention is $200,000 of new money on top of the existing program, the figure 
should be $450,000. Note also that the 2026 Budget Message states paving of $95,000 while the 
adopted budget carries $270,000 across two funds. 
5. The workers’ compensation increase is a classification problem, not a rate problem   
IMMEDIATE 
CIRSA’s 2027 workers’ compensation quotation is $218,411.90 before credits, less a $6,645.00 loss 
control audit credit, for a net $211,766.90. Against $178,757.66 in 2026 at the same $1,000 
deductible that is an increase of $33,009 or 18.5%, and $14,692 above the $197,075 the draft 
budget carried. CIRSA’s cover letter states that pool-wide workers’ compensation rates are 
FALLING for 2027 — so none of the Town’s increase is rate-driven. It comes entirely from how 
payroll was allocated across job classifications on the renewal application. The largest swings: 
Streets, Roads and Bridge payroll rose 152% from $72,500 to $182,533, adding $28,703; Cemetery 
Operations appear for the first time at $69,032 of payroll, adding $16,256; Garage and Vehicle 
Maintenance rose 646%; while Waterworks fell 25% and Street Cleaning and Snow Removal fell 
46%.

Closing 
This budget is built on audited figures, presented in the Town's new account structure, and 
honest about what is assumed rather than known. Where a number rests on a placeholder — 
capital outlay, debt service, health insurance, the Water Department vehicle  — this book says so 
rather than presenting an estimate as a decision.

real infrastructure to Grand Lake at little local cost. It also has recurring expenditure running

and a difficult one addressed late.
