SUBJECT: Ghosts & Gourds Gathering 2026 event review   
SUMMARY  
Ghosts & Gourds was held on Saturday, October 24, 2026, in the Town of Grand Lake. Attendance grew from last year: 
the car counter recorded 1,781 vehicles, up 10.5% from 2025. The event spent $4,278.64 against its $4,200.00 budget, 
finishing $78.64 (1.9%) over budget. 
Community enthusiasm was evident throughout the day. All 11 pallets of pumpkins found new homes, with none left by 
the end of the event. Sagebrush BBQ generously donated four 5-gallon jugs of apple cider, and every drop was enjoyed 
before the event wrapped up. The 8th grade class from East Grand Middle School volunteered their time running face 
painting, handing out pumpkin-painting supplies and assisting staff with the games, adding to the festive spirit of the day. 
They raised $180 in donations for the annual trip to Washington, D, C. Stan Yan once again provided festive caricature 
drawings for attendees, which are always a huge hit. The costume contest was a highlight of this year’s event, with 30 to 
40 enthusiastic participants and Mike Lagfoged serving as emcee.

New for 2026, the Grand Lake Christkindlmarket will bring a traditional German-style holiday market to Grand Lake, 
featuring vendors, food, live music, beer and mulled wine. The market is organized by Grand Lake Christkindl Market 
LLC, a local business formed in May 2026 by Isaac Elliot.

MEMORANDUM 
 
TO:  Mayor Christina Bergquist and the Board of Trustees FROM:  Steve Kudron, Town Manager 
DATE:  November 9, 2026  
SUBJECT: Resolution approving Grand Foundation promissory note to close Space to Create funding; 
direction to the Mayor to sign  
Mayor and Trustees: 
Staff recommends the Board adopt the attached resolution approving a $175,000 promissory note with The Grand 
Foundation, which closes the final funding gap on Space to Create Grand Lake, and authorizing and directing the Mayor 
to sign the note, deed of trust and assignment of rents. The note carries 2% fixed interest paid quarterly, a one-year term 
with principal due at maturity, and no prepayment penalty. It is secured by the Space to Create residential building and its 
rents.  
BACKGROUND  
● September 13, 2025. The Town broke ground on Space to Create: nine workforce live/work units with 
storefronts, the Grand Spirit Makerspace and a transit stop on the Town-owned site at Park Avenue and Hancock 
Street. 
● July 17, 2026. The Certificate of Occupancy was issued. The same day, staff submitted an Impact Investment 
application to The Grand Foundation for $175,000 to close the project’s remaining gap. 
● August 1 and September 18, 2026. The residences went into service under Grand County Housing Authority 
management, and the Town celebrated the official opening. 
● October 12, 2026. The Grand Foundation issued the attached promissory note for the Town’s signature.  
WHY THE LOAN IS NEEDED  
All project costs are now paid. Final project cost is $6,162,256, including Elk Mountain’s final pay applications and 
$70,000 in reimbursable Town labor to close the grant funding. Every committed grant, Town contribution and donation 
together totals $6,034,853, leaving a gap of $127,403 even after all committed funds are collected. The Grand Foundation 
note closes that gap with a $47,597 cushion. 
Source Amount Share 
Colorado Creative Industries grant $3,000,000 48.3% 
Strong Communities grant (80% of actual eligible cost) $2,011,902 32.4% 
Grand Lake Creative District fundraising $517,451 8.3% 
Town Affordable Housing Fund (20% match) $488,000 7.9% 
Pledged donations, not yet collected $17,500 0.3% 
Grand Foundation note (this item) $175,000 2.8% 
Total sources $6,209,853 100% 
Final project cost $6,162,256  
Cushion after close-out $47,597   
The Strong Communities grant reimburses the Town after it pays. DOLA allowed the 20% match to be counted on actual 
eligible costs, which raised the reimbursement to $2,011,902. The Town has paid all $6,162,256 and advanced about 
$1.39 million from reserves while waiting on reimbursements. As of closeout, $700,000 of that has been returned; the 
remaining receivables, about $559,719, return the rest. Those funds repay the Town’s own outlay; they do not cover the 
$127,403 gap. That is why this note matters.

THE LOAN TERMS  
Term Note dated October 12, 2026 
Principal $175,000, or so much as is advanced 
Interest rate 2.00% fixed, 365-day year 
Interest payments Quarterly, within five days after each calendar quarter ends 
Term and repayment One year; principal and accrued interest due in a single payment on October 12, 2027 
Prepayment Allowed at any time, in whole or part, without penalty 
Security Deed of Trust and Assignment of Rents on the Space to Create residential building, 1128 Park Avenue, Units B–J 
Default 8.00% default rate after 10 days’ written notice and opportunity to cure; 5% late charge on late payments  
The Town asked for an interest-free, 24-month loan. The Foundation approved a one-year loan at 2%, well below 
commercial lending rates, with no fees and no prepayment penalty. Staff considers these reasonable terms for gap 
financing on a municipal housing project. Pledging the residential building and its rents keeps the makerspace and the rest 
of the site unencumbered. 
Principal waiver. Megan Ledin, Executive Director of The Grand Foundation, told staff that after two years of 
satisfactory performance on the note, the Foundation would consider waiving the principal. That is not written into the 
note, and it is a willingness to consider, not a commitment. Staff will ask the Foundation to confirm its intent in writing. 
Until then, the Board should approve the note assuming the Town repays it in full.  
CORRECTIONS TO THE NOTE  
Staff has marked up the Foundation’s draft with the following changes, which go to the Foundation and the Town 
Attorney before signing: 
1.  Interest rate. Section 1(b) now reads “two percent (2.00%),” matching the approved rate. 
2.  Collateral. Section 5 now defines the Property as the residential building at 1128 Park Avenue, Units B–J, and its 
rents. The Deed of Trust needs a legal description of that parcel as Exhibit A. 
3.  The Town Attorney will also confirm the note’s structure meets TABOR’s limits on obligations that extend 
beyond one budget year, since the note runs from 2026 into 2027.  
FISCAL IMPACT  
At 2%, interest is about $3,500 a year, or $875 a quarter. The first payment, due in early January 2027, would be about 
$767 for October 12 through December 31. Staff proposes paying interest from Space to Create enterprise fund rents, and 
will include it in the 2027 budget. 
The $175,000 principal is due October 12, 2028 and will be appropriated in the 2028 budget. About $47,597 of it would 
come from the project cushion and $127,403 from Town resources: the Space to Create enterprise fund, the Affordable 
Housing Fund or the general fund. If the Foundation later renews the note and waives principal, the appropriation would 
not be spent. The Town’s cash contribution to the project remains $488,000 from the Affordable Housing Fund.  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION  
Staff recommends the Board adopt the resolution approving The Grand Foundation promissory note and authorizing and

EXECUTION ASSESSMENT 
The project was delivered on schedule, close to budget and with very little Town money at risk. Its weak 
point was cash flow: reimbursement grants left the Town carrying about $1.39 million, of which $700,000 
has been returned, and a small permanent gap surfaced only at the end. 
What went well 
● Speed. About 10 months from groundbreaking to Certificate of Occupancy, using NorthStar 
modular units set on site and a separate post-frame makerspace. 
● Cost certainty. A Guaranteed Maximum Price contract with Elk Mountain held construction cost; 
final cost landed within 0.4% of the July 30 ledger. 
● Leverage. $488,000 of Town cash drew $5,546,853 in grants and donations, and DOLA counted 
actual costs toward the match. The full $3,000,000 CCI grant has been drawn.

SUBJECT: Resolution waiving sign code provisions so the Humphries Historic Landmark Sign at 1100 Grand 
Avenue can be restored to its historic condition  
Mayor and Trustees: 
Staff recommends the Board adopt the attached resolution waiving the provisions of the Town’s sign code that would otherwise 
keep the Humphries sign at 1100 Grand Avenue from being restored as it originally stood. The Grand Lake Area Historical 
Society (GLAHS) has received a grant to restore the sign. The waiver lets the restoration match the sign’s historic size, location, 
design, colors and lighting rather than the standards that apply to new signs today. Building, electrical and permit requirements 
still apply.  
BACKGROUND  
● More than 80 years on Grand Avenue. The Humphries Store has been a landmark business for over 100 years, and 
the Humphries sign has hung on the building for more than 80 of them. It is one of the most recognizable pieces of the 
historic streetscape on Grand Avenue. 
● November 11, 2019. At the request of Jim Cervenka, the Board adopted Resolution No. 15-2019 (7–0), designating the 
sign at Lots 7 & 8, Block 4, Town of Grand Lake (1100 Grand Avenue) as a Historic Landmark Sign under Municipal 
Code § 6-5-2(B)1 and allowing restoration to begin. That section is how the Board allows an owner to retain a 
nonconforming sign. 
● March 6, 2026. The Board sent History Colorado a letter supporting GLAHS’s grant request. The restoration will 
repaint the sign field and lettering and restore the lighting of the lettering. The building owners are paying 40% of the 
cost. John C. Akolt, the fourth generation of the family that owns the building, and Jim Cervenka have led the 
restoration effort. 
● October 2026. GLAHS received a History Colorado State Historical Fund grant for the restoration. GLAHS has asked 
the Board to grant the waiver before it commissions final restoration plans, so the work can be designed to the sign’s 
historic condition from the start.  
WHY A WAIVER IS NEEDED  
Resolution 15-2019 lets the sign stay where it is even though it does not meet today’s sign code. It does not say what happens 
when the sign is substantially rebuilt. Rebuilding worn components and re-lighting the lettering could be read as replacing or 
altering a nonconforming sign, which would normally require the new work to meet current standards. Meeting those standards 
would mean changing the very features that make the sign historic, and the grant pays for a restoration, not a redesign. 
The resolution waives, for this sign only, the sign code provisions that would otherwise apply to the restored sign, to the extent 
needed to return it to its historic condition. Depending on the final restoration plans, these may include standards for: 
1.  Sign area, height, projection and location on the building; 
2.  Illumination, including the type and placement of lighting on the lettering; 
3.  Materials and construction; and 
4.  Limits on repairing, restoring or replacing nonconforming signs. 
Because restoration plans have not yet been prepared, the resolution describes these standards generally rather than citing 
individual code sections. The conditions below tie the waiver to the sign’s documented historic condition, and staff will check 
the plans against that record when the sign permit is reviewed. 
The Board considered a similar request on February 23, 2026, to allow continued use, repair and maintenance of six 
nonconforming signs at the Historic Rapids Lodge. Granting this waiver applies the same approach to a sign the Board has 
already designated as a Historic Landmark Sign.  
CONDITIONS IN THE RESOLUTION  
The waiver is limited so it cannot be used for anything other than restoring this sign: 
1.  It applies only to the Humphries sign at its existing location at 1100 Grand Avenue, restored to its historic size, design, 
colors, lettering and lighting as documented by historic photographs and records. 
2.  Restoration plans must be submitted with the sign permit application, and the Town Manager or designee must confirm 
they match the sign’s historic condition before the permit is issued. 
3.  A sign permit, and any building and electrical permits, are still required. Building and electrical codes are not waived. 
4.  The building owner must maintain the sign in good repair. 
Page 29 of 306
5.  The waiver ends if the sign is removed (other than temporarily for the restoration), relocated, enlarged or materially 
changed from its historic design. Any such change comes back to the Board.  
FISCAL IMPACT  
None to the Town. The restoration is paid for by the GLAHS grant and the building owners’ contribution. Normal permit fees 
apply unless the Board directs otherwise.  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION  
Staff agrees that restoring the Humphries sign in its historic condition supports the Town’s effort to preserve the historic 
character of Grand Lake, and recommends the Board adopt the resolution.

commonly referred to as 1100 Grand Avenue, to its historic condition, subject to the conditions in the resolution.”

Town of Grand Lake Public Comment and Meeting Recording Policy 
1. Background and Purpose 
This Policy establishes a consistent, adopted procedure for public participation at meetings of the Town 
of Grand Lake, including in-person comment, written comment, and audience recording of meetings. It 
is intended to encourage public participation while allowing meetings to proceed in an orderly and 
efficient manner. Nothing in this Policy is intended to limit the right of any citizen to exercise their right 
to public access and participation in public meetings held by the Town. 
2. Applicability 
This Policy applies to all regular and special meetings and public hearings of the Board of Trustees, the 
Planning Commission, and any other Town boards, commissions, and committees. 
3. Definitions 
a. "Body" means the Board of Trustees, the Planning Commission, or other Town board, commission, or 
committee to which this Policy applies. 
b. "Presiding Officer" means the Mayor, Chair, or other member presiding over the meeting. 
c. "Public Comment" means the agenda item set aside for members of the public to address the Body on 
matters not scheduled for a public hearing. 
d. "Public Hearing" means a hearing required by statute or the Municipal Code, conducted under the 
procedures for that hearing. 
e. "Recording" means any audio or video recording, photography, or live streaming of a meeting by a 
person other than the Town. 
f. "Written Comment" means a comment submitted in writing to the Town Clerk under Section 5 of this 
Policy. 
4. In-Person Public Comment 
a. Each regular meeting of a Body will include a Public Comment agenda item. The Presiding Officer may 
also open comment on a specific agenda item before the Body takes action. 
b. Speakers are asked to sign in on the sheet provided by the Town Clerk before the meeting begins. 
When called, speakers will state their name and may state their residency. 
Page 37 of 306
c. Each speaker is allotted three (3) minutes and may speak once per comment period. A speaker may 
not yield time to another speaker. The Presiding Officer may extend or reduce the time limit for all 
speakers equally, based on the number of people wishing to speak. 
d. The total Public Comment period may be limited to thirty (30) minutes. Remaining comments may be 
heard at the end of the meeting or submitted in writing. 
e. Speakers shall address their comments to the Body as a whole, not to individual members or staff. 
f. The Body will generally not respond to, discuss, or act on Public Comment matters that are not on the 
agenda. The Body may direct staff to follow up or place the matter on a future agenda. 
g. Comment during a Public Hearing follows the procedure announced by the Presiding Officer for that 
hearing and any requirements of state law or the Town of Grand Lake Municipal Code. 
h. Public comment may be made orally and in person at the meeting held at Town Hall or in writing 
under Section 5. Remote public comment, including by phone, video conference, or live chat, is not 
permitted, except where deemed necessary by the Body for purposes of a public hearing. 
5. Written Public Comment 
a. Written comments may be submitted to the Town Clerk by: 
• Email to acarrell@toglco.com; or 
• Mail or hand delivery to Town Hall, 1026 Park Avenue, Grand Lake, CO 80447. 
b. Public Comments for purposes of public meetings sent to individual Trustees, Commissioners, or 
other staff are not considered received by the Town under this Policy. 
c. Written comments should include the author's name, physical address, and the agenda item or topic 
addressed. 
d. Written comments must be received by the Town Clerk by 10:00 a.m. on the Wednesday before the 
meeting, which is the meeting packet deadline. Comments received by the deadline will be included in 
the meeting packet. Comments received after the deadline will not be included in the packet and will be 
distributed to the Body at the next regular meeting, unless the matter is a Public Hearing that remains 
open. 
e. Written comments will not be read aloud at the meeting. The Presiding Officer will note for the record 
that written comments were received and distributed, and the Town Clerk will retain them as part of the 
meeting record. 
f. Written comments are public records and may be disclosed under the Colorado Open Records Act, 
C.R.S. § 24-72-200.1, et seq. (the "CORA"), and the Town's Public Records Policy. 
6. Rules of Decorum 
a. To keep meetings orderly, members of the public are asked to: 
• Speak only when recognized by the Presiding Officer; 
Page 38 of 306
• Stay within the time allowed; 
• Refrain from disruptive applause, shouting, or other interruptions; and 
• Keep signs and displays in a manner that does not block the view of others. 
b. A person who substantially disrupts the meeting may be warned by the Presiding Officer. If the 
disruption continues, the Presiding Officer may ask the person to leave, call a recess, or request 
assistance from law enforcement. 
c. The Presiding Officer will not limit a speaker based on the viewpoint expressed, including criticism of 
the Town, its officials, or staff. No speaker shall use fighting words or commit any acts that violate the 
law, such as making or conveying threats of violence or harm or committing disorderly conduct. 
7. Audience Recording and Live Streaming 
a. Members of the public may record or live stream open public meetings, subject to the reasonable 
requirements in this Section. 
b. To eliminate distractions, tripping hazards, and ensure unobstructed views of meetings, all privately-
owned cameras and video recorders shall be located only at the rear of the meeting room or as 
otherwise designated by the Town. To protect public safety, no cameras, tripods, or crews may be 
permitted in any exit path or pathway and cords may not be run across any pathway or exit. 
c. Stationary equipment is subject to the following additional requirements: 
• Equipment may not be placed at or behind the dais or staff tables; and 
• Equipment must be set up before the meeting begins and may not be moved during the 
meeting in a manner that disrupts the meeting. 
d. Recording may not interfere with the conduct of the meeting, including through the use of bright 
lights, flash photography, loud equipment, or where equipment is moved around the room. The 
Presiding Officer may ask a person to adjust or relocate equipment that causes a disruption. 
e. The Town's recording is the official audio or video record of the meeting. Recordings made by the 
public are not official records of the Town. 
8. Notice and Amendment 
a. A summary of this Policy, including written comment instructions and deadlines, will be printed on 
each agenda and posted on the Town website. Copies will be available at meetings and from the Town 
Clerk. 
b. The Board of Trustees may amend this Policy by resolution. The Presiding Officer may waive a 
procedural provision for a specific meeting when necessary, provided it is applied equally to all speakers. 
c. This Policy replaces all prior unwritten practices for public comment and recording at Town meetings.

firewood. To qualify, the owner must already have mitigated the HIZ, have a wildfire risk assessment from 
GCWC or the fire district, and follow the terms of a cost share award letter. 
How this year’s work fits. The recommended 2026 award treats the HIZ and the driveway egress, which is the first step 
in GCWC’s plan and the prerequisite for Strategic Fuels cost share on Phase 1. GCWC suggests doing the HIZ next year. 
Staff still recommends doing it now, for three reasons: the hazard trees near the buildings are the most urgent risk on the 
site, the current prices expire this month, and GCWC’s 2026 fund is already fully allocated. Doing the HIZ in 2027 might 
earn up to $6,000 in HIZ cost share, but that is less than the risk and the likely cost of rebidding. Staff will confirm with
