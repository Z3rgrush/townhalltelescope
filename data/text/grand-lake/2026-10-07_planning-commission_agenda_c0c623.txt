A. Resolution 01-2026: Variance Request from Side Yard Setback Requirements for 
Property Located at 842 Lake Avenue 
Emily Weber of Ayres Associates presented the variance request for 536 Cairns 
Avenue on behalf of property owner Kay Shaffer. The request is for a variance 
from the required 10-foot side-yard setback to allow an approximately 2-foot 
setback for a new stairway providing access from the existing residence to the 
existing boathouse. 
Ms. Weber explained that the property has an existing nonconforming residence, 
steep topography, and limited access to the shoreline. The proposed stairway 
would provide private access separate from the neighboring property. She noted 
that the stairway complies with applicable shoreline regulations. 
Staff reviewed the variance criteria and found that the site’s unique conditions 
limit reasonable alternatives, the circumstances were not created by the property 
owner, and the proposal would not adversely impact neighboring properties or 
public safety. Staff recommended approval of the variance request pursuant to 
Section 12-3-5 of the Town Code. 
William Carrier, property owner of 842 Lake Avenue, also spoke on behalf of his 
mother in support of the variance request. He explained that the existing residence 
is nonconforming and that the original shared staircase was located along the west 
side of the property. He stated that the neighboring property has been used as a

FROM: Emily Weber, Contract Staff - Planning Department 
SUBJECT: QUASI JUDICIAL (PUBLIC HEARING) – Resolution 03-2026 –Variance Request from Setback 
Requirements for Property Located at 1670 Sunnyside Drive 
Overview 
• Property Owner/Applicant: Aragorn, LLC / Larry Broderick 
• Location: 1670 Sunnyside Drive 
• Zoning: Single Family Residential – High Density 
• Request: The applicant is requesting a variance from 
the required 10-foot side yard setback to allow an 
existing 64-square-foot utility shed to remain in its 
current location.  
Background 
The subject property is located at 1670 Sunnyside Drive. 
The applicant owns this parcel as well as adjacent 
residential lots and uses the property in conjunction with 
those lots. The site contains a landscaped garden area 
with water features, pathways, and associated site 
improvements. 
 
The property includes an existing 64-square-foot utility 
shed containing a toilet to support operation and 
maintenance of the site. Additional improvements on the 
property include a gazebo with an outdoor kitchen. The 
utility shed was constructed behind an existing 10-foot 
fence that received variance approval from the Town in 
2021. 
Staff Analysis 
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-3-6, a property owner 
may request a variance from zoning regulations through the Board of Adjustment. The variance process 
requires notice by certified mail to property owners within 200 feet of the subject property and publication in 
a newspaper of general circulation. Staff has confirmed that all notice requirements have been satisfied for 
this application. 
 
Town Code Section 12-2-12 Regulations for Single Family Residential – High Density (RSH) 
Properties zoned RSH are subject to minimum setbacks of 25 feet for the front yard, 10 feet for side yards, 
and 10 feet for the rear yard. The applicant is requesting a variance from the required 10-foot side yard 
setback to allow an existing utility shed to remain in its current location.

Page 2 of 3  
Based on the submitted survey, the shed is located approximately two feet from the side property line, 
resulting in an encroachment of approximately eight feet into the required setback. Accordingly, a variance is 
required for the structure to remain. 
Variance Criteria of Approval 
Town Code Section 12-3-5 establishes the following review criteria for variance requests, paraphrased 
below: 
1. By reason of exceptional shape, size, or topography of the lot, or other exceptional situation or 
condition of the building or land, practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship would result to the 
owner from strict enforcement of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-
29 (if applicable). 
2. A literal interpretation of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable) would deprive the applicant of rights commonly enjoyed by other properties in the same 
zoning district. 
3. The special conditions and circumstances do not result from actions taken by the applicant. 
4. Granting the variance will not confer a special privilege upon the applicant that is denied to other 
lands, structures, or buildings within the same zoning district. 
5. Granting the variance will not be detrimental to the public good and will not substantially impair the 
intent and purpose of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable). 
 
The applicant has submitted a written narrative explaining how the request satisfies these criteria. A 
summary of the applicant's statements is provided below, and the full narrative is attached to this report: 
• The applicant states that the property's irregular shape, steep topography, large rock outcroppings, 
and other natural features significantly limited the number of practical locations available for the 
utility shed. According to the applicant, the selected location was one of the few areas capable of 
accommodating the structure while minimizing disturbance to the site. 
• The applicant indicates that the location of existing water, sewer, and electrical infrastructure 
influenced placement of the shed. The applicant states that locating the structure near these 
utilities reduced the need for additional excavation and site disturbance and allowed the facility to 
function as intended. 
• The applicant states that the shed was constructed adjacent to an existing stone retaining wall that 
previously received variance approval from the Town. According to the applicant, the wall provides 
visual screening, integrates the structure into the existing site design, and helps mitigate potential 
sight distance concerns. 
• The applicant states that the encroachment was not intentional and resulted from the understanding 
that a structure of this size did not require a building permit. The applicant further notes that the 
structure was constructed in good faith and without the intent to circumvent Town regulations. 
• The applicant states that the shed is largely screened from public view by the existing stone wall, 
mature landscaping, and surrounding vegetation. The applicant contends that the structure does not 
negatively affect adjacent properties, impair views, create safety concerns, or alter the character of 
the surrounding neighborhood. 
Board of Adjustment Discussion and Determination 
The Board of Adjustment is asked to evaluate the application based on staff's analysis, public testimony, 
the applicant's evidence, and the applicable review criteria contained in Town Code Section 12-3-5.

RE: Application for Variance from the Town of Grand Lake 
  
A. By way of introduction, this is a Variance request to construct in a “setback”, a 64 sq. ft. 
utility shed with toilet, a corner of which was constructed a couple of feet into the Town’s 
“zoned” setback requirement. Construction into the setback happened because we were led to 
believe by the Town Planner at the time that a structure under 120 sq ft., did not require a 
building permit. The shed was situated where it is for reasons having to do with the shape and 
topography of the property as well as access to the utilities serving the property. Moreover, it 
was constructed behind, and very close to a 10-ft. high stone wall which was approved by 
Variance by the Town Planning Commission in 2021. 
  
The subject utility shed is located on a property owned by Applicant which we call the 
“Secret Garden”. The lots upon which the Secret Garden is constructed used to be called 
“Coker Lots A & B,” which Applicant purchased in 2009–2012 for the purpose of 
constructing the Secret Garden. Applicant contracted with Pinon Sage Landscaping Architects 
to design the Secret Garden in 2012, which they did. Applicant then contracted Harrington 
Landscapes (Paul Harrington) in 2020 to be general contractor on the project. In 2021, Paul 
Harrington hired an old friend and business associate, Waterscapes, Inc., to construct the 
“water feature” aspect of the Secret Garden — three streams and a pond. Paul Harrington 
worked closely on the project with Grand Lake’s Town Planner, Kim White. 
  
a) The location of the utility shed in question was driven by the shape and topography of 
the property. The property includes three (3) definitive ridges (some sections, along 
West Portal Road and Grand Avenue, with 60º slopes), as well as massive boulders 
above and below ground throughout. 
  
     Additionally, the placement of the shed was heavily influenced by the location of the 
three utilities that serve the property (water, sewer, electric) and where they enter the 
Secret Garden property. These three utilities run under Grand Avenue from 1620 
Grand Avenue (a Lot owned by Applicant) and are directly across the street from the 
Secret Garden. Access by these utilities to the Secret Garden is immediately to the 
north and west of the stone wall. The utility shed is immediately behind the stone wall 
and as close to the utility hookups as we could manage and as close as we could get to 
the “pump-vault” (3 water pumps) which sits in the pond approximately 19 ft. east of 
the utility shed. 
  
     For informational purposes, the three pumps in the pump-vault are powered by three 
electrical controllers in the utility shed. The water that feeds the pumps that supply the 
streams also runs through the utility shed. The sewer for the toilet exits from the utility 
Page 11 of 49
shed and runs under Grand Avenue to the sewer main across the street at 1620 Grand 
Avenue.         
  
Our objective in locating the utility shed where it is — was to avoid degrading the 
property as much as possible, as well as to save money in cutting trenches through the 
very rough, boulder-strewn, topography. 
  
b) Many Variances have been approved by the Board of Adjustment over the years in the 
same district and under the same terms of the same zoning regulations. To reject my 
application would deprive the Applicant of rights enjoyed by others. 
  
c) The special conditions and circumstances of this construction are the result of the 
exceptional shape and topography of the property as well as the presence of massive 
boulders throughout (see “a” above). 
  
d) Granting this Variance request will not confer any special privilege to Applicant. 
  
e) Granting of this Variance does not pose a detriment to the public good. The Variance 
requested is for approval of a building permit for a 64 sq. ft. utility shed (with toilet), a 
corner of which encroaches 2 feet into the town setback but which is behind a 10-ft. 
high, stone wall (described above), which itself was approved by Variance by the town 
five years ago. Moreover, it is surrounded by sizable trees and is nearly invisible from 
outside the wall.

SUBJECT: QUASI JUDICIAL (PUBLIC HEARING) – Resolution 08-2026 –Variance Request from Shoreline 
Setback Requirements for Property Located at 134 Mad Moose Lane. 
Overview 
• Property Owners: Seth and Mandy Tamisiea 
• Applicants: Michael and Debra Taber 
• Location: 134 Mad Moose Ln 
• Zoning: Single Family Residential – Medium 
Density 
• Request: The applicant is requesting a variance 
from the required 30-foot water quality setback 
to allow future development within the setback 
area on the property. Approval of the request 
would reduce the setback to approximately 13 
feet from the irrigation pond's high-water line on 
the property. 
Background 
The subject property is a vacant residential lot 
located at 134 Mad Moose Lane within the Ridge at 
Elk Creek Subdivision. The original subdivision plat 
established a no-build area associated with the 
Howard Ditch on several lots within the subdivision. 
Subsequent to approval of the subdivision, the Town 
adopted the Shoreline and Surface Water 
Regulations, including the requirement for a 30-foot 
water quality setback from streams, lakes, ponds, 
irrigation ditches, wetlands, and other surface water 
features. 
 
As a result, development of the subject property is constrained by both the platted no-build area and the 
Town's shoreline setback requirements. The applicant is seeking a variance from the shoreline setback 
requirements while maintaining compliance with the platted no-build area. The application is not associated 
with a specific development proposal at this time. Rather, the applicants are requesting approval of a 
reduced water quality setback to establish a future building envelope for residential development. No 
building plans, grading plans, drainage plans, or site development plans have been submitted as part of this 
application.

Staff Analysis 
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-2-29, a property owner may request a variance from the Shoreline and 
Surface Water Regulations. Variance requests are reviewed by the Planning Commission at a public hearing, 
with a recommendation forwarded to the Board of Trustees for final consideration. Notice requirements 
include certified mail to property owners within 200 feet of the property and publication in a newspaper of 
general circulation. Staff has verified that all required notice procedures have been satisfied. 
 
Town Code Section 12-2-29 Shoreline and Surface Water Regulations 
A minimum 30-foot water quality setback is required from streams, lakes, ponds, and other surface water 
features. The applicants are requesting a variance to allow a reduced water quality setback of approximately 
13 feet from the irrigation pond on the property. The requested reduction would provide additional buildable 
area while maintaining the existing platted no-build area associated with the Howard Ditch. 
Variance Criteria of Approval 
Town Code Section 12-2-29 establishes the following factors for consideration when evaluating a request for 
a variance from the shoreline regulations: 
1. The shape, size, topography, slope, soils, vegetation, and other physical characteristics of the 
property. 
2. The location of all bodies of water on the property, including any water bodies located adjacent to 
property boundaries. 
3. The location and extent of the proposed encroachment into the required setback. 
4. Whether alternative site designs are feasible that would reduce or eliminate the need for the setback 
encroachment. 
5. The sensitivity of the affected water body and any associated critical habitat areas. 
6. The intensity of existing and proposed land uses adjacent to the water body affected by the 
requested encroachment. 
7. Potential impacts on floodplains and stream functions. A variance shall not be approved if the 
requested reduction would result in a setback that is narrower than the applicable floodplain. 
 
The applicants have provided a written narrative describing how they believe the request satisfies the 
approval criteria. The complete narrative is attached to this report. A summary of the narrative is provided 
below: 
• Application of the 30-foot water quality setback, combined with the existing platted no-build area, 
significantly limits the buildable portion of the lot. The applicants estimate that strict application of 
the setback would leave a building envelope of approximately 41 feet by 33 feet. 
• The lot was platted prior to adoption of the Town's current shoreline setback regulations. According 
to the applicants, the resulting hardship stems from the interaction of the existing plat restrictions 
and subsequently adopted regulations rather than actions taken by the current property owners. 
• Construction of a typical single-family residence comparable to others within the subdivision would 
be challenging under the existing setback requirements. The applicants note that a standard two-car 
garage would occupy a substantial portion of the remaining building area. 
• Future development is anticipated to occur outside of the platted no-build area and would be 
designed to minimize impacts on the adjacent irrigation pond and surrounding natural features. 
• Any future residence would be subject to additional Town and County review processes, including 
applicable drainage, grading, erosion control, and building permit requirements.
