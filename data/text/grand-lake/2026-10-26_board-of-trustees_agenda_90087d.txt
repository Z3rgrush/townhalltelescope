MEMORANDUM 
 
TO Mayor Christina Bergquist and the Board of Trustees 
FROM Steve Kudron, Town Manager 
DATE October 22, 2026 
 
RE:  Board workshop October 26, 2026 — Boots, Bass & Big Air downtown winter event concept  
Mayor and Trustees: 
Cooper Roberts of Troublesome Group, LLC proposes Boots, Bass & Big Air, a free evening event on Grand Avenue on 
Saturday, January 30, 2027. The working budget is $40,000. This is a workshop item to introduce the concept. No action 
is requested; staff asks for Board direction before the organizer seeks permits, property approvals or sponsor 
commitments.  
THE CONCEPT  
The event pairs a professional freestyle snowmobile aerial show with a country-meets-EDM DJ set, an emcee and 
coordinated sound and lighting. It is scheduled for the same weekend as Grand Lake Skijoring at Winding River Ranch 
and the Three Lakes ice fishing tournament. The organizer frames it as a complement to those events, giving skijor 
spectators, anglers, the snowmobile community and the Winter Park ski crowd a reason to stay downtown for the 
evening. 
Time Program 
4:30–5:00 p.m. Downtown arrival, DJ warm-up, sponsor recognition 
5:00–5:20 p.m. Country-meets-EDM opening set 
5:20–5:40 p.m. Professional snowmobile aerial demonstration 
5:40–6:00 p.m. DJ feature set 
6:00–6:20 p.m. Second aerial demonstration 
6:20–7:00 p.m. Music and Big-air finale  
Site: Grand Avenue as the center of the event, with Town Park as an adjacent gathering area. A DJ position on the roof 
of the one-story Gifts Unlimited building is shown as a concept only, subject to owner permission, structural review and 
safe access. Champagne Productions is a possible sound and lighting partner, and a professional stunt producer would 
independently approve jump timing, engineering, spectator setbacks and emergency provisions.  
PROPOSED BUDGET  
Item Estimate 
Professional snowmobile show and ramps $13,000 
DJ / featured artist $4,000 
Sound and lighting $5,000 
Rooftop platform / site infrastructure $1,500 
Permits, safety, insurance and security $3,500 
Event development, promotion, coordination, logistics and contingency $13,000 
Total $40,000  
Funding is shown as $20,000 in private sponsorships and $20,000 in “other funding.” The proposal does not say where 
the other funding would come from. All costs are preliminary and subject to vendor quotes and site feasibility.  
THE ORGANIZER  
Cooper Roberts has produced events for the Town before.  
Page 3 of 216
CONSIDERATIONS FOR THE BOARD  
• Same-weekend commitments. The Town has committed to services that maintain parking capacity and to The 
Lift shuttle between town and Winding River Ranch during skijoring. A downtown evening event adds street 
closures, parking demand and staff time on the same weekend. 
• Public safety and liability. A snowmobile jump on a closed street in front of spectators is the highest-risk 
element. Any approval should depend on a qualified stunt producer, engineered ramps, set spectator setbacks, 
a Sheriff and fire/EMS plan, and insurance naming the Town as additional insured, reviewed by CIRSA and the 
Town Attorney. 
• Private property. The rooftop DJ position needs the Gifts Unlimited owner’s consent and a structural review. 
• Town funding. If any of the $20,000 “other funding” is expected from the Town, it is not in the 2027 preliminary 
budget, which goes to public hearing November 9 and adoption November 23. 
• Timeline. January 30 is about 14 weeks away. The organizer’s own next steps (stunt-show quote, site survey, 
Town and property approvals, sponsor commitments) all must happen before any binding bookings. 
• Fit with the Board’s direction. The concept speaks to the retreat goals of multi-night winter visits and the 
snowmobile heritage, but also to the concern about day-tripper congestion downtown.  
DIRECTION REQUESTED  
Staff asks the Board to indicate: 
1. Whether it supports the organizer developing the concept further for January 30, 2027. 
2. Whether Grand Avenue and Town Park are acceptable locations, including a street closure for the evening. 
3. Whether the Town would consider any financial or in-kind contribution, and if so at what level. 
4. The conditions the organizer must meet before a special event permit is considered, such as a safety plan, 
insurance, property owner consent and confirmed sponsor funding. 
If the Board supports moving forward, staff will work with the organizer on a formal application and return to the Board 
with a permit and any funding request.

RE:  Grand PLACES 2050 (now “Grand Places”) Regular Meeting – October 22, 2026, Winter Park Town Hall 
 
PURPOSE 
 
To update the Board on the October 22 regular meeting of the Grand PLACES 2050 regional coalition, which I attended 
on behalf of the Town. Grand PLACES is a coalition of local governments, state and federal land managers, nonprofits 
and businesses working on conservation and outdoor recreation in Grand County. The Town is a participating member 
and pays an annual contribution. The near-final 2025 Annual Report was distributed at the meeting and is attached. 
 
GRAND LAKE UPDATE PROVIDED 
 
• Stormwater: Phase 1 infrastructure work is underway. Phase 2, the Little Columbine Creek restoration 
(simulated beaver structures to build upstream resilience before flows reach Shadow Mountain), is out to bid. 
• Transit: Since The Lift began serving Grand Lake in May, it has become a real way for residents and visitors to 
get to and from Grand Lake without a car. Our Impact Accelerator work on emissions and last-mile trailhead 
access is starting. 
• CDT Gateway Park: The Town’s OLRT request, supported by Headwaters Trails Alliance, has been recommended 
for funding. It would set up a Continental Divide Trail Gateway Park at the Town’s north boundary with Rocky 
Mountain National Park. The final award is pending County action. 
 
COALITION DECISIONS & STRATEGY 
 
• New name and logo: Voting members dropped “2050” and adopted the name Grand Places, with “PLACES” kept 
in capitals. They also chose a new logo; a basic website will be shown at the November 6 workshop. 
• Defined role: Grand Places is a convener and communicator, not a policymaking body. Specific projects are 
moving to an appendix framed as a needs assessment. The coalition will back projects that uphold at least two 
core values and match the priorities of multiple voting members. 
• Strategy timeline: Keystone Policy Center’s near-final strategy (formerly the Strategic Action Plan, funded by 
CPW) goes to graphic design after partner comments close on November 13. The updated charter will set the 
voting thresholds (simple majority, supermajority or consensus). 
 
FUNDING & REGIONAL OPPORTUNITIES 
 
• CPW grant reissue – $243,000: A partner agency is returning $243,000 in awarded CPW funds that its agency 
will no longer spend on recreation improvements. CPW will reissue the funds next fiscal year to eligible local 
partners if partners and projects are identified before the end of 2026. Staff will review whether a Town project 
qualifies. 
• Windy Gap Environmental Fund: Seven applications request more than $6 million against about $4 million 
available; awards are expected in November. 
• Community Priorities Fund (1A lodging tax): 21 requests received; review is October 23, with BOCC approval 
targeted for mid-November. 
• Grand County capital plan: The County is drafting a 10-year capital plan with more than $5 million budgeted for 
2027, mostly for trail construction and improvements. 
 
COALITION FINANCES & GOVERNANCE

Grand PLACES 2050 is a coalition of local government entities, nonprofit 
groups, state and federal land managers, and businesses. We focus on Grand
County’s PLACES - people, land, access, conservation, ecosystems, and
sustainability. Grand PLACES 2050 collaborates to protect and conserve natural
and cultural resources while providing equitable access and a quality outdoor
recreation experience for current and future generations.

RE:  Board meeting October 26, 2026 — Approval of revised SALT Workshop proposal to complete the Town Square 
marquee sign 
 
Mayor and Trustees: 
Staff recommends the Board approve SALT Workshop, Ltd.’s revised proposal to design, fabricate and install the Town 
Square Park marquee sign for $18,455.50, with a 15% contingency of $2,768 to be used only with Town Manager 
approval. The total would not exceed $21,223.50. SALT’s revised estimate is $827 above the proposal presented in 
August. 
BACKGROUND 
• October 2025. Staff presented options from Lunsford Signs. The Board directed staff to consider other options: 
finalize the Town’s logo designs and bring back a design closer to the original renderings used in fundraising. 
• August 10, 2026. Staff presented SALT Workshop’s preliminary proposal of $17,628.50. SALT, working with 
MetalMorphic Methods, helped design the marquee structure itself. 
• October 22, 2026. SALT submitted the revised estimate attached, reflecting design changes discussed since 
August. 
THE PROPOSAL 
SALT Workshop (design and CNC fabrication) and MetalMorphic Methods (steel fabrication and welding) would build a 
two-sided sign: a welded corten steel frame with reclaimed Wyoming snow fence wood panels, CNC-carved in relief. 
Each face carries “GRAND LAKE,” “EST. 1881” and “EL. 8369′,” a 24-inch Grand Lake logo medallion to Brand Book

MEMORANDUM 
 
TO Mayor and Board of Trustees 
FROM Emily Weber, Contract Staff - Planning Department 
DATE October 26, 2026 
 
RE:  QUASI JUDICIAL (PUBLIC HEARING): Resolution 87-2026 - Variance Request from Shoreline Setback Requirements 
for Property Located at 134 Mad Moose Lane. 
OVERVIEW 
• Property Owners: Seth and Mandy Tamisiea 
• Applicants: Michael and Debra Taber 
• Location: 134 Mad Moose Ln 
• Zoning: Single Family Residential – Medium Density 
• Request: The applicant is requesting a variance from the required 30-foot water quality setback to allow future 
development within the setback area on the property. Approval of the request would reduce the setback to 
approximately 13 feet from the irrigation pond’s high-water line on the property. 
 
BACKGROUND 
The subject property is a vacant residential lot located at 134 Mad Moose Lane within the Ridge at Elk Creek Subdivision. 
The original subdivision plat established a no-build area associated with the Howard Ditch on several lots within the 
subdivision. Subsequent to approval of the subdivision, the Town adopted the Shoreline and Surface Water Regulations, 
including the requirement for a 30-foot water quality setback from streams, lakes, ponds, irrigation ditches, wetlands, 
and other surface water features. 
As a result, development of the subject property is constrained by both the platted no-build area and the Town’s 
shoreline setback requirements. The applicant is seeking a variance from the shoreline setback requirements while 
Page 48 of 216
maintaining compliance with the platted no-build area. The application is not associated with a specific development 
proposal at this time. Rather, the applicants are requesting approval of a reduced water quality setback to establish a 
future building envelope for residential development. No building plans, grading plans, drainage plans, or site 
development plans have been submitted as part of this application. 
The Planning Commission reviewed the request at their October 7, 2026 meeting. 
STAFF ANALYSIS 
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-2-29, a property owner may request a variance from the Shoreline and Surface Water 
Regulations. Variance requests are reviewed by the Planning Commission at a public hearing, with a recommendation 
forwarded to the Board of Trustees for final consideration. Notice requirements include certified mail to property 
owners within 200 feet of the property and publication in a newspaper of general circulation. Staff has verified that all 
required notice procedures have been satisfied. 
Town Code Section 12-2-29 Shoreline and Surface Water Regulations 
A minimum 30-foot water quality setback is required from streams, lakes, ponds, and other surface water features. The 
applicants are requesting a variance to allow a reduced water quality setback of approximately 13 feet from the 
irrigation pond on the property. The requested reduction would provide additional buildable area while maintaining the 
existing platted no-build area associated with the Howard Ditch. 
VARIANCE CRITERIA OF APPROVAL 
Town Code Section 12-2-29 establishes the following factors for consideration when evaluating a request for a variance 
from the shoreline regulations: 
1. The shape, size, topography, slope, soils, vegetation, and other physical characteristics of the property. 
2. The location of all bodies of water on the property, including any water bodies located adjacent to property 
boundaries. 
3. The location and extent of the proposed encroachment into the required setback. 
4. Whether alternative site designs are feasible that would reduce or eliminate the need for the setback 
encroachment. 
5. The sensitivity of the affected water body and any associated critical habitat areas. 
6. The intensity of existing and proposed land uses adjacent to the water body affected by the requested 
encroachment. 
7. Potential impacts on floodplains and stream functions. A variance shall not be approved if the requested 
reduction would result in a setback that is narrower than the applicable floodplain. 
The applicants have provided a written narrative describing how they believe the request satisfies the approval criteria. 
The complete narrative is attached to this report. A summary of the narrative is provided below: 
• Application of the 30-foot water quality setback, combined with the existing platted no-build area, significantly 
limits the buildable portion of the lot. The applicants estimate that strict application of the setback would leave 
a building envelope of approximately 41 feet by 33 feet. 
• The lot was platted prior to adoption of the Town’s current shoreline setback regulations. According to the 
applicants, the resulting hardship stems from the interaction of the existing plat restrictions and subsequently 
adopted regulations rather than actions taken by the current property owners. 
• Construction of a typical single-family residence comparable to others within the subdivision would be 
challenging under the existing setback requirements. The applicants note that a standard two-car garage would 
occupy a substantial portion of the remaining building area. 
• Future development is anticipated to occur outside of the platted no-build area and would be designed to 
minimize impacts on the adjacent irrigation pond and surrounding natural features. 
• Any future residence would be subject to additional Town and County review processes, including applicable 
drainage, grading, erosion control, and building permit requirements. 
 
Page 49 of 216
  
PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION AND RECOMMENDATION 
The Planning Commission reviewed the request at its October 7, 2026, meeting. During the discussion, Commissioners 
sought clarification regarding the function, operation, and management of the irrigation pond and Howard Ditch. Town 
Manager Steve Kudron was present to answer questions. 
Staff explained that the Town manages the ditch and is one of several entities with water rights associated with the 
system. Four downstream properties are entitled to receive water from the pond. Historically, the ditch supported 
agricultural uses, although it now serves primarily landscape irrigation purposes and there is no active agricultural use in 
the area. The Town coordinates closely with Columbine Lake regarding water levels and completed repairs to portions of 
the ditch system within the last year. Water is diverted from Columbine Lake and released based on downstream water 
rights demands. 
Commissioners expressed concern regarding the potential for the pond to overflow. Staff stated that any overflow is 
conveyed through the ditch system and that the Town controls the volume of water entering the pond. Water was not 
released into the system during the current year, and only limited flows occurred during the previous year. Staff stated 
that water releases are controlled and that the risk of flooding is very low. The Planning Commission remained 
concerned about environmental protections associated with the site. Commissioners discussed whether a wetlands 
assessment should be completed to ensure that future development would not impact any wetlands that may exist on 
or near the property. As a result, the Commission considered requiring the applicant to provide a wetlands survey as 
part of any future building permit application. 
The Planning Commission recommended approval of the shoreline variance request, allowing an approximately 13-foot 
water quality setback for the property located at 134 Mad Moose Lane, subject to the condition that the applicant 
provide a wetlands survey prior to issuance of a building permit. The motion passed with one dissenting vote. 
Page 50 of 216
APPLICANT REQUEST 
Following the Planning Commission recommendation, the applicant has requested that the Board of Trustees remove 
the wetlands survey condition when considering the variance request. The applicant submitted a letter to the Board of 
Trustees, which is attached to this staff report. A summary of the applicant’s position is provided below: 
• The original subdivision plat already addresses water features and development limitations. The plat identifies a 
no-build area adjacent to the Howard Ditch, and the applicant contends that these conditions were reviewed 
and accepted during the original subdivision approval process. The applicant argues that the requested variance 
simply restores a buildable area that was anticipated when the lot was originally created. 
• The proposed building area does not exhibit visible indicators of hydric soils, wetland hydrology, or other 
characteristics typically associated with wetlands. The site is predominantly vegetated with lodgepole pine and 
aspen, which are generally representative of upland rather than wetland environments. 
• Existing site conditions make wetlands unlikely within the proposed building envelope. The irrigation pond and 
ditch are located at a lower elevation than the anticipated building area, and the applicant contends that the 
elevation difference prevents the sustained surface saturation necessary for wetland formation. 
• The applicant asserts that there is no evidence of critical habitat within the proposed development area and that 
the seasonal pond does not support significant wetland vegetation or sensitive wildlife habitat that would be 
adversely impacted by development. 
• The applicant argues that the wetlands survey requirement is unnecessary and burdensome. They note that the 
Town Code does not require a wetlands survey as part of a variance application and contend that the 
requirement would create additional costs and delays without evidence that wetlands are present within the 
future building envelope. 
BOARD OF TRUSTEES CONSIDERATION AND DECISION 
The Board of Trustees is asked to determine whether the requested shoreline setback variance satisfies the criteria of 
Town Code Section 12-2-29. In making its decision, the Board should consider the property’s physical characteristics, the 
extent of the requested setback reduction, the Planning Commission recommendation, the applicant’s request, and any 
public testimony. 
A primary consideration for the Board is whether the condition requiring a wetlands survey prior to building permit 
issuance is necessary to protect potential environmental resources or whether the condition is unwarranted based on 
the information provided. 
After reviewing the application materials, staff report, Planning Commission recommendation, applicant 
correspondence, and public testimony, the Board may take one of the following actions: 
1. Approve the shoreline setback variance as recommended by the Planning Commission, including the condition 
requiring a wetlands survey prior to building permit issuance. 
2. Approve the shoreline setback variance with modified conditions, including modification or removal of the 
wetlands survey requirement. 
3. Approve the shoreline setback variance without the wetlands survey condition. 
4. Deny the shoreline setback variance if the Board determines that the request does not satisfy the applicable 
review criteria of Town Code Section 12-2-29.
