TO W N  O F  G R A N D  L A K E ,  C O L O R A D O 2027 Preliminary Budget · Questions and answers
Questions you may have
Are my property taxes going up?
No. The Town's mill levy stays at 9.409, unchanged. For every
$100,000 of assessed value, the Town's share is about $941 a
year. Total assessed valuation across Town rose only 0.09%, so
the Town's property tax revenue is essentially flat at $800,448.
Is the Town spending more than it takes in?
Yes — the General Fund plans to spend $233,665 more than it
receives, drawn from savings. Reserves are healthy enough to
absorb that, ending 2027 at about $2,707,952. That gap is now
capital projects and the Town’s share of grant work, not day-to-
day operations: ongoing revenue covers ongoing costs, by about
$18,835. That margin is thin — less than one percent — but it is
on the right side of the line, and a month ago it was not.
Why is spending so much higher than last year?
Most of the increase is the four grant contracts, which are new
and almost entirely paid for from outside. Setting those aside,
day-to-day spending rises because of the new Marketing and
Communications department, more for town events, the Town's
new financial software, and about $90,000 of insurance
increases. Wages go the other way: four sets of wage lines were
rebased on what the Town actually pays, taking $269,966 out —
the largest single item a Public Works Director position that had
been budgeted twice.
Is staff getting a raise?
Yes — 2.3% across the board, costing $54,917 across all funds.
It applies to wages and the payroll taxes and retirement match
that move with them. Separately, the Town's health plans
renewed 13% higher for 2027, adding about $77,443 — a cost
increase, not a benefit increase.
What is happening with the trash bag program?
Bag sales fell 48% in 2025, to $41,058 against $80,027 of costs.
A count found $40,000 of bags already on hand, so none need
buying in 2027, and the Board removed $20,000 of site
maintenance on 8 October. Together those narrow the 2027 gap
to $11,253 — but they do not close it, and the bag purchase
returns in 2028. The Board will need to choose during 2027
between raising bag prices, changing how retailers are paid,
moving the service into the General Fund, or ending it.
What is the Town doing about roads?
$310,000 for paving in the Capital Improvement Fund, funded by
the dedicated 1% sales tax voters approved in 2016 — a
$160,000 appropriation plus $150,000 budgeted in 2026 that was
not spent and is carried forward. A further $50,000 goes to
boardwalks and $50,000 to capital maintenance.
Are water rates going up?
Yes — 3%, directed by the Board on 8 October 2026. The in-town
quarterly charge goes from $160.00 to $164.80 for up to 27,000
gallons, with usage above that from $2.00 to $2.06 per 1,000
gallons; out-of-town goes from $320.00 to $329.60. The Board
amends the fee schedule separately before the new rate can be
billed.
Why is the Town changing financial systems?
The Town is moving from Caselle to OpenGov for clearer real-
time reporting and a better budgeting process, and this budget is
presented in the new account structure. GovOS, which handles
licensing and permits, continues alongside it. The change costs
about $45,866 a year more than the system it replaces.
Is the Town in good financial shape?
Broadly yes. The 2025 audit was clean — an unmodified opinion
with no material weaknesses. The Town holds about $7,428,382
across all funds and has $5.4M of long-term debt, all of it on
schedule. The issue is not the balance sheet; it is how thin the
margin on day-to-day operations is.
What this budget still needs
A capital plan for roads, buildings and equipment. Exact debt service figures from the loan schedules. Completion of the
reconciliation between the old and new financial systems. A correction to how payroll was reported to CIRSA, which is adding
about $16,000 to the Town's workers’ compensation cost. And a decision on the trash bag program. These are listed in full in the
budget book.
How to have your say
15 October Budget formally submitted to the Board and published for public inspection at Town Hall
Oct – Nov Public hearing. Any registered elector may object to any part of the budget
15 December Mill levy certified to Grand County
31 December Board adopts the budget and the appropriation resolution

A preliminary 2027 operating and capital budget for Board workshop. This is a planning document, not an adopted budget — it 
precedes the statutory calendar described below.
Per Town Manager direction, the 2027 revenue base is 2025 AUDITED ACTUAL revenue, not the 2026 adopted budget. Four 
exceptions, all flagged: (1) non-recurring 2025 grant and contribution revenue is excluded from the base; (2) property tax is reset to the 
2026 Certification of Valuation; (3) sales tax is rebased on January–August 2026 actual receipts plus September–December 2025 
(Assumptions B56 and B57); (4) Marina operating revenue is rebased on 2026 actual (Assumptions B58).
The 2027 expenditure base is the 2026 ADOPTED budget, adjusted for: a 2.3% across-the-board wage increase (and the payroll taxes 
and retirement match that move with wages); a $160,000 paving appropriation in the Capital Improvement Fund; a $52,966 increase to 
the Marketing line and $14,000 to Professional Services, both set to the department’s own 2027 plan; a rebuilt Town Events line; four 
sets of wage lines rebased on the Paychex payroll report; removal of completed one-time capital; and the OpenGov ERP subscription.
Every line carries its OpenGov account number, mapped from the legacy Caselle account using the Town's conversion crosswalk (869 
accounts). Fund, department and object codes follow the new OpenGov chart. See the Crosswalk Notes tab for the 13 lines that 
required manual assignment.
Open the Assumptions tab and change the yellow cells. Almost every figure in the workbook is a formula that recalculates from those 
drivers; the Space to Create lines and a handful of department-ask amounts are entered directly and are noted where they occur. Blue 
text = a hardcoded input or a source figure. Black text = a formula. Green text = a link to another tab.
The Findings & Flags tab lists 38 items requiring a decision or a correction, including one material error in the ADOPTED 2026 budget 
(Marina Fund under-appropriated by $16,350), a duplicated Public Works Director position in the 2026 wage line, and a structural 
deficit in the PAYT Enterprise Fund.

FUND STRUCTURE AND WHAT STILL NEEDS SETTLING
The analysis assumes rents at 120% AMI under 2025 CHFA limits. Those limits are republished annually, so confirm the 2027 maximums before the rents in this budget are treated as achievable.
Two bank accounts, both in the Town’s name: one for rent receipts and one for maintenance and expenses. GCHA has access alongside Grand County Finance staff and the Town Treasurer, collects and 
deposits rents and security deposits, and provides monthly deposit slips for reconciliation. Both accounts belong to this single enterprise fund — two accounts, one fund, one appropriation. Do not let the 
account split become a de facto second fund.
Security deposits are NOT revenue. They are a liability held for tenants and must sit on the balance sheet, not in the rent line. Confirm the Treasurer has set up the deposit liability account before the first 
reconciliation.
The 2026 budget booked $23,000 of debt service with no supporting schedule, and this workbook carries it forward unchanged. Get the actual amortization before adoption — debt service is the one line with 
no estimating uncertainty.
The enterprise question worth asking now: an enterprise fund must be self-supporting and, to keep its TABOR enterprise status, must receive less than 10% of annual revenue in grants from Colorado state 
and local governments. At the modeled level this fund covers its costs comfortably. Watch that test as capital needs arrive, and keep any Town subsidy visible rather than buried in shared maintenance staff 
time.
The Town still carries every cost GCHA excludes: maintenance, unit turnover cleaning, retail space operation and capital project oversight. Residential operating expenses of $50,596 need to be tested 
against what the Town’s own crew will actually spend on this building.
