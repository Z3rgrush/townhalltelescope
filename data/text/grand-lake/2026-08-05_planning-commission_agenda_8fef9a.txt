BOARD OF ADJUSTMENT STAFF MEMORANDUM 
 
DATE: August 5, 2025 
TO: Board of Adjustment   
FROM: Emily Weber, Contract Staff - Planning Department 
SUBJECT: QUASI JUDICIAL (PUBLIC HEARING) – Resolution 01-2026: Variance Request from Setback 
Requirements for Property Located at 842 Lake Avenue. 
Overview 
• Property Owner and Applicant:  Gay Shaffer 
• Applicant’s Representative:  William Carrier 
• Location: 842 Lake Avenue 
• Zoning: Single Family Residential – High 
Density 
• Request: The applicant is requesting a variance 
from the required 10-foot side yard setback to 
permit a 2-foot side yard setback for the 
construction of a stairway providing access 
between the existing house and the existing 
boathouse on the property. 
 
Background 
The subject property contains an existing residence 
and boathouse and is located on a lakefront lot. 
Historically, the property shared a staircase with 
the neighboring property to the west, providing 
access to both properties' lakeshore areas and 
boathouses. Portions of the shared staircase were 
located on both properties. 
 
The neighboring property is used as a short-term 
rental. The owner of 842 Lake Avenue wishes to 
eliminate the shared access arrangement and 
construct a private staircase entirely on their own 
property. The proposed staircase would be located on the east side of the existing residence. 
 
The existing residence does not meet current side yard setback requirements on either side of the property 
and is therefore considered a legal nonconforming structure. Due to the site's topography, the location of the 
existing home, and the need to provide access to the boathouse and shoreline, the applicant is requesting a 
variance from the required side yard setback to construct a new staircase. 
 
Staff Analysis 
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-3-6, any property owner may request a variance from the zoning 
regulations through the Board of Adjustment. The variance process requires public notice by certified mail to 
property owners within 200 feet of the subject property and publication in a newspaper of general circulation.

All notification requirements have been satisfied for this application. The following sections of the Town 
Code are applicable to the review of this variance request. 
 
Town Code Section 12-2-12 Regulations for Single Family Residential – High Density (RSH) 
The minimum side yard setback requirement for properties zoned RSH is 10 feet. The applicant proposes to 
construct the staircase with a 2-foot setback from the eastern property line. Staff finds that the proposed 
location accommodates the unique topography of the site and provides a practical means of access 
between the residence, the boathouse, and the shoreline while remaining within the applicant's property 
boundaries. 
 
Town Code Section 12-2-29 Shoreline and Surface Water Regulations 
Town Code requires a minimum 30-foot setback from the mean identifiable high-water mark and includes a 
5-foot non-disturbance zone. An exception exists for stairways four feet or less in width. The proposed 
stairway is four feet wide and complies with applicable shoreline setback requirements. 
 
Variance Criteria of Approval 
Town Code Section 12-3-5 establishes the following review criteria for variance requests, paraphrased 
below: 
1. By reason of exceptional shape, size, or topography of the lot, or other exceptional situation or 
condition of the building or land, practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship would result to the 
owner from strict enforcement of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-
29 (if applicable). 
2. A literal interpretation of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable) would deprive the applicant of rights commonly enjoyed by other properties in the same 
zoning district. 
3. The special conditions and circumstances do not result from actions taken by the applicant. 
4. Granting the variance will not confer a special privilege upon the applicant that is denied to other 
lands, structures, or buildings within the same zoning district. 
5. Granting the variance will not be detrimental to the public good and will not substantially impair the 
intent and purpose of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable). 
 
The applicant has provided a written narrative addressing each of the required criteria. The narrative is 
included in the attachments to this memo.  
 
Staff Findings and Recommendations 
Based on the information submitted, staff finds: 
1. The property contains unique physical constraints, including steep topography and an existing 
nonconforming residence, which limit feasible locations for a private staircase. 
2. The request will allow the property owner to maintain independent access to the boathouse and 
shoreline without relying on a structure shared with an adjoining property. 
3. The circumstances leading to the request arise primarily from the property's physical characteristics 
and existing development pattern rather than actions taken by the current applicant/property owner.  
4. The variance would permit a residential accessory improvement that is consistent with the use of 
surrounding lakefront residential properties. 
5. The proposed stairway is not anticipated to create adverse impacts to neighboring properties, public 
safety, or the intent of the zoning regulations.

Background 
The subject property is located at 536 Cairns 
Avenue and consists of an approximately 0.43-acre 
lakefront lot containing an existing single-family 
residence. The property contains topographic 
constraints, including a steep slope rising from 
Cairns Avenue to the residence and a steep 
descending slope toward Grand Lake. The original 
residence was constructed in the 1930s and is 
considered a legal nonconforming structure 
because portions of the existing garage, covered 
entry, and exterior stair encroach into the required 25-foot front yard setback along Cairns Avenue. 
 
The applicant proposes an addition to the existing residence, including the construction of an elevator from 
the garage to the main living level and an addition on the lake front side of the existing residence. The 
proposed elevator would extend into the required front setback. All other additions and site improvements 
comply with current zoning standards, including building height, side and rear setbacks, and the required 
water quality setback.

BOARD OF ADJUSTMENT 
Staff Analysis  
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-3-6, any property owner may request a variance from the zoning 
regulations through the Board of Adjustment. The variance process requires public notice by certified mail to 
property owners within 200 feet of the subject property and publication in a newspaper of general circulation. 
All notification requirements have been satisfied for this application. The following sections of the Town 
Code are applicable to the review of this variance request.  
 
Town Code Section 12-2-12 Regulations for Single Family Residential – High Density (RSH) 
Properties within the RSH zoning district are required to maintain a minimum front yard setback of 25 feet. 
The proposed elevator would be attached to the existing garage located along the Cairns Avenue frontage. 
The garage itself is already located within the required setback as part of a longstanding nonconforming 
structure. The proposed elevator is approximately 19 feet from the front property line and is intended to 
provide accessible access between the garage level and the main living area of the home. 
 
Variance Criteria of Approval 
Town Code Section 12-3-5 establishes the following review criteria for variance requests, paraphrased 
below: 
1. By reason of exceptional shape, size, or topography of the lot, or other exceptional situation or 
condition of the building or land, practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship would result to the 
owner from strict enforcement of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-
29 (if applicable). 
2. A literal interpretation of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable) would deprive the applicant of rights commonly enjoyed by other properties in the same 
zoning district. 
3. The special conditions and circumstances do not result from actions taken by the applicant. 
4. Granting the variance will not confer a special privilege upon the applicant that is denied to other 
lands, structures, or buildings within the same zoning district. 
5. Granting the variance will not be detrimental to the public good and will not substantially impair the 
intent and purpose of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable). 
 
The applicant has provided a written narrative addressing each of the required criteria. The narrative is 
included in the attachments to this memorandum. 
 
Staff Findings and Recommendations 
Based upon the application materials, site characteristics, and review criteria contained in Town Code 
Section 12-3-5, staff finds: 
 
1. The property contains exceptional physical characteristics, including a steep slope that rises from 
Cairns Avenue to the residence and then descends toward the lake. In addition, the existing

residence was constructed prior to adoption of current setback requirements and contains existing 
nonconforming encroachments within the front setback area. These conditions limit opportunities to 
locate an elevator elsewhere on the site. 
 
2. Strict application of the 25-foot front setback requirement would create practical difficulties by 
preventing installation of an elevator at the only feasible location connecting the street-level garage

residential in character, and is not anticipated to negatively impact adjacent properties, public 
safety, access, drainage, or neighborhood character. The proposal therefore does not substantially 
impair the intent or purpose of the zoning regulations.

PLANNING COMMISSION 
Staff Analysis 
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-2-32, the Planning Commission may authorize expansion of a 
nonconforming structure when the applicable review criteria are satisfied. The following sections are 
applicable to this review.  
 
Town Code Section 12-2-32 Nonconforming Uses and Structures 
The existing residence is a legal nonconforming structure because portions of the building predate the 
current setback requirements and are located within the required front yard setback. The applicant proposes 
two forms of expansion: 
• A lakeside addition that complies with all current zoning standards, including building height, side 
and rear setbacks, and water quality setbacks. 
• An elevator tower attached to the existing garage that would extend within the front setback area 
(see the above variance request). 
 
Variance Criteria of Approval 
Town Code states that the following are the approval criteria for expanding nonconforming structures:  
1. The structure or use is nonconforming as defined by this Article and is in full compliance with all 
requirements of this Article applicable to nonconforming structures or uses; 
2. That, owing to exceptional and extraordinary circumstances, literal enforcement of the provisions of 
this Article regarding nonconforming structures or uses will result in unnecessary hardship; 
3. That the exception will not substantially or permanently injure the appropriate use of adjacent 
conforming property in the same zone district or other zone districts; 
4. That the exception will not alter the essential character of the district in which is located the property 
for which the exception is sought; 
5. That the exception will not weaken the general purposes of this Article or the regulations established 
herein for the specific district; 
6. That the exception will be in harmony with the spirit and purposes of this Article; 
7. That the exception will not adversely affect the public health, safety, or welfare. 
 
The applicant has provided a written narrative addressing each of the required criteria. The narrative is 
included in the attachments to this memorandum. 
 
Staff Findings and Recommendations 
Based upon the application materials and the review criteria contained in Town Code Section 12-2-32, staff 
finds: 
1. The residence is a legally established single-family dwelling that qualifies as a legal nonconforming 
structure because portions of the building encroach into the required front yard setback. The use 
itself remains conforming within the Single Family Residential High Density district.   
 
2. Owing to the property's exceptional topography and the location of the existing residence, literal 
enforcement of the nonconforming structure provisions would result in unnecessary hardship. The 
home is accessed from Cairns Avenue at the garage level, which is already located within the front 
setback. Although the project includes a substantial addition on the lake-facing side of the 
residence, that addition has been designed to comply with all current zoning standards, including 
height, setbacks, and water quality requirements. Relocating the elevator to another location would 
require substantial demolition and reconstruction of the existing home, significant alteration of 
interior circulation patterns, or additional encroachment toward the lake.

3. The proposed expansion will not substantially or permanently injure the use of adjacent properties. 
The elevator addition is limited in scale, does not increase residential density, and does not create 
additional impacts related to access, drainage, light, air, or privacy. All other additions comply with 
current zoning standards. 
 
4. The character of the neighborhood is preserved by retaining the original structure and making 
modifications rather than constructing a new home. This approach maintains the rustic aesthetic, 
comparable to adjacent residences, and is more compatible with the existing neighborhood 
character.  Additionally, many of the adjacent residences are also legal nonconforming and were 
constructed prior to current zoning regulations.  
  
5. Approval of the exception will not weaken the purposes of the zoning regulations. The requested 
relief is limited to a small accessibility improvement associated with an existing nonconforming 
frontage, while the remainder of the project complies with current dimensional requirements. 
 
6. The Town's nonconforming provisions are intended to allow legally established nonconforming 
structures to continue and, where appropriate, accommodate reasonable improvements. The 
project improves the property's functionality and accessibility without expanding the encroachment 
beyond what is necessary to achieve its purpose. 
 
7. Approval of the exception will not adversely affect public health, safety, or welfare. The proposed 
elevator improves accessibility and safety within a steeply sloped residence, and the overall remodel 
includes upgrades to building systems and life-safety features. 
 
Based on the findings above and the approval criteria contained in Town Code Section 12-2-32, staff 
recommends approval of the request to expand the existing nonconforming structure.

Dear Members of the Board and Commission, 
Applicants / Owners:  Susan and Steve Struna 
Property:  536 Cairns Avenue, Grand Lake, Colorado 80447; Lots 37–40, Cairns Addition to Grand Lake, 
County of Grand, State of Colorado 
Zone District:  SFR_HD — Single Family Residential, High Density 
Project:  Struna Addition and Remodel (MA Studios — Project No. 2122; drawing set dated June 22,

Request:  A variance from the required twenty-five foot (25′-0″) front yard setback of the SFR_HD district 
to permit construction of a new residential elevator tower attached to the existing garage at the Cairns 
Avenue frontage, together with authorization under Section 12-2-32 to expand a lawfully 
nonconforming structure. The elevator tower is the only nonconforming portion of the project; all other 
proposed work complies with current zoning standards. 
I. Background and Existing Nonconformity  
The property is a 0.426-acre (18,556 SF) lakeside lot that falls steeply from Cairns Avenue down to the 
shoreline of Grand Lake, with an existing three-story, wood-framed (Type VB) single-family residence 
built into the hillside. The existing residence was lawfully established, and portions of it — including the 
existing garage and the covered entry, roof cover, and exterior stair along the Cairns Avenue frontage, 
noted on the Project Site Plan (Sheet A1.00) as “to remain, no change” — sit within the twenty-five foot 
front yard setback. These existing Cairns Avenue conditions are documented in the photograph attached 
as Exhibit A. The residence is therefore a legal nonconforming structure whose continuance is expressly 
permitted under Section 12-2-32(A), which states the Town’s intent “to allow the continuance of such 
nonconforming use.” The structure is not a violation-based nonconformity under 12-2-32(A)(1); it was 
constructed with the approvals required at the time.

The proposed project is a whole-house addition and remodel that includes new living area on the lake 
side of the residence, interior renovations, insulation and mechanical upgrades, and a small residential 
elevator connecting the street-level garage to the main living level. The elevator shaft (Room 003, 
Sheets A3.00–A3.02) is attached to the existing garage — itself within the front setback — with a 
footprint of approximately 6′-3½″ by 5′-3½″ (61-inch clear cab), adding approximately 32 square feet to 
the garage level (690 SF existing; 722 SF proposed, per Sheet A3.00). As shown on the Overall Building 
Elevation – North (Sheet A5.02), the tower is finished in drystacked Telluride greystone (STN-1) with 8x8 
timber accents and a gabled cap matching the architecture of the home. The garage-level plan (Sheet 
A3.00) shows the 25′-0″ front setback line passing through the new shaft: the elevator tower occupies 
the front yard setback, encroaching by approximately 4’-0” at the point of greatest encroachment. The 
encroachment occurs at the existing garage, on the same frontage where the existing structure already 
lawfully encroaches. 
No other zoning nonconformity is created. The building height remains unchanged at 36′-10″, below the 
37′-0″ maximum permitted with a grade change greater than five feet; the story count remains three; 
and the lake-side addition respects the 10′-0″ side and rear setbacks and the 30′-0″ water quality 
setback (Sheet A1.00, Project Zoning Summary). 
II. Compliance with Variance Requirements — Section 12-3-6 
A. Eligibility and Standing — 12-3-6(A)  
The Applicants are the owners of the subject property and are adversely affected by application of the 
SFR_HD front yard setback requirement of the Zoning Regulations (Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24) as 
it relates to Section 12-3-5(A)(2). This application is submitted on the Town’s furnished form, specifies 
the grounds of appeal, and is accompanied by the required fee. The Applicants confirm that they are 
current with the Town and all Town enterprises for all fees, assessments, charges, taxes, and amounts 
due of any type, as required by 12-3-6(A)(1). 
B. Findings Required by 12-3-6(B)(5)  
Before granting a variance, the Board of Adjustment must include a written finding in its minutes stating 
specifically the exceptional conditions  and the practical difficulties or unnecessary hardships involved. 
The record supports both findings, and the Applicants offer the following to assist the Board in framing 
them: 
1. Exceptional conditions.  The property presents physical conditions that are exceptional and not 
generally shared by other properties in the SFR_HD district. The lot climbs steeply from Cairns Avenue to 
the existing house and then falls steeply to Grand Lake, a grade change exceeding five feet that 
produces a three-story residence stepping down the hillside. The existing residence — lawfully built 
decades before the current standards — already occupies the front setback along Cairns Avenue with its 
Page 28 of 87
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
PO Box 21 
Granby, CO 80446 
970-887-9366 
garage, covered entry, roof cover, and stair. These conditions predate the Applicants’ ownership and 
were not self-created. Because the home’s main street-level access is through the garage at the Cairns 
Avenue frontage, the only location where an elevator can connect the garage to the main living level — 
without demolishing and rebuilding the interior structure of the home or pushing new construction 
toward the lake and the water quality setback — is at the existing garage, within the front setback. 
2. Practical difficulties and unnecessary hardship. Literal enforcement of the 25-foot front setback 
would make it practically impossible to provide accessible circulation from the street-level garage to the 
main living level of this steep hillside home, because the garage — the only point of vehicular access to 
the property — already sits within the front setback. The only code-compliant alternative would require 
gutting and reconfiguring the interior structural core of the residence or extending the building 
envelope toward Grand Lake, at far greater cost, disturbance, and environmental impact. The difficulty 
arises from the physical circumstances of the lot and the existing structure rather than from 
convenience or a desire for greater profit, and it deprives the Applicants of a right commonly enjoyed by 
other owners in the district: the ability to enter their home from the garage and reach the main living 
level without climbing multiple flights of exterior and interior stairs. The relief requested is the minimum 
necessary to overcome these difficulties — an elevator shaft of approximately 32 square feet, sized to a 
61-inch clear residential cab, encroaching only as far as the shaft itself requires. All other dimensional 
standards are fully met: building height remains 36′-10″ (below the 37′-0″ maximum), the story count 
remains three, and the side, rear, and water quality setbacks are respected. 
Although 12-3-6 requires only the findings above, the Applicants note for the Board’s benefit that the 
requested variance also will not injure adjacent conforming property, will not alter the essential 
character of the district, and will not adversely affect the public health, safety, or welfare. The elevator 
tower is a slender, architecturally integrated element — drystacked stone with timber accents and a 
gabled cap consistent with the mountain character of the home and the neighborhood — located at the 
existing garage, on a frontage where the existing structure already encroaches, and it does not reduce 
light, air, drainage, or access to any neighboring parcel. Exterior lighting will comply with the Town’s 
dark-sky standards (Section 12-7-6), and all exterior colors will be approved by Town Staff per Section 12-
7-4(A).  
3. Precedent on the same street.  The Board and Commission have previously considered materially 
identical circumstances on this very block of Cairns Avenue. In January 2019, the owners of 518 Cairns 
Avenue (Lots 32–34, Cairns Addition) — two lots from the subject property, in the same SFR_HD district 
— sought and received authorization to expand a nonconforming garage and accessory dwelling located 
almost completely within the 25-foot front setback. That application rested on the same physical 
realities present here: steep slopes falling from Cairns Avenue, structures lawfully established at the 
street long before adoption of the current Zoning Code, and the practical impossibility of a conforming 
alternative. It also documented that the buildings along this section of Cairns Avenue are generally

character of the block. The relief requested here is substantially narrower than what was granted at 518 
Cairns Avenue — a single elevator shaft of approximately 32 square feet attached to an existing 
encroaching garage — and consistent treatment of like circumstances supports the same result. 
C. Scope of Request and Notice — 12-3-6(B)(6)  
The Applicants understand that the Board may approve all or a portion of this request but may not grant 
a variance greater than the noticed request. The variance requested is stated with specificity so the 
notice fully encompasses the relief sought: a variance to build within the front yard setback along the 
Cairns Avenue frontage, solely to the extent of the proposed elevator tower as shown on Sheets A1.00, 
A3.00–A3.02, and A5.02 of the referenced drawing set. 
D. Conditions, Utilization, and Duration — 12-3-6(C), (D), (F)  
The Applicants will accept and comply with any conditions and safeguards the Board prescribes under

Regulations. The Applicants intend to utilize the variance well within the nine (9) month period required

fifty percent (50%) of the building is destroyed. 
III. Compliance with Section 12-2-32 — Nonconforming Uses and Structures  
A. Lawful Nonconforming Status — 12-2-32(A)  
As set forth in Section I, the residence was lawfully established before the current regulations, is entitled 
to continue, and is in full compliance with all requirements of the Article applicable to nonconforming 
structures. This satisfies the threshold finding of 12-2-32(B)(a)(1)(i). The use — a single-family residence 
in the SFR_HD district — is and remains a conforming use; the nonconformity is dimensional only. 
B. Expansion or Enlargement — 12-2-32(B)  
Section 12-2-32(B) requires that a nonconforming building to be extended or enlarged conform with the 
provisions of the Article, and 12-2-32(B)(1) permits expansion pursuant to subsection (C) where the 
expansion complies with the Code. The lake-side addition and remodel comply fully with current 
standards and are permissible on that basis. Because the elevator tower increases the floor area of the 
structure within the front setback, the Applicants additionally request a special exception from the 
Planning Commission under 12-2-32(B)(a)(1), and each of the seven required findings is satisfied: 
(i)  The structure is nonconforming as defined by the Article and is in full compliance with all 
requirements applicable to nonconforming structures, as set forth in Section I;

IV. Conclusion  
The request satisfies the eligibility and procedural requirements of Section 12-3-6, and the record fully 
supports the written findings of exceptional conditions and practical difficulties or unnecessary 
hardships required by 12-3-6(B)(5): a steeply sloping lot that climbs from Cairns Avenue to the existing 
house and then falls steeply to Grand Lake, an existing lawfully nonconforming residence whose garage 
and street-level access sit within the front setback at the Cairns Avenue frontage, and no practical

will result in a safer, more accessible, and more conforming structure that benefits the neighborhood 
and the Town. The Applicants respectfully request approval.

Background 
The subject property is located at 536 Cairns 
Avenue and consists of an approximately 0.43-acre 
lakefront lot containing an existing single-family 
residence. The property contains topographic 
constraints, including a steep slope rising from 
Cairns Avenue to the residence and a steep 
descending slope toward Grand Lake. The original 
residence was constructed in the 1930s and is 
considered a legal nonconforming structure 
because portions of the existing garage, covered 
entry, and exterior stair encroach into the required 25-foot front yard setback along Cairns Avenue. 
 
The applicant proposes an addition to the existing residence, including the construction of an elevator from 
the garage to the main living level and an addition on the lake front side of the existing residence. The 
proposed elevator would extend into the required front setback. All other additions and site improvements 
comply with current zoning standards, including building height, side and rear setbacks, and the required 
water quality setback.

Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-3-5, the Board of Adjustment reviews requests for variances from zoning 
standards, including setback requirements. Additionally, because the proposal involves expansion of a

BOARD OF ADJUSTMENT 
Staff Analysis  
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-3-6, any property owner may request a variance from the zoning 
regulations through the Board of Adjustment. The variance process requires public notice by certified mail to 
property owners within 200 feet of the subject property and publication in a newspaper of general circulation. 
All notification requirements have been satisfied for this application. The following sections of the Town 
Code are applicable to the review of this variance request.  
 
Town Code Section 12-2-12 Regulations for Single Family Residential – High Density (RSH) 
Properties within the RSH zoning district are required to maintain a minimum front yard setback of 25 feet. 
The proposed elevator would be attached to the existing garage located along the Cairns Avenue frontage. 
The garage itself is already located within the required setback as part of a longstanding nonconforming 
structure. The proposed elevator is approximately 19 feet from the front property line and is intended to 
provide accessible access between the garage level and the main living area of the home. 
 
Variance Criteria of Approval 
Town Code Section 12-3-5 establishes the following review criteria for variance requests, paraphrased 
below: 
1. By reason of exceptional shape, size, or topography of the lot, or other exceptional situation or 
condition of the building or land, practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship would result to the 
owner from strict enforcement of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-
29 (if applicable). 
2. A literal interpretation of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable) would deprive the applicant of rights commonly enjoyed by other properties in the same 
zoning district. 
3. The special conditions and circumstances do not result from actions taken by the applicant. 
4. Granting the variance will not confer a special privilege upon the applicant that is denied to other 
lands, structures, or buildings within the same zoning district. 
5. Granting the variance will not be detrimental to the public good and will not substantially impair the 
intent and purpose of Municipal Code Sections 12-2-8 through 12-2-24 and Section 12-2-29 (if 
applicable). 
 
The applicant has provided a written narrative addressing each of the required criteria. The narrative is 
included in the attachments to this memorandum. 
 
Staff Findings and Recommendations 
Based upon the application materials, site characteristics, and review criteria contained in Town Code 
Section 12-3-5, staff finds: 
 
1. The property contains exceptional physical characteristics, including a steep slope that rises from 
Cairns Avenue to the residence and then descends toward the lake. In addition, the existing

Page 3 of 6  
residence was constructed prior to adoption of current setback requirements and contains existing 
nonconforming encroachments within the front setback area. These conditions limit opportunities to 
locate an elevator elsewhere on the site. 
 
2. Strict application of the 25-foot front setback requirement would create practical difficulties by 
preventing installation of an elevator at the only feasible location connecting the street-level garage 
to the home's primary living area. The garage serves as the property's only vehicular access point, 
and the proposed elevator provides accessible circulation between levels of the residence. 
 
3. The need for the variance results from the unique topography of the property and the location of the 
existing legal nonconforming structure rather than actions taken by the current property owners. 
 
4. Granting the variance would not confer a special privilege. Similar development patterns exist along 
this section of Cairns Avenue where homes were historically constructed within current setback 
requirements. The requested encroachment represents the minimum relief necessary to 
accommodate the elevator and therefore accessible access to the residence.  
 
5. The proposed elevator tower is architecturally integrated into the existing residence, remains 
residential in character, and is not anticipated to negatively impact adjacent properties, public 
safety, access, drainage, or neighborhood character. The proposal therefore does not substantially 
impair the intent or purpose of the zoning regulations. 
 
Based on the findings above and the approval criteria contained in Town Code Section 12-3-5, staff 
recommends approval of the variance request to permit the proposed elevator within the required front yard 
setback.

PLANNING COMMISSION 
Staff Analysis 
Pursuant to Town Code Section 12-2-32, the Planning Commission may authorize expansion of a 
nonconforming structure when the applicable review criteria are satisfied. The following sections are 
applicable to this review.  
 
Town Code Section 12-2-32 Nonconforming Uses and Structures 
The existing residence is a legal nonconforming structure because portions of the building predate the 
current setback requirements and are located within the required front yard setback. The applicant proposes 
two forms of expansion: 
• A lakeside addition that complies with all current zoning standards, including building height, side 
and rear setbacks, and water quality setbacks. 
• An elevator tower attached to the existing garage that would extend within the front setback area 
(see the above variance request). 
 
Variance Criteria of Approval 
Town Code states that the following are the approval criteria for expanding nonconforming structures:  
1. The structure or use is nonconforming as defined by this Article and is in full compliance with all 
requirements of this Article applicable to nonconforming structures or uses; 
2. That, owing to exceptional and extraordinary circumstances, literal enforcement of the provisions of 
this Article regarding nonconforming structures or uses will result in unnecessary hardship; 
3. That the exception will not substantially or permanently injure the appropriate use of adjacent 
conforming property in the same zone district or other zone districts; 
4. That the exception will not alter the essential character of the district in which is located the property 
for which the exception is sought; 
5. That the exception will not weaken the general purposes of this Article or the regulations established 
herein for the specific district; 
6. That the exception will be in harmony with the spirit and purposes of this Article; 
7. That the exception will not adversely affect the public health, safety, or welfare. 
 
The applicant has provided a written narrative addressing each of the required criteria. The narrative is 
included in the attachments to this memorandum. 
 
Staff Findings and Recommendations 
Based upon the application materials and the review criteria contained in Town Code Section 12-2-32, staff 
finds: 
1. The residence is a legally established single-family dwelling that qualifies as a legal nonconforming 
structure because portions of the building encroach into the required front yard setback. The use 
itself remains conforming within the Single Family Residential High Density district.   
 
2. Owing to the property's exceptional topography and the location of the existing residence, literal 
enforcement of the nonconforming structure provisions would result in unnecessary hardship. The 
home is accessed from Cairns Avenue at the garage level, which is already located within the front 
setback. Although the project includes a substantial addition on the lake-facing side of the 
residence, that addition has been designed to comply with all current zoning standards, including 
height, setbacks, and water quality requirements. Relocating the elevator to another location would 
require substantial demolition and reconstruction of the existing home, significant alteration of 
interior circulation patterns, or additional encroachment toward the lake.

3. The proposed expansion will not substantially or permanently injure the use of adjacent properties. 
The elevator addition is limited in scale, does not increase residential density, and does not create 
additional impacts related to access, drainage, light, air, or privacy. All other additions comply with 
current zoning standards. 
 
4. The character of the neighborhood is preserved by retaining the original structure and making 
modifications rather than constructing a new home. This approach maintains the rustic aesthetic,

character.  Additionally, many of the adjacent residences are also legal nonconforming and were

5. Approval of the exception will not weaken the purposes of the zoning regulations. The requested 
relief is limited to a small accessibility improvement associated with an existing nonconforming 
frontage, while the remainder of the project complies with current dimensional requirements. 
 
6. The Town's nonconforming provisions are intended to allow legally established nonconforming 
structures to continue and, where appropriate, accommodate reasonable improvements. The 
project improves the property's functionality and accessibility without expanding the encroachment 
beyond what is necessary to achieve its purpose. 
 
7. Approval of the exception will not adversely affect public health, safety, or welfare. The proposed 
elevator improves accessibility and safety within a steeply sloped residence, and the overall remodel 
includes upgrades to building systems and life-safety features. 
 
Based on the findings above and the approval criteria contained in Town Code Section 12-2-32, staff 
recommends approval of the request to expand the existing nonconforming structure.
