October 6, 2026 Board Meeting 
  
Legal Briefing on State of Regional Water Projects and Water Issues in Colorado 
 
Windy Gap Project: 
• Windy Gap Reservoir on West Slope 
o 480 Units 
o Legally reusable water 
• Platte River Power Authority (“PRPA”) is selling 2 Units to Town of Fort Lupton 
o Approximately $5 million / Unit (going to Northern Board for approval on 
October 9; price not published) 
o Not expecting PRPA to sell further units in the near term 
• Windy Gap Participants will likely pursue a new electrical switchyard at the Windy Gap 
Pumping Plant  
o Approximately $25-30 million project; funding mechanism not yet identified) 
 
Windy Gap Firming Project (Chimney Hollow Reservoir): 
• 90,000-acre-foot storage reservoir (Chimney Hollow) next to Carter Lake 
o Connects to CBT system (into Carter Lake and/or Flatiron Reservoir) 
• Uranium detected in Chimney Hollow Reservoir dam embankment in summer 2025 
o Uranium mitigation options, such as recirculating water treatment within the 
Reservoir, being considered now 
o Water samples and data collection underway 
o Hoping for Participant group and Northern Board to decide soon and start 
implementing approach 
o First full fill and operations of Reservoir are delayed (years until operational 
deliveries), without a definitive start time 
 A lot in flux at the moment (Uranium implications, new Operating 
Criteria, new annual allocation or “order” process) 
• No storage capacity for sale or proposed transfer currently

Northern Integrated Supply Project (“NISP”) – Current Status: 
• Glade Reservoir, Galeton Reservoir, delivery pipelines (no ditch exchanges) 
o 40,000 AF annual yield originally 
o Glade is single use; Galeton is reusable; together, they create reliable yield 
o Glade = 170,000-acre-foot storage capacity  
o Diversification of water supplies 
• Participation changes: 
o 15 Participants  9 Participants 
 Withdrawn (i.e., permanently forfeited):  Fort Collins-Loveland Water 
District, Eaton, Evans, Firestone, Severance, and Morgan County Quality 
Water 
 Reduced participation:  Erie (6,500 AF to 4,500 AF)*, Central Weld 
County Water District (3,500 AF to 250 AF), Fort Morgan (3,600 AF to 
2,700 AF), Left Hand Water District (3,500 to 3,000), Fort Lupton (1,050 
AF to 100 AF) 
 No change in participation level:  Lafayette (1,800 AF), Windsor (3,300 
AF), Frederick (2,600 AF), Dacono (1,250 AF) 
o Current participation total:  19,500 AF  Glade Reservoir only, with option 
for Galeton Reservoir in the future 
 Matches firm yield of Glade 
 Potential new Participants may join this fall (reducing cost and yield per 
Participant) 
• Approximately $2 billion+ construction cost 
o Grant Subcommittee established 
o U.S. Environmental Protection Agency WIFIA loan:  up to 49% of total Project 
cost 
 Debt sculpting (time / layer different funding sources; defer certain 
financing costs until the new NISP-based taps can be sold by capitalizing 
interest – i.e., deferred financing structure instead of traditional) 
o CWCB $100 million grant 
o $71.4 million of additional funding opportunities applying for 
o Recently awarded $23.5 million in grants 
o Components of Project other than the dam itself, typically so far 
• Tentative schedule: 
o November 2026:  Construction bids to Northern 
o August 2027:  Final Allotment Contracts to be signed  
o Fall 2027:  Highway 287 relocation construction start 
o January 2028:  Glade Reservoir construction start 
o Approximately 2032:  Construction to complete

State of Drought and Interstate Issues: 
• CBT is Colorado River water 
• 2026 was worst drought year of record in Colorado in terms of lowest snowpack 
o Fingers crossed for Super El Nino winter 
• Northern Board considering initial CBT quota in October, for beginning of 2027 CBT 
Water Year on November 1 
o Multi-year drought considerations  
o 2003:  Initial quota of 50%, April quota of 20%, for total quota of 70% 
o 2023:  Initial quota of 40%, April quota of 30%, for total quota of 70% 
o Green Mountain Reservoir (West Slope) is nearly empty. 
 (Compensatory West Slope mitigation) 
• Federal DOI plan (Record of Decision) on Colorado River released in August: 
o Lower basin states:  mandatory reductions of their Colorado River water usage by 
1.25 million AF annually in 2027 and 2028 
o Upper basin states:  no mandatory reductions; voluntary conservation programs 
o State of Nevada sued the federal government 
• State of Nebraska v. State of Colorado  
o South Platte River Compact 
o June 2026:  U.S. Supreme Court is exercising its jurisdiction to consider 
Nebraska’s claims 
o SCOTUS will decide whether to evaluate Colorado’s Motion to Dismiss or 
appoint a Special Master to oversee the process
