lot of information that needs to be gathered prior to officially creating a district this is something

Staff is requesting Town Council approval to extend the Mobile & Modular Business Pilot 
Program through December 31, 2027. The revised Administrative Rules and proposed Fee 
Schedule are attached for Council consideration. 
The Pilot Program was initially established in 2026 to evaluate whether small-scale mobile and 
modular businesses could operate appropriately on Town-owned property on a temporary 
basis before the Town considers any permanent policy or Town Code amendments. 
Initial Pilot Program Experience 
Two businesses participated in the initial pilot program: Little Blue Bakery and Nomadic Roots 
Sauna. 
Little Blue Bakery has operated from a mobile bakery trailer since the beginning of the pilot. 
Staff has received no complaints and has identified no significant operational issues associated 
with the mobile bakery unit. Staff believes the operation can continue under the extended pilot 
program. If the program is extended, Little Blue Bakery would be subject to the applicable 
monthly license fees established in the proposed Fee Schedule. 
Nomadic Roots Sauna established a temporary operation at Eagle River Park in June 2026. 
Due primarily to staffing capacity limitations, the sauna operated only intermittently during the 
summer despite remaining on site. The sauna has since been removed from Town property. 
Staff received no complaints or negative feedback regarding the operation. 
Proposed 2027 Extension 
Staff recommends extending the Pilot Program through December 31, 2027 to provide 
additional time to evaluate the operational, administrative, fiscal, and land-use considerations 
associated with mobile and modular businesses operating on Town-owned property. 
The additional evaluation period would provide staff and Council with more information before 
determining whether a permanent Town Code amendment or other long-term regulatory 
framework should be considered. 
Staff does not intend to significantly expand the program during the extension period. Due to 
limited staff capacity and the continued pilot nature of the program, staff anticipates limiting 
participation to approximately two to three active licensees at any one time.

 D. Ordinance 11 - Series 2026 An Ordinance Amending the Water Moratorium 
Mike S.  
Updated Council on how the Town’s water rights work, especially as it relates to the most 
extreme drought in recent years.   
The Town’s water supply comes almost exclusively from Cross Creek.  Cross Creek is not a 
very large drainage.  The Town has a senior water right that is subject to certain limitations.  
Although we refer to it is a senior water right, it is junior to the Shoshone Call (the hydroelectric 
plant in Glenwood Canyon).  This typically does not really affect the Town because of 
something called the Historic User Pool (HUP).  The HUP is a pool of 66,000 acre feet of water 
storing Green Mountain Reservoir. It was part of a compromise when they built the what's 
called the Northern Project, where they take water out of Grand Lake and send it over to the 
Front Range. They had to dedicate this 66,000 acre foot pool for West Slope users. 
And normally, we simply rely on the Bureau of Reclamation to release water on our behalf out 
the Green Mountain Reservoir and It's just shown the call is fulfilled, and we don't think about it. 
Well, this year, the historic user pool only filled to two-thirds of its capacity.  And that pool ran 
dry, uh, on August 15th. So there was for a good part of the summer season that the historic 
user pool was replacing depletions on behalf of the town. But at that point we had to begin 
operating under the town's plan for augmentation and under that plan - We have water 
contracts for water in Eagle Park Reservoir, as well as in Wolford Mountain Reservoir, and the 
town's water engineer, Martin and Wood, began essentially having to account for directing that 
releases begin to occur from those reservoirs, and to account for the town's usage based on 
that. 
The town was to some degree helped by the fact that there's a kind of an umbrella 
organization, the Colorado River Water Conservation District, a West Slope water provider and 
advocacy group. They actually own Wolford Mountain Reservoir and they decreed an 
emergency substitute water supply plan that covered municipalities who agreed to limit 
watering to only one day a week. And because the town had ample water in Eagle Park that we 
pay for every year, we chose not to go under that plan immediately, but we augmented our 
depletions out of our supplies through October 1. 
The idea was, as we have the water, we might as well allow the citizens to benefit from that. 
Starting October 1, as you'll remember at the Prior Council meeting, we adopted revised 
watering restrictions, which is one day a week. We have since gone under the substitute water 
supply plan from the river district. 
 
And that plan will carry us through the end of the year and hopefully as snow and rain come to 
the west slope again, some of those calls start to drop off and we're able to divert under our 
senior water right again. 
We will have, a little later in the year, a debrief with the water engineers and kind of come up 
with a recap of what we learned from this route, and how it affected the town's operations and 
town's water rights, and we'll probably do a memo and follow up with you on that (early 2027). 
We did find two things out that are fairly important. 
The most critical of which is that in an extremely dry year, that Cross Creek continued to 
provide all of the physical supply that the town needed to run its operations. 
And it did so in an amount where we didn't even have to go up and like shore up the check dam 
to put the water in the creek. There was actually water flowing into the town's intake, and some 
water continuing to flow in the creek. That's really good news, because, and this is the second 
thing we learned, is that the agreement that the town entered into with the Colorado Water 
Conservation District about 2-3 years ago, where they acknowledge that we have some pre-
existing use that predates their in-stream flow - that we were able to operate under the town's 
plan for augmentation by releasing water from Eagle Park Reservoir down the Eagle River. We 
did not physically pick it up on the Eagle River and put it in our plant. It just went by. And we 
were able to take water by what's called exchange, and that held up throughout even a very 
serious drought year, and stayed within the limits of the agreement that we have with Colorado 
Water Conservation Board (CWCB).   
Those are 2 important things. We learned that it's all good, and it plays into one of the 2 
reasons that we implemented the moratorium which you'll recall from those of you who've been 
on the board longer (Initially entered into in 2020, modified in 2023). 
The moratorium was enacted due to 2 concerns. One was that we potentially would have a 
legal constriction on the town's water rights based on the limitations in the veil put on our senior 
water right, and that we might have periods where we actually could no longer divert on Cross 
Creek. The implementation of the agreement with the CWCB, and knowing that that can 
operate by exchange in a dry year. 
This has given us more confidence that the legal water right scenario that we've built for the 
town will hold up even in dry years, that we'll be able to augment by exchange, even if we hit 
the limits that that are in the stipulation with Vail.  And that we can use those legal water rights 
to supply more growth within their decreed place of use than what we had previously had a 
concern about. 
That decreed place of use, I would simply note, because this may be something we talk about 
in the next little while, does not include Battle Mountain. Battle Mountain is expressly excluded 
from the place of use limitations, in the town's water right portfolio. We cannot use Cross Creek 
water to supply Battle Mountain. 
But for the remainder of town, including growth that happens within those boundaries, we now 
have more confidence about our legal water supply and ability to divert to meet existing 
demand plus some growth. 
The second thing that's happened of course, is we're building a new water treatment plant. And 
the other limitation that required the imposition of the moratorium was that the old sand filter 
plants had some real reliability question marks around them, and we didn't want to further 
stress their ability to perform by adding a lot of new growth. 
 
But we're building a new plant.  And within the physical treatment calculations that your 
consultants had done when we enacted the moratorium, there was an assumption that we were 
going to reserve essentially 120 SFEs of service for the school district. That's actually a 
requirement in some agreements that we have with the school district. And while the school 
district is working on moving from preliminary plan to the final plat, they still have not filed.  That 
will take a number of months before a plat can be approved, and then they have to start moving 
dirt, and they would not have a vertical product actually using water from the town system, 
likely until 2028, after the new water treatment plant comes online. 
So, we have reached a level of comfort in consultation with our water consultants that we 
actually have both some additional legal supply and some additional physical supply that can 
be made available for new uses in the town. And it's been a long 6 or 7 years to get to this 
point, and we recognize that that it has been some hardship on landowners and persons 
desiring to build new water-using projects in Minturn.   
We are now able to recommend that we amend the moratorium at this point to allow for 50 
additional SFEs of service to be made available. 
The prior moratorium essentially was you got your historic use plus one SFE. So if you had a 
vacant lot in town, you could build a house. If you had a house, you could build an ADU with 
the house. This 50 SFEs could be used by either existing developments that have been platted

again, given that we're starting the winter season, won't happen until 2027.   
We've drafted an ordinance to this effect. The ordinance does have a couple of provisions that

You're limited to what your zoning allows, and we're going to be very critical about making sure

the 50 SFEs are spoken for, we go back to existing historic use plus one. Because we feel like 
we can make 50 available now, then once the new water treatment plant comes online, we 
foresee rescinding the moratorium. 
And it will be on a first-come, first-served basis.  We're anticipating that there will be the ability 
to serve approximately an additional 450 SFUs of service at that time. 
We feel this is a responsible way forward with the water system, given where we're currently at. 
Council is presented an ordinance continuing the existing water moratorium previously modified 
on February 15, 2023. The attached ordinance updating the moratorium takes into 
consideration updates to the Town of Minturn’s (the “Town”) legal limitations, supply concerns, 
and treatment capacity since the Town’s prior moratorium modification. Specifically, the 
allowance of fifty (50) additional Single-Family Equivalents (“SFEs”) made available on a first-
come, first-served basis.  
Background  
In May 2020, the Council passed Ordinance No. 5, Series 2020 enacting a moratorium on the 
allocation of new SFEs of water service from the Town's water system. A version of the 
moratorium has been in place since 2020. The 2020 water moratorium was in reaction to 
analysis of the Town's capacity to supply water undertaken as part of the 2019 Capital 
Improvement Plan (“CIP”). The moratorium was based on limitations in both the Town’s water 
treatment capacity, due to the aging treatment plant, and its senior water rights, which limited 
the Town’s legal ability to divert water for municipal use under certain circumstances.  
In February 2023, additional data had been gathered, and new circumstances required the 
Town to revisit the 2020 moratorium. Specifically, the Town hired HDR Engineering to provide 
a more detailed analysis of the ability of the Town's existing water treatment plant to meet, at 
that time, current and future service needs. The Town also received guidance from water rights 
engineers Martin & Wood regarding the Town's capacity to provide water under the Town's 
existing water rights.  
Most significantly, the 2023 update reduced SFE availability from Historical Use plus three (3) 
additional SFEs per property to Historical Use plus one (1) additional SFE per property.  
In response to the 2019 CIP, the findings that informed both the original 2020 moratorium and 
the updated moratorium in 2023, the Town pursued addressing the three most restrictive 
elements justifying imposition of the moratorium. Those were, and to some extent continue to 
be:  
1) Legal restrictions imposing monthly consumptive use limits that restrict that the Town’s 
ability to source its water supply from Cross Creek under certain circumstances; and  
2) Capacity constraints due to limitations on the existing water treatment plant’s ability to treat 
water due to age and limitations on operation of certain sand filter trays.  
Recommendation for Modification and Continuation of the Moratorium  
How the Town has addressed or is in the process of addressing the two limitations and the 
rationale provided for the proposed update to the moratorium follow.  
1. Legal Restrictions on Source Water. The Town has confirmed its augmentation plan 
for Cross Creek and the Eagle River can operate under draught conditions. In conformance 
with the agreement with the CWCB, the Town can divert up to 2.1 cfs of direct diversion from 
Cross Creek. In 2026, the Town had adequate augmentation supplies to serve existing growth 
plus additional SFEs.  
2. Potable Water Capacity Constraints w/ Existing Water Treatment Plant. This 
constraint remains. However, the Town is in the process of constructing a new water treatment 
plant with anticipated activation in late 2027. It is estimated that the new water treatment plant 
will nearly double the treatment and delivery capacity over the existing water treatment plant. 
The existing water treatment plant has some additional capacity as the Town had received 120 
SFEs of service for the school district property which have not come on-line.  
With condition one being resolved, and condition two being resolved soon, Staff recommends 
making fifty (50) additional SFEs available upon adoption of the proposed ordinance. It is 
recommended that these be made available on a first-come, first-served basis determined by 
either submission of a final plat creating individual lots (not blocks or tracts) or the issuance of a 
building permit for existing lots.  
Due to the ongoing capacity constraints with the existing water treatment plant and until such a 
time as the new water treatment plant comes online, Staff recommends maintaining the current 
allocation limit of Historical Use plus one (1) additional SFE on either submission of a final plat 
or the issuance of a building permit once the additional allotment of 50 SFEs is exhausted.  
Summary  
Staff believe that the legal and supply-related uncertainties that supported the original 
moratorium have been or will be soon substantially resolved. While treatment capacity 
constraints remain until the new water treatment plant is operational, staff finds that sufficient 
capacity exists to support an additional allocation of fifty (50) SFEs while maintaining the 
existing moratorium framework thereafter.

 A. 2027 Staffing, Compensation and Employee Benefits – Discussion and Direction 
Staff is recommending several targeted staffing, compensation, and employee benefit changes 
for 2027 intended to support organizational continuity, recruitment and retention, and the 
Town’s ability to meet increasing operational and community needs. The recommendations are 
summarized below, with additional background, comparison data, and fiscal impacts provided 
in the sections that follow. 
• Communications, Events & Community Engagement: Establish a part-time, term-
limited coordinator as a 12-month pilot, budgeted at approximately 24 hours per week 
at $30–$51 per hour. 
• Clerk/Treasurer Transition: Use 2027 to evaluate the long-term staffing structure for 
Clerk/Treasurer and finance functions ahead of the anticipated 2028 transition. 
• Public Works Housing Stipend: Provide up to $550 per month to eligible Public 
Works employees who do not occupy Town-provided housing. 
• 2027 Salary Ranges: Apply a 2.5% market-based adjustment to Town salary ranges 
and eligible employee base pay. 
• Merit-Based Adjustments: Allow eligible employees to receive an additional 0.5% to 
2.5% merit increase based on performance and position within the salary range. 
• Paid Holidays: Increase paid holidays from 10 to 12 days by adding Juneteenth and 
Christmas Eve. 
• Vacation Accrual: Maintain 11 days through the first year, increase to 13 days after 
one year through five years, and retain the existing 16-day and 21-day long-term 
accrual levels. 
Communications, Events & Community Engagement Coordinator – 12-Month Pilot  
Staff recommends establishing a part-time, term-limited Communications, Events & Community 
Engagement Coordinator as a 12-month pilot, initially budgeted at approximately 24 hours 
per week. 
The position would assume responsibility for communications, community engagement, event 
planning and promotion, vendor and sponsorship coordination, and related revenue-producing 
activities currently shared by the Deputy Clerk. This shift would allow the Deputy Clerk position 
to focus more directly on Clerk/Treasurer and administrative functions while giving the Town 
dedicated capacity to strengthen event programming, sponsorships, vendor participation, 
attendance, and cost recovery. 
Staff recommends a non-benefited hiring range of $30-$51 per hour, depending on 
qualifications and experience. For comparison, Avon's full-time Special Events Coordinator 
range of $72,000-$94,000 equates to approximately $34.62-$45.19 per hour. The upper 
portion of Minturn's proposed range reflects the part-time, non-benefited nature of the position. 
At 24 hours per week, annual base wages would range from approximately $37,440 to 
$63,648. 
Staff proposes beginning recruitment in November 2026, with the goal of filling the position by 
December 2026. Before preparation of the 2028 budget, staff would evaluate the pilot based on 
workload, communications and engagement outcomes, event performance, cost recovery, and 
the continuing need for dedicated staffing. 
Deputy Clerk Transition and Future Clerk/Treasurer Staffing 
Staff recommends using 2027 as a transition and evaluation period for future Clerk/Treasurer 
staffing. As communications and event responsibilities shift to the new part-time position, the 
Deputy Clerk role can focus more fully on Clerk/Treasurer and related administrative functions. 
The Town's current Clerk-Treasurer has successfully performed both functions within a single 
position, but staff is not yet certain whether that structure will remain the most sustainable 
model following the anticipated spring 2028 retirement. Rather than immediately creating an 
additional finance position, staff recommends evaluating workload, technical needs, and 
available staffing options during 2027. 
Potential options include rehiring a Deputy Clerk, adding specialized finance or accounting 
support, sharing a finance position with the Town of Red Cliff, using contracted financial 
support, or some combination of these approaches. Staff anticipates evaluating the long-term 
structure by mid-2027 so that any necessary changes can be incorporated into planning for the 
2028 transition. 
Public Works Housing Stipend  
Staff recommends establishing a housing stipend of up to $550 per month for eligible Public 
Works employees who do not occupy Town-provided employee housing, initially including 
Maintenance Technicians, Equipment Operators, and the Public Works Lead. 
The stipend is intended to support recruitment and retention while providing a partial alternative 
to the Town's limited employee-housing benefit. For 2027, Town employee rents are 
approximately $840 to $1,630 per month below estimated market rents, depending on unit 
size. A $550 monthly stipend would therefore provide only a portion of that benefit and would 
not fully offset private-market housing costs. 
The proposed amount is also comparable to Eagle River Water & Sanitation District, which 
currently provides a $559 monthly housing stipend based on the gap between local market 
rents and employee-housing rents. Staff recommends reviewing the stipend annually based on 
housing costs, recruitment and retention needs, the value of Town-provided housing, and 
available funding. Employees occupying Town-provided housing would not be eligible. 
2027 Salary Ranges 
In fall 2025, the Town established salary ranges for all positions using compensation data from 
comparable regional local governments and existing Town compensation. Several positions 
required market adjustments as part of that process, most notably the Code Enforcement 
Officer/Permit Technician and planning classifications. After reviewing the ranges and current 
regional comparisons, staff does not recommend substantial position-specific realignments for 
2027. 
Staff does, however, recommend annually adjusting salary ranges to help prevent the Town's 
compensation structure from falling behind the labor market over time. The purpose of this 
adjustment is to maintain the competitiveness of the ranges themselves and is distinct from 
merit-based increases that recognize individual performance, experience, and development 
within an established range. 
Consumer prices have increased approximately 2.4% nationally from January through 
August 2026, while the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood CPI-U increased approximately 2.85% from 
January through July 2026. Based on these measures and current regional compensation 
comparisons, staff recommends a 2.5% market adjustment to the 2027 salary ranges and a 
corresponding 2.5% base-pay adjustment for eligible employees, separate from any merit-
based increase. 
Merit-Based Employee Adjustments 
In addition to the proposed 2.5% market adjustment to the 2027 salary ranges and eligible 
employee base pay, individual employees may be eligible for a merit-based pay increase of 
0.5% to 2.5% based on performance and their position within the applicable salary range. 
The market adjustment is intended to keep the Town's compensation structure aligned with the 
regional labor market, while merit increases recognize individual performance and 
development. Merit increases are not automatic and would be considered through the Town's 
annual performance review process, subject to satisfactory performance, available budget, and 
the maximum of the employee's salary range. 
An eligible employee receiving both adjustments could receive a combined 2027 base-pay 
adjustment ranging from 3.0% to 5.0%, depending on individual performance and position 
within the applicable salary range. 
Paid Holiday Schedule 
Staff recommends expanding the Town's annual paid holiday schedule from 10 to 12 holidays 
by adding Juneteenth (June 19) and Christmas Eve (December 24). 
Proposed Vacation Accrual Adjustment 
Staff recommends a modest adjustment to the Town’s vacation accrual schedule for 
employees in their early years of service. Under the current policy, full-time employees receive 
11 vacation days annually from hire through completion of five years of continuous 
service, increasing to 16 days after completion of five years and 21 days after 
completion of 10 years.
