Community E-bike Youth Safety Campaign 
Regina O’Brien, Deputy County Manager introduced this topic, and shared a 
presentation.  The County (based on feedback from local municipalities Mayors and 
Managers, and HOAs) established an e-bike safety committee, which is now referred 
to as the Micro-Mobility Committee.  This committee has members from other 
municipal partners, Mountain Youth, Eagle County School District (ECSD) and Vail 
Health.  Primary areas of concern within this topic are helmet use, visibility, and speed 
management.  The campaign that they have been working on with partners is called 
Own the Ride.  The hope is that by working collaboratively in our local region, that we 
can establish consistency.   
Collateral pieces / materials are available in multiple formats, including:  a social 
media video series and large format posters, stickers, etc.  The materials are 
available to everyone.  The program actually launched in the spring, with the hope of 
getting kids as they were leaving school and then heading into summertime.  Posters 
and stickers were visibly placed in high schools.  Core Transit placed them at bus 
shelters.  Town of Avon used them at their sustainability fair.  Mountain Youth used 
them at Youthapalooza.  ECSD included them in their newsletters.   
Other items still being worked on include a before and after the campaign survey, a 
Parents Checklist, a Helmet Pledge, and a full website.    
Town Staff will share these materials soon on both the Town website, and in 
upcoming newsletters.

Shooting Range Project 
The Town of Minturn was recently awarded a $225,000 grant from CPW to make

Regional Housing Authority (RHA) 
Rob G. noted the Town recently had a planning intern (Jessica Copeland) who spent 
some time with Planning Commission and Council and worked with Rob to construct a 
bit of a vision of how participation in a RHA could work for Minturn.  Rob recapped 
that the Town’s primary housing concerns are echoed throughout the Valley (and 
beyond) including increased displacement of working class families, a high amount of 
unoccupied homes, high costs to operate a business and the associated issues of 
finding employees and employee housing.  The Town sees the RHA as a way to 
maintain the Town’s character, make sure there’s space in the community for working 
people and working families.  The Town is appreciative of the work already done by 
the County and Avon in this realm, and would like to keep the conversation going.   
 
Jeff S. noted that the RHA is still a moving target.  The housing group is still on a fact-
finding mission.  The comments from one end of the valley to the other are all over the 
place, so still trying to find the model of how to do this.  A hybrid model seems to be 
the best option, given that each of our local communities have different needs, and 
some communities will quickly run out of developable real estate, where others have 
more capacity for that.  Traffic and other impacts also need to be considered.   
 
Jeff S. noted that they want to hear from the business community.  He provided an 
example of how CORE Transit was formed.  This was more of a grassroots effort (led 
by local businesses, vs. government).  The business community came to the County 
and expressed that the EcoTransit model was no longer working.   
 
Jeff S. feels that housing should also be led by the business community (business 
meaning all types of business + medical/hospital, schools, etc) – any organization that 
generates sales tax in the community.  Government is running out of its ability to just 
ask for taxation.  It’s a frustrating conversation across the country.  Jeff S. noted that 
the County had this on the ballot twice before and it failed.   
 
Rob G. noted that the past ballot measures perhaps lacked clarify or a specific plan.   
 
Jeanne M. touched on the State Land Board housing project example.   
 
There was consensus amongst the Commissioners regarding a hybrid model being 
the best approach to an RHA.   
 
MacKenzie H. inquired about the target timeline for something like this.   
Tom B. responded that he would be surprised if there was a solid proposal in time for 
the 2028 ballot.  This is more long term.  More clarity is needed before bringing this to 
voters.   
 
It was also noted, based on the initial survey results, that there is not yet widespread 
support for an RHA.  They need to go back to the drawing board, and include more 
partners that perhaps weren’t initially included.

 A. New Application for a Spiritous Liquor Manufacturer's Expanded Alcohol Beverage 
Liquor Permit - Eagle River Distilling LLC, dba Minturn Whisky Co. 
Council is asked to consider approval of a Spiritous Liquor Manufacturer's Expanded Alcohol 
Beverage Liquor Permit. This application is from Eagle River Distilling llc, dba Minturn Whisky 
Spence Neubauer Member/Manager located at 161 Main St.. As there is a current and active 
distilled spirits tasting room liquor permit on the premises, the owners have filed the necessary 
application to expand the current menu to allow sales of beer, wine, and other spirituous liquors 
specifically NOT distilled or brewed on sight or solely by Minturn Whisky as required under the 
existing permit.  Their own product needs to maintain at least 51% of their overall sales.   
This request is a request for a brand new class of license that was approved by the 2026 
legislature and signed into law effective August 2026 to allow for expanded liquor offerings.  
Minturn Whisky Co. is actually the first applicant in the state for this type of license. This 
expanded use will include limited food service (sandwiches and light snacks). 
Council had an opportunity to ask questions of the applicant, of which there were none.

new facility (once their new distillery is built).  It was confirmed that it would, but that would 
require an amendment of the licensed premises.   
Motion by Gusty K., second by Kate S., to approve the application for a Spiritous Liquor

Minturn Whisky Co.

 A. Resolution No. 43 - Series 2026, A Resolution supporting S. 5101 and H.R. 9826, The 
Glenwood Hot Springs Protection Act 
In July, Representative Hurd and Senators Bennet and Hickenlooper introduced H.R. 9826 and 
S. 5101, the Glenwood Hot Springs Protection Act (the “Act”), which will prohibit expansion of 
the Mid-Continent Limestone Quarry (the “Quarry”) on Bureau of Land Management (“BLM”) 
land outside of Glenwood Springs. The Quarry has been in operation since 1982 and currently 

operates under a 14-acre permit. In 2019 the new owner submitted an application to BLM to 
expand the Quarry permit to over 400 acres. The expansion proposal would essentially remove 
the hillside behind the Glenwood Caverns Adventure Park above Glenwood Springs, require 
hundreds of daily truck trips to the rail loadout, and be visible from most locations in the City. It 
would also potentially threaten the hot springs aquifer that supplies water to the Glenwood Hot 
Springs Pool and the Iron Mountain Hot Springs. 
The Quarry owner continues to press for expansion, though presently at a smaller scale, that 
continues to threaten the hot springs and Glenwood Springs’ economic engine. As a result of 
years of advocacy by the City, local businesses, and concerned residents, the area’s 
Congressional representatives introduced the Act in order to block processing of any application 
to expand the Quarry. 
The Glenwood Springs City Council has declared September 4 as “Love Glenwood Day” in 
partnership with the Glenwood Springs Citizens Alliance, which has additional information on 
the Quarry and proposals to expand it at https://loveglenwood.org/. 
The City of Glenwood Springs respectfully requests that the Town of Minturn demonstrate their 
support for the Act by passing the resolution attached below.  
Motion by Gusty K., second by MacKenzie H., to approve Resolution No. 43 – Series 2026, A 
Resolution supporting S. 5101 and H.R. 9826, The Glenwood Hot Springs Protection Act.  
Motion passed 5-0.   
B. Resolution No. 44 - Series 2026, A Resolution Updating Minturn's Outdoor
Emergency Watering Restrictions
The Town has been operating under emergency water restrictions this summer, allowing 
outdoor watering two days per week between 8:00 p.m. and 8:00 a.m. Due to this year’s water 
supply conditions and the Town’s use of its water augmentation plan, the Town is reducing 
outdoor watering to one day per week for the remainder of the 2026 watering season. The 
Town’s water consultants and attorneys determined that utilizing the Town’s augmentation 
supplies will allow the Town to continue meeting its water needs while maintaining appropriate 
protection for its water rights. The one-day-per-week restriction is expected to remain in effect 
through the end of the watering season. 
This change applies only to outdoor watering and will remain in effect for the remainder of the 
watering season. With most irrigation systems typically being winterized by mid-October, the 
change will have a relatively short-term impact. 
New restriction: Outdoor watering is limited to one day per week, between 8 p.m. and 8 
a.m., for the remainder of the 2026 watering season.
