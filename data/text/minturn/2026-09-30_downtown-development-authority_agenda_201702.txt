of said policy.

Incentive Policy.

Purpose and Intent 
The purpose of the Investment and Incentive Policy document is to establish a clear framework for how the 
Minturn Downtown Development Authority (MDDA) evaluates, prioritizes, and invests its resources in potential 
projects and proposals. The MDDA recognizes that Tax Increment Financing (TIF) and other public resources are 
limited and should be invested strategically to maximize public benefit. Investments made by the MDDA are 
intended to support goals and priorities identified in the MDDA Plan of Development and other adopted plans, 
while advancing projects that strengthen downtown Minturn as a vibrant commercial, cultural, and community 
destination, while also considering the anticipated return on public investment.   
MDDA recognizes that long-term success of the district depends upon maintaining a sustainable and growing tax 
base. Accordingly, investments should be structured to encourage private investment, strengthen property values,

Project Funding 
The MDDA Board may annually establish funding allocations for grant programs, infrastructure projects, 
redevelopment partnerships, strategic property acquisition activities, administration, and other eligible 
investment categories authorized by this policy and as funding becomes available. 
The purpose of annual funding allocations is to support financial planning, maintain flexibility, and ensure that 
MDDA resources remain available to address a variety of district needs and opportunities. Funding allocations 
may be modified by the Board throughout the year based on project demand, available revenues, emerging 
opportunities, or changing priorities. 
Establishment of a funding allocation does not oblige the MDDA to expend funds, nor does it guarantee funding for 
any specific project or program. The MDDA Board retains sole discretion regarding all funding decisions and may 
approve investments that differ from established allocations when it determines doing so advances the goals of 
the Plan of Development and serves public interest. 
Guiding Investment Principles 
The following principles are intended to guide all MDDA investments and incentive decisions. While not every 
project will advance each of the following principles shown, proposed investments should generally support one 
or more principles while contributing to the long-term success of downtown Minturn. 
• Keep Minturn Authentic - The MDDA shall prioritize investments that preserve and strengthen Minturn's unique 
identity, historic character, and small-town scale. Projects should reinforce qualities that make Minturn 
distinctive, support locally owned businesses where possible, and contribute to a downtown environment that 
reflects the community's heritage, culture, and sense of place. 
• Invest Local, Thrive Local - The MDDA shall encourage reinvestment in existing properties, support business 
occupancy, activate underutilized spaces, and promote a vibrant mix of commercial, residential, and 
community uses. Investments should strengthen the local economy by encouraging long-term property 
stewardship, private investment, and increased economic activity within the district. 
• Connected Minturn - The MDDA shall support projects that promote interconnectivity within the district and 
strengthen cohesion between downtown and surrounding areas. Priority may be given to investments that 
enhance streetscapes, improve access to the Eagle River and recreation amenities, strengthen public spaces, 
and coordinate infrastructure improvements that support economic activity, mobility, and community 
gathering.

Project Review Process and Application 
To ensure consistency, transparency, and alignment with the MDDA Plan of Development, the MDDA shall use a 
structured process for reviewing and evaluating investment and incentive requests. The following section provides 
a guiding framework for the MDDA’s review process. The level of review may vary based on the size, complexity, 
and funding request of a proposed project. 
Pre-Application Coordination 
For any request, either large catalyst redevelopment project or small grant application, applicants are encouraged 
to participate in a pre-application meeting with MDDA staff. This meeting is intended to: 
• Discuss project concept and eligibility 
• Review applicable MDDA priorities and evaluation criteria 
• Identify potential public benefits and areas of alignment with the Plan of Development 
• Identify other sources of funding that may be available, including state housing grants, and other assistance 
• Outline application requirements, timelines, and next steps 
 
Pre-application meetings are advisory in nature and do not constitute a commitment of funding by the MDDA. 
Formal Application Requirements 
An application shall be required for all incentive requests exceeding an amount established by the MDDA Board. 
The Board may establish different application requirements based on program type or funding level. 
At a minimum, applications shall include sufficient information for the MDDA to evaluate: 
• Project scope and description 
• Project costs and sources and uses of funds 
• Project concept designs if available 
• Development timeline and milestones 
• Demonstration of financial feasibility and need for assistance 
• Anticipated public benefits and economic impacts 
• Consistency with the MDDA Plan of Development and adopted policies 
• The MDDA may require additional materials as necessary to evaluate the proposal. 
 
Applicants seeking MDDA participation in a larger project as defined in this policy as exceeding $100,000  in total 
investment (see section Large Redevelopment and Catalyst Projects) shall be required to submit a formal request 
to the MDDA through the designated contact person or office (Cindy Krieg, Interim Executive Director). The request 
should provide sufficient information for the MDDA to evaluate the project, its public benefits, financial feasibility, 
and consistency with district goals. 
At a minimum, applicants shall demonstrate: 
• Consistency with the MDDA Plan of Development and this Investment and Incentive Policy 
• Compliance with local regulations, approvals, zoning requirements, permitting requirements, and design 
guidelines if applicable.

• Evidence of project readiness, including project schedule, development milestones, and anticipated

Incentive Agreement Requirements 
The MDDA's preferred approach is to structure redevelopment assistance as a performance-based partnership. 
Accordingly, projects receiving financial assistance through tax increment sharing, reimbursement agreements, 
development agreements, grants, or other forms of MDDA participation may be required to enter into a written 
Incentive Agreement approved by the MDDA Board. 
The purpose of an Incentive Agreement is to establish clear expectations, protect public resources, and ensure 
that the anticipated public benefits are delivered. 
Depending on the nature and scale of the project, an Incentive Agreement may address: 
• Project scope and descriptions 
• Development milestones and performance requirements 
• Construction commencement and completion deadlines 
• Reporting and documentation requirements 
• Verification of project costs and eligible expenses 
• Estimated property valuation upon completion and stabilization 
Staff Review and Analysis 
MDDA staff (or a designated representative) shall review submitted applications for completeness and provide an 
initial evaluation of the proposal. 
Staff review may include: 
• Verification of application completeness and eligibility 
• Assessment of project readiness and feasibility 
• Review of financial information and funding gaps (as applicable) 
• Review of consistency with local regulations and guidelines including but not limited to the Plan of 
Development, investment priorities, and evaluation criteria 
Based on this review, staff shall prepare a recommendation for the MDDA Board’s consideration.

Board Consideration 
For projects requiring Board approval, the MDDA Board shall receive a summary of the proposed investment, 
which will generally include: 
• A staff memorandum outlining the project and recommendation 
• A financial summary of project costs, funding sources, and requested assistance 
• An evaluation of the project’s alignment with the Plan of Development and public benefit objectives 
 
The Board may consider the proposal at a public meeting in accordance with applicable laws and procedures. 
Board Action 
Following review and discussion, the MDDA Board may take one or more of the following actions: 
• Approve the request as presented and direct staff to enter into a contract with the applicant. 
• Deny the request.  
• Request revisions or additional information from the applicant. 
• Authorize staff to enter into negotiations regarding the terms and structure of MDDA participation with the 
intent of completing a development agreement. Project / development agreements then would come back to 
the board for final contract approval.   
 
Approval of a funding request may be conditioned upon specific requirements, available budget, performance 
measures, or the execution of formal agreements as determined by the Board. 
Flexibility and Discretion 
The MDDA Board retains sole discretion in all funding decisions. The review process may be adjusted by the Board 
as necessary to respond to unique project circumstances, funding availability, or evolving district priorities, 
provided principles of transparency, fairness, and alignment with the Plan of Development are maintained.

Eligible Investment Categories 
The MDDA may invest in projects, programs, and initiatives that advance the goals of the Plan of Development and 
support the economic vitality of the district. Investment programs may be established and modified by the MDDA 
Board based on available funding, community priorities, and district needs.  
Small Project Matching Grant Program 
The MDDA may establish a Small Project Matching Grant Program to encourage property improvements, business 
investment, building rehabilitation, and revitalization. The purpose of the program is to attract private investment, 
improve appearance and building functionality and safety, support local businesses, and preserve Minturn’s 
downtown character. 
Eligible applicants may include property owners, business owners with property owner authorization, nonprofit 
organizations, and other entities approved by the MDDA. 
Eligible improvements include: 
• Building façade improvements 
• Accessibility and ADA-related improvements 
• Limited tenant improvements that enhance occupancy or business operations 
• Historic preservation and restoration activities 
• Safety or other improvements determined by the MDDA to further the goals of the district 
Grant Amount 
The MDDA may provide matching grants for eligible projects on a reimbursement basis. 
• Grants may provide up to 50 percent of eligible project costs, with a maximum of $12,500 per project 
• Properties are limited to one small matching grant every five years 
• Applicants shall provide documentation demonstrating the required private match 
• The MDDA may establish annual funding limits based on available revenues 
Evaluation Criteria 
Applications may be evaluated based on: 
• Consistency with the MDDA Plan of Development 
• Visibility and impact on the public realm 
• Contribution to economic activity and property activation 
• Length of continuous ownership or operation of a business 
• Historic preservation benefits 
• Leveraging private investment 
• Readiness to proceed 
• Long-term benefit to the district

Program Requirements 
The MDDA Board may at its discretion establish one or more matching grant programs to advance the purposes of 
this policy. The Board shall adopt and periodically update application materials, program guidelines, evaluation 
criteria, funding priorities, and administrative procedures as necessary to implement the program. The application 
process shall be designed to ensure transparency, consistency, and accountability while allowing the MDDA 
flexibility to respond to changing district needs and available funding. 
The MDDA Board shall retain sole discretion regarding the award of grant funds and may establish annual funding 
limits, application deadlines, minimum project requirements, reimbursement procedures, and other program 
requirements through separate program guidelines. 
Grant awards shall be approved by the MDDA Board. Improvements must comply with all applicable local 
regulations and design requirements. The MDDA reserves the right to prioritize projects that provide the greatest 
public benefit, leverage significant private investment, or support strategic redevelopment objectives within the 
district. 
Public Infrastructure 
The MDDA may invest in public infrastructure projects that support economic vitality, improve the visitor 
experience, enhance connectivity and safety, facilitate access to the river, and encourage private investment 
within the district. 
Eligible infrastructure investments may include, but are not limited to: 
• Streetscape improvements, including sidewalks, landscaping, lighting, street furnishings, and wayfinding 
• Utility improvements that support redevelopment, business activity, housing, or district-wide infrastructure 
needs 
• Public parking facilities, parking management improvements, and related access enhancements 
• Trail connections and multimodal transportation improvements that improve access to downtown and 
community destinations 
• Eagle River access improvements, riverfront amenities, and projects that strengthen connections between 
downtown and the river corridor 
• Public spaces, gathering areas, plazas, and other improvements that support community activity and 
economic development 
In evaluating infrastructure investments, the MDDA shall consider the project's public benefit, contribution to 
economic activity, support for redevelopment objectives, coordination with Town infrastructure priorities, and 
ability to leverage additional public or private investment. 
The MDDA may participate in infrastructure projects through direct investment, cost-sharing agreements, grants, 
reimbursement agreements, or other financing mechanisms authorized by law and approved by the Board.

The MDDA recognizes that certain redevelopment projects may have a transformative impact on downtown 
Minturn by increasing economic activity, supporting housing opportunities, improving underutilized properties, 
enhancing public spaces, or addressing site constraints that would otherwise limit investment. The MDDA may

participate in larger redevelopment and catalyst projects that advance the goals of the Plan of Development and 
provide a substantial public benefit. 
For purposes of this policy, a Redevelopment or Catalyst Project is generally defined as a project involving total 
eligible project costs exceeding $100,000. 
Eligible projects may include, but are not limited to: 
• Mixed-use development and redevelopment projects 
• Rehabilitation or repurposing of underutilized properties 
• Housing-supportive investments and workforce housing projects 
• Site readiness and infrastructure improvements necessary to facilitate development 
• Environmental remediation and brownfield redevelopment activities 
• Historic preservation and adaptive reuse projects 
• Other catalytic investments determined by the MDDA to significantly advance district objectives 
Performance-Based Partnership Approach 
The MDDA's preferred approach is to structure assistance as a performance-based partnership. Assistance 
should generally be tied to measurable outcomes, completed improvements, or the generation of new tax 
increment revenue. The MDDA may utilize reimbursement agreements, tax increment sharing agreements, 
development agreements, or other mechanisms authorized by law. 
As a general guideline, the MDDA may consider sharing up to 50 percent of the incremental tax revenue generated 
by a qualifying project, subject to Board approval, project merits, financial feasibility, and the public benefits 
provided. The MDDA reserves the right to provide a lesser amount based on available resources, project 
characteristics, and competing funding priorities.  The following are the primary considerations for the MDDA 
when reviewing requests for a TIF-share partnership.   
• Tax increment sharing calculations and payment procedures 
• Public improvements or public benefit commitments 
• Maintenance obligations, where applicable 
• Events of default and remedies 
• Recapture, repayment, or termination provisions in the event of non-performance 
• Other terms and conditions deemed necessary by the MDDA Board to protect the public interest 
The MDDA Board reserves the right to tailor Incentive Agreement requirements to the specific circumstances of 
each project. The execution of an Incentive Agreement may be required prior to the disbursement of any MDDA 
funds or the commencement of any tax increment sharing arrangement.

In evaluating redevelopment and catalyst projects, the MDDA will consider: 
• The project's contribution to economic vitality and district goals 
• The amount of private investment being leveraged 
• The estimated tax increment generation 
• The project's impact in the following areas:

Strategic Property Acquisition 
The MDDA may acquire, hold, convey, lease, or otherwise participate in strategic property activities when doing so 
advances the goals of the Plan of Development and provides a clear public benefit. These activities may be 
undertaken to support redevelopment, improve public access, facilitate infrastructure improvements, address 
site constraints, or create opportunities for future investment within the district. 
Eligible activities may include, but are not limited to: 
• Acquisition of land or property for future redevelopment opportunities 
• Land banking to preserve strategic sites for future public or private investment 
• Acquisition of easements necessary to improve public access, including access to the Eagle River, trails, 
public spaces, and community amenities 
• Easements or property interests needed to facilitate utility improvements, transportation projects, stormwater 
management, or other public infrastructure investments 
• Acquisition of small parcels or property interests that improve site functionality, redevelopment potential, or 
economic development outcomes 
• Property assembly activities that combine multiple parcels to facilitate redevelopment projects or address 
barriers to investment 
• Strategic acquisitions that support housing, mixed-use development, public amenities, historic preservation, 
or other priorities identified in the Plan of Development 
The MDDA shall generally pursue strategic property activities only when a clear public purpose exists and when 
acquisition, land banking, or property assembly is expected to facilitate a future public improvement, 
redevelopment opportunity, infrastructure project, or other community benefit that would be difficult to achieve 
through private action alone. 
Prior to acquiring real property or property interests, the MDDA may require appraisals, environmental reviews, 
title investigations, development feasibility assessments, or other due diligence deemed appropriate by the Board.

The MDDA may also partner with the Town of Minturn, property owners, developers, public agencies, nonprofit 
organizations, or other entities to accomplish strategic property objectives. In evaluating potential acquisitions, 
the MDDA shall consider both the strategic importance of the property and the anticipated costs of ownership and 
maintenance. Relevant factors may include acquisition costs, insurance, property taxes (if applicable), security, 
environmental obligations, maintenance requirements, opportunity costs, and the expected duration of 
ownership. 
The MDDA should generally seek to avoid acquiring property without a reasonable strategy for future use, 
disposition, redevelopment, or public benefit. Long-term holding costs and financial risks shall be weighed against 
the anticipated economic, community, and redevelopment benefits of the acquisition. 
The MDDA Board retains sole discretion regarding strategic property acquisitions and shall consider factors 
including public benefit, consistency with the Plan of Development, financial feasibility, long-term maintenance 
obligations, redevelopment potential, and the anticipated return on public investment.
