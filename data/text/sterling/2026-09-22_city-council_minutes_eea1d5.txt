STERLING CITY COUNCIL RULES OF PROCEDURE 
1. Rules of Procedure - Generally  
1.1  Authority : The Charter of the City of Sterling provides that the Council shall 
determine its own rules of procedure. 
1.2  Adoption of Rules : These Rules of Procedure shall be in effect upon their 
adoption by the Council and shall remain in effect until amended as required in 
Sec. 1.5.  
1.3  Review of Rules: The City Manager shall arrange for and coordinate the 
orientation of all newly elected officials, including a review of these Rules, within 
two (2) months after the election.   
1.4  Rules of Order : These Rules shall govern the proceedings of Council. Where 
these Rules are silent, the current published version of “Bob’s Rules of Order" 
shall govern. 
1.5  Amendment : These Rules may be amended by the affirmative vote of four 
Council Members.  
1.6  Suspension : Unless prohibited by Charter or Municipal Code, any Rule may be 
temporarily suspended at any Meeting by a majority vote of all Council Members 
participating in the Meeting.  If a Rule is suspended, such suspension only 
applies to the Meeting in which the suspension took place.   
2. Meetings and Quorum  
2.1 Quorum : Four members of the Council shall constitute a quorum for the 
transaction of business.  If a quorum is not present, those in attendance shall be 
recorded and they shall adjourn until a later time.   
2.2  Meetings to be Public : All regular, special, and study session meetings shall be 
open to the public as required by the Colorado Open Meetings Law. 
2.3  Holidays : The Council shall hold regular meetings twice each month on the 
second and fourth Tuesday of each month at 5:30 p.m. When a regular 
meeting date falls on a holiday or a day of special observance that is officially 
recognized by the City, the regular meeting shall be canceled and the City 
Council may, at the Council's discretion, reschedule the meeting for 
another date and time. 
 
3. Types of Meetings  
3 
 
3.1 Regular Business Meetings : Forums for public hearings, formal Council action 
(ordinances, resolutions, or motions), and an agenda item for general public 
comment. 
3.2  Study Sessions: Conducted for the presentation and discussion of information 
in a deeper dialogue format. No formal action shall occur, and public comments 
are typically not taken unless otherwise speciﬁed in the agenda in advance or 
permitted by the Presiding Officer.  Council may offer and accept reports, direct 
City administrative staff, and provide general guidance concerning public 
business under discussion. A quorum of Council is not required to hold a study 
session.  
3.3  Executive Sessions : Conﬁdential sessions convened for speciﬁc topics as 
authorized by the Colorado Open Meetings Law, such as property negotiations, 
legal advice, or personnel matters. 
3.4  Special Meetings :  A special meeting is any meeting that is called by the mayor 
or the majority of Council Members in accordance Sec. 2-32, Article II, Chapter 2 
of the Sterling City Code.   
3.5  Emergency Meetings :   Emergency meetings are unscheduled meetings that 
are necessitated by national, statewide, or local catastrophe, riot or 
insurrection, requiring immediate action by Council to protect the public health, 
safety, or welfare.  
a.  An emergency meeting may be called by the Mayor, the City Manager, or 
by any three (3) members of the City Council.  
b. The City Clerk shall make a good faith effort to provide notice of an 
emergency meeting to the public and all Council members.  
c. A quorum shall consist of a majority of the Council members then in 
office.  
d. In the event of a catastrophe that prevents physical gathering, members 
may participate and vote via telephonic or video conference.   
e. Any action taken at an emergency meeting shall be by a two-thirds (2/3) 
vote of the members present.   
f. Any ordinance or formal action adopted at an emergency meeting shall 
be placed on the agenda of the next Regular City Council Meeting or a 
sooner Special Meeting for public affirmation or ratiﬁcation to ensure a 
permanent record of the proceedings. Voting for ratiﬁcation or affirmation 
of emergency action shall proceed as outlined in Section 9.2 below.   
g. If such emergency action is not affirmed or ratiﬁed at such subsequent 
meeting, then the action shall be deemed rescinded.   
4 
 
4. Mayor and Presiding Officer  
4.1 Presiding Officer : The Mayor, or in his/her absence the Mayor Pro Tem, shall 
serve as the presiding officer/Chair at all meetings. 
4.2  Temporary Chair : If both the Mayor and Mayor Pro Tem are absent, the City 
Clerk shall call the meeting to order, and the Council shall elect a Temporary 
Chair by majority vote of Council Members present.   
4.3  Mayor Pro Tem Selection:  Following each general election, the newly sworn 
Council shall elect a Mayor Pro Tem to serve until a successor is chosen. The 
procedure shall be as follows: 
a.   The chair will announce that the ﬂoor is open for nominations for the                        
position of Mayor Pro Tem. 
b.   Nominations will be taken from City Council Members by voice.   
c.    Each nominee will have the opportunity to address the Council. 
d.  All members present will then vote by secret ballot, collected by the City 
Clerk, on one of the named nominees.   
e. Council Members may nominate themselves and vote for themselves if 
nominated.   
f. Any nominee that receives a majority vote of Council Members present 
will be elected Mayor Pro Tem.  
5. Attendance  
5.1 Expectations : All Council Members are expected to attend all regular meetings, 
study sessions, executive sessions, and special meetings.      
5.2  Absences : In the event any Council Member is unable to attend any meeting of 
the City Council, such member shall advise the Mayor, City Manager, or City 
Clerk 7 days in advance of the meeting or, in emergency circumstances, as soon 
as practicable.   
5.3  Electronic Participation:   Electronic participation shall not be allowed in 
quasi-judicial hearings or executive sessions.  A member of City Council may 
participate in regular, special meetings, and study sessions (with prior 
arrangements made with the City Clerk), by telephone or other electronic means 
of participation, such as video-conferencing under one of the following 
circumstances:   
a.  With prior consensus of Council or 
b.  In an emergency situation, with a majority vote of Council Members 
present.   
6. Order of Business and Agenda  
5 
 
6.1 General  Order  of Business .  The general order of business is as follows, with 
changes made as necessary to conduct business:   
a. Meeting Called to Order 
b. Invocation  
c. Pledge of Allegiance  
d. Roll Call 
e. Agenda Changes  
f. Consent Agenda  
g. Public Comment  
h. Unﬁnished Business  
i. New Business  
j. Council Updates and Communications 
k. Adjournment 
 
6.2  Agenda Preparation : Items are placed on the agenda by the City Manager, in 
consultation with the Mayor and City Attorney.  Council Members with items for 
future agendas should contact the City Manager and request the agenda item be 
added.   
7. Consent Agenda  
7.1 Deﬁned : Routine items (more or less) normally passed with little or no 
controversy, including approval of minutes of previous meetings.   
7.2 Removal: During approval of the Consent Agenda or prior to approval of the 
Consent Agenda, a Council Member may make a motion requesting that one or 
more items be removed from the Consent Agenda and added to the Unﬁnished or 
New Business Agenda. Any motion to remove Consent Agenda items must be 
seconded and voted upon before an item is removed from the Consent Agenda. 
Items moved from the Consent Agenda will be considered in order following the 
remaining Business items. 
8. Public  Comment  
8.1 Opportunity  to Speak : Residents have a reasonable opportunity to be heard at 
each Regular Business Meeting.  Public Comment is a time set aside for citizens 
to address the Council on City business that is not scheduled for a separate 
public hearing or public comment later on the agenda.  If an item is listed for a 
separate public hearing or open to comment later in the meeting, speakers must 
wait until that speciﬁc agenda item is called.   
8.2 Time Limits : To accommodate all people wishing to speak, speakers are 
requested to sign up and limit comments to no more than three (3) minutes per 
6 
 
individual.  The Public Comment portion of the agenda will be held to an overall 
time limit of 15 minutes.  Speakers may only speak once and may not cede time 
to other commenters.  When the time expires, the speaker may be permitted to 
complete a sentence but should make no further remarks.  At the discretion of 
the Presiding Officer, these timeframes may be extended, and Public Comment 
may be closed and reopened at a later time.   
8.3 Topics: During Public Comment, individuals may comment regarding any City 
business  not  on Council’s agenda for separate public hearing or public 
comment.  Comments speciﬁc to agenda items scheduled for public hearings 
should be reserved and delivered during the public hearing to ensure 
compliance with due process.   During the Public Comment segments of Council 
meetings and to ensure due process protections, the Presiding Officer has the 
authority and discretion to direct a speaker to terminate all remarks on 
upcoming matters where the Council may hold a quasi-judicial hearing. A 
speaker prohibited from making a statement on such a matter during a Public 
Comment period may speak at the public hearing during the quasi-judicial 
proceedings on that matter. 
8.4 Decorum : All remarks from the public must be directed to the Presiding Officer.  
Courtesy, civility, and respect for others are expected.  Comments directed at 
persons other than the Presiding Officer and interruptions  of other commentors 
or speakers are not allowed.   
9. Ordinances, Resolutions, and Motions  
9.1  Voting : Voting ultimately decides all questions and items before Council. 
Council Members may not abstain from voting except as outlined in 9.3.   
a. Simple Majority: Except for Motions to Appoint Council Vacancies 
outlined in subsection (c) below, all motions and resolutions require a 
simple majority to pass.  A simple majority means the majority of 
Council Members present if a quorum exists.   
b. Ordinance Voting:  Passage of an ordinance requires the majority vote 
of all seven (7) Council Members for passage.   
c. Council Vacancies: All Council vacancies must be ﬁlled by 
appointment of Council as required in Sec. 2-4, Article 2, of the 
Sterling City Charter.  Any motion to appoint an individual for a 
Council vacancy requires four (4) affirmative votes for passage. 
d. Tie Votes: In the case of a tie vote, the motion fails. 
9.2 Quasi -Judicial Voting:   All quasi-judicial decisions shall be done through roll 
call voting with a basis for any approval or denial articulated on the record.  The 
7 
 
Council alternatively may render a decision on a quasi-judicial matter by written 
resolution.  
 
9.3  Methods of Voting:   City Council may use any of the following methods of 
voting:   
a. Electronic Voting: Using electronic controls located at each Council 
Member’s seats, with a display located in Council Chambers.  The 
Mayor shall ask members to cast their vote, then notify them that if 
they wish to change their vote they should do so now, and ﬁnally ask to 
display the votes on the electronic display.   
b. Roll Call:  The Clerk shall call the roll of the members and each 
member present votes “yes” or “no” as the member’s name is called.   
c. Voice Vote:  The Presiding Officer asks for all those in favor to say “YES” 
and all opposed to say “NO” .  The Presiding Officer then rules on 
whether the “yeses” or “noes” predominate the question posed.   
  
9.4  Abstaining:  All Council Members must vote unless one of the following 
circumstances exists:   
a.  They have a conﬂict of interest as described in the Code of Ethics 
found in Chapter 2, Article VIII of the City Code; 
b. The issue on which a vote is to occur involves a question concerning   
the Council Member’s own conduct. (This does not include a Member 
nominating or voting for themselves in Mayor Pro Tem nominations); or 
c.  The Council Member’s impartiality might be reasonably questioned, in    
the case of a quasi-judicial matter, including but not limited to a 
personal bias, a close relationship, or a prejudice concerning facts in 
question.   
9.5 Motions : Each ordinance or resolution must be introduced and moved for 
adoption by name and number. (“I move for the introduction and adoption of 
Ordinance/Resolution No._____, Series of _____”).   
9.6  Voting by Proxy : Voting by proxy is not permitted.   
9.7  Ordinances : Each proposed ordinance shall be read by title or in full two times.  
When an Ordinance is amended after the ﬁrst reading, it shall be published as 
amended and shall be read on second reading by title and considered as 
amended at the next meeting scheduled for that purpose.   
8 
 
9.8  Intervention: The  Mayor may intervene in Council debate in order to determine 
whether the Council wishes to postpone the Council action if more information 
or staff work appears to be warranted to facilitate a Council decision.  
10. Council  Member Duties  
10.1 Review of Packet:  If, upon review of the packet, a Council  Member has a 
question for City staff, the City Manager, or the City Attorney that can be 
addressed prior to the Meeting, the Council Member is encouraged to contact 
the City Manager or City Attorney in a reasonable time prior to the Meeting so 
that they may prepare a response or be prepared to respond.   
10.2  Conﬂict of Interest : A Council Member shall recuse themselves from 
consideration of any Agenda items concerning which such member has a 
substantial interest in the transaction before Council (See Article VIII of 
Chapter 2 of the City Code).   If the Council Member believes they may have a 
conﬂict of interest, such member should confer with the City Attorney prior to 
the meeting, whenever possible.  In circumstances where recusal is not 
required but which may give risk to an appearance of impropriety, council 
members are encouraged to announced their relationship to the transaction for 
the purposes of transparency and public trust.   
  
11. Courtesy Requirements : As a courtesy to all who use the City Council Chamber, the 
City Council shall require that everyone:  
a.  Remove hats, caps and sunglasses;  
b.  Refrain from chewing gum or tobacco, eating or drinking;  
  c.  Turn off or silence all cell phones and pagers;  
 d . Refrain from interrupting others while speaking.

Subject: Land Use Planning Grant Incentive Funds 
 
BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE 
The City of Sterling participates in the Proposition 123 initiative, which provides incentives to 
communities that take steps to support affordable housing. As a result of the City's expedited 
review of qualifying low-income housing projects, Sterling is eligible to receive $50,000 in Local 
Planning Capacity (LPC) incentive funds. 
The funds have restrictions on their eligible uses and do not require a local match. The City is 
required to complete the LPC grant application process to receive the funds, but the $50,000 
incentive allocation is guaranteed based on the City's participation in Proposition 123. 
Staff proposes using the $50,000 in LPC funds as part of a joint effort with Logan County. The 
City and County will each contribute $50,000 in LPC funds toward hiring a consultant to update 
their respective comprehensive plans. 
FINANCIAL IMPACT 
The City will receive and utilize $50,000 in LPC incentive funds with no required local match. 
RECOMMENDATION 
Staff recommends that City Council approve the resolution accepting the $50,000 in LPC 
incentive funds and authorize participation in the joint comprehensive planning effort with 
Logan County.

Subject: Approval of Citizen Advisory Board Project Request - Website Update 
 
 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
 
The City’s current website is limited and the agreement with the current vendor goes into 2027. The 
vendor has indicated that we can update the website but wants a five-year agreement. Due to not 
wanting a long-term agreement and concerns with technical support, staff have been researching other 
vendors. Switching vendors will require that we maintain our existing website as we develop the new 
website, which will result in additional one-time costs for implementation.  
 
Since the website is important to promote tourism along with providing information to the public, the 
Citizens Advisory Board was approached to fund the one-time additional cost for the switch. At their 
meeting on September 17, 2026, they approved the request for an amount of up to $10,000 and 
recommended that it be moved forward for City Council approval. The funding would be from the Hotel 
Tax Fund, which is separate from the General Fund, and has adequate funds to cover the cost. Annual 
hosting costs will continue to come out of the General Fund. 
 
 
RECOMMENDATION 
 
Staff recommends City Council approve the Citizens Advisory Board recommendation to provide 
$10,000 for the City’s website update from the Hotel Tax Fund.
