Building Official Report: 
Building Official McAllister provided the council with a copy of the open building permits report, 
noting 31 active building permits. Four of those being new home construction, eight being 
remodels or additions and the remainder for various other permits. 
 
Charles also presented an example memo of an enforcement letter for consideration to 
construction workers who continue to park and impede traffic flow on roadways at their job 
sites. The intention of the letter is to give them a warning, as a first step, second step, issue a 
stop work order, and third step issue a citation with fines if they continue to impede the 
roadway where regular traffic and/or more importantly emergency vehicles cannot get 
through. Fines could be taken from their performance bond as one way of collection. The 
council will take this into consideration.

Police Department Report:  
The Council received the July Safety and Security Report as part of the agenda packet. Chief 
Owens stated that future reports will be expanded to provide more detailed information on 
Police Department initiatives, activities, and goals. 
 
Chief Owens also addressed concerns raised by Charles regarding construction vehicles 
obstructing roadways, noting that the Standard Traffic Ordinance contains provisions that allow 
officers to cite violations involving the obstruction of emergency vehicles. Additionally, he 
reported plans to increase traffic enforcement along Holliday Drive and to work with Q Inc. to 
ensure speed limit signs are properly located within the appropriate designated areas.

Council Reports:  
 
Council President McCauley: 
Council President McCauley reported that the generator pad has been poured and the 
generator has been installed. The company is currently awaiting approval to connect the 
utilities to the generator, which will complete the project. 
 
Council President McCauley reported weather has delayed the installation of the curb wall on 
Terrace Trail West but should be completed soon, and Todd Towery, GM for Q Inc. has agreed 
to cost sharing the project with the city 50/50. The total cost of the project is $5000.00. 
  
Councilmember Pasley:  
Councilmember Pasley proposed using the unused portion of the police garage bay as an 
Amazon pickup and drop-off location to help reduce delivery truck traffic within the 
community. He expressed concern about delivery vehicles frequently traveling at excessive 
speeds and navigating curves unsafely, creating a potential safety hazard. 
After discussion, the Council concluded that the garage bay was likely not a suitable location for 
such a project. As an alternative, it was suggested that guard house staff provide delivery 
drivers and visitors with informational flyers reminding them of community rules, particularly 
the 20-mph speed limit and Lake Quivira’s commitment to safety. 
 
Councilmember Zimmer:  
Councilmember Zimmer reported on preparations for the dam inspection required by the State 
of Kansas, which must be completed by April 2027. As a Class C High Hazard Dam, inspections 
are required every three years. McClure Engineering conducted the most recent inspection in 
2024 and has provided a proposal of $4,800 for the 2027 inspection. The State of Kansas also 
offers inspection services, with a cost of $3,000 for a dam of this classification. The state's 
inspection requires completion of a survey, which Councilmember Zimmer volunteered to 
conduct on behalf of the city. He recommended that the city utilize the State of Kansas for the 
2027 inspection and notify McClure Engineering of its decision. 
 
Motion: Councilmember Vedock made a motion that the council award the Dam Inspection 
Contract to the State of Kansas in an amount not to exceed $3000.00 and authorize the mayor 
and/or City Administrator to execute all necessary contract documents, subject to legal review 
and approval. Second: Councilmember Kurland seconded the motion. Vote: 5-0. Motion 
carried. 
 
Councilmember Zimmer also raised the issue of weight limits on the dam roadway and whether 
additional restrictions should be considered. Discussion indicated that weight restrictions are 
already in place but may not be clearly posted, and improvements to signage should be 
evaluated.
