New Business: 
i. Consider the purchase of a 2024 Durango police vehicle through Kansas Highway 
Patrol in an amount not to exceed $45,000.  
The Council received information in the agenda packet regarding the Kansas Highway Patrol 
(KHP) pre-owed vehicle program. Chief Owens advised that the vehicle has low mileage and will 
be delivered largely equipped, though some additional items will need to be purchased to 
complete the outfitting. He further noted that the total cost of the vehicle and equipment is 
expected to remain within the requested budget of $45,000. This vehicle will replace the 2023 
Hybrid vehicle which has had several mechanical issues and needs replacement ahead of 
schedule. Chief Owens noted his intention of moving away from hybrids and getting the fleet 
back on a three-year replacement schedule. 
Page  3 | 6 
 
Motion: Councilmember Kurland made a motion that the council authorize the purchase of a 
2024 Durango police vehicle from the Kansas Highway Patrol according to the discussed 
specifications, in an amount not to exceed $45,000, payable from the Equipment Reserve Fund, 
and authorize the Chief of Police to execute all necessary documents to finalize such purchase. 
Second: Councilmember Vedock seconded. Vote: 4-0. Motion carried. 
ii. Consider a Professional Services Agreement with the League of Kansas 
Municipalities to assist in modifying the City’s Personnel Policy Manual for a cost 
not to exceed $3,500.00. 
City Administrator Bounds provided an executive summary outlining recommended updates to 
the personnel manual. As the manual has not undergone a comprehensive review since 2015, 
she recommends engaging the League of Kansas Municipalities to assist with updating policies 
and procedures, ensuring compliance with current laws and supporting the City's evolving 
workforce and operational needs. 
Motion: Councilmember Zimmer made a motion to approve a Professional Services Agreement, 
in an amount not to exceed $3,500.00, with the League of Kansas Municipalities, to assist the 
city in modifying and updating its city personnel policies. Second: Councilmember Kurland 
seconded. Vote: 4-0. Motion carried.

Police Department Report:  
The Council received the August Safety and Security Report in their agenda packet. Chief Owens 
reported that the Police Department experienced a substantial increase in enforcement activity 
during August 2026, recording 16 cases and 14 arrests. Officers issued 50 traffic citations with 
67 total violations, noting speeding accounted for 60% of all violations. Holliday Drive remained 
the primary enforcement corridor, representing nearly 70% of violations issued. Holliday Drive 
remained the primary enforcement area, accounting for 46 violations (69%) of all violations 
issued during the month.
