SUBJECT:   September Special Board of Trustees Meeting  
Consideration of a letter of support for the Dolores Ranger District 2027 Non-
Motorized Trail Grant, Montelores Bridge restoration (Pg. 2-3) 
The Dolores Ranger District has requested that the Town of Rico provide a letter of 
support for its application to the Colorado Parks and Wildlife (CPW) Non-Motorized Trail 
Grant Program. The grant proposal seeks approximately $80,000 for repairs to the 
Montelores Bridge, located across the Dolores River near the Montezuma and Dolores 
County line south of Rico on Highway 145. 
The bridge is more than 100 years old and requires replacement of decking and 
stringers. The bridge is an important component of the regional non-motorized trail 
network, providing a connection between the Rio Grande Southern River Trail and other 
regional trails and serving hikers, anglers, bicyclists, skiers, and equestrian users. 
The Forest Service has requested letters of support from local partners by September 
15, 2026, for inclusion in the grant application. The Town's support would be limited to 
endorsing the project and recognizing the benefits of the Montelores Bridge repairs to 
the regional non-motorized trail system and surrounding communities. As the local 
municipality and community most directly connected to the project, a letter of support 
from the Town may provide an important local perspective and demonstrate community 
support for the proposed improvements. 
Recommended Action: I move to approve the letter of support for the Dolores Ranger 
District 2027 Non-Motorized Trail Grant application.

RE: Letter of Support – 2027 Colorado Parks and Wildlife Non-Motorized Trail Grant 
 
Dear Ms. Schmieg: 
 
The Town of Rico is pleased to provide this letter in support of the Dolores Ranger 
District's application to the Colorado Parks and Wildlife Non-Motorized Trail Grant 
Program for funding to repair the Montelores Bridge. The proposed project seeks 
approximately $80,000 to replace the decking and stringers on the historic Montelores 
Bridge, which crosses the Dolores River near the Montezuma and Dolores County line 
south of Rico along Highway 145. More than 100 years old, the bridge is an important 
piece of the area's mining and logging history and serves as an important connection 
within the region's non-motorized trail network. 
 
The Montelores Bridge provides access for a diverse range of recreational users, 
including hikers, bicyclists, anglers, equestrians, skiers, and other trail users. The bridge 
also provides an important connection between the Rio Grande Southern River Trail and 
other regional trails, helping connect the Rico community and surrounding area to a 
broader network of recreational opportunities. 
 
The Town recognizes the importance of maintaining and improving regional trail 
infrastructure. These facilities provide recreational opportunities for residents and visitors 
while also supporting tourism, local businesses, and the outdoor recreation economy. 
Maintaining a safe, continuous, non-motorized connection along the Dolores River is 
particularly valuable to the Rico community and the surrounding region. 
As the local municipality serving the Rico community, the Town supports the proposed 
repairs to the Montelores Bridge and believes the project will provide meaningful benefits 
to residents, visitors, and trail users throughout the region. 
 
Thank you for considering this important project and for your continued efforts to 
maintain and improve the region's non-motorized trail system. 
 
Sincerely,
