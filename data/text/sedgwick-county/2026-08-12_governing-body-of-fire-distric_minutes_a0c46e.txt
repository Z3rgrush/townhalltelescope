NEW BUSINESS
Item B.
TEMPORARY CONTINUATION OF FIRE AID AGREEMENTS.
Chairman Blubaugh
said, “Good Afternoon, Chief.”
Mr. Douglas Williams
, Fire Chief, greeted the Commission and said, “We're bringing this here today so that we're going to give an extension on these aid agreements for the four communities in an effort to work through the getting the new mutual aid agreement signed. At the current time, we are still working through worrying and some discussion with the cities and that takes time legally. So we're asking, we're going to give that extension. I take this very serious because you know we brought this a year ago almost. We brought this to your attention through the Steering Council that we're having these safety issues. So as we give 30-day extension, I don't take that lightly. It is still having safety issues. So, that being said, we're willing to do that in order to get these agreements pushed through.”
Chairman Blubaugh
said, “Okay. Commissioners, do you have any questions or comments? Commissioner Baty.”
Commissioner Baty
said, “Yep. Hey thanks, Chief, for this. Just two quick points. Number one, the biggest factor here is we want to allow these cities to get through their budget process at the end of August, early September. That way, they have an idea of what they're doing, how they're doing it. That's part of this conversation, and this is coming at the request of many of these cities, saying, ‘Hey, if we just get some more time.’ On the flip side of that and because this is extending until September 23rd, so it gets them through their budget adoption process for 2027. On the flip side, there has been several conversations too, asking for more time.”
Commissioner Baty
continued, “Can we get a year? Can we get two years? I have not been supportive of that, and I will not support that. The main reason I won't support a longer time period than what we're doing here, is that the factor that is motivating these conversations is a safety factor. Yes, there is a taxing and equity conversation. That's, that certainly has merit, but the main factor that's motivating the sense of urgency, is we're acknowledging there is a safety situation and a safety risk to our firefighters, firefighters from volunteer and part-time departments, and also citizens. If we continue to push this out one year, two years, and beyond, that's not mitigating the safety concern. So, us allowing this sort of an extension is a nod that we want them to get through their budget processes, but we want process made. We want to continue progress. We want progress to continue being made, and we're actually aiming at something to modify these aid agreements or cancel the aid agreements outright. So, what this is a nod that we do want to help our cities. We want to be in those conversations. We're listening to what their needs are, but we also have a sense of urgency here that we cannot ignore and will not. I will not ignore the sense of urgency with these safety issues. So, I appreciate the accommodation. I know these conversations are ongoing, particularly with Valley Center in my district. Significant conversations. So, thank you for this recommended action, and I will fully support it.”
Chief Williams
said, “I appreciate what you're saying, and I think you're right. We can get more time to get these done. But one of the conversations that we're having with these communities, and I think it's gotten taken out of context, is NFPA (National Fire Protection Association) for a volunteer fire department calls for 10 responders in 10 minutes, and now that seems to be the target, and that target is being used. Well, if we get six people there and you bring five, then we have 10, and that's not the goal. The goal is that we're aiding you, not providing part of your service. You know, I think to touch on that, to understand for a fire in Park City, in Bel Aire, we're sending 18 firefighters minimum. With that being said, in the city of Wichita, Wichita's normal dispatch for a residential house fire is 25 personnel and then they also dispatch a rehab unit to boost that up to possibly 28 personnel. So, I think this conversation has went off track from, you know, we as a department think it's safe to operate with 18 people on an initial response to a residential home fire, and so I think the conversation has gotten caught up into well, we get 10, we're okay, and that's not okay. We're not wanting to go that route. So, I think that's one of the conversations that has gotten taken a little bit off track.”
Commissioner Baty
said, “Chief, I appreciate that. I think part of this has actually been an educational experience for a lot of people, where we've been able to really define what is or isn't appropriate, what is or isn't expected. I think this is very healthy in many regards. I think we're going to have a better product, a better fire response for all of Sedgwick County because of these conversations. Sometimes that takes bold action and takes a little bit of moral courage, and sometimes it takes forcing the issue so that we can ensure safety. We cannot compromise on safety.”
Commissioner Baty
continued, “But I had a great conversation yesterday with one of my constituents from Valley Center, and this was the conversation: is that in their neighboring cities that are fire district cities, Park City and Maize, Bel Aire in particular, these are fire district cities, Kechi as well. If there is a structure fire in these cities, they will get 18 to 19 firefighters minimum, and I think Valley Center deserves the same. You know, to settle that 10 on a structure fire is sufficient or okay, and that's the minimum threshold that should be hit, and that's just satisfactory. I don't think that's satisfactory for citizens in Valley Center. I don't think that citizens in Maize or Park City should have that big of a difference of a standard of what we do or don't allow for our citizens in our fire district cities. I want citizens of the Valley Center or have that same sort of threshold that there's an expectancy there that we won't compromise on. So, that's part of the conversation. It's been an educational opportunity and I think it's been helpful. I think what we get as a byproduct, a finished product of all of this is going to be a better fire response for everyone in Sedgwick County.”
Chief Williams
said, “Yeah. That's a very good point because one of the things that's taught real early in in Company Officer Training, that we give to Lieutenants and Captains across the nation is given to first level officers, is when you get on scene by the time you realize that you need three more, five more. then you need to call double because of the time they get there. You're going to need more help because the fire will extend. So you know that's getting the people there when you need them. There is very vital to what we do.”
Chairman Blubaugh
said, “Okay. Any other questions or comments? If not, I'd like to make a motion to recommend the approval of the temporary continuation of certain Fire Aid Agreements until September 23rd, 2026, and authorize the Board Chair to sign the continuation letters, and authorize the Board Chair to sign substantial similar continuation letters for any other municipalities as recommended by Fire District Number One Fire Chief.”
