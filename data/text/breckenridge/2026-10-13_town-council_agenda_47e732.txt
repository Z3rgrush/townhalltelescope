Metrics  Summary
GOALS AND OBJECTIVES
By 2030, Breckenridge aims to grow its housing inventory from
1,665 to 2,200 units, reduce unfilled jobs, and increase the share
of positions filled by local residents. At least 35% of homes
should remain resident-occupied, deed-restricted households
should reflect community diversity, and overall housing stability
will improve with fewer cost-burdened households. The Town
also aims to raise awareness of the Summit County Housing
Authority (SCHA), particularly among Spanish-speaking
households, increase use of the housing website, and ensure the
Housing Department is fully staffed.

General Fund #001 
The General Fund encompasses the Recreation Center, Fiber 
Network, Committees, Public Works, Community Development, 
Finance, Executive and Management office, Public Safety and 
Advice & Litigation.  The primary revenue into this fund is a 
transfer from the Excise Tax Fund. 
Restrictions exist on this fund for the outstanding loan repayment 
from the Breck Nordic Center, Employee Down Payment 
Assistance Program, and 4 months of operational expense.   
Reserves are held for TABOR (Taxpayer Bill of Rights) and net 
income for the Nicotine program.

Town Council  
Background 
The Town Council cost center includes all expenses related to the elected officials of the Town (Mayor and 
Town Council) from wages to meals, supplies to membership fees, conference travel to retreats, and any other 
costs associated with their positions.   
Expenditures Budget Highlights 
In 2027, the proposed expense budget for Town Council is $701,301. This amount represents a decrease of 
$59,326 (-8.5%) from the 2026 budget. This decrease is primarily driven by an anticipated reduction in grants to 
other agencies. 
Municipal Court  
Background 
The Municipal Court cost center is responsible for action on complaints filed with the court, including penal, 
traffic, animal, and other municipal code violations. The presiding judge works part-time to conduct hearings, 
arraignments, trials, and sentencing twice a month. This division includes one full-time employee, 10% of the 
Director of Municipal Services and Engagement’s salary, and all contract services of the Municipal Judge. 
Expenditures Budget Highlights 
In 2027, the proposed expense budget for Municipal Court is $196,354. This amount represents a decrease of 
$8,143 (-4%) from the 2026 budget. This decrease is primarily driven by personnel reorganization. The Deputy 
Town Attorney is also acting as a part-time prosecutor and the Municipal Judge has become a contractor. Court 
revenues in 2027 are expected to decrease by $9,300 (-10%) from 2026 budgeted amounts.  
Advice & Litigation  
Background 
The Advice & Litigation cost center is for the Town Attorney and all expenses incurred by that position, 
including, but not limited to, wages, health benefits, supplies, subscriptions, and legal fees from outside law 
firms. 
Expenditures Budget Highlights 
In 2027, the proposed expense budget for Advice & Litigation is $716,614. This amount represents a decrease 
of $6,850 (<1%) from the 2026 budget. This decrease is primarily driven by personnel reorganization, which has 
decreased legal services costs while increasing wages and benefits costs to a smaller degree.

The Town-owned commercial building at 130 Ski Hill Rd, known as the Breckenridge Professional Building 
(BPB), houses ten commercial tenants, the Breckenridge Tourism Office, The Town’s Housing Department, and 
the Town’s Communication and Community Engagement Division. At this time, the building is 100%

Facilities Division maintains the building.

penditures Budget Highlights

budget of $222,634 is an increase of 4.5% from 2026 budget. This increase is a result of broker fees for new

ministrative Management  
Background 
This cost center covers the Town Manager’s Office, including the Town Manager, Deputy Town Manager, 
Assistant Town Manager, and Executive Administration Assistant.  Wages, supplies, subscriptions, 
memberships, travel for conferences, meetings, etc. are all covered in this cost center.  
Ex
penditures Budget Highlights 
2027 changes to the Administrative Management cost center include: 
• Conversion of the Assistant Town Manager position to a Deputy Town Manager position to better
facilitate management of the Senior Leadership Team and the split of responsibilities within the Town
Manager’s office.
• Removal of the previously funded grant administrator position. Staff will continue to evaluate the need
for a grant administrator and will fill the position, if necessary, in the future.
• Addition of a Risk Manager position in the Town Manager’s office, which will manage the Town’s
comprehensive risk management program, oversee insurance coverage and claims, and support risk
mitigation strategies for the Town. This position has evolved from a currently vacant role (Liability
Coordinator), represents an increased personnel investment, and has been moved from Municipal
Services to Administrative Management to be overseen by the Deputy Town Manager.
• To effectively facilitate the ongoing conversion to a hybrid-shared delivery model for arts and culture,
staff recommend adding additional funding to the 2027 budget. Staff request funding up to $110,000 for

Summit Street Group (SSG) and Turner & Townsend Heery (TTH) consultants. These monies would

Human Resource Admin 0443 
Background 
The Human Resources Department plays a vital role in supporting the Town of Breckenridge’s most important  
Asset: its people. Our five-person team is dedicated to recruiting and retaining top talent, fostering a workplace  
culture with the Town’s Core Values and ensuring compliance with employment laws and policies.  
Human Resources provides essential services such as employee relations, recruitment, training and  
professional development, benefits, and compensation administration, performance management, and strategic  
workforce planning. As the organization continues to grow and adapt to new challenges, Human Resources  
serves as both a partner and a resource for all departments, helping the Town attract a strong workforce and  
create an environment where employees can develop and grow personally and professionally. Continued  
investment in Human Resources allows the Town to deliver on council’s priorities, sustain high levels of  
employee engagement and support the long-term success of the community we serve. 
 
Expenditures Budget Highlights 
The proposed 2027 Human Resources expense budget is $1,214,529. This represents an approximately flat  
budget request and a $6,859 decrease from the prior year.  
 
The modest changes to the budget are primarily attributable to increased personnel costs, which were partially  
offset by a reduction in the number of retirees accounted for within the Human Resources budget. Operating  
costs remain essentially flat.  
 
Nicotine Program 
Background 
In 2019, the Town participated in a countywide effort to impose a tax on all nicotine products. The tax was 
passed by voters in November 2019 and became effective on January 1, 2020. Tax revenues are required to be 
used for the protection and improvement of the public health and welfare of the community. Over the past six 
years, revenues have been used to fund prevention and cessation efforts, coordinated through Summit County 
Public Health, as well as funding for FIRC, Building Hope, and Elevated Community Health in the combined 
amount of $1,000,000.  
 
In 2027, the Town will continue funding cessation and prevention programming through Summit County Public 
Health, as well as contributing to the $1M for FIRC, Building Hope, and Elevated Community Health. The 
Towns and County are also considering how available funds may be prioritized and used in the future to assist 
with essential community needs at risk for reduced state and/or federal funding.  
 
Expenditures Budget Highlights 
Effective January 1, 2026, all jurisdictions except Summit County Government enacted a ban on flavored 
tobacco products. Staff budgeted for about a 30% decrease in revenue from 2025 to 2026. We have budgeted 
for flat revenue in 2027. Expenses remain in line with budget.

Background 
In 2027, the Breckenridge Police Department will continue to provide full-service law enforcement coverage 
24 hours a day, 365 days a year. The Department is staffed by 30 full-time employees, including 25 sworn 
police officers, and also manages code enforcement through our Community Service Officer program. 
The Department remains committed to a community policing philosophy focused on partnerships, problem 
solving, and working collaboratively with the community and other Town departments to address crime, 
public safety, and quality-of-life issues. 
The past year has been one of transition and modernization for the Police Department. With new leadership 
in place, much of 2026 has focused on evaluating operations, strengthening internal systems, implementing 
new technology, and developing our employees. As we move into 2027, continued investment in training, 
retention, supervision, leadership development, and succession planning will be important, particularly as 
many of our patrol officers are relatively early in their careers and tenure with the Town. 
Public Safety Budget Highlights 
The proposed 2027 budget is focused on maintaining core public safety services while continuing strategic 
investments in personnel, technology, and operational readiness. 
One of the Department's most significant projects in 2026 was the transition to the Axon public safety 
technology platform. This included new body-worn cameras, in-car video, digital evidence management, 
automated license plate recognition, and related evidence-sharing technology. FY2027 will be the first full 
budget year following implementation and includes the recurring costs associated with maintaining these 
systems. 
Just one example of the several operational benefits of the Axon platform is its body-worn camera 
translation capability, which supports communication in up to 63 languages. Within hours of deploying the 
technology, one of our officers used this feature while investigating a traffic accident involving a language 
barrier, allowing the officer to communicate directly with those involved without relying on our contracted 
language line service. This capability helps reduce communication barriers and provides another tool for 
delivering effective and timely service to our residents and visitors. 
Another significant issue affecting future public safety costs is the ongoing regional evaluation of Computer-
Aided Dispatch and Records Management System (CAD/RMS) technology. The Department continues to 
participate in this process with an emphasis on identifying a solution that meets our operational needs, is 
financially sustainable, and provides value to Breckenridge and our regional public safety partners.

and develop our employees, responsibly leverage technology to improve public safety,  and ensure our

Blue River Pathways priorities for 2027.

budget. This decrease is primarily driven by the reduced need for an outside consultant’s assistance with the 
Town’s Comprehensive Plan update since most of the work related to that effort will be completed in 2026.

fees were collected in 2026 for every developable property under the BGV Development Agreement other than 
Parcel 5. While this represents an economic win for the Building Division this year, it also means increased

Background 
The Public Works budget includes the following Divisions: Administration, Streets, Parks, and Engineering.  
These four divisions of Public Works are comprised of 33.38 FTEs of full-time staff and 6.73 FTEs of seasonal 
staff. The Public Works Department is tasked to maintain, protect, and ensure the future of the Town’s 
infrastructure while educating the public on available services as well as supporting a wide variety of special 
projects and events. 
The Administration Division provides oversight, strategic planning, budget control, and staff support to all Public 
Works Divisions. 
The Streets and Parks Divisions’ priorities include snow and ice control on roadways and sidewalks; roadway 
repair and maintenance; streetlight, signage, and safety feature maintenance; park repair and maintenance; and 
Town beautification including flowers and holiday lighting. 
The Engineering Division priorities include ensuring all Town infrastructure is designed and built to our 
standards, which protects the safety of the community. The Engineering Division oversees the Capital 
Improvement Projects for the Town, design review of all developments, construction inspection services, and 
GIS mapping.  
Public Works Department Budget Highlights 
The proposed 2027 revenue budget is $745,973, with most line items remaining fairly flat compared to 
2026. 
The proposed 2027 expense budget is $8,564,239, which represents an increase of $422,412 (5%) from 
the 2026 budget. This increase is primarily driven by wage and health insurance cost increases ($205,263 
increase from 2026) and the addition of AssetWorks software as an IT cost allocation ($214,365 increase 
from 2026).  In the Streets division we have budgeted a $27k increase for roadway/parking lot painting and 
striping.  In the 2026 season we were strategic with the areas that were painted and would like to complete 
a slightly larger refresh in 2027.  We also have budgeted $20K for contracted event support to assist with 
event security and road closures for events such as Ullr Fest, Rockstar Energy event, or any additional 
events.

Background 
 
The Recreation Department consists of 31 full-time and 231 part-time employees. It operates through six 
general fund cost centers and manages five facilities: Gold Run Nordic Center, Stephen C. West Ice Arena, 
Tennis Center, Recreation Center, and Carter Park. 
 
Our priorities are to deliver excellent customer service, offer dynamic programs for all ages, plan professional 
events, maintain top-tier facilities, and promote active, healthy living. We also focus on expanding activities for 
the many visitors who use our facilities each year. 
Annually, the department serves over 400,000 residents and guests. 
 
Recreation Budget Highlights 
 
The proposed 2027 revenue budget is $5,235,896 a 2% increase over 2026 projections and 1% above the 
2026 budget. Expenses are budgeted at $8,085,692, maintaining a minimal increase of less than 2% from 2026 
figures. Cost recovery (our revenue for each dollar spent) is projected at 65%, matching last year's rate and 
historical trends. 
 
The Programs Division maintains a significant subsidy for all programs. We continue to respond to rising 
scholarship demand and strive for cost to never be a barrier to our facilities or any of our programs for all local 
community members. This commitment is further reinforced by employee discount benefits, which supports 
recruitment, retention, and wellness while helping TOB workforce families access affordable recreation 
opportunities throughout our department. Projected cost recovery this year for the Programs Division is 51%.  
 
The Programs Division serves over 25,000 participants annually, including more than 12,000 children in youth 
programs. Outdoor programs reach 2,000 people, providing activities like mountain biking, climbing, fly fishing, 
and environmental education. Adult sports leagues remain popular and highly engaged. RaceBreck events 
consistently draw more than 3,000 athletes each year, serving as a major attraction for both residents and 
visitors. The weekly RaceBreck series demonstrates sustained growth, with an average participation of 100 
mountain bikers and 75 runners per event. 
 
The Tennis Center continues to grow, with steady summer camp enrollment (140 children) and rising 
participation in lessons. Cost recovery is projected at 91%, up 25% in the last two years. 
 
The Recreation Center has adopted new software for membership and registration, enhancing user experience 
and operational efficiency. This is expected to reduce bank charges and improve cost recovery, projected at 
85% this year. 
 
Over the past few years, financial performance has greatly improved at the Ice Arena, with projected cost 
recovery for 2026 reaching 83%. This positive shift is due to opportunistic programming, effective facility 
management, and greater community involvement in all ice programs.

Background 
The Other Miscellaneous Fund is comprised of three cost centers:   
General Revenue 1111 
Committees 4000 
Fiber 9600 Network 
These three programs exist within the General Fund but are not assigned to a specific department. 
Other Miscellaneous Department Budget Highlights 
General Revenue 
Background 
The General Revenue cost center allows Town Council to fund specific projects on an as-needed basis. The 
revenue for General Revenue includes the transfer from the Excise Tax Fund to support General Fund 
expenses, property taxes from the Summit County assessed valuation, investment income, and 
Breckenridge Nordic Center debt interest. Expenditures for General Revenue are charges assessed by the 
Summit County Treasurer’s Office for providing administrative services for property tax collections. 
Budget Highlights 
Major drivers of revenue increases in 2027 compared to budget 2026 are a $3,000,000 (21%) increase in 
transfers from the Excise Fund and a $151,600 (41%) increase in budgeted investment income. Transfers from 
the Excise Fund are increasing next year to maintain an appropriate fund balance across the entire General 
Fund.  
Internal Committees 
Background 
Internal Town Committees are designed to provide guidance and training to all Town employees to ensure 
thoughtful DEI implementation, integrate a stewardship focus, promote a safety-first culture, enhance leadership 
skills, promote employee wellness, support Town employees’ retirement planning, and to celebrate employees. 
Specific Internal Committees include DEI, Green Team, Safety, Leadership Development Team, Wellness, 
Retirement, and Employee Engagement. Each Internal Committee includes one Town Senior Leader and a 
representative from all Town departments. 
Internal Committee Budget Highlights 
The 2027 budget includes $128,500 in budgeted expenses for internal Town committees, representing a 
modest decrease from budgeted 2026 expenses. These expenses cover annual meetings, events, and 
programs to support Town employees. 
Fiber 9600 
Background 
The Fiber 9600 project’s goals are to provide high-speed, reliable, and affordable internet services to residents, 
businesses, and visitors. The goals of the Town-owned fiber network include stimulating competition in the 
market and providing residents with more reliable internet connections that would position Breckenridge for the

alpine environment. We provide leadership and encourage community involvement.

increase in expected revenue stems from the Allo fiber network lease changes and a cost-sharing 
agreement on the Verizon dark fiber lease that Allo recently signed. The proposed 2027 expense budget is

Subject: 2027 Utility Fund/Water Division Budget Summary 
____________________________________________________________________________________ 
Background 
The Utility Fund/Water Division is comprised of eleven full-time employees and two seasonal hydrant 
technicians. The Utility Fund/Water Division also pays 20% of the Director of Public Works’ salary and 80% of 
the Revenue Coordinator’s salary (water billing).  Priorities include operating two drinking water treatment 
plants, one Industrial Wastewater Plant (Wellington Oro - funded by Open Space except for personnel), and 
nine water pumping stations; as well as maintaining over 1,000 fire hydrants, six water storage tanks, and 
thousands of various underground valves. Additional priorities include maintaining a backflow prevention 
program, storage tank inspection program, utility line locates, meter reading, construction inspections, and 
collecting hundreds of water samples to meet CDPHE regulations.  
The Water Division’s goal is to deliver high-quality water for residential and commercial needs, as well as 
provide adequate fire flow. The Water Division adheres to Regulation 11: Colorado Primary Drinking Water 
Regulations.   
Water Division Budget Highlights 
2027 Revenue is forecasted to be $11,734,612, which is a $324,803 (2.3%) decrease from the 2026 
budget. This decrease is primarily due to the projected decrease of Plant Investment Fees with less large 
projects on the horizon.  
The proposed 2027 operations budget is $4,289,810, which is a $302,696 (6.6%) decrease from the 2026 
budget. This decrease is due the Water Master Plan update expenses being paid in 2026, and less of 
repairs and maintenance contingency repair budget being forecasted in 2027 (a higher amount was 
forecasted in 2026 due to the meter replacement project and full system leak survey identifying more leaks 
to repair). Operational expenses include salaries, chemicals, laboratory services, utilities, and repair and 
maintenance expenses. Additional operational expenses include master planning to determine water 
needed for future build-out. This planning effort is a required element in Community Development’s 
Comprehensive Plan. The last master planning effort was undertaken over 10 years ago. Following the 
completion of a water master plan will be a drought contingency plan to proactively prepare for potential 
water shortages.  
The proposed 2027 Capital Projects budget is $22,988,800, which is a $11,497,000 (100%) increase from 
the 2026 budget. 2027 Capital Projects include $17M for phase one of the Gary Roberts Water Treatment 
Plant rehabilitation; $500k for engineering costs, $5.4M for phase 2 of the Silver Queen Water Main 
project, and $60k cost share for Clinton Reservoir enlargement. 
The remaining expenses include $148,680 for water rights management which include legal fees, 
engineering fees, water storage contract fees, and Operations & Maintenance cost share. This amount is a 
decrease of $160,396 (51.9%) from 2026 due to the contribution to the Shoshone water rights acquisition 
being paid in 2026. The water utility will also conduct a water rate study ($75K) to evaluate the Town’s 
water rates for future budgets and utility needs.  The loan interest payment for the North water treatment 
plant is $950,160 in 2027.

Capital Fund #003 
The Capital Fund supports the capital improvement projects of 
the Town, which are generally managed by the Engineering 
Department.  Revenue transfers from Recreation, Open Space, 
Parking & Transportation, Conservation Trust and Sustainability 
support their respective projects.  The largest transfer of revenue 
is received from the Excise Tax Fund to support Public Works 
projects.

Background 
The Capital Fund budgets projects as determined by the annual Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) approved by 
the Town Council. The CIP includes new projects, roadway maintenance, and infrastructure replacement. 
Funds are transferred from Parking and Transportation, Sustainability, and Open Space and are expensed to 
the Capital Fund with Excise Tax funding the remaining projects. 
Most projects in the categories of Recreation, Public Works, and Parking and Transportation are managed by 
the Engineering Division staff including the Senior Engineer, Senior Project Manager, and the Town Engineer. 
Open Space and Sustainability projects and budgets are typically managed by staff from those respective 
divisions. 
CIP Budget Highlights 
As presented and discussed at the September 8th Town Council meeting, the following capital projects are 
prioritized for 2027: 
 Little Daisy Open Space Amenities
 Airport Road Improvement Design
 Blue River Flow Analysis
 Roadway resurfacing
 Public Works Master Plan & Bus Barn Design/
Construction
 Blue River Pathways projects
The proposed 2027 expense budget for the capital fund is $5,575,954. This includes $1,182,667 for new 
projects, $4,375,000 for ongoing projects previously approved, and the remaining amount is attributed to 
broker and investment fees. This budget is a decrease of $7,959,728 (59%) from the 2026 budget due to 
the projects selected for the 2027 CIP.

2027 BTO Business Plan/Budget Request      
             
Current Trends 
Domestic US travel has been strong so far this summer.   Breckenridge is holding at 
slightly over 2025 summer/fall room nights.     This is encouraging given the business 
challenges resulting from last  summer and winter’s downturn.     It is still early to measure 
our out-of-state business, but we expect that it will be back to  normal levels of  50% of 
total summer/fall room nights.   We expect that overall room nights will fall short of budget 
at 162,000  but stay ahead of 2025 summer/fall actuals.  
2026 Destination Marketing  
BTO has made key marketing changes to rebuild 2026 summer business.    A new, highly 
sophisticated media company has been contracted with promising media results so far 
this summer.    We have  contracted for updated cohort ( audiences) segmentation, and 
national research to assess  why potential  customers may be choosing alternative 
destinations.    We have  listened to input from last summer’s “Meeting of the Minds” 
sessions and, along the ToB and VR, are adding new events to re -energize the offerings.     
We are building the culinary brand for Breckenridge and fully participating in the Michelin 
possibilities.     We are hosting Global at the Mine in October.   This will  extend our event 
calendar and corresponding business and will offer media opportunities with the 
Michelin-recognized talent who will be participating.   G lobal also has a local extension, 
Global on Ridge , utilizing the same stellar culinary talent in an event geared for locals  
including a ticketed culinary component and a free concert. 
BTO has  expanded its inﬂuencer and social media footprint signiﬁcantly.   We are seeing 
solid results that will beneﬁt future branding.    Our 2026 media hosting schedule is strong 
and highlighted by national coverage with a recent satellite media tour.    We should see 
signiﬁcant media attention with  the Governor’s Conference on Tourism in Breckenridge 
this September. 
2027 Destination Marketing 
Target.    BTO will continue to focus on out-of-state overnight guests arriving May-October.  
The updated target audiences are similar, yet more reﬁned , allowing for more speciﬁc 
targeting in 2027.   The income range is extended as well.  Media is BTO’s single largest 
expense.   Our overall business goal for summer/fall 2027 is for lodging, retail, restaurant 
to deliver at least 40% of overall ToB tax collections, consistent with DMP goals.

Our 2027 room night goal  is 144,900 (4.5% over 2025 actual).   Media spends will be front 
loaded, yet ﬂexible,  in keeping with current booking trends.    We will extend 2026 
strategies to enhance PR and social media initiatives in-state .     We will maintain 
intercept research work in the winter to measure guest service consistently, and to better 
understand our guests’ winter/summer cross -over visitation.  We are ensuring that our 
website is AI ready. 
Community Engagement/Service Initiatives   
Breckenridge Welcoming  and NPS  scores remain very high year -round  We are 
highlighting local inclusion in all of our events and creating new events speciﬁcally for the 
local residents, per Resident Sentiment input.  
The DMMO Download continues as  a very trusted and valuable resource for the 
community.  Open rates consistently beat industry standards.  Overall recognition of our 
BLikeBreck campaign  and App is growing.    
BTO Annual Meeting, Winter &  Summer Previews provide valuable content and attract 
over one hundred participants per session.   Sharing local business and industry data at 
these meetings, in the DMMO Download, Lodging Roundtable, and other meetings serve 
as our community communications cornerstone. 
 
2027 Request 
Our 2027 funding request is $5,796,237 which is 1% less than 2026.    One-time expenses 
for the Governor’s Conference and moving our Pride event to BCA result in savings which 
will cover our expected inﬂationary increases.   Funding will be slightly reallocated among 
departments to put more emphasis on destination-driving initiatives. 
The BTO BOD of Directors and management team remain deeply committed to creating 
the best desired outcomes for our residents, business community, and visitors.  We take 
the trust and conﬁdence that the Town and community have shown for our work very 
seriously.   We strive to be the best and most responsible stewards of community 
resources and the Breckenridge brand.   Thank you for your consideration and support.
