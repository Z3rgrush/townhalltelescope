ummary 
In May 2025, Town Council established six targeted priorities during the coming one-to-three-year timeframe, 
including: Regulation Enforcement and Code Compliance, Communications/Community Engagement, Workforce 
Housing, Childcare, E-Delivery, and Blue River Pathways. This review and Q3 2026 update highlights progress 
made on the Council Priorities since the last discussion on March 10, 2026. This update also offers Council the 
opportunity to evaluate, confirm, or revise the Town Council priorities. 
B
ackground  
At its 2025 spring retreat, Town Council identified six priorities on which staff should focus during the coming 
one-to-three-year timeframe. 
T
he attached documents reflect Town Council’s strategic guidance related to the Council priorities and progress 
made since the last update in March of 2026. An abbreviated summary of those updates is provided in the 
memo below. Town senior leaders will be available at the work session to discuss these priorities and respond 
to any Town Council questions or feedback.

Staff completed an internal survey evaluating code compliance needs across all Town departments
and initiated a gap analysis to determine where best to focus education, compliance, and code revision

Town departments; the development of standard operating procedures (SOPs) is underway to ensure

• Maintenance, Upkeep, Affordability-HOAs 
o Status: Q3 2026 New. The condition and affordability of the deed 
restricted inventory is critical to ensuring that those units continue to 
serve the workforce. The inventory in Breckenridge is aging, with some 
units 25-30 years old. Current maintenance policies have not been 
effective in ensuring appropriate upkeep and maintenance, and staff is 
concerned that steps may be necessary to promote more upkeep, more 
improvements (sustainability) as well as important regular maintenance. 
This applies to dispersed units, neighborhoods, and multi-family projects 
with HOAs where maintenance and upkeep is impacting affordability.  
 
o Next Steps Q4: Continue to investigate the issues, the barriers to 
maintenance, and opportunities to provide additional incentives and/or 
support to maintain the liveability, quality, condition, and affordability of 
the inventory. Present preliminary findings in Q4.

The Five-Year Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) is presented herein for consideration and discussion at the work 
session. Funding for 2027 projects and funding for the three following years (2028-2030) are restricted as per 
Town Council policy. 
The Five-Year Capital Plan Summary table includes proposed projects and expenditures for 2027-2031, as well 
as a “Future Years” column for reference to Council priority projects that may be implemented beyond a five-
year timeframe. Detail pages for projects that will be implemented within the five-year timeframe are included 
and hyper-linked from the summary table. Budgets for CIP projects are approved on an annual basis; however, 
many projects take multiple years to complete or may not begin in the same year the budget is approved. The 
previously appropriated budget remaining for ongoing projects is also displayed in the summary. 
CIP projects include required investment in infrastructure as well as projects that support the Council and 
Community Goals. 
Goal 1: Deliver Balanced, Year-Round Economy
Under this first Council and Community Goal is a directly stated sub-goal: broadband access. 
In support of this goal, the proposed CIP includes continued development of the Fiber 9600 
network.  
Goal 2: Hometown Feel & Authentic Character 
The Blue River Pathways projects directly contribute to this goal with public art projects and 
many projects aimed at the revitalization of the downtown riverwalk area. Recreation capital 
projects, such as the Kingdom Park Ballfield Pavilion, also support our hometown sports team 
such as Summit High School baseball. 
Goal 3: More boots & bikes, less cars 
Many projects in the CIP focus on multimodal improvements to support bikes and pedestrians: 
Blue River Pathways Project, Pedestrian Bridge at Runway Neighborhood, 
Airport Road Improvements, Sidewalk Master Plan, and Little Daisy Open Space projects. 
 Goal 4: Leading Environmental Stewardship
The proposed CIP includes many leading-edge projects from the Sustainability Division: EV 
Charger Implementation, E-Delivery, Geothermal Energy Network, and Materials Management 
Centers. Additionally, the Blue River Pathways projects include river health initiatives including 
bank erosion repair, stormwater and vegetation management, and the Little Daisy Open 
Space project incorporates a high level of environmental stewardship in its design. 
Below is a summary provided for a limited number of proposed projects to provide the Council additional 
background and detail to support discussion at the work session.

Kingdom Park Ballfield Pavillion: 
This project will reconstruct the area between the newly resurfaced Kingdom Park ballfields, providing modern 
restroom facilities, improved circulation and spectator spaces, and a safer, more accessible east/west 
connection to the recreation path and Summit Stage bus stops on SH9 that can be reliably maintained in winter. 
The project integrates both site improvements and full reconstruction of the restroom structure, as each 
component is required to meet code and ensure ADA access throughout the site. Because the site work and 
restroom facility are interdependent, they must be completed together. Parking lot upgrades, signage, and other 
amenities have been intentionally deferred and can be delivered as a separate future phase of the project. 
To support a robust value-engineering effort for the project, Staff is recommending utilizing a remaining portion 
of the design funds (previous spending authority of $230,000) to engage a contractor for preconstruction 
services to identify potential savings prior to any commitment of a construction phase of the project. The current 
budget has been established based on the unawarded 2025 CMGC solicitation. 
Blue River Pathways Project: 
This project is an identified Town Council Priority and remains a focus in the proposed CIP. The 2025 Five-Year 
CIP first contemplated an annual expenditure of $1M for improvements related to the overall pathways project 
but did not identify specific projects. Following the 2026 CIP, the 2027 Five-Year Plan proposes a series of 
smaller projects for Council consideration which total to approximately $1M dollars per year as shown in the 
summary table. Larger infrastructure projects, such as the pedestrian bridges and underpasses, are proposed in 
2030 and future years. 
Airport Road Improvements: 
The Airport Road project will improve the stormwater system, pedestrian and bike facilities, and drive lanes on 
Airport Road from Fraction Road to Coyne Valley Road. As staff proceed with the design, we will be engaging 
stakeholders and the broader community, as well as seeking Council input on sidewalk layouts, lighting, bicycle 
facilities, and other amenities such as landscaping. The $7.5 million construction budget, shared between the 
Capital and Housing Funds, is a placeholder budget and will need to adjust based on the design program 
selected for the corridor.
