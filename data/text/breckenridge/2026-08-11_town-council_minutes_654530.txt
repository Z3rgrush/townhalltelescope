IV) COMMUNICATIONS TO COUNCIL 
A) PUBLIC COMMENT (NON-AGENDA ITEMS ONLY; 3-MINUTE TIME LIMIT) 
Mayor Kelly Owens opened public comment. 
Chuck Anderson, a Board Member for Columbine HOA, spoke in opposition of the 
Maggie Pond Special District. Anderson provided Council the proposed map of the 
special district. Anderson stated he met with a reporter. Anderson described potential 
plans of the Village to renovate Maggie Pond and noted concerns about the need for extra 
land that includes protected wetlands and open space. Anderson stated he believes this is 
a land grab by the Village and Vail Resorts and the proposed tax structure is unfair. 
With no additional public comments, Mayor Owens closed public comment. 
B) BRECKENRIDGE TOURISM OFFICE UPDATE (BTO) 
Lucy Kay, BTO President, reported that Wildflower Week generated approximately 
$80,000 in preliminary media value. She noted there are five transferable passes available 
for Council Members interested in attending the Governor's Conference. Oktoberfest 
volunteer slots are full, and stein sales are ahead of last year; BTO hopes to further grow 
Oktoberfest as a culinary-focused event. Kay stated Global at the Mine has secured a 
strong lineup of Michelin- and James Beard-recognized chefs, and ticket sales for Global 
on the Ridge will begin in the coming weeks. Kay also shared that the Snow Sculpture 
Committee selected 11 finalists from 35 applicants.

IX) REPORT OF MAYOR AND COUNCIL MEMBERS 
A) CAST/MMC 
Council Member Saade reported for MMC and noted a presentation on the Strong 
Futures 2026 ballot initiative, highlighting its community impact. If the county places the 
measure on the ballot, the Town may be asked to pass a resolution supporting it. The 
measure would not include a sunset clause. 
Town Manager Haynes reported the local organizations supporting the community's 
social safety net are struggling as state and federal funding decreases. The group needed 
to determine how to fund the First Steps tuition program for 2027. With declining 
nicotine tax revenue, funding for First Steps has dropped. The county has agreed to fund 
the program for 2027. Haynes noted that Summit County governments will need to set 
priorities and determine how to address potential shortfalls, but it is unlikely they could 
collectively cover the entire funding gap. 
Council Member Page also noted that Medicaid will now be a twice a year renewal.
