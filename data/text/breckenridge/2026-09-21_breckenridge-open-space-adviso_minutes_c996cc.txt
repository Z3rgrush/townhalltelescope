III) PUBLIC COMMENTS 
 
Sam Parker spoke in favor of developing steeper, hand-built downhill trails rather than wide, 
machine-graded paths. He explained that less machine-intensive trails could be more cost-
effective for the Town, easier to maintain, and stand up better to local weather conditions. 
Mr. Parker emphasized that adding dedicated downhill routes would reduce user conflicts on 
popular multi-use trails currently shared by hikers, uphill riders, and dogs. He assured the 
commission that local mountain bikers are eager to support these efforts and will readily 
volunteer for trail construction and ongoing maintenance. 
 
Teague Holmes thanked BOSAC for their ongoing work on the town network and highlighted 
the positive community impact of the Summit Mountain Challenge race series. Speaking on 
behalf of local riders, Holmes offered volunteer design and maintenance support for 
progressive or directional singletrack, suggesting potential areas like Little Mountain and 
referencing SCOMBA's successful partnership with the U.S. Forest Service on the Wheeler 
Trail. He stressed the importance of creating technical, challenging terrain for local youth and 
young racers, drawing a comparison to Durango's rider development success. Holmes 
concluded by asking for clearer public access to upcoming volunteer schedules and guidance 
on how community groups can stay involved in future planning.

E. SGM Quandary Peak Trailhead Design Contract 
Mr. Beckerman requested clarification on how the SCOC grant relates to the SGM 
consulting contract. Staff clarified that they are separate: SCOC refers to the outdoor 
coalition grant application through which the Town pledged $400,000 in matching funds 
for Quandary Trailhead improvements (with award results expected at year-end), while 
SGM is the consulting firm hired in partnership with the County to handle trailhead 
design. Staff explained that SGM’s work is phased—Phase 1 covers initial technical 
studies (such as traffic and geological assessments) needed for a County PUD parking 
expansion application, while Phase 2 design work is slated to begin after the SCOC grant 
award decisions are finalized. 
 
F. Aspen Alley Seasonal Management Plan 
Staff presented the Aspen Alley Seasonal Management Plan, which will feature a free 
public shuttle to the Wakefield Trailhead fully funded by a State of Colorado Clean 
Transit Enterprise grant. The shuttle will run a quick, high-frequency loop from the ice 
rink, welcome dogs and all trail users, and exclude bikes (since cyclists can ride directly 
from town). Because the vehicles lack ADA equipment, the two designated ADA parking 
spaces at the Wakefield Trailhead will remain open while the rest of the lot will be closed 
to public vehicular traffic. Commission members raised potential logistics issues—
including overflow parking at the ice rink requiring secondary bus transfers, wait times 
during peak hours without secondary van capacity, and queue management.  Once the 
shuttle service is complete, staff will receive hourly user data to help inform future 
shuttle offerings/. To prevent illegal parking on nearby roads, the Breckenridge Police 
Department will conduct regular patrols, issue citations, and respond directly to parking 
violation reports from shuttle drivers. 
 
G. Trail Matrix Update 
Ms. Zanca asked whether specific hiking groups would be contacted for feedback 
regarding the Trails Matrix. Staff clarified that partner outreach for the update is focused 
on land management agencies (such as the U.S. Forest Service and Summit County) 
rather than specific user groups or a formal public engagement campaign. In response to 
a concern that mountain bikers are highly organized while hikers lack structured 
representation at meetings, staff noted that while they welcome input from all trail users, 
executing a comprehensive public engagement process is beyond the scope of this phase.

The proposed 2027 workplan totals $310,000 across four main historic sites. At the Mountain

the 1980s that will retain its dormer and deck to interpret the site's entire living history

weatherproof the late 19th-century blacksmith shop and protect its remaining tools, while 
$25,000 will go toward clearing vegetation and placing gravel base support under the 
Reliance Dredge stacker to prevent ground rot. Additionally, the complex $130,000 Arctic 
Stamp Mill relocation originally approved for 2026 is rolling over into 2027 while boundary 
surveys resolve land ownership discrepancies between Colorado Springs Utilities and the 
U.S. Forest Service in Monte Cristo Gulch, with plans to relocate the stamp battery under a 
roof near the Jesse Mill on Gold Run Road. 
 
Ms. O’Neil concluded by highlighting recent instances of graffiti at local historic sites, noting 
that Breckenridge History is coordinating with Town staff to evaluate security camera 
monitoring. She emphasized that active preservation and public interpretation serve as key 
deterrents to vandalism by showing community care for the structures. Funding for the 
proposed 2027 projects will be formally reviewed by BOSAC in September. 
 
Following the presentation, Mr. Barlow clarified that the goal of the discussion was to 
address any questions, gauge BOSAC's conceptual support for the proposed 2027 projects, 
and identify any additional information needed for the upcoming September meeting, where 
formal funding support will be discussed. Ms. O’Neil also confirmed the ballpark project 
estimates: $125,000 for Mountain Pride, $30,000 for Dry Gulch, $25,000 for Reliance 
Dredge, and $130,000 for the Arctic Stamp Mill. Commission members expressed strong 
enthusiasm for the workplan—particularly highlighting the Mountain Pride preservation and 
upcoming interpretive signage—and praised Breckenridge History for efficiently executing 
restorations that protect the Town's unique historical character.
