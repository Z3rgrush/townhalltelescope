Subject: 2027 Property Tax Mill Levy 
Summary 
Please find attached the ordinance setting the mill levy within the Town of Breckenridge for 2027 at 
5.07 mills. The ordinance is submitted for first reading.  
B
ackground  
The 5.07 mill levy is the amount the Town is authorized to impose and cannot be increased without 
voter approval. This mill levy has maintained the same rate since 2014. For the 2027 budget year, we 
are forecasting the 5.07 mill levy to result in property tax revenues of $5.48M. If there are any 
adjustments made to our 2027 gross total taxable assessed valuation at the County or State level, we 
will come back to Town Council with an appropriation. 
T
own Council Goals 
The proposed mill levy is a critical tool to help achieve our Town Council’s long-term goals. By securing 
additional funding, we can invest in key areas such as public works, recreation, community 
development and public safety—aligning with our commitment to responsible stewardship of resources. 
This levy supports the council’s vision of a thriving, resilient town by ensuring it remains a place that 
visitors return to and residents can live and work. Through this measure, we can meet today’s needs 
while planning for tomorrow’s opportunities, in line with our values of adaptability, innovation, and 
community. 
F
inancial Implications 
The 5.07 mills are for the purpose of defraying expenses within the General Fund.  
E
quity Lens 
As we consider the mill levy proposal, we are mindful of our commitment to fostering a community that 
values diversity, equity, and inclusion. This initiative aligns with our core values by ensuring that all 
residents, regardless of background or socioeconomic status, can benefit from the enhanced services 
funded by the mill levy. By prioritizing equitable resource distribution and inclusive decision-making, we 
aim to strengthen our community fabric and create opportunities for all to thrive. This commitment will 
guide both the planning and implementation phases, ensuring that no group is left behind. 
S
taff Recommendation 
Staff recommend that Town council approve the Mill Levy at 5.07 Mills upon first reading.

Summary 
The Finance Department is submitting the resolution to adopt the 2027 budget and the 2027-2031 capital 
improvement plan. 
Background 
In development of the 2027 budget, all departments considered the 2024 and 2025 actuals for analysis 
along with updated 2026 projections based on the 2026 budget. 2026 projections and the 2027 budget 
were developed collaboratively with the Town budget team. Although the Town faces fiscal pressures in 
2027, including the working capital demands of Workforce Housing projects, Utility projects, the Capital 
Improvement Plan (CIP), anticipated revenue softening, and increased personnel and operating costs, 
staff across all departments demonstrated notable fiscal discipline in responding to these pressures. 
The 2027 budget assumes Sales Tax, Accommodations Tax, and Parking income to be flat from 2026 
Budget. This assumption includes a slight increase from 2026 projection due to the lack of snow this past 
winter. On the expense side, personnel costs increase up to 4% and operating costs assume a 
continuation of the current purchasing plan. Several Town priority projects, including the Runway 
Neighborhood workforce housing, the critical rehabilitation of the Gary Roberts Water Treatment Plant, 
replacement of essential Town vehicles/equipment, software upgrades, Town facility maintenance, and 
the expansion of Little Red School House Childcare Center are driving expenses in 2027. These 
increases are offset by remaining funds that are, in part, available as a result of budget conscious 
expense allocations. 
To look at the impact of future capital projects across all Funds, we present a 5-year projection. 
Assumptions for the projection include flat revenue growth beginning in 2027 and a general 3% increase 
in operational expenses each year based upon anticipated inflation. In addition, we applied the planned 
purchases for future projects of our major Funds including Capital, Workforce Housing, Child Care, Utility, 
Facilities, Garage, and Golf. 
The 5-year forecast process is designed to project working capital needs of the Town through 2031. The 
Town Council CIP review on September 8th resulted in prioritization of projects and the Town will continue 
to pursue the CIP and the operational budget as proposed while monitoring cash flow during the year to 
determine if the 2027 budget remains viable. 
Mission: The Town of Breckenridge protects, maintains, and enhances our sense of community, historical heritage, and 
alpine environment. We provide leadership and encourage community involvement.

Staff provided Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) in accordance with Government Finance Officers 
Association (GFOA) best practices. These KPIs evaluate the Town’s financial health across several 
dimensions, including liquidity, fund balance stability, capital investment sustainability , and long-term 
financial flexibility. Collectively, the indicators show the Town remains in a strong financial position even 
while navigating substantial capital commitments. Taken together, the KPIs provide an important context 
for understanding both the resilience of Town finances and sustainability of the projected budget.  
Public outreach/engagement 
At the Town Council work session held on September 8, 2026, the public was invited to attend the Town 
Council work session presentation and discussion of the Capital Improvement Plan. The October 13, 2026 
budget retreat, also open to the public, allowed residents to hear directly from departments and senior 
leadership regarding their budget proposals. Before the adoption presentation on October 27, 2026, the public 
has further opportunities to provide input and feedback during open meetings. 
Financial Implications 
The 2027 budgeted revenues (net of transfers) are $138.8M and expenses (net of transfers) are $160.3M. 
The total 2027 forecast, including continuing operations and long-term financing, results in an overall deficit of 
approximately $25M. A significant portion of this deficit (approximately $17M) is attributable to the 
rehabilitation of the Gary Roberts Water Treatment Plant, which is planned to begin construction in 2027. 
Although 2027 is proposed as a budget deficit year, it is driven heavily by intentional capital investment and is 
well supported by our current cash balance. Ongoing operating revenues remain stable, and long-range KPIs 
indicate the Town continues to be financially strong. 
 
Equity Lens 
The Town considered the impact to our residents and community to provide continual support for Town 
programs and offerings. Whether revenue is tax or fee based, each department considered the welcome factor 
for residents and visitors to remove barriers and remain an inclusive community. The budget process embodies 
Town Council’s philosophy of considering who benefits from the Town’s various programs, the intention and 
impact of those expenditures, and how we as a community uplift and build trust with underrepresented groups. 
Staff Recommendation 
Staff recommend adoption of the 2027 Budget and appropriation of funding via the attached resolution.

Background  
The Joint Upper Blue Master Plan (JUBMP) provides policy direction on several land use issues in the Upper 
Blue Basin. The JUBMP was adopted by the towns of Breckenridge and Blue River and Summit County. One 
of the major policy discussions that occurred in the 2011 update to the JUBMP was density for affordable 
housing. A policy related to this issue requires the Town of Breckenridge to extinguish density it owns to 
account for density in new affordable housing projects. 
 
On January 28, 2020, the Council adopted an ordinance amending the Town’s Development Code as follows: 
 
E. Density For Employee Housing Projects: When new employee housing projects are developed 
within the corporate limits of the Town, the Town government may, in its sole discretion, transfer 
density it owns to the employee housing project at a one to one (1:1) ratio (i.e., transfer 1 
development right for every 1 employee housing project units permitted to be built).

Equity Lens 
The Town’s TDR program for workforce housing allows for the construction of attainable workforce 
housing, creating a more economically diverse local community comprised of workers and community 
members that span across the income spectrum who are able to live where they work. This specific 
workforce housing project focuses on seasonal workers and includes a rental cap of 85 percent of the 
60 percent Area Median Income studio unit rental rate established in the Development Agreement.

Material Management 
 
Bear Update 
Staff are working to reduce bear activity at the Town’s shared trash and recycling enclosures. The Facilities team is 
working diligently to repair enclosure doors at a few locations. We have sent messages to all enclosure users asking 
them to dispose of materials properly and close the enclosure doors fully behind them each time. We are piloting a bear 
deterrent mat at Peak 8 while the door is under repair. The goal is to change bear behavior by making the enclosures an 
unpleasant place to visit, so bears learn to look elsewhere for food. We will monitor how the mats perform and share 
what we learn. 
 
Reminder for the public 
• Keep all refuse containers closed and locked 
• Store containers inside whenever possible, including between collection days 
• Request a wildlife-resistant container from their hauler if they do not already have one 
 
 
Bear/human activity in Colorado has increased in 2026 due to drought impacts on food sources. Bears become 
habituated to food sources and may be euthanized if problems persist with individual bears. The public and businesses 
are urged to be responsible with waste storage to help keep bears safe.

ADU policy for deed restricted homes 
 During the December 9th, 2025 and January 13th, 2026 work sessions, Town Council discussed Accessory 
Dwelling Units (ADUs) specifically for the Runway Neighborhood and provided staff with policy directions 
regarding their construction within the neighborhood, maximum rental rates, ability to recoup expenses for 
the construction, and policies for the utilization of the ADUs. Further details specifying the final policies were 
discussed with the Housing Workgroup at the May 14th, 2026 and the September 8th, 2026 and submitted to 
Town Council in the Housing Update via minutes from the workgroup meetings in the subsequent Council 
session. Based on the direction received during these multiple meetings staff prepared an ADU Addendum 
to the Deed Restriction for the Runway Neighborhood with the following details and would like to apply this 
policy to all deed restricted properties that can accommodate the buildout of an ADU. 
 For units with the ADU shell, at the time of resale, the owner may recoup up to $150,000 to cover costs they incur as part 
of the buildout (this includes cash expense as well as a credit for sweat equity if the work is performed by the 
owner). This figure was rounded up to account for construction cost increases over time to help account for the 
phased construction of the units over the 4-year buildout. 
 For units that were not sold initially with a garage and shell (not ADU ready), the owner may recoup up to $250,000 for 
adding a garage and an ADU. 
 Owners are not required to rent the ADU, there is flexibility to use as an office or additional bedroom, and owners may 
still recoup their expense at time of resale. 
 If a unit is rented, there is a maximum rental rate to ensure the units are affordable ( currently the rent cap is set at 70% 
AMI for a one-bedroom unit) and the rental must be to a qualified workforce occupant. 
 The primary goal for the council work session is to both confirm the policies for Runway ADUs and to 
determine if the Council is comfortable extending similar polices for other deed restricted neighborhoods 
or dispersed deed restricted units that can accommodate an ADU. Staff feel it is important to incentivize 
ADUs helping to ensure a large diversity of housing inventory and to create consistency for owners of deed 
restricted home while also balancing affordability over time.

Example Scenario - Wellington II 
Purchase price of $600,000 in October of 2022 and selling in October 2026. Utilizing the 3% 
appreciation method the maximum allowable sales price would be $672,000 after 4 years 
of ownership comparatively by utilizing the change in AMI the maximum allowable sales 
price would be $835,537. In this scenario a $163,537 difference is already allowed within 
the covenant. If Town could incentivize the buildout of an ADU with negotiating an 
upgrade to the deed restriction that removes the change in AMI calculation the result 
would still be a lower sales price than the AMI calculation and an additional unit would 
have been built.

Aspen Alley Seasonal Shuttle 
Based on Aspen Alley Trail user data and staff observations during past foliage visitation periods, staff 
concluded that parking demand far exceeded capacity at the Wakefield Sawmill trailhead (Wakefield), 
leading to resource damage, poor user experience, and decreased accessibility. To address this 
excess parking demand, staff conducted a pilot program providing a free shuttle service from the Ice 
Rink parking lot to and from the Wakefield trailhead during a three-week period, from September 12- 
October 3, 2026. The goal was to use 14-passenger shuttles to take advantage of the ample parking at 
the Ice Rink while protecting Wakefield from resource damage. The pilot program was a resounding 
success, with the shuttles transporting nearly 16,000 passengers across 22 operating days. 
To safely and efficiently 
accommodate 
passenger pick-up/ drop-
off and shuttle 
maneuvering, parking at 
the Wakefield parking lot 
was closed except for a 
single ADA parking 
space. A temporary 
Breck E-ride bicycle hub 
was also installed at the 
trailhead during this 
period.   
To communicate the 
temporary parking 
restriction and shuttle 
alternative, VMS boards 
were deployed on 
Boreas Pass Road at 
the Ice Rink and at the 
Wakefield entrance. The 
Town’s Outreach team assisted staff with digital messaging across multiple platforms and in drafting a 
press release which was picked up by Krystal 93, KUNC, Summit Daily News, the Denver Post, 
multiple Denver regional television stations, and Fox News. For those who opted to access the Aspen 
Alley Trail without using the shuttle system, temporary wayfinding signage was installed along the 
Illinois Creek Trail. 
The pilot shuttle program was funded by a Colorado Clean Transit Enterprise Grant (grant) secured by 
the Town’s Mobility Department, with the 10% match requirement paid for with OST funds. Trail use

Based on data collected with a Trafx trailcounter located near the bottom of Aspen Alley Trail, overall 
trail use numbers during the three-week shuttle service were slightly higher than last year: 17,553 in 
2026 compared with 16,145 in 2025.  While impossible to know for certain, this increase in user 
numbers suggests that the shuttle was successful in improving user access to the Aspen Alley Trail 
during its three-week operating period.  While it is likely some shuttle riders never triggered the Trafx 
trailcounter- perhaps by choosing an alternative route such as the Blue River Trail, or not venturing far 
enough up the Aspen Alley trail to reach the counter- the similarity between the total number of shuttle 
passengers (15,671) to the total number of Aspen Alley Trail users (17,553) suggests that the vast 
majority of trail users participated in the shuttle program.  Finally, by all accounts, the Wakefield closure 
was respected by the public and no resource damage was observed.      
The new hiking and uphill biking trail paralleling the Aspen Alley Trail, to be named “Aspen Incline” by 
the USFS, is under construction with expected completion later this fall. Staff anticipate this new trail will 
reduce user conflict by separating user types but is not likely to cause significant change in hiking use 
numbers during future foliage visitation periods. Past trail user data has indicated a drastic decline in 
biking use levels during this period, with bikers presumably opting to avoid Aspen Alley Trail due to high 
hiking use, and it’s reasonable to assume biking use will increase significantly with the additional trail 
providing a separate user experience.  However, given that the Aspen Alley Seasonal Shuttle is not 
designed to serve bikers, shuttle demand is not expected to change significantly as a result of the 
addition of the Aspen Incline Trail. 
This pilot program was a collaborative effort across many departments. OST staff worked closely with 
the Town’s Community Service, Mobility, Police, Streets & Parks, and Sustainability Departments, as 
well as the Town’s Outreach Team and County’s OST staff and Sheriff’s Office to safely provide this 
service to our residents and guests. Staff hope to offer this program in future years and will continue to 
pursue grant opportunities for funding while looking for cost-saving opportunities- e.g. by decreasing 
daily shuttle operating hours- in the process.

The Breckenridge Events Committee evaluates events according to the following 
(strategic) criteria: 
• Build Business - An event designed to drive revenue for greater business community. 
• Branding/Media - An event designed to draw external media (national & international) promoting 
the Breckenridge brand. 
• Fundraising - An event designed to raise awareness and funding for a non-profit organization's 
mission. 
• Resident Focused - An event designed specifically for residents vs. an event more broadly 
marketed to visitors and residents.
