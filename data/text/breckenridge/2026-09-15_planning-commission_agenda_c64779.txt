1.  Comprehensive Plan Underutilized Parcels Analysis 
Mr. Kulick presented the topic for the work session which will include a focus on three areas: the 
Conservation District, Breckenridge South (including Breckenridge Elementary, 555 S. French St. and the 
rodeo grounds), and Park Avenue Corridor.  The continuing analysis of underutilized parcels is a 
requirement for the Town’s Comprehensive Plan Update. 
The following specific questions were asked of the Commission: 
1. Does the Commission agree with the staff recommendation of applying the existing Conservation 
District density limitations to any workforce housing developments within the Conservation 
District? 
2. Does the Commission agree with the staff recommendation of an allowed workforce housing 
density of 20 UPA for the Breckenridge South area? 
3. Does the Commission agree with the staff recommendation of an allowed workforce housing 
density of 30 UPA for the Park Avenue corridor area? 
4. Are there other specific areas of Town the Commission thinks should be reviewed in the same 
way?

Item Background 
The Little Red Schoolhouse was constructed in 2004. Its floor plan has remained largely unchanged, 
with minor exterior improvements, including solar panels and play areas. The Town is partnering with 
the school to accommodate additional students and improve the facility for students and staff, consistent 
with the Town Council’s goal of expanding childcare. 
Page 27 of 85
The project retains six age-group classrooms and adds a summer classroom/flex room designed for 16 
children and additional office space. It also expands the entrance and reconfigures the existing 
multipurpose area for storage and circulation.  
At this preliminary work session, staff requests Commission feedback on architectural compatibility, 
parking and drop-off circulation, landscaping, and outdoor play areas. Final compliance findings and a 
complete point analysis will be presented at a future Town Project Hearing. 
Staff Comments 
Land Use (Policies 2/A & 2/R): The proposal continues the existing childcare use. Staff considers the 
project an institutional use, including its accessory offices and support spaces. Staff supports the 
proposed use. The property is located within the Vista Point Master Plan area  and complies with the 
existing institutional/childcare use contemplated in the Master Plan documents. Staff has no concerns 
regarding the existing use. 
 
Density/Intensity & Mass(3/A, 3/R, 4/A & 4/R): The addition increases the main building from 5,723 
sq. ft. to 7,365 sq. ft. Policy 3/A(F) exempts community facilities and institutional uses from transferring 
density to accommodate the use. Staff finds this exemption applies to the proposed childcare expansion 
and has no concerns. The final density calculation must include any countable enclosed area in the 
separate trash enclosure. 
  
Architectural Compatibility (5/A & 5/R): The addition incorporates gable and shed roof forms below 
the existing central roof element. The entry and classroom gables break up the primary frontage. Staff 
finds the proposed scale and roof forms compatible with the existing schoolhouse. 
 
Proposed materials include fiber -cement lap siding and trim, brick, aluminum windows, asphalt 
shingles, and standing- seam metal roofing. The siding, brick, and roofing are proposed to match the 
existing building. Policy 5/R allows fiber -cement siding and brick  is allowed  for institutional uses 
without a material-related point deduction. Staff supports the proposed materials, including the red body 
color and exposed metal elements. 
 
Building Height (6/A & 6 /R): The one-story addition measures approximately 13 feet 4 inches from 
grade to the mean roof elevation, below the recommended height of 26 feet. The addition remains below 
the existing central roof element. Staff finds the proposed height and scale appropriate and has no 
concerns under this policy. 
 
Site and Environmental Design (7/R): The addition and parking improvements are concentrated in 
previously developed areas, including the existing parking lot. Staff has no concerns with the overall 
extent of site disturbance. 
Placement of Structures (9/A & 9/R): The addition is located along the primary frontage, with the 
replacement trash enclosure near the eastern driveway. Both structures exceed the minimum one -foot 
setback required for nonresidential development under Policy 9/A. Staff has no concerns with the proposed 
placement. 
 
Snow Removal and Storage (13/R): Policy 13/R encourages snow storage equal to approximately 25% of 
the area to be cleared. The project has not provided a snow-storage calculation or designated storage areas. 
Page 28 of 85
The final plan must identify functional snow storage coordinated with parking, landscaping, sight lines, 
accessible routes, and play areas prior to the Town Project hearing. 
Refuse and Recycling (15/A & 15/R): The replacement trash enclosure uses materials coordinated with 
the schoolhouse. The final design must accommodate refuse and recycling, address bear resistance, and 
provide collection access that avoids conflicts with drop-off, pedestrian circulation, and play areas. Staff 
supports the enclosed facility. 
 
Access / Circulation and Parking (16/A & 16/R; 17/A; 18/A & 18/R): The parking lot is 
reconstructed with angled parking that will replace existing perpendicular spaces and new accessible 
spaces near the expanded entrance. Both driveways to Reiling Road and the connection to the recreation 
path are retained. Staff has no concerns regarding the circulation. 
 
Childcare facilities are not separately listed in the Town’s parking requirements. Staff considers the 
elementary and junior high school category the most comparable use under Section 9 -3-8(F) and 
proposes applying its classroom -based parking standard. The expanded facility includes seven 
classrooms, requiring a minimum of 14 parking spaces. The project increases parking spaces  from 22 
existing spaces to 25 proposed spaces, including two accessible spaces. Although the proposal meets the 
minimum requirement, it exceeds the maximum allowance of 2.2 spaces per classroom. Staff would note 
that the number of parking spaces is  inadequate during peak hours (~5:00 -5:30 pm)  and having 
additional spaces available would be a positive by- product of the expansion. Staff will review  and 
discuss the proposed parking before the Town Project Hearing. 
 
Proposed parking grades include slopes of approximately 4.57% and 4.63%, exceeding the 4% 
sustained-grade maximum under Section 9-3- 9(F). Engineering review will address driveway grades, 
aisle widths, turning movements, emergency access, and accessible circulation  which may require 
revisions. 
 
Landscaping (22/A & 22/R): The applicant proposes removing 41 trees and planting one lodgepole 
pine, 50 shrubs, and 115 perennials. Staff finds the proposed tree replacement insufficient to offset the 
loss of established landscaping. A more substantial landscape plan is required, including additional trees 
to restore screening and buffering along Reiling Road. The revised plan must also address required 
buffers, interior parking landscaping, plant sizes, and coordination with snow storage. 
 
Plant selection and placement must comply with Policy 22 and the Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code, 
including immediate  zone requirements  (within five feet of structures). The immediate zone should 
incorporate noncombustible ground cover, with ignition- resistant plantings only where permitted by the 
applicable standards. Staff requires removal of juniper from the planting schedule due to its high 
flammability and replacement with less flammable alternatives in appropriate locations. 
 
The site/landscape plan must identify the ignition  zones and address planting, wood mulch, play 
surfacing, and fencing near the building. Staff recommends resolving these details with Building and 
Fire District reviewers before finalizing the landscape and play-area design. 
 
Page 29 of 85
Social Community (24/A & 24/R): The expansion advances the Town Council’s goal of expanding 
childcare and provides additional services encouraged under Policy 24/R. The Little Red Schoolhouse 
previously received positive eight (+8) points under Policy 24/R(C). Valley Brook also received positive 
eight (+8) points under Policy 24/R(C) for a new childcare center. The Montessori School received 
positive three (+3) points under Policy 24/R(C) for renovating an existing building into a childcare 
facility. Staff is not aware of comparable precedent specifically for an expansion of an existing childcare 
facility. 
 
Staff recommends consideration of positive three (+3) points under Policy 24/R(B), Community Needs, 
for addressing the Town Council’s childcare goal, and positive four (+4) points under Policy 24/R(C), 
Social Services, for expanding childcare services. These represent the lowest positive awards under each 
subsection, for a combined total of positive seven (+7) points. Staff considers these awards appropriate 
for an expansion of an existing facility and requests Commission feedback on establishing this 
precedent. Final point recommendations will be presented at the Town Project Hearing. 
 
Institutional uses are exempt from employee housing impact mitigation under Policy 24/A(B)(3). The 
proposed offices are accessory to the childcare facility. Staff has no concerns regarding compliance with 
this provision. 
 
Drainage (27/A & 27/R):  Parking lot regrading includes a new inlet and storm connection. Engineering 
review will address drainage capacity, downstream impacts, and utility and easement conflicts. 
 
Exterior Lighting (Sec. 9-12): The plans indicate three new wall -mounted sconces, with the remaining 
fixtures shown as existing. The most recent exterior lighting compliance survey identifies the property 
as dark sky compliant. The applicant shall provide cut sheets for the new fixtures to demonstrate 
compliance with the Town’s exterior lighting standards. 
 
The applicant shall also clarify whether the existing light posts along the building’s frontage will be 
retained, relocated, or replaced to accommodate the expansion and revised circulation. Any new or 
relocated fixtures shall be reviewed for compliance. A site photometric plan prepared by a registered 
Colorado engineer is required to evaluate lighting levels and light spill under the proposed layout. 
  
Point Analysis (Section: 9 -1-17-3): Staff will complete the point analysis after receiving the 
outstanding information and referral comments. Potential positive points for childcare services will be 
evaluated with any remaining relative -policy issues. Absolute-policy compliance remains subject to the 
revisions and verification identified above.

GENERAL.  Footings and slabs on grade shall be placed on prepared subgrade.  Requirements for  
earthwork, including excavation, fill and backfill shall be in accordance with the International Building Code.
SITE CLEARING.  All vegetation and other organic matter, pavement, existing construction and man-made 
fill (except as otherwise directed), and any other unsuitable material shall be removed from site and 
properly disposed of.
FILL AND BACKFILL.  All fill and backfill material shall be clean, free of organic or frozen matter, and any 
other unsuitable material, and is to be approved by the Architect before use.  If site material does not meet 
the requirements, use imported materials or a uniform mix of site and imported materials which do meet the 
specified requirements.  See the EARTHWORK SCHEMATIC for gradation, plasticity index and other fill 
material requirements.
QUALITY CONTROL.  Maximum densities and optimum moisture contents of fill shall be determined in 
accordance with ASTM D1557 or ASTM D698, as indicated.  Density of in-place material shall be determined 
in accordance with either ASTM D1556 (sand cone method), D6938 (nuclear method) or D2167 (rubber 
balloon method), whichever is applicable.  Gradations of materials shall be determined in accordance with 
ASTM D422.  Liquid limits, plastic limits and plasticity indices shall be determined in accordance with ASTM 
D4318.  See the SPECIFICATIONS for additional requirements and testing schedule.
EXCAVATION AND PROTECTION OF EXCAVATIONS.  Remove existing material as required to meet 
site grading elevations.  See the EARTHWORK SCHEMATIC for extent of excavation and fill/backfill 
required at buildings.  Do not undercut existing construction.  Provide positive surface drainage away from 
excavations and promptly remove any surface water which may enter the excavations.  Remove any 
subgrade material and any previously placed fill or backfill which has been softened or otherwise damaged 
by moisture. Replace with properly placed and compacted fill or backfill.  Slope sides of excavations as 
required for slope stability and provide barricades, lights and warning signs as necessary for the protection 
of existing property, construction personnel and the public.
PREPARATION OF SUBGRADE.  After excavations have been completed and/or surface has been 
cleaned and grubbed, the subgrade shall be prepared to meet the conditions indicated in the 
EARTHWORK SCHEMATIC.
PLACING AND COMPACTION OF FILL AND BACKFILL.  See the EARTHWORK SCHEMATIC for 
compaction requirements for fill and backfill.  Perform compaction when the material to be compacted is at its 
optimum moisture content (see EARTHWORK SCHEMATIC for tolerance).  Thoroughly mix water into the soil 
mass to be compacted so the moisture content of the soil is uniform.  Perform compaction using appropriate 
equipment and methods as necessary to achieve the required density percentages without damage to existing 
construction.  Do not use ponding, flooding, jetting or similar methods to aid in compaction.

WOOD TRUSSES
GENERAL.  Wood roof trusses shall be designed, fabricated and erected to resist the loads given below in 
accordance with following criteria and the SPECIFICATIONS.  Design calculations sealed by a licensed 
Professional Engineer shall be submitted to the Architect for review.
SPECIFICATIONS.  Except where more restrictive requirements are shown on the Drawings, are listed below, 
or are contained in the local building code, metal plate connected wood roof trusses shall be designed, 
fabricated, handled and erected in accordance with the applicable provisions of the latest edition or revision of 
each of the following documents published by the Truss Plate Institute:
a. "Design Specification for Metal Plate Connected Wood Roof Trusses"
b. "Quality Standard for Metal Plate Connected Wood Trusses"
c. "Commentary and Recommendations for Handling and Erecting Wood Trusses"
d. "Commentary and Recommendations for Bracing Wood Trusses"
SUBMITTALS.  Design, materials, fabrication and erection are subject to the approval of the 
Architect/Engineer.  Design calculations showing, as a minimum, the calculated stresses in all members 
and calculated deflections under all loading conditions, locations of splices in chord members, and design 
of connections shall be submitted to the Architect/Engineer for review.  Fabrication and erection drawings 
showing truss designations, locations, member sizes, metal plate sizes, and orientation, bracing 
requirements, and supplemental framing if required shall be submitted to the Architect/Engineer for review 
prior to beginning fabrication of the trusses.
GEOMETRY.  Spacing of trusses shall be as shown on the Roof Framing Plan and Architectural Drawings.  
Truss layout shall be coordinated with ceiling and lighting layouts.  Outer configuration of trusses shall be 
as shown in the truss elevations.  Configuration of the diagonal members may be designed by the 
Fabricator, however, configuration chosen must allow passage of equipment within attic space.
DESIGN ASSUMPTIONS.  Top and bottom chords of trusses shall be designed to resist both axial and 
flexural loads.  Chords shall be assumed to be continuous except at splices.  Web members shall be 
assumed to be pinned at their ends.  Roof trusses may be designed for  115% of nominal unit stresses of 
wood under their full loads and for 133% of nominal unit stresses under combined gravity and lateral (wind 
or seismic) loads.  All connections shall be designed to safely resist and transmit not less than 125% of 
design forces.  Connection plates between members shall be assumed to transmit shear and axial loads 
only.  Truss deflections shall be calculated using accepted engineering methods.  Maximum deflections 
shall not exceed L/240 under full load and L/360 under live load only.  L is the span length; center to center 
of supports.
