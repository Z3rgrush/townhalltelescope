Summary 
The Town’s arts and culture master planning consultants, Cultural Planning Group (CPG), will present findings 
from the community engagement efforts from March-September 2026. This input will inform the recommendations 
in the Arts and Culture Master Plan (Arts 9600’). 
 
Background  
In November 2025, staff released a Request for Proposals (RFP) for consultant assistance in the development of 
the Arts & Culture Master Plan and Organizational Management/Funding Analysis in November. In December 
2025, a subcommittee of the Arts & Culture Master Plan Steering Committee (Tamara Nuzzaci Park, Dave 
DePeters, Lucy Kay, Julia Puester, Jay Beckerman) unanimously selected two uniquely qualified consultants for 
each section of the RFP, the Cultural Planning Group (CPG) for the master plan and Summit Street Group 
(SSG)/Turner Townsend, for the organizational management and funding analysis from fourteen consultant firms.  
 
CPG has completed this portion of community engagement for the Arts 9600’. The information gathered will serve 
as the foundation of the Plan by directly informing its priorities, goals, and implementation strategies (which will 
also allow for public comment). Between March and September 2026, more than 650 full and part-time residents, 
employees, and visitors participated in interviews, discussion groups, pop‑up events, focus groups, and bilingual 
polling.  This extensive and in depth outreach ensures that the Plan reflects Breckenridge’s cultural identity, local 
needs, future desires, and Town Council priorities.  
 
Engagement findings are used in the following ways: 
• Aligning the Plan recommendations with community priorities 
• Identifying key challenges and opportunities 
• Shaping and refining strategies and initiatives 
• Validating assumptions with lived experience 
• Ensuring equity and inclusivity 
• Recommendations and implementation readiness 
Page 3 of 500
 
CPG presented the Community Engagement Report to the Arts and Culture Master Plan Steering Committee on 
September 16.  The Committee was in support of the presentation, range of outreach efforts and amount of community 
engagement. Members voiced that they anticipated many of the results received and it was positive confirmation from 
numerous voices of the community and visitors. Questions were raised regarding destination driving events, what visitors 
want to see, timing of implementation and hiring of Town arts position, communication improvement for programs, how 
respondents felt about existing programming, and clarity around investment in the Riverwalk Center. CPG will address 
these during the Council presentation. 
 
Public outreach/engagement 
The master planning process incorporates a broad community visioning with prioritization of arts and culture 
offerings and specific programming and facility investment areas. Overall, the engagement efforts outlined in the 
report ensures the Arts 9600’ Master Plan is rooted in Breckenridge’s culture, aligned with Council priorities, 
reflective of diverse community voices, and prepares to guide investment for the next decade. After preliminary 
recommendations are developed based on the input received to date, CPG will review those with the Arts & 
Culture Master Plan Steering Committee, Breckenridge Social Equity Advisory Commission, Planning 
Commission, and Town Council. The draft Plan will then be available for public comment via a bilingual platform. 
 
Financial Implications 
There are no financial implications being proposed by staff at this time. General future funding implications will be 
presented with the recommendations of the Plan and future Town Council direction.   
 
Equity Lens 
The Town aims to provide access and high-quality arts and culture programs to our community and visitors.  
The engagement efforts ensure that there is an equitable deployment of funds to provide such programs and 
services. 
 
Staff Recommendation 
This item is informational only. No Town Council action is required but questions and comments are 
encouraged.  
 
Next Steps 
Following questions and feedback from the Town Council, CPG will prepare recommendations for review with 
the Steering Committee, advisory bodies, the public and Town Council.  Staff anticipate returning to Town 
Council in November for this review, with modifications being made in December and final adoption before the 
Town Council in January.

What Stakeholders Told Us (1 of 2)
1
Institutional Strength & a Distinctive “Fusion”
NRO, Breck Create, Backstage Theatre, Breck Film, and Breck
History give Breckenridge a density of arts organizations rare for
its size. Board leaders describe a “rising tide”: strengthening every
organization lifts them all.
2
Strengthening Breck Create's Role & Governance
Stakeholders want a clearer definition of BCA's role, and durability
built into governance with defined review cycles and
accountability.
3
Reimagining the Riverwalk Center & Arts District
Opportunities to grow seating capacity for a broader range of
touring acts, improve ADA access, activate winter shoulder-season
use, and give the Arts District stronger street-level visibility.
4
Building One Shared Communications Hub
A single, coordinated events calendar across the Town, BTO, and
Breck Create, paired with a communications mix that reaches
every generation and every language.

SECTION 05 • CROSS - CUTTING THEMES
Cross-Cutting Themes (1 of 2)
1
A Level of Cultural Density Few Towns This Size Can Match
Participants consistently described Breckenridge as unusually
strong for a town of about five thousand people, pointing to NRO,
The Arts District, Backstage Theatre, Breck Film, Breck History
and others as a genuine differentiator worth protecting.
2
Clarifying Breck Create's Role and Rebuilding Trust
The relationship between Breck Create and the Town was one of
the most discussed topics. The Town has provisionally accepted a
hybrid shared-responsibility model, and stakeholders want the
plan to provide a stable, ten-year foundation for that transition.
3
Investing in the Riverwalk Center and Arts Campus
Investment opportunities converged on the Riverwalk Center:
growing capacity to compete for a broader range of touring acts,
adding restroom capacity, and building flexibility for smaller
events and a more active Arts District.
4
Building One Shared Communications Hub with Clear
Messaging
Stakeholders see a clear opportunity for a shared, coordinated
events calendar and a predictable communications digest, and an
opportunity to brand its investments as Town of Breckenridge
destinations (e.g. The Arts District).

SECTION 05 • CROSS- CUTTING THEMES
Cross-Cutting Themes (2 of 2)
5
Year-Round Programming Is the Community's Clear #1
Priority
Community polling is unambiguous: “Year-Round Arts Programs
and Music Events” ranked first by a substantial margin across
every respondent group, echoing input from businesses,
volunteers, and patrons alike.
6
Expanding Affordability and Language Access
Cost and language access surfaced as two of the clearest
opportunities to widen participation, including broader program
pricing for workforce families and stronger ,direct Spanish-
language communication.
7
Embracing All Generations
Stakeholders identified an opportunity to grow programming for
residents in their twenties and thirties built around something
other than alcohol, and more informal, accessible event formats for
younger residents and families.
8
Building a Plan That Lasts
Stakeholders see an opportunity for this plan to build in its own
durability: defined review cycles, clear accountability metrics, and
benchmarking against comparable mountain-town peers.

Cross-Cutting Themes Across All 
Engagement	
Several	themes	surfaced	independently	and	consistently	
across	stakeholder	interviews,	discussion	groups,	
virtual	focus	groups,	and	the	community	polling.	
1	
A Level of Cultural 
Density Few Towns This 
Size Can Match	
	
Stakeholders	independently	described	Breckenridge	as	
unusually	strong	for	a	town	of	about	five	thousand	
people,	pointing	to	the	National	Repertory	Orchestra,	
Breck	Create’s	multi-discipline	campus,	Backstage	
Theatre,	Breck	Film,	Breck	History,	and	a	growing	
creative	business	sector	as	a	genuine	differentiator	for	
the	Town.	Participants	consistently	framed	the	plan’s	
central	challenge	as	protecting	and	building	on	this	
strength.	
2	
Clarifying Breck Create’s 
Role and Rebuilding Trust	
	
The	relationship	between	Breck	Create	(BCA)	and	the	
Town,	and	between	BCA	and	partner	organizations,	was	
one	of	the	most	discussed	subjects	across	stakeholder	
engagement.	A	parallel	governance	and	financial	review	
(SSG)	recommended	a	hybrid	shared-responsibility	
model,	which	the	Town	has	provisionally	accepted	—	
returning	facility	maintenance	to	the	Town	and	
evaluating	a	new	arts	and	culture	manager	role,	with	
transition	details	still	being	finalized.	Stakeholders	want	
the	cultural	plan	to	provide	the	stable,	ten-year	strategic	
foundation	this	transition	can	build	on.	
3	
Investing in the 
Riverwalk Center and 
Arts Campus	
	
Investment	opportunities	converged	repeatedly	on	the	
Riverwalk	Center.	Participants	see	room	to	grow	its	
roughly	capacity	to	compete	for	a	broader	range	of	
touring	acts,	add	restroom	capacity,	and	build	in	more	
flexibility	to	reconfigure	the	space	for	smaller	events	
and	a	wider	range	of	acoustics	and	seating	uses.	
Participants	also	pointed	to	real	potential	in	activating	
the	venue	and	surrounding	Arts	Campus	more	
consistently,	making	fuller	use	of	their	central,	town-
owned	footprint.	
4	
Building One Shared 
Communications Hub with 
Clear Messaging	
	
Stakeholders	see	a	clear	opportunity	to	help	residents	
and	visitors	discover	more	of	what’s	already	happening.	
Requests	converged	on	a	shared,	coordinated	master	
events	calendar	and	a	predictable,	recurring	events	
digest	through	multiple	channels.	The	Town	can	brand	
its	investments	as	Town	of	Breckenridge	destinations	
with	the	Arts	District	as	a	main	example.		
5	
Year-Round Programming 
Is the Community’s Clear 
#1 Priority	
	
Community	polling	is	unambiguous:	“Year-Round	Arts	
Programs	and	Music	Events”	ranked	as	the	#1	priority	
by	a	substantial	margin	across	every	respondent	group.	
This	finding	directly	echoes	qualitative	input	from	retail	
and	restaurant	owners,	the	Events	Committee,	and	
volunteers	and	patrons,	all	of	whom	pointed	to	the	same	
opportunity:	consistent,	off-peak,	and	shoulder-season	
programming	that	builds	on	the	town’s	three	or	four	
annual	flagship	events.	
6	
Expanding Affordability 
and Language Access	
	
Cost	and	language	access	surfaced	as	two	of	the	clearest	
opportunities	to	widen	participation.	Participants	
pointed	to	opportunities	to	broaden	program	pricing	
options	for	workforce	families	and	to	strengthen	access	
for	Spanish-speaking	residents.

Stakeholders	identified	an	opportunity	to	grow

Stakeholder Themes	
The	following	eight	themes	emerged	from	the	individual	
interviews	and	discussion	groups.	
1	
Institutional Strength and a 
Distinctive "Fusion" of 
Organizations	
	
—		Cultural	institutions:	the	National	Repertory	
Orchestra,	Breck	Create,	Backstage	Theatre,	Breck	Film,	
and	Breck	History	give	Breckenridge	a	density	and	
breadth	of	arts	organizations	unusual	for	its	size,	and	
participants	pointed	to	this	cross-organization	"fusion,"	
together	with	whimsical	programming	(stilt	walkers,	
the	Troll,	BIFA),	as	the	town’s	real	differentiator.	
—		Public	investment:	Stakeholders	cited	strong	public	
investment	in	arts	facilities	as	evidence	residents	
already	value	culture	as	a	civic	priority.	
—		Peer	models:	Stakeholders	pointed	to	arts	districts,	
business-improvement	partnerships,	and	chamber	
alliances	in	comparable	communities	as	frameworks	
worth	adapting.	
—		Strength	in	numbers:	Board	members	from	NRO,	
Backstage	Theatre,	and	Breck	Film	echoed	a	"rising	
tide"	principle	of	strengthening	all	arts	organizations	
collectively.	
	
2	
Strengthening Breck 
Create’s Role, Governance, 
and Long-Term Continuity	
	
—		Role	Definition:	BCA	was	originally	conceived	to	
coordinate	and	promote	the	arts	sector,	and	
stakeholders	across	nonprofit	partners.,	There	is	a	need	
for	a	clearer	definition	of	BCA’s	role	alongside	partner	
organizations.	
—		Simplifying	facility	agreements:	All	see	an	
opportunity	to	update	and	clarify	the	facilities	
agreements.	
—		Building	durability	into	governance:	Donors	and	
part-time	homeowners	recommended	the	plan	include	
defined	review	cycles,	clear	accountability	metrics,	and	
benchmarking	against	comparable	mountain-town	
peers	like	Park	City,	UT	and	Bend,	OR	so	that	
Breckenridge’s	arts	governance	carries	a	stable,	ten-
year	direction	through	future	Town	Council	transitions.	
—		Confirming	the	hybrid	model:	As	of	August	2026,	the	
Town	has	provisionally	accepted	SSG's	hybrid	shared-
Page 33 of 500

responsibility	recommendation	—	returning	facility	
maintenance	to	the	Town	and	evaluating	a	new	arts	and	
culture	manager	position.	
	
3	
Reimagining the Riverwalk 
Center and Arts District 
Facilities	
	
—		Expanding	capacity	for	today’s	touring	market:	
Donors,	volunteers	and	patrons	see	an	opportunity	to	
grow	the	Riverwalk	Center’s	seating	capacity	and	
refresh	its	seating	to	compete	for	a	broader	range	of	
touring	acts.	
—		Increasing	flexibility	and	everyday	use:	Stakeholder	
sessions	pointed	to	real	potential	in	reconfiguring	the	
venue	(additional	restrooms,	movable	walls)	and	
activating	the	complex	and	its	central,	town-owned	
footprint	more	consistently.	
—		Improving	accessibility:	Stakeholders	identified	an	
opportunity	to	improve	ADA	access	in	historic	Arts	
District	buildings,	including	the	elevator	and	ramp	at	
Old	Masonic	Hall.	
—		Increasing	visibility	of	existing	programming:	
Stakeholders	see	an	opportunity	to	raise	awareness	of	
the	strong	programming	already	happening	inside	Arts	
District	buildings	through	better	street-level	signage	
and	visibility,	so	that	even	long-time	residents	discover

—		Aligning	with	sustainability	goals:	All	facilities	
should	work	toward	decarbonization	plans	and	facility	
events	should	follow	sustainability	and	mobility	
recommendations	(such	as	leading	with	car	free	options

—		Activating	winter	capacity:	With	its	comparatively

cost	potential	in	adding	film	screenings	or	smaller	
productions	at	the	Riverwalk	Center	during	the	winter	
shoulder	season.	
—		A	county	film-venue	opportunity:	With	Dillon's	
movie	theater	closing,	Breck	Film's	Eclipse	Theatre	
stands	to	become	the	only	film	venue	in	the	county	—	
arts	organization	boards	flagged	this	as	a	significant	
opportunity	worth	planning	around.	
—		Consistent	public	hours	and	outdoor	art:	Proposed	
consistent	daily	public	hours	(for	example,	10am–7pm)	
with	free-access	components,	moving	artwork	outdoors,	
and	integrating	art	into	benches,	sidewalks,	and	storm	
drains,	and	want	the	campus	to	be	viewed	as	a	local	and	
regional	destination.	
—		Refreshing	wayfinding:	Updated	signage	at	Old	
Masonic	Hall	stands	out	as	a	straightforward	way	to	
help	visitors	discover	the	programming	already	
happening	inside	Arts	District	buildings.

Building One Shared

Clear Messaging

—		Modernizing	existing	tools:	Stakeholders	see	an

one	destination	—	website	and	physical	—	for	all	
events,	ticketing,	and	arts	information,	with

Town	of	Breckenridge	destinations	with	the	Arts	
District	as	a	main	example	(rather	than	branded	as	
Breck	Create).	
—		Reaching	visitors	at	the	point	of	booking:	Arts

Multigenerational

—		Balancing	destination	and	community

cultural	investment	as	shifting	between	resident-

Strengthening Support for

—		Growing	an	existing	coordination	table:	The	Youth

Summary 
Staff recommends that Council approve an ordinance concerning the refunding of certain outstanding 
certificates of participation (COP).  
 
Background  
In 2016, the Town of Breckenridge financed the Huron Landing Development with a Certification of Participation 
(COP) for $10,060,000, of which $4,575,000 is currently outstanding. The COP is callable as of December 2026 
and can be refunded on a tax-exempt basis up to 90 days prior. The refunding process would also allow us to 
potentially remove certain Town assets from being encumbered by leases related to the COPs and would result 
in savings to the Town. 
 
Public outreach/engagement 
The memo, ordinance and documents for the COP refunding are part of the work session packet and are available 
for public inspection through the second reading. No additional public outreach has occurred. 
Financial Implications 
The Town will only move forward with refunding if it creates financial savings for the Town. This savings will be net of 
any costs that would be incurred to complete the refunding. Staff will continue to monitor interest rates and only 
proceed if staff determines that appropriate savings can be achieved. Approval of the ordinance will constitute Town 
Council approval of the draft COP documents on file with the Town Clerk, allow for execution of the documents by the 
Town Manager and Finance Director, and allow for any minor changes that must be made in order for the COPs to be 
issued.  
 
Equity Lens 
Savings achieved by refunding the COP leaves more Town resources to meet other Town goals. 
 
Staff Recommendation 
Staff recommends the Council approve the ordinance concerning the refunding of certain outstanding 
certificates of participation on second reading.
