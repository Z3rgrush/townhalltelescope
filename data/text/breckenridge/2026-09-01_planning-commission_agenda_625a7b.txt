Subject: Comprehensive Plan Strategic Growth and Underutilized Parcels Density Analysis 
Work Session 
At the July 21 and August 4, 2026 work sessions, the Planning Commission reviewed staff’s 
preliminary screening of 186 underutilized candidate parcels and an initial identified Top 30 list of 
parcels that scored highly using the proposed analysis criteria for development/redevelopment as 
workforce housing. Previous discussions with the Planning Commission centered around the 
methodology for the underutilized parcel analysis and taking a deeper dive on specific parcels where 
future redevelopment might occur. The Commission previously agreed that the proposed analysis 
methodology for parcel ranking and identification was sound and also identified challenges for some 
parcels that need more consideration. Commissioners commented on site parameters such as 
current subdivision and lot size, density, parking constraints, and how redevelopment of certain sites 
or surface parking may preclude redevelopment of others. The Commission also showed interest in 
identifying parcels that are privately owned that may have scored lower within the analysis but where 
there could be opportunity for development for workforce housing through public/private partnerships.  
As the underutilized parcel map continues to be refined, the work session today will focus on three 
areas: the Conservation District, Breckenridge South (including Breckenridge Elementary), and Park 
Avenue. Staff would like specific input on these three areas to direct the appropriate future density 
should parcels within these areas be redeveloped for workforce housing. Please consult the attached 
worksheets for each of the three areas which show existing density, adjacent land uses, and identity 
the underutilized parcels under consideration for each area. This exercise is hypothetical and does 
not mean any particular parcel will be developed or upzoned to allow for more density. The goal is 
not to select parcel densities, but to frame later discussion of unit per acre (UPA) guidance, land use 
district (LUD) revisions, or area-specific direction. Input from this work session will help form goals 
and policy statements that may be included in the revised Comprehensive Plan. 
Story maps of the previous underutilized parcel analysis work sessions are linked here: Finding 
Room to Grow and Finding Room to Grow Part 2. 
Conservation District 
This area is subject to strict density limitations, historic design guidelines, and parking constraints 
found within the Handbook of Design Guidelines. These regulations, which are more stringent than 
rules applied outside the District, serve to protect the enduring character of the National Register and 
Local Historic Districts, along with a transitional buffer. Staff recommends allowing workforce housing 
infill to be considered on identified sites within the Conservation District while continuing to apply the 
Page 13 of 17
existing District density limitations. This is typically an allowed density of 9 UPA above ground for the 
Historic District and 12 UPA for the Transition areas. 
 
Breckenridge South  
Staff recommends evaluating an allowed density of 20 UPA for workforce housing on identified 
parcels in Breckenridge South. An allowance for 20 UPA reflects the higher-density residential 
pattern (generally ranging from 12 to 55 UPA) immediately surrounding Breckenridge Elementary 
while recognizing the transition to lower-density neighborhoods (generally ranging from 2 to 15 UPA) 
to the south and east.  
 
Park Avenue  
The Park Avenue Corridor is an appropriate area for higher-density workforce housing and staff 
suggests considering an allowed workforce housing density of 30 UPA for Commission discussion. 
The corridor is close to transit, jobs, service commercial, and existing higher-density development 
(generally ranging from 25 to 40 UPA) especially to the south and would be well suited for higher 
density development.  
 
Staff Comments 
The three identified areas above were generated by staff without considering the existing Land Use 
District boundaries. Their identification was instead based on geographic proximity, congruency in 
land use, and existing development. Some parcels within the identified areas may already be well 
served by existing LUD guidance that would allow a higher UPA. 
The existing density of parcels within the areas above may be better understood through the existing 
land use district guidelines, master plans, project-specific approvals, development agreements, or 
surrounding development patterns. Commission feedback will help identify where more analysis is 
warranted and how future density ranges or policy options should be structured.  
Questions for the Commission 
Staff requests high-level Planning Commission direction on the following questions: 
1. Does the Commission agree with the staff recommendation of applying the existing Conservation 
District density limitations to any workforce housing developments within the Conservation 
District? 
2. Does the Commission agree with the staff recommendation of an allowed workforce housing 
density of 20 UPA for the Breckenridge South area? 
3. Does the Commission agree with the staff recommendation of an allowed workforce housing 
density of 30 UPA for the Park Avenue corridor area? 
4. Are there other specific areas of Town the Commission thinks should be reviewed in the same 
way?
