Since 2023, the Town has had a Legislative Policy Statement  to guide legislative advocacy. In addition, 
the Town has a legislative committee to review state and federal legislation that may impact the Town. 
The committee, currently consisting of the Town Manager, Town Attorney, two Council members and the 
Sustainability & Parking Manager, meets regularly during the state legislative session. The committee is 
recommending a simplification of the policy statement, which is attached and described below.  
At the September 22, 2026 council meeting, Council considered a draft legislative policy statement and provided 
some feedback and comments. The revised legislative policy statement is attached for Council’s review, and, 
if acceptable, formal adoption at the evening meeting. 
L
egislative Policy Statement 
As discussed previously, t he Town’s legislative committee is recommending simplification of the policy 
statement to help the Town achieve its legislative goals without excessive process, while still providing guidance 
and parameters for advocacy. The policy supports advocacy tied to Council goals and generally aligning with 
the 2026-2027 Colorado Municipal League Policy Statement. The policy aims to streamline decision-making 
around legislative advocacy so that Council does not need to weigh in on every single item that comes up during 
the legislative session when support or opposition clearly falls within a previously adopted Council policy and/or 
the CML Policy Statement. Council will still receive updates from staff on legislative issues and items that fall 
outside of the policy or that present a potential conflict within the Town’s own policies will be referred to Council 
for discussion and direction. The changes to the draft policy that was reviewed on September 22nd have 
removed local and federal legislative issues from the list of “pre-authorized” matters that Council members and 
legislative committee members can take positions on without having to take those matters to the full Council 
each time. 
If Council supports the revisions and adopts the policy, Staff will monitor the amount and type of requests that 
the Town receives for action related to local and federal legislative issues and will keep Council informed of 
such requests on a regular basis.   
Recommendation 
Staff recommends that Council adopt the revised legislative policy statement by resolution.

Town of Breckenridge Legislative Advocacy Policy 
 
Introduction  
The Town of Breckenridge frequently engages in advocacy regarding federal, state, and 
local legislative and/or policy matters. In order to promote a cohesive and consistent 
approach to the Town’s advocacy, the Town has adopted this policy which will guide 
participation in legislative advocacy by Council members and staff.  
 
Authorization to Participate in Legislative Advocacy  
The Town has established a Legislative Policy Committee (“Committee”) which is 
comprised of at least one member of Town Council approved by the Mayor, but no more 
than three, the Town Attorney, and at least one member of the Town administration.  The 
purpose of the Committee is to receive input from internal and external stakeholders, 
attend meetings of advocacy groups in which the Town participates, and make 
recommendations on policy positions to Town Council as needed.     
The Committee meets regularly during the Colorado General Assembly’s Regular 
Session. The intent of the legislative advocacy policy is to provide advance 
authorization to the Committee and Council members to advocate and take policy 
positions on predetermined matters. For matters falling outside of those set forth below 
in this policy, the Committee will make recommendations to Town Council and present 
such recommendations to Town Council at its regular meeting(s).  Council members 
wishing to participate in legislative advocacy outside of those matters discussed in this 
policy may do so provided they are 1) not in direct conflict with an expressed position by 
Council and 2) make it clear that they are acting in their personal capacity and not on 
behalf of Council or the Town. Council members may also seek authorization from 
Council at a regular meeting to advocate on behalf of Council and/or the Town on a 
matter that is not covered in this policy. 
Action taken by a member of the Committee or a Council member under this policy may 
include signing position letters, providing hearing testimony, voting on policy positions 
with an outside advocacy group such as the Colorado Municipal League, or making 
contact with state legislators, etc.

Town of Breckenridge Legislative Advocacy Priorities 
The following legislative advocacy priorities are matters which Council and Committee 
members are authorized to take action on, as defined above, without additional Council 
approval or consideration.  When in doubt, Council input should be sought before a 
public position is communicated.  
1. Colorado Municipal League 2026-2027 Policy Statement. Generally speaking, 
the Town of Breckenridge has historically been aligned with the vast majority of 
CML policy positions as they pertain to legislation pending or introduced in the 
Colorado legislature. Unless CML has taken a position that would directly conflict 
with one of the Town’s other goals and priorities outlined below, Committee and 
Council members are authorized to take action consistent with CML’s policy 
positions and priorities during the 2026-2027 legislative session.  
2. Town of Breckenridge Council Goals: Town Council has adopted four strategic 
goals that are reflected in the Town’s Destination Management Plan (DMP): 
Deliver a balanced, year-round economy; Hometown Feel & Authentic Character; 
More boots and bikes, less cars; Leading Environmental Stewardship. These four 
goals are set forth in depth in the DMP.  Legislative advocacy at the state level 
that promotes one or more of these four goals, within the context and intent of 
the DMP, is allowed under this policy without further Council action.  
3. Other Advocacy Organizations: The Town of Breckenridge is member of or 
participates in several other political and legislative advocacy organizations. 
Council and Committee members are authorized to take action in concert with or 
as part of those other organizations on state legislation so long as such action is 
consistent with the Town of Breckenridge Council Goals.  
Local and Federal Issues 
Council and Committee members seeking to take political advocacy action on behalf of 
Town Council on local or federal legislative issues shall submit such matters to the 
Committee which will then evaluate the request and present it to Town Council at a 
regular or special meeting with a recommendation regarding the requested action.

Background 
The Colorado General Assembly, through Senate Bill 26-135, has referred Proposition NN to the November 3, 
2026 ballot. The proposition asks voters to increase K -12 public education funding by 2% each year for 10 
years without raising taxes. The additional funding would come from raising the existing Taxpayer’s Bill of Rights 
(TABOR) cap and dedicating those dollars to K-12 public education. The funding would be used to increase 
teacher pay, improve teacher retention, lower class sizes, and increase access to career and technical courses. 
The ballot language includes an independent audit requirement to show where the funds are being spent. 
Summary 
The bullets below summarize the intent and need for Proposition NN, based on publicly available information, as laid 
out in the attached resolution. 
• Over the next 10 years, proposed to increase K-12 education funding statewide, including for Summit School 
District RE-1, by approximately 30 percent, providing a longer-term, statewide complement to locally raised 
funding. 
• The State of Colorado currently underfunds public education by an estimated $4.1 billion annually, this 
shortfall has a significant detrimental impact on working families and rural and mountain communities such 
as Summit County, including within the Town of Breckenridge. 
• Proposition NN is intended to support smaller class sizes, competitive educator compensation, and expanded 
career and technical education opportunities for students across the state, including students in the Town of 
Breckenridge. 
Council has expressed support for the passage for Proposition NN which, if approved by voters, will increase K-12 
public education funding by 2% each year for 10 years without raising taxes. Additional funding would come 
from raising the existing Taxpayer’s Bill of Rights (TABOR) cap and dedicating those dollars to K -12 public 
education. The funding is intended to increase teacher pay, improve teacher retention, lower class sizes and 
increase access to career and technical courses.
