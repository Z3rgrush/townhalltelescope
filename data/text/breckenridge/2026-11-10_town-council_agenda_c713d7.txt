Summary 
With consultants from DJ&A, staff will present the results of the Town-wide parking study completed by DJ&A (and supported by 
Parkicity and Parkalytics) and seek Town Council direction on implementation priorities. The study evaluated parking utilization, 
visitor and employee parking needs, pricing structures, enforcement practices, and technology opportunities. The resulting action 
plan includes thirteen parking management strategies organized into six themes that focus on improving management of existing 
parking resources rather than constructing additional parking supply.  
 
The study concludes that Breckenridge does not have a parking supply shortage. Instead, Town has a logistical issue- parking 
demand is heavily concentrated in the downtown core while significant capacity remains available in peripheral parking facilities. 
The recommended strategies are intended to improve distribution of parking demand, enhance employee parking reliability, 
improve resident and visitor access, strengthen enforcement, and support future coordination opportunities with Breckenridge Ski 
Resort (BSR). 
 
Background  
In 2017, the Town implemented the paid parking and enforcement program currently in place. Aspects of the program have been 
modified over time to improve occupancy and utilization. At the time the program was implemented, the Town contracted 
Interstate Parking Company (IPC) to provide parking management services for the Town and has utilized their services since 
then. The Town has reassessed the agreement with IPC twice since 2017, going out to RFP in 2021 and signing an updated five-
year agreement in November of this year. In 2025, the Town retained DJ&A to complete a comprehensive parking study. The 
effort included an inventory of approximately 4,000 parking spaces operated by both the Town and Breckenridge Ski Resort, 84 
hours of drone-based occupancy counts, more than 1,000 visitor surveys, and 229 employee surveys. A review of permit 
programs operated by both the Town and Breckenridge Ski Resort was also conducted. 
 
The study recommends a comprehensive parking management strategy focused on improving utilization of existing parking 
assets rather than constructing additional parking supply. The proposed approach includes the following components: 
- Improve employee parking reliability – Employee survey responses consistently identified parking reliability as one of 
the most significant challenges facing the local workforce. While employee parking supply is generally sufficient 
systemwide, the current permit structure does not effectively match parking demand with available capacity. The study 
recommends transitioning from the existing district-based permit system to a more reliable and demand-responsive 
program that includes lot-specific employee permit lotteries, dedicated employee parking areas, and a lower-cost 
“economy” parking tier at facilities with excess capacity, such as the South Gondola Garage. These changes are 
intended to provide employees with greater certainty of finding parking while preserving access to parking near 
employment centers.  
- Increase turnover in high-demand core parking areas – Parking demand in the downtown core is concentrated in the 
highest-value parking areas, including Main Street and core off-street lots, where occupancy frequently approaches or 
exceeds practical capacity during peak periods. The study recommends implementing a coordinated parking hierarchy 
that prioritizes short-term customer and visitors access in these locations through updated pricing, time limits, and 
parking management practices. Recommended strategies include demand-responsive pricing, revised duration limits,

and improved occupancy monitoring to encourage turnover and ensure parking remains available for customers and 
visitors accessing downtown businesses.  
- Better utilize underused peripheral parking facilities – The study found significant underutilized parking capacity 
exists outside the downtown core, particularly at the South Gondola Garage, North French Lot, and other peripheral 
facilities. Future parking management efforts should focus on shifting long-term parking demand, employee parking, and 
lower-cost visitor parking to these locations while maintaining convenient access to downtown through transit, 
wayfinding, and improved customer information. This strategy seeks to maximize the value of existing parking 
infrastructure, reduce congestion in the core, and defer the need for costly parking expansion projects.  
- Strengthening enforcement and parking technology – The success of future parking management strategies will 
depend on consistent enforcement and modern technology tools. The study recommends upgrading License Plate 
Recognition (LPR) technology, enhancing payment system reliability, strengthening citation and collections processes, 
and implementing improved occupancy monitoring tools. These investments would improve operational efficiency, 
support future parking management initiatives, reduce customer service issues, and provide better data for decision-
making. The study also recommends maintaining a visible ambassador presence to assist visitors and support customer 
service objectives.  
- Enhance coordination with Breckenridge Ski Resort (BSR) – Given the significant role that BSR-owned parking 
facilities play within the community’s overall parking ecosystem, the study recommends expanded collaboration between 
the Town and BSR. Key opportunities include coordination on South Gondola Garage operations, employees parking 
programs, parking utilization data sharing, seasonal operating plans, enforcement practices, transit integration, and 
future parking planning efforts. The study identifies this coordination approach as critical to achieving the long-term goals 
of improving parking efficiency, employee access, and overall visitor experience throughout the community. 
 
In summary, the study concludes that Breckenridge’s primary parking challenge is not a lack of parking supply, but rather the 
need to better manage, distribute, and utilize existing parking resources. Recommended investments and policy changes focus 
on improving employee parking reliability, preserving convenient access to downtown businesses, leveraging underused parking 
assets, modernizing parking technology and enforcement systems, and strengthening partnerships with Breckenridge Ski Resort 
to create a more coordinated and effective community-wide parking program. 
 
Public Outreach/Engagement 
Public engagement included 1,036 out of state visitor (58%) and Colorado resident or local (42%) surveys collected across three 
seasons spanning December 2025 through July 2026, 229 employee surveys, and multiple rounds of parking occupancy 
analysis. Survey respondents consistently identified employee parking reliability, parking information, payment technology, and 
improved wayfinding as key priorities. 
 
Financial Implications 
Near-term recommendations primarily involve policy updates, enforcement enhancements, payment system improvements, and 
parking technology investments. Future capital needs may include new License Plate Recognition (LPR) technology, wayfinding 
signage, occupancy monitoring systems, and transit stop improvements. Determination of needs and budget estimating will take 
place between December 2026 and May 2027, with budget requests prepared for the 2028 budget season. Implementation is 
expected to improve utilization of existing parking assets and reduce pressure for future parking expansion. 
 
Equity Lens 
In seeking responses from all community members, including those who might otherwise be untapped or marginalized, the online 
survey allowed non-English speakers to voice their opinions. The recommendations from the parking study aim to improve 
access for employees, residents, and visitors through more predictable permit programs, affordable parking options, enhanced 
transit connections, and improved customer information systems. Taken together, these recommendations would help ensure a 
more reliable and equitable parking environment that would serve diverse audiences and community members.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The Breckenridge Parking Study assesses parking demand and utilization across multiple seasons, evaluates 
and recommends strategies to enhance parking management, and includes an implementation plan aligned 
with the Town’s goals. This study presents recommended parking management strategies, translating the 
findings of the Inventory Audit, three rounds of visitor and employee surveys, and six days of drone-based 
utilization counts into a focused, actionable set of recommendations. 
The consistent finding across the data sources is that Breckenridge does not have a parking supply shortage. 
Given this observation, recommendations focus on improving parking distribution and management. Central 
parking reservoirs such as Wellington, F Lot, Tiger Dredge, French Lot, East Sawmill, and Tonopah regularly 
operate at or above 95% utilization, while substantial peripheral capacity (most notably the 952-space South 
Gondola Garage, comprising 710 spaces in the structure and 242 in the adjacent surface lot, and the North 
French Lot) sits largely unused. The strategies detailed in this report aim to build on this conclusion and 
focus on redistributing demand and improving the approach to parking pricing, enforcement, and 
information dissemination. 
This report helps to inform the Town’s upcoming negotiations with Breckenridge Ski Resort (BSR) on 
parking, and several strategies in this report are aimed at better aligning the Town's and BSR's separate 
parking systems. 
The project team reviewed a longer list of candidate recommendations drawn from the Existing Conditions 
and Needs Assessments, as well as survey and utilization count efforts. The team then consolidated and 
prioritized the recommendations into thirteen strategies organized into six themes: 
• Employee Parking Permit Program: replacing the geography-based permit system with a tiered, 
demand-based structure and reallocating employee parking away from high-visitor-demand lots. 
• Program & Policy: adopting a coordinated parking hierarchy with curbside duration management, and 
introducing seasonal, demand-responsive operating plans. 
• Pricing & Revenue: shifting to variable, demand-responsive pricing in the core and resolving South 
Gondola Garage management and pricing. 
• Enforcement: strengthening fines and collections, upgrading license plate recognition technology, and 
adding targeted physical and access controls. 
• Technology & User Experience: deploying real-time occupancy information and wayfinding, updating 
the payment experience, improving digital wayfinding, and expanding EV charging and carpool 
incentives. 
• Transit & Wayfinding: improving transit amenities and physical conditions at peripheral and free lots. 
A companion Implementation Matrix and Action Plan, assigning lead departments, partners, order-of-
magnitude cost estimates, and phased timeframes to each strategy, supports moving the strategies into 
implementation.

1. INTRODUCTION AND PURPOSE 
The Town of Breckenridge is a year-round 
mountain destination that must balance the daily 
parking needs of residents and employees with 
substantial seasonal visitation tied to skiing, 
festivals, and outdoor recreation. In late 2025, 
the Town retained DJ&A to complete an updated, 
townwide Parking Study, with Parkicity and 
Parkalytics supporting parking management 
strategy development and drone-based parking 
data collection, respectively. 
The study timing coincides with the Town's 
upcoming negotiation of an updated parking 
agreement with Breckenridge Ski Resort (BSR). 
Because BSR independently owns, prices, and 
operates a significant share of parking in and 
around Town, a clear, shared, and current 
picture of how both the Town's and BSR's 
parking systems perform is a prerequisite for 
this negotiation. 
The study area includes the Breckenridge Main 
Street area, and additional lots located outside of 
the Main Street area, including Beaver Run/Peak 
9, the Ice Arena Lot, and the Free Skier Lot. The 
study evaluates core on-street parking areas, 16 
Town-managed off-street lots and garages, and 
BSR-managed parking facilities. The locations of 
study parking reservoirs are shown in Figure 1. 
The study's stated objectives are to: 
• Assess parking demand and utilization 
across seasons, 
• Evaluate and recommend strategies to 
enhance existing parking management 
operations and practices, 
• Develop an implementation matrix and 
action plan aligned with Town goals and 
objectives, and 
• Thoughtfully engage the public to develop 
context-specific strategies that meet the 
needs of residents, employees, and visitors 
alike.

2. STUDY APPROACH & DATA 
COLLECTION 
The strategies in this report are grounded in three connected work efforts, completed over the course of 
the study. These include the Inventory Audit, Visitor and Employee Surveys, and Parking Demand Data 
Collection. These work efforts are discussed below in more detail. 
Inventory Audit 
Completed in March 2026, the Inventory Audit catalogued 4,017 existing parking spaces (including 82 ADA 
and 30 EV spaces) across on-street downtown parking and 16 Town-managed off-street lots and garages, 
plus Beaver Run/Peak 9, the Ice Arena Lot, and the Free Skier Lot. It established the four-tier parking 
hierarchy (Skier Lots, Tiger Dredge & F Lots, Downtown Core Area, and Free Shuttle Lots) used to organize 
analysis throughout the remainder of the study. For the full Inventory Audit, see Appendix A. 
Visitor and Employee Surveys 
Visitor intercept and online surveys were conducted across three periods, with surveying taking place on 
six total days within each period: December 26, 2025–January 4, 2026; March 8–29, 2026; and June 24–
July 11, 2026. The surveys were administered in three distinct seasons, Winter, Spring, and Summer, to 
capture the varied parking demand and behaviors throughout the year. 
The survey (available both online and in-person to residents and visitors) drew 1,036 responses with an 
approximate 85% on-site response rate. Surveys were conducted in-person and online to capture the widest 
share of residents and visitors alike. There were two versions of the intercept survey, the first was “In-
Town” and the other “Free Skier” depending on where the survey was administered at a priced lot or a free 
lot. The two intercept surveys featured generally the same questions, but some wording was changed to 
ensure price-specific questions were asked appropriately. Table 1 shows the number of responses captured 
in each of the three seasonal survey periods.

Pricing, Regulatory Framework, and Funding 
Pricing varies by reservoir type: flat daily rates apply at the Skier Lots (aligned with all-day ski use), 
progressive/tiered hourly rates apply through most of the Downtown Core, and time-limited free parking 
applies at French Street and the Klack Placer Lot. Off-street parking is regulated under Title 9, Chapter 3 of 
the Town Code, which distinguishes a downtown Service Area from the rest of Town and requires certain 
commercial uses to either provide on-site parking or pay a fee in lieu. The Town's Parking and Transportation 
Fund (Title 3, Chapter 11) is funded by the Town's lift-ticket tax, parking revenue, grants awarded through 
the State of Colorado, and transfers from the excise fund, and supports transit operations, public parking 
facilities, and related mobility improvements through the Town's budget and capital planning process. 
Parking at the South Gondola Garage sits within a distinct set of agreements with BSR: a 2003 agreement 
under which BSR maintains a minimum of 2,500 parking spaces for winter recreation use across several 
properties; a 2012 Memorandum of Understanding integrating the Town's Free Ride transit system with 
BSR's services; and a 2019 fifty-year ground lease (with two optional ten-year extensions) under which BSR 
controls winter pricing and the Town controls summer pricing at the garage.

Enforcement 
Breck Park (operated by Interstate Parking) provides parking enforcement  under Title 7 of the Town Code. 
Enforcement is conducted using a mix of license-plate-recognition-equipped vehicles, handheld devices, 
and on-foot ambassadors, with enforcement timing deliberately varied to avoid predictable patterns. Static, 
camera-based LPR technology (currently installed only at the South Gondola Garage) cannot reliably track 
vehicle entries and exits, limiting both enforcement accuracy and occupancy monitoring. The current 
violation fee structure sets a $40 first offense fine for payment-required violations, escalating with repeat

offenses, with vehicles eligible for towing once they have three or more citations that are each 21 days or 
more unpaid. Enforcement also faces a structural gap: short of towing, there is no formal collections process 
for unpaid citations, leaving the Town with no effective mechanism to compel payment. 
BSR has a separate contract with Interstate Parking to provide enforcement for its lots, and issued 3,440 
citations at North Gondola, Beaver Run, Stables lower level, and the Gold Rush Lot over the 2025/26 season. 
Day-to-day enforcement practices closely resemble the Town's; what differs is that the two programs are 
contracted, administered, and reported separately. For scale, the Town issued 8,585 citations over the same 
period, so BSR accounts for a minority of total enforcement activity across the system. There is value in 
coordinating given the shared goal of managing the same broader pool of mountain-access parking demand.

Parking payment is managed through BreckPark.com, the Passport app, and Parkeon kiosks, supplemented 
by Honk Mobile's Reserve N' Ski reservation service. The BreckPark.com free-parking filter does not reflect

4. SUMMARY OF PARKING NEEDS 
Observed parking conditions point to a consistent set of needs related to parking management rather than 
a parking supply shortfall. The core finding, echoed across the utilization data and both surveys, is that 
Breckenridge's parking system faces challenges related to parking distribution. Several central facilities 
operate at or above practical capacity while peripheral capacity (principally the 952-space South Gondola 
Garage and the 63-space North French Lot) is underused. Key identified needs include the following: 
• Employee needs: reliable, affordable, and geographically flexible permit access that matches how 
employees work (including late shifts, midday returns, and multi-job or mobile work) and reduced 
conflict with visitor demand in the centrally located reservoirs currently used for employee parking. 
• Visitor and resident needs: better turnover in high-value core spaces, more reliable and flexible 
payment experience, real-time information before committing to a parking location, and peripheral 
lots attractive enough to use as viable alternatives to the downtown core. 
• Operational and revenue needs: pricing and enforcement structures that reinforce one another, 
consistent year-round management of the South Gondola Garage, and enforcement technology 
capable of supporting accurate occupancy monitoring. 
• BSR relationship and negotiation needs: a coordinated approach to South Gondola Garage 
management, permit programs, and enforcement between the Town and BSR, developed with an 
accurate shared picture of how each party's parking system currently performs, the central need 
behind the Town's ongoing renegotiation of its parking agreements with BSR. 
Future conditions add a further dimension to these needs. The Town's 2025 parking overview anticipates a 
net gain of approximately 280 guest parking spaces as Block 11 is redeveloped, with the Free Skier Lot 
developed as a partial replacement. Employee parking is expected to be retained in full but redistributed, 
primarily to the Gold Rush Lot, which formalizes 245 employee spaces as its capacity grows from 265 to 
400 spaces, while the North Gondola Lot's employee allocation falls from 200 to 40 spaces and the 
Administration (Peak 8) Lot loses its 120 employee spaces to the BGV development. With 35 employee 
spaces added at the Imperial Hotel (estimated completion in FY28), the employee-space total in Table 5 
holds at 499 because these gains and losses net to zero. 
The anticipated 50-space addition takes place on the guest side, prior to Colorado Mountain College parking 
considerations. New supply is anticipated from Colorado Mountain College (230 spaces as a conditional, 
shared-use overflow strategy). The Colorado Mountain College spaces are included in the post-construction 
totals shown in Table 5. Because the memorandum of understanding would limit use to weekends and 
holidays and permits the college to withdraw with notice, this capacity is treated as contingent rather than 
committed.

Improve management of overall parking demand while

The Town's parking supply has historically been organized around geography and legacy designations, 
district boundaries, individual lot history, and ad hoc permit arrangements. The mismatch between existing 
conditions and intended purpose is a key need that these strategies address.  
This section identifies a simplified, function-based network in which each parking reservoir, on-street 
segment, and employee lot have a clear assigned job: priced parking or free, employee or visitor, year-
round or seasonal. 
Visitor Parking 
The visitor parking network breaks into eight functional categories: 
• Core Off-Street: Wellington, Courthouse, the Exchange Lots, Ice House, Tiger Dredge, and most of 
F Lot and Tonopah Lot, the Town's highest capacity, most centrally located off-street facilities. 
Portions of F Lot and Tonopah Lot are proposed as employee parking (see Employee Parking 
below). 
• Skier (winter) / Fringe (summer): North Gondola Lot, the South Gondola surface lot, and Peak 9 
(Beaver Run), which shift function seasonally between ski-access parking and general fringe visitor 
parking.  
• Free, Time-Restricted Zone: French Street, French Lot, and Klack Placer Lot, managed as a single 
zone rather than per-space, so a vehicle cannot reset its free period by re-parking anywhere within 
the zone. 
• Free Skier Lot: The Free Skier Lot continues to operate as it does today. The Ice Arena Lot converts 
in part to employee parking under Strategy 1 (see Employee Parking below); the remainder 
continues as Ice Arena parking. Overnight parking would shift to the South Gondola Garage. 
• South Gondola Garage: The South Gondola Garage structure (710 spaces), being the largest 
parking reservoir, would host new uses, including paid overnight parking and economy employee 
parking. The garage structure would operate as a priced visitor release valve in the Winter and 
would continue to be free for all users in the summer. 
• Gold Rush Lot: The Gold Rush Lot was closed during the study period and has since reopened. 
Its role in this network is as a peripheral and special event parking  reservoir, anticipated to hold 
155 visitor spaces and 245 employee spaces post-construction (see Table 5). The limited use 
means the lot represents additional capacity that can absorb demand displaced by the Block 11 
redevelopment. 
• Core On-Street: Main Street from Watson Avenue to Park Avenue, the highest-priced parking in the 
system, reflecting its location and turnover priority. 
• Fringe On-Street: Main Street north of Watson, Ridge Street, Ridge Alley, Lincoln, Washington, and 
Adams Avenues, priced below Core On-Street to reflect lower demand.

Theme 1: Employee Parking Permit Program 
The Employee Parking Permit Program strategy redesigns the organization and pricing of 
employee parking, shifting from a fixed geographic system to a four-corner lottery structure 
built around demand and reliability. It frees up centrally located capacity for visitors while 
giving employees a permit that reliably delivers a space, including for late shifts and midday 
returns. 
Strategy 1: Consolidate Employee Parking into Four Lottery Lots and the  
South Gondola Garage  
Priority: High   |   Timeframe: Mid-term   |   Effort: High 
Existing Condition / Need 
The employee permit program is currently organized around three geographic districts (North, South, and 
East) each priced at a flat $100 per year regardless of demand, plus a set of lottery permits (South Gondola 
Employee at $500, Klack Placer at $100, Lower Exchange at $500, Courthouse at $300) and special permits 
for delivery, construction, and late-night use. Permit-to-capacity ratios show this geographic allocation does 
not track actual demand. The North permit is oversold at 2.2 permits per space, the South permit runs at 
1.3 permits per space, and at the opposite extreme the $500 South Gondola Employee permit sits at just 
0.03 permits per space. Fifty-four percent of permit holders report difficulty finding a space, and the single 
strongest theme in the employee survey's open-ended responses is a midday-return problem. Employees 
report that parking is available in the morning, but running an errand, taking lunch, or a shift change means 
coming back to a full lot and potentially having to pay for parking. 
This is most noticeable in the North French Lot, which averages around 50% maximum utilization while 
nearby, mixed employee/visitor lots run near capacity. This suggests that permits are spread too thinly 
across too many competing locations rather than concentrated where employees can reliably find a parking 
space.  
BSR issues its own 1,397 employee and tenant parking permits for its own mountain base lots, and today 
the two systems are not coordinated, which matters for any Breckenridge employee whose job requires 
access to both a Town-managed lot and a BSR-managed one. 
Rationale for Recommended Strategy 
Consolidating from 11 potential employee-eligible lots and street segments down to four dedicated lottery 
lots, one per corner of the core, plus the South Gondola Garage and Ice Arena Lot, concentrates demand 
into fewer, more clearly defined locations.  
Running the permit lottery for each corner lot independently, rather than as a single town-wide pool, gives 
the Town a year-over-year way to refine the number of permits each specific lot can support, while keeping 
a single permit price across all four lots. It is recommended that all four corner lots carry the same annual 
price, at $100 to $150 for a permit valid 365 days, so that permit cost does not vary by location. The South 
Gondola Garage and Ice Arena Lot permits, the Town's shared economy tier, would both be priced below 
the corner lots, at $50 to $100 per year, preserving their role as a more affordable, high-capacity fallback. 
Because both locations shift to free, registration-only access in summer, the lower rate reflects both a real 
savings and the fact that each facility is open to all users for half the year.

Within each lottery, priority may be given first to applicants with disabilities, including those holding a state-
issued disability placard or plate, so that employees in need of parking close to their place of employment 
have access before the remaining lottery permits are drawn. 
The initial permit-to-space ratio at each lottery lot is recommended to be set at a conservative 1.2-to-1, 
though the actual ratio for lottery lots may be closer to 1.5-to-1 to achieve practical capacity, depending on 
the employee reservoir. Utilization should be monitored on an ongoing basis, with permits adjusted 
incrementally as observed demand dictates, so that each employee reservoir settles at an average of 95% 
utilization. 
All lottery lot permits should also provide access to the shared economy tier in the South Gondola Garage 
(the uppermost uncovered level, 93 spaces) or the Ice Arena Lot (158 spaces, generally 6:00 AM to 5:00 
PM, matching East Sawmill Lot hours) when the lottery lots are at capacity. All remaining employees that 
do not receive a lottery permit would be eligible for a space in either location. 
This strategy provides a common foundation for several other employee-focused recommendations in this 
report, including the seasonal garage split (Strategy 5) and the hierarchy that governs how the lottery lots 
relate to surrounding visitor parking (Strategy 2). This new structure also provides an opportunity for the 
Town to coordinate with BSR on shared or cross-honored permit options for employees who need access 
to both systems.
