III) PUBLIC COMMENTS 
 
Teague Holmes spoke in support of the Tiger Road Rec Path to improve pedestrian and 
cyclist safety amidst growing traffic, while endorsing the Rose Trail and beginner-friendly 
options. Highlighting a gap in the local trail network, he urged BOSAC to consider more 
natural, technical, and steep trails that challenge advanced riders without relying on high-
speed jump features. He also advocated for expanding and directing riders toward designated 
downhill/gravity zones (like Slalom and Hard Luck) to meet high-level riding demand while 
reducing speed-related user conflicts on shared, bi-directional flow trails 
 
Jonathan Heckman proposed building a hub-and-spoke style trail network on Town-owned 
land at Little Mountain, taking advantage of nearby parking and facilities at the Ice Rink and 
Wakefield trailheads, the concept features an easy, green-graded climb trail leading to a hub 
that feeds 6–10 downhill trails spanning green to double-black difficulties. He emphasized 
that this would give local riders a legal, close-to-town space for technical progression, fill a 
major gap in Breckenridge's system, and help to separate high-speed downhill traffic from 
hikers to eliminate user conflicts. 
 
Kris Carlsted echoed previous comments, arguing that the nationwide trend of machine-built 
flow trails has become oversaturated and belongs in bike parks rather than natural settings. 
Preferring a return to traditional "hand-built" trails—citing Idaho Springs as a success model 
and local routes like Game Trail as inspiration—he advocated for natural, steep, and rocky 
fall-line terrain. He also endorsed the Little Mountain concept as an ideal testing ground for a 
hand-built network, noting that concentrating high-speed, technical riding into a dedicated, 
controlled area is the best way to satisfy expert mountain bikers while curbing user conflict 
elsewhere. 
 
Becky Walter supported previous speakers, emphasizing that while Breckenridge’s cross-
country network is world-class, the trail system is missing both ends of the spectrum: true

V) OPEN SPACE DISCUSSION 
 
Trails Workplan (0:27:50) 
 
Mr. Overlock led the Trails Workplan discussion, which focused on refining the Trails Matrix 
Work Plan—a draft tool designed to prioritize a multi-year “wish list” of potential and 
proposed trails. BOSAC commissioners debated whether the "Community Context" scoring 
criteria was too rigid, suggesting a change from "and” to "and/or" so specialized trails aren't 
penalized for not meeting every connectivity metric. There was also discussion about the 
similarity between the User Experience and Community Context columns and that they could 
perhaps be combined and the Community Context column could be relabeled as 
“Connectivity”.  Concerns were also raised over discrepancies between localized wildlife 
habitat data and on-the-ground observations in French Gulch, prompting the group to propose 
adding dedicated location assessment column to better evaluate real-world impacts and 
parking access. 
 
Working through the exercise, specific trail evaluations highlighted trade-offs between user 
experience, cost, and redundancy. A proposed Phase 1 of the Tiger Road Rec Path scored 
high for user experience and neighborhood connectivity (Vista Verde, Shores, Highland 
Greens, Wayside, Vic’s Landing, Ten Mile Vista and other neighborhoods in the area east of 
HWY 9), but low on management due to its $1M-per-mile cost, prompting debate over 
whether Open Space should fund construction of recpaths while Streets & Parks handles 
maintenance. Concepts like American Gulch to Lincoln Park received moderate scores due to 
redundancy with Forest Queen Road, and the Rose Trail sparked conversation over beginner 
safety and access constraints. 
 
Moving forward, BOSAC agreed that the matrix serves as a helpful discussion tool rather 
than a final decision-maker. To allocate resources effectively across competing projects, staff 
will adjust the matrix wording and consider developing a secondary prioritization tool. The
