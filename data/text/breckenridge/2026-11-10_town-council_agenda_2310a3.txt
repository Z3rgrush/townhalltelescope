Ba
ckground  
The 5.07 mill levy is the amount the Town is authorized to impose and, according to state law, cannot be 
increased without an election. This same mill rate has been consistently applied within the Town since 2014. 
For the 2027 budget year, we are forecasting the 5.07 mill levy to result in property tax revenues of $5.48M. 
To
wn Council Goals 
The proposed mill levy is a critical tool to help achieve Town Council’s long-term goals. By securing additional 
funding, we can invest in key areas such as public works, recreation, community development and public 
safety—aligning with our commitment to responsible stewardship of resources. This levy supports the council’s 
vision of a thriving, resilient town by ensuring it remains a place that visitors return to and residents can live and 
work. Through this measure, we can meet today’s needs while planning for tomorrow’s opportunities, in line with 
our values of adaptability, innovation, and community. 
Fi
nancial Implications 
The 5.07 mills are for the purpose of defraying expenses within the General Fund.  
Eq
uity Lens 
As we consider the mill levy proposal, we are mindful of our commitment to fostering a community that values 
diversity, equity, and inclusion. This initiative aligns with our core values by ensuring that all residents, 
regardless of background or socioeconomic status, can benefit from the enhanced services funded by the mill 
levy. By prioritizing equitable resource distribution and inclusive decision-making, we aim to strengthen our 
community fabric and create opportunities for all to thrive. This commitment will guide both the planning and 
implementation phases, ensuring that no group is left behind. 
Sta
ff Recommendation 
We recommend that Town council approve the mill levy at 5.07 mills on second reading.

Summary 
Staff seek direction on  three ordinances authorizing consistent leases with Little Red Schoolhouse, Carriage 
House Early Learning Center, and Timberline Learning Center, at first reading. The leases create a consistent 
framework for Town- owned childcare facilities, clarify maintenance and financial responsibilities. These leases  
support of the Town Council priority of ensuring long-term, affordable, quality childcare for the local workforce in 
the community. 
 
Background  
One of the Town Council’s Priorities for 2026-2028 is to support childcare. One goal under that priority is to provide 
for the long-term future of affordable, quality childcare for our workforce with a task to secure Town ownership of 
all childcare centers’ infrastructure and aim for a consistent lease for all centers. These leases meet that goal. 
Little Red Schoolhouse (LRSH): The building was constructed by the Town and entered into a land lease in 2007 
when the Town paid off the mortgage for the center (original lease 2005). LRSH’s ground lease ends 2035, after 
which the Town would take ownership of the building. The center desires to renegotiate the lease to have the 
Town take ownership of the building prior to 2035, in conjunction with the construction of proposed addition to the 
building. The Surrender and Acceptance Agreement proposed separately from the lease, would surrender all 
LRSH interest in the improvements and would have the Town accept ownership of the improvements effective 
December 31, 2026. At that time the Town will be the fee simple owner of both the property and the improvements. 
This enables the Town and LRSH, Inc to execute a new lease for both the property and the improvements. 
 
Timberline Learning Center: The center was constructed by the Town in 2008. As the newest center, the existing 
lease is more updated compared to others however, staff have worked to further refine roles and responsibilities 
within the center. 
 
Carriage House: The building was constructed by the Town and Carriage House selected as the operator since 
1995. Carriage House has been under a land lease with the Town since 2007, after the Town paid off the 
mortgage for them in an effort to financially stabilize the center. The current lease amendment executed in 2025 
has the lease term ending December 31, 2026 at which time, the Town will take ownership of the building. 
 
Primary changes since October 27 Town Council work session: 
• Added language on maximum enrollment goal (Section 4.3 Childcare Center Utilization). 
Page 10 of 61
• Added language requiring Tenants to contract with a reputable snow removal company and ensure that 
snow removal is done in a workman like manner, utilizing industry-standard care and equipment to avoid 
damages to the Leased Premises, including but not limited to the asphalt, concrete, curbs,  sidewalks, 
landscaping and utility structures (Section 4.7 Snow Removal). 
• Modified date of  annual budget submittal from June 1 to July 15 (Section 11.1 Landlord’s Right To 
Approve Tenant’s Annual Budget). 
• Added language to emphasize a collaborative process in the annual budget review. Should the Town 
and Tenant not come to a mutual agreement on the budget by December 31 of the year in which the 
budget would be adopted, Landlord, in its discretion, may convert this Lease into a month-to-month lease. 
Previously presented language had the Tenant’s operation ceasing which has been deleted.  (Section 
11.1 Landlord’s Right To Approve Tenant’s Annual Budget). 
 
Primary aspects of the lease (5-year term): 
Tenant Responsibilities 
• Rent $1,200 per month. 
• Maintain all required licenses (Town and childcare licensure). 
• Participate in tuition assistance programs. 
• Provide best efforts to achieve the maximum allowable number of children. 
• Pay all utilities and any taxes associated with tenant’s possessory interest. 
• Maintain non‑structural portions of the building, landscaping, and playground. 
• Handle regular repairs and maintain a contracted HVAC provider. 
• Keep janitorial services, reputable snow removal, and security services in place. 
• Maintain separate account for Town‑provided maintenance funds. 
• Provide quarterly financial reports which will assist the Town in knowledge of fiscal health of centers 
and sustainable centers overall. 
 
Landlord Responsibilities 
• Replace structural elements and major building systems based on Town ProForma, per Exhibit A. 
• Facilities Division to conduct quarterly maintenance inspections, report needed repairs to center to 
address. 
• Provide annual $15,000 maintenance subsidy starting in 2027; unused funds may need to be returned. 
 
Public outreach/engagement 
Staff worked directly with the three childcare operators in developing the consistent lease terms. No broader 
public outreach was conducted because the agreements concern the management and operation of Town 
property.  
 
Financial Implications 
Beginning in 2027, the Town will provide $15,000 annually to each of the three centers for routine maintenance, 
for a total of $45,000 per year, subject to annual appropriation. The Town will collect $1,200 per month from each 
center, totaling $43,200 annually. Rent remains fixed during the lease term to allow the centers to adjust to the 
revised maintenance and reporting model. 
 
The Town will continue to be responsible for qualifying structural repairs and scheduled replacement of major 
systems per Exhibit A of the lease. These costs will be addressed through the Town’s facility planning and annual 
budget process.  
 
Equity Lens 
The leases support access and safe environment for high-quality childcare for families across income levels and 
support the local workforce with subsidized rent and services . A consistent lease framework sets expectations 
and Town support to the three operators while requiring  participation in tuition assistance programs  and 
sustainable fiscal practices to ensure stable childcare.

(“Landlord”) and _______________________, a Colorado nonprofit corporation (“Tenant”). 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Breckenridge owns numerous buildings and/or land that it leases to 
various child-care providers as part of a community-wide effort to ensure quality child-care is 
available and affordable to local families; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Breckenridge participates in a county-wide intergovernmental 
agreement that functions to subsidize child-care costs for local families in the form of tuition 
assistance; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Breckenridge leases its properties to child-care providers at below-
market rates in order to further subsidize the provision of child-care services in the community; 
and 
 
WHEREAS, in 2026 the Town of Breckenridge and the child-care providers agreed to a one-year 
pilot agreement whereby the Town provided funding and a schedule for routine building 
maintenance items that would be performed by the child-care providers; and 
 
WHEREAS, the parties agree that it is in their mutual best interest to incorporate the building 
maintenance items and lease provisions into one document to provide the most clarity and 
consistency for all involved; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Breckenridge, as landlord, and the undersigned child-care provider as 
tenant acknowledge the unique provisions underlying this Lease and desire to enter into this 
Lease under the specific terms and conditions set forth herein.

Summary 
Staff is requesting the Council approve the attached Little Red Schoolhouse Surrender and Acceptance 
Agreement which will transfer ownership of the improvements at 600 Reiling Road to the Town. Improvements 
include the building as well as anything fixed to the land. It does not include the personal 
property/contents/furniture unless permanently fixed to building. 
 
Background  
The Little Red Schoolhouse (LRSH) in Breckenridge has served the community in its current location (600 
Reiling Road) since 2005 as a licensed early childhood education facility. A ground lease was executed in 2007 
by the Town and LRSH, Inc. (Tenant) and pursuant to that lease, the Town retained ownership of the land and 
the Tenant retained ownership of the building/improvements that they constructed. That ground lease is set to 
expire in 2035, at which time it could be renewed, or the ownership of the improvements could transfer to the 
Town thru a Surrender and Acceptance Agreement.  
 
With the upcoming addition and remodel of the building, and with a new standardization of the other childcare 
facility leases, both the Tenant and the Town believe it is in the best interest of both parties to execute the 
Surrender and Acceptance Agreement early. Under this Agreement, the Tenant surrenders all interest in the 
improvements and the Town accepts ownership of the improvements effective December 31, 2026. At that 
time, the Town will be the fee simple owner of both the Property and the Improvements. This Agreement will 
enable the Town and LRSH, Inc. to then execute a new lease for both the Property and the Improvements, and 
this new lease will be in the Town’s new format, specifically created for childcare centers throughout Town. This 
transfer will not affect the Little Red Schoolhouse remodel/addition project which is currently underway because 
the change of ownership was already contemplated with the project. The execution of the new lease, which will 
replace the current ground lease, will be presented to the Council under a separate legislative item on 
November 10th and 24th because a multi-year property lease requires a two hearing ordinance process. 
 
Public Outreach/engagement 
Staff has been working with LRSH and two other centers to create a consistent framework and clarify the Town’s 
support to the Centers through new lease agreements. Staff has coordinated with the centers and their boards of 
directors on the new lease format. 
 
Financial Implications 
The Town contemplated ownership of the Property and the Improvements in the 2027 Budget.

Equity Lens 
Transfer of ownership of the LRSH building will allow the Town to continue supporting childcare within 
Breckenridge which remains an important community need.  
 
Staff Recommendation 
Staff request Council approve the attached resolution authorizing the execution of the Surrender and 
Acceptance Agreement as presented. Staff will be available at the meeting to answer any questions.

Summary 
Staff seek Council approval for an Intergovernmental Agreement with the Town of Silverthorne to share 
Breckenridge’s additional affordable housing units with the Town of Silverthorne to satisfy Silverthorne’s Prop 
123 unit count requirement. 
 
Background  
Proposition 123, which was codified in 2022, requires participating jurisdictions to increase their affordable 
housing stock in order to be eligible for a variety of grant opportunities. For the initial three-year cycle (2024-
2026) the Town of Breckenridge was required to provide 18 affordable housing units by December 31, 2026. 
The Town of Silverthorne was required to provide 16 affordable housing units. The Town of Breckenridge ended 
up providing 40 qualified affordable housing units during the eligible time period while the Town of Silverthorne 
was only able to provide 8 qualified affordable housing units, due to Smith Ranch being outside the eligible time 
period. Instead those previously constructed Smith Ranch units resulted in a higher required commitment. 
 
It should be noted that during that time the Town of Breckenridge actually added over 300 units (Vista Verde II, 
Larkspur, Stables, and Miller Flats/Riverfront) but in order to count towards the commitment the units needed to 
meet the state’s definition of Affordable Housing, specifically for sale units under 100% AMI or rental units under 
60% AMI. The Town’s qualified units were Stables Village Townhomes and Miller Flats Condos. Excess units 
provided during the three-year period are not eligible to be rolled to the following cycle but are allowed to be 
shared with other participating communities. The Town of Breckenridge is offering to share 8 of the 22 eligible 
units in the Miller Flats development with the Town of Silverthorne to satisfy their commitment requirements. In 
exchange, the Town of Silverthorne is offering the Town of Breckenridge the right of first refusal on any surplus 
units provided by the Town of Silverthorne during the 2027-2029 cycle, if needed. 
 
Public Outreach/engagement 
None 
 
Financial Implications 
None 
 
Equity Lens 
The Town of Silverthorne has substantially committed to the supply of affordable housing in Summit County, 
and the Town of Breckenridge remains committed to supporting affordable housing. This intergovernmental 
Page 49 of 61
agreement will allow the Town of Silverthorne to remain eligible for the next Prop 123 funding cycle and 
encourage continued investment by Silverthorne in local workforce housing. 
 
Staff Recommendation 
Staff request Town Council review the attached intergovernmental agreement, provide any additional direction 
or revisions regarding the documents, and then take action to approve the attached resolution which authorizes 
the execution of the attached IGA.
