LETTER OF SUPPORT; CRESTED BUTTE LAND TRUST; ELK MOUNTAIN AND TAYLOR PARK 
WATERSHED AND ACCESS PROJECT FOR THE LAND AND WATER CONSERVATION FUND 
(LWCF): Crested Butte Land Trust (CBLT) Conservation Manager Rachel Sabbato and CBLT Executive 
Director Jake Jones were present for discussion. 
 
Conservation Manager Sabbato explained that the Land and Water Conservation Fund through the U.S. 
Forest Service has an allocation of money that they are looking for projects  to fund. This project was 
scheduled last spring but was on hold until this June. They currently have a portfolio of 17 parcels, and the 
plan is to cluster the parcels onto this application which will then be purchased and go back to public lands

approach. Moved by Commissioner Houck, seconded by Commissioner Smith to approve the letter of 
support presented this morning  for the Crested Butte Land Trust  on the Elk Mountain and Taylor P ark 
Watershed Access Project for the Land and Water Conservation Fund  and authorize the signature of the 
full Board on the letter. Motion carried unanimously.

UNSCHEDULED PUBLIC COMMENT:  Due to the amount of people in the room, Commissioner Puckett 
Daniels explained the unscheduled public comment procedur e, promoted an area of safety and respect, 
and thanked the public for joining today.  
 
Numerous community members expressed concerns and apprehension about the Sheriff’s Office working 
with the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), which they believe has led to about 60 people 
being taken from the community. Residents shared stories about friends and family disappearing after 
being released from the jail, and said this has made immigrants afraid to call the police, go to the doctor, 
or even show up to public meetings. They asked the county to stop cooperating with  ICE, be more 
transparent, review things like license plate reader cameras, and take steps to make Gunnison feel safe 
and welcoming again.  The speakers that made comments were Joe Burgess, Laurie Gery, Ever Morrow , 
Salif Mahamane, Kelly Jo Clark, Kate Clark, Ren Hoskin, Henry Meier, John Czernicki, Nancy Gruber-Meier, 
Laura Guccione, Ian Wrisley, James Milski, Charlie Doan, Danica Ramgoolam, Talie Morrison, Tom Stompf, 
Marcia Collins, and Marketa Zubkova. 
 
Commissioner Puckett Daniels thanked everyone for attending today and the respected conducted during 
the meeting.  
 
Sheriff Adam Murdie emphasized the position he is in  between his humanity, his job, and doing the best 
thing he can for community. He expressed that he fears that not cooperating with ICE will cause them to 
run rampant in the community like elsewhere in the country . However, he announced that  the Sheriff’s 
Office will not cooperate with ICE any longer and hopes that his fears do not come to fruition.  
 
Undersheriff Josh Ashe clarified they have not done anything that has not been legally in their bounds to 
do, and they do adhere to community safety. However, he does not feel that the Sheriff’s Office has catered 
to ICE in any way that has not occurred across the country. Lastly, he asked not to be branded based on 
his uniform and job.  
 
Commissioner Smith emphasized the courage shown by the public who spoke on this topic today and the 
importance of taking care of each other’s humanity. She also acknowledged the tremendous work of the 
Sherrif’s Office to keep the community safe and reiterated how the uniform is very complicated for some 
people. She agreed with every sentiment that was stated and she will continue participating in Welcoming 
Week, as she has since its inception, as well as attending the vigil.  
 
Commissioner Houck reiterated gratitude to the community  and relayed that the Board is not immune to 
what is going on . He emphasized thanks to the Sheriff’s Office for their communication s and the great 
relationship they have with the Board . Commissioner Houck expressed the Board’s commitment to listen 
by stating that in many communities, they have the elected officials look down on the people from a dais, 
whereas here in Gunnison County  it is a table for people to come speak to them as equals . Lastly, he 
emphasized he continues to maintain hope over the fact that there are people willing to stand up for others 
who are afraid to speak. He shared Sheriff Murdie’s concerns and stated he will continue to balance the 
line between standing  up for what the  community believes and not shining a spotlight on  them for the 
Federal Government to push harder than they already have.  
 
Commissioner Puckett Daniels noted the last 18 months has had turmoil and w hile she has moral clarity, 
she also is pragmatic and worries on how to keep the people safe. She condemns the conduct of ICE 
throughout the country and relayed that ICE is not doing law enforcement, and she sees the conflation of 
the two roles. Her commitment to Gunnison County is to continue to engage with compassion and respect, 
to listen, and facilitate an ongoing dialogue with  County staff,  the community , the immigrants in the 
community and the Sheriff’s Office.

A RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF GUNNISON COUNTY, 
COLORADO, SUPPORTING THE SUBMITTAL OF A COLORADO FEDERAL LANDS ACCESS 
PROGRAM APPLICATION FOR THE GUNNISON COUNTY CUMBERLAND PASS 
REHABILITATION PROJECT AND COMMITTING TO PROJECT IMPLEMENTATION, MATCHING 
FUNDS, AND LONG-TERM MAINTENANCE 
 
WHEREAS, Gunnison County is a political subdivision of the State of Colorado and an eligible applicant for 
the Colorado Federal Lands Access Program (“FLAP”); and 
 
WHEREAS, the Federal Lands Access Program provides funding for transportation facilities that are located 
on, are adjacent to, or provide access to Federal lands; and 
 
WHEREAS, Gunnison County owns, operates, and/or maintains public road facilities that provide access to 
Federal lands and Federal land recreation destinations within and around Gunnison County; and  
 
WHEREAS, Gunnison County seeks Colorado FLAP funding for the Gunnison County Cumberland Pass 
Rehabilitation Project (“Project”), which would rehabilitate approximately 40 miles of County Road 76 and 
Forest Service Road 765 from Parlin and over Cumberland Pass to the Taylor Park Trading Post; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Project is eligible for Colorado FLAP funding because it will improve public transportation 
access to high -use Federal recreation destinations, historic sites, cultural resources, campgrounds, 
trailheads, fishing and boating areas, dispersed r ecreation sites, and extensive motorized and non -
motorized recreation networks; and 
 
WHEREAS, Gunnison County intends to coordinate with the U.S. Forest Service and historic corridor 
communities, including Parlin, Ohio City, Pitkin, and Tincup, to design and construct a comprehensive 
rehabilitation of the corridor; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners finds that the Project advances Gunnison County’s goals 
for safe transportation access, rural connectivity, Federal lands access, recreation access, historic 
preservation, emergency preparedness, and long-term infrastructure stewardship. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of Gunnison County, Colorado, 
that: 
 
1. Support for Application . The Board of County Commissioners hereby supports the submittal of a 
Colorado Federal Lands Access Program application for the Gunnison County Cumberland Pass 
Rehabilitation Project.  
 
2. Authorization to Submit. The Board authorizes the County Manager, Public Works Director, or other 
authorized County representative to submit the FLAP application and any required supporting 
materials, certifications, forms, or assurances necessary for consideration of the Project.  
 
3. Project Implementation Commitment. If awarded, Gunnison County commits to working with the 
Federal Highway Administration, the Colorado FLAP Programming Decisions Committee, the U.S. 
Forest Service, and other project partners to advance the Project through design, environmental 
review, right-of-way, permitting, construction, and closeout, as applicable.  
 
4. Matching Funds Commitment. Gunnison County intends to provide matching funds in either cash 
or in-kind work for the Project in an amount to be determined and approved by the County, subject 
to final project scope, cost estimate, award amount, and available County budget appropriat ions. 
 
5. Maintenance Commitment. Gunnison County commits to provide sufficient maintenance funds for 
the life of the constructed facility.  
 
6. Authority to Execute Documents . The Board authorizes the Chair of the Board of County 
Commissioners, County Manager, Public Works Director, or other authorized County representative 
to execute any documents necessary to effectuate the purposes of this Resolution, subject to 
review and approval as to form by the County Attorney where required.  
 
7. Effective Date. This Resolution shall be effective immediately upon adoption.
