appeal was filed incorrectly unknowingly.

the petition for abatement for refund of taxes for 719 Prospect CB LLC for the year 2024.  Motion 
carried unanimously.

A RESOLUTION APPROVING LUC-25-00043 A LAND USE CHANGE PERMIT APPLICATION FOR 
A MINOR IMPACT PROJECT FOR BARKMAN SUBDIVISION FILING NO. 2 
 
WHEREAS, Jesus Lopez (the “Applicant”), submitted an application for a Land Use Change Permit, 
designated LUC-25-00043 (the “Application”), to Gunnison County, Colorado (the “County”), proposing a 
Major Impact review of the Barkman Subdivision Filing No. 2  on Parcel No. 3701- 000-07-001, an 18.81 -
acre parcel located approximately 1-mile north of the City of Gunnison, Gunnison County, Colorado, legally 
described as Lot 1, Barkman Subdivision (the “Property”); and  
 
WHEREAS, the Applicant, Jesus Lopez, proposes to subdivide the Property into 5 residential lots (“Project”) 
as follows:  
- Lot 1 (14.68 acres, existing development)  
- Lot 3 (1.05 acres)  
- Lot 4 (1.02 acres)  
- Lot 5 (1.02 acres) 
- Lot 6 (1.04 acres); and  
 
WHEREAS, The Project would be classified as a Major Impact Project based upon Gunnison County Land 
Use Resolution Section 7-101:A More Than Four Units, because the application is for a five-lot subdivision. 
The Applicant requested an Impact Classification Reduction from a Major Impact to a Minor Impact. On 
February 19, 2026 the Planning Commission granted the applicant an Impact Classification Reduction from 
a Major Impact to a Minor Impact; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Application was referred for comment to the Colorado Geological Survey (“CGS”), Colorado 
Division of Water Resources (“DWR”), City of Gunnison, Gunnison Conservation District, Gunnison County 
Fire Protection District (“GCFP”) and Gunnison County Public Works; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Planning Commission held work sessions on February 19 and June 4, 2026, and conducted 
a site visit on June 4, 2026, to review and consider the Application; and  
 
WHEREAS, a public hearing, with notice as required by LUR Section 3-112, was held before a joint meeting 
of the Planning Commission and Board of County Commissioners  
(the “Board”) on August 6, 2026, at which time staff reports, applicant testimony and all submitted 
documents were considered; and  
 
WHEREAS, at the August 6, 2026  hearing, there was a motion by Commissioner Baca and second by 
Commissioner Adkins to recommend approval of the Minor Impact Project in a 5 to 0 vote resulting in a 
recommendation of approval being forwarded from the Planning Commission to the Board; and  
 
WHEREAS, at the ________ meeting, the Board has considered the Application, the staff report, the 
applicant and the entire record in this matter; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Board finds the following:  
1. Pursuant to LUR Section 6-103: Standards of Approval for Minor Impact Projects, the Board 
concludes:  
a. A. Compliance with all Applicable Standards. The Application has addressed, and the 
Board has evaluated this submittal for, its integration of the standards of the LUR .  
This also includes standards as identified within LUR Article 10, Locational Standards; 
Article 11, Resource Protection Standards; Article 12, Development Infrastructure 
Standards; and Article 13, Project Design Standards, which are analyzed in Exhibit A. 
Staff Report and Exhibit B. Plat.  
 
b. B. Compatibility with Community Character . The Applicant has submitted sufficient 
evidence to demonstrate that the Project shall be compatible with, or an enhancement 
of, the character of existing land uses in the area, and shall not adversely impact the 
future development of the surrounding area. The proposed subdivision is compatible 
with the surrounding community because it extends an existing pattern of residential 
development along County Road 14 while maintaining a transition between the more 
urban development adjacent to the City of Gunnison and the agricultural character of 
the adjacent Van Tuyl Ranch. The proposed lots will be served by centralized sewer 
infrastructure, consistent with the availability of urban services in the area, and the 
one-acre lot sizes are generally compatible with nearby residential development.  
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of the County of Gunnison, 
Colorado, that no additional public hearing on the Application need be conducted by the BOCC, and further, 
the BOCC hereby APPROVES the Application for LUC -25-00043, Barkman Subdivision Filing No. 2 Minor 
Impact Project. We provide the following notations for the Applicant’s referral.

RESOLUTION NO. 2026-36 
 
A RESOLUTION AMENDING THE GUNNISON COUNTY LAND USE RESOLUTION  AND 
AMENDING RESOLUTION NO. 25-19 SCHEDULE OF FEES FOR THE COMMUNITY 
DEVELOPMENT DEPARTMENT PERMIT APPLICATIONS 
 
WHEREAS, the Gunnison County Board of County Commissioners (“BOCC”) Strategic Plan, revised June 16, 
2026, includes the following goal to develop a licensing program for short -term rentals (STRs): “By 
December 31, 2026, Gunnison County will implement licensing procedures for short-term rentals”; and 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to the Gunnison County Land Use Resolution (“LUR”), Section 1-113: Amending this 
Land Use Resolution  details a process for initiation, review, and BOCC action on amendments to the 
Resolution; and  
 
WHEREAS, LUR Section 3 -108:C. Application and Review Fees states: “Application and review fees are 
intended to cover the County’s costs in determining the compatibility of a proposed development with the 
land use policies and development guidelines contained in this Resolution. If the BOCC determines that the 
cost of review is likely to exceed such fees substantially, the BOCC may, after consultation and discussion 
with the applicant, assess an additional fee. Such additional fee shall be set in an amount that will, as far 
as can be determined, cover the costs of review  of the application including reasonable administrative 
expenses, additional professional expertise, and overhead expenses.”; and

WHEREAS, Gunnison County has the authority to license and regulate short -term rentals pursuant to 
Colorado Regulatory Statute (CRS) Sec. 30-15-401(s)(I); and 
 
WHEREAS, at a work session on February 5, 2026, the Gunnison County Planning Commission 
recommended approval to the BOCC regarding the proposed amendments; and  
 
WHEREAS, the BOCC has conducted a duly noticed public hearing on these proposed amendments on April 
7, 2026; and  
 
WHEREAS, the following summarized public testimony was received: 
 
• Public comment generally supports establishing a short-term rental licensing program. 
 
• Distinguish STRs from activities such as home exchanges or house sitting to avoid unintended 
regulation.  
 
• Require inspections for public health, safety and welfare. 
 
• Concerns regarding traffic safety on rural roads, access via easements, trespassing, and site -
specific hazards such as steep terrain or proximity to agricultural operations.  
 
• Establish performance-based review standards for each STR license. 
 
• Applicability of fee structure and properties with existing land use approvals. 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to LUR Section 1-113, the BOCC have evaluated the proposed amendments and found 
the amendments comply with the following criteria: 
 
1. CONSISTENCY WITH ANY COMPREHENSIVE PLAN ADOPTED BY GUNNISON COUNTY.  
 
The proposed STR license regulations are consistent with the BOCC Strategic Plan, revised June 
16, 2026, goal to develop a licensing program for short-term rentals: “D. DELIVER HIGH-QUALITY 
SERVICES: 1. By December 31, 2026, Gunnison County will implement licensing procedures for 
short-term rentals”. 
 
2. CHANGED CONDITIONS.  
 
While STRs are not a new use, the LUR does not currently have a definition for STRs nor any 
mechanism for tracking these uses within unincorporated Gunnison County. The proposed STR 
license regulations respond to how owners use their properties within a ru ral resort gateway 
county.  
 
3. EFFECT ON THE NATURAL ENVIRONMENT.  
 
The draft regulations include recommendations for verification that the STR complies with 
applicable regulations, including the LUR, the Gunnison County On -Site Wastewater Treatment 
System Regulations, and the adopted building code in use at the time of building construction.   
 
4. COMMUNITY NEEDS. 
 
The proposed STR license regulations require an applicant establish an Owner’s Agent who shall 
be available 24 hours per day, 7 days per week to respond to complaints, issues of concern, and 
violations related to this Regulation. This will create accountability for STR owners and their guests. 
 
5. DEVELOPMENT PATTERN.  
 
The proposed STR license regulations are not anticipated to impact development patterns within 
unincorporated Gunnison County.  
 
6. CHANGES IN APPLICABLE LAW.  
 
Gunnison County has the authority to license and regulate short-term rentals pursuant to Colorado 
Regulatory Statute (CRS) Sec. 30-15-401(s)(I), created by Colorado House Bill 20-1093 which went 
into effect March 23, 2020.  
 
7. PUBLIC HEALTH, SAFETY AND WELFARE.
