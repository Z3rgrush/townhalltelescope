MEMORANDUM 
 
 
Date: September 21, 2026 
To: Mayor and Town Board 
From: Omar Herrera, Director of Engineering 
Scott Pearson, Transportation Engineer 
Re: Crossroads/Colorado Blvd. Roundabout Construction Phasing Plan - O. Herrera, Director of 
Engineering; S. Pearson, Transportation Engineer 
Item #: 1. 
 
Background / Discussion: 
Construction is underway on the Crossroads Boulevard and Colorado Boulevard Roundabout project. 
The original construction phasing was developed to maintain two-way traffic on Crossroads Boulevard 
throughout construction and minimize impacts to the traveling public. 
 
The original plan relies on temporary pavement widening during Phases 1 and 2 to maintain two 11-foot 
travel lanes. Phase 3 would require construction within a narrow section of Crossroads Boulevard while 
maintaining live traffic immediately adjacent to the work area. 
 
Following commencement of construction, the contractor AB Underground has encountered field 
conditions, utility conflicts, and constructibility constraints that differ from the conditions anticipated 
when the original traffic phasing was developed during Design. 
 
Contractor's Request 
AB Underground is requesting that the Town modify the construction phasing to fully close 
Crossroads Boulevard east of Colorado Boulevard during Phase 2. The closure would occur in 
addition to the south Colorado Boulevard closure already included in the original Phase 2 plan. 
 
Under the proposed approach, AB would use the larger work area created by the closure to complete 
Phase 2 while advancing substantial portions of Phase 3 at the same time. 
 
The primary reason for the request is that maintaining two-way traffic under the current field conditions 
would require construction to occur within a significantly constrained work area. AB has identified 
worker and public safety, winter construction conditions, constructibility, roadway quality, and utility 
conflicts as the primary concerns with continuing under the original phasing. 
 
Potential Benefits of the Alternate Phasing 
The proposed closure would provide AB with a larger and less restricted construction area. The 
contractor identifies several potential benefits: 
• Safety: Eliminates live through-traffic immediately adjacent to active construction. 
• Construction efficiency: Provides additional space for equipment, grading, compaction, and 
material movement. 
• Roadway construction: Allows longer, continuous roadway construction and full-width HMA 
placement. 
• NISP Water casing: Allows the steel casing to be installed in one continuous operation rather 
than being divided between traffic phases and provides safe clear zones further away from open 
excavations and traveling through-traffic. 
• Winter conditions: Allows more permanent roadway work to be completed before winter and 
Page 2 of 49
reduces the amount of temporary traffic control that may need to remain in place during winter. 
• Schedule: AB estimates the alternate phasing could potentially accelerate the project by more 
than 30 working days. 
 
The alternate phasing may also reduce the number of construction joints, transitions, and repeated tie-
ins between completed and new roadway sections. AB indicates that this would provide greater control 
over grading, drainage, compaction, pavement placement, and final roadway smoothness. 
 
Traffic Impacts 
The primary tradeoff associated with the proposed change is the impact to the traveling public. 
 
Under the original phasing, Crossroads Boulevard would remain open to east-west traffic. However, AB 
indicates that construction activities would require frequent flagging and temporary interruptions as 
equipment, trucks, and materials move through the work area. This could result in periodic traffic stops, 
queues, and delays even though the roadway technically remains open. 
 
Under the alternate phasing, Crossroads Boulevard east of Colorado Boulevard would be fully closed 
during the affected phase. Traffic would instead use the proposed detour developed by AB 
Underground and Loveland Barricade. The contractor indicates that the detour was developed to 
accommodate the anticipated range of vehicle types and provide reasonable access around the project. 
 See attached detour plan.

Financial Impact: 
The total financial impact has not been determined. Under the original configuration, more than 1,500 
linear feet of concrete barrier could be required. The contractor estimates approximately $90,000 in 
additional barrier overrun costs and approximately one week for installation and removal. 
 
Staff will work with the contractor to determine any financial impacts or savings related to traffic control, 
detours, schedule acceleration, utility coordination, and any additional construction costs associated 
with the alternate phasing. 
 
 
Relationship to Strategic Plan: 
The alternate phasing supports sustainable infrastructure and quality of life by prioritizing worker and 
public safety, reducing winter exposure, improving constructability, and supporting the durability and 
Page 3 of 49
long-term performance of the completed roadway. 
 
The approach could also support fiscal responsibility by avoiding temporary work, barrier costs, 
repeated pavement transitions, winter maintenance, and rework. Final cost responsibility remains 
subject to staff confirmation. 
 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends that the Town Board approve AB Underground’s alternate construction phasing and 
an approximately two-month temporary closure of Crossroads Boulevard east of Colorado Boulevard, 
concurrent with the planned south Colorado Boulevard closure, subject to the following conditions: 
• Town approval of the final traffic-control and detour plan. 
• Maintenance of emergency access and reasonable access to affected properties. 
• Coordination with law enforcement, fire and emergency medical services, utilities, adjacent 
jurisdictions, schools, businesses, residents, and other affected stakeholders, as applicable. 
• Advance public notification and ongoing construction updates. 
• Compliance with the approved closure window and reopening milestones. 
• Continued monitoring of safety, traffic operations, schedule, and weather conditions, with staff 
authorized to require adjustments when necessary.

04-429-8440-000 Sustainable Infrastructure
Project Description and Location: Throughout Town Limits/Streets
The Streetlight Safety & Modernization Improvement Project is a multi-year capital improvement initiative focused on replacing aging and deteriorated streetlight infrastructure 
throughout the Town of Windsor. The project includes the replacement of approximately 105 streetlight poles that have reached or exceeded their useful service life due to 
corrosion, structural deterioration, material failure, and safety concerns.
The project will replace approximately 25 deteriorating fiberglass streetlight poles and 80 streetlight fixtures currently mounted to Xcel Energy distribution poles with new 
dedicated municipal streetlight poles, foundations, wiring, and LED fixtures where feasible. Installing dedicated municipal streetlight infrastructure will improve system 
reliability, enhance public safety, provide greater maintenance accessibility, reduce dependence on utility-owned infrastructure, and support long-term asset management 
objecti ves.
The project is proposed as a five-year replacement program, with approximately 21 streetlight poles replaced annually. The estimated construction cost is $20,000 per pole 
installation, including engineering, foundations, conduit, wiring, poles, fixtures, and associated electrical work, via Tanko Lighting, Inc Consultant.  The total estimated project 
cost is $2.1 million, with an annual investment of approximately $420,000.
The Town of Windsor currently owns and maintains more than 3,300 streetlights that provide critical nighttime illumination for motorists, pedestrians, bicyclists, and 
emergency responders. A recent assessment identified approximately 105 streetlight locations requiring replacement due to advanced deterioration and aging infrastructure.
Of these locations, approximately 25 fiberglass poles exhibit structural degradation and material failure associated with age and environmental exposure. An additional 80 
streetlight fixtures are mounted to Xcel Energy distribution poles, creating maintenance challenges, coordination requirements with the utility provider, and increased long-
term operational risk. As these assets continue to age, the likelihood of pole failures, lighting outages, public safety hazards, and emergency repairs will increase.
Im ple m e ntation of this proje ct will:
•	 Improve public safety by replacing structurally deficient streetlight infrastructure before failures occur.
•	 Enhance roadway, sidewalk, trail, and intersect ion visibility for residents and visitors.
•	 Reduce the risk of emergency maintenance costs and unplanned outages.
• 	 Im prove  re liability and re silie ncy of the  T own's stre e tlight syste m .
•	 T ransit ion streetlights from ut ility-owned distribut ion poles to dedicated municipal infrastructure where feasible.
•	 Support proact ive asset management and infrastructure lifecycle replacement pract ices.
•	 Protect the T own's investment in public infrastructure and maintain expected service levels for a growing community.
As Windsor continues to grow, maintaining safe, reliable, and modern streetlight infrastructure is essential to supporting transportation safety, community livability, and the 
Public Works mission of providing essential services that protect public health and improve quality of life for residents and businesses.
