MEMORANDUM 
 
 
Date: September 14, 2026 
To: Mayor and Town Board 
From: 
 
Re: Request for Funding from the American Legion 
Item #: A.4.b. 
 
Background / Discussion: 
The American Legion Forbes-McKay Post 109 is respectfully requesting financial assistance from the 
Town’s ‘Outside Agency Fund’ to assist with plans for a wheelchair lift and other Americans with 
Disabilities Act (ADA) improvements to the existing building. 
 
The Legion has been a fixture at 624 Ash Street in Windsor since the 1940’s, supporting veterans from 
all branches of the service. The Post is also open to the general public, who wish to support military 
personnel and the benefits provided by the Legion. 
Shortly after World War II, the building that houses the Post’s activities was moved to its current 
location, from the original location at the POW camp that was located between Windsor and Greeley. It 
has served the veteran community since that time. 
The 6,000 SF main floor of the building sits approximately five feet above grade. The building also has 
a fully finished basement. Over the course of the last 10 years, several efforts have been made to 
improve accessibility into the building, including proposals for exterior wheelchair lifts and accessible 
ramps – which have not come to fruition. This may have been due to project costs, the need for 
extensive site improvements, and/or limitations with the current lot and building configuration. 
In April of this year, the Legion began working with an architect to develop accessibility solutions for 
the building, with the process resulting in the preferred option of adding an exterior mechanical lift from 
street level to the main entry. Ramp solutions were evaluated, but it was determined that the lot 
configuration, building location, and impacts to the public sidewalk system would be very challenging to 
mitigate. 
At this time, the lift solution appears to be the most straightforward solution. However, it too comes with 
perceived site constraints – namely the proximity to the public sidewalk, location of existing established 
landscaping and location of the public right-of-way. Many of these site concerns require more 
extensive knowledge of the relationship of the boundary of the lot to the location of the building and 
other site infrastructure and public improvements. As such, a Minor Subdivision is required to clean up 
existing boundary questions and inform a detailed site plan to show the layout of the lift and other 
proposed site features and improvements (such as signage and landscaping). 
While the results obtained from the Minor Subdivision and Site Plan process will certainly benefit the 
Legion in the long run, the need for these documents has added costs to an already strained project 
budget for the Legion. With that said, the Legion is respectfully requesting financial assistance from the 
Town’s ‘Outside Agency Fund’. Please see the attached letter from The American Legion Forbes-
McKay Post 109.

volunteerism. The organization belongs to the people it serves and the communities in which it thrives.”

MEMORANDUM 
 
 
Date: September 14, 2026 
To: Mayor and Town Board 
From: Sandra Mezzetti, Senior Planner 
Re: Public Hearing - Ordinance 2026-1750 Amending Windsor Municipal Code Chapters 7 and 
16, Concerning the Keeping of Chicken Hens and Chicken Coops in Single-Family 
Residential Areas 
Item #: C.1. 
 
Background / Discussion: 
Executive Summary 
The purpose of the proposed code amendments is to resolve inconsistencies between the existing chicken coop 
requirements and the land use code.  The amendments move location and design standards for chicken coops 
from Chapter 7 (Keeping of Chickens) to Chapter 16 (Land Use), where they are more appropriate.  This also 
clarifies requirements and eliminates the permit process and fee.   
 
The July 13, 2026 Town Board  Work Session provided an overview of the proposed code amendments, with 
direction to staff to proceed with the required public hearings. 
 
The first public hearing was held at the September 02, 2026 Planning Commission meeting, resulting in a 
recommendation of approval  to Town Board, subject to any Planning Commission comments being addressed. 
Background 
The Town Clerk’s office manages the Town's Keeping of Chickens code (Article IX, Chapter 7).  However, 
inconsistencies between the chicken coop requirements and the land use code prompted staff to recommend 
relocating these standards.  The existing permit process is rarely used and the permit fee of ten dollars ($10.00) 
does not cover administrative costs.  
 
Residents requested increasing the maximum coop height  from six feet (6') to ten feet (10'), aligning with the 
height allowed for accessory structures. This change would allow sufficient clearance for a pedestrian-sized door 
and facilitate cleaning and maintenance. 
Key Proposed Changes  
Chapter 7 (Article IX) 
• Clarify the requirements for keeping chickens. 
• Eliminate the permit process and fee requirement.  
• Relocate the chicken coop location and design requirements from Chapter 7 to Chapter 16. 
Chapter 16 (Article III) 
• Added new section for location and design of chicken coops. 
• Coops must be on single-family residential parcels, behind the residence, and meet ventilation and 
predator protection standards. 
• Minimum space per hen:  two (2) square feet. 
• Maintain fifteen (15) foot setbacks for coops (from the structure to the rear and side property line), with an 
optional encroachment if the coop is one hundred twenty (120) square feet or less and written consent 
Page 51 of 106
from adjacent property owners is obtained.  
• Increase maximum coop height to ten (10) feet, consistent with accessory structures.  This change is 
based on resident feedback indicating that owners would like to be able to walk into the chicken coop to 
clean it. A six-foot (6') chicken coop does not allow for a six-foot-tall door. Chicken coops sold in local 
hardware and agricultural supply stores, as well as kits, are routinely sold at a height of ten (10) feet, 
either at the pitch of the roof, on a portion of the roof, or along the entire length of the roof. 
• Coops over one hundred twenty (120) square feet require a building permit; smaller coops do not. 
• Setback encroachment allowed for coops equal to or less than one hundred twenty (120) square feet with 
written adjacent property owner consent. 
• Homeowners Associations or similar associations or metropolitan districts may impose stricter standards. 
Below is an overview of the sections proposed for updates: 
Section 1. Windsor Municipal Code ("Code") Sec.7-9-20 – Keeping of chickens; limitations and requirements 
Removed all items related to the chicken coop location and design. Clarifications and 
renumbering have been added to the remaining items. 
Section 2.  Code Sec. 7-9-40 – Declaration of nuisance and unlawful acts; duty to maintain chicken habitat 
Deleted items related to permitting and renumbered remaining items 
Section 3. Code Sec.16-3-70 – Location and design of chicken coops/structures – New section added 
-------------------------------------------- 
The proposed amendments to the Municipal code, by section, are included below. Please refer to the ordinance 
for the entirety of the code language.  
 
Section 1.  Sec. 7-9-20 – Keeping of chickens; limitation and requirements 
(1)(a) The keeping of chicken roosters or more than six (6) chicken hens is prohibited upon any parcel of 
land within the Town. However, up to six (6) chicken hens may be kept per single-family residential 
parcel of property. subject to the following requirements and subject to all other applicable provisions of 
this Article:

Section 2.  Sec. 7-9-40. - Declaration of nuisance and unlawful acts; duty to maintain chicken habitat.  
(a) The unsanitary or unsafe keeping of chicken hens within the Town is hereby declared unlawful and a 
public nuisance. It shall be the duty of every owner or keeper of chicken hens to maintain the chicken hen 
habitat in a secure, neat, tidy, methodical, systematic, clean and orderly condition, permitting no 
accumulation of odor, dust, droppings, feed, organic material, pests or loitering of predators. Any owner 
or keeper who is found to have violated the provisions of this Section shall be deemed subject to the 
nuisance abatement measures provided in Article I of this Chapter. 
 
(b) Any person found to be in violation of the provisions of this Article shall be subject to immediate and 
permanent revocation of any permit issued to such person under this Article.

Section 3. Code Sec. 16-3-70. – Location and design of chicken coops/structures.  
a) All structures devoted to the keeping of chicken hens shall be located on single-family 
residential parcels. 
b) All structures devoted to the keeping of chicken hens shall be located between the rear-most 
portion of the residence and the rear lot line of the parcel in question. No chicken hens shall be 
permitted within any other portion of the lot or parcel. 
c) If a parcel has more than one (1) dwelling unit, all adult residents and the owners of the parcel 
must consent in writing to allow the chicken hens on the property. 
d) Each chicken hen must be housed in a covered, predator-resistant coop that is properly 
ventilated. The coop must be designed for easy access and cleaning, and it must provide a 
minimum of two (2) square feet of space per chicken hen. 
e) The chicken coop shall be no taller than ten (10) feet, as measured by the vertical distance from 
the ground level adjacent to the structure to the highest point of the roof surface. Chicken coops 
which are larger than one hundred twenty (120) square feet in area, as measured around the 
perimeter of the building, require a building permit. Chicken coops one hundred twenty (120) 
square feet or less in area do not require a building permit. 
f) Neither the chicken coop nor any portion of the outdoor enclosure may be located less than 
fifteen (15) feet from any abutting side and rear property line and outside of any abutting front yard. 
g) Chicken coop encroachments into the required side or rear setback(s) may be allowed when: 
 
1.  The structure is one hundred twenty (120) square feet or less in area and 
2. The property owner obtains written approval from the adjacent property owner(s) whose 
property is affected by the setback encroachment. This written approval must be 
presented to the Town upon notification that a complaint has been received. 
h) During daylight hours, the chicken hens must have access to the chicken coop, must have 
access to adequate fresh water, and must also have access to an enclosure that is screened on all 
sides and on top and adequate to protect them from predators. 
i) The chicken hens must be protected from predators by being closed in the chicken coop from 
dusk until dawn. 
j) The chicken hens must be sheltered or confined in such fashion as to prevent them from coming 
into contact with wild ducks or geese or their excrement. 
k) If the parcel upon which the chicken coop is proposed falls within the jurisdiction of a 
homeowners' association or similar covenant-based property owners' association, the 
requirements of this section shall be considered minimum requirements. Any such association 
shall have the right to lawfully adopt more stringent chicken coop standards, including the outright 
prohibition of chicken-keeping, for any parcel within the regulatory authority of such association.

• The provisions titled “Keeping of Chickens; Limitation and Requirements” in Chapter 7, Article 9, and 
“Location and Design of Chicken Coops/Structures” apply only to single-family residential parcels. The

current code does not specify which types of parcels are permitted to keep chickens or chicken coops. 
• Question: Does the neighbor’s consent requirement apply only to the fifteen (15)-foot setback 
encroachment for chicken coops, or does it also apply to accessory structures? 
Response: The required neighbor’s consent for setback encroachment applies only to chicken coops. 
Only chicken coops that are less than one hundred twenty (120) square feet in area are eligible for 
setback encroachment with neighbor consent. Additional language clarifying the eligibility of chicken 
coops for setback encroachment was added to the ordinance at the Planning Commission’s request.
