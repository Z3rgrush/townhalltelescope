1. Public Hearing – An Ordinance Amending Portions of Chapters 14 and 15 of the Town of Windsor 
Municipal Code Concerning Sign Regulations – Kimberly Lambrecht, Long Range Planner, Town of 
Windsor Community Development Department 
 Long Range Planner Kimberly Lambrecht presented the final draft of the ordinance. It would 
completely replace the sign regulations in Chapter 15, Article 17, and amend the definitions in 
Chapter 14, Article 5. She was joined remotely by Roger Eastman of Logan Simpson, the 
consultant project manager. 
  
Ms. Lambrecht said the update was prompted by the U.S. Supreme Court's Reed v. Town of 
Gilbert decision and the need for a content-neutral code. It also aligned the code with the 
Comprehensive Plan and Windsor's small-town character, incorporated community and 
stakeholder input, and produced a practical, enforceable, user-friendly code. She stated that the 
ordinance would go to the Town Board for first reading on Monday, October 26, 2026 and second 
reading on November 9, 2026 with the code taking effect in December. 
 
Ms. Lambrecht then described last-minute revisions made after the town attorney's final review that 
had not been included in the packet. The revisions corrected sign areas for miscellaneous signs, 
removed substitution clauses and limitations on text area from banners to further content neutrality, 
made time limits for portable and temporary signs consistent, clarified changeable copy standards 
in the sign permit section, and corrected definitions in the Chapter 15 definitions section. She 
stated that none of the changes altered the intent of the update. 
 
Chair Reddick asked that the updated and amended version of the code be the version forwarded 
to the Town Board. Ms. Lambrecht confirmed it had been submitted to the Town Clerk's office and 
would be part of the record. The public hearing was closed and the Commission returned to its 
regular meeting.

3. Public Hearing – Wireless Telecommunication Facility (Tower) Site Location within the 
Subdivision Commercial (GC) Zone District, Municipal Code Section 16-5-40(k)(3); Trautman 
Subdivision 4th Filing, Lot 1, 29918 County Road 17 – Timberline Church, property owner; Brandon 
Alverson, property owner’s representative, Mike Mathison, Horizon Tower, applicant; Trevor Crow, 
Hoss Consulting, Inc,. authorized representative 
 
Chairman Reddick noted that two public hearings pertained to the same property, the tower site 
location and the conditional use permit, and that staff would present both together. He closed the 
regular meeting and opened the public hearing on the site location. 
 
Senior Planner Sandra Mezzetti stated that the request was for a new wireless telecommunication 
tower site and a conditional use permit, and that staff recommended approval of both. She 
reviewed the governing code provisions and explained that federal law preempts consideration of 
environmental and health effects for facilities that meet FCC regulations. The Commission may 
consider only location, aesthetics, and height. 
 
Ms. Mezzetti stated that the application met the telecommunications facility requirements, the 
conditional use permit criteria, and the goals of the Comprehensive Plan. No water or sewer is 
required, and the silo design is compatible with the area's character. The Town's independent 
telecom consultant found the technical aspects compliant, and notice requirements were met. Staff 
asked that the application materials, staff memorandum, hearing testimony, and staff 
recommendations be entered into the record. 
 
Mr. Crow, of Hoss Consulting, Inc. explained that the site was selected based on the RF search 
ring and the willingness of landowners. Four prospects were contacted, and Timberline Church 
viewed the tower as complementary to its farm campus. The facility would be unmanned, would 
need no FAA lighting, and could accommodate multiple carriers. Verizon Wireless is the anchor 
tenant. Water would be needed for the landscaping for the first year or two, supplied from the farm 
campus or temporary tanks. 
 
Mr. Kennedy, an RF engineering consultant with Horizon Tower,explained that the site is needed 
for both coverage and capacity. Existing Verizon sites are 1.74 to 3.39 miles away, and the area 
has weak signal and overloaded sectors amid significant growth. He presented third-party 
crowdsourced data and propagation maps showing current and proposed indoor, in-vehicle, and 
outdoor coverage across the low, mid, and high frequency bands. He also discussed FCC specific 
absorption rate limits and the general-public exposure standard and stated that the site would 
improve 9-1-1 service and first responder broadband. He stated that no existing structure within 
one mile is tall enough to serve the need. 
 
The public hearing was closed, and the regular meeting was opened.

Background / Discussion: 
Executive Summary 
The Town of Windsor had the opportunity to purchase a property, called the River's Edge property (formerly 
known as Hermanns property), for Town recreation and open space. The property is located directly outside of 
the Town's existing Growth Management Area (GMA), south and west of the existing GMA boundary identified in 
the 2024 Town of Windsor Comprehensive Plan Future Land Use Map (“Land Use Map”). The extension of the 
GMA boundary would include approximately 75.5 acres of agricultural land, located generally north-east of SH 
257, approximately one-half mile south of Poudre Trail Road, and west of the Town's Water Treatment Plan. 
 
The area is also directly adjacent to the Town's Corporate Limits on the northern and eastern boundary. 
Therefore, the area is a logical extension of the Town’s GMA boundary. The GMA boundary is known as the 
“Windsor Three-Mile Plan Area”, required by the State (C.R.S. 31-12-105), and described further in the Town’s 
2024 Comprehensive Plan. 
 
The subject area is proposed to contain the land use designation of "Parks and Open Space" on the Town's 
Future Land Use Map. 
 
Comprehensive Plan Amendments 
The Town’s Comprehensive Plan is a tool for guiding long-range planning decisions for the benefit of existing 
and future residents. The Town’s Comprehensive Plan states that the Planning Commission shall consider seven 
(7) criteria when evaluating any proposed amendment to the Plan. Those criteria have been evaluated by staff 
and included within this report under the Review Critieria section.  
Staff is recommending that the Planning Commission approve and adopt the subject land use map amendment 
to the Town's Comprehensive Plan Future Land Use Map. After adoption by the Planning Commission and 
approval by the Town Board, the Town would proceed with the annexation of the property and initial zoning of 
ROL (Recreation Open Lands) zoning. There are no plans for development at this time. 
 
Background 
The Town recently purchased the River's Edge property with the Town’s open space fund and will be used as 
such. There are no plans for development at this time. It is proposed that the added area be designated as 
“Parks and Open Space”, consistent with adjacent land use designations on the Town’s Future Land Use Map, 
as illustrated in the exhibit below. Also, see attached enlarged exhibit in the Staff presentation.

Review Criteria for Comprehensive Plan 
Amendment 
The proposed amendment was evaluated based on the seven (7) plan amendment criteria set forth in the Town’s 
2024 Comprehensive Plan below. The appropriateness of a plan amendment request should be determined by 
the Planning Commission with the following criteria:  
1. Conforms with the vision, goals, and policies outlined in the Plan. 
2. Serves a substantial public purpose and will not be substantially detrimental to the surrounding lands. 
3. Considers the nature and degree of impacts on neighboring lands and land uses. 
4. Provides for the orderly physical growth of the Town. 
5. Minimizes detrimental impacts to the transportation system, water and sewer infrastructure, water supply, 
and emergency services. 
6. Considers the long-term fiscal impact and rate of return of providing associated services and 
infrastructure. 
7. Consider impacts to overall population and costs to provide services. 
1.  The proposal conforms with the vision, goals and policies outlined in the Plan. 
Vision:  As a community, we envision a safe, sustainable, and thriving Town that prioritizes a high quality of life 
through thoughtful growth and development, fostering a sense of community, and responsible stewardship of its 
natural resources and open space. 
  
Guiding Principal 6: Exceptional and memorable park, recreation, open space, and trail experiences accessible 
to everyone, that incorporate wild landscapes and a sense of discovery. 
Page 13 of 42
Guiding Principal 8: Exceptional and memorable park, recreation, open space, and trail experiences accessible 
to everyone, that incorporate wild landscapes and a sense of discovery. 
 
Chapter 5 – Parks, Recreation, Open Space & Trails 
Goal 1: Create equitable access to parks, open spaces, and trails to promote community identity and quality 
of life. 
Policy 5: Preserve open spaces through acquisition and conservation easements and work with developers 
to establish functional open space.  
Policy 7: Use open space as community separators or buffers between adjacent cities and towns to retain 
community identity. 
2. Serves a substantial public purpose and will not be substantially detrimental to the surrounding lands. 
The open space will serve a substantial public purpose for the Town, its residents and visitors. It will provide a 
valuable resource, add compatible land uses to those adjacent uses, and provide an attractive entry to Windsor. 
There will be no impact on the existing or planned land uses. 
 
The proposal is consistent with the adjacent Parks and Open Space land use map designations in the 
surrounding area, ROL (Recreation Open Lands) zoning on adjacent properties, adjacent open space 
acquisitions of the City of Greeley to the south and west, as well as existing agricultural uses, and the proximity 
of the Poudre River and its regional trail system. 
 
3. Considers the nature and degree of impacts on neighboring lands and land uses. 
The proposal will have no impact on neighboring lands and land uses. The land will be for open space use. 
 
4. Provides for the orderly physical growth of the Town. 
The proposal provides consistency with the open space land uses surrounding the property and area, 
complementing the orderly physical growth of the Town. 
 
5. Minimizes detrimental impacts to the transportation system, water and sewer infrastructure, water 
supply, and emergency services. 
No development is proposed. There will be no impact on the transportation system, water and sewer 
infrastructure, water supply, and emergency services with the proposed land use map amendment. 
 
6. Considers the long-term fiscal impact and rate of return of providing associated services and 
infrastructure. 
There are no planned services or infrastructure improvements proposed at this time. 
 
7. Consider impacts to overall population and costs to provide services.  
There are no impacts on the overall population or costs of providing public services for additional residents 
associated with open space.

Review Criteria: 
1. Conforms with the vision, goals, and policies outlined in the Plan.
2. Serves a substantial public purpose and will not be substantially detrimental to 
the surrounding lands.
3. Considers the nature and degree of impacts on neighboring lands and land uses.
