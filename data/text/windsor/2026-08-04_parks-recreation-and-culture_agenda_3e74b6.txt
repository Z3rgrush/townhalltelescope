C. BOARD REVIEW/DISCUSSION 
1. 50th Anniversary Train Depot Tour at Windsor History Museum 
• The Depot is the oldest building in downtown Windsor, started constrcution in 
1882 and was completed in 1883, Windsor Lake did not fully exist at that time  
• It has been a museum since 1976, first Windsor History Museum ran by what is 
now the Historical Society, moved from its original location about a ¼ mile down 
the track, Civic Committee saved it and moved it to the current location, open 
typically Memorial Day to Labor Day, working on expanding those hours 
• Started rennovation work in 2023, knowing that this year would be America’s 
250th, Colorado’s 150th and the Depot’s 50th anniversary 
• Three years of planning, research and collaboration between Ms. 
Heusser/Museum Curator and Ms. Fuhrman/Museum Education Coordinator 
• History, locations and stories are found through a variety of sources – 
newspapers, Weld County Records, maps, Sanborn Fire Insurance maps, 
Census records, oral/recorded history, in 2018 Ms. Heusser worked with 
Newspapers.com and digitized the Windsor Becon/Poudre Valley from 1898-
2011, missing 1914  
• Utilized an exhibition company and fabricator to do the more fancy interactives, 
with electronics and lights 
• Grand Opening of the new exhibition is this Saturday 10:00 am – 1:00 pm 
• The tour moved through the Depot whle highlighting the following – 
o How the Depot became a museum  
o Stories of all the people who worked there 
▪ Station Agents Charlie Tucker, Walter Woodward and Ida DeMarr 
▪ Original features – telegraph sounder, speaker extender, switchboard, 
lights switches when electricity was brought in 1909, replacement safe 
o People who came to Windsor through the Depot 
▪ The waiting room was not the most comfortable and people didn’t really 
like it in the past, benches were uncofortable, it was cold and drafty and 
wasn’t pretty enough 
▪ The Miller Family came as immigrants from Russia and the Sorensen 
Family came as immigrants from Denmrk, Theo Sorensen served on 
Town Board and Mayor from the 1940’s to 1960 
o The railroad, freight and how Windsor was built with goods/mail coming in 
and going out 
▪ The graffiti on the walls is one of the most substantial original features of 
Page 4 of 23
 
 
the depot and the stories they tell 
▪ How the rollers and sled were used 
▪ The freight area often held dances, since it was the largest space in 
touwn at the time 
▪ Glenn Tucker was the last Station Agent, Cobbs & Woodward later 
opened a store together and Otis Shin road the on the roof of the freight 
car and lost his arm returned as a Station Agent  
o Numerous interactive dispays, play and discovery areas for children and 
Windsor train table

Description and Provenance of Object(s):  
Piano and stool donated by the Golden History Museum.  
Originally purchased by John Evans, second Territorial Governor of Colorado from 1862 -1865, Evans sold the piano around 
1910 to Charles Albert Yancey, the donors grandfather. The piano was purchased by Yancey from Evans, purchased the piano in 
Central City. 
Charles Yancey was one time mayor of Windsor, CO. The piano stayed in Windsor and was eventually moved to their ranch in Red 
Feather Lakes. The piano  remained in the Yancey family for three generations before Mr. and Mrs. Keith Simons donated it to 
Golden Landmarks Association in 1972. In 2026, the piano was deaccession from the Golden History Museum collection and 
offered to the Town of Windsor Museums, due to it’s strong tie to the Yancey family and the Windsor community.  
The piano is manufactured by the J. M. Pelton of New York City. Serial number 19414.
