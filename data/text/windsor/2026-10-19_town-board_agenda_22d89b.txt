Background / Discussion: 
The Town Board adopted the Downtown Parking Management Plan (DPMP) on June 9, 2025. This 
plan outlines a vision, guiding principles and strategies (action Items) intended to provide the Town 
with a path forward to address downtown parking conditions in an incremental fashion, along with 
presenting criteria for ongoing infrastructure assessment of the on and off-street parking located in the 
Downtown area. (See map delineating boundary of the assessment and data collection area.) 
 
  
 
Furthermore, several options were presented to begin implementation of the DPMP. These options 
were generally based on infrastructure assessment, data collection and municipal code amendments, 
with each carrying a differing level of intensity of incremental steps forward, along with varying 
flexibility to be able to manage parking. After review and discussion, the Town Board 
approved Implementation Option #2, which set the stage for ongoing downtown parking data 
collection and evaluation (with budget approval), along with an incremental path for Municipal Code 
amendments for parking management. This option provides maximum flexibility, while setting the stage 
for future parking management, when certain thresholds are met. 
 
In response to Plan Strategy A-4 (budget for and begin annual data collection), results from these 
DPMP implementation efforts will be presented annually to the Town Board at work sessions, with the 
results of the 2025 and 2026 data collections being presented this evening, alongside

contributing factor in this year’s higher on-street readings.

Executive Summary 
Parking demand in Downtown Windsor remains moderate, with ample availability throughout 
most of the day. Overall occupancy across on- and off-street facilities did not exceed 85%, 
indicating that the existing supply generally meets current demand. Peak activity occurred near 
Main Street and Walnut Street, where occupancy approached capacity during evening hours 
and community events. 
Event-driven surges were the primary factor inﬂuencing higher demand. The Summer Concert 
Series at Boardwalk Park and the Windsor Farmers Market temporarily increased occupancy in 
adjacent blocks, but conditions normalized outside of event hours. Compared to the industry’s 
85% target utilization benchmark, Downtown Windsor’s parking system remains balanced, 
with localized pressure in the most active zones and underused capacity elsewhere. 
Turnover trends show that most vehicles park for short durations, supporting business access 
and visitor circulation. Approximately 85% of on-street vehicles stayed less than two hours, 
while most off-street users stayed under four hours. Longer stays were more common in the 
residential areas south of Walnut Street. 
The study area included all public and private parking assets within the downtown core and 
surrounding blocks. Data was collected between July 24 and July 26, 2025, using a 
combination of vehicle-mounted LPR technology and drone imagery. These observations 
captured hourly activity from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. under typical summer conditions, with limited 
weather impacts. 
When compared to historical data from 2016 through 2024, overall utilization in Downtown 
Windsor remains stable. Slight ﬂuctuations were primarily associated with event activity rather 
than sustained growth in daily demand, suggesting that the existing parking system continues 
to provide adequate capacity to support downtown activity.

an overview of the study area, then dive into how we collected the inventory, the data collection 
methods we used, the key things to consider during the collection period, and how we 
integrated historical data to get a complete picture.

Key Considerations 
The parking industry considers the ideal target parking occupancy rate to be 85%, meaning 
that there are approximately one or two parking spaces available per block face, or in a desired 
parking area. This occupancy target minimizes congestion and maximizes utilization of parking 
assets. Areas that regularly exceed 85% occupancy can appear full to the parking public and 
contribute to congestion as motorists circle looking for an available space. Likewise, areas that 
regularly are well below 85% are considered underutilized. The goal is to achieve a balanced 
parking system that remains at or near 85% occupancy on average, recognizing that there will 
realistically be some exceptional or outlier periods. 
 
When parking durations are long, turnover of parking spaces is low. Conversely, shorter 
durations indicate higher turnover. High turnover can maximize the number of motorists that 
can utilize available parking assets, which is especially important in commercial areas due to 
proximity to goods and services and the need for customer access. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Occupancy 
Overall, occupancy in Downtown Windsor is low; however, there are geographic and event-
based hot spots around key downtown destinations—ﬁgures 2 and 3 show on- and off-street 
average occupancy rates throughout the day. While occupancy rates increased signiﬁcantly 
during events and at peak times in hotspots, the overall average occupancy did not exceed 
85% across the combined on- and off-street locations within the Study Area.

Length of Stay: The amount of time a car remains in a given parking space. Also referred 
to as the duration.  
Turnover: The rate at which a parking facility is used, or the number of vehicles that can use 
a parking space within a given time. 
Block face: A single side of the street between two cross streets.

the same methodology each year and supports a more precise year-over-year comparison. 
Results 
Occupancy 
Overall, occupancy in Downtown Windsor is low; however, geographic and event -based hot 
spots exist around key downtown destinations (Appendix B, Figures 10 and 11; Appendix C, 
Figures 14–16). While occupancy rates increased significantly during events and at peak times 
in hot spots, the overall average occupancy did not exceed 85% across the combined on- and 
off-street locations within the Study Area. The most consistent hot spot was the block face along 
Main Street between Third Street and Fourth Street, home to the Windsor Mill, which 
consistently registered occupancy above 85% during evening hours across all three collection 
days. 
From approximately 4 p.m. to 6 p.m., on - and off -street occupancy was consistently high 
between Main Street and Walnut Street, between Third Street and Fourth Street, with nearby 
streets also elevated. 
The Summer Concert Series, h eld at Boardwalk Park on Thursday, July 23, 2026, brought 
increased occupancy in on - and off-street spaces in the north section of the Study Area near 
the park beginning at 5 p.m. A brief storm passed through Downtown Windsor earlier that 
afternoon but had cleared by approximately 5 p.m., and evening occupancy near the park still 
climbed to its highest level of the study. Friday's Movies in the Park also drew increased evening 
activity near the park, and Saturday's Windsor Farmers Market, held in the Boardw alk Park 
roundabout parking area from 8 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. (Appendix D, Figure 3 5), similarly raised 
occupancy of on- and off-street spaces in the north and central sections of the Study Area.

2026 Windsor Parking Utilization Study Report  6 
 
The highest average occupancy rates were observed at 6 p.m. on Thursday, July 23, 2026. On-
street occupancy reached 61%—the highest single value recorded during the study —while off-
street occupancy was 56%, as shown in Appendix A, Figures 4 and 5. This shift toward on-street 
parking is consistent with concert attendee s arriving as the afternoon storm cleared and 
choosing curb spaces closest to Boardwalk Park. With the nearby off -street lot and block face 
closed for construction (Appendix C, Figures 14 –16), some of the demand that lot would 
normally absorb may have shifted to on-street spaces instead; had the weather stayed clear all 
afternoon, on-street occupancy near the park may have climbed even higher than the 61% 
observed. Throughout all days of the study period, both on - and off-street occupancy varied 
little between 9 a.m. and 4 p.m. 
Length of Stay 
The average vehicle length of stay in Downtown Windsor is short, with most vehicles parking 
for less than 2 hours in both on - and off -street spaces (Appendix B, Figures 12 and 13); 
however, off-street spaces had somewhat longer stays than on-street spaces. 
On average, 95% of on -street vehicles and 75% of off -street vehicles stayed less than 4 hours 
(85% and 65%, respectively, stayed less than 2 hours). 
Greater lengths of stays were generally observed in on-street spaces in the southern section of 
the Study Area, south of Walnut St, as shown in Appendix C, Figure 29. This area is more 
residential. The average length-of-stay data was affected by some vehicles that did not move 
all day (2%). 
Historical Comparison (2024–2026) 
To evaluate multi -year trends under consistent methodology, DIXON compared hourly 
occupancy across the three most recent collection rounds: July 25–27, 2024; July 24–26, 2025; 
and July 23–25, 2026 (Appendix A, Figures 8 and 9). Across all nine collection days, average 
on-street occupancy was 30% and average off-street occupancy was 36%. 
On-street occupancy has trended modestly upward across the three years, rising from an 
average of 24 –28% in 2024 to 30 –34% in 2026 across the three collection days. Off -street 
occupancy, by contrast, has remained comparatively stable to slightly lower, ranging from 33–
41% in 2024 to 33 –38% in 2026. The clearest shift occurred during the Thursday evening 
concert hour: on -street occupancy at 6 p.m. rose each year (55% in 2024,  57% in 2025, and 
61% in 2026), while off-street occupancy at the same hour fell from 63% in 2025 to 56% in 2026. 
This pattern is consistent with concert attendees increasingly choosing on -street spaces near 
Boardwalk Park, a shift that coincided with this  year’s brief afternoon storm. The off -street 
construction near Boardwalk Park noted above may also be a contributing factor in this year’s 
higher on-street readings.

Historical Comparison (2016–2020) 
The Town of Windsor provided data from previous data co llection efforts in 2016, 2018, and 
2020. Because that data was recorded using broader occupancy categories rather than exact 
percentages (see Historical Data above), this comparison is approximate. Based on these 
results, overall occupancy has historicall y been low in Downtown Windsor: historical 
occupancy did not exceed 50% during any of the data collections before 2024. Occupancy in 
DIXON’s 2024–2026 studies has remained within a similar range, exceeding 50% only after 5 
p.m. and during special events su ch as the Summer Concert Series, Movies in the Park, the 
Windsor Farmers Market, and the Labor Day Parade, suggesting that overall parking demand 
has remained relatively stable over the past decade, with event activity continuing to drive peak 
utilization. 
Conclusion 
This study set out to understand current parking utilization patterns in Downtown Windsor and 
to compare current conditions with prior data collection efforts. Overall, Downtown Windsor’s 
parking system continues to provide adequate capacity, with demand concentrated in specific 
hot spots rather than spread evenly across the Study Area. Key takeaways include: 
• Average occupancy across all three 2026 collection days did not exceed the industry -
standard 85% threshold at any hour, and most vehicles turned over within two hours. 
• The block face along Main Street between Third and Fourth Street, home to the 
Windsor Mill, consistently exceeded 85% occupancy during afternoon and evening 
hours across all three collection days and is the clearest candidate for targeted parking 
management activities. 
• Community events remain the primary driver of high -demand periods —the Summer 
Concert Series, Movies in the Park, and the Windsor Farmers Market each produced 
measurable, short-duration increases in occupancy near Boardwalk Park. 
• Recommended focus areas: the Main Street/Windsor Mill block and parking availability 
during the Town’s recurring summer events.

The Town hereby establishes a target average parking occupancy rate of eighty-five percent (85%) 
for the Downtown Parking Management Area, as defined by the Downtown Parking Management 
Plan, to maintain turnover, support economic vitality, promote parking, and curbside access for all 
users. Periodic reviews of occupancy data shall guide implementation or adjustment of parking

Sec. 8-2-60. – Time-Limited Parking. 
 
(a) The Town, by resolution and posted signage, may establish and modify parking time limits, 
including limits ranging from fifteen (15) minutes, up to ten (10) hours, or as otherwise needed 
to meet occupancy targets and operational needs. The Town Manager or designee is authorized 
to adjust time limits seasonally or for special events, provided that all changes are clearly 
posted on site.  
 
(b) No person shall move or repark a vehicle within the same block face, defined as a single side 
of a street between two cross streets, for the purpose of circumventing posted time restrictions.  
 
(c) No person shall park a vehicle in any parking stall longer than the posted maximum time limit. 
 
Sec. 8-2-70. - Paid Parking. 
 
(a) The Town Board may authorize the implementation of paid parking in the public right-of-way 
and in public parking lots when occupancy, turnover, or access objectives cannot be achieved 
through non-monetary parking management strategies. Paid parking zones may utilize 
payment hardware and/or software, including but not limited to, meters, payment kiosks, 
access revenue control, and/or mobile payment technology.  
 
(b) The Town Board is authorized to set rates by Resolution. The Town Manager or designee is 
authorized to create schedules, and approved payment technologies, and to implement 
complimentary or validation programs for public benefit.  
 
(c) The Town Board, by Resolution, shall approve the establishment of any new paid parking zone 
or any material change to paid parking rules.  
Sec. 8-2-80. - Parking Permits. 
(a) The Town may establish and administer residential and employee permit parking programs to 
address parking impacts and provide prioritized access for neighborhood residents and 
commercial district employees. The Town Manager or designee may define permit areas, set 
eligibility requirements, establish terms and fees, and issue physical or electronic permits. Any 
fees established shall be approved by the Town Board by Resolution.
