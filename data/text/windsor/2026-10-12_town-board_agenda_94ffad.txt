3. Public Hearing - Ordinance No. 2026-1752 - An Ordinance Amending Certain Portions of 
Windsor Municipal Code Chapters 14 and 16 Concerning Conditional Use Permits - Carlin 
Malone, Planning Manager, Community Development Department  
 Planning Manager Carlin Malone presented the ordinance on first reading. The presentation 
covered proposed amendments to the conditional use permit (CUP) framework within the 
Windsor Municipal Code. Following the staff presentation, the public hearing was opened. No 
members of the public came forward to comment, and the hearing was closed. 
 
Board discussion touched on several substantive concerns. Town Board Member Jones 
raised a question regarding the duration of temporary modular buildings approved under a 
conditional use permit, noting that applicants could potentially request use periods of two to 
three years and questioning whether a codified time limit would be more appropriate. Mayor 
Cline agreed with the sentiment, observing that without a defined ceiling, a five-year 
conditional use permit might not feel meaningfully "conditional." Staff indicated that when 
modular buildings are addressed separately in code, time limits could more appropriately be 
applied to that specific use category. 
 
Town Board Member Symsack raised concern about the provision allowing "unique in nature 
or character" uses in any zoning district, seeking clarification on who determines "uniqueness" 
and whether this provision could be used to circumvent standard zoning requirements. Staff 
clarified that a use already permitted in another zoning district would not qualify under this 
provision, and that an example such as a residential pickleball court would not be eligible.

Re: Ordinance No. 2026-1751 - An Ordinance Amending Section 15-14-70 of the Town of 
Windsor Municipal Code and 2026 Larimer County Flood Insurance Rate Maps and Flood 
Insurance Study Concerning Flood Damage Prevention Measures Applicable to Land Use

Item #: C.1. 
 
Background / Discussion: 
Executive Summary 
The Town Board approved the first reading of the ordinance on September 28, 2026, of the ordinance 
amending Section 15-14-70 Flood Damage Prevention:  Amend Code Sec. 15-14-70 to adopt the new 
Larimer County Flood Impact Study ("FIS") and Flood Impact Rate Maps ("FIRM"), effective November 
27, 2026.  There have been no changes between the first and second reading of the ordinance. 
Therefore, staff recommends that the Town Board approve the second reading of the ordinance.  
 
Background 
Well over a decade ago, the Federal Emergency Management Agency ("FEMA") launched a project to 
restudy the Poudre River floodplain. The new FIRM and the new FIS for Larimer County were the result 
of over 15 years of work. A FIRM displays the 100-year floodplain boundary (i.e. 1% annual flood risk), 
and many times includes a floodway boundary. The last major floodplain mapping change in Windsor 
took place in January 2016, when the digital flood insurance rate map ("DFIRM") was adopted. Windsor 
adopted the Weld County portion of the FIRM and FIS in 2023 by Ordinance No. 2023-1687. However, 
there were still issues being worked through outside of Windsor that significantly delayed adoption of 
the Larimer County FIRM and FIS.  
 
The Letter of Final Determination for the Larimer County mapping was issued by FEMA on May 27, 
2026, notifying communities that they have a six-month period to adopt the FIRM mapping and 
associated Flood Insurance Study (FIS). In order to adopt the FIRM mapping, the Town Floodplain 
Ordinance needs to be amended to adopt the Larimer County FIRM mapping and FIS as the official 
Town floodplain mapping, in conjunction with the previously adopted Weld County FIS and FIRM. The 
Town of Windsor must adopt the new Larimer County FIRM and FIS before November 27, 2026, in 
order to demonstrate to FEMA that the Town is compliant with National Flood Insurance Program 
regulations.  
 
The current remapping effort is part of the project known as the Colorado Hazard Mapping Program 
(CHAMP). Another closely associated project with the remapping effort is the Colorado RiskMAP. The 
general history of the floodplain remapping effort associated with CHAMP and RiskMAP is as follows:  
• The first meeting Engineering staff was invited to was on May 9, 2011, in Fort Collins. This is 
when staff became aware of the RiskMAP project that would re-study the Poudre floodplain and 
result in a new FIRM.  
• Formal comments were submitted to the Colorado Water Conservation Board ("CWCB") by the 
Town Engineering Department on May 23, 2018. These comments included technical 
comments prepared by Ayres. Page 7 of 106  
• A meeting was held on June 28, 2018 (Windsor Town Hall) with CWCB, Ayres, and Water 
Page 26 of 72
Valley representatives.  
• Formal comments were submitted to CWCB by the Town Engineering Department on June 11, 
2019 (Ayres provided assistance with review and comments).  
• A meeting was held on July 26, 2019 (Windsor Town Hall) with AECOM, Greeley, Weld County, 
and CWCB.  
• FEMA issued preliminary maps on September 17, 2020. • FEMA & CWCB held an open house 
at the Weld County Administration Building on September 24, 2020. Windsor staff attended the 
open house. Windsor invited property owners with land in the proposed floodplain (postcards 
mailed).  
• Windsor received a notice dated February 10, 2021, from FEMA regarding the start of a 90-day 
appeal period.  
• Windsor staff still had concerns about the revised mapping and Ayres Associates Inc. was hired 
to prepare an appeal submittal on behalf of the Town.  
• The appeal prepared by Ayres was submitted to FEMA on April 16, 2021.  
• On August 20, 2021, FEMA provided a formal response to Windsor’s appeal. All areas of appeal 
were resolved and agreed with the appeal submitted. However, staff provided a comment to 
FEMA illustrating that the Town's limits were not correct because the Kyger Reservoir 
Annexation had been excluded.  
• Due to the annexation boundaries changing and delays in Larimer County mapping, a decision 
was made by FEMA to split Windsor’s mapping at the Larimer/Weld County line.  
• The Larimer County mapping delay pertained to issues outside of Windsor rather than any 
outstanding concerns in Windsor related to Larimer County mapping.  
• The Letter of Final Determination was issued by FEMA on May 27, 2026, notifying communities 
that they have a six-month period to adopt the Larimer County FIRM mapping and associated 
Flood Insurance Study (FIS). The Weld County FIRM and FIS are scheduled to be effective 
November 27, 2026. Now that Larimer County mapping has been completed, Windsor must 
adopt the Larimer County FIRM and FIS.  
• CWCB oversees the implementation of FEMA floodplain regulations and has reviewed the 
Town's Ordinance Amending Code Sec. 15-14-70. 
National Flood Insurance Program ("NFIP"): 
Community participation in the NFIP results in property owners being able to purchase flood insurance 
at subsidized rates. Windsor participates in the NFIP. According to the State NFIP Coordinator, at the 
time of the 2023 Weld County FIRM and FIS adoption, Windsor had 48 NFIP policies totaling 
$15,000,000 in coverage. Structures covered in those policies include 47 single-family homes and 10 
other structures.  
FEMA provides the following overview of the NFIP, “Congress established the National Flood Insurance 
Program (NFIP) with the passage of the National Flood Insurance Act of 1968. Since the inception of 
NFIP, additional legislation has been enacted. The NFIP goes through periodic Congressional 
reauthorization to renew the NFIP’s statutory authority to operate.” 
FIRM and FIS adoption: 
The Town of Windsor must adopt the new Larimer County FIRM and FIS before November 27, 2026, in 
order to demonstrate to FEMA that the Town is compliant with the NFIP. Failure to adopt the FIRM 
mapping and FIS will result in residents being unable to purchase flood insurance policies.  
 
The Town Floodplain Ordinance will need to be amended to adopt the Larimer County FIRM mapping 
and FIS as the official Town floodplain mapping for Larimer County. The previously adopted Weld 
County FIRM and FIS will remain unchanged.

Background / Discussion: 
Executive Summary 
Windsor Municipal Code Sec. 14-2-130. – Conditional use permit, states that the purpose of the CUP is 
to allow consideration of uses which are unique when not specifically provided as a use by right within 
any specific zone district. However, this section also states that the conditional use permit (CUP) is only 
applicable to uses identified as conditional uses in particular zone districts. 
  
The purpose of the proposed amendments is to allow CUP consideration for unique uses that are not 
enumerated in the zoning code, and to add applicable conditional uses for consideration in particular 
zone districts: 
• In particular zone districts, applicability for temporary modular buildings, and 
• Adding applicability for temporary accessory outdoor storage, when used in conjunction with a 
phased development for an enclosed storage facility, in the LI (Limited Industrial) zone district. 
 
Planning Commission Summary 
At their regular meeting of September 16, 2026, the Planning Commission forwarded to the Town 
Board a recommendation of approval of the ordinance amending Chapters 14 and 16, as stated in the 
staff recommendation section. There were no Planning Commission comments on the ordinance. 
Background: 
The proposed changes will make the Town’s Land Use Code clearer by specifying temporary uses in 
certain zone districts that can qualify for CUP consideration. The proposed changes will also address 
unique uses that the zoning code does not specifically address and those that were not anticipated 
when the code was written. 
Proposed Amendments: Article II of Chapter 14, and Articles I of Chapter 16 
Please refer to the attached ordinance for the proposed changes by section. Below is an overview of 
the proposed updates: 
  
 §14-2-130 – Conditional use permit.  
• Striking conflicting language in the applicability section would prohibit unique uses from being 
considered. 
• Allowing consideration of temporary modular buildings in all zone districts. 
• Allowing consideration of temporary accessory outdoor storage when used in conjunction with a 
phased development for an enclosed storage facility:
