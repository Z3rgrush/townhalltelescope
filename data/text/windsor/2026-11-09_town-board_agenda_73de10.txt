1. Public Hearing - Ordinance No. 2026-1753 - An Ordinance Amending Portions of Chapters 14 
and 15 of the Town of Windsor Municipal Code Concerning Sign Regulations - Kimberly 
Lambrecht, Long Range Planner, Town of Windsor Community Development Department 
Long Range Planner Lambrecht presented the item as a first reading and public hearing. 
Consultant Roger Eastman of Logan Simpson attended by Teams. She said the Planning 
Commission forwarded a recommendation of approval on October 21, 2026, provided all staff, 
Planning Commission, and Town Board comments were addressed. 
 
Long Range Planner Lambrecht said the update was undertaken to: 
• align the code with state and federal law, particularly Reed v. Town of Gilbert on 
content neutrality; 
• reflect the Comprehensive Plan and preserve Windsor's small-town character; 
• incorporate community and stakeholder input; 
• clarify outdated standards; and 
• produce a practical, enforceable, user-friendly code tailored to different development 
types. 
She described a year-long process: 
• public outreach through a survey, stakeholder meetings, and an open house; 
• a code analysis with assessment and recommendations reports; 
• several drafts reviewed by staff and the Board at a work session; and 
• posting of the final draft on Project Connect. 
The hearing was noticed in the newspaper and on the Town's website. Second reading is 
scheduled for November 9, with implementation of the regulations anticipated in December. 
 
Mr. Eastman summarized the reorganized Article 17. The assessment found some content-
based standards that needed correction and some standards that needed simplification. His 
main points were: 
• Structure: The code now covers purpose and intent, applicability and exempt signs, 
general display and location standards, illumination, prohibited signs, permanent 
signs, incidental signs, portable and temporary signs, administration, enforcement, 
nonconforming signs, and rules of measurement. 
• Illumination and electronic message centers: Electronic message centers are limited to 
a maximum of 50% of a sign's area, so a monument sign cannot be entirely electronic. 
Neon is limited in the CB zone district. 
• Permanent signs: Standards are set by sign type and zone district. Following the 
August work session, one wall sign and one projecting sign are allowed in the CB 
district. Hanging post signs are a new permanent sign type. 
• Murals: Murals are treated separately from signs and limited to the CB district. A mural 
may cover up to 90% of a building wall, with one mural per building. 
• Portable and temporary signs: These saw the largest changes from the current code. 
• Administration: Changes include a clarified sign permit process, new requirements for 
raceways, a master sign plan option for innovative proposals, maintenance standards, 
and enforcement tied to existing provisions. 
• Nonconforming signs: A legal nonconforming sign may continue but may not be 
extended, expanded, or reconstructed. If damaged by 50% or more, it must be rebuilt 
in compliance with current standards.

3. Resolution No. 2026-56 - A Resolution Expressing Intent to Commit to the Local Jurisdictional 
Requirements Related to Colorado 
Proposition 123 for Affordable Housing - Sandra Mezzetti, Senior Planner 
 
Senior Planner Mezzetti explained that Proposition 123, approved by Colorado voters in 2022, 
requires a local government seeking funding to file a commitment with the state no later than 
November 1, 2026, for the second three-year cycle (2027 onward). In the first cycle, Windsor's 
commitment was 44 units, and the Town met it through 46 qualifying affordable units at 
Jacoby Meadows, a rental property where 46 of 62 units qualified at 60% AMI. 
Senior Planner Mezzetti said House Bill 26-13, passed in May, changed the calculation. It 
previously used a flat 3% per year, or 9% over three years. It now averages annual residential 
building permits over the first cycle and multiplies the result by the job growth rate assigned by 
the Department of Local Affairs, which is 15% for Windsor, and then by three. The resulting 
commitment for the next cycle is approximately 176 units, which she said is not achievable. 
She outlined three options: 
• Adjustment waiver: The waiver is unlikely to be approved. 
• Opt in: The Town would file the commitment knowing it will not be met, which keeps it 
eligible for housing, transportation, and energy funding. The only penalty would be 
sitting out one year of the following cycle, and that cycle's calculation would be based 
on two years. 
• Opt out: The Town would lose access to that funding. 
 
Staff recommended opting in. 
 
Board discussion. Mayor Pro Tem Hallett asked whether the Town could seek the waiver and 
opt in if it was denied. Senior Planner Mezzetti said the Town could apply, but the waiver 
requires extensive criteria and the Town has no valid justification. She had consulted the 
Department of Local Affairs and did not expect approval. Town Manager Hale added that even 
a partial reduction would not bring the commitment within reach. Mayor Cline noted that no 
qualifying projects are in the pipeline to support a waiver, and raised the possibility that the 
state could change the requirements again. Board Member Steinbach said the state had put 
the Town in this position and that he saw no option other than the staff recommendation. 
Senior Planner Mezzetti noted that eligibility also keeps the Town in the running for 
transportation and energy grants.

4. Ordinance No. 2026-1754 - An Ordinance Approving the Service Plan for Hollister Lake 
Metropolitan District and Authorizing the Execution of an Intergovernmental Agreement 
Between the Town and the District 
Town Attorney Money explained that Windsor Municipal Code section 19-1-100 requires 
notice but not a public hearing. Because the published notice referenced a public hearing, he 
asked the Board to open one at first reading. As with all Windsor ordinances, public comment 
would also be taken at second reading on November 9. Mayor Cline opened the public 
hearing. 
Counsel Becker, representing the applicant, Evergreen, summarized the request: 
• District: The Hollister Lake Metropolitan District would finance, construct, and maintain 
public improvements for a development of approximately 33 acres within Town 
boundaries. 
• Improvements: Streets, landscaping, bike lanes, storm water, sanitary sewer, and 
water. All improvements must go through the Town's regular approval processes. 
• Costs and debt: Initial public improvement costs of approximately $17 million with a 
20% contingency, and a requested $25 million debt limit. 
• Mill levy: An aggregate cap of 50 mills, consisting initially of 40 mills for debt and 15 for 
operations and maintenance. Once debt service is imposed, the cap breaks down to 
10 mills for operations and 40 for debt, subject to adjustment. Because the property is 
commercial, adjustments are expected to be small. 
• Other limits: A 40-year maximum debt imposition term, a 12% maximum voter-
approved interest rate, and a 3% maximum underwriting discount. 
• Compliance: Annual reporting, a district website, state election laws, transparency 
notices, annual meetings, public bidding, and budget and audit laws. 
 
Outside Counsel for the Town reviewed the request against the Town's model service plan. 
The Town code caps debt at 34 mills, operations at 20 mills, and the aggregate at 39 mills, 
while the applicant requests 40, 50, and 50 respectively. The ordinance therefore makes 
specific exceptions to Town Code sections 19-1-120 and 19-1-130. She noted that Article 19 
is a guide and that approving variances is within the Board's legislative authority. She stated 
that the statutory criteria were met, assuming the Board approved the higher mill levies and 
the public improvement fee (PIF) was imposed. She also noted a new letter in the packet 
stating that the PIF is intended for the development lots and in-line stores but not for the 
grocery store. The Board could approve, disapprove, or approve with conditions, and 
additional information could be brought back at second reading. 
 
Board discussion. Board Member Jones asked about the PIF term. Counsel Becker said the 
PIF can be pledged only to capital infrastructure debt, the 40-year limit applies, and it cannot 
extend beyond 40 years without approval of a non-developer board. Debt may be refinanced 
within that term. 
 
Board Member Symsack asked whether the letter exempting the grocery store was 
enforceable. Outside Counsel said the letter is a representation by the developer, not an 
agreement, and that the Board could consider adding language to the plan or the 
intergovernmental agreement. Town Manager Hale said the exemption reflected that King 
Soopers does not want its store and fuel station disadvantaged against nearby retailers, 
including a new Walmart. Board Member Symsack asked whether the exemption covers non-
food merchandise. The applicant's representative said the entire Kroger parcel, including the 
Page 8 of 415
 
 
pharmacy, would be exempt. 
 
Board Member Jones asked about ownership. The applicant's representative said Kroger 
currently owns the entire site. Evergreen would purchase the surrounding property, install 
master infrastructure, and own and build the multi-tenant lots, while Kroger would build its own 
store and fuel center. He said King Soopers had confirmed it does not want the PIF applied to 
its sales. 
Mayor Cline asked whether the ordinance acknowledged that the higher mill levies were an 
exception for a commercial development, so that a future residential district could not rely on it 
as precedent. Outside Counsel read section 4 of the ordinance, added after the work session. 
It states that, notwithstanding the mill levy limits in sections 19-1-120 and 19-1-130, the Board 
approves the levies, maximum debt authorization, and debt imposition term in the service plan 
in consideration of the unique nature of the project. Those factors include its commercial 
nature, the cost of public improvements, and the consent of the property owner. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem Hallett asked about an inconsistency in the service plan's acreage. The 
applicant's representative explained that Lot 13, about 10 acres, is owned by the same owners 
but is not part of the development planned to begin next year. The detention ponds and storm 
facilities are at its southern edge, and it may be better combined with future development to 
the east. Outside Counsel acknowledged that the plan's boundary language contained a 
misstatement. She explained that inclusion of Lot 13 would require a petition by the property 
owner, a hearing, and approval by the district board at that time, and the property would also 
have to be within the Town. 
 
No members of the public commented, and Mayor Cline closed the public hearing.

Date: November 9, 2026 
To: Mayor and Town Board 
From: Carlin Malone, Planning Manager 
Re: Resolution No. 2026-57 - A Resolution Approving a Land Use Map Amendment to the Town 
of Windsor 2024 Comprehensive Plan - C. Malone, Planning Manager 
Item #: B.3. 
 
Background / Discussion: 
Per Windsor Municipal Code, Sec. 14-2-200, the Town’s Comprehensive Plan and Land Use Map, 
and/or any amendments thereto, shall be presented to the Town Board for approval by Resolution. The 
Planning Commission adopted a Land Use Map Amendment on November 4, 2026. Therefore, the 
request is to approve the Land Use Map Amendment adopted by the Planning Commission, as set forth 
in PC Resolution No. 2026-01, attached hereto and incorporated herein by reference. 
 
Comprehensive Plan Amendment Approval and Adoption Procedure, Sec. 14-2-200:  
• The Planning Commission may amend, supplement, change or repeal the Comprehensive Plan 
and Land Use map boundaries set forth in the Code. 
• A public hearing at a Planning Commission meeting. 
• Approval and Adoption by the Town Planning Commission.  
• Presented to the Town Board for Approval by Resolution. 
 
Background:  The Town of Windsor had the opportunity to purchase a property, called the River's 
Edge property (formerly known as Hermanns property), for Town recreation and open space. The 
property is located directly outside of the Town's existing Growth Management Area (GMA), south and 
west of the existing GMA boundary identified in the 2024 Town of Windsor Comprehensive Plan Future 
Land Use Map (“Land Use Map”). The Land Use Map Amendment extends the GMA boundary to 
include approximately 75.5 acres of agricultural land, located generally north-east of SH 257, 
approximately one-half mile south of Poudre Trail Road, and west of the Town's water treatment plant. 
 
The area is within unincorporated Weld County and directly adjacent to the Town's Corporate Limits on 
the northern and eastern boundary. Therefore, the area is a logical extension of the Town’s GMA 
boundary. The GMA boundary is known as the “Windsor Three-Mile Plan Area”, required by the State 
(C.R.S. 31-12-105), and described further in the Town’s 2024 Comprehensive Plan. 
 
The subject area contains the land use designation of "Parks and Open Space" on the Town's Future 
Land Use Map. 
 
Comprehensive Plan Amendment 
Staff has reviewed the Land Use Map Amendment (LUMA) in accordance with the Comprehensive 
Plan Amendment Criteria, as described within this report. 
On November 4, 2026, the Town’s Planning Commission approved and adopted the land use map 
amendment.

Driver Safety on U.S. Highway 287 (CO) 
Applicant:  Northern Integrated Supply Project (NISP) Water Activity Enterprise 
NFWF Priority: Big game winter range and migration corridors 
Grant Due Date: November 12th, 2026 
 
The RESTORE Colorado Program is a competitive grant program administered by the National 
Fish and Wildlife Foundation (NFWF) in partnership with the State of Colorado, the Natural 
Resources Conservation Service (NRCS), and various private foundations (e.g., Chevron, Gates 
Family Foundation). The program funds landscape-scale conservation projects that protect, 
restore, and enhance vital habitats. 
 
The RESTORE Colorado Program was selected because it funds the implementation of 
conservation priorities identified by the State of Colorado. Our project falls within the big game 
winter range and migration corridors priority, which is a key focus for the 2027 funding cycle. 
Funds will support the construction of a wildlife underpass at the Confluence Bridge location and 
its associated wildlife mitigation system along the realigned U.S. Highway 287 north of Laporte, 
Colorado. This project is integrated into the larger effort to relocate the 8.75-mile stretch of U.S. 
Highway 287 for the construction of Glade Reservoir. While a separate underpass at Owl Creek 
meets regulatory mitigation, the Confluence crossing is identified as an essential, non-mitigation 
enhancement that completes a full, functional wildlife system. 
 
The primary activities we are seeking NFWF funding include:  
- Construction of the Confluence Underpass: Building the large, dedicated structure (a 125’ 
single span bridge with minimum 22’-4” vertical clearance) identified in the final, 
approved designs. 
- Installation of Exclusion Fencing: Placing permanent wildlife exclusion fencing to funnel 
animals from the highway right-of-way directly toward the Confluence structure. 
- Installation of Egress Features: Implementing associated safety features, including game 
ramps and deer guards, to ensure system integrity and allow wildlife to safely exit the 
highway corridor. 
 
This project addresses a significant safety issue on the U.S. 287 corridor. The Colorado 
Department of Transportation (CDOT) US 287 Ted’s Place to Wyoming Border Study (2023) 
confirmed that Wildlife-Vehicle Collisions (WVCs) are the single most common crash type in 
the study area. The underpass system is projected to achieve a significant reduction in WVCs 
along the fenced segment. The project is shovel-ready with final designs complete. Placement of 
the Confluence underpass was informed by a 2021 Wildlife Movement Monitoring Report by 
Pinyon Environmental that used wildlife cameras and data from Colorado Parks & Wildlife 
(CPW) and CDOT. The Confluence wildlife underpass will help maintain critical east-west 
movement for elk, mule deer, and pronghorn.

Glade Reservoir. The existing highway is a barrier to east-west wildlife movement, and the new reservoir 
is expected to shift movement patterns around the project area. The Confluence Bridge underpass will 
help maintain habitat connectivity for elk, mule deer, white-tailed deer, pronghorn, and other wildlife 
while directing animals away from the traveled roadway. This is particularly important along a corridor 
where wildlife-vehicle collisions are a documented safety concern. The underpass location and design 
were informed by wildlife movement monitoring and coordinated with Colorado Parks and Wildlife and 
the Colorado Department of Transportation. Final designs are complete, allowing the project to be 
constructed as part of the larger highway realignment. RESTORE Colorado funding would help close a 
critical funding gap and support timely implementation of the full crossing system.

Date: November 9, 2026 
To: Mayor and Town Board 
From: Sandra Mezzetti, Senior Planner 
Re: Public Hearing and Consideration of a Wireless Telecommunication Facility (Tower) Site 
Location and Consideration of a Conditional Use Permit to allow a fifty-foot (50') Stealth Silo 
Wireless Telecommunication Facility (Tower), Trautman Subdivision 4th filing, lot 1, 29918 
County Road 17 - Timberline Church, property owner,  Brandon Alveson, owner's 
representative; Mike Mathison, Horizon Tower, applicant, Trevor Crow, Hoss Consulting, Inc., 
authorized representative. 
Item #: C.1. 
 
Background / Discussion: 
Executive Summary 
The applicant, Mr. Mike Mathisen, on behalf of Horizon Tower, future tower owner, has submitted 
two applications to the Town: 
1. Wireless Telecommunication Tower (site plan) 
2. Conditional Use Permit – to construct a new telecommunications tower. 
 
The proposal is generally located south of Crossroads Boulevard and east of Weld County Road 
17, on an existing agricultural property within the Town. 
 
Staff has reviewed the proposed telecommunications facility, site plan (location), in accordance 
with review criteria set forth in Section 16-5-40 of the Town’s Municipal Code and a Conditional 
Use Permit, for a fifty-foot (50’) telecommunication tower, in accordance with the review criteria set 
forth in Section 14-2-130 of the Town’s Municipal Code. Both applications comply with the 
requirements set forth in the Land Use Code. The Planning Commission has reviewed and concurs 
that the applications comply with these requirements, as reflected in the recommendation section. 
 
Under federal law, a local government may not "materially inhibit" the provision of wireless service, 
including the ability of providers to improve service or densify their networks. Local governments 
are preempted from regulating wireless facilities based on the environmental or health effects of 
EMF (Electromagnet Field) or RMF (Radio Frequency) emissions. Items for review and 
consideration include: 
• Location – Where the facility is located 
• Aesthetic – What it looks like 
• Height – How tall is necessary 
 
Planning Commission Summary 
At the meeting of October 21, 2026, there was no public comment during the public hearing for the 
subject applications. During the public hearing, the applicant presented a thorough presentation 
pertaining to their application, satisfying infrastructure needs in this area. 
The Planning Commission raised the following questions and received clarification: 
• Co-location – is there adequate room for multiple carriers with the proposed tower only 
Page 39 of 415
being fifty feet, and will there be multiple antennas? 
Each carrier will be at a different height and have its own antennas. The lower carriers will 
have less coverage due to the lower heights. Each carrier will determine what coverage 
gaps they are trying to fill and if their location on the tower will achieve their coverage goal. 
• What is the construction material of the silo? 
The silo will be constructed from structural-grade fiberglass, which allows the RF 
wavelength to penetrate through. The tower inside is constructed with steel. There will be 
no lattice or structural beams visible from outside the tower. 
• Will the equipment be installed and mounted behind the fence at a height that it will screen 
all equipment. 
Equipment within the fence will be installed at a height that is screened by the fence and 
landscaping proposed. 
• What type of composite material is the fence? 
The fence material is similar to Trex decking material. 
• How will the landscaping be kept alive until it is established? 
Temporary irrigation may be installed from water on the Timberline farm campus, or a water 
tank will be installed behind the fence to water the landscaping until it is established. This 
will take approximately two years. 
• Are there existing trees and vegetation in near proximity to the proposed tower? 
Yes. Existing vegetation is visible in the photo simulations.  
• Were other existing tower sites considered for co-location? 
Four other sites were considered for co-location prior to selecting the proposed location. 
However, the co-location height on existing towers did not provide sufficient coverage to fill 
the gap that the anchor provider was trying to address. 
 
Once the public hearing was closed, the Planning Commission forwarded to the Town Board the 
following, with no conditions or comments on the application: 
1. Wireless Telecommunications Facilities – Site Plan (Tower Location) 
A recommendation of approval of the stealth silo wireless telecommunications tower location, by a 
vote of 7-0, based on the application satisfying all applicable review criteria requirements set forth 
in Section 16-5-40 of the Town’s Municipal Code, as presented, subject to all remaining Planning 
Commission and staff comments being addressed 
2. Conditional Use Permit 
A recommendation of approval of the Conditional Use Permit to Town Board, by a vote of 7-0, 
based on the application satisfying all applicable conditional use permit criteria requirements set 
forth in Section 14-2-130 of the Town’s Municipal Code, as presented, and subject to all remaining 
Planning Commission and staff comments being addressed, subject to the telecommunications 
tower location approval. 
 
Application 
The applicant is requesting approval for the location of a new telecommunications tower utilizing 
2,500 square feet on the northern portion of a 890,802 square foot parcel (20.45 acres), located at 
Page 40 of 415
29918 County Road (CR) 17 within Lot 1, Trautman Subdivision 4th filing, and within the General 
Commercial (GC) zone district. The Town’s Municipal Code, Sec. 16-5-40(a), allows for the 
establishment of wireless telecommunication towers, not to exceed fifty feet (50’) in height, for the 
purpose and intent of accommodating the communication needs of the Town’s residents and 
businesses. 
 
The applicant is requesting approval of a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) to allow the construction of 
a fifty (50) foot wireless telecommunications tower (silo), and associated equipment yard. Per Sec. 
16-5-40(k)(2): Conditional use grants. Wireless communication facilities may be allowed as 
conditional uses on private property or public property owned by an entity other than the Town 
within all zone districts, if approved as a conditional use in compliance with the Town's standards

Horizon Tower: Windsor 50’ Silo Tower: Zoning Narrative 
Horizon Tower is proposing a 50’ tall “stealth silo" communication tower & facility to be 
located on a parcel zoned General Commercial, owned by the Timberline Church.   
This proposed communication tower is located east of County Rd 17 and south of 
Crossroads Blvd and is designed to appear to be a farm-style silo.  This facility would be 
an unmanned facility with no water or sewer needs.  The 50’x50’ fenced compound 
would host telecommunications base radios and equipment for up to four carriers - 
serving the voice, data, and emergency needs of the nearby residents and roads.   
Landscaping will surround the composite fencing to help shield the view of the 
compound.  The proposed gravel road to the Compound would also facilitate parking 
outside of the Compound’s locked gate.  Because this stealth silo is to be 50’ tall there 
is no FAA requirement for tower top lighting nor strobe lights. 
Ground equipment would have work lighting that would be directed by a timer; there will 
be no constant lighting on the ground nor the tower.  There are no anticipated adverse 
effects to adjacent properties.  The ground equipment for the carriers creates minimal 
sound, approximately the same level as a small air conditioner.      
The Compound for the Tower would have Stymie Lock to allow several locks to be 
placed to access the inside of the Compound for the respective carriers, power 
company, and emergency services to access twenty-four hours every day.  Tower 
signage would show Horizon Tower’s Site ID Number and emergency contact phone 
number.  Tower signage would illustrate “keep out” and/or “no trespassing”.   
Impact to local traffic or pedestrian circulation would be occasional visits by 
maintenance vehicles to visit the Site, which is anticipated to occur on average once or 
twice every month, year-round.   Time to construct the stealth silo is estimated to be 
thirty to forty-five days.   
Driven by the need to provide better, faster infill service of voice, data, and emergency 
support to this growing community, we kindly ask for your consideration and approval of 
this telecommunications facility.

Sec. 16-5-40. Wireless telecommunications facilities. 
(a) Statement of purpose, intent and applicability. The within Town of Windsor Telecommunications Land Use 
Code (hereinafter "Code") is established with the purpose and intent of accommodating the communications 
needs of the Town's residents and businesses, while protecting the public health, safety and general welfare 
of the community. The Town finds these regulations are necessary in order to:  
(1) Facilitate the provision of telecommunications services to the residents and businesses within the 
Town.  
(2) Minimize the adverse visual effects of towers through careful design and siting standards.  
(3) Avoid potential damage to adjacent properties from tower failure through structural standards, radio 
frequency emissions standards and setback requirements.  
(4) Encourage and maximize the use of existing and approved towers, buildings and other structures to 
accommodate new telecommunication antennae in order to reduce the number of towers needed to 
serve the community.  
This Code shall not govern any tower or wireless communication facilities owned or operated by a federally 
licensed amateur radio station operator or used exclusively for received-only antennae, provided that all other 
zoning district requirements are met.  
(b) Definitions. As used in this Section, the following terms shall have the following meanings:  
Director means the Community Development Director for the Town or authorized designee.  
Owner means an individual or entity holding an ownership interest in property that is subject to this Code, as 
well as any other applicant for approval under this Code who is acting with authority of a record owner. Once any 
approvals are granted pursuant to this Code, any references to owner shall also mean operators, managers or any 
other person or entity authorized by an owner with the responsibility for the approved facilities. Any other such 
person or entity shall be jointly and severally liable with an owner for any violations of this Code associated with 
the approved facilities.  
Telecommunications site means the site or lot utilized for an unmanned telecommunications facility that 
uses radio signals to transmit conversation, visual imagery or data to a user.  
Wireless communication facilities means facilities that transmit and/or receive electromagnetic wireless 
communications signals. It includes antennae, nodes, microwave dishes, horns and other types of equipment for 
the transmission or receipt of such signals, communications towers or similar structures supporting said 
equipment, equipment buildings, equipment cabinets, equipment closets, parking areas and other accessory 
development. The following types of facilities are included within this definition:  
Alternative communication facility means a communication facility with an alternative design that 
camouflages or conceals the presence of antennae or towers such as, but not limited to, artificial trees, clock 
and bell towers and steeples.  
Antenna shall mean, but shall not be limited to), any exterior transmitting or receiving devices 
mounted on a pole, tower, building or structure and used in communications that radiate or capture 
electromagnetic waves, digital signals, analog signals, radio frequencies (excluding radar signals), wireless 
telecommunications signals or other communication signals;  
Freestanding communication facility means a communication facility that is mounted and supported on 
the roof or any rooftop appurtenance of a legally existing building or structure.  
Tower means any structure that is designed and constructed primarily for the purpose of supporting 
one (1) or more antennae, including self-supporting towers, guy towers or monopole towers. The term 
Page 49 of 415
includes, but is not limited to, radio and television transmission towers, microwave towers, common carrier 
towers, cellular telephone towers and alternative telecommunication facilities.  
Wall-mounted communication facility means a communication facility that is mounted and supported 
entirely on the wall of a legally existing building, including the walls of architectural features such as 
parapets, chimneys and similar appurtenances.  
(c) General requirements, location and design criteria. In addition to the applicable zoning requirements for the 
telecommunications site (including, where required, any variance approved by the Board of Adjustment 
pursuant to this Code), all wireless communication facilities and telecommunications sites shall be designed 
and located in compliance with the criteria set forth in this Section. The Director or, where Town Board 
action is required under this Chapter or any other provisions of this Code or Home Rule Charter, the Town 
Board may withhold approval of any facility that does not meet any of these criteria:  
(1) Applications must contain an applicant's name, address, telephone number and an emergency 
telephone number at which a representative of the applicant may be reached twenty-four (24) hours 
per day, seven (7) days per week. Should any information found within an application change, the 
applicant must provide updated information in writing to the Town within a reasonable period 
following the change. The application shall include a clear map, photographic rendering or other 
reliable depiction of the location of all facilities for which approval is sought by the applicant. Such 
map, photographic rendering or other depiction shall be to scale and shall identify with specificity the 
location, size, appearance and height of all wireless communication facilities proposed by the 
applicant.  
(2) Wireless communication facilities shall be co-located with other wireless communication facilities or 
public utilities whenever possible and to the extent consistent with the scale and appearance of 
surrounding structures.  
(3) The applicant shall:  
a. Demonstrate how the proposed communication site fits into the overall communication network 
for the community, confirming the necessity for the site.  Silo design and surrounding landscaping 
relate to surrounding aesthetics of growing area. 
b. To the extent that it seeks approval to address gaps in coverage, demonstrate by a 
preponderance of the evidence that there are no viable alternatives to remedy gaps in the 
applicant's existing network. RF Justification attached, showing there are no nearby structures to 
allow the ability to collocate on.  Nearest available structure is 1.74 miles away.   
c. To the extent that the applicant provides services under a license granted by a governmental 
authority, that a failure to approve the application will result in the applicant's inability to provide 
the minimum coverage it is required to provide pursuant to its license and any applicable law. 
FAA attached.   
(4) The location and development of wireless communication facilities should, to the maximum extent 
possible, preserve the existing character of the site's topography and vegetation.  Minimal earthmoving 
required and no trees are expected to be removed, preserving the original site conditions. 
(5) Wireless communication facilities should be designed and located to avoid dominant silhouettes and to 
preserve view corridors of surrounding areas to the maximum extent possible.  Silo design helps to 
preserve historical character of the growing neighborhood.  
(6) The visual impact of wireless communication facilities shall be mitigated through the use of alternative 
communication facilities and compatible architectural elements such as colors, textures, surfaces, scale 
and character. The facilities shall be screened with vegetation, structures or topographical features. 
The facility should be integrated to the maximum extent possible through its location and design, into 
the natural setting and the structural environment of the area. Accessory equipment in areas of high 
visibility shall, where possible, be sited below the ridgeline or designed (e.g., placed underground,

depressed or located behind earth berms or structures) to minimize its profile. Photo Simulations 
attached. 
(7) Where possible, wireless communication facilities should be concealed in accessory structures 
consistent with the architectural scale and character of the area.  Stealth Silo design allows for 
antennae and equipment to be concealed within the fiberglass façade while ground equipment will be 
shielded by composite fence surrounded by suggested landscaping by Windsor.  
(8) Roof-mounted and wall-mounted facilities shall be architecturally compatible with and colored to 
match the building or structure to which they are attached. Wall-mounted facilities shall be mounted 
as flush to the building wall as possible. A wall-mounted facility may encroach a maximum of thirty (30) 
inches into the required setback for the building to which it is attached, but shall not extend across any 
property line.  Not applicable to Silo design. 
(9) Freestanding wireless communication facilities shall not be artificially lighted, unless required by the 
Federal Aviation Administration or other applicable governmental authority. If lighting is required, the 
Town may review available lighting alternatives and approve the design that will cause the least 
disturbance to surrounding views. Lighting must be shielded or directed to the greatest extent possible 
so as to minimize the amount of light falling onto nearby properties, particularly in and near residential 
areas.  Proposed silo will not artificially lighted.   
(10) Towers shall be designed to allow for co-location to the maximum extent possible.  Proposed Silo will 
be collectable for future Carriers. 
(11) No portion of any antenna array may extend beyond any property line.  No part of the project extends 
over any property line. 
(12) Wireless communication facilities should be screened to mitigate visual impacts to the maximum 
extent practicable.  Silo design and shielding composite fence surrounded by vegetation mitigates all 
visual impacts.   
(13) The use of any portion of a wireless communication facility for promotional or advertising purposes, 
including but not limited to company name, phone numbers, banners, streamers and balloons is 
prohibited. The Town may require the installation of informational signage, including safety and owner 
contact information.  No words or letters will be placed on Silo.  Emergency contact information and 
site identification will be installed on gate of Compound. 
(14) Fencing should not be used extensively and shall be supplemented with vegetation and other barriers 
to screen a wireless communication facility. Security fencing shall be designed in such a fashion as to 
blend into the character of the existing environment.  Compound fencing will be composite to last 
longer than natural wood and will shield Carrier equipment.  Surrounding landscaping will be installed 
and maintained to shield composite fence. 
(15) The wireless communication facility shall be designed, maintained and operated as required by 
applicable Federal Communication Commission licenses and regulations.  All FAA regulations and 
requirements will be satisfied. 
(16) Every application for approval of any wireless communication facility shall be accompanied by the 
owner's good-faith projection of anticipated technological advances for all antennae contemplated 
within the application, together with the owner's good-faith plan addressing anticipated technological 
advances to assure that the wireless communication facility does not experience functional 
obsolescence over time. In the event the owner cannot provide the projection or commitment 
required in this Subsection, the agreement shall require the owner to submit to periodic review of 
wireless communication facility obsolescence by the Town as a condition of continued approval.  As 
new technologies are applied to Silo, outside appearance would not be affected.   
(17) Lattice towers shall not be approved under any circumstances within the Town.  Silo design eliminates 
lattice.

(d) Compliance with setback requirements. All wireless facilities shall comply with the setback requirements 
within the zone district in which the telecommunications site is located.  Proposed Silo will comply with all 
setback requirements.   
(e) Towers. Towers shall not exceed fifty (50) feet in height, as measured from the natural grade.  Silo is 
designed to be fifty feet with no equipment to exceed 50’ from natural grade.  
(f) Radio frequency emissions (RFEs). All owners and operators of wireless communication facilities shall comply 
with federal regulations for radio frequency emissions (RFEs). At the time of application for a wireless 
communication facility, and thereafter at the request of the Town upon complaint (but not more than 
annually), the owner or operator shall submit a report that provides cumulative field measurements of RFEs 
of all antennae installed at the subject site and that compares the results with established federal standards. 
If, upon review, or at any time any wireless communication facility with the Town is operational, the Town 
finds that the facility does not meet federal regulations, the Town may require corrective action within a 
reasonable period of time and, if not corrected, may require removal of the wireless communication facilities 
at the owner's expense. Any reasonable costs incurred by the Town, including reasonable consulting costs to 
verify compliance with these requirements for removal of wireless communication facilities, shall be borne 
by the owner.  Attached are outside consultant, Biwabkos Consultants LLC, EF Safety & NIER Analysis Report 
along with RF Design Analysis for the proposed Timberline Church location. 
(g) Structural integrity of towers, freestanding communications facilities. To ensure the structural integrity of 
towers, freestanding communication facilities and wall-mounted communication facilities upon which other 
wireless communication facilities may be mounted, the owner of such structures shall ensure that they are of 
sufficient structural strength to accommodate reasonable co-location and are maintained in compliance with 
the following standards: any standards contained in applicable Town building codes; the applicable standards 
for towers published by the Electronic Industries Alliance, as amended from time to time (presently TIA/EIA-
222-G as of January 1, 2006); and all other applicable codes of the Town. In addition to any other applicable 
standards and requirements, the following shall apply to all structures upon which wireless communication 
facilities are located:  
(1) Sufficient anti-climbing measures must be incorporated into each facility to reduce potential for 
trespass and injury.  Compound fencing will be secured by stymie lock and “No Trespassing” signs will 
be installed at gate of Compound.  Base of Silo Tower will be locked to prevent unauthorized people 
from climbing tower. 
(2) No guy wires employed may be anchored within the area in front of any principal buildings or 
structures on a parcel.  Silo design eliminates any guy wires; no guy wires are to be utilized.   
(3) All wireless communication facilities shall comply with the power line clearance standards set forth by 
the Colorado Public Utilities Commission.  Silo location complies with power line clearance standards.  
(4) All wireless communication facilities must be structurally designed and physically sited so that they do 
not pose a potential hazard to nearby residences or surrounding properties or improvements. Any 
tower or freestanding communication facility shall be designed and maintained to withstand, without 
failure, maximum forces expected from wind, tornadoes, hurricanes and other natural occurrences, 
when the facility is fully loaded with antennae, transmitters, other wireless communications facilities 
and camouflaging. Initial demonstration of compliance with this requirement shall be provided via 
submission of a report included in the application to the Director, prepared by a structural engineer 
licensed in the State, describing the structure, specifying the number and type of antennae it is 
designed to accommodate, providing the basis for the calculations done and documenting the actual 
calculations performed. Proof of ongoing compliance shall be provided upon request. If, upon 
inspection, the Town concludes that a wireless communication facility fails to comply with such codes 
and standards and constitutes a danger to persons or property, then, upon notice being provided to 
the owner of a wireless communication facility, the owner shall have thirty (30) days to bring such 
facility into compliance with such standards. Upon good cause shown by the owner, the Director may 
extend such compliance period, not to exceed ninety (90) days from the date of said notice. If the

owner fails to bring such facility into compliance within said time limit, the Town may remove such 
facility at the owner's expense.  Silo is designed to be structurally sound to withstand expected outside

loading of Carrier equipment and maximum area wind loads throughout the year.  Silo will be designed

(h) Application fees. The fee and any additional application-related financial requirements for wireless 
communication facilities shall be fixed and published by resolution of the Town Board, and any amendments 
to such fees and financial requirements shall likewise require a resolution of the Town Board.  
(i) Wireless communication facilities in public rights-of-way. Proposed Silo location is out of Public ROW. 
(1) Subject to the Town's applicable right-of-way permitting process administered by the Director or 
designee, wireless communication facilities located solely within public rights-of-way shall be 
permitted based upon the following criteria:  
a. The facilities must be placed on existing poles, upon replacement poles of the same dimensions 
or upon replacement poles of a dimension that would otherwise be permitted under existing 
regulations for any utility operating within the Town.  
b. Any necessary wiring or cabling shall be located within the pole or camouflaged to blend with the 
color and texture of the pole.  
c. The area of the facilities on any pole shall be designed to minimize the visual impact of such 
facilities. The facilities shall add no more than five (5) feet of additional height to any pole and 
shall not project outward in any direction a distance of more than eighteen (18) inches.  
d. Any ground equipment shall be buried or screened by landscaping approved by the Town. The 
owner of such facilities shall irrigate and maintain such landscaping.  
e. Notwithstanding the administrative approval contemplated in this Section, if an applicant is 
proposing to add to the total number of poles located in the area impacted by its application, the 
request shall require a separate application and approval, unless otherwise agreed in writing 
between the Town and the applicant/owner.  
f. The applicant shall provide confirmation that it has the written consent of any owner of poles or 
other structures upon or to which the applicant intends to fasten wireless communication 
facilities.  
(2) Any permit customarily required for the location of facilities within public rights-of-way may be waived 
by the Director, following review of the applicant's proposed facilities.  
(j) Co-location.  
(1) The shared use of existing towers or other freestanding communication facilities upon which wireless 
communications facilities can be mounted shall be preferred to the construction of new facilities. As a 
condition of approval of any tower or other freestanding communications facilities, the applicant shall 
be required to allow co-location on such facilities in the future if: (1) the facility is capable of 
supporting co-location, (2) the entity wishing to co-locate is willing to pay fair market value for the 
space; and (3) the Town requests such co-location.  Silo is designed to facilitate collocation of multiple 
Carriers.  
(2) The application for any wireless communication facility involving a new tower or other freestanding 
communication facility shall include evidence that reasonable efforts have been made to co-locate 
within or upon an existing wireless communication facility within a reasonable distance of the 
proposed site, regardless of municipal boundaries. The applicant must demonstrate that the proposed 
wireless communication facility cannot be accommodated on existing facilities due to one (1) or more 
of the following reasons:

(k) Town action on applications.  
(1) The Director may, in his or her discretion, refer any application for wireless communication facilities for 
review by the Planning Commission and Town Board consistent with the requirements of this Code for 
conditional use permits. If not so referred or not otherwise required by this Code, the Director shall 
review and determine any application for siting of wireless communication facilities within the Town in 
accordance with the standards set forth in this Code. If approved, the Director shall issue a written 
statement of approval containing any conditions for such approval. If denied, the Director shall likewise 
issue a written statement of denial to the applicant.  
(2) Conditional use grants. Wireless communication facilities may be allowed as conditional uses on 
private property or public property owned by an entity other than the Town within all zone districts, if 
approved as a conditional use in compliance with the Town's standards and procedures for approval of 
conditional use grants as set forth in Section 14-2-130. Wireless communication facilities may be 
allowed as conditional uses on property owned by the Town in any zone district if approved as a

(3) Tower approvals. All applications for siting of any tower shall first be reviewed by the Director and then 
referred to the Planning Commission for review and recommendation. After the Planning Commission 
has completed its review of a tower application, the matter shall be referred to the Town Board for 
determination of the application with due regard to the Planning Commission's recommendation. The 
Town Board shall conduct a public hearing before considering any application for the construction of a 
tower. Notice of the Town Board public hearing shall be conspicuously posted upon the 
telecommunications site, shall be published in a newspaper of general circulation within the Town and 
shall be mailed by first-class mail to all property owners within a radius of three hundred (300) feet 
from the proposed tower site. The applicant shall provide the Director with written verification that all 
mailings required by this Section have been issued no less than thirty (30) days prior to the Town Board 
hearing. The Town Board may continue any public hearing to assure a full and fair presentation of 
evidence. The Town Board's decision on whether to approve or deny an application for construction of 
a tower shall be issued within thirty (30) days of the conclusion of the Town Board public hearing. The 
Town Board's decision on whether to approve or deny an application for construction of a tower shall 
be in writing, based upon evidence presented to the Town Board at the public hearing and shall take 
into account a recommendation of the Planning Commission. Nothing in this Section shall be deemed 
to prohibit the Planning Commission from conducting a public hearing upon any tower application 
referred to it, provided that the Planning Commission hearing complies in all respects with the notice 
requirements of this Section.  
(l) Telecommunications provider agreement. Any approval for the installation, erection or construction of

Horizon Tower agrees to cure any non-compliance within thirty days or have the facility removed by 
Tower at Horizon Tower’s expense.   
(3) Removal of any wireless communication facility that is not operated for a continuous period of one

Code, and the Town may remove and dispose of the same at the owner's expense. If there are two (2)
