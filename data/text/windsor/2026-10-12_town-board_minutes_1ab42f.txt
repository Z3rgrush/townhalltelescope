5. Board Liaison Reports 
 
 
Town Board Member Steinbach - Larimer County Behavioral Health Policy Council, 
Windsor Housing Authority 
Town Board Member Steinbach reported that the Larimer County Behavioral Health Policy 
Council would not begin meeting until December. The Windsor Housing Authority met 
twice in the past month because its first regularly scheduled meeting, on the 15th, lacked a 
quorum for a needed action item. The primary issue was banking services. Following a 
bank merger, the Authority's existing bank had provided poor service, so the Authority 
voted at a special meeting to move its primary banking to South State Bank. South State 
has no Windsor facility, and the Authority is still working out how best to serve residents. 
The Authority was to meet again the following week. 
Town Board Member Jones - Chamber of Commerce, Tree Board 
Town Board Member Jones reported that the September Tree Board meeting was 
canceled but a work session was held on September 15. The Tree Board partnered with 
the Clearview Library on a guided walking tour at Main Park that covered 14 to 15 trees 
and drew 25 to 30 people. At the September 23 Chamber of Commerce meeting, members 
recapped the golf tournament and planned the gala. The gala was held October 2, and he 
believed it sold out with more than 200 attendees. 
Town Board Member Peltz - Clearview Library Board, Housing Literate Leaders, Great 
Western Trail Authority 
Town Board Member Peltz said she did not attend the September 24 Clearview Library 
Board meeting and relayed highlights from the library director. The meeting lasted three 
and a half hours. The Library of Things policy remains in progress, with discussion of 
waivers and of distinguishing no-waiver kits from maker-space kits that may require them. 
Alternate trustee Michael Kessler will become a trustee, and the board will interview two 
candidates for the alternate position on October 22. Dr. Adam Murray, executive director of 
the Marmot Library Network, presented on the new consortium that Clearview has joined, 
which expands physical and digital collections. The Summer Adventure program set 
records, including the highest adult participation and nearly 3,000 total participants. The 
director's evaluation and board self-evaluation are in progress and will be discussed 
October 29, along with review of the Colorado Public Library standards and strategic 
planning goals. 
 
Town Board Member Peltz reported that the Great Western Trail Authority lacked a 
quorum at its most recent meeting and could not vote. The main need is to update the trail 
map and promotional materials, which predate completion of the trail. She also reported on 
Housing Literate Leaders, whose first session on September 16 covered introductions and 
terminology. Larimer and Weld Counties presented housing data and common barriers to 
housing. The next session will address AMI levels, vouchers, zoning, NIMBYism, funding 
gaps, and affordable homeownership models. According to the state demographer, 80 
percent of Larimer County households and 65.5 percent of Weld County households 
cannot afford available homes. The NoCo Housing Fund offers down payment assistance, 
with a goal of a $20 million revolving investment fund. She also noted that Estes Park 
 
 
directs its lodging tax to affordable housing and childcare. 
Town Board Member Symsack – Parks, Recreation and Culture Advisory Board, Windsor 
Arts Commission 
Town Board Member Symsack reported that the Parks, Recreation and Culture Advisory 
Board meeting was canceled for lack of a quorum and that two positions are vacant. The 
Windsor Arts Commission approved revisions to the public art policy, which: 
• rename "community art" as "public art"; 
• add an AI policy requiring original work and disclosure of AI use; 
• streamline the review and record-keeping process; and 
• expand the Town Manager's authority, including some veto authority. 
 
The Commission approved the Halfway Homestead Red Barn mural design by Katie 
Curso, and the mural has been installed on 15th Street. The Commission also noted 
positive feedback on the sculpture loan program and the turnout at the reveal. Signage at 
the Community Recreation Center has been installed. The Noble Winds benches were 
expected within the week, and the Ray King entryway sculpture is in production. 
Town Board Member Klimek - Poudre River Trail Corridor Authority, Water and Sewer 
Board 
Town Board Member Klimek reported that there was no report from the Poudre River Trail 
Corridor Authority. At the September Water and Sewer Board meeting, members 
discussed a raw water dedication and an upcoming homeschool day. They also saw a leak 
detection demonstration and a demonstration of alternative turf grass at the Community 
Development location. 
Mayor Pro Tem Hallett - Planning Commission, RE-4 School District, Historic Preservation 
Commission 
Mayor Pro Tem Hallett announced that the Historic Preservation Commission would meet 
Wednesday and would hold its November meeting at Town Hall at 5:45 PM on November 
4. For the Weld RE-4 School District, he reported that most schools are below maximum 
enrollment capacity, while Orchard Hill is at 104 percent, which is why modular buildings 
were used, and Windsor High School is at 94 percent. The district passed several policy 
updates by consent, including the salary schedule and a child abuse reporting policy with a 
tighter reporting timeframe, both driven by state changes. A policy on how parents and 
staff work together on behalf of non-verbal or disabled children drew public comment, and 
the superintendent and board will re-review it. The next RE-4 meeting is October 19. The 
Planning Commission did not meet last week. For the North Front Range MPO, he 
reported: 
• discussion of ozone compliance challenges after a hot summer; 
• a request that members submit planned housing and business developments to 
UrbanSim for the regional travel demand model, which will inform the 2055 
Regional Transportation Plan; 
• debate over whether the congestion management process update is worthwhile, 
since congestion has not changed meaningfully; 
• that Windsor has no delayed projects, while Loveland and Evans do; and 
• that the price of the MPO's prospective office space was lowered.

a. Financial Report August 2026 
 
b. Police Statistical Report - September 2026 
Finance Director Dean Moyer summarized the August financial report in the board packet: 
• Sales tax: August collections were slightly below August of last year, but year-to-
date collections were up roughly 3 to 5 percent over 2025. 
• Use tax: Collections continued to lag, down about 39 percent from last year. This is 
largely because single-family building permits are down by about 100, and the 
budget had assumed 375 permits for the year. He did not expect the Town to reach 
that figure and said staff would monitor construction use tax going into next year, 
hoping for several large commercial permits in the coming months. 
• Property tax: Collections were at about 96 percent, essentially where they were at 
this time last year. 
• Expenditures: These were generally in line with expectations at the end of August. 
The exception was one fund's operations, which ran high because the first payment 
on newly issued plant construction debt came due in July and was not budgeted at 
the start of the year. 
 
3. Communications from Town Manager 
 
 
a. Monthly Board Report - Focus Area Updates 
 
1. Strategic Growth 
Increase attainable housing by 10% for those who work in Windsor so they 
can live in Windsor (84.17% completed) 
Develop a Down Payment Assistance Program for Town employees, 
first responders and teachers (80% completed) 
Staff is working with Pikes Peak Capital to develop a Down Payment 
Assistance Program for Town Employees, first responders, and Windsor 
school employees. This will assist those who work in Windsor to be able 
to live in Windsor. 
Page 6
 
 
The consultant scheduled three focus group meetings for town staff for 
the week of September 21, 2026. The purpose of these groups was to 
better understand the needs and challenges of those looking to purchase 
a home in Windsor. 
A survey for town staff was posted on August 27, 2026. We received 
103 total responses, representing a 33.2% response rate. 
2. Sustainable Infrastructure 
Provide a safe and reliable water supply for the community (37% completed) 
Staff will contact additional treatment providers to see if temporary 
treatment capacity is available until the Cobb Lake Plant is up and 
running. Staff is reviewing current water service agreements to ensure 
proper notice provisions are met when seeking additional capacity. Staff 
has met with other water providers to explore short-term treatment. They 
are waiting to hear back from those entities and have a draft lease within 
the next few months. 
The City of Fort Collins has approved its water leasing policy. It is now 
drafting a water lease agreement for the Town of Windsor to lease 
additional water supply and treatment capacity in their system until the 
Northern Integrated Supply Project (NISP) is up and running. 
3. Vibrant and Healthy Economy 
Leverage entertainment opportunities in and around Windsor to capture 
revenue (50% completed) 
We are working to identify optimal travel corridors and residents' needs 
to support increased public transportation use. A new bus stop has been 
added along Fair Grounds/CR5, and we remain committed to expanding 
services to deliver a more robust community and regional transportation 
solution. 
 
Economic Development is collaborating with the Chamber director to 
create a town-wide events calendar to support and market activities 
happening throughout our community. While there is an initial buy-in, 
progress is currently limited by capacity constraints. We do, however, 
have a partner eager to see this initiative move forward and has verbally 
committed to supporting its development.
