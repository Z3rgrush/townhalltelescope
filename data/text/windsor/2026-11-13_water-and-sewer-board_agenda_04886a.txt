Subject: Request for Waiver or Reduction of Water and Sewer Fees – Windsor-Severance 
Fire Rescue Training Facility 
 
Dear Members of the Water and Sewer Board: 
On behalf of Windsor Severance Fire Rescue (WSFR), I respectfully request that the Board 
recommend waiving, or in the alternative reducing, the water and sewer Plant Investment Fees 
(PIFs) for the WSFR Training Facility being built at 31750 Great Western Drive, Windsor, CO 
80550. We ask that the Board advise on this request at the November 13, 2026 meeting. 
These fees are driven by meter size, not by the demand the facility will place on the Town’s 
system. The training hydrants require a 6-inch service so crews can flow water at realistic 
fireground rates, but that flow occurs only intermittently, during scheduled training. Total annual 
training use is estimated at approximately 205,000 gallons, roughly the annual use of three 
single-family homes according to the 2018 Windsor Water Report. Assessing PIFs and raw 
water on a 6-inch meter would charge WSFR for capacity the site will never use. In addition, 
training water is discharged to grade and the storm system rather than the sanitary sewer, so 
the training service places no demand on the Town’s wastewater system. 
Based on preliminary estimates discussed with Town staff, the fees in question are: 
Training hydrant service (6-inch): raw water requirement of approximately 0.63 acre-feet 
(0.79 acre-feet with the 26% adjustment), estimated at $70,000 to $88,000, plus a PIF and 
meter hardware, an estimate which has not yet been provided. 
Domestic building service (1-inch): raw water requirement of approximately 0.29 acre-feet 
including the 26% adjustment, estimated at $32,000, plus a PIF and meter hardware 
estimated at $28,686. 
WSFR is a special district funded by the same residents and businesses the Town serves. 
Development fees paid by WSFR are public dollars moved from one taxpayer-funded entity to 
another, and every dollar spent on fees is a dollar not spent on apparatus, equipment, and 
firefighters. A local training facility allows WSFR crews to gain realistic training experience while 
remaining in the district rather than traveling to outside facilities. This facility is vital to ensure
