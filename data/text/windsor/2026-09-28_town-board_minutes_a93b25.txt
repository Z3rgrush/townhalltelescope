4. Resolution No. 2026-53 - A Resolution Accepting Right of Way Dedications and Temporary 
Construction Easements Concerning Certain Properties for the Development of Windsor 
Lake Road - D. Roth, Civil Engineer 
 The Town Board has approved and is proceeding with the Weld County Road 70 
Extension Project (the “Project”), which involves the construction of a roadway 
connecting Hollister Lake Road to Colorado State Highway 257 (“SH 257”), consisting of 
one travel lane in each direction, a crossing over the Springer Ditch, the installation of a 
traffic signal at SH 257, and the construction of detached sidewalks and buffered bicycle 
lanes, together with all related grading, drainage, utility, and other appurtenant public 
improvements. To facilitate the construction and long-term maintenance of the Project 
improvements, it is necessary for the Town to acquire certain property interests within the 
Project limits, including right-of-way in fee simple, permanent public improvement 
easements, and temporary construction easements. 
 
Engineering staff, along with a ROW agent, ROW appraiser, contract ROW attorney and 
title company, are working on the acquisition of needed easements and ROW. One area of 
 
 
ROW acquisition is along the south side of Windsor Estates. Offers on 4 out of the 5 
properties where acquisition is needed in Windsor Estates have been accepted. Resolution 
No. 2026-53 is for acceptance of ROW and temporary construction easements obtained 
from 913 Shetland Ct. and 900 Palomino Way. 
  
   
5. Resolution No. 2026-54- A Resolution Approving the Third Amended and Restated 
Intergovernmental Agreement Between the Town of Windsor and the Windsor Downtown 
Development Authority - D. Money, Town Attorney 
 Since its establishment in 2011, the Windsor Downtown Alliance (WDA) has partnered 
with the Town through a series of intergovernmental agreements (IGAs) that provide 
financial support and establish the parties’ respective responsibilities for the revitalization 
and redevelopment of downtown Windsor. These agreements have been reviewed and 
renewed every five years, beginning in 2011, with subsequent amendments in 2016 and 
2021. The proposed Third Amended and Restated IGA continues this partnership through 
2031, when the parties will again evaluate the WDA’s progress and future funding needs. 
Ultimately, the Agreement reflects the Town’s and WDA’s shared commitment to 
fostering a vibrant and economically sustainable downtown while promoting the WDA’s 
long-term financial independence.

3. Public Hearing - Ordinance No. 2026-1752 - An Ordinance Amending Certain Portions of 
Windsor Municipal Code Chapters 14 and 16 Concerning Conditional Use Permits - Carlin 
Malone, Planning Manager, Community Development Department  
 Planning Manager Carlin Malone presented the ordinance on first reading. The presentation 
covered proposed amendments to the conditional use permit (CUP) framework within the 
Windsor Municipal Code. Following the staff presentation, the public hearing was opened. No 
members of the public came forward to comment, and the hearing was closed. 
 
Board discussion touched on several substantive concerns. Town Board Member Jones 
raised a question regarding the duration of temporary modular buildings approved under a 
conditional use permit, noting that applicants could potentially request use periods of two to 
three years and questioning whether a codified time limit would be more appropriate. Mayor 
Cline agreed with the sentiment, observing that without a defined ceiling, a five-year 
conditional use permit might not feel meaningfully "conditional." Staff indicated that when 
modular buildings are addressed separately in code, time limits could more appropriately be 
applied to that specific use category. 
 
Town Board Member Symsack raised concern about the provision allowing "unique in nature 
or character" uses in any zoning district, seeking clarification on who determines 
"uniqueness" and whether this provision could be used to circumvent standard zoning 
requirements. Staff clarified that a use already permitted in another zoning district would not 
qualify under this provision, and that an example such as a residential pickleball court would 
not be eligible.
