4. Proclamations 
 
 
      
a. Suicide Awareness and Prevention Month  
 Mayor Cline read a proclamation recognizing September 2026 as Suicide Awareness and 
Prevention Month in the Town of Windsor. The proclamation acknowledged the significant 
public health impact of suicide at the national, state, and local levels, noting 48,824 deaths 
by suicide in the United States in 2024, 1,293 in Colorado in 2025, and 70 in Weld County 
in 2025. It recognized the work of North Range Behavioral Health Suicide Education and 
Support Services (SESS), Imagine Zero at Weld County, and other community partners in 
providing prevention, intervention, crisis response, education, and support services. The 

 
 
proclamation called upon all Windsor residents to strengthen connections, foster hope, 
and promote suicide prevention efforts. A community representative was present and 
offered brief remarks following the reading. 
      
b. Women's Equality Day 
 Mayor Cline read a proclamation recognizing August 26, 2026 as Women's Equality Day 
in the Town of Windsor, commemorating the certification of the 19th Amendment to the 
United States Constitution on August 26, 1920. The proclamation celebrated the 
generations of women who have contributed to the Windsor community through 
leadership, public service, education, business, agriculture, and volunteerism, and 
reaffirmed the Town's commitment to equality and civic participation for all. A 
representative of the League of Women Voters of Larimer County was present and offered 
remarks, noting that Colorado granted women the right to vote in 1893—27 years before 
the 19th Amendment—and that the League is celebrating its 75th year in Larimer County. 
   
5. Public Invited to be Heard 
 Laura Hiebenthal at 210 E. Chestnut #3 in Windsor addressed the board regarding light 
pollution from recently upgraded sports field lighting near her neighborhood. She expressed 
disappointment with the impact the lights have had on the quality of life for herself and 
neighboring residents, stating that the brightness has made it difficult to enjoy outdoor spaces 
and has intruded into her home. She acknowledged the investment the Town made in the 
field upgrades but questioned whether the fields were being used significantly more than 
before. 
 
Mayor Cline thanked the resident for her comments.

C. BOARD ACTION  
   
1. Motion to Send Letter of Support to the Bureau of Reclamation for the NISP Enterprise Glade 
Inlet Canal Diversion Improvements 
 Town Manager Shane Hale presented this item. He noted that the overall cost of the 
Northern Integrated Supply Project (NISP) has been increasing over time, and that the NISP 
Enterprise is submitting a Bureau of Reclamation grant application for $3,000,000 to help 
offset costs associated with the Glade Inlet Canal Diversion project. Town Manager Hale 
indicated that the Town of Windsor holds approximately 15 percent of the project, meaning 
that a successful grant award would result in an estimated $450,000 in savings to the Town. 
 
 
The Town's letter of support was presented as a means of assisting the Enterprise in 
securing the grant funding. 
 
Town Board member Peltz moved to send a letter of support to the Bureau of 
Reclamation for the NISP Enterprise Glade Inlet Canal Diversion Improvements, Town 
Board Member Jones seconded the motion. Roll call on the vote resulted as follows; 
Yeas - Julie Cline, Ron Steinbach, Brian Jones, Lainie Peltz, Ken Symsack, Rick 
Klimek, Jason Hallett; Nays - None; Motion Passed.
