Background / Discussion: 
Executive Summary 
The Town Board asked staff to compare current and past code language regarding lot area 
requirements for keeping animals in the Estate Residential (ER) zone and the former Estate Residential 
E-1 and  E-2 Districts. This memorandum summarizes staff findings and recommends changes if the 
Town Board decides to amend the standards. 
 
Ordinances Regarding Keeping of Animals 
•    Ordinance 1999-1044 established the E-1 and E-2 estate residential zone districts. These districts 
were based on density standards in Code Section 16-205, which set minimum lot sizes and allowed 
densities depending on the sewage disposal system. E-2 encouraged cluster subdivisions with smaller 
lots served by public sewer and larger preserved open spaces. E-1 required a minimum of 2.5 acres for 
lots with septic systems and allowed lots as small as 7,500 square feet for those on sanitary sewer. 
        •   This ordinance allowed one large animal per acre in E-1, governed by density standards in 
Section 16-205. Properties with septic systems needed at least 2.5 acres; those with public sewer could 
be as small as 7,500 square feet. The one-acre minimum was tied to animal keeping as an accessory 
use. 
 
•    In 2020, Ordinance 2020-1620 (Section 16-1-30) consolidated E-1 and E-2 into the ER district. ER 
lots may be larger or smaller than 2.5 acres, but new subdivisions cannot use individual septic systems. 
Smaller lots must follow single-family zoning and setback standards. The ER district was created as 
septic-served lots were phased out and larger lots were no longer proposed. 
•    Ordinance 2020-1620 created the ER district, allowing large domestic animals only on parcels of at 
least 2.5 acres. 
 
Estate Residential - Neighborhood Context 
Staff reviewed the evolution of the ER district to show how neighborhood contexts have changed. 
Some neighborhoods were designed to allow large domestic animals, while others were not. It is 
important to consider the context of each estate residential neighborhood when amending the code, as 
not all are suitable for large animals. 
ER Subdivisions in Windsor:  
Highland Meadows Golf Course, Ventana, Steeplechase, Trautman 4th (Crossroads), North Shores, 
Hilltop Estates, High Pointe, Alexander Estates, Windsor Estates, and Fossil Ridge. Portions of the 
following: Bison Ridge, Westwood Village 2nd & 7th, Vista Grande Addition. 
The Windsor Estates and Steeplechase neighborhoods were developed with the intent to allow horses 
Page 2 of 32
and therefore included trails and other amenities. Windsor Estates has amended their covenants to 
allow permitted animals that align with the Windsor Municipal Code. With restrictions to the type of 
animals allowed per Chapter 7 Article 5 of the Windsor code. 
The Ventana and North Shores neighborhoods did not anticipate large animals, and their respective 
declaration of covenants restrict keeping of animals outside of domesticated household animals, with 
further restrictions on breeding and commercial enterprises associated.  
The Highland Meadows Golf Course neighborhood included “clustered” homes but those homes are 
clustered around a golf course and did not anticipate large animals. 
Portions of the Westwood Village Subdivision 2nd and 7th Filings, as well as the Vista Grande Addition, 
were part of a larger effort following annexation proceedings to establish formal lots through the 
subdivision process within the Town. These same subdivisions are among those where the Town has 
received inquiries regarding the keeping of animals, including the recent public hearing regarding a 
Conditional Use Permit to allow the keeping of goats or a horse on a 1.47-acre property. 
Proposed Next Step 
Based upon previously received feedback and the analysis of prior and existing Code provisions 
concerning large animals in the Town, if Town Board is so inclined, staff recommends reverting to the 
language from earlier versions of the Code to allow for the keeping of large animals on lots one (1) acre 
in size or greater. Staff further recommends adding language to establish additional acreage 
requirements for the keeping of more than one large animal and to reference any applicable HOA or 
Metro District covenants, restrictions, and rules. 
 
The recommended code language is as follows:  
Keeping of animals. Contrary provisions of this Code notwithstanding, and subject to any applicable 
Homeowners Association (HOA) and or any Metropolitan District covenants and rules, large 
domestic animals shall be permitted as an accessory use in the Estate Residential (ER) District on lots 
two and one-half (2.5) acres one (1) acre in size or greater. For the purpose of this Section only, large 
domestic animals are defined as and shall be limited to horses, ponies, mules, donkeys, and llamas. 
For each permitted animal, one (1) acre of lot area inclusive of improvements  shall be required. 
Offspring shall be allowed until the weaning process is complete.

Re: Prop 123 Opt-In Discussion - S. Mezzetti, Senior Planner

Background / Discussion: 
Overview 
The purpose of this work session is to provide information on the Proposition (Prop) 123 new calculation formula, 
the required number of affordable units for the 2027-2029 cycle, options, and the Opt-In process. 
 
Proposition 123 Background 
Colorado voters approved Proposition 123 in November 2022 to create the State Affordable Housing Fund, a 
dedicated revenue stream for affordable housing development. Funded by 0.1% of state income tax revenue, this 
program aims to support a broad range of housing initiatives, including new construction, preservation, land 
banking, and rental assistance. Funding is provided through grants and loans to nonprofit agencies, community 
land trusts, nonprofit and for-profit developers, and local governments.  
 
To be eligible for funding, affordable housing projects seeking support from the State Affordable Housing Fund 
must be located in a jurisdiction that has completed a Proposition 123 Local Government Affordable Housing 
Commitment with the Colorado Department of Local Affairs.  
 
The Town opted into Proposition 123 in November 2023, committing to build a minimum of 44 affordable housing 
units. This commitment was exceeded through the construction of Jacoby Meadows Senior Apartments, which 
includes 46 affordable housing units targeted to households at 60% of Area Median Income (AMI) or below. 
 
Although Jacoby Meadows Senior Apartments has a total of 62 affordable housing units, only the 46 units 
targeted at households at 60% AMI or below can be counted toward the Town’s Proposition 123 commitment. 
The remaining 16 units are targeted to households at 80% AMI and therefore do not qualify towards the Town’s 
Proposition 123 affordable housing unit commitment. 
  
Prop 123 Affordability Thresholds 
Eligible units must meet the affordability thresholds established by Proposition 123, which are as follows: 
• Rental housing at or below 60% AMI, or 
• For-sale housing at or below 100% AMI (120% AMI starting 7/1/26), and 
• Which costs the household less than 30% of its monthly income. 
  
Prop 123 Cycle 2023-2026 
• Opted into the program in November 2023 (3-year cycles) 
o Required affordable units - 44 
o Affordable units added – 46 (Jacoby Meadows Senior Apartments) 
 
Prop 123 Cycle 2027-2029  
HB26-1313 modified the way jurisdictions calculate their affordable housing unit goals, shifting the commitment 
calculation from the original baseline method to one based on residential building permits and county job growth. 
Page 14 of 32
The purpose of this modification is to make it more adaptable to local economic realities, shifting away from a flat 
3% mandate and introducing localized numbers based on existing development patterns and job growth data. 
This new methodology focuses development where Colorado needs housing most.  
 
Local governments will provide their own building permit data for the calculation. DOLA will establish and publish 
the job growth figures and the thresholds that determine a jurisdiction’s specific factor. For municipalities located 
in multiple counties, the job growth calculation will use each county's job growth rate, weighted by the portion of 
the municipality's population in each county. 
 
Windsor’s specific job growth factor is 15%. 
 
 
The new calculation is as follows: 
• Average number of annual residential new construction building permits (or equivalent) over the past 
three years 
• Multiply by 10%, 15%, or 20% (based on county job growth rate in relation to state job growth rate)  
• Multiply by 3 (or number of years of commitment) 
The resulting number equals the local government’s three-year unit growth commitment.  
 
Town building permits issued (single-family attached, single-family detached, and multi-family): 
2023: 183 
2024: 555 
2025: 433 
Total permits issued 2023-2025: 1,171 permits 
Average number of permits per year: 390 
390 x 15% x 3 = 176 affordable units required over the 2027-2029 three-year cycle. 
 
Next cycle submittal is due November 01, 2026. 
 
Justification for the calculation change: 
Residential building permits are a common metric that each jurisdiction collects data on and has direct access to, 
and they reflect population and economic growth. This metric will lead to more equitable statewide targets, 
encouraging the largest development where it is needed (high-growth areas) and allowing stable/declining areas 
to focus on other housing priorities, such as preservation and infrastructure. Using residential building permits 
creates a metric that encompasses the jurisdiction’s pace of development.  
 
Options: 
• Adjustment Waiver:

For the 2027 to 2029 cycle, if a jurisdiction’s new unit goal is significantly higher than its previous unit 
goal, and this new unit goal is unattainable due to specific, verifiable factors, you may be eligible to file an 
Adjustment Waiver to adjust your community’s Proposition 123 unit goal. Although the State allows 
jurisdictions the opportunity to apply for an adjustment waiver, this is not an option for Windsor because 
we will be unable to provide a reasonable justification for unattainability or meet the verifiable data 
requirements: 
• Justification of Unattainability: Describe the specific local realities—such as economic shifts, market 
conditions, or labor shortages—that make the calculated unit goal impossible to achieve. 
• Verifiable Data Upload: You will be required to provide information that supports your narrative, 
including verifiable data relating to at least one of the following: 
• Local housing needs assessment completed no earlier than three years before submittal  
Per Windsor’s 2026 Housing Needs Assessment: Cumulative housing needs, which combine 
today’s estimated shortfall with the housing required to accommodate future household growth, is 
projected to range from 6,359 to 7,734 units by 2033. This total includes between 4,816 and 
5,927 owner units and 1,543 and 1,807 rentals, with an estimated 5,030 units required just to 
house expected new households. 
• Inventory of deed-restricted units recorded with the local government or county clerk for the 
county in which the local government is located 
DOLA Clarification: If you have data showing you exceeded or did extremely well in creating 
income-restricted affordable housing over the past three years, this is data that could support that 
story. 
• Comprehensive plan or area plan growth projections demonstrating that the annual increase 
requirement in the number of affordable housing units exceeds the total projected growth of the 
jurisdiction by a significant margin 
DOLA Clarification: If you have other data-backed planning documents outside of an HNA that 
speak to Windsor’s affordable housing needs, and the needs those documents show are less 
than the calculation, that could support a request for a lower goal. 
• Utility or service provider documentation 
DOLA Clarification: For communities that are limited by some kind of utility or resource, like 
water, you could supply data on how this limits development. 
• Infrastructure capacity limitations 
DOLA Clarification: For communities limited by existing infrastructure or infrastructure problems, 
you could supply data showing how this limits development. 
• Environmental or geographic constraint maps 
DOLA Clarification: For communities that are bound or limited by geography, and have reached 
the extent of developable space, you could supply data on how this limits development 
• Opt-in: 
The Town may submit a commitment, knowing it will not meet the required three-year cycle commitment. This 
will allow the town to remain eligible for funding opportunities for the next three years. 
Penalty for not meeting the 2027-2029 three-year cycle commitment: 
If unable to meet the 2027-2029 commitment, the Town will be ineligible for Prop 123 funding opportunities for 
the first year of the next cycle, 2030-2032. This means the Town is ineligible during 2030. After sitting out for 
the year of the 2030-2032 cycle, the Town may opt in for the remaining two years. New calculations will only 
be for the remaining two years.  
• Opt-out and Penalty: 
The Town may choose not to participate in the 2027-2030 commitment cycle. However, municipalities that 
Page 16 of 32
refuse to participate in Proposition 123 could lose access to funding for housing, transportation, and energy 
projects. 
  
 
 
 
 
 
  
 
Financial Impact: 
Submitting a commitment for the 2027-2030 cycle allows the Town to remain eligible for DOLA 
funding.  
Choosing not to submit a commitment for the 2027-2030 cycle results in a loss of DOLA funding for not 
only housing, but transportation and energy projects as well. 
 
Relationship to Strategic Plan: 
Filing a commitment to increase the Town’s affordable housing stock is generally consistent with 
the Strategic Plan’s Mission:  The Town of Windsor promotes community and hometown pride 
through sustainable, excellent, and equitable delivery of services in a fiscally responsible manner. 
 
Recommendation: 
An adjustment waiver is unlikely to be approved. Therefore, staff recommends that the Town submit a 
commitment, with the understanding that the commitment will not meet the required three-year cycle target. 
Submitting the commitment will allow the Town to remain eligible for Proposition 123 funding during the next 
three-year cycle.

The Town’s current Municipal Code provision governing door-to-door solicitation reads as 
follows: 
Sec. 10-4-160. – Signs prohibiting solicitation and/or trespassing. 
No person shall enter or remain upon any public or private premises in the Town, unless invited 
or requested by the occupant or occupants thereof, when a “No Solicitation” or “No 
Trespassing” sign is posted at or near the entrance to such premises. 
To provide greater clarity and guidance to both law enforcement and the public regarding the 
application and enforcement of this provision, the Windsor Police Department and Town 
Attorney’s Office have prepared a proposed revision to Section 10-4-160. The proposed 
amendments are intended to more clearly define the activities regulated by the ordinance, 
distinguish between solicitation and other protected door-to-door activities, and clarify the legal 
effect of posted signage. 
The proposed revised ordinance presented to the Board for consideration provides as follows: 
Sec. 10-4-160. Signs prohibiting soliciting, canvassing, or trespassing. 
 
(a) Definitions. 
 
(1) Canvassing means going to a residence or other premises, without prior invitation, 
primarily to advocate for or share political, religious, or other ideological views or 
causes, including any incidental request for donations in support of such views or 
causes. This Section does not regulate the content of canvassing or of any other 
speech. 
 
(2) Occupant means an owner, tenant, resident, lessee, or other person who possesses 
private premises or is lawfully authorized to manage or control access to the private 
premises. 
 
(3) Private Premises means privately owned or privately occupied real property, together 
with buildings and improvements upon it. The term does not include public property 
or a public right-of-way. 
 
(4) Sign means a written or symbolic communication posted by or with the authority of 
an occupant and reasonably conveying a restriction on entry, remaining, soliciting, 
canvassing, or another identified activity. 
 
(5) Soliciting means going to a residence or other premises, without prior invitation, to

(b) No person shall knowingly enter or remain upon any private premises in the Town, 
unless invited or requested by an occupant, when a sign posted by an occupant as 
required by Subsection (c) prohibits that person's activity or presence. 
 
(c) A sign stating "No Soliciting" prohibits soliciting. A sign stating "No Canvassing" 
prohibits canvassing. A sign stating both, in any order or combination, prohibits both. A 
"No Trespassing" sign prohibits uninvited entry or remaining upon premises, except by a 
person having consent, lawful authority, or legal privilege to enter the premises. 
 
(d) A person acts knowingly for purposes of this section when a person is aware of the 
applicable posted restriction, or after becoming aware of the restriction, knowingly 
remains upon the private premises, or continues the prohibited activity. 
 
(e) A sign is enforceable under this Section only if the sign: 
 
(1) Is posted by or with the authority of an occupant; 
 
(2) Is conspicuously placed at or near the entrance used by the person charged; 
 
(3) Is reasonably visible and legible to an ordinarily observant person approaching by 
that entrance before the person enters or begins the restricted activity; and 
 
(4) Reasonably communicates the restriction alleged to have been violated. 
 
(f) A sign lawfully posted at a controlled entrance to a multiunit building or common area 
need not be repeated at each individual unit to be enforceable as to the controlled area. 
 
(g) A person who promptly leaves the private premises or promptly ceases the restricted 
activity upon first receiving notice, either through a compliant posted sign or the 
occupant’s request for that person to leave, does not violate this Section based solely on 
conduct occurring before the person received notice. 
 
(h) The exclusion of any person or activity from any premises is the occupant's decision. The 
Town does not select any person, activity, or message for exclusion.
