Steve Burt, Housing and Community Services, stated absolutely. Thank you, Mayor, Council Members. The SAMHSA Street Grant is a four-year cooperative agreement designed to expand street-based behavioral health treatment and housing pathways for individuals experiencing unsheltered homelessness with serious mental illness, substance use disorders, or co-occurring conditions. It will be structured as an integrated field-based clinical response that brings behavioral health treatment directly to unsheltered residents. Multidisciplinary teams will meet individuals where they are at in encampments, transit nodes, parks, commercial corridors, and other high activity micro locations using the GIS and RTM models from the WPD to conduct screening, initiate medications for substance use disorders, and establish ongoing psychiatric and recovery supports. Participants will be connected to housing through existing pathways: Home TRA, ESG, rehousing, PSH and HCB Homes preference as appropriate. With units selected to ensure access to medication and treatment participation. And Council Member Shepard, to your question, it's a cooperative agreement with SAMHSA. It's a four-year cooperative agreement. That means we will be talking to SAMHSA on an ongoing basis about how do we utilize this program. So that at the back end of the four-year project, we're setting ourselves up for success. Also, this also addresses one of the Mayor's questions. We have an integrated evaluation piece and a project Manager external to us that will help us track the data, build dashboards, build progress, track progress towards essentially ending homelessness for all of the people that we are talking to and setting them up for success. I absolutely presume that throughout the course of this four year process, we're going to identify gaps in our system that we are going to be actively rectifying along the way. So that we have a path forward, should this just completely end. The other opportunity here is that this is only one of eight awards across the country, and if we succeed at this, this may set the stage for ongoing funding coming from SAMHSA to address these matters across our country.
