William Glander - Storm Drains
William Glander stated good evening Council Members. I'm William Glander. I live at 235 North Elizabeth. I'm speaking to you tonight because our city's infrastructure is failing to keep up with... and it is directly causing the flooding that we are seeing in our streets. I have over 15 years hands-on experience in earthwork, grading, water management. I know how water moves, and more importantly, I know why it's backing up in our City. The problem is overdevelopment, bottlenecks. The core issue is simple. We are covering up ground with concrete faster than the drainage system can handle. Loss of green space, water cannot soak into concrete. It needs soil and root system when large developments like new, like the new Love's gas station. Our massive apartment complexes take over acres of dirt that rainwater has nowhere to go except to our storm systems all at once. System overload. The massive volume of runoff is overtaxing the main canal. We need the river and the river rises at the outlets, incoming runoff collides with rising water, creating a tidal wave effect. This pressure forces flap gates stuck, closed, sending water straight back up through the structures and out into our streets. Internal blockages inside the drainage network have design flaws where flowing water hits crossing pipes, forcing water to spill over instead of flowing freely.  that creates immediate backflooding. The solution :, we cannot just keep building bigger concrete boxes and hoping for the best. We need smart, natural structures fixes. Establish a secondary bollard. We need an overflow mechanism for the canals so pressure doesn't force the flap gates shut during heavy storms. Restore and maintain our ditches and canals. Decades of neglect means parts of our canal system are near collapse. We must reestablish in these style all dirt ditches, especially when paving dirt roads, so runoff doesn't pool on the pavement. Integrate smart green infrastructure requires developments to pressure, functional dirt and tree space for natural absorption when old driveways are removed. Don't just leave the concrete. Fill them in with curb and trees to capture street runoff. Our growth is great for the city, but if we do not fix the water where the water goes, we are setting our residents up for costly flood damage. I care deeply about making this City the best, safest place it can be. I have the hands - on experience and practical knowledge to help fix these drainage bottlenecks before the system collapses. I urge the council to review these drainage points and reach out, so we can get to work on real solutions. To add to this, someone brought up drainage ditch in West Wichita on the news. We do not need that brought up at all. We need to be able to maintain our drainage ditches and be on top of them. It makes our City look bad when we get brought up on the news and people say that, "Hey, this is a problem." We got standing water. Thank you.
