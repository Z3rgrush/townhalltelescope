VII. UNFINISHED BUSINESS 
 
 
 
 1. Project 64 Talking Points 
 
  Jeff Sams, Housing Coordinator, said Project 64 (formerly Project 102) has many moving 
parts. In an effort to ensure accurate and consistent information is being communicated by 
our team, staff has developed talking points. The Town was successful with a winning bid for 
the Village Pointe Condos at $4.9 million. This 64-unit property will require renovation before 
leasing. The property is scheduled to close before late March 2027. Staff is working on 
grants for renovation and the purchase of the property; COPs will be obtained for the 
balance. 
Council Member Martinez said the potential grants could reduce the rents necessary to 
cover the COP payment, which is a unique feature for this project. Council Member Williams 
said the property should be in a land trust to keep it available to the community.  
VIII. NEW BUSINESS 
 
 
 
 1. Ordinance 1037, First Reading, Data Center Moratorium 
 
  Ordinance 1037 allows staff to closely monitor data center development across the nation 
and specifically the State of Colorado regarding technological advancements, especially as 
it pertains to human and ecological impact. The 6-month moratorium on data centers will 
require an extension every 6 months to achieve the two years as requested by Council. 
Council Member Lindner said the council should keep in mind new questions and ways of 
thinking about these data centers. Council Member Martinez said policies have not been set 
up around this new technology and they need to be created so the Town can take 
advantage of these centers. Mayor Pierce said this allows the Town to get out in front of the 
technology and wants to work together with the County on this policy.  
Mr. Bill Hudson said there may be a question of national security for this technology and 
said it is dangerous and threatens our country.   
 
Council Member deGraaf moved to approve the first reading of Ordinance 1037 placing a 6-
month moratorium on all development that would include data centers, Council Member 
Lindner seconded. Motion Passed 
 2. Discussion regarding potential traffic calming device on San Juan Alley and 
discussion regarding developing a policy and ordinance regarding traffic calming 
devices on public roads 
 
  Staff reviewed traffic and safety concerns on San Juan Alley between South 8th Street and 
South 10th Street. Based on residential feedback, staff said installing a speed dip is not the 
correct solution at this time. The dip could create additional concerns with noise, drainage, 
snow removal, vehicle clearance, and ongoing maintenance. 
The one-way option also received limited support. Staff recommends leaving the existing 
two-way traffic pattern unchanged and continuing to monitor traffic and safety concerns in 
the area. The issue can be reconsidered if conditions change or additional neighborhood
