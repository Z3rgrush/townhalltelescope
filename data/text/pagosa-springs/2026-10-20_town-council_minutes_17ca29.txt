 1. 2026 Fireworks Discussion 
 
  Due to extreme fire risk, the 2026 4th of July Fireworks show was canceled.  Per the 
contract with Acme, the Town must schedule a show in 2026 or forfeit the entire $32,000 
cost. Staff suggests scheduling the fireworks show during Old Fashion Christmas event 
December 19, 2026 or on New Year's Eve. 
Council Member Bergon moved to shoot off the fireworks during Old Fashion Christmas on 
December 19th and use New Year's Eve as a back up date, Council Member deGraaf 
seconded. Motion Passed 
 2. Ordinance 1042, First Reading, Continued from October 6, Ponderosa Mountain 
Planned Development Agreement and Zoning Map Amendment 
 
  Modern Community Builders LLC has applied for the approval of Ponderosa Mountain, a 
major subdivision, near Hwy 160 and Gold Mine Drive. The developer is proposing to fully 
develop the site, in phases, with a mix of residential and commercial.  
Ordinance 1042 amends the official zoning map and approves a planned development 
overlay district and a planned development agreement for property known as Ponderosa 
Mountain, located near Hwy 160 and Goldmine Drive. The development will include 
commercial lodging, housing, including workforce housing (90%-130% AMI and deed-
restricted), and amenities. This development covers 70 acres and is proposed in three 
phases. A complete major subdivision must be filed within one year of the PD approval, with 
an extension to encourage development moving forward. The Town Council recommended 
continuing this item and public hearing at the October 6th meeting. 
Staff presented the project overlay plan, approval criteria, Comprehensive Plan goals and 
LUDC criteria. Staff said it is the developers' responsibility to contact the utility providers to 
determine the needs for the development. Mr. Jeff Sams, Multi-Jurisdictional Housing 
Coordinator, said the Housing Action Plan addresses the housing needs in the community. 
The developers provided information about the proposed development. Mr. Fink said this 
type of housing project is needed in Pagosa Springs and appreciates the unique vibe of the 
community. He said the pricing of 24 homes in Phase 1 is planned to be priced between 
$365 and $500k each. He said it will take up to ten years to complete the full build out of this 
development. This includes eighty home sites for sale, thirty workforce houses and fifty 
single-family homes. He said there will be another twenty condo-type units and lodging 
facilities in the commercial space. Mr. Phil Williams said the developer will bear the costs of 
the utilities. He said there is a natural barrier between the Alpha Rockridge development and 
this development. He said a light at Great West Ave and Hwy 160 is not required by CDOT 
per their traffic study at this time, but they will invest in a light should CDOT require it.  
Ms. Emily Lashbrooke said the current economic data shows 1,267 vacant jobs, with 
employers reporting lack of housing as the reason.  
Ms. Jane Weitzel said this development will not help Pagosa by bringing in more tourism. 
She said people will cut through on the Alpha Rockridge roads. 
Ms. Sharon Carter said the LUDC and Comprehensive Plan are outdated. She said the plan 
is not compatible and harmonious with Pagosa. 
Ms. Terri Pritchard said the community is a mess with not enough hospital workers or law 
enforcement. She said the wildlife are suffering from the drought and the grocery stores are 
empty.  
Ms. Lisa Gamber said a California developer can't really know the community and just wants 
to make money. She said this is a large resort development and not affordable homes. She

Mr. Bill Hudson said the development department did not provide a balanced presentation.

Council Member Martinez said infrastructure issues could have ended Pagosa Springs and it 
is not captured in the LUDC and Comprehensive Plan. He said making this decision now is 
not appropriate and infrastructure will not be ready in the ten years it takes to complete this 
development. He said there will be issues with traffic and emergencies and the locals will 
have to pay the price. He said the timing is wrong and more needs to be done to diversify 
our economy. Council Member Shabro said the proposal provides benefits to the community 
adding housing supply and deed-restricted homes will help to bring housing prices down. He 
said adding these homes will add community workers. He said he would support this 
ordinance and there will be final steps by the developer to get all the conditions as 
required. There will be another review by the Town Council during the preliminary approval 
after the developer cooperation with utilities and CDOT. Council Member Bergon said the 
open space and trail connectivity are important. She said the scaling of the phases is 
aggressive. She said she is uncomfortable with the ratio between housing and commercial 
units. She wants to protect the STR rules and the owners who already rent STRs in town.  
Mr. Fink said the developer will pay for any utility upgrades and required traffic light. Mr. Carl 
Bomerito said eighteen of the units in the commercial zone district will provide fractional 
ownership of a home to include, for instance one-third ownership for visiting two to three 
times per year. He said there will also be 82 lodging rental units for short-term rentals.   
Council Member deGraaf said this proposal is not for Pagosa and said the developer should 
pedal this project to another community. Council Member Lindner said staff needs direction 
on the criteria of future projects and the LUDC and Comp Plan need to be reviewed. He said 
he looked at the approval criteria and felt some are subjective and some were not. He said 
when reviewing the criteria, he has to support an approval of this PD. He said Pagosa Views 
was approved two years ago and has more units than the Ponderosa Mountain 
development. Council Member Williams said he is impressed how meaningful the comp plan 
is on looking into the future. He said Pagosa needs people to stay here and raise their 
family. He said the people around these development are upset because their views might 
change, but the owner of the property is allowed to develop their property. He said Pagosa 
Springs has good plans in place, good staff, and a good outlook for our future. He said the 
Town needs to develop from the core outward to infill those empty areas. Mayor Pierce said 
she talked with Mr. Dickhoff to understand the developer's rights. She said the Town can't 
punish developers who are following the Town's rules. She said there needs to be an 
agreement for the adjoining roads. She said there needs to be restrictions on lodging units if 
permanent houses are not built, and deed-restrictions on workforce housing units.

Mr. Dickhoff said the sequencing of building units is addressed in the PD agreement that an 
equitable portion of workforce housing be built while the lodging rentals and residential units 
are built in Phase 1 to include 82 lodging rentals, 82 residential (18 being fractional housing), 
and 30 workforce housing units. Council Member Martinez said this development will cause 
the Town to no longer fit within a low income level to allow grant availability for needed 
infrastructure.   
Council Member Martinez moved to deny the first reading of Ordinance 1042 Ponderosa 
Mountain Planned Development Agreement, finding the applicant does not comply with the 
following approved criteria, which is to fit within the criteria that we deem appropriate for how 
we want to move forward, so we have some work to do on our end, Council Member 
deGraaf seconded, motion failed with five nays (Mayor Pierce, Council Member Shabro, 
Bergon, Lindner, and Williams).  
Mr. Fink said the lodging properties will not be sold individually in order to develop a 
hospitality business. He said the developer would share the cost to improve Great West 
Avenue with the Alpha Rockridge metro district. He said they will be completing all the 
infrastructure first before they begin construction of the cabins. They will provide 18-19% of 
the workforce housing while they are building the cabins and other homes during the 
phases.   
Council Member Bergon said she would like a larger buffer along the five Alpha Rockridge 
properties adjacent, perhaps 1/2 acre. A maximum housing size would be limited to 2,800 
square feet. 
Mayor Pierce said approval criteria five reads quality of air, water, noise, stormwater mgm, 
wildlife, and vegetation. She said perhaps there is a way to reduce noise. Council Member 
Shabro said the PD should include permanent deed restrictions, and strong 
collateral/bonding for road, infrastructure, and trail improvements.  
Mayor Pierce moved to continue this item until a future council meeting, the motion died for 
lack of a second. 
Council Member Shabro moved to approve Ordinance 1042, first reading, continued from 
October 6, Ponderosa Mountain Planned Development Agreement and Zoning Map 
Amendment, contingent on additional feedback given to town development planners to 
include concerns brought up by the Council Members today in the second reading for 
approval, Council Member Williams seconded. Motion passed with two nays (Council 
Member Martinez and deGraaf).
