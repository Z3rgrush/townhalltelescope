 1. Ordinance 1037, Second Reading, Authorizing a Moratorium on the Acceptance or 
Processing of Certain Permit Applications and Land Development Applications 
Where a Data Center is Included as a Proposed Principal or Accessory Use  
 
  At the July 21st Town Council meeting, the Planning Division requested direction regarding 
the use of data centers in Pagosa Springs. Staff identified the lack of a use classification in 
the Town's Land Use Development Code and expressed the need to codify such use. After 
deliberation between Council and staff, it was directed that staff present Council with a 2-
year moratorium on data centers. This ordinance is intended to allow staff to closely monitor 
data center development across the nation and specifically in the State of Colorado 
regarding technological advancements, especially as it pertains to human and ecological 
impact. 
Council Member Bergon said what we're doing is really important, and she would also love 
for the community to have a say, so that this decision isn't solely resting on the Council.   
 
Council Member deGraaf moved to approve the second reading of Ordinance 1037, An 
Ordinance of the Town of Pagosa Springs Authorizing a Moratorium on the Acceptance or 
Processing of Certain Permit Applications and Land Development Applications where Data 
Center is Included as a Proposed Principle or Accessory Use, Council Member Lindner 
seconded. Motion Passed 
 2. Naming the new park at 1040 E US 160 
 
  The Town purchased the Bob's LP/Basin Coop property at 1040 E US 160 in May 2025. At 
its October 21, 2025 meeting, staff asked Town Council to select a park name from a list of 
options. Following solicitations from the public, the Parks and Recreation Board 
recommended "Gateway River Park."  After further discussion with staff and Council 
member Martinez, staff recommend naming the park "San Juan River Park." This name 
accomplishes several goals: it honors the Spanish origin of the river and mountain range 
names, and it evokes "water" - the root meaning of "Pagosah." Site signage could tell the 
story of water's influence on this region. This name also leaves room for interested Native 
communities to propose a second name for the site in the future, should that opportunity 
arise. 
 
Council Member Martinez moved to approve naming the formerly known Bob Sivers Park to 
the San Juan River Park, and leave it up to staff to add on any names in the future, Council 
Member Shabro seconded. Motion Passed

 2. Resolution 2026-14, Granting a Temporary License to La Plata Electric Association 
at 21040 CR 500 (Trujillo Rd) 
 
  Town Manager Harris said La Plata Electric Association (LPEA) has begun construction on 
the new Trujillo Road electric substation on property being leased from the Town. They now 
require an easement across portions of the property for the transmission lines.  As an 
ordinance is required to grant this easement with first reading tonight and second reading on 
September 1st with the ordinance taking effect 30 days after the second reading and 
considering LPEA is interested in beginning construction on this line before then, the Town 
Attorney's office created the attached Resolution and Temporary License Agreement to 
allow them access in advance of the ordinance.   
 
Council Member Bergon wanted to know if the lines would be buried or overhead. Ryne 
Waggoner, LPEA Senior Director of Engineering, said it would be both because it is a busier 
corridor, and they are trying to make the impact as minimal as possible.  
 
Council Member Martinez, move approval of Resolution 2026-14, Granting a Temporary 
License to La Plata Electric Association at 2140 CR 500 (Trujillo Rd), Council Member 
deGraaf seconded. Motion Passed
