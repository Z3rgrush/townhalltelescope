RepresentaSves of the Town and Archuleta County joined the Board for porSons of the day.

success, and set prioriSes for the 2027 calendar year and beyond. The Board also held a facilitated 
discussion with Town and County representaSves on the working relaSonship between the two 
governments that fund the tourism program. 
This report documents what the Board produced in the room. It is intended as a working reference for 
the Board. Where the Board voted or reached agreement, the report says so. Where an idea was raised 
but not decided, the report presents it as such.

HOW SUCCESS IS MEASURED 
Local lodging operators do not report average daily rate, revenue per available room, or occupancy to

measure its own tourism economy. The retreat produced a pracScal path around it. 
The lodging associaSon has indicated it is not willing to disclose average daily rate and prefers not to

Monthly occupancy reporSng unlocks more than it appears to. Lodging tax collecSons are a funcSon of 
room rates and occupied rooms. The two inputs reveal the direcSon of average daily rate even though 
no operator discloses it. There are nine logical combinaSons:

Seven of the nine cases resolve cleanly. The two that do not, where collecSons and occupancy move in

typically rise together into summer and ski season and fall together through the shoulder months. In

TWO ASSUMPTIONS TO KEEP IN VIEW 
The inference rests on two condiSons that will usually hold month to month but can break, and when 
they break the index will mislead unless the change is accounted for: 
•Stable room supply. Occupancy rate is a percentage of available rooms. If the supply changes 
materially, for example a wave of short-term rentals entering the market or a property closing, 
occupancy can move without any change in rooms actually sold, and the index moves with it. As an 
illustraSon: if supply grows 10 percent and the same number of rooms are sold at the same rates 
for the same collecSons, occupancy falls from 80 percent to about 73 percent and the index rises 
to roughly 137,500, falsely suggesSng a rate increase of 10 percent. Year-over-year comparisons 
are the exposure. The supply inventory in the forthcoming lodging market study establishes the 
baseline for tracking this. 
•Stable tax rate and compliance. A change in the lodging tax rate obviously breaks the comparison, 
but a change in compliance does the same thing more quietly. Stepped-up enforcement of short-
term rental remifance, for example, would raise collecSons with no change in actual rates or 
occupancy, and would appear in the index as rate growth. When either changes, the Board should 
note it and reset the comparison baseline.
