II. BOARD ANNOUNCEMENTS 
 
 Pagosa Nursery Donation: 
Parks and Recreation Director Darren Lewis reported that Pagosa Nursery donated 12 trees to 
the Town. The trees were planted in the Shepherd area near Walmart and were incorporated 
into landscaping improvements around the eastern entrance sign welcoming visitors to Pagosa 
Springs. Director Darren Lewis expressed appreciation to Pagosa Nursery for the donation. 
 
Walmart/Shepherd Trail Tree Planting: 
Town of Pagosa staff is working on a grant application to add trees along the trail near Walmart 
and extending toward the Shepherd area and school. The project would provide additional 
shade along the trail and include irrigation improvements. 
They have discussed the project with the school district, which is supportive of allowing the 
Town to connect to its water system. A separate meter would be installed to track the Town's 
water usage. The grant decision is anticipated around May 2027.

VII. BOARD ANNOUNCEMENTS 
 
  
Kids Camp – 2027 
Parks and Recreation Director Darren Lewis announced that the Town will no longer operate 
Kids Camp beginning in 2027. The program has been successful since 2016; however, 
increasing childcare regulations, facility security concerns, staffing requirements, and impacts 
on other Community Center programming have made the program increasingly difficult to 
operate. 
The Community Center's open public access presents additional challenges for maintaining a

Potential Transition to PCI 
Director Lewis reported that discussions are underway with PCI regarding providing a summer 
camp program beginning in 2027. PCI has expressed interest and has access to a more secure 
facility at the high school. 
The Town plans to assist with the transition to help maintain summer programming opportunities 
for local families. 
 
 
Kids Camp Funding 
The Board discussed the approximately $40,000 annual Town subsidy for Kids Camp and 
whether funding could potentially be used to support another provider. 
Director Lewis explained that any future funding arrangement would require a decision by Town 
Council as part of the budget process.  
 
 
Notification to Families 
Director Lewis reported that families currently participating in Kids Camp have been notified of 
the program's discontinuation beginning in 2027. 
Director Lewis will continue working with families and potential providers to help make the 
transition as smooth as possible.

 1. Parks and Recreation Report 
 
   
Recreation Master Plan / Community Survey 
Parks and Recreation Director Darren Lewis provided an update on the Parks and Recreation 
community survey. 
The survey closed on August 31, 2026, with slightly more than 300 participants. 
Parks and Rec staff plans to compile the survey results and provide the information to Board 
members for review. The Board will use the results to compare public priorities with goals 
previously discussed by the Board. 
The Board agreed that reviewing community feedback before establishing short- and long-
term Parks and Recreation goals would be beneficial.

Reservoir Hill 
Staff provided an update regarding the Town's efforts to acquire Reservoir Hill. 
 
GOCO Grant 
The Town is completing a GOCO grant application requesting approximately $1.8 million 
toward the acquisition. 
Director Lewis reported that GOCO received 49 letters of intent and invited 17 applicants, 
including Pagosa Springs, to submit full applications. 
The application is due September 10, 2026. Staff hopes to be invited to make a presentation 
in November, with a final funding decision expected in December. 
 
Forest Legacy Grant 
The Town is also pursuing a Forest Legacy Grant. The project was included among the top 
applicants, with approximately $5.7 million potentially available. 
Funding would not be available until approximately fall 2028. 
 
Funding Gap and Fundraising 
The total purchase price is approximately $8.2 million. Depending on grant awards, the 
Town could still have an estimated $500,000–$600,000 remaining to raise. 
Director Lewis discussed developing a community fundraising effort beginning in 2027 and 
continuing into 2028. 
The Board also discussed the importance of establishing an appropriate structure for 
accepting donations and determining the Town's role in future fundraising efforts. 
 
 
Parks Project Updates 
Bike Park 
Director Lewis reported that construction of the Bike Park at Yamaguchi South is underway. 
Completion is anticipated sometime in the fall, potentially around November. 
 
River Center Ponds 
Work is continuing on the River Center ponds following previous flood damage. 
The ponds will be dredged, and material removed from the ponds is being stored and reused 
in areas along the trail. 
The Board discussed the trail near 5th Street and Apache, which has experienced repeated 
washouts. Town Council has decided to abandon that portion as a formal trail due to 
continued damage. 
Director Lewis and parks staff is also looking at replacing the damaged head gate and installing 
a culvert to improve water flow into the ponds. 
 
Trail Maintenance 
Director Lewis reported that the Walmart Trail and Town Park Trail have been sealed. The 
River Center Trail will be sealed once current work in the area is complete. 
 
AmeriCorps 
An AmeriCorps work crew has been assisting the Town for approximately seven weeks.

 1. Astronomy Shed Proposal (Dena Laterza) 
 
  
Astronomy Program / Pagosa Sidewalk Astronomers 
The Board received a presentation from Dina Leterza of the Pagosa Sidewalk Astronomers 
regarding the possibility of establishing an astronomy storage and educational facility.

Dina discussed her involvement in astronomy and the development of the local astronomy 
group. She described the group's interest in public education, community events, and dark-
sky advocacy. 
The group would like to provide more frequent astronomy programming for residents and 
visitors, including telescope viewing and educational events. 
Proposed Astronomy Facility 
The proposal involves a small shed-type structure that could be used to securely store 
telescopes and other equipment. 
Potential uses include: 
• Telescope storage 
• Astronomy education 
• Public viewing events 
• Youth programs 
• Astrophotography 
• Multimedia presentations 
• Solar astronomy programs 
Yamaguchi South was discussed as one possible location. However, other locations may 
need to be considered. 
 
 
Dark Sky Considerations 
Dina discussed the Town's efforts to preserve its dark skies and pursue International Dark 
Sky Community designation. 
The Board discussed the benefits of astronomy programming as it relates to the Town's 
dark-sky efforts. 
Ir was also discussed potential conflicts with existing lighting, particularly near the high 
school and other facilities. 
Parks and Recreation Director Lewis and the group agreed that any proposed location should be 
evaluated at night to determine how existing lighting would affect the program. 
 
 
School District Coordination 
The Board discussed potential collaboration with the school district. 
Concerns were raised regarding lighting required for school safety and transportation 
operations. The Board recognized that necessary safety lighting could conflict with 
astronomy programming. 
It was suggested that the astronomy group, town staff, and school district coordinate before 
a final location is selected. 
The Board also discussed the possibility of developing astronomy programming in 
partnership with the school district. 
 
 
Public Park Use, Security & Liability 
The Board discussed concerns regarding placing a privately operated storage facility in a 
public park.

Issues raised included: 
• Security of expensive telescope equipment 
• Potential vandalism or theft 
• Town liability 
• Public access 
• Frequency of use 
• Appearance of the structure 
• Potential precedent for other organizations requesting storage buildings 
The Board recognized that other recreational organizations currently have storage structures 
or arrangements in Town parks, but members emphasized the importance of establishing 
clear criteria for these types of partnerships. 
Director Lewis suggested that a formal Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) could outline 
responsibilities, liability, security, programming expectations, and the relationship between 
the Town and the astronomy organization. 
 
 
14. Astronomy Proposal – Next Steps 
The Board expressed general support for astronomy programming and its connection to the 
Town's dark-sky efforts. However, the Board was not prepared to make a final 
recommendation regarding the proposed facility. 
Additional information is needed regarding: 
• Potential locations 
• Security 
• Liability and indemnification 
• School district coordination 
• Town/astronomy group partnership 
• Programming opportunities 
• Public benefit 
Director Lewis will continue researching the proposal and bring the topic back to the Board in 
November. 
The Board requested that the November presentation include information showing how the 
astronomy program could potentially become a formal Recreation program or partnership. 
The proposal will not be taken to Town Council on September 15. The intent is to further 
develop the proposal, return it to the Advisory Board in November, and then potentially take 
it to Town Council following the Board's recommendation.

Parks Department: 
 
  - Staff continue performing ongoing maintenance throughout all parks and properties, 
including pruning bushes and trees along riverwalks and near buildings. 
  - Staff are monitoring flower planters and baskets for dieback due to changing weather 
and temperatures, and are moving planters accordingly. 
  - Fall cleanup has begun throughout the parks 
  - Waterline repair excavation at the dog park is tentatively scheduled, pending weather 
and excavator accessibility. 
  - Staff repaired two sidewalk sections and installed a bicycle rack pad, with assistance 
from the sanitation department for concrete finishing. 
  - At Bell Tower Park, staff repaired grading and irrigation, spread new mulch, and 
seeded the grass area damaged by the Highway 160 reconstruction project. 
  - Irrigation blowouts were completed during the week of October 5th–9th. 
  - Staff are addressing and repairing turf damage at the Visitor Center and Yamaguchi 
Park caused by recent rain, event vehicle traffic, and sewer maintenance. Shade 
structures will be taken down over the next two weeks. 
  - River levels are being monitored, with tables and benches near the water's edge 
moved to prevent flood damage. 
  - Preparations and layout marking have begun for the rail fencing installation in the 400 
block medians. 
   
 
Recreation Department: 
 
Free Community Programs 
In September, 690 community members participated in our free program 
offerings, including yoga, dance, mommy and me sessions, drop-in 
volleyball, open gym, outdoor skills classes, and pickleball lessons 
and play. 
 
 
Sports & Registration 
The youth soccer season has wrapped up, with all age groups completing 
their seasons despite several weather-related delays. Girls ages 10–12 
and co-ed basketball ages 7–9 start this week. With 108 youth 
registered, participation is slightly ahead of last year's numbers. 
The beginner Wrestling Wednesdays program for ages 3–4 received 
positive feedback, and staff will explore opportunities to expand the 
program after the wrestling season concludes. Registration is also 
open for boys basketball ages 10–12, with the season beginning in

Reservoir Hill 
Dolecek Enterprises continues collecting data to support the 
development of a 10-year forest management plan funded through the 
Colorado State Forest Service Forest Restoration and Wildfire Risk 
Mitigation (FRWRM) Grant. Sticks and Stones Management has also begun 
forest health and wildfire mitigation work, which is expected to 
continue for approximately two weeks. Signage has been placed around 
active work areas. Trail users are asked to avoid these areas while 
work is underway to ensure public and worker safety. 
 
 
TOPS Parks and Recreation Master Plan 
Staff continues assessing existing parks, recreation facilities, and 
trail mapping as part of the Parks and Recreation Master Plan process. 
The Combined Parks and Recreation Board will review the assessment 
findings and community survey feedback at its November meeting to help 
refine goals and establish future priorities based on community needs. 
 
 
Parks Area Recreation Coalition (PARC) 
PARC representatives recently participated in a panel at the Colorado 
Outdoor Industry Leaders (COILS) Conference, answering questions about 
Colorado's Outdoor Strategy and the role of funding supported through 
Colorado Parks and Wildlife's Regional Partnerships initiative. 
Collaboration with Southwest Regional Partnerships continues to grow, 
strengthening regional coordination and shared recreation priorities. 
The Town continues supporting PARC's collaborative efforts, including 
facilitation, regional coordination, and early-win initiatives.
