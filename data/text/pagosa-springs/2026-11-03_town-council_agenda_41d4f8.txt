 2. Ordinance 1042, First Reading, Continued from October 6, Ponderosa Mountain 
Planned Development Agreement and Zoning Map Amendment 
 
  Modern Community Builders LLC has applied for the approval of Ponderosa Mountain, a 
major subdivision, near Hwy 160 and Gold Mine Drive. The developer is proposing to fully 
develop the site, in phases, with a mix of residential and commercial.  
Ordinance 1042 amends the official zoning map and approves a planned development 
overlay district and a planned development agreement for property known as Ponderosa 
Mountain, located near Hwy 160 and Goldmine Drive. The development will include 
commercial lodging, housing, including workforce housing (90%-130% AMI and deed-
restricted), and amenities. This development covers 70 acres and is proposed in three 
phases. A complete major subdivision must be filed within one year of the PD approval, with 
an extension to encourage development moving forward. The Town Council recommended 
continuing this item and public hearing at the October 6th meeting. 
Staff presented the project overlay plan, approval criteria, Comprehensive Plan goals and 
LUDC criteria. Staff said it is the developers' responsibility to contact the utility providers to 
determine the needs for the development. Mr. Jeff Sams, Multi-Jurisdictional Housing 
Coordinator, said the Housing Action Plan addresses the housing needs in the community. 
The developers provided information about the proposed development. Mr. Fink said this 
type of housing project is needed in Pagosa Springs and appreciates the unique vibe of the 
community. He said the pricing of 24 homes in Phase 1 is planned to be priced between 
$365 and $500k each. He said it will take up to ten years to complete the full build out of this 
development. This includes eighty home sites for sale, thirty workforce houses and fifty 
single-family homes. He said there will be another twenty condo-type units and lodging 
facilities in the commercial space. Mr. Phil Williams said the developer will bear the costs of 
the utilities. He said there is a natural barrier between the Alpha Rockridge development and 
this development. He said a light at Great West Ave and Hwy 160 is not required by CDOT 
per their traffic study at this time, but they will invest in a light should CDOT require it.  
Ms. Emily Lashbrooke said the current economic data shows 1,267 vacant jobs, with 
employers reporting lack of housing as the reason.  
Ms. Jane Weitzel said this development will not help Pagosa by bringing in more tourism. 
She said people will cut through on the Alpha Rockridge roads. 
Ms. Sharon Carter said the LUDC and Comprehensive Plan are outdated. She said the plan 
is not compatible and harmonious with Pagosa. 
Ms. Terri Pritchard said the community is a mess with not enough hospital workers or law 
enforcement. She said the wildlife are suffering from the drought and the grocery stores are 
empty.  
Ms. Lisa Gamber said a California developer can't really know the community and just wants 
to make money. She said this is a large resort development and not affordable homes. She

This Planned Development Agreement (the “Agreement”) is made and entered into 
this ______ day of _______, 2026, by and between the Town of Pagosa Springs, a municipal 
corporation (hereinafter referred to as the “Town”), and Modern Community Builders, Inc. 
(hereinafter referred to as the “Developer”), collectively referred to herein as the “Parties”. 
 
RECITALS 
A. The Developer is the owner (condition of approval) of certain property within the Town limits 
comprised of 3 properties totaling approximately 70 acres (the “Property”), more specifically 
described on the attached Exhibit A. 
 
B. The Developer has submitted an application for a Planned Development (“PD”) with a multi-
phased Concept Plan as set forth in Exhibit B, the “Project”, incorporated herein by reference, 
and has sought approval to rezone the Property to develop as a Planned Development 
containing residential housing, lodging rental units/cabins and associated light commercial 
uses. The Parties agree to replace Exhibit B with the Final Approved Major Subdivision Plan 
approved by the Town through the subsequent required approval processes.

section 2.4.2.D.

future Project phasing plans shall be consistent with the final approved PD Agreement and PD

through the Major Subdivision application process and this approved Agreement shall run with 
the property and be an obligation of all successors of ownership, for all or any portion of the 
subject Property, subject to any Performance Contingencies identified in section 19 of this 
Agreement.  
 
F. The Developer proposes the inclusion of workforce housing units as a primary community 
benefit for considering the PD Proposal. Additional community benefits include; secondary 
public roadway, Town to Lakes non-motorized commuter trail, interior public trails and 
dedicated Open Space.

AGREEMENT 
For good and valuable consideration, including the covenants, agreements, and pledges 
contained herein, the parties agree as follows: 
 
1. Compliance with all Laws, Ordinances, Approvals, and Permits:   
The Developer agrees to design, construct, install, and operate the Project in accordance 
with approvals received from all governmental entities and utility providers with applicable 
jurisdiction. The Developer agrees to comply with all state and local laws, ordinances, and 
regulations as well as the terms of this Agreement, the Town’s Land Use Development Code 
(LUDC), and approved Development Applications.  
 
2. Town Development Applications Required:  The Developer shall submit required 
Development applications as required by the Town, for consideration and approval by the 
respective Town Board/Commission, and its various departments, for consistency with the 
Land Use Development Code, adopted building codes, regulations, ordnances and standards 
within this Agreement and Approved PD site plan, prior to work being conducted.  
a. Phased Development Applications. Prior to the issuance of building permits for each phase 
of the Project, and prior to any construction  of any infrastructure/utility improvement, 
building, or structure commencing on the Property, the Developer shall submit 
development applications and receive approval for the following:  
i. Preliminary Major Subdivision applications.

11. Attainable Workforce Housing Units:

the Ponderosa Mountain Planned Development. Thus the following provisions are meant 
to ensure that as the project advances through multiple phases, attainable workforce 
housing units are being constructed concurrently with residential unit and resort cabin 
unit construction.  
i. Developer shall provide a minimum of 18% of the total of all resort cabin units and 
residential units combined in phase-one as  attainable workforce housing units.  
Construction of phase-one attainable workforce housing units shall occur 
concurrently with the construction of resort (cabin) units and residential units at a 
rate of 18% of the combined total of units being constructed in each phase-one plat. 
Developer proposes 82 residential units and 82 resort cabins units in phase-one, 
equating to 30 attainable workforce housing units in phase-one.  
ii. Subsequent to and separate from phase-one, the Developer shall provide a 12% 
minimum of attainable workforce housing units compared to the total number of 
residential units as the Project advances after the phase-one commitments stated 
in section 11.a.i above. At no time shall the total percentage of attainable workforce 
housing units be less than 12% in relation to the total number of cumulated 
residential units.  
iii. Attainable workforce housing ownership units shall serve 90-130% of Area Median 
Income (AMI) consistent with the annua lly published Colorado Housing Finance 
Authority (CHFA) AMI table for Archuleta County.  
iv. Developer shall provide an equal split of the attainable workforce housing 
ownership units as all phases advance with an equal number of units in each AMI 
Range as follows: 90%-100%, 101%-110%, 111%-120%, 121%-130%. Applicable 
monthly housing expenses shall be inclusive of mortgage, utilities, HOA dues, taxes 
and insurance. 
v. Attainable ownership units shall be deed-restricted in perpetuity to ensure 
attainability is sustainable throughout future successors of ownership. 
vi. Town shall approve all attainable workforce housing sale agreements, deed 
restrictions, lease agreements, and ot her related documentation to ensure 
compliance with adopted regulations, policies and guidelines. 
vii. Short-term vacation rentals shall be prohibited for all deed-restricted attainable 
workforce housing units, including rental and ownership units.

REVIEW PROCEDURE 
Article 2.4.2 D.2 of the Town of Pagosa Springs’ Land Use Development C ode (“the Code”) r equires the procedure for 
rezoning to the Planned Development overlay district to undergo public he arings before the Planning Commission and 
Town Council.   The Planning Commission considered the  c omments a nd evidence pr esented in a public he aring o n 
September 8, 2026, where they approved the presented PD Agreement with amendments.  
All public notice requirements in Article 2.3.6 and Table 2.3-1 have been complied with for this project. 
DISCUSSION 
The a pplicant, M odern Community Builders  L LC, is pr oposing a m ulti-phased subdivision development,  o therwise 
known as a major subdivision. The Sketch Plan of the major subdivision was also heard, and approved, by the Planning 
Commission on September 8, 2026. However, sketch plans for major subdivisions do not require Town Council approval. 
Town Council will  ha ve a n opportunity to r eview t he s ubdivision proposal at t he P reliminary Plan review,  a fter 
substantial engineering and site design is completed.  
This subdivision is anticipated to be built in phases, with phase 1 focused on the commercial lodging development and 
the construction of 54 for-sale Single-Family Residences (SFR). Of the 54 homes, 30 of them would be required to be 
dedicated as Affordable Workforce Housing Ownership Units, with deed restrictions for AMI Categories 
ranging between 90%-120%. The remaining phases of this development are under a Residential Medium Density 
(R-12 = 12 units per acre) base zoning and requires a minimum of 12% of the housing units to be deed restricted 
Affordable Workforce Housing ownership units.  
Page 39 of 143
 
What is being presented before you today is specific to the request of a Planned Development overlay (rezone). 
Article 3.5.1 of the Land Use Development Code (LUDC) defines the intent of the Planned Development Overlay (OPD) 
to:  
A. Allow and encourage compatible uses to be developed in accordance with a unified development plan in 
harmony with the environment and surrounding neighborhood;  
B. Permit greater flexibility in the application of zoning and development standards and greater freedom in 
providing a mix of land uses in the development of a balanced community; and  
C. Encourage the preservation of critical environmental resources, provide above average open space and 
recreational amenities (as applicable), include exceptional and innovative design, and provide greater 
efficiency in the layout and provisions of roads, utilities, and other infrastructure.  
 
The Planned Development agreement creates a PD Overlay district and defines specific development requirements, 
uses, design criteria and allowances that may vary from the adherence of base zoning regulations contained in the LUDC. 
The PD Agreement also identifies benefits to the Town that align with established needs and priorities. This agreement, 
if approved, will run with the land in perpetuity, unless the development does not move forward pursuant to the 
agreement terms, in which case the Town Council may reverse the PD Agreement approval and zoning map amendment. 
if the PD approval is kept in place, Items like workforce housing commitments will be required for anyone that aims to 
develop the land in the future. Pla nned Development agreements can be approved by Town Council by an ordinance, 
of which, is considered the first of two readings.  
  
This OPD acts as an umbrella overlay zoning for the entire property with base zoning designations of Mixed Use Corridor 
(MU-C), Medium Density Residential (R- 12), and Parks and Open Space (OS), all of which replacing the existing Rural 
Transition (R-T) zoning on the property.  This property was identified as an Area of Change in the 2018 Comprehensive  
Plan from Rural Transition to High Density Residential (R- 22). The portion of land that is being allocated as the 
commercial lodging units is requesting a zoning designation of Mixed -Use Corridor, which staff finds appropriate given 
its proximity to U.S. Highway 160, and other surrounding commercial developments along the Hwy 160 corridor having 
commercial business exposure, in this case a lodging facility. In the initial PD application, that was presented to the 
Planning Commission, the applicant requested Mixed-Use Residential for all subsequent phases, but staff recommended 
R-22 to maintain alignment with the Comprehensive Plan. However, the Planning Commission, and numerous members 
of the public, felt that zoning designation would promote too high of density in the area given the adjacent 
neighborhoods to the south and west. It was negotiated that the subsequent phases beyond co mmercial development 
be considered Medium Density Residential (R-12).  
 
Other aspects of this PD agreement worth noting would be the 5 acre open space dedication along the highway corridor, 
the green space at the northwest portion of the property and 10% of the gross acreage in each phase being dedicated 
open space. There are also design criteria for all SFRs and infrastructure development within the property.  
 
Staff recommends, prior to the execution of the OPD, that the applicants own the property. They are currently applying 
for these approvals with the consent of the current property owner, Ruth Stanger. If approved, the OPD must be signed 
by the property owner and staff does not find it appropriate for Ruth to sign, rather the proposed developers once the 
purchase is completed.

This PD request provides substantial benefit to the Town of Pagosa Springs with the workforce housing 
commitment. With 30 units in phase 1 and 12% in every subsequent phase, it aids in providing a missing

This chapter opens with an introduction of the human environment within the Town, that has been 
perpetuated by the land use patterns, transportation systems, and the built environment. It noted 
the significant growth that the Town, and County, was undergoing in 2018 that has yet to subside. It

supply, which any economist could conclude would be a significant contributor to rising housing

Another issue for housing, and our human environment, is the demographic age of our area. 
Archuleta County is one of the oldest counties per capita in Colorado. At the time of the creation of 
the Comprehensive Plan, the median age of the County residents h ad grown from 40.8 years old in 
2000 to 48.8 years old in 2018. Now, the median age is 52.1 years old compared to the state average 
of 37.7 years old. Pagosa Springs has become a retirement community, which is difficult to sustain. 
As the quantity of older adults rise, communities like Pagosa Springs can face challenges related to 
labor supply, productivity, and economic growth. These residents rely heavily on the already 
depleted workforce, without significant change in the housing stock the problem could potentially 
compound to significantly worse conditions.
