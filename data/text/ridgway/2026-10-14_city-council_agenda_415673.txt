Housing Services Quarterly Report Q3 (July - September 2026) 
 
This quarterly report is provided as part of the established reporting cadence for the regional 
Housing Services Administrator between Home Trust of Ouray County (HTOC), the Town of 
Ridgway, the City of Ouray, and Ouray County. Quarterly reports are provided in January, April, 
July, and October to summarize Housing Services activities and accomplishments from the 
preceding quarter and identify priorities and planned activities for the upcoming quarter. 
This Q3 2026 report covers activities from July through September and provides a look ahead to 
Q4 2026. It summarizes work across regional Housing Services administration, deed-restriction 
administration and compliance, intergovernmental coordination, stakeholder collaboration, 
program and policy development, and ﬁnancial and grant management. 
The quarterly reporting process provides a consistent opportunity to communicate progress, 
identify upcoming priorities, and maintain coordination among HTOC and the three 
participating jurisdictions as regional housing programs and initiatives continue to develop.

Re: Notice of Intent to Sell property at 683 Frederick Street and Consideration of Exercising the

INTRODUCTION 
The Housing Services Administrator has received a Notice of Intent to Sell for the deed-
restricted housing unit located at 683 Frederick Street (the “Housing Unit”). The Housing Unit is 
subject to the recorded Deed Restriction and Covenant for Ridgway Wetterhorn Homes (the 
“Covenant”) and the applicable Aﬀordable Housing Regulations and Guidelines for Ridgway 
Wetterhorn Homes (the “Guidelines”). 
The purpose of this memorandum is to provide the Ridgway Workforce & Aﬀordable Housing 
Committee with the information necessary to consider whether the Town of Ridgway should 
exercise its Right of First Refusal (“ROFR”) to purchase the Housing Unit at the Maximum Sale 
Price (“MSP”). 
The memorandum also outlines the process and requirements that would apply under each 
potential outcome: 
1. The Town exercises its ROFR and proceeds with the purchase; or 
2. The Town declines to exercise its ROFR and the Seller proceeds with the resale of the 
Housing Unit to an eligible third-party purchaser. 
______________________________________________________________________________ 
BACKGROUND 
The Housing Unit is subject to the recorded Deed Restriction and Covenant for Ridgway 
Wetterhorn Homes (the “Covenant”), which establishes requirements governing ownership, 
resale, Maximum Sale Price, purchaser eligibility, and the Beneﬁciaries’ Right of First Refusal 
(“ROFR”). The Beneﬁciaries identiﬁed in the Covenant include the Town of Ridgway and Rural 
Homes, LLC, subject to the deﬁnitions and priority provisions contained in the Covenant. 
September 25, 2026 – Notice of Intent to Sell Received. The Housing Services Administrator 
(“HA”) received the Seller’s Notice of Intent to Sell for the Housing Unit located at 683 Frederick 
 
Street. The Notice is included as Attachment 1. Under Section 7.1.1 of the Covenant, the Notice 
initiates the resale process and provides notice to the Beneﬁciaries of the Owner’s intent to sell. 
September 25, 2026 – Beneﬁciaries Notiﬁed. On the same date, the HA notiﬁed both the Town 
of Ridgway and Rural Homes, LLC by email of the Notice of Intent to Sell and the applicable 
Right of First Refusal. 
September 28, 2026 – Rural Homes Declines ROFR. Rural Homes, LLC conﬁrmed that it would 
not exercise its Right of First Refusal to purchase the Housing Unit. 
October 14, 2026 – Town Consideration. The matter is before the Ridgway Workforce & 
Aﬀordable Housing Committee for consideration of whether the Town will exercise its ROFR to 
purchase the Housing Unit at the Maximum Sale Price. 
October 26, 2026 – ROFR Period Expires. Pursuant to Section 7.4.2 of the Covenant, the 
Beneﬁciaries’ 30-day period to exercise the ROFR runs from receipt of the Notice of Intent to 
Sell. Based on the September 25, 2026 receipt date, the current ROFR period expires Monday, 
October 26, 2026. 
______________________________________________________________________________ 
NOTICE OF INTENT TO SELL 
Section 7.4 of the Covenant provides that, upon the Owner’s Notice of Intent to Sell, the 
Beneﬁciaries have a Right of First Refusal (“ROFR”) to purchase the Housing Unit for the oﬀered 
sales price or the Maximum Sale Price, as applicable. 
Section 7.4.2 provides that a Beneﬁciary exercising the ROFR must execute a written and 
binding commitment to purchase and provide the required deposit within 30 days after 
receiving written Notice of Intent to Sell. 
Both the Town and Rural Homes, LLC received the Seller’s Notice of Intent to Sell on September 
25, 2026. Accordingly, the 30-day ROFR period expires October 26, 2026. The Workforce & 
Aﬀordable Housing Committee’s October 14th meeting occurs within the applicable ROFR 
period. 
The Ridgway Wetterhorn Aﬀordable Housing Regulations and Guidelines separately provide in 
Section 6.1.2 that written notice of intent to sell must be provided to the Housing Administrator 
(“HA”) at least 60 days prior to the sale of the Housing Unit. HA understands this provision to 
address the Seller’s required advance notice of a contemplated sale, while the Covenant’s 30-
day period governs the Beneﬁciary’s exercise of the ROFR. HA is noting the distinction because 
both provisions establish timelines within the resale process. HA understands the 60-day notice 
 
requirement to apply to the Seller’s notice to the HA and the 30-day period to apply to the 
Beneﬁciaries’ decision to exercise the ROFR. The 60-day notice requirement therefore does not 
extend or delay the Town’s 30-day ROFR period. 
 
MARKETING AND SUBSEQUENT OFFERS 
Section 6.7 of the Guidelines provides that a Seller may advertise, market, and sell a Housing 
Unit directly or list the Housing Unit with a real estate broker licensed to do business in 
Colorado, subject to the requirements of the Covenant and Guidelines. 
The Covenant and Guidelines do not expressly require the Seller to wait until the Beneﬁciaries’ 
ROFR period has expired before advertising or marketing the Housing Unit. In addition, Section 
7.1.1 of the Covenant requires the Owner to provide the Notice of Intent to Sell to the 
Beneﬁciaries before oﬀering the Housing Unit for sale. Based on these provisions, HA 
understands that the Seller may advertise and market the Housing Unit during the ROFR period, 
following delivery of the required Notice of Intent to Sell. 
Marketing the Housing Unit does not, however, eliminate or bypass the Beneﬁciaries’ ROFR or 
the other requirements governing the resale. Covenant Section 7.4.1 provides that the 
Beneﬁciaries’ ROFR applies upon the Owner’s Notice of Intent to Sell and also if the Owner 
receives an oﬀer to purchase or tenders an oﬀer of sale for the Housing Unit. 
Guidelines Section 6.6 further provides that, if an Owner receives any oﬀer to purchase or 
tenders any oﬀer of sale for the Housing Unit, each Beneﬁciary or its successor must be notiﬁed 
within ﬁve (5) days of such event and has the Right of First Refusal to purchase the Housing Unit 
for the Maximum Sale Price or at the oﬀered sales price, as provided in the Covenant. 
Accordingly, if the Seller receives an oﬀer to purchase the Housing Unit while the Town’s ROFR is 
pending, the Seller must provide the required notice to the Beneﬁciaries within ﬁve (5) days. 
The receipt of an oﬀer does not eliminate the existing ROFR or permit the Seller to proceed with 
a sale without complying with the Covenant and Guidelines. 
The current ROFR period was triggered by the September 25, 2026 Notice of Intent to Sell and, 
pursuant to Section 7.4.2 of the Covenant, expires October 26, 2026. Thus, an oﬀer received 
during the current ROFR period remains subject to the Beneﬁciaries’ ROFR and the applicable 
notice requirements of the Guidelines.

MAXIMUM SALE PRICE 
Section 7.3 of the Covenant establishes the Maximum Sale Price for the Housing Unit. 
The Original Purchase Price (“OPP”) is the basis for calculating the MSP . Section 7.3.2 provides 
that the MSP may not exceed the sum of: 
1. The Original Purchase Price paid by the Owner; 
2. An increase of 3% of the Original Purchase Price per year, compounded annually and 
prorated as provided in the Covenant; 
3. Applicable special improvement district assessments paid by the Seller during the 
Seller’s ownership that are not transferable; and 
4. The cost of qualifying Permitted Capital Improvements, subject to the requirements and 
limitations of the Covenant. 
5. Housing Unit must meet the applicable Minimum Resale Standards in order to sell at 
M S P.
