WORK SESSION WITH PLANNING COMMISSION 
 
10.  Review of draft 2026 Housing Action Plan 
 
The Council met with members of the Planning Commission to review the draft Housing 
Action Plan, project progress and provide feedback on the items being proposed in the plan.  
 
Representatives from Points Consulting LLC, Mike Manning and Brian Points presented the 
8-3-26 draft Housing Action Plan required under Colorado Senate Bill 24 -174.  They noted 
the project is expected to be completed by mid October.  They displayed slides and reviewed 
recommended goals and policies  organized into five implementation areas which were  
policies and procedures; provide for future housing; workforce housing enhancement; 
momentum building; life cycle housing provisions.  It was noted these areas address 
development review and regulatory processes, land and infrastructure planning, workforce 
and affordable housing programs, funding and implementation capacity, and housing needs 
across different stages of life.   
 
Mr. Manning presented the action plan for each of the categories and the implementation 
approaches to meet the required actions and timelines  established by the new state 
regulations.  These included enhancing the speed of public review and hearing processes; 
simplifying the townhome and condominium subdivision process; supporting affordable and 
workforce housing; exploring opportunities for ADU and modular units; modernized the 
development review process; pre -approve small in- fill housing units ; implement a land 
banking program; public education and promotion of deed restrictions and covenants; 
expansion of community land trust; explore opportunities for employer sponsored programs; 
indicate highest priority building locations for workforce and affordable housing; align housing 
growth with long- term water supply and infrastructure capacity; implement a land banking 
program; public education and promotion of deed restrictions and covenants ; explore 
opportunities for employer sponsored programs and housing coops.   
 
There were comments from the Planning Commission and Council regarding items which 
should be researched for addition into the plan.

10.  Presentation of Ridgway Free Box concept  
 
Ariel Hessler, a junior at Ridgway Secondary School,  presented slides which outlined her 
memorandum pertaining to creation of a Free Box in Town.  She stated the Free Box would 
be a publicly accessible, weatherproof structure where members of the community could 
donate gently used clothing and outdoor gear, and residents could take what is needed, free 
of charge.  She noted the goal is to create “a simple, community supported system based on 
generosity, sustainability and mutual aid”.  Estimates to construction the box is $1920 and 
she asked the Council for funding of $1000, noting she is also seeking support from 
community and local foundations. She has explored potential locations allowing access every 
day, at all hours and is hoping to place it on the north side of the public library.  The intention 
is installation by spring of 2027 and maintenance to be provided by volunteers from high 
school students who are affiliated with the National Honor Society, or seeking to fulfill 
volunteer hours.  Maintenance would be weekly checks of the box to sort donations, remove 
unusable and inappropriate items, and ensure the area is clean and well-organized.  
 
There were questions and comments from the Council, and discussion with Ms. Hessler.   
 
Speaking from the audience Sue Husch noted the local thrift store has a problem with 
disposing of unusable items, and requires dumpsters to be emptied every week. She 
questioned who will carry the program forward after Ms. Hessler graduates from school.  
 
The Council agreed to see how the program works with the caveat the box may not be placed 
on Town property, and will not become a program which Town staff has to manage. It was 
agreed to donate $750 to the project. 
 
ACTION: 
 
M
oved by Councilor Kroger, seconded by Councilor Scoville to contribute $750 to the Ridgway 
Free Box with the condition the National Honor Society must make a commitment to oversee the 
maintenance.  The motion passed unanimously.

13.  Organization of Alpenglow Vista Metropolitan District Numbers 1 through 4 and approval of 
a Consolidated Service Plan 
 
Memorandum from Town Attorney  Bo Nerlin regarding a Service Plan for Alpenglow Vista 
Metropolitan District No 1 through 4 at  N. Laura Street, west side of McCall Street  an d  
Roundhouse Street, and west of N. Cora Street, which is zoned Light Industrial and Mixed 
Residential and owned by Ridgway Triangle LLC. 
 
  The Town Attorney explained staff has been working with Ridgway Triangle LLC and its 
principal, Charles Mueller, also the applicant, since May of this year with respect to the 
possibility of creating a series of metropolitan districts within the Town.  A metropolitan or 
metro district, is a special district, quasi-municipal corporation and political subdivision of the 
State, and subject to the Colorado Special District Act. The applicant submitted a 
Consolidated Service Plan to the Town on July 21 st and additional exhibits were presented 
on August 8th.  The plan proposes the creation of four separate metropolitan districts to serve 
approximately 19.06 acres of residential and commercial property  located off of N. Laura 
Street, west side of McCall and Roundhouse Streets, west of N. Cora Street.  It anticipates 
approximately 150 residential units, 50,000 square feet of commercial and light -industrial 
development, and phased buildout over approximately 20 to 25 years.  The proposal  is to 
establish four metro districts to finance and coordinate construction of public improvements

to service the development and operate and maintain the improvements.  After completion 
of the capital improvements, it is possible they would be dedicated to the Town. The applicant 
is seeking Council approval of the Service Plan, before proceeding with a development

Attorney Nerlin presented the staff analysis of the Service Plan and recommended conditions 
of approval, and Resolution No. 26 -06 approving the Consolidated Service Plan for

boundaries of the district. Approval will establish mill levies with indebtedness over the next 
40 years.  A district is given tax exemptions, which yields lower costs in the end to persons 
purchasing within the district.

14.  Proposed County Sales Tax to support disaster mitigation and response  
 
Antonio Mendez, Ouray County Manager, explained the County Commissioners are discussing 
presenting the voters with a ballot question to increase county sales tax by .5 percent to 
fund emergency preparedness.  The funds would help to build the fund after the recent 
unexpected costs to due to the Gold Mountain Fire, and floodings from monsonal rains.  He 
noted the Town and City of Ouray would need to agree to a split in the tax, which is 
currently anticipated to be 1 million, and would be split 80% county, 10% city and 10% 
town.  He asked for support from the Town.  
 
There were questions and comments from the Council.  There were questions to the Town 
Manager, and comments by the Manager.  There was conversation between the Council 
land Mr. Mendez.

13.  Presentation on Destination Marketing Plan  
 
Sara Pinnix with Destination by Design presented a draft marketing strategy  for the Town 
which was “led by community input and ideas”, “to help you tell your story”.  The three core 
principles and strategic framework of the plan are local life leads; purposeful stewardship; 
tourism that works here. She presented the first year marketing objectives which included 
stabilizing the shoulder season, guiding area visitors to  the Town core and promot ing

August 12, 2026

stewardship messaging; audience segmentation and market positioning addressing heritage, 
culture and regional travelers. Focusing on the facts the Town is “unique” “welcoming” and 
has “some really great things to offer” she suggested positioning should include authenticity, 
dark sky community, outdoor recreation access, and a unique arts community.  The first 
campaign concept would be “Rooted in Ridgway” and include “rooted in earth” focus on the  
outdoors and stewardship; “rooted in story” history, heritage and local life; “rooted in culture”

cosmos” focusing on fall foliage and dark skies. 
 
There were questions and comments from the Council.
