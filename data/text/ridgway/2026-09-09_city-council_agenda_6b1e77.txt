RE: Letter of Support for Proposed Recreation Fees for Blue Lakes Area 
 
 
To Whom It May Concern: 
 
On behalf of the Town of Ridgway, I am writing to express support for Grand Mesa, 
Uncompahgre and Gunnison (GMUG) National Forests’ Ouray Ranger District’s proposal for 
new recreation fees for the Blue Lakes area in the Mount Sneffels Wilderness.  
 
With its close proximity to the Town of Ridgway, the Blue Lakes area is utilized and revered by 
many members of our community. Our community members understand the need to address the 
significant impacts of increased visitation to one of the most iconic destinations in southwest 
Colorado and that the fee proposal is a necessary next step in a larger effort to address 
overcrowding and the resulting impacts in the Blue Lakes area.  
 
We believe GMUG recognizes the importance of balancing the need for resource protection with 
the desire to keep Blue Lakes accessible and affordable for the community, both local and out-
of-town visitors. We further believe that the proposed day‑use and overnight permit fees would 
do just that by directly supporting trail and site maintenance, restoration of damaged areas, 
sanitation needs, and a stronger field presence to help visitors recreate responsibly. At least 80% 
of revenue would remain on the GMUG to reinvest locally in Blue Lakes and surrounding 
wilderness areas. 
 
Thank you for taking our input into consideration and thank you for working to ensure that the 
Blue Lakes area can be enjoyed for years to come. 
 
Sincerely, 
 
 
 
John I. Clark 
Mayor

The Honorable Donald J. Trump 
The President of the United States 
The White House

Dear President Trump, 
 
As a member and leader representing one of the communities in southwestern Colorado 
impacted by the Gold Mountain Fire and subsequent post-fire flash flooding and debris flows, I 
write to you, on behalf of the Ridgway Town Council, to express strong support of the State of 
Colorado’s Major Disaster Declaration request submitted on August 23, 2026, and urge you to 
approve this much-needed federal financial aid. 
 
The Gold Mountain Fire started on June 27, 2026. To date, the fire has burned 39,691 acres and 
is still burning with 87% containment. The fire started northeast of Ouray and severely impacted 
our communities. The fire damaged our infrastructure and the post-fire flooding has severely 
impacted our public infrastructure, mountain transportation networks, and local economy. 
 
Most tragically, this incident claimed the life of firefighting helicopter pilot Nicholas Dale on 
July 12 while he was supporting fire suppression operations. We owe an immense debt of 
gratitude to those who responded, but the ongoing hazards from this disaster continue to 
endanger our residents and emergency personnel. 
 
Repeated post-fire debris flows out of the Cutler Creek and Dexter Creek drainages have buried 
county roads and clogged major culverts. Crucially, debris flows and mudslides off the burn scar 
have repeatedly severed U.S. Highway 550 between Ouray, Ridgway, and Silverton, the sole 
transportation artery through this mountain corridor. Each closure isolates our residents from 
emergency medical care, employment, and critical services, while shutting down our tourism-
dependent economy at the peak of the summer season. On July 29 alone, seventeen people 
required emergency rescue from Cutler Creek debris flows, and several properties in Ponderosa 
Village were rendered uninhabitable. 
 
In addition to public infrastructure damage, private properties below the burn scar face 
uncompensated losses. Standard homeowners insurance policies in Colorado do not cover flood 
or mudflow damage, meaning properties damaged or rendered uninhabitable by post-fire debris 
flows, such as those in Ponderosa Village, and on Dexter Creek, have no private policy in force 
to pay for damages. 
 
Because the hydrophobic burn scar will continue to shed massive amounts of rock, mud, and 
timber during monsoonal rains for the next three to five years, our small rural local governments 
face recurring, crippling costs for road clearance and infrastructure stabilization that far exceed 
local budgets. 
 
President Trump, our mountain communities cannot carry the burden of this multi-year disaster 
recovery alone. We respectfully ask for your swift approval of the Major Disaster Declaration for 
Ouray County to unlock FEMA Public Assistance (Categories A-G) and Hazard Mitigation 
Grant Program funding to repair our transportation corridors, clear ongoing debris hazards, and 
protect our communities from future flooding. 
 
Thank you for your consideration.  
 
Sincerely, 
 
 
 
John I. Clark 
Mayor 
Town of Ridgway

OFFICIAL PROCLAMATION 
 
A Proclamation Declaring September 2026 as  
Suicide Prevention Awareness Month 
 
WHEREAS, September is known throughout the United States as “National Suicide Prevention Awareness 
Month” and is intended to help promote awareness surrounding each of the suicide prevention resources 
available to us and our community; and 
 
WHEREAS, in 2024, our region experienced 41 community member deaths by suicide, predominantly 
middle to late aged white males (Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE)); and 
 
WHEREAS, each year, approximately 13 million adults seriously think about suicide, 3.8 million make a 
plan for suicide, and 1.5 million adults attempt suicide (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)); 
and 
 
WHEREAS, in 2024, 1,306 Coloradans died by suicide with the majority being in the age range of 35-44 
(CDPHE); and 
 
WHEREAS, our local Suicide Prevention, Awareness, and Recovery Coalition (SPARC) and 
organizations such as Tri-County Health Network are dedicated to saving lives and bringing hope to those 
affected by suicide through education, advocacy, and resources for those wh o have experienced suicidal 
thoughts or lost someone to suicide, and together urge that we: 
 
1. Recognize suicide as a preventable national and state public health problem, that there is no single 
cause of suicide, that anyone can experience suicidal thoughts, and that recovery from suicidal 
ideation is possible. 
 
2. Acknowledge that no single suicide prevention program or effort will be appropriate for all 
populations or communities and address the disparity in access to mental healthcare for underserved 
and underrepresented groups, and advocate for ending these disparities. 
 
3. Develop and implement strategies to improve and increase access to quality mental health, 
substance abuse, and suicide prevention services and programs as well as support Postvention Plans 
for a community response to a death by suicide. 
 
4. Support the efforts of SPARC in fostering connection and spreading the message that We Are The 
Ones Who Talk About Suicide.  
 
THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that we, the Ridgway Town Council, do hereby recognize the month 
of September 2026 as “Suicide Prevention Awareness Month” in the Town of Ridgway.  
 
Dated this 9th day of September 2026

Agenda Topic: Notice of Award for Pre-Approved Accessory Dwelling Unit Architectural Design 
Plans 
________________________________________________________________________________ 
 
BACKGROUND: 
The 2025 Town of Ridgway & Ouray County Housing Needs Assessment quantifies upcoming housing needs 
within the Town and also provides for a number of recommendations for improving the housing supply and 
increasing housing affordability and attainability for the Town. One of those recommendations is to expedite 
the proce ss of building ADUs through the adoption of pre -approved plans for ADUs. This can assist with 
reducing review times on Town staff and allow units to be developed more easily and more efficiently. 
Property owners that want to build an ADU and who are willing to use pre-approved building plans would 
see immediate cost savings as the plan check portion of the Town’s building permit fee would be waived. 
 
The Town has been certified as an ADU Supportive Jurisdiction and has been awarded an Accessory Dwelling 
Unit Grant (ADUG) from the Colorado Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) to assist with the “Ridgway Permit-
Ready ADU Program.” This funding is to be used to hire a qualified  architectural/engineering consultant to 
develop pre-approved ADU architectural plans suitable for parcels in Town that adhere to the Town’s Zone 
District Dimensional Standards found in Section 7-4-4 of the Ridgway Municipal Code. 
 
SUMMARY: 
In July, the Town issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) seeking proposals from qualified design consultants 
experienced and interested in providing pre -approved ADU architectural plans suitable for parcels in Town 
that adhere to the Town’s Municipal Code standards in Chapter  6 Building Regulations and Chapter 7 Land 
Use Regulations. By January 15, 2027, the selected consultant is expected to submit four (4) ADU architectural 
plans that must be free and clear for the Town to replicate and promote as a means of Town-supported infill 
development within the Town.  
 
Proposals were due by 4:00 p.m. on August 24th. The Town received the following five proposals: 
• 2757 Design Co    $29,000 
• Caddis Collaborative   $28,106 
• Keo studioworks   $74,500 
• Shape Architecture Studio  $54,500 
• StudioMW Design + Planning   $142,950 
 
The Selection Committee reviewed the proposals carefully against the review criteria listed in the RF P and 
based on the  quality of the proposals , reference checking, and the selection criteria outlined in the RFP, 
specifically cost and experience with the requested scope of work, the Selection Committee  is 
recommending that the Town enter into a Professional Services Agreement with 2757 Design Co for the 
project. This company is based out of Carbondale, CO, and was able to showcase some impressive examples 
of previous work relating in many ways to the goals and scales of the pre-approved ADU design plans. Some 
on the same scale, and some larger.  
 
 
 
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS: 
The Town’s Procurement Manual states that contracts for the purchase of supplies, goods, equipment and 
services in excess of $25,000 shall be approved by Council. Additionally, and as stated in the Procurement 
Manual, “Awards shall generally be made to the bidder whose bid best meets the requirements and criteria 
set forth in the RFB.” An award should be made  to the best qualified, responsible and responsive bidder 
whose evaluation by the Town indicates that the award will be in the best interests of the overall project.  
 
This project was budgeted for in the 2026 Annual Budget with an estimated cost of $75,000. Please note that 
the expenditure will be offset by the ADUG from DOLA. The grant will cover 75% of the project cost. Based 
on the amount that 2757 Design Co has proposed for this project, the ADUG will cover $21,750 of the project 
cost and the Town will only need to cover $7,250 (25%) of the project cost.  
 
PROPOSED MOTION: 
“I move to authorize staff to enter into a Professional Services Agreement with 2757 Design Co to develop 
pre-approved ADU architectural plans.”

Agenda Topic: Consideration of proposed modifications to Marshal’s Office Overtime 
Policy  
________________________________________________________________________________ 
 
ACTION BEFORE COUNCIL: 
Council is asked to consider adopting the revised Marshal’s Office Overtime Policy (Policy) 
attached to this staff report.  
 
BACKGROUND: 
The current version of the Policy, which was adopted in 2023, lays out that the non-exempt 
deputies are subject to overtime after 86 hours in a two-week pay period. Hours 81 to 86 are paid 
at the regular time rate. Hour 87 and above are paid at time and one-half of the deputies’ 
standard hourly rate (i.e., overtime rate). All overtime is subject to review unless there was prior 
approval, or in instances of emergent circumstances. Deputies are required to fill out timesheets 
at the end of each pay period that are reviewed and approved by the Marshal, or his/her designee, 
before submitting to payroll. To keep overtime expenditures at minimum, deputies have been 
offered flex time in lieu of paid overtime. The Marshal, in a department head/administrator role, is 
exempt from overtime. 
 
SUMMARY: 
As the years have passed and with the current staffing levels and creative scheduling, we very 
seldom dip into the overtime budget. However, there are times when we cannot staff shifts 
without offering overtime. Non-exempt deputies have not been interested in taking on a shift 
where they would only receive four hours of compensation at the time and a half rate and six 
hours at their regular hourly rate. One of the concerns voiced from deputies is that taking extra 
shifts takes them away from their off time without financial benefit to them. This leaves us with no 
other option than to do shift trades or the only exempt member of the Marshal’s staff, the Town 
Marshal, having to take on extra shifts or cover shifts out of their normal workdays or on nights, 
weekends or holidays. The shift trades only work if we have staffing to allow a day off for the day 
worked.  
 
According to federal law, for a 14-day work period, federal overtime generally does not become 
mandatory until 86 hours. So, 86 hours is a federal-law threshold, not necessarily the maximum or 
minimum overtime policy a municipality may choose. A municipality can agree to pay overtime at 
a lower threshold, such as 80 hours in a 14-day period. Montrose, Boulder, Gunnison and Rifle are 
just a few examples of municipalities that currently pay their non-exempt law enforcement 
personnel overtime after 80 hours in a 14-day work period 
 
 
 
 
 
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS: 
Pinpointing an amount for the Town to anticipate for overtime going forward, if the revised policy 
is approved, is difficult. Staff believes continuing to earmark $10,000 for overtime for each fiscal 
year going forward would be good and comparable to the communities that staff has evaluated. If 
approved, staff would continue aiming to keep overtime to a minimum.     
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION: 
Staff recommends that Council agree to pay overtime to personnel in the Marshal’s Office at a lower 
threshold, specifically 80 hours in a 14-day period.  
 
RECOMMENDED MOTION: 
“I move to adopt the revised Marshal’s Office Overtime Policy.” 
 
ATTACHMENT: 
Revised Marshal’s Office Overtime Policy 
 
 
 
 
  
Chapter 28: Marshal’s Office Overtime Policy 
 
Purpose: 
 
The purpose of this policy is to provide a structure for monitoring, managing, and controlling the use of 
personnel Overtime. 
 
Policy: 
 
All personnel of this agency must be mindful of and exercise fiscal responsibility in the use of public 
funds and resources. Overtime pay requires particular attention because it constitutes a sizeable 
expenditure of agency revenue that is provided at premium rates. Without adequate controls, 
unplanned expenditures can create budget overruns and divert resources from key operational areas. 
Therefore, it is the policy of this agency to effectively manage the use of overtime and that of each 
employee to use overtime in a responsible manner and judicious manner. 
 
Definitions: 
 
Overtime: Work performed more than 86 80 hours in a 14-day pay period or as otherwise 
established by state law. 
 
Fair Labor Standards Act (FSLA) (29) U.S.C. 207 (a): Federal law regulating wages and work hours 
to include provisions for overtime pay. 
 
Procedures: 
 
A. General Provisions 
 
1. This agency conforms to the overtime provisions of FSLA and applicable state laws. 
Personnel shall refer to this agency’s personnel pay policy, FSLA policy and labor 

 
agreements for details on exempt and non-exempt positions circumstances in which 
overtime pay may be granted, rates of payments for all overtime that qualifies for 
payment at the premium rate and related matters. 
 
2. Whenever reasonably possible, paid overtime will be used in lieu of unpaid 
compensatory time off. 
 
B. Reporting, Recording and Analysis 
 
1. All overtime shall be approved for payment by the designated supervisor. The category 
of overtime work performed shall be completed and forwarded to the Marshal for 
review and approval. 
 
a. Paid overtime and unpaid compensatory time will be recorded separately. 
 
b. Overtime expenditures shall be broken down separately based on categories 
based on type such as (call out, type of call, agency assist, or other functions). 
Individual categories will be recorded and checked monthly. 
 
c. Overtime funds expended under federal or state grant programs, or special 
events will be separated from the general budget. 
 
2. The designated entity shall maintain overtime records and provide individual and 
summary data of overtime worked monthly to the Marshal.  
 
3. Unusual or questionable overtime will be evaluated, and corrective actions will be taken. 
 
a. Expenditure of overtime at a rate that could exceed or negatively affect the 
agency’s budget will be evaluated and adjusted. Unless in the case of a critical 
incident or unforeseen circumstances.  
 
C. Overtime Management 
 
1. No task or function shall be performed on overtime by agency personnel that could 
otherwise be performed during regular work hours.  
 
2. Supervisors shall establish and hold personnel responsible for level of performance 
during standard work hours that minimizes the need for overtime and/or need for 
additional personnel.  
 
 
3. Only overtime required to meet vital service demands of the department shall be 
authorized.  
 
4. All overtime must receive advance authorization unless unreasonable due to emergency 
circumstances.  
 
a. The Marshal or, in the absence of the Marshal, the Sergeant, will be 
responsible for authorizing and managing overtime.  
b. The Marshal must approve overtime requests designed to fill on-going 
personnel vacancies or meet an unusually high yet unforeseen workload.  
 
c. The Marshal shall take measures and issue directives where reasonably 
possible to reduce or limit the demand for overtime.  
 
d. Non-emergency service requests received near end shift can be moved to next 
shift or in the case of on call can be held and passed to next shift.  
 
e. The Marshal can authorize the use of part-time officers in the event there are 
uncovered shifts, or situations that would otherwise require overtime.  
 
f. In the event where overtime is required due to (arrest, scene security, agency 
assists, or other large-scale event) staffing should be held to a safe minimum 
to perform tasks required.  
 
g. Ensure that agency overtime policy, rules and regulations and the particulars 
of any labor agreement are consistently adhered to by agency personnel as 
they relate to overtime for court appearances, standby, travel time, training, 
holiday leave, vacations, and related matters.  
 
h. Unpaid compensatory time is another option available upon agreement of 
Marshal and affected employee/employees. Unpaid compensatory rates will 
be the same as paid overtime rates per employee agreements.

AGENDA ITEM #14 
 
 
 
To:    Honorable Mayor Clark and Ridgway Town Council 
From:   Joanne Fagan, Town Engineer 
Date:   September 4, 2026 
Agenda Topic: Discussion and direction regarding Wastewater Treatment Plant siting options 
________________________________________________________________________________ 
 
SUMMARY: 
Based on some of the suggestions from the pre -public hearing  for the Town of Ridgway Wastewater 
Treatment Plant Renovation Project Needs Assessment  that was held during the May 13, 2026 Council 
meeting, staff reached out to representatives of Alta Land LLC (Alta Land)  about potential sites that would 
move the treatment works farther from Town.   
 
Several sites were evaluated after conversations about the Alta Land parcel and they are shown  on the 
graphic in Exhibit A . This report will refer to them as: purple site, south yellow site, and north yellow site.   
Also attached in Exhibit B is a table that compares the sites for several of the key concerns.  Note that the 
areas and elevations included in the report and exhibit B are based on data from Google Earth. 
 
Purple Site 
It is the site closest to the existing Wastewater Treatment Plant (Plant) site.  It is also the smallest as sketched.  
There is a smaller, somewhat flat area within the site and some of the site appears to slope more than 10%.  
Access from Railroad St. to the site thru Alta Land property look to have slopes as steep as 15%.  There are a 
couple of houses within about 600 ft of the middle of the site.  Because the site is small, there would likely 
be little room for future expansions or modifications.   
 
South Yellow Site 
The southern yellow site is just east of the school ballfield. It is the largest of the proposed sites and has the 
least slope. According to Google Earth, the point in that site farthest from the ballfield is only 500 ft from the 
east edge of the field.  A ballfield is not technically “habitable” space, but there could be concerns with part 
of site only being a couple hundred feet from the kids playing ball . That site might be accessible from the 
gravel pit and, according to Google Earth, it’s 35 ft higher than the existing Plant and is relatively flat. Though 
it’s probably not a big enough concern to sway the selection process, when walking that site, there were 
several other folks walking/biking on the informal trails within the parcel. Thus, some of the public might be 
concerned or dismayed by use of that site.    
 
North Yellow Site 
The north site topographically would likely make the most sense in that it needs the least lift. It ’s actually 
lower than the existing Plant, but in walking around the area, it seems like it would require pumping uphill to 
then go downhill. The shape of the site is a bit awkward as it ’s long and thin. There is a house only 300-400 
feet from the site and the site is only about 2.25 acres, so there’s not a lot of room to move things around on 
the site to create more setback or for plant modifications. It’s about the area of existing cells 2 and 3  at the 
Plant for comparison.  It would also likely be a challenge to find a route for either the sewer line or access for 
the large equipment it would take to build the new facilities.    
 
 
 
 
 
 
Pumping Requirements  
All the sites proposed would require a lift station, but it’s looking like we may need a lift station at the existing 
site as well. What would vary would be pump horsepower and thus on-going power consumption needed to 
get to the sites. The smallest lift would likely be at the existing site. 
 
Existing Site vs New Site 
The major advantage of a new site is that it is easier to build at an undeveloped site while continuing to treat 
at the existing site. However, we should have enough capacity in the 3 rd pond to be able operate with just 
that cell while we build new facilities where the first two cells are now.     
 
Moving the Plant to a new site would free up some space for Public Works at the existing site.  However, the 
sites to the south of the existing Plant, are more space constrained for future needs than the existing Plant 
site.   
 
Given the challenges with extending utilities to any of the new sites, i t’s likely to be expensive to do so. The 
same goes with extending access for construction equipment in the short term and operational access in the 
longer term. 
 
The existing site already has access and utilities. Each of the other sites would require extension of services 
from another location.  Even though i t’s i n Town now,  the Plant does setback at least 500 ft from most 
habitable structures.  With a mechanical plant the setback goes to 1000 ft for some of the unit processes. 
None of the sites really meet that setback so all would require some mitigation if we do a mechanical plant.   
 
Land Acquisition Costs 
Based on discussions with representatives of Alta Land, they made no commitments but were amenable to 
continuing conversations about the Town siting a new Plant on their property and  they brought up the 
possibility of trading some land in exchange for a portion of their property. It’s worth noting that the Town 
and Glenn Pauls’ parents did a land swap as part of the River Park development, by which the Town acquired 
some of the land that adjoins the Business Park in exchange for land in the old Railroad Street right-of-way. 
Needing to acquire one of the areas discussed above by land swap would reduce the lands available for Public 
Works at the existing site.  Purchasing the property out right would be a significant expense. Also, to use a 
portion of the Alta Land property would require a re -subdivision application and potentially other 
applications in accordance with the Town’s Land Use Regulations.  
  
Conclusions 
Staff appreciates the time Alta Land has taken to present some options for moving the Plant.  However, staff 
feels that based on the above assessment, the initial and operational costs and other challenges to move the 
facilities downstream outweigh the benefits of the move.   
 
Direction Requested 
Staff requests direction from Council on how to proceed with siting the Plant upgrades prior to the public 
hearing for the Town of Ridgway Wastewater Treatment Plant Renovation Project Needs Assessment that is 
slated for either the regular Council meeting in October or November and that will focus on treatment.

To:    Honorable Mayor Clark and Ridgway Town Council 
From:   Preston Neill, Town Manager 
Date:   September 1, 2026 
Agenda Topic: Confirmation or amendment of rescission of Stage 2 Fire Restrictions and 
reinstatement of Stage 1 Fire Restrictions 
________________________________________________________________________________ 
 
SUMMARY: 
On September 3, 2026, the Town of Ridgway followed the lead of Ouray County and other agencies 
in our region in moving from Stage 2 to Stage 1 Fire Restrictions. That change is now subject to 
“confirmation or amendment by the Town Council”, as described in Section 12-2-1(C) of the Ridgway 
Municipal Code.   
 
Ouray County representatives participate in weekly fire restriction coordination meetings with our 
dispatch zone. These meetings include representatives from the U.S. Forest Service, Bureau of Land 
Management, National Weather Service, Interagency Dispatch, National Park Service, and  the 
counties within our zone. 
 
During these calls, participants review  a wide range of factors, including recent and forecasted 
weather, precipitation, fuel moisture levels, fire danger indices, human -caused fire starts, fire 
behavior and spread on current incidents, availability of firefighting resources, and other conditions 
affecting wildfire risk throughout the region. Fire restrictions are not based on any single factor . 
They are based on the overall conditions and the best available fire-weather and fuels information. 
 
Recent precipitation and improved fuel moisture conditions have reduced the immediate wildfire 
risk within Ouray County. Based on current and forecasted weather conditions, fuel moisture levels, 
fire danger indices, and consultation with our local, state, and federal fire management partners, 
conditions no longer warrant Stage 2 Fire Restrictions. 
 
However, this does not mean that the wildfire risk is gone. Fuels can quickly dry with warmer 
temperatures, lower humidity, and wind. Maintaining Stage 1 Fire Restrictions remains appropriate 
to reduce the potential for human -caused wildfire while ensuring  that the level of restrictions 
reflects current conditions. 
 
PROPOSED MOTION: 
“I move to confirm the rescission of Stage 2 Fire Restrictions and the reinstatement of Stage 1 Fire 
Restrictions.”
