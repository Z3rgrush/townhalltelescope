10.  Review of draft 2026 Housing Action Plan 
 
The Council met with members of the Planning Commission to review the draft Housing 
Action Plan, and provide feedback on the items being proposed in the plan.  
 
Representatives from Points Consulting LLC, Mike Manning and Brian Points presented the 
8-3-26 draft Housing Action Plan required under Colorado Senate Bill 24-174.  They noted 
the project is expected to be completed by mid October.  They displayed slides and reviewed 
recommended goals and policies  organized into five implementation areas which were  
policies and procedures; provide for future housing; workforce housing enhancement; 
momentum building; life cycle housing provisions.  It was noted  these areas address 
development review and regulatory processes, land and infrastructure planning, workforce 
and affordable housing programs, funding and implementation capacity, and housing needs 
across different stages of life.   
 
Mr. Manning presented the action plan for each of the categories and the implementation 
approaches to meet the required actions and timelines  established by the new state 
regulations.  These included enhancing the speed of public review and hearing processes; 
simplifying the townhome and condominium subdivision process; supporting affordable and 
workforce housing; exploring opportunities for accessory dwelling units and modular units; 
modernized the development review process; pre -approve small in -fill housing units ; 
implement a land banking program; public education and promotion of deed restrictions and 
covenants; expansion of community land trust; explore opportunities for employer sponsored 
programs; indicate highest priority building locations for workforce and affordable housing; 
align housing growth with long-term water supply and infrastructure capacity.  
 
There were comments from the Planning Commission and Council regarding items which 
should be researched for addition into the plan.

11.  Presentation of concept for a free box in Town 
 
Ariel Hessler, a junior at Ridgway Secondary School,  presented slides which outlined her 
memorandum pertaining to creation of a Free Box.  She stated the box would be a publicly 
accessible, weatherproof structure where members of the community could donate gently 
used clothing and outdoor gear, and residents could take what is needed free of charge.  She 
noted the goal is to create “a simple, community supported system based on generosity, 
sustainability and mutual aid”.  Estimates to construction the box is $1920 and she asked the 
Council for funding of $1000, noting she is also seeking support from community and local 
foundations. She has explored potential locations allowing access every day at all hours, and 
is hoping to place it on the north side of the public library.  The intention is installation by 
spring of 2027 and maintenance to be provided by volunteers from high school students who 
are affiliated with the National Honor Society, or seeking to fulfill volunteer hours.  
Maintenance would be weekly checks of the box to sort donations, remove unusable and 
inappropriate items, and ensure the area is clean and well-organized.  
 
There were questions and comments from the Council, and discussion with Ms. Hessler.   
 
Speaking from the audience Sue Husch noted the local thrift store has a problem with 
disposing of unusable items, which requires dumpsters to be emptied every week. She 
questioned who will carry the program forward after Ms. Hessler graduates from school.  
 
The Council agreed to see how the program works with the caveat the box may not be placed 
on Town property, and will not become a program which Town staff has to manage . It was 
agreed to donate $750 to the project. 
 
ACTION: 
 
Moved by Councilor Kroger, seconded by Councilor Scoville to contribute $750 to the Ridgway 
Free Box with the condition the National Honor Society must make a commitment to oversee the 
maintenance.  The motion passed unanimously.

14.  Organization of Alpenglow Vista Metropolitan District Numbers 1 through 4 and approval of 
a Consolidated Service Plan 
 
Memorandum from Town Attorney  Bo Nerlin regarding a Service Plan for Alpenglow Vista 
Metropolitan District No 1 through 4 at  N. Laura Street, west side of McCall Street  and  
Roundhouse Street, and west of N. Cora Street , which is zoned  Light Industrial and Mixed 
Residential and owned by Ridgway Triangle LLC. 
 
  The Town Attorney explained staff has been working with Ridgway Triangle LLC and its 
principal, Charles Mueller, also the applicant, since May of this year with respect to the 
possibility of creating a series of metropolitan districts within the Town .  A metropolitan or 
metro district, is a special district, quasi-municipal corporation and political subdivision of the 
State, and subject to the Colorado Special District Act. The applicant submitted a 
Consolidated Service Plan to the Town on July 21 st and additional exhibits were presented 
on August 8th.  The plan proposes the creation of four separate metropolitan districts to serve 
approximately 19.06 acre s of residential and commercial pro perty located off of  N. Laura 
Street, west side of McCall and Roundhouse Streets, west of N. Cora Street.  It anticipates 
approximately 150 residential units, 50,000 square feet of commercial and light -industrial 
development, and phased buildout over approximately 20 to 25 years.  The proposal is to 
establish four metro districts to finance and coordinate construction of public improvements 
to service the development and operate and maintain the improvements.  After completion 
Town Council Minutes 
August 12, 2026 
Page 5 
 
of the capital improvements, it is possible they would be dedicated to the Town. The applicant 
is seeking Council approval of the Service Plan  before proceeding with a development 
application. 
 
Attorney Nerlin presented the staff analysis of the Service Plan and recommended conditions 
of approval, along with Resolution No. 26 -06 approving the Consolidated Service Plan for 
Alpenglow Vista Metropolitan District Numbers 1, 2, 3 and 4.  
 
The applicants attorney Alan Haug with Icenogle, Seaver, Pogue presented slides of the 
property location, and explained approval of the Service Plan will allow the applicant to seek 
voter approval; financing; and begin the planning process with the Town.  He noted formation 
of the special district requires voter approval and will only affect property owners within the 
boundaries of the district. Approval will establish mill levies with indebtedness over the next 
40 years.  A district is given tax exemptions, which yields lower costs in the end to persons 
purchasing within the district. 
 
There were questions from the Council to Mr. Haug. 
 
Speaking from the audience Richard Penney questioned higher property taxes if there is a

providing economical land sufficient service to the area within their proposed boundaries and the 
area to be included within the proposed Districts has, or will have, the financial ability to discharge 
the proposed indebtedness on a reasonable basis.  Councilor Scoville seconded, and the motion

15.  Proposed County Sales Tax to support disaster mitigation and response  
 
Ouray County Manager, Antonio Mendez  explained the County Commissioners are 
discussing presenting the voters in November with a ballot question to increase county sales 
tax by .5 percent to fund emergency preparedness.  The funds would help to build the fund 
after the recent unexpected costs due to the Gold Mountain Fire, and floodings from 
monsoonal rains.  He noted the Town and City of Ouray would need to agree to a split in the 
tax, which is currently anticipated to be 1 million  annually, and would be split 80% county, 
10% city and 10% town.  He asked for support from the Town.  
 
There were questions and comments from the Council.  There were questions to the Town 
Manager, and comments by the Manager.  There was conversation between the Council and 
Mr. Mendez.

12.  Proposed modifications to Marshal’s Office Overtime Policy  
 
In a Staff Report from the Town Manager dated 9-3-26, it stated the 2023 policy lays out 
that the non-exempt deputies are subject to overtime after 86 hours in a two-week pay 
period. Hours 81 to 86 are paid at the regular time rate. Hour 87 and above are paid at time 
and one-half of the deputies’ standard hourly rate (i.e., overtime rate). All overtime is 
subject to review unless there was prior approval, or in instances of emergent 
circumstances. Deputies are required to fill out timesheets at the end of each pay period 
that are reviewed and approved by the Marshal, or his/her designee, before submitting to 
payroll. To keep overtime expenditures at minimum, deputies have been offered flex time in 
lieu of paid overtime. The Marshal, in a department head/administrator role, is 
exempt from the overtime policy. 
 
As the years have passed and with the current staffing levels and creative scheduling, we 
very seldom dip into the overtime budget. However, there are times when we cannot staff 
shifts without offering overtime. Non-exempt deputies have not been interested in taking on 
a shift where they would only receive four hours of compensation at the time and a half rate 
and six hours at their regular hourly rate. One of the concerns voiced from deputies is that 
taking extra shifts takes them away from their off time without financial benefit to them. This 
leaves us with no other option than to do shift trades or the only exempt member of the 
Marshal’s staff, the Town Marshal, having to take on extra shifts or cover shifts out of their 
normal workdays or on nights, weekends or holidays. The shift trades only work if we have 
staffing to allow a day off for the day worked. 
 
According to federal law, for a 14-day work period, federal overtime generally does not 
become mandatory until 86 hours. So, 86 hours is a federal-law threshold, not necessarily 
the maximum or minimum overtime policy a municipality may choose. A municipality can 
agree to pay overtime at a lower threshold, such as 80 hours in a 14-day period. Montrose, 
Boulder, Gunnison and Rifle are just a few examples of municipalities that currently pay 
their non-exempt law enforcement personnel overtime after 80 hours in a 14-day work 
period. 
 
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS: 
Pinpointing an amount for the Town to anticipate for overtime going forward, if the revised 
policy is approved, is difficult. Staff believes continuing to earmark $10,000 for overtime for 
each fiscal year going forward would be good and comparable to the communities that staff 
has evaluated. If approved, staff would continue aiming to keep overtime to a minimum. 
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION: 
Staff recommends that Council agree to pay overtime to personnel in the Marshal’s Office 
at a lower threshold, specifically 80 hours in a 14-day period.

 MEMORANDUM  
 
To: Mayor Clark – Council Members, Town of Ridgway 
From:  Bo James Nerlin, Esq.  
Re:  Helios Charginig  
Date:  10/8/2026  
CC: Mr. Preston Neill  
 
 
At the May 13, 2026, Regular Town Council Meeting, the Town Council approved a Revocable Electric 
Vehicle Charging Station License Agreement between the Town and Helios Charging, Inc. (“Helios”).  The 
licensed premises are located at the Ridgway Visitor’s Center / Ouray County Fairgrounds.   Last month, Daniel 
Benhammou, Helios’ CEO, requested some changes to the approved License Agreement.  Town Staff has since 
conferred with Mr. Benhammou and has negotiated some additional changes to the original License Agreement.  
Staff believes these changes are minimal and, given the Town Council’s prior approval of the concept, Staff 
recommends adopting the revised License Agreement, included in the packet, as part of the consent agenda.   
 
OUTLINE OF CHANGES  
 
1. Revenue calculation (Section 2). The Town’s share remains 20% of net revenue. The October draft 
adds payment-processing and third-party transaction/session fees as allowable deductions from the 20% 
net revenue collection.   
 
2. Restroom (Section 5). Helios has reaffirmed its commitment to design, install, and fully fund a vault 
restroom regardless of grant availability. Upon completion and acceptance of the restroom by the Town, 
the Town will be responsible for maintaining the restroom.  
 
3. Maintenance and liability (Sections 4 and 6). The Town must use reasonable care during maintenance 
and snow removal and avoid unreasonable obstruction of charging access. Helios’s indemnification of 
the Town and waiver of liability exclude losses to the extent caused by Town negligence or willful 
misconduct. 
 
4. Insurance (Section 8).  Written notice and a 30-day cure period replace the prior language, which 
allowed for immediate revocation of the license by the Town for a missing insurance certificate.   
 
5. Renewal (Section 9). The revised license recognizes Helios’ desire to maintain the license for five years 
to obtain and maintain grant funding; however, the license is not a multi-year obligation, and the Town 
retains the right to revoke the license and assumes no grant repayment obligation. 
 
6. Compliance (Section 17). The revised license expressly requires compliance with Town building and 
outdoor lighting regulations.  
 
RECOMMENDED ACTION 
 
  The revisions are limited in scope and retain the basic structure of the previously approved License 
Agreement. Staff and legal counsel recommend that Council approve the revised License Agreement through 
the consent agenda.

1. Project Overview
Helios Charging respectfully subm
its this proposal for the installation of four DC fast charging 
stations (DCFC) at the Ridgway Visitor Center near the Ouray County Fairgrounds. The project 
is funded by the Colorado Energy Office (CEO) under the DCFC Plazas Grant Program, with 
Helios providing matching funds. 
The chargers will enable electric vehicles to recharge in 30-45 minutes, making Ridgway a 
natural stop for EV travelers along U.S. Highway 550 and Highway 62. Helios will design, 
construct, own, and operate the chargers at no cost to the Town of Ridgway, providing ongoing 
maintenance, utility payments, and site stewardship while generating revenue for the Town. 
2. Project Benefits
• Zero cost to the Town – All design, permitting, equipment, construction, and long-term
maintenance are covered by Helios and the grant.
• Economic development – Fast charging takes 30-45 minutes, drawing EV travelers
into Ridgway to patronize restaurants, shops, galleries, and local attractions while they
wait.
EXHIBIT A
• Tourism catalyst – Positions Ridgway as an EV-friendly gateway to the San Juans, 
attracting a growing demographic of environmentally conscious visitors. 
• Revenue opportunity – Helios will share a percentage of net charging revenue with the 
Town or contribute equivalent value in site improvements. 
• Environmental leadership – Supports Colorado’s clean transportation objectives and 
reinforces Ridgway’s commitment to sustainability. 
• Future-proofing – The installation will include oversized conduit and service 
infrastructure to accommodate additional chargers or Town fleet electrification in the 
future. 
3. Proposed Site Layout 
The proposed location is the parking area adjacent to the Ridgway Area Chamber of Commerce 
and Visitor Center, near the intersection of Highway 62 and Racecourse Road. This highly 
visible, high-traffic location maximizes exposure to both through-travelers and visitors. 
• BTC Power DCFC Chargers: Four fast charging stations positioned along the existing 
parking area for convenient pull-in access. 
• Transformer and Switch Gear: Located on the adjacent open area to the northeast, 
minimizing visual impact and keeping clear of parking and pedestrian circulation. 
• Power Cabinets: Sited between the chargers and transformer for efficient conduit 
routing. 
• Parking Accessibility: Dedicated accessible parking stalls with compliant surfacing and

OVERVIEW OF FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
This discussion and analysis is intended to serve as an introduction to the Town’s basic financial 
statements. The basic statements are comprised of three components: 1) government-wide 
financial statements, 2) fund financial statements, and 3) notes to the financial statements.
Government-wide Financial Statements
The government-wide financial statements are designed to provide readers with a broad overview 
of the Town’s finances in a manner similar to a private-sector business.
The statement of net position presents information on all the Town’s assets, liabilities and 
deferred inflows of resources, with the difference reported as net position. Over time, the 
increases or decreases in net position may serve as a useful indicator of whether the financial 
position of the Town is improving or deteriorating.
The statement of activities presents information showing how the Town’s net position changed 
during the most recent fiscal year. All changes in net position are reported as soon as the 
underlying event giving rise to the change occurs, regardless of the timing of related cash flows. 
Thus, revenues and expenses reported in this statement for some items will result in cash flows in 
future periods (e.g. uncollected taxes and earned but unused personal time).
The government-wide financial statements distinguish functions of the Town that are principally 
supported by taxes and intergovernmental revenues (Governmental Activities) from other 
functions that are intended to recover all or a significant portion of their costs through user fees 
and charges (Business-type Activities). 
4
The Governmental Activities of the Town include general government administration, police, 
public works, and community center. The Business-type Activities of the Town include the 
following utilities: water and sewer funds.
Fund Financial Statements
A fund is a grouping of related accounts that is used to maintain control over resources that have 
been segregated for specific activities or objectives. Fund accounting is used to ensure and 
demonstrate compliance with finance-related legal requirements. The Town’s funds can be 
divided into three categories: Governmental Funds, Proprietary Funds and Fiduciary Funds.
Governmental Funds – Governmental funds are used to account for essentially the same 
functions reported as governmental activities in the government-wide financial statements. 
However, unlike the government-wide financial statements, governmental fund financial 
statements focus on short-term inflows and outflows of spendable resources, as well as on 
balances of spendable resources available at the end of the fiscal year. Such information may be 
useful in evaluating a government’s short-term financing requirements. The accounting method is 
called modified accrual accounting.
Because the focus of governmental funds is narrower than that of the government-wide financial 
statements, it is useful to compare the information presented for governmental activities in the 
governmental funds with similar information presented for governmental activities in the 
government-wide financial statements. By doing so, readers may better understand the long-term 
impact of the government’s short-term financing decisions. Both the governmental fund balance 
sheet and the governmental fund statement of revenues, expenditures, and changes in fund 
balances provide a reconciliation to facilitate this comparison between governmental funds and 
governmental activities.
The Town maintains two major government funds, the General Fund and the Capital Projects 
Fund. Information is presented separately in the governmental fund balance sheet and in the 
governmental fund statement of revenues, expenditures, and changes in fund balances for the 
general fund.
Proprietary Funds – The Town maintains one type of proprietary fund. Enterprise Funds are 
used to report the same functions presented as business-type activities in the government-wide 
financial statements. The Town uses a separate enterprise fund to account for each of its utility 
funds: Water and Sewer Fund. 
Fiduciary Funds – The Town maintains one type of fiduciary fund, the Ridgway General 
Improvement District #1.
Notes to Financial Statements
The notes provide additional information that is essential to a full understanding of the data 
provided in the financial statements.

Net Position
As noted previously, net position may serve over time as a useful indicator of the Town’s 
financial position. For the year ended December 31, 2025, the Town’s combined assets exceeded 
liabilities by $ 21,589,219. Of this amount, $ 9,059,669 is unrestricted and available to meet the 
Town’s ongoing financial obligations.
By far the largest portion of net position is the investment in capital assets (net of related debt) of 
$ 12,379,243 (57% of net position). This amount reflects the investment in all capital assets (e.g. 
infrastructure, land, buildings, and equipment) less any related debt used to acquire those assets 
that are still outstanding. These capital assets are used to provide services to citizens; 
consequently, these assets are not available for future spending. Although the investment in 
capital assets is reported net of debt, it should be noted that the resources needed to repay this 
debt must be provided from other sources since capital assets themselves cannot be used to 
liquidate these liabilities.

FINANCIAL ANALYSIS OF GOVERNMENT’S FUNDS
Governmental funds 
The focus of the Town’s governmental funds is to provide information on near-term inflows, 
outflows, and balances of spendable resources. Such information is useful in assessing the 
Town’s financing requirements. In particular, unreserved fund balance may serve as a useful 
measure of the Town’s net resources available for spending at the end of the fiscal year.
As of the end of 2025, the Town’s governmental funds reported combined ending fund balances 
of $ 5,020,564, decrease of $ 124,089 in comparison with the prior year. Of the combined ending 
fund balances for all governmental funds 97% of this total amount, $ 4,870,257, constitutes 
unassigned fund balance, which is available for appropriation at the Town’s discretion. The 
remainder of the fund balance is reserved to indicate that it is not available for new spending 
because it is already committed to meet a State constitution mandated emergency reserve, 
$150,307 and for capital improvements of $0.
The Town has one major governmental fund, the General Fund, which is the primary operating 
fund for the Town. At the end of 2025, the unassigned fund balance of the General Fund was                       
$ 4,870,257, while the total fund balance was $ 5,020,564. As a measure of the General Fund’s 
liquidity, it may be useful to compare both unreserved fund balance and total fund balance to total 
fund expenditures. The fund balance in the Town’s General Fund decreased by $ 124,089 during
