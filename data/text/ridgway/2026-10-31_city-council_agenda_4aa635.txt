BUDGET ADOPTION PROCESS
The Charter of the Town of Ridgway
Article IX - FINANCE
9-4: Budget.
(A) The Town Council shall cause the preparation of a proposed annual budget by the first regular Council meeting in October of each year for 
the next fiscal year.
(B) The budget shall contain at a minimum the following information:
 (1) Detailed revenues, expenditures and balances for each fund and account for
(a) the prior fiscal year,
(b) the adopted budget for the current fiscal year,
(c) the estimated actual revenues and expenditures for the current year and
(d) the budget year.
(2) Prior to the adoption of the budget, a public hearing shall be held, notice of which shall be posted and published once in a 
newspaper of general circulation within the Town at least 15 days prior to the hearing.
 (3) Prior to adoption of the budget, the Council may amend the proposed budget as appropriate.
 (4) The budgeted expenditures shall be balanced by budgeted revenues, which may include fund balances and reserves.
 (5) The Council shall adopt the budget by the minimum vote of 4 members of Council by resolution prior to December 15. 
 Adoption of the budget shall constitute the appropriation of the budgeted expenditures by fund.

ECONOMIC & REVENUE FORECAST
Greg Sobetski, Chief Economist with the Colorado Legislative Council, presented an Economic 
and Revenue Forecast for Colorado in September 2026. Here are a few things to note:
- The General Fund is estimated to have ended FY 2025-26 with an 11.9% reserve, $140.7 
million below the statutory reserve requirement, primarily owing to departmental over-
expenditures relative to appropriations. General Fund revenue grew by 0.3% and totaled $17.23 
billion.
- The General Fund is expected to end FY 2026-27 with a 13.5% reserve, $124.5 million above 
the 13.0% statutory reserve requirement. General Fund revenue is expected to grow at a robust 
9.5 percent rate and total $18.86 billion, with strong growth in individual and corporate income 
taxes and moderate growth in sales taxes. 
- General Fund revenue for FY 2027-28 is expected to grow 2.6% and total $19.36 billion, with 
state revenue subject to TABOR exceeding the Referendum C cap by $578.7 million. 

ECONOMIC OUTLOOK
The U.S. and state economies are forecast to continue to expand over the next few years, while risks to the forecast remain 
elevated on both the upside and downside. Economic output for the nation is expected to increase at a moderate pace through 
the forecast period, supported by AI-related business investment, but employment growth remains stuck in a “low-hire, low-fire” 
trajectory. While corporate profits continue to post record highs, consumer spending faces mounting headwinds from inflationary 
pressures and weak employment growth, and household finances are deteriorating.
Inflation remains persistently above the Federal Reserve’s two percent target at the state and national levels, driven by volatile 
energy prices and fluctuating tariff policies. The Federal Reserve raised the federal funds rate by 25 basis points in September 
and is expected to implement another 25-basis point increase later in 2026 to cool price growth, a reversal from early 2026 
expectations for easing. Long-term interest rates have already risen in recent weeks amidst mounting concerns over federal 
government debt levels. 
Risks to the forecast remain elevated on both the upside and downside. AI-related investment headlines upside risks, while 
consumer and business resiliency could sustain stronger growth for longer. On the downside, higher interest rates due to 
persistent inflationary pressures and mounting federal government debt, financial market correction, weakening household 
finances, weak labor markets, and AI-related disruptions could derail the growth forecast. For Colorado, additional risks stem 
from ongoing drought and uncertain weather conditions into the fall and winter, along with a still-sluggish housing market 
statewide.

2027 PAY MOVEMENT RECOMMENDATIONS
Logic Compensation Group (LCG) is recommending that the Town’s salary structure be 
adjusted by 2.1% to ensure that the Town maintains its competitive position among the public 
and private sectors in which the Town competes for talent. Further, LGC recommends that the 
Town consider a merit adjustment that is reflective of the aggregate median of the market, 
which would be 3.5%.
From an implementation standpoint, the recommendation is for the Town to move eligible 
employees their one step in the updated salary step structure provided by LGC. The 
proposed increases are closely aligned with the market average pay movement of 5.6% and 
will help to ensure that the Town maintain its competitive position in the market.
As a percentage of total payroll, the proposed increase is $87,852, which is 5.3% above total 
payroll in 2026.

Program Operations & Capacity | $29,000 
Narrative: Home Trust of Ouray County (HTOC) has evolved into a countywide 
affordable housing organization with an expanding housing portfolio, development 
pipeline, and responsibilities related to housing stewardship, compliance, financing, 
partnerships, and community engagement. 
As the region’s Housing Services Administrator, supported in part through the Colorado 
Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) Local Planning Capacity Grant, HTOC serves as a 
countywide housing resource, connecting residents, employers, local governments, and 
community partners with housing information, resources, and opportunities.  HTOC’s 
multidisciplinary team, supported by specialized legal, accounting, and other contracted 
expertise as needed, provides expertise across affordable housing development, 
finance, property stewardship, grant administration, communications, fundraising, 
events, and community engagement. This capacity allows HTOC to connect housing 
needs with available resources, manage and preserve affordable housing, support new 
construction, pursue new opportunities, coordinate with public and private partners, and 
leverage local, state, and federal resources. 
As a nonprofit organization serving communities throughout Ouray County, HTOC relies 
on public, private, philanthropic, and earned resources to sustain this work. Town 
investment in HTOC’s program operations and capacity provides flexible support for the 
organizational infrastructure and ongoing resources necessary to effectively deliver 
these services. Funding may support technology, communications, supplies, 
professional services, training, community engagement, travel, staffing capacity, and 
other operational needs. This flexible support complements other funding sources, 
including the DOLA Local Planning Capacity Grant, and allows HTOC to direct 
resources where they are most needed as organizational and community needs evolve. 
This support helps ensure that affordable housing assets are responsibly stewarded, 
partnerships are strengthened, and housing opportunities continue to be advanced for 
Ouray residents and the local workforce. 
HTOC’s annual fundraising Home Tour is one example of this community engagement, 
providing an opportunity for residents to learn about local affordable housing while 
generating support for HTOC’s housing work. The 2026 Home Tour featured the 
Parkside Duplex in Ridgway. 
This funding supports HTOC’s broader organizational capacity and is distinct from the 
project-specific development work required to advance individual housing opportunities.

Project Development Capacity | $16,000 
 
Narrative: As HTOC’s housing portfolio and development pipeline continue to grow, the 
organization needs capacity to identify, evaluate, and advance promising affordable 
housing opportunities. Town support would provide flexible resources for project 
coordination and management, financial analysis, grant development, partnership 
coordination, community engagement, and other professional or technical assistance 
needed to move projects forward. 
The 660 Sherman Street property in Ridgway is one example of this work. Based on 
extensive community outreach and identified housing and community-service needs, 
HTOC is in the pre-development phase of exploring a three-story mixed-use 
development that could include approximately 16–19 affordable rental homes, childcare 
or community-serving offices such as health and human services, an HTOC office, and 
commercial space for local businesses. With support from a Colorado Housing and 
Finance Authority technical assistance grant, HTOC has completed conceptual plans, a 
countywide rental survey, and preliminary financial modeling. 
While Program Operations & Capacity supports HTOC’s broader organizational 
capacity, this funding supports the project-specific work needed to evaluate and 
advance individual housing development opportunities. Continued capacity will be 
needed to evaluate development and financing strategies, pursue additional funding, 
coordinate partners, and advance this and other opportunities. The Town’s investment 
would support project development work without representing a commitment to any 
specific future project or capital contribution. As projects become sufficiently developed, 
HTOC can bring project-specific funding requests or partnership opportunities to the 
Town for consideration. 
Impact of Town Support: 
The Town of Ridgway’s investment would strengthen HTOC’s capacity to respond to 
local housing needs, responsibly steward existing affordable housing, engage the 
community, and advance new housing opportunities. The flexibility of these funds within 
the identified program areas allows HTOC to respond to emerging needs and 
opportunities while leveraging other public, private, and philanthropic resources.

Note 1: Organization and Nature of Operations 
Home Trust of Ouray County ("HTOC" or the "Organization") is a Colorado nonprofit corporation recognized as tax-
exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code and operates as a community land trust. The 
Organization acquires land and residential properties and preserves their long-term affordability through ground leases 
and resale-restriction agreements with income-qualified homebuyers and tenants. HTOC operates in Ouray County, 
Colorado. 
 
Note 2: Summary of Significant Accounting Policies 
Basis of Accounting 
The financial statements have been prepared on the accrual basis of accounting in accordance with U.S. generally 
accepted accounting principles applicable to not-for-profit entities, primarily ASC 958, Not-for-Profit Entities.  
 
Net Asset Classification 
Net assets are classified as net assets without donor restrictions or net assets with donor restrictions based on the 
presence or absence of donor-imposed restrictions, in accordance with ASC 958-210. The accompanying financial 
statements present net assets on a combined basis pending further refinement of the underlying classification.  
 
Cash and Cash Equivalents 
The Organization considers highly liquid investments with original maturities of three months or less to be cash 
equivalents. Restricted cash consists of amounts subject to donor or grant restrictions and is unavailable for general 
operating purposes unless the applicable restrictions are satisfied or otherwise authorized.  
 
Property and Equipment 
Property and equipment are stated at cost. Expenditures greater than $5,000 per item with useful lives exceeding one 
year are capitalized; expenditures below that threshold are expensed as incurred. Depreciation is calculated using the 
straight-line method over the following estimated useful lives: 
 
• Residential buildings - 27.5 years 
• Commercial buildings - 39 years 
• Land improvements - 15 years 
• Computers and IT equipment - 5 years 
• Furniture and equipment - 7 years 
 
Land is not depreciated. Construction in progress is not depreciated until the related assets are placed in service. The 
Organization recorded a prior-period catch-up depreciation adjustment to beginning net assets for fiscal 2024 in 
accordance with ASC 250-10-45. Depreciation expense for 2025 includes partial-year depreciation for assets placed in 
service during the year. 
 
Revenue Recognition 
Contributions are recognized when received or unconditionally promised. Contributions subject to donor restrictions are 
reported as revenue with donor restrictions and are released from restriction when the applicable purpose or time 
restrictions are satisfied. Program service fees and other earned revenue are recognized when the related services are 
provided.
