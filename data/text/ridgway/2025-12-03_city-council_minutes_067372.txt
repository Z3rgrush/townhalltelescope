          RIDGWAY TOWN COUNCIL  
 
MINUTES OF BUDGET WORKSHOP MEETING 
 
DECEMBER 3, 2025 
 
 
 
CALL TO ORDER 
 
The meeting was held both in person and via virtual meeting portal  Zoom Meeting, pursuant to 
the Town’s Electronic Participation Policy.  
 
The Mayor called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.    The Council was present in its entirety with 
Councilors Grambley, Kroger, Montague, Schuyler, Scoville, Mayor Pro Tem Lakin and Mayor 
Clark in attendance.  
 
1.     Citizen proposal for budget consideration 
 
Tia Mihelarakis presented a PowerPoint presentation entitled “Keep Ridgway Creative”  
stating “to forget art is to forget who we are”.    She expressed the importance of art as it 
relates to culture and society; “creativity is humanities most important trait” and “people are 
our most valuable resource”.  She explained the proposal includes explaining the problem, 
providing a solution, prese nting examples, notes on urgency and a plan of action. She 
presented slides depicting “the problems Ridgway creatives ar e facing” and comments 
regarding them from residents.  
  
She noted that “many locals are facing challenges due to high housing costs; reduction in 
tourism; lack of studio space; “corporate overshadowing” and “creative theft”; threat of 
artificial intelligence “devaluing human artistic skills”.  The solution “basic income for 
creatives” involving a “regular cash payment to members of the community, without a work 
requirements or other conditions” which can create “immediate positive economic impacts  
by circulating money back into our local economy” .  She stated this is an urgent problem, 
and noted a number of Values within the Town Master Plan would b e met by “allowing 
creatives to survive here for another year without relying on an immediate tourism increase”.   
Future plans could include a Creative Arts Center “functioning as a nonprofit to provide 
equipment, space, supplies and training for creatives”.  Noting “when artists thrive, 
communities thrive” and “creatives are the community builders” she suggested a program 
in which 100 residents would receive $2000 a month for 12 months. 
 
There were comments by the Council and discussion with Ms. Mihelarakis. 
 
2.      Overview of budget adoption process 
 
The Town Manager presented an overview of the budget adoption process; financial 
overview of the draft budget; operational reserves; personnel and pay movements; revenue 
projections and trends in 2026 including a proposed decrease in sales tax receipts.   
 
3.      Review of draft 2026 Fiscal Year Budget and Five- and Ten-Year Capital Improvement 
Plans  
 
The Town Clerk/Treasurer reviewed the draft budget document with the Council; presented 
line item location of expenditures; presented and reviewed the five and ten year capital 
improvement plans.

The Council requested a change to the general fund budget.  
 
Tia Mihelarakis expressed concerns with the changing of a local lodging facility to provide 
employee housing instead of short term housing.  There was discussion with the Council.  
 
4.      Community Grant Program recommendation of awards  
 
The Town Manager explained the second annual community grant program received 20 
funding requests before the September 22nd deadline, totaling $117,385.  At the October 1st 
meeting the Council allocated funding at two percent of the 2025 general fund expenditures 
as a maximum amount, or $92,500.  A community Ad Hoc Review Committee reviewed the 
requests, and the Manager presented the recommendations totaling $98,205.  
 
The Council agreed with the proposed funding allocations from the Ad Hoc Review 
Committee. 
 
5.      Draft Strategic Plan 
 
The Town Manager presented the draft 2026 Strategic Plan noting it is the work plan for 
staff for the next year, and asked for Council input.   
 
There were comments by the Council. 
 
ADJOUNMENT 
 
The meeting adjourned at 7:30 p.m.
