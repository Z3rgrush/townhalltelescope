The City Council of the City of Gardner, Kansas met in regular session on September 21, 2026, at 7:00 p.m. in the 
Council Chambers at Gardner City Hall, 120 East Main Street, Gardner, Kansas, with Mayor Todd Winters 
presiding. Present were Councilmembers Mark Baldwin, Kacy Deaton, Mark Wiehn, Steve McNeer, and Kelly 
Johnson. City staff present were City Administrator Jim Pruetting; Deputy City Administrator Amy Nasta; Finance 
Director Matt Wolff; Deputy Police Chief Brittanie Pruitt; Parks Director Jason Bruce; Community Development 
Deputy Director Magi Brewster; Public Works Director Kellen Headlee; Utility Director David Hunter; City Attorney 
Ryan Denk; Clerk Renee Rich. Others present included those listed on the sign-in sheet and others who did not 
sign in.  
There being a quorum of Councilmembers present, Mayor Winters called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m.

2. Consider adopting an ordinance approving a rezoning from RP-2 (Planned Two-Family Residential) to 
RP-3 (Planned Garden Apartment) and associated preliminary development plan for 5 row house lots 
on 2.01 acres located at the intersection of Kill Creek Road and 174 th Street 
 
Community Development Deputy Director Brewster said this is for the last five lots of this planned development 
area. The current owner was not involved in the original development and proposes to rezone these five lots and 
develop a single building quadplex on each of the lots for a total of 20 units. There was concern about the proposed 
layout and driveways leaving little room for on-street parking. Since the Planning Commission meeting, the applicant 
has submitted a final development plan. They are proposing to rotate the building at the corner of 174 th Street and 
Pratt Street 90 degrees so the units would face Pratt Street which would allow for more on-street parking. Protest 
Petitions were submitted which requires three-quarters of the governing body to vote in the affirmative to pass the 
ordinance.  
Tim Gates was the original realtor that helped with the rezoning back when the development was being built. 
Believes this plan is too dense for the area and won’t allow for on-street parking. Would like this to be sent back for 
review.  
Emil Schutzel said this is double the units in half the space. He has a big investment over there and hopes the 
council respects that.

3. Consider authorizing the City Administrator to execute an amendment for the Progressive Design Build 
Phase II construction services contract to Crossland Heavy Contractors, Inc. for the Kill Creek Water 
Resource Recovery Facility Expansion, CIP Project No WW8012 
 
Utilities Director Hunter said this amendment is to complete train three at the treatment facility. Trains one and two 
are currently under construction. This additional funding will upgrade train three to 2.5M gallon per day treatment 
giving a total capacity of 7.5M gallon per day treatment. The original treatment train has not been taken out of 
service since it was installed in 2002. This allows train three to be brought up to compliance to match the two trains 
currently being worked on.  
Councilmember McNeer made a motion to authorize the City Administrator to execute an Amendment to the 
Progressive Design Build Phase II contract with Crossland Heavy Contractors, Inc. for the Kill Creek Water 
Resource Recovery Facility Expansion, CIP Project No WW8012., in the amount of $3,150,755.00 for a total 
contract value of $42,600,755.00. 
Councilmember Wiehn Seconded.   
With all of the Councilmembers voting in favor of the 
motion, the motion carried. 
4. Consider authorizing the City Administrator to execute contracts with Schweitzer Engineering 
Laboratories, Inc. (SEL) for the SCADA & RTU Replacement Project 
 
Utlities Director Hunter said the current SCADA system is multi-vendor and outdated. This causes coordination 
problems for repairs. Plans for this to be a closed system. It will have remote access capabilities for the company 
to remote in if necessary and upon approval by the city. 
Councilmember McNeer made a motion to authorize the City Administrator to execute contracts with SEL 
Engineering Services, Inc., for a combined total amount not to exceed $303,268.83. 
Councilmember Baldwin Seconded.   
With all of the Councilmembers voting in favor of the 
motion, the motion carried.
