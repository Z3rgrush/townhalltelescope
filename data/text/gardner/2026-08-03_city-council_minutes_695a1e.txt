August 3, 2026 
  
The City Council of the City of Gardner, Kansas met in regular session on August 3, 2026, at 7:00 p.m. in the Council 
Chambers at Gardner City Hall, 120 East Main Street, Gardner, Kansas, with Mayor Todd Winters presiding. Present 
were Councilmembers Mark Baldwin, Kacy Deaton, Mark Wiehn, Steve McNeer, and Kelly Johnson. City staff 
present were City Administrator Jim Pruetting; Deputy City Administrator Amy Nasta; Finance Director Matt Wolff; 
Police Chief Kelly Herron; Parks Director Jason Bruce; Community Development Director David Knopick; Public 
Works Director Kellen Headlee; Utility Director David Hunter; City Attorney Ryan Denk; Clerk Renee Rich. Others 
present included those listed on the sign-in sheet and others who did not sign in.  
There being a quorum of Councilmembers present, Mayor Winters called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. 
PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE 
Mayor Winters led those present in the Pledge of Allegiance. 
PRESENTATIONS 
PUBLIC HEARING 
PUBLIC COMMENT 
Eric Arnold discusses the Flock system the City has a contract with. Asks the city to not allow the contract to renew 
and remove the cameras. 
 
Steve Shute echoes what Mr. Arnold said about the Flock cameras. Shared several cases across the US where 
information from cameras has been misused. Asks the city get the cameras offline. 
 
Allan Flesberg provides follow up of his report from last year of fireworks being discharged on the property of the 
Gardner Municipal Airport. He said the signs and other efforts had a positive effect on this year’s July 4th celebration 
at the airport.  
 
Andy Akright recently moved to Gardner. Said there were a lot of fireworks here, but they just stopped at the right 
time following the rules. Thanks Parks & Rec for another great soccer season in the spring. Thank you for funding 
sports because it gives many kids an opportunity to play. His son shared some things he likes about soccer.

COUNCIL UPDATES 
Chief Herron discusses the one stationary Flock camera deployed. PD is going through accreditation, so all policies 
are being reviewed. The General IT policy covers most of the software services. Some technologies having a more 
robust policy would be a better thing as some systems may need more robust policies. Tomorrow night is National 
Night Out at the pool hosted by PD from 6-8 pm.  
 
Clerk Rich says there is an opening on the Planning Commission and looking for applicants. 
 
Finance Director Wolff reported the draft budget book is on the website right now which is earlier than usual. 
 
City Administrator Pruetting said he had a request for a supplemental budget report for all expenses over $50k.  
 
Councilmember Johnson thanks all who spoke tonight and reminds everyone to vote tomorrow. 
 
Councilmember McNeer thanks PD for showing how community policing is at its best. Said the County Fair was 
phenomenal. Would like to look at spending and feels we need to watch budgets fairly close. 
 
Councilmember Wiehn speaks about fireworks comments. Said the school district appreciated the signs and said 
they seemed to have had the desired effect. They will continue the efforts for next year. Go vote. 
 
Council Vice-President Deaton asks why the stationary camera rather than just the fleet ALPR. The stationary 
cameras are useful for general area of movement. Those on the cars are moving so the general direction is moving. 
Asks about audits. Most of the audits are complaint driven. Camera use follows the same policies as criminal justice 
systems which require a reason or specific purpose to run a query. 
  
Baldwin asks how many ALPR cameras are in the city. There is one flock camera in this city that the city owns. 
There are other non-flock cameras owned by the county. Glad the signs helped with the fireworks this year. 
Encourages Andy to keep up the positive attitude and keep coaching.
