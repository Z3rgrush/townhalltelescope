The City Council of the City of Gardner, Kansas met in regular session  on September 8, 2026, at 7:00 p.m. in the 
Council Chambers at Gardner City Hall, 120 East Main Street, Gardner, Kansas, with Mayor Todd Winters 
presiding. Present were Councilmembers Mark Baldwin, Kacy Deaton, Mark Wiehn, Steve McNeer, and Kelly 
Johnson. City staff present were City Administrator Jim Pruetting; Deputy City Administrator Amy Nasta; Finance 
Director Matt Wolff; Police Chief Kelly Herron; Parks Director Jason Bruce; Community Development Director David

Bradley Steinmetz is concerned about  the increases in budget costs. Discusses information from the budget book

NEW BUSINESS 
2. Consider adopting an ordinance adopting the Fiscal Year 2027 Budget 
 
Finance Director Wolff explains the year end fund balances based upon the Proposed 2027 Budget. The proposed 
mill levy is 17.814 mills which is the same as the prior year. 
Administrator Pruetting explains the budget process. Requests are made for items needed for city business. Then 
the budget is reviewed and cuts are made. Review includes one- time expenses and if items are pushed, we lose 
operational efficiency and pay a higher fee for the following year. It’s based upon growth of the city and additional 
needs we will have. Some purchases come out of different funds that are not coming out of property taxes rather 
out of revenue funds. 
The baseball fields at Celebration Park is coming out of the Transient Guest Tax, which comes from people staying 
in the hotels, but that money can only be spent on certain items. The cost of road repairs comes through a bidding 
process, and grants are sought after to help fund those. The contracts are included in council packets.

A question came up about the $300k dump truck  included in the budget. The fleet has not been expanded since 
2009 when the city was about 1/3 of the size it is now. This new addition is about double the load capacity of our 
current dump trucks. This adds capacity and is more equipped to handle the additional roadways we have picked 
up in the last ten years. Other items of discussion are current vacancies and vehicle replacements. Pruetting 
explains the VERP program based upon costs and repairs. Some positions stay open for a longer  period of time 
because they require specialized experience. The people living here are grateful for the city services and amenities. 
There is concern about cutting some of the openings. Pruetting explains that if  we put off purchases and staff 
additions, we will be doing catch-up later. As the city grows, we will have to provide additional city services that we 
are not budgeting right now. Items discussed for cutting from the budget included closing the pool, canceling the 
Independence Day Festival, and pushing the phone system purchase another year.  
Councilmember Wiehn made a motion to adopt Ordinance No. 2888, an ordinance adopting the 2027 Budget of the 
City of Gardner, Kansas and setting the mill levy rate as specified in the previously adopted resolution.  
Councilmember Baldwin Seconded.   
With a majority of the Councilmembers voting in

EXHIBIT A  SCOPE OF SERVICES 
Main Street / Elm Avenue Traffic Signal Concept Study 
August 5, 2026 
Purpose: The City of Gardner has identified the need to replace aging and unreliable traffic 
signal infrastructure at the intersection of Main Street and Elm Street. Existing signal hardware 
and wiring are causing malfunctions, and the signal enters flash operations often. The City 
intends to fund and reconstruct the traffic signal with new signal infrastructure including 
signal/light poles, signal cabinet, signal heads, pedestrian pushbuttons and pedestrian heads, 
underground conduit system, and wiring. 
 
The city also has a streetscape planned for Main Street through this intersection; however, the 
project is currently unfunded. The purpose of this scope is to complete construction plans for a 
replacement traffic signal including other previously identified necessary improvements 
including replacement subsurface stormwater system, new curb and gutter, ADA ramps, and 
necessary sidewalk and roadway repairs. Aesthetics will be limited to colored, stamped 
concrete in the space between ADA ramps.  
 
This work will final Construction Plans, Specifications, and Estimate to be used for bidding and 
construction purposes.

Background/Description of Item: 
In December of 2024, the Parks and Recreation Dept. received a LWCF grant from the National 
Park Service in the amount of $102,200.00 for the replacement of a new, ADA compliant restroom 
at Veterans Park. 
 
In accordance with the City’s Purchasing Policy, a Requests For Proposals (RFP) was issued 
earlier this month for the selection of a contractor to perform the design and installation for t his 
project. Two proposals were submitted and subsequently reviewed by a selection committee 
comprised of members of City staff. Cahills Construction Inc. was  selected by the committee 
based upon the contents of their RFP response. This new ADA-accessible restroom facility will 
allow individuals with disabilities, older  adults, caregivers, and families with young children to 
enjoy the park more independently and for longer periods of time.  
 
Construction will be scheduled to begin in October 2026.

Utilities / Infrastructure 
Electric 
The proposed development is within the City of Gardner’s electric utility service area. Based on 
preliminary load numbers provided by the applicant, the Utilities Department may consider relinquishing 
this service area, allowing Evergy to serve the project.  
Water 
The proposed development is split between two service areas; WaterOne (80%) and City of Gardner 
(20%). A Will-Serve letter from WaterOne was submitted with this application for that portion of the 
development within their service area.  Based on preliminary information provided by the applicant, the 
Utilities Department may consider allowing WaterOne to serve the entire development.  
Sanitary Sewer 
The City of Gardner is the service provider for this area ; however, based on preliminary information  
provided by the applicant it is anticipated that Johnson County Wastewater or City of Olathe Wastewater 
are better positioned to serve this development. A Will-Serve letter from either provider will need to be 
submitted if the area is not served by the City of Gardner. 
Storm Sewer 
Stormwater detention is proposed in two ponds  shown in Tracts B and D. In add ition, the preliminary 
development plan shows proposed underground detention . These detention a reas will be underneath 
proposed surface parking lots. At this stage in the review / approval process, t he submitted preliminary 
stormwater plan demonstrates that stormwater can be handled in accordance with the requirements of 
Title 14 of the Gardner Municipal Code. 
Roadway Network & Vehicular Access 
Within the Velocity Arts campus, the roads will be private. The only public road within the property is a 
proposed collector street on the perimeter of the proposed development. This public road is considered 
a Collector – Parkway / Boulevard Street Type per the Land Development Code (LDC) and is shown to 
become 167th Street along the north side of the property. Two points of access are shown from Clare 
Road. The northernmost access point connects to an internal, private “ring road”. The southernmost 
access point provides access to the proposed college and connects to other internal, private roads.  In 
the vicinity of the development area both Clare Road and 167th Street will continue to be public and will 
require significant improvements to support the level of development proposed.  
Access to the Velocity Arts campus is proposed from 175th Street, 167th Street, and Clare Road. Off-site 
improvements at 175th Street include a reconfigured interchange at I-35 feeding a proposed on-site entry 
roundabout. The Kansas Department of Transportation (KDOT) is in the process of designing 
improvements to the I-35 and 175th Street interchange. It is anticipated that the improvements will include 
a diverging diamond interchange; however a final design has not been determined pending the evaluation 
of multiple concepts for handling traffic at the 175th Street and Cedar Niles intersection.  
Sidewalks 
A series of sidewalks, both internal and external, private and public, are proposed. The preliminary 
development plan illustrates pedestrian access along the public collector street, as well as along the 
proposed private Local-Activity and Local -Standard streets. In addition  to sidewalks, the proposed 
pedestrian network includes trails / greenways  within the development as depicted in the preliminary 
development plan.
