PROCLAMATION 
 
WHEREAS, Rotary International, founded on February 23, 1905 in Chicago, Illinois, USA, is the world’s first and 
one of the largest non-profit service organizations, and 
WHEREAS, Rotary is a global network consisting of over 1.4 million members in more than 46,000 clubs in 221 
countries and geographical areas who unite to take action to create lasting change in communities across the globe; 
and 
WHEREAS, the Rotary motto, Service Above Self, inspires members to provide humanitarian service, follow high 
ethical standards, and promote goodwill and peace in the world; and  
WHEREAS, Rotary in 1985 launched PolioPlus and in 1988 helped establish the Global Polio Eradication Initiative, 
which today includes the World Health Organization, U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, UNICEF, and 
the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, and Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, to immunize the children of the world against 
polio; and  
WHEREAS, polio cases have dropped by 99.9 percent since 1988 and the world stands on the threshold of eradicating 
the disease; and  
WHEREAS, to date, Rotary has contributed more than $2.6 billion and countless volunteer hours to protecting nearly 
3 billion children in 122 countries; and 
WHEREAS, Rotary is working to raise an additional $50 million per year, which would be leveraged for maximum 
impact by an additional $100 million annually from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation; and  
WHEREAS, these efforts are providing much- needed operational support, medical staff, laboratory equipment, and 
educational materials for health workers and parents; and 
WHEREAS, in addition, Rotary has played a major role in decisions by donor governments to contribute more than 
$10 billion to the effort; and 
WHEREAS, there are 48 members of the Rotary Club of Gardner who sponsor service projects to address such critical 
issues as poverty, disease, hunger, illiteracy, and the environment in our local community and abroad, and  
WHEREAS, Gardner Rotarians have committed their time, talent and treasure to continue support of PolioPlus until 
Polio is finally eradicated from the earth, contributing $3,800 over the last three years,  
NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that I, Todd Winters, Mayor of the City of Gardner, Kansas, do hereby 
proclaim October 24, 2026 as 
World Polio Day 
in the City of Gardner and encourage all citizens to participate in this observance.

Utility Director Hunter said t he increased costs are due to unexpected repairs  during teardown. The contractor 
uncovered several critical issues in both the turbine and the generator systems that must be completed to return 
unit number one to service. The last major turbine repair was more than 20 years ago.  When turbines and 
generators sit for long periods of time, deterioration accelerates. The problems are only visible when the unit is 
open, and compressor blades are fully open. This must be repaired for reliable operation. We have extreme 
temperatures and this unit helps to keep our city away from the volatile market. Unit 2 has already been overhauled. 
Theoretically we should be good for another twenty years after the completion of this overhaul. 
Councilmember McNeer made a motion to move to recommend that the City Administrator be authorized to execute 
Change Order No. 1 for the Unit #1 Turbine Generator Overhaul, including the generator stator rewind, rotor 
refurbishment, turbine and compressor corrective repairs, and the required 9th  Stage Hook Fit Modification, in the 
total amount of $2,751,942, and to incorporate the associated project costs into the final bonding package upon 
completion of the overhaul. 
Councilmember Wiehn Seconded.   
With all of the Councilmembers voting in favor of the 
motion, the motion carried. 
2. Consider adopting a resolution authorizing the offer for public sale of certain general obligations of the 
City of Gardner, Kansas 
 
Finance Director Wolff said the city is scheduled to sell approximately $10.225M principal amount of taxable general 
obligation bonds series  2026D and approximately $46.25M of tax-exempt general obligation bonds series 2026E 
bonds on November 2 nd, at 10 am. Series 2026D is to reimburse or pay the cost of certain electrical system 
improvements including substation 4 and represents the first series of bonds planed for these improvements. Series 
2026E is for the reimbursement or to pay the costs of t wo different projects. Hillsdale Lake water intake structure 
improvements is approximately $22.305M and the remaining is associated with the Kill Creek Water Resource 
Recovery Facility expansion.   
Since the last debt sale in August, estimated interest rates have increased significantly. Discussions with our bond 
council and our municipal advisor determined there’s no clear indication that delaying the sale would result in lower 
borrowing costs.

Background/Description of Item: 
The City of Gardner’s Hillsdale Water Treatment Plant Roof Replacement project is being funded 
out of the Water Treatment Operation & Maintenance Budget (O & M). The existing roof is a 
combination of ages dating back from the 2005 plant expansion and repairs made to the original 
plant roof. Multiple repairs to sky lights and some wood rot require repairs.     
 
Bidding Process: 
The Invitation for Bids was advertised on the City of Gardner website, Drexel website and The 
Legal Record, from September 8, 2026 to September 24, 2026. Four bid proposal packages were 
submitted to the Utilities Department, and all met and exceeded the requirements set out in the 
bid documents, which laid out the requirements for the project. 
 
The apparent low bidder for the 2026 Hillsdale Water Treatment Plant Roof Replacement project 
was determined to meet or exceed the request requirements and are shown in the table below.
