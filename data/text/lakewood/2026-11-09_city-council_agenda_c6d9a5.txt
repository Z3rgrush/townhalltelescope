Subject: Public Benefit Agreement with Natural Grocers 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: The Public Benefit Agreement keeps the national headquarters of Natural 
Grocers in Lakewood and supports the company’s move into a partially vacant office building at 3609 
S. Wadsworth Blvd. Natural Grocers will invest about $5.5 million to remodel the 80,005-square-foot 
building, keep at least 150 headquarters jobs in the city, and add 50 new headquarters jobs that pay 
at or above the Jefferson County average wage. The agreement is performance-based. The City will 
provide up to $150,000 only after the remodel is complete and has passed inspection, and Natural 
Grocers must repay the incentive if it moves its headquarters out of Lakewood during the retention 
period. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: Natural Grocers is a family-run Colorado company founded in 
1955. It has kept its corporate headquarters in Lakewood and now operates more than 175 stores in 
22 states. In February 2026, the company bought a five-story, 80,005-square-foot office building at 
3609 S. Wadsworth Blvd. (Parcel ID 59-022-04-004). The building was constructed in 1983 and is 
partially vacant. Natural Grocers plans to remodel it into its permanent national headquarters. 
 
This is the second agreement Council has considered with Natural Grocers. On February 13, 2023, 
Council considered a Public Benefit Agreement (Resolution 2023-11) for a two-tier project at the 
company’s existing location, 12612 W. Alameda Parkway. Tier 1 was a new 13,300-square-foot 
prototype store, and Tier 2 was a remodel of the existing 43,616-square-foot building to expand the 
headquarters. Natural Grocers also committed to hire 25 new full-time employees at the Jefferson 
County average annual wage, then $69,898. In return, the City offered $157,333 for each of the three 
milestones and a five-year rebate of 75% of incremental sales tax, capped at $28,000, for a total of 
up to $500,000 on roughly $4.95 million in private investment. That project did not move forward. The 
public improvements the City required grew beyond what the original agreement covered, and the 
parties could not find a way to fund them. 
 
The new agreement is built to avoid that outcome. It is smaller and tied to a single building remodel, 
and the improvements eligible for the incentive are listed in Exhibit A before the agreement is signed, 
so the scope is settled up front. It also carries forward the job creation commitment from the 2023 
agreement at a larger scale, 50 new jobs instead of 25, and adds a requirement that the headquarters 
stay in Lakewood. Below is a summary of the agreement.

If Natural Grocers meets all the requirements in the Agreement, it will receive up to $150,000, paid in 
one installment after the remodel passes inspection and the City accepts the improvements. 
Payments cannot exceed the actual, documented costs. The incentive equals about 2.7% of the 
company’s $5.5 million investment. Natural Grocers has told the City that the incentive is a material 
factor in its decision to complete the project at this scale and on this schedule, and the 2023 
agreement shows how a project like this can stall without help. In return, Lakewood keeps a national 
headquarters with at least 200 jobs and puts an underused building on Wadsworth Boulevard back 
into full use. 
 
BUDGETARY IMPACTS: A maximum of $150,000 from the Economic Development Fund, paid in a 
single installment after the remodel is complete. Up to $70,000 of that amount comes from rebated 
City use tax and waived or reimbursed permit and development fees, so part of the cost is revenue 
the project itself generates. Payment is subject to annual appropriation by City Council. The City can 
recover the full amount if Natural Grocers moves its headquarters out of Lakewood during the 
retention period, and a prorated share if the company falls short of the new job target.

I. DEFINITIONS 
 
Unless the context otherwise clearly indicates, the following words and phrases, as used 
in the Agreement, shall be defined as follows: 
 
A. "Certificate of Occupancy" shall have the meaning as set forth in Title 14 of the Code.  
B. "Project" shall mean the remodel of the existing five-story, 80,005 SF, building on the Property 
to expand the Natural Grocers headquarters to include additional office space and related 
improvements. 
C. "Opening Date" shall mean the first day of the second calendar month, following issuance 
of a Certificate of Occupancy for the Project. 
D. "Average Annual Wage" shall mean average annual wage as indicated by the U.S. 
Bureau of Labor Statistics for Jefferson County, Colorado, as most recently published at 
the time of measurement. 
E. "Baseline Employment" shall mean the number of Headquarters Jobs located in the 
City as of the Effective Date, which Natural Grocers represents is at least 150. 
F. "Headquarters Jobs" shall mean full-time, year-round positions with Natural Grocers or 
its affiliates, each scheduled for at least thirty (30) hours per week, whose primary work 
location is the Property or another location within the City. Positions at retail stores are not 
Headquarters Jobs. 
G. "Retention Period" shall mean the period beginning on Effective Date and ending on 
the fifth (5th) anniversary of the Opening Date. 
H. “Capital Improvements” are permanent structural changes to a property that enhance 
its value, increase its useful life, or allow for a new use.

City finds Natural Grocers' is eligible for public investments in the form of incentive funds, 
pursuant to subsection (C) of chapter 3.26 of the Code. Prior to signing, Natural Grocers shall

VI. PROJECT ANALYSIS 
Overview 
The property at 7801 W Duquesne Ave is under contract by Bear Creek Collaborative 
LLC with the intent of redeveloping the site with townhome units. The request to rezone 
the property to the R-MF zone district is needed to accommodate the proposed 
attached residential (townhome) land use. The proposed R-MF zone district and 
attached residential land use is compatible with existing land uses surrounding the site 
and with land uses in the Comprehensive Plan. 
 
The applicant has included a Conceptual Land Use Plan (Attachment C) to generally 
show the proposed development pattern and location of site access, vehicle circulation, 
pedestrian connectivity, and building envelope. The applicant intends to construct an 8-
foot-wide publicly accessible paved trail across the property frontage from the existing 
sidewalk to the east and the Bear Creek Greenbelt to the west as part of the project  
This is an improvement proposed by the developer to accommodate public access to 
the boundary with the City-owned greenbelt. Although this information was included to 
support the rezoning request, the Planning Commission should keep in mind that a 
rezoning approval does not constitute the approval of a specific development proposal 
or land use. 
 
Comprehensive Plan 
The primary document for guiding land use decisions is the Envision Lakewood 2040 
Comprehensive Plan. It is a policy document that provides guidance to City Council, 
Planning Commission, City staff, residents, businesses, and developers to make 
informed decisions about the current and future needs of the community. The 
Comprehensive Plan is available in digital format on the City’s website. The purpose of 
the Comprehensive Plan is to identify and articulate guiding principles, goals and 
strategies to help the community achieve its desired vision. The City’s Vision Statement 
is articulated on pages 16-19 of the Comprehensive Plan, and it is intended to set a 
direction for the future of Lakewood.  
 
The Future Land Use Map (See attachment G) on page 161 of the Comprehensive 
Plan, shows the future land use category for the subject site as Mid Form Residential.  
 
The Mid Form Residential future land use designation supports residential 
neighborhoods that allow a wide range of housing types with limited and complementary 
non-residential uses.  These areas will provide a scaled transition between lower and

II. SUMMARY OF REQUEST 
The applicant, Bear Creek Overlook, LLC, is requesting approval of a proposed 
rezoning at 2501 S Kipling Street from the One-Acre Lot Residential (R-1-43) to the 
Medium Sized Lot Residential (R-1-9) zone district. The subject site is approximately 
2.83 acres in size. 
 
As depicted in Figure 1 below, the project site is located in the 2500 Block of S Kipling 
St, to the west of S Kipling St between W Morrison Rd and S Kipling Pkwy. 
 
 
Figure 1 | Aerial Map, See Attachment A 
 
III. PROCESS – REQUIRED CITY APPROVALS 
Overview 
The rezoning request from One Acre Lot Residential (R-1-43) to Medium Sized Lot 
Residential (R-1-9), is proposed to accommodate the single-family land use. If the 
rezoning request is approved, the applicant, Bear Creek Overlook, LLC, intends to 
develop the site with approximately 7 single-family dwelling units (see Attachment B). 
 
The rezoning process for all parcels of land currently in the City includes a 
neighborhood meeting, formal application, public hearing with the Lakewood Planning 
Commission and a public hearing with the Lakewood City Council. The Planning 
Commission reviews the rezoning request at a public hearing and then provides a 
recommendation to City Council. The City Council will review the Planning Commission 
recommendation, meeting minutes, staff report, and then hold a second public hearing,

Plans 
All rezoning applications are required to submit a Conceptual Land Use Plan (CLUP), 
included as Attachment C to this staff report. The CLUP outlines the specific elements 
that are unique to the site. It is intended to supply enough information about the 
rezoning request for the Planning Commission to make a recommendation and for City 
Council to make a decision.  
 
Major Site Plan & Minor Subdivision applications (which are separate from the rezoning 
process) will determine final layout of site access, site circulation, buildings, open 
space, building architecture, landscape design and other site elements. The site plan 
and final plat will be reviewed against the standards in the Zoning Ordinance, the 
Engineering Regulations and the Conceptual Land Use Plan. It may be approved 
administratively provided the proposal meets the City’s development standards.

VI. PROJECT ANALYSIS 
Overview 
The property at 2501 S Kipling St is under contract by Bear Creek Overlook LLC with 
the intent of redeveloping the site with single family dwelling units. The request to 
rezone the property to the R-1-9 zone district is needed to accommodate the proposed 
single family dwelling unit land use. The proposed R-1-9 zone district and the single-
family land use is consistent with the surrounding land uses, and with the 
Comprehensive Plan. 
 
The applicant has included a Conceptual Land Use Plan (Attachment C) to generally 
show the proposed development pattern and location of site access, vehicle circulation, 
pedestrian connectivity, and building envelope. Although this information was included 
to support the rezoning request, the Planning Commission should keep in mind that a 
rezoning approval does not constitute the approval of a specific development proposal 
or land use. 
 
Comprehensive Plan 
The primary document for guiding land use decisions is the Envision Lakewood 2040 
Comprehensive Plan. It is a policy document that provides guidance to City Council, 
Planning Commission, City staff, residents, businesses, and developers to make 
informed decisions about the current and future needs of the community. The 
Comprehensive Plan is available in digital format on the City’s website. The purpose of 
the Comprehensive Plan is to identify and articulate guiding principles and goals, and 
the strategies to help the community achieve its desired vision. The City’s Vision 
Statement is articulated on pages 16-19 of the Comprehensive Plan, and it is intended 
to set a direction for the future of Lakewood.  
 
The Future Land Use Map (See attachment G) on page 161 of the Comprehensive 
Plan, shows the future land use category for the subject site as a Neighborhood Hub. 
The Neighborhood Hub future land use includes a variety of housing types & smaller 
scale and mixed-use activity areas that prioritize pedestrian safety, transit, and bike 
access.  
 
Lakewood Zoning Ordinance 
The Lakewood Zoning Ordinance provides information about zone district standards, 
specific development regulations and the planning process.  As stated in Article 3 – 
Zone Districts: 
 
The R-1-9 district is intended to provide for medium-sized lots, including single-family 
residential development.
