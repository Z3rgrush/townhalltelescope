STAFF MEMO 
 
DATE OF MEETING: NOVEMBER 16, 2026 / AGENDA ITEM NO. 4 
 
To: Mayor and City Council 
From: Ben Goldstein, Deputy City Manager 
Subject: Policy Guidelines for Data Privacy, Security, and Third-party Vendor 
Accountability in the Use of Automated License Plate Reader and Automated 
Vehicle Identification Systems 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: The City Council is asked to review and provide feedback on the 
proposed language staff drafted in response to concerns identified at the September 21, 2026, Study 
Session. The City Council should also use this opportunity to identify any additional language or 
areas of concern to be considered when staff procures a new vendor to provide advanced license 
plate reader technology used to assist law enforcement investigations (ALPR) and automated vehicle 
identification technology used for red-light and speed enforcement (AVIS). 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: At its September 21, 2026, Study Session, the City Council 
expressed its desire to establish privacy and security standards applicable to both ALPR and AVIS 
systems, including safeguards governing third-party vendors and the sharing of City data. The City 
Council also provided guidance that it wanted to limit the amount of time the City might be without a 
system, as it recognized the importance of these technologies for legitimate law enforcement and 
traffic safety. Staff have begun the process of separating from the existing ALPR vendor and are 
identifying options for new vendors for both ALPR and AVIS. 
 
The City Manager will seek to incorporate any guidance provided by the City Council into future 
vendor agreements to the extent reasonably feasible, taking into consideration available technology, 
associated costs, operational requirements, the ability of prospective vendors to provide such 
protections, and commercially reasonable contractual terms. 
 
Based on City Council feedback, staff developed criteria to guide future agreements with ALPR and 
AVIS vendors. Listed below are the items for the City Council’s review and consideration: 
a. Data Ownership and Control. The City shall retain ownership and control of data collected on 
its behalf through ALPR and AVIS systems. Vendor agreements should prohibit the sale, 
licensing, commercialization, or unauthorized disclosure of City data, including the use of such 
data for the development or training of artificial intelligence or machine-learning systems 
without the City's express written authorization. 
b. Data Sharing and Access. The City shall maintain reasonable control over access to and 
sharing of data collected through ALPR and AVIS systems. Vendor agreements should, to the 
extent technically and commercially feasible, prevent the inclusion of City data in national 
databases, shared data repositories, or cross-agency search networks without the City's 
express authorization. The City should retain the ability to authorize, restrict, and terminate 
access by outside agencies and require reasonable safeguards against unauthorized further 
disclosure of City data. 
c. Vendor Access and Disclosure. Vendor agreements should restrict access to and disclosure 
Page 10 of 12
of City data by third-party vendors except as authorized by the City or required by applicable 
law. Agreements should prohibit disclosures inconsistent with Colorado law, including 
restrictions relating to civil immigration enforcement and legally protected health care activity. 
Vendors should be required to provide notice of requests for City data and afford the City a 
reasonable opportunity to respond or object when legally permissible. Exceptions permitting 
disclosure without prior City authorization should be narrowly defined and subject to 
reasonable documentation and notification requirements. 
d. Data Retention and Destruction. The City shall maintain reasonable limitations on the 
retention of data collected through ALPR and AVIS systems, consistent with applicable law 
and the distinct operational requirements of each system. Vendor agreements should establish 
appropriate retention and deletion requirements, including reasonable procedures to verify the 
destruction of data following the expiration of applicable retention periods, to the extent 
technically feasible and consistent with applicable legal preservation requirements. 
e. Data Security. Vendor agreements should require commercially reasonable technical and 
administrative safeguards appropriate to the nature and sensitivity of the data collected, 
including encryption, access controls, multifactor authentication, access logging, and 
appropriate restrictions on vendor personnel access. Vendors should maintain reasonable 
cybersecurity monitoring, vulnerability assessments, and procedures for identifying, reporting, 
and responding to unauthorized access or disclosure. 
f. Auditing and Verification. The City should seek contractual provisions permitting reasonable 
verification of vendor compliance with data privacy and security requirements. Such provisions 
may include independent security assessments, third-party audit reports, security certifications, 
access logs, and documentation of corrective actions. Vendors should be required to 
cooperate with the City in investigating and remedying unauthorized access, disclosure, or 
other material violations of contractual obligations. 
g. Changes to System Functionality. Vendor agreements should require advance notice of 
material changes to system functionality affecting the collection, retention, storage, use, 
sharing, or security of City data. To the extent reasonably practicable, the City should retain 
the ability to reject or disable functionality that materially expands the collection, use, or 
sharing of data beyond the purposes authorized by the City. 
h. Contractual Accountability. Vendor agreements should include commercially reasonable 
and enforceable remedies for material violations of privacy and security obligations, including 
corrective action, suspension of access, termination, appropriate indemnification, and recovery 
of costs associated with unauthorized access or disclosure, subject to applicable law and 
negotiated contractual limitations. 
 
BUDGETARY IMPACTS: Staff does not currently know the budget impact associated with these 
modifications to the provision of ALPR or AVIS systems within the City. 
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS: Staff recommends the City Council provide guidance on the criteria 
for selecting a new ALPR or AVIS to ensure their concerns with the use of ALPR or AVIS systems are 
met and to limit the time without any system in place 
 
ALTERNATIVES: The City Council may provide staff with guidance on the use of ALPR or AVIS 
systems within the City in addition to the items that were previously raised and addressed above. 
 
PUBLIC OUTREACH: This item has been promoted through the regular communication channels to 
be considered by the Lakewood City Council.
