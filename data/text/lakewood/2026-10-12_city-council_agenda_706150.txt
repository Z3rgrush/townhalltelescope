BREAST CANCER AWARENESS MONTH 
 
WHEREAS, October 2026 marks the 41st year of National Breast Cancer Awareness 
Month, an effort that has educated millions of Americans about the 
importance of early detection, diagnosis, and treatment; and 
WHEREAS, breast cancer impacts people of all genders, with one in eight women and 
thousands of men facing a breast cancer diagnosis in their lifetime; and 
WHEREAS, when breast cancer is detected early and remains localized, the five-year 
survival rate is as high as 99%, underscoring the importance of regular 
screenings, self-exams, and timely medical care; and 
WHEREAS, Breast Cancer Awareness Month serves as a time to honor those who have 
lost their battle, to stand with survivors and their families, and to recognize 
the tireless advocates, caregivers, and healthcare professionals who 
provide care, support, and education; and 
WHEREAS, organizations such as the American Cancer Society and local providers, 
including the Lutheran Breast Care Center at Lutheran Hospital, continue to 
advance research, expand awareness, and deliver compassionate, patient-
centered care from screening to survivorship; and 
WHEREAS, the City of Lakewood encourages all residents to take action in prevention 
and awareness by learning their family history, adopting healthy lifestyles, 
scheduling regular screenings, supporting loved ones impacted by breast 
cancer, and advocating for continued research and community resources. 
NOW, THEREFORE, on behalf of the City Council and the people of the City of 
Lakewood, I, Wendi Strom, Mayor of the City of Lakewood, Colorado, by 
virtue of the authority vested in me, do hereby proclaim: 
 
OCTOBER 2026 AS BREAST CANCER AWARENESS MONTH 
 
GIVEN under my hand and Seal of the City 
of Lakewood, this 12th day of October 2026.

 HISPANIC AND LATIN HERITAGE MONTH 
WHEREAS, the United States Congress has designated September 15th to October 
15th to recognize and celebrate the heritage, history, traditions, and 
cultural diversity of Hispanic & Latiné communities in the United States. 
The timing was selected because the anniversaries of independence for 
several Latin American countries are in mid-September; and 
WHEREAS, there are a variety of labels used to describe the population; we distinguish 
the community as Latiné, a term inclusive of the entire population as well 
as being gender neutral. Hispanic defines the population with lineage from 
a Spanish-speaking nation. Latino identifies the community with ancestry 
from the Americas and is a gender-specific term; and 
WHEREAS, the United States Census Bureau approximates the Hispanic & Latiné 
population in the United States to be greater than 62 million people, around 
19%. Locally, Lakewood’s Hispanic & Latiné population constitutes just 
under 35,000 or 22.3%, and is thus the biggest ethnic minority group in 
Lakewood; and 
WHEREAS, throughout history, Hispanic & Latiné leaders have paved the way for 
social change, cultural enrichment, entrepreneurship, and progress in 
education, politics, arts, and activism;  
WHEREAS, in accordance with the 2026 nationwide theme, “Together We Are More”, 
which celebrates family, resilience, creativity, faith, and service, and honors 
the diverse stories that strengthen our nation; we ask Lakewood residents 
to recognize the significant achievements of the Hispanic & Latiné 
communities have played in addressing prejudice and discrimination, 
advancing change, and fostering innovation that continues to shape a more 
prosperous, inclusive, and just future for all. 
WHEREAS, locally and nationally, countless Hispanic and Latin educators, 
entrepreneurs, artists, and community organizers continue to foster 
innovation, strengthen communities, and inspire pride and purpose in 
generations to come. 
NOW, THEREFORE, on behalf of the City Council and the people of the City of 
Lakewood, I, Wendi Strom, Mayor of the City of Lakewood, Colorado, by virtue of the 
authority vested in me, do hereby proclaim 
 
SEPTEMBER 15 TO OCTOBER 15, 2025, IS HISPANIC AND LATIN 
HERITAGE MONTH 
 
GIVEN under my hand and Seal of the City of 
Lakewood, this 12th day of October 2026 
 
 
Wendi Strom, Mayor

Subject: SUPPORTING LW BALLOT ISSUE 2G - 0.5% SALES TAX INCREASE 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: Through this Resolution of Support, the Lakewood City Council 
encourages the registered electors of the City of Lakewood to vote "YES" on Ballot Issue 2G at the 
November 3, 2026, for the purpose of authorizing a voter-approved revenue change of one-half of a 
percent of sales tax to allow the City of Lakewood to provide greater funding for public safety and 
traffic related infrastructure. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: The City Council for the City of Lakewood (the “City”) is permitted 
to pass a Resolution taking a position of advocacy on a local ballot issue as authorized by Section 
2.54.060(A)(2)(b)(iii)(A) of the Lakewood Municipal Code, .   
 
On August 24, 2026, the City Council adopted Resolution 2026-58, to submit Ballot Issue 2G to the 
registered electors of the City at the November 3, 2026, coordinated General and Special Municipal 
Election. 
 
If approved, Ballot Issue 2G would authorize an increase in the City’s sales and use tax rate from 
three percent (3%) to three and one-half percent (3.5%), with “food” as defined in Section 
3.01.430(G) of the Lakewood Municipal Code, exempt from the additional one-half percent tax, and 
with all resulting revenue dedicated to public safety purposes and traffic safety improvements. 
 
The City Council has identified public and traffic safety as a community priority, including investments 
in crime prevention, homelessness response, and other strategies supporting safer streets for 
pedestrians, bicyclists, and motorists.  
 
The City Council recognizes that the City has substantial unmet transportation and pedestrian safety 
needs, including missing sidewalk segments, bicycle facilities, crossing lighting, and neighborhood 
traffic-safety improvements, and the City’s ability to address the unmet safety needs is constrained by 
existing funding. 
 
The passage of Ballot Issue 2G will provide additional resources to address the City's unmet safety 
needs. 
 
Ballot Issue 2G proposes a modest increase in the City's sales tax rate from 3.0% to 3.5%, and would 
be the first increase in the City's sales tax rate in many years;  A sales tax rate of 3.5% remains lower 
than the rates levied by neighboring cities, and the proposed increase exempts most groceries while 
supporting a competitive environment for local businesses.   
 
If Ballot Issue 2G passes, revenues from the tax increase would be expressly limited to public safety-
related equipment, services, and personnel, as well as the maintenance and improvement of streets,

Subject: SUPPORTING LW BALLOT QUESTION 2H - CHARTER SECTION 2.6(A) 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: Through this Resolution, the Lakewood City Council encourages the 
registered electors of the City of Lakewood to Vote "YES" on ballot question 2H at the November 3, 
2026 election, to amend Section 2.6(a) of the City’s Home Rule Charter to provide that the terms of 
office of the Mayor and City Councilmembers shall commence at the first regular meeting following 
certification of the results of the regular municipal election. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: The City Council for the City of Lakewood (the “City”) is expressly 
authorized to pass a Resolution taking a position of advocacy in regard to a local ballot question in 
accordance with Section 2.54.060(A)(2)(b)(iii)(A) of the Lakewood Municipal Code.   
 
The Lakewood Home Rule Charter (Charter) was adopted on November 1, 1983, to serve as the 
primary document establishing the governance of the City. Charter Section 14.9 allows for the 
Charter to be amended in the manner provided by Article XX of the Colorado Constitution and any 
statute enacted by the Colorado General Assembly. C.R.S. § 31-2-210(1)(b) authorizes the City 
Council to adopt an ordinance submitting a proposed charter amendment to a vote of the registered 
electors of the City. 
 
On August 10, 2026, the City Council adopted Ordinance 2026-25 to send a ballot question to the 
voters regarding an amendment to Charter Section 2.6.  Charter Section 2.6 currently mandates that 
the term of office of a newly elected Mayor or City Councilmember begins at the first regular City 
Council meeting following the regular municipal election, even though the first regular City Council 
meeting is routinely scheduled to occur before official election results are certified.  Certification of 
election results typically occurs ten (10) to fourteen (14) days after the date of the election. 
 
Because a newly elected Mayor or City Councilmember cannot begin serving until election results are 
certified, compliance with Charter Section 2.6 requires canceling what would otherwise be the first 
regular City Council meeting in November, routinely preventing the City Council from conducting 
regular City business for up to thirty (30) days following the election.   The proposed amendment to 
Charter Section 2.6 will not change the longstanding practice of a newly elected Mayor or City 
Councilmember taking the oath of office and beginning their service on the date of what would be the 
second regular meeting in November, as that meeting occurs after certification of the election results. 
  
 
The proposed amendment to Charter Section 2.6 is being submitted to the voters so they may decide 
whether to conform the City's Charter to current election certification practices which will allow the 
City Council to hold, rather than cancel, its first regular November meeting following the regular 
election, thereby preventing interruptions or delays in the conduct of City business.

Subject: SUPPORTING LW BALLOT QUESTION 2I - CHARTER SECTION 12.12 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: Through this Resolution the Lakewood City Council encourages the 
registered electors of the City to Vote "YES" on ballot question 2I at the November 3, 2026 election, 
which seeks voter approval to repeal and replace Section 12.12 of the City’s Home Rule Charter to 
update the Charter to require any increase in property taxes be approved by a vote of the electorate 
in conformance with the mandates of the Colorado Constitution. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: The City Council for the City of Lakewood (the “City”) is expressly 
authorized to pass a Resolution taking a position of advocacy in regard to a local ballot question in 
accordance with Section 2.54.060(A)(2)(b)(iii)(A) of the Lakewood Municipal Code. 
 
The Lakewood Home Rule Charter (Charter) was adopted on November 1, 1983, to serve as the 
primary document establishing the governance of the City.  Charter Section 14.9 allows for the 
Charter to be amended in the manner provided by Article XX of the Colorado Constitution and any 
statute enacted by the Colorado General Assembly.  C.R.S. § 31-2-210(1)(b) authorizes the City 
Council to adopt an ordinance submitting a proposed charter amendment to a vote of the registered 
electors of the City. 
 
On August 10, 2026, the City Council adopted Ordinance 2026-27 to send a ballot question to the 
voters regarding the repeal and replacement of Charter Section 12.12.   Charter Section 12.12 was 
adopted on November 1, 1983, and predates subsequent state laws and constitutional requirements 
establishing limitations and voter approval requirements applicable to property taxes and mill levies, 
including Article X, Section 20 of the Colorado Constitution, commonly known as the Taxpayer’s Bill 
of Rights (TABOR), establishes constitutional limitations and voter approval requirements applicable 
to property taxes, mill levies, and the collection and retention of certain tax revenues. 
 
WHEREAS, amending Charter Section 12.12 with language that conforms to the Colorado 
Constitution will eliminate potential conflicts between state and local requirements and provide 
greater clarity and transparency, and such revision of language does not change any existing tax or 
create any new tax.   Any increase in property taxes would be required to be submitted for voter 
approval.  
 
Amending Charter Section 12.12 to conform to the Colorado Constitution will ensure the Charter is 
clearly interpreted to be consistent with current and future state constitutional requirements.

Subject: SUPPORTING LW BALLOT QUESTION 2J - CHARTER SECTION 13.1(D) AND 
13.2(E) 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: Through this Resolution, the Lakewood City Council encourages the 
registered electors of the City to Vote "YES" on ballot question 2J at the November 3, 2026, election, 
which seeks voter approval to replace the language establishing time frames for holding regular or 
special elections as established within Charter Section 13.1(d) regarding initiative procedures and 
Charter Section 13.2(e) regarding referendum procedures to better conform to the requirements of 
modern mail ballot elections and provide greater inclusivity for all residents, including military 
personnel residing and serving outside of Lakewood. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: The City Council for the City of Lakewood (the “City”) is expressly 
authorized to pass a Resolution taking a position of advocacy in regard to a local ballot question in 
accordance with Section 2.54.060(A)(2)(b)(iii)(A) of the Lakewood Municipal Code.  
 
The Lakewood Home Rule Charter (Charter) was adopted on November 1, 1983, to serve as the 
primary document establishing the governance of the City.   Charter Section 14.9 allows for the 
Charter to be amended in the manner provided by Article XX of the Colorado Constitution and any 
statute enacted by the Colorado General Assembly.  C.R.S. § 31-2-210(1)(b) authorizes the City 
Council to adopt an ordinance submitting a proposed charter amendment to a vote of the registered 
electors of the City. 
 
Previous amendments to the City’s Charter have sought to revise language to bring the Charter into 
conformance with revised State election laws, including on November 2, 1999, when voters approved 
a ballot question amending Charter Section 13, regarding initiative and referendum procedures. 
 Subsequent to the November 1999 amendment, the State has revised numerous provisions of the 
Municipal Election Code enacted under Title 31 of the Colorado Revised Statutes to recognize the 
additional procedural steps required for mail ballot elections, as opposed to traditional in-person 
elections, including securing a qualified ballot printing company, printing and delivering ballots and 
envelopes to the City, mailing ballots to voters, and receiving completed ballots, all of which 
specifically pertain to active and overseas military electors, a group that requires additional time to 
receive and return their ballots as described within C.R.S. 31-10-102.8.   Currently, C.R.S. 31-11-104 
establishes the election procedures for initiative ordinances, and C.R.S. 31-11-105 establishes the 
election provisions for referendums.  Both statutes authorize elections to be held not less than sixty 
(60) days and not more than one hundred fifty (150) days after the City Clerk’s final determination of 
petition sufficiency to allow for compliance with C.R.S. 31-10-102.8. 
 
Locally, the City's Charter has not kept up with the statutory changes.  Charter Sections 13.1(d) 
regarding initiative procedures and 13.2(e) regarding referendum procedures continue to mandate 
that elections for both initiative ordinances and referendums be held between thirty (30) and ninety 
Page 18 of 26
(90) days following a sufficiency determination by the City Clerk and presentation to the City Council, 
a time frame that was workable for traditional in-person elections but is impractical for modern mail 
ballot elections, and complying with these outdated time frames creates significant logistical 
challenges in completing the essential steps of a mail ballot election. 
 
In recognition that the City's municipal elections are held within a timeframe that doesn't prioritize 
including military personnel residing and serving outside the City, on August 10, 2026, the City 
Council adopted Ordinance 2026-28 to send a ballot question to the voters regarding amendments to 
Charter Sections 13.1(d) and 13.2(e).  
 
The City Council believes that amending the Lakewood Home Rule Charter to provide a sufficient 
time frame for conducting elections associated with both initiative ordinances and referendums is 
essential to supporting the City’s use of mail ballot elections and ensuring full participation by all 
residents, including military personnel residing and serving outside Lakewood, including overseas.

Subject: Purchase of Property at Two Creeks Park 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: An Ordinance declaring the intent to purchase .3 acres (13,300 sq. ft.) of 
land at 1081 Vance Street, adjacent to Lakewood’s Two Creeks Park, in Ward 2 for municipal open 
space and park purposes. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: Lakewood’s new Two Creeks Park, located at 7540 W. 12th Ave., 
officially opened in November of 2025 following the completion of important channel improvements to 
Dry Gulch. Two Creeks Park is a one-of-a-kind, 3.8-acre neighborhood park shaped by decades of 
community vision and collaboration. The park features a neighborhood-designed playground, 
accessible trails, low water crossings for interactive play, park features from repurposed trees, and 
111 newly planted trees supporting Lakewood’s sustainability goals. 
 
The vision for Two Creeks Park dates to the 1990s, when students from St. Bernadette’s Catholic 
School imagined a park in the area. That early spark of inspiration set in motion more than two 
decades of planning. 
In 2021, the city updated the site plan to incorporate neighborhood feedback and addressed 
necessary improvements to stabilize Dry Gulch. 
 
The City has recently had the opportunity to explore an additional property acquisition to further 
expand the new park. There are sufficient funds available to acquire an additional .3 acres of land on 
the eastern boundary of the park at 1081 Vance Street to expand the park to a total of 4.1 acres.  
 
BUDGETARY IMPACTS: The City Council has allocated a portion of the Conservation Trust Fund for 
the acquisition and improvement of parks and open space properties in the 2026 budget. These funds 
will be used to purchase the property. 
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS: The Department of Community Resources recommends adoption of 
the Ordinance to support this request to approve the acquisition of property located at 1081 Vance 
Street for municipal open space and park purposes, and to authorize the necessary transactions. 
 
ALTERNATIVES: The City may elect not to purchase the property at this time. The property is zoned 
M-R-U which allows multifamily residential development. (Mixed use -Residential- Urban). The 
current owner could sell the property as is. 
 
PUBLIC OUTREACH: This item was promoted through the regular communication channels for an 
item coming before City Council. No specific public outreach has been conducted regarding the 
acquisition of property, although the property is located in an area of Ward 2 lacking adequate park 
space for residents, according to city planning documents.
