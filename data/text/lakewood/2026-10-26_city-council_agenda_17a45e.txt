Subject: Neighborhood Participation Program 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: Staff is requesting that City Council approve the recommended projects 
identified in Table 2 for funding in the 2026 Neighborhood Participation Program year. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: The Neighborhood Participation Program (NPP) was adopted by 
City Council in 1992 as part of the Five-Year Capital Improvement and Preservation Program (CIPP). 
Applications are accepted once a year and compete directly for funding with others received for that 
year. The application process is divided into two phases: pre-applications and final applications. 
During the pre-application phase, neighborhood groups submit brief project proposals to ensure that 
their projects meet the program criteria. If a project is eligible, the neighborhood group is invited to 
submit a final application, which is more comprehensive. In 2026, pre-applications were accepted from 
March 1st to May 1st and final applications were accepted until July 1st. 
 
The intent of the Neighborhood Participation Program is to encourage neighborhood groups to identify 
unmet needs in their immediate neighborhood and propose projects that qualify for funding under this 
program. These projects are usually small and might not otherwise be addressed as a priority item in 
the CIPP. One hundred and seventy-six (176) projects have been selected for the program since its 
inception. Many different types of neighborhood projects have been completed through this program 
including playground improvements, public art installations and community gardens. 
City Council Resolution 2015-34 (Attachment A) describes how the program is to be administered and 
identifies criteria for project selection. Briefly summarized, the evaluation process begins with a staff 
team representing Sustainability and Community Development, Public Works, and Community 
Resources thoroughly reviewing each application for completeness and clarity. Preliminary cost 
estimates are prepared for each project submitted. Projects that meet program requirements are then 
evaluated based on the criteria set forth in the resolution. These criteria are weighted by relative 
importance in order to arrive at an overall ranking for each project. This evaluation approach 
represents a reasoned method of ranking projects of a very disparate nature.

Subject: Mile High Flood District IGA for a Flood Risk Assessment Program 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT: This resolution would authorize the City to enter into an intergovernmental 
agreement (IGA) with the Mile High Flood District (MHFD) to develop a District-wide Flood Risk 
Assessment Program. The Program will create a consistent, District-wide approach for measuring 
urban flood hazards and translating them into clear, measurable flood risks for people and 
infrastructure. This work will support prioritization of capital projects, increase transparency in 
investment decisions, and improve how flood risk is communicated across jurisdictions.  
 
The Public Works Department recommends that City Council adopt the resolution. 
 
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) creates 
floodplain maps, based on modeling, that show where flooding might occur when streams or rivers 
overflow after significant rain events. These maps help identify areas of flood risk and prioritize capital 
investment in major drainage improvements. 
 
However, not all flooding comes from streams. Sometimes heavy rain collects on streets and in low 
spots because the water can’t soak into the ground or the storm drains can’t handle heavy flows. This 
is called surface-water flooding, and it can happen anywhere — even far from a creek. Lakewood is 
especially vulnerable to this type of flooding because of its terrain and its aging stormwater system. 
Currently, there are no maps or models that show where this kind of flooding might occur. 
 
The MHFD’s Flood Risk Program will model flooding for the entire Denver metro area, including 
Lakewood. These models will show both types of flooding: from overflowing streams and from surface 
water. The program will combine the flood maps with information about where people live and where 
buildings are located to better understand overall flood risk. This information will help identify where 
flooding problems are most serious and will guide decisions about which stormwater projects & efforts 
should be prioritized. 
 
BUDGETARY IMPACTS: The total anticipated program cost is $4,962,017. MHFD will fund 50% of 
the program costs and the other 50% will be made up from contributions from 31 other local 
governments in the metro area. A breakdown of the costs, by entity, is attached.  
 
Lakewood’s total obligation is $90,950, with 50 percent ($45,475) to be paid in 2026 and the 
remaining 50 percent ($45,475) in 2027. The originally planned contribution was $110,400, but this 
was reduced to $90,950 because Lakewood will be conducting a similar analysis for the Dry Gulch 
and Sloan’s Lake drainage basins as part of the Stormwater Master Plan. The Master Plan is 
currently underway and is expected to be completed in late 2027. Adequate funding is available in the 
Stormwater Management Utility budget for both 2026 and 2027 to cover these expenditures.

ALTERNATIVES: If Lakewood chooses to opt out of this program, it would create a data gap across 
the district. In addition, Lakewood would likely be responsible for independently funding the full cost of 
analyzing flood risk across the city.

MHFD FLOOD RISK EXHIBIT A 
Intent, Assumptions, and Outcomes 
1. Program Overview and Intent 
The MHFD Flood Risk Program establishes a district-wide, technically consistent methodology for quantifying flood hazards and 
translating them into measurable flood risk to people and infrastructure. The Program supports Capital Improvement Program 
(CIP) prioritization, increases transparency in investment decisions, and improves communication of flood risk across 
jurisdictions. The Program does not modify FEMA regulatory floodplains or create new regulatory designations . However, the 
Program develops foundational riverine models that may support future FEMA regulatory mapping or local regulatory adoption.  
Guiding Policies Primary Objectives 
• Clarity, accessibility, and transparency 
• Integration of flood risk into broader decision-making 
• Consistent, defensible technical standards 
• Defined governance with flexibility to support regulatory 
needs 
• Achievable lifecycle management and sustainability 
• Communicate flood risk clearly beyond binary mapping 
Quantify flood hazards using consistent modeling 
• Translate hazard into risk using defined receptors 
• Provide defensible risk metrics to evaluate CIP impacts 
• Strengthen alignment of MHFD and Local Government CIP 
prioritization processes 
2. Data Development Workflow 
The steps on the right will be completed by 
watershed, in batches across multiple 
geographies, to develop district-wide existing 
hazard and risk information by 2029 (Phase 1 
Production). Additional steps will occur in future 
efforts at the request of local governments 
(Phase 2 Planning). 
The process begins with foundational datasets 
(Step 1), which are used to build hydrologic and 
hydraulic (H&H) models (Step 2). These models 
produce pluvial and fluvial flood hazard maps. 
The hazard maps are then combined with people 
receptor datasets from Step 1 to develop flood 
risk information (Step 3). 
The existing hazard and risk results are used to 
identify flooding problem areas and hotspots 
(Step 4) and then flood risk is aggregated into problem areas to establish baseline risk values (Step 5). 
Phase 2 builds on the Phase 1 models by updating them to represent future or post -project conditions (Step 6) and recalculating 
hazard and risk (repeating Steps 2 and 3). The difference between existing and post-project risk is then aggregated across 
problem areas (Step 5) and used to calculate cost-based risk-reduction ratios (Step 7). These ratios support CIP project 
selection based on cost-effectiveness, focusing on projects that reduce the most risk for the least cost.  
3. Core Assumptions in Modeling Hazard 
Flood Risk will create two-dimensional (2D) Rain-on-Mesh (RoM) models calibrated to historic events, primarily via HEC -RAS v7, 
with flexibility to use InfoWorks ICM based on local government preference. Models will be constructed with a detailed mesh, 
typically 50-foot in developed areas that are further refined to 25 feet along features or 100 -foot in undeveloped areas. Model 
details will be based on readily available data (e.g. MHFD FHADs/MDPs, local government partner data, Denver Regional Council 
of Governments (DRCOG) data, etc.) and often require assumptions based on engineering judgement where data and attributes 
are missing, which is common for sewer datasets and occasionally for structures in the channel. Assumptions made to address 
data gaps will be documented and may be addressed in a future effort. The storm sewer systems will generally be represented for 
pipes 2 feet and larger in diameter (and their contributing inlets)  with flexibility to include smaller pipes based on data quality . 
Hazards will be simulated based on the most recent terrain and existing land cover collections (typically 2020), with the 
application of 13 design storms spanning from the 1- to 1000-year recurrence interval. Additional technical assumptions, 
methodologies, and scoring approaches are documented in MHFD Flood Risk technical guidance documents, which may evolve 
over time as the Program matures.

VI. PROJECT ANALYSIS 
Overview 
The property owner of 2555 S Kipling St currently resides at the property. He is requesting 
to rezone the property to the M-G-S zone district to accommodate his glass installation

proposed M-G-S zone district and the contractor shop land use is consistent with the 
surrounding land uses, and with the Comprehensive Plan. 
The applicant has included a Conceptual Land Use Plan (Attachment B ) to generally

The M-G zone district is intended to allow for mixed-use and community commercial

necessary to support the community. The district is intended to accommodate a higher

and encouraged. 
The Suburban context reflects a more auto-oriented environment, where the existing 
surrounding street pattern and access to adjacent residential neighborhoods is not 
conducive to the highest level of pedestrian connectivity. The context allows for a limited 
amount of parking to be provided between adjacent public streets and the development. 
Additionally, auto-oriented design elements, such as drive-through facilities are 
permitted.

2555 S Kipling Street Rezone Application- from, R-1-43 to M-G-S 
Written Description of Proposed Project and Approval Criteria Review 
This written description reviews the proposed rezoning and improvements to 2555 S. Kipling Street. 
The property, parcel number 49-284-00-014, is roughly 1.99 acres owned by Michael Jordan and is 
currently zoned R-1-43 (One Acre Lot Residential) within the City of Lakewood, in Jefferson County, 
Colorado (see Figure 1 below.) The property currently has a single-family dwelling and two storage 
buildings. There is also a small storage shed on the property.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Fig. 1 
 
 
This application is requesting a  rezone to Mixed Use – General – Suburban (M-G-S) to allow for 
contractor shop land use and to convert the existing single-family home into office space with an 
accessory dwelling unit. During the preapplication review it was found that the lot was un-platted 
so a final plat application was approved and recorded.  
Mr. Jordan would like to rezone the property to M-G-S to utilize the existing buildings on the site for 
the administration of his glass and window  installation business. The business is managed from  the 
property and provides commercial and residential window installation, repair, and custom shower 
glass services at customer locations around the Denver metro and Lakewood communities.

The section below provides more details to address the Lakewood rezoning review criteria shown in 
italics. 
1) The proposed zoning or rezoning promotes the purposes of this Zoning Ordinance as stated in 
Section 17.1.2: 
A. To implement the vision, goals, and recommendations of the City’s Comprehensive Plan. 
B. Promoting the orderly development and redevelopment of land within the City of Lakewood. 
C. To ensure the effective integration of development and redevelopment with surrounding land 
uses. 
D. To prioritize environmental and energy sustainability in land use development and 
redevelopment. 
E. To ensure economic vitality. Promoting mixes of commercial and residential uses within mixed-
use zones. 
 
The purpose of this rezoning request to M-G-S is to align the property zoning to the desired use of 
M-G-S. The proposed use will be in alignment with Lakewood’s criteria and permitted uses for the 
M-G-S district as follows: 
• The property offers a unique opportunity to provide a mix of residential, office and 
contractor shop space, providing economic opportunity through office support jobs 
and working on crews for window installation, and increased support for other local 
businesses for services used by having more workers in the area who may use 
shops and services nearby. 
 
2) The proposed zoning or rezoning is compatible with existing surrounding land uses or the land 
uses envisioned in the Comprehensive Plan: 
To the north of the property is zoned R-1-43 to the South and West is zoned M-G-S, and to 
the east is zoned M-N-S (Mixed Use-Neighborhood-Suburban). Based on the proximity of 
the property to the mixed-use properties to the south, west and east, the proposed rezone is 
compatible and will not impact the nature of the surrounding neighborhood.  No new 
building or major construction is proposed.  The residential zoning to the north is 
significantly higher in elevation and presents a good transition to residential zoning without 
impacting the Comprehensive Plan for that area.

3) The proposed zoning or rezoning meets at least one of the following additional criteria: 
A. The proposed zoning or rezoning promotes implementation of the Comprehensive Plan; or 
B. There has been a material change in the character of the neighborhood or in the City 
generally, such that the proposed zoning or rezoning would be in the public interest and 
consistent with the change; or  
C. The property proposed for zoning or rezoning was previously zoned in error. 
 
The property meets Criteria A with the newly adopted Comprehensive Plan, Envision 
Lakewood 2040, showing the property is in a Neighborhood Hub. The proposed rezoning 
and land use will promote the implementation of the comprehensive plan for future land 
use. Land uses intended for this category include:  
• Smaller-scale commercial and mixed-use activity areas 
• Vertical or horizontal mixed use; ground floor commercial with residential or office 
above 
• Lower intensity office, retail, services, hospitality, restaurants 
• Community gardens 
Criteria B addresses a material change in the character of the neighborhood and also 
applies to this rezoning request. The neighboring mixed-use properties have been operating 
for over 20 years. The car wash since 2002; office and  business space since 2003; the 
North Bear Crossing retail and restaurant space since 2004; and the adjacent townhomes 
were built in 2022. Continuing the M-G-S zone across 2555 S. Kipling will be consistent with 
the material changes in this area. The changing character of the neighborhood is consistent 
with a change to M-G-S zoning for this property and meets Criteria B.  
Additionally, the current nature of this property and historic previous use, was presented as 
a potential business  operation by his real estate agent, which led the  owner to assume he 
could operate his glass business from this location.  The County tax records have the tax  
class as industrial-mixed, which presents a confusing dilemma to the owner with the 
conflicting city zoning.  
The presentation included with this application provides more details on what is proposed 
along with the Conceptual Land Use Plan with the proposed uses of each existing structure.
