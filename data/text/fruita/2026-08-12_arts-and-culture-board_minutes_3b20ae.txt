3. HeART of Fruita Submission Review 
a. Ms. Amann reviewed the board's mission, the Fruita Arts & Culture Board, 
established in 2009, is committed to promoting, supporting, and encouraging 
arts and culture opportunities in Fruita and advancing the cultural, educational, 
and economic growth of the community. 
b. Each selected artist will receive a $1,000 honorarium, and their sculpture will be 
displayed in downtown Fruita for one year with a promotional plaque that 
includes a QR code to the artist's website. Artists are invited to the Art Walk in 
October to talk with the community about their work. 
c. The board received 17 submissions for review. There are currently five sculpture 
locations, and the board is expanding the program by three to four new 
locations, for a total of up to eight or nine sculptures. 
d. Locations include the southeast corner of Mulberry Plaza (concrete installation 
for larger pieces); three pedestals on Aspen Avenue in front of Visit Fruita and 
Kim's Auto (typically smaller pieces); the corner of the Circle and Aspen Avenue 
next to Town & Country Finance (concrete installation); two new concrete 
locations and a new pedestal on South Mesa Street; the pedestal in the Dinosaur 
 
 
Journey parking lot next to the Bark Park; and in front of the FARM (concrete 
installation). 
e. Placement of selected sculptures will be decided after scoring, based on size and 
site access; placement is not tied to score rank. Adjacent businesses will be 
presented the final selections and may voice concerns, so members were 
encouraged to include placement notes in their scoring comments. 
f. Members scored each sculpture on a scale of 1 (very dissatisfied) to 5 (very 
satisfied) in the following categories: artistic quality; site fit and context; 
community identity; durability; safety; public accessibility; and accurate 
representation. 
g. Installation feasibility was removed from the rubric, as staff will determine it 
with each artist after selection. All 17 submissions were from Grand Valley artists 
and received the one-point local bonus automatically. 
h. In response to member questions, Ms. Amann clarified that accurate 
representation should reflect whether the artist provided clear and sufficient 
photos of the full piece, since its current condition will be confirmed during the 
in-person review, and that safety should be judged on whether a piece is 
generally safe in nature, using each member's own judgment. 
i. Ms. Amann read each artist's description from their application while members 
viewed photos on screen and in their packets and completed individual scoring 
sheets, allowing roughly three to five minutes per piece. 
j. The submissions were reviewed. 
k. Members raised questions on several pieces, including whether the three-piece 
submission would need to be ground-mounted, whether The Frog and the 
Dragonfly move, how the finish on Earl would weather, what materials and base 
Voyager is built on, and climbing and safety considerations for pieces accessible 
to children. Questions the applications did not answer will be followed up with 
the artists. 
l. Ms. Amann noted that every submission could be accommodated at one of the 
available locations, with modifications to bases or pedestals where needed. 
Members also discussed that the community identity and site fit categories are 
subjective, that Fruita embraces quirky public art, and that scoring by a large 
group helps balance individual preferences. 
m. Ms. Amann collected all scoring sheets and will tally the scores the following day, 
then contact the artists of the top-scoring pieces. 
n. The board will meet on Monday, August 17, at approximately 3:00 p.m. to view 
the selected sculptures in person, confirm accurate representation, and 
determine placement. The meeting will be posted publicly. 
 
 
o. Attendees who provided their contact information on the sign-in sheet will be 
notified of the selections. The board will decide whether to announce selections 
publicly before the Art Walk; the selections will be public information. 
 
4. Adjourn 
a. The meeting was adjourned at 6:41 p.m.
