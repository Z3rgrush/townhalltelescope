1. UPDATE FROM FRUITA POLICE DEPARTMENT (6:30 – 7:40 P.M.) 
 
Police Chief Dave Krouse presented a handout of the Fruita Police Department’s Organizational Chart 
and noted that for the first time in quite a  while, his Department is currently fully staffed. He also 
presented handouts that included the number and categories of the top ten calls for service in 2025, the 
June 2026 Monthly Incident Report and Monthly  Average Response Times Report of the Grand 
Junction Regional  Communications Center  and the 2026 -2030 Fruita Police Department Strategic 
Plan. 
 
For the first time, the crime of trespassing made the list of the top ten calls for service in 2025. Krouse 
attributed this to an increase in unhoused individuals in Fruita and people breaking the curfew at City 
parks. 
 
Mayor Breman asked Chief Krouse if he could determine when the Police Department might need to 
expand and Chief Krouse said that in order to ascertain that, he would need to look at the number of 
calls for service as well as listen to his officers themselves. He added that more in-depth conversations 
will be held during the 2027 Budget discussions due to the Public Safety sales tax increase that will go 
into effect on January 1, 2027. 
 
Chief Krouse took several questions from Council members concerning factors such as staffing, levels 
of service, animal control procedures and policies and the dynamics and nuances related to increases 
in calls for services and incidents. 
 
Krouse stated that the i nformation included in the Fruita Police Department Strategic Plan has been 
taken from feedback from department staff members, the latest Community Survey and City Council. 
Areas that the Fruita Police Department wants to focus on over the next five years include keeping up 
with technology and paying attention to new legislation. Chief Krouse noted that it can be difficult to 
keep up with the latest technology such as drones, AI, robots and speed cameras, among other things. 
 
Chief Krouse also pointed out that he and his department decided a few years back not to participate 
in using flock cameras but to instead pay attention to how other jurisdictions handle it. He stated that 
those jurisdictions are currently experiencing a lot of pushback on it.

There was some brief discussion about residents’ perception of speeding vehicles on 18 Road near the 
Adobe Creek Golf Course. Chief Krouse said he would hesitate  to use a flock camera in the area and 
if an officer is placed there, it changes the behavior of drivers, so that doesn’t do much good. He added 
that the Fruita Police Department has no  other crash data for that road except the one car that drove 
into a house, which by itself is not a good demonstration of the need for enforcement of the speed limit 
in that area. 
 
Councilor Hancey noted that Mesa County has installed a  flock camera on Highway 340  and Chief 
Krouse confirmed that neither he nor his staff were made aware in advance that it was being installed. 
He added that flock cameras are very effective in pursuing criminal activity and are not meant to gather 
personal information about the general public.  
 
City Manager Shannon Vassen pointed out that staff has communicated to the residents at Adobe Falls 
Subdivision that there’s no data to support the need for  traffic calming measures  on 18 Road and in 
fact, there could even be an engineering recommendation to increase the speed limit.  
 
Councilor Purser asked if Chief Krouse has experience with any other traffic calming options. Krouse 
stated that there are  engineering responses for it , but he’s of the mind that there’s only so much 
governmental influence that’s going to affect human behavior and that there are no roads anywhere on 
which speeding hasn’t been a problem at some point.  
 
2. DISCUSSION OF THE CITY OF FRUITA AMENITY -BASED METROPOLITAN 
DISTRICT POLICY (7:40 – 9:10 P.M.) 
 
Assistant City Manager Dan Caris noted that Attorney Dalton Kelley with the law firm of Butler Snow 
was present via video and phone conference into the room.  
 
Mayor Breman began the conversation by saying that he preferred to start at a high level before getting 
down to the nuts and bolts concerning Metro Districts. 
 
Assistant City Manager Dan Caris recalled that  at last month’s Council W orkshop meeting, staff 
received direction to bring forward document s that are specific to Fruita and  aren’t examples from 
other jurisdictions.  
 
Kelley reviewed the documentation in the Council packet. He explained that the Metro District Policy 
Statement is an amenities-based approach that lines out what the City is looking for and is not intended 
to limit the Council’s direction. He said it puts developers in a position where they can check the boxes 
to confirm that the development makes sense.  He further explained that the Policy Statement guides 
developers in a framework of what should and should not be submitted.  
 
Kelley explained that the Model Service Plan  sets forth the application process such as concept and 
staff reviews but that it is the City Council who is responsible for the ultimate decisions. He pointed 
out that City Council cannot deny an application if their decision could be considered arbitrary and 
capricious. 
 
Kelley stated that Metro Districts are their own governmental entity; requests are quasi-legislative and 
operate as another government within the City of Fruita. They allow a developer to apply for tax exempt 
bonds and approval of the development comes later. 
 
  
Fruita City Council Minutes                                       3                                                       July 28, 2026 
  
 
The were numerous questions from Council  members and several said that it seemed that the Model 
Service Plan and Metro District Policy do not align, flow or correlate with each other. Council directed 
staff to create a basic flow chart of the steps and processes.  
 
Mayor Breman requested that the words “commercial, industrial (not residential)” be added to Section 
6 of the Metro District Policy.   
 
There was also some discussion concerning  administrative approval in the Metro District process, so 
Assistant City Manager Dan Caris suggested that Kelley include provisions in the City’s Model Service 
Plan for a requirement that the City Council needs to  approve Metro District applications and the 
development plans that follow. 
 
Kelley stated that he works with communities all over the state on Model Service Plans and has seen a 
lot of different proposals and feedback. He said once adopted, it would be in the best interest of the 
City to post Fruita’s Model Service Plan and Metro District Policy on its website  to encourage 
developers to help the City achieve its goals.  
 
There was discussion concerning whether the next step should be putting Metro Districts on a Regular 
meeting agenda or another workshop agenda  with some City Councilors  interested in taking it to a 
Regular Council meeting and others wanting to take it to another Workshop meeting. Mayor Breman 
made the comment that the City Council has never regretted going slow on big policy decisions.  
 
The City Council came to a consensus that the Metro District discussion would be continued to at least 
one more Council Workshop meeting when staff will present the final drafts of the documents . The 
goal for now is that it will be the final Workshop discussion on Metro Districts.  
 
Councilors Kumro and Parrish stated that they would like to hear a developer’s perspective on Metro 
Districts, but Councilor Purser expressed  discomfort with that.  Mayor Breman stated that he  would 
rather not have a developer talk to one Council member directly, but rather to all Council members as 
a group. Kelley advised that the City could include  in its Ordinance/Municipal Code that all ex parte 
communication for Metro Districts  is prohibited. All Council members were in agreement that this 
would be a good idea.  
  
After further discussion, Council directed staff to reach out to Associated Members Growth & 
Development (AMGD) and Housing & Building Association of Western Colorado (HBA) to have them 
provide written feedback concerning Metro Districts that staff can bring back to Council. 
 
Mayor Breman called for a recess at 9:12 p.m. The meeting reconvened at 9:16 p.m.  
 
3. IDEA FACTORY PROJECT (8:30– 10:00 P.M.) 
 
Communications and Engagement Manager Ciara Amann  kicked off the discussion by reviewing the 
first slides in a previously prepared presentation named “Fruita’s Urban Canopy.” The information she 
shared included: 
 
• What it means to be a Tree City USA: 
 
✓ Representation: A community Tree Board, department or position with legal authority 
and responsibility for public tree care decisions

✓ A public tree care Ordinance: Establishment of community-wide laws, ordinances or 
legally enforceable policies (Chapter 8.06 of the Fruita Municipal Code) 
 
✓ Annual expenditure of $2 per capita on community tree care activities : The 
community must document a minimum of $2 per capita annual expenditure on 
community tree care activities. Expenses can include staff time, contracted work and/or 
volunteer time. 
 
✓ An Arbor Day Observance and Proclamation:  The community must have a public 
Arbor Day Observance and have an official, annual community Proclamation issued 
and signed by the Mayor, City Council or equivalent official or representative. 
 
Amann stated that trees provide  immeasurable value; they bring us outside, cool down warm areas, 
and when there are many, they make areas need less water. Right now, Fruita has a lot of older trees 
and younger trees but not a lot in the middle. 
 
City Planner Joey Morris reviewed the remaining slides in the presentation. These included: 
 
• Planning for infill:  
 
✓ The majority of the City’s tree canopy currently relies on private trees on private 
property. As private property develops, trees either present a barrier to flexible 
development or get cut down. 
 
✓ Trees located in the right-of-way are not at risk of being cut down due to development. 
 
✓ One challenge that may present itself is getting irrigation water to newly planted trees. 
 
What is the mission? 
 
✓ Care for the trees Fruita has 
 
✓ Encourage planting more 
 
Where this could go: 
 
✓ City tree farm: Grow Fruita’s own stock over time to cut cost per tree. 
 
✓ Canopy as a priority: Treat the urban canopy as standing infrastructure. 
 
✓ A City arborist or forester: In-house expertise that knows Fruita’s trees 
 
✓ Crush our Tree City goals: Go above and beyond and lead among City’s Fruita’s size 
 
✓ Trees become an attraction: Trees bring shade, benefit wildlife and help other plants 
like grass, and attract people.

✓ Dedicated Tree Fund: Some cities have a Tree Fund dedicated solely for the purpose 
of planting and managing trees. 
 
Morris concluded the presentation by requesting that the City Council fund both a Tree Survey and the 
first round of trees in next year’s Budget. This would include a comprehensive survey of Fruita’s public 
trees and an initial allotment of trees to plant and give away  while staff also explores creative options

also been a Call for Artists to paint murals on the backs of buildings in the alley starting with the Mason

Annual Budget for these two murals . The goal is to have the artist(s) begin working on the murals in  
spring of 2027. 
 
Mayor Breman wondered if the City should adopt an Ordinance concerning  art in Fruita that could
