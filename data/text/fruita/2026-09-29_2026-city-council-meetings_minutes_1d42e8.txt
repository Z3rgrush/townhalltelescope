3. DISCUSSION OF CITY OF FRUITA AMENITY -BASED METROPOLITAN 
DISTRICT POLICY (8:54 – 9:30 P.M.) 
 
Special Counsel Dalton Kelley with Butler Snow  kicked off his presentation of drafts of the 
Metropolitan (Metro) District Policy, Model Service Plan and regulating Ordinance that were revised 
to illustrate more common threads throughout all documents. He recalled that the last time he and staff 
received feedback, it was on the Model Service Plan and Ordinance to make sure that they reflect the 
policy directions included in the Metro District Policy, which is amenities-based. 
 
He noted that there were some questions last time concerning what the best mechanism  might be for 
making sure the Metro District  Policy delivers on the  public improvements that an applicant  has 
represented that they will undertake.   
 
  
Fruita City Council Minutes                                       6                                             September 29, 2026 
  
 
Kelley stated that he and staff evaluated whether it was best to enter into an Intergovernmental 
Agreement (IGA) in the Model Service Plan or handle it when developmental approvals come through, 
but ultimately, it was decided that the improvements will be included in the Model Service Plan based 
on case law that has been upheld by Colorado courts. Kelley said that if the Metro District requires an 
improvement in a Model Service Plan, the applicant is required by law to carry out the Service Plan as 
much as is practical and are able to do it. 
 
Kelley continued that the Model Service Plan  is now set up so  that certain improvements can be 
designated as mandatory and when the Service Plan is issued , it needs to be issued for those 
improvements first before the applicant can financ e other improvements that are in the “permissible” 
category.  He stated that hopefully, Council finds this mechanism satisfactory to ensure Metro Districts 
deliver what an applicant has represented they are going to build. 
 
Kelley stated that it was a good segway into the Mayor’s question about a letter that was received by 
City Council regarding what a law firm perceive s to be legal.  He said the law firm  is trying to push 
forward the idea that somehow it’s a land use approval, but the Model Service Plan is the approval for 
the creation of a quasi -municipal governmental entity to organize within Fruita’s boundaries and has 
nothing to do with land use.  Additionally, there’s no mandatory approval criteria under Title 32, so no 
one is entitled to a Metro District coming into the City to finance development. 
 
Kelley also explained that:  
 
• The mechanism for ensuring that a Metro District actually delivers the improvements proposed 
under a Service Plan is legally permissible, 
• That the City is regulating Metro Districts as a Home Rule community, 
• That most of Title 32 by its terms do not apply to the City of Fruita, and  
• That it’s commonplace for Home Rule municipalities across the state to regulate Metro Districts 
beyond what’s in Title 32. 
 
Kelley advised that if the Council still had questions and needed further legal advice,  it could be 
provided in Executive Session, but as far as just responding to the letter, he  wanted the public to 
understand the distinction between what a landowner is allowed to do through zoning versus whether 
a quasi-judicial governmental entity has governmental powers  to provide public improvements and 
services.  
 
Vassen noted that Assistant City Manager Dan Caris  and Councilor Miller gathered feedback on the 
Metro District Policy at the A ssociated Members for Growth and Development (AMGD)  monthly 
meeting in September  of 2026 . In addition, the Metro District Policy was submitted to  the City’s 
economic development partners for review.  
 
Caris reported that about a dozen people attended the AMGD meeting, where discussion addressed 
concerns based on past Metro District problems. He said the conversation was constructive, with no 
negative feedback from attendees. Criticism he had heard elsew here focused on Fruita’s decision to 
prioritize commercial development over mixed -use Metro Districts, which he clarified was a City 
Council decision. 
 
Caris pointed out that Metro Districts are not a tool that’s commonly used in the Grand Valley and he 
finds it somewhat questionable whether or not they are something that is widely known about  within

the development community, but it seems like it has been enough of a topic in other places like Grand 
Junction and Mesa County as people have become more interested in them.  
 
Caris noted that t he class about Metro Districts followed the presentation given by him self and 
Councilor Miller and staff immediately put the information on the City’s website and made sure it was 
spread all throughout the associations. He added that he expected to receive more information from an

Caris pointed out that Fruita will be the only entity in the Valley that has done what it is doing for 
Metro Districts. He said that the City of Grand Junction has taken the approach that they will handle 
the applications as they come in, which will likely translate into quite a larger legal expense. 
 
Kelley said developers and attorneys often push for more developer -friendly Metro District plans. 
Establishing a City policy will create consistent standards and expectations for all Metro Districts, 
allowing staff to compare plans objectively, identify deviations and reduce future City costs. He added 
that it is not uncommon for jurisdictions to receive pushback from developers like Fruita has received. 
 
Mayor Breman pointed out  that the City Council has rarely regrett ed taking more time to make 
decisions in a careful and thought-out manner and doesn’t want to be pressured or rushed into adopting 
something that they are uncomfortable with. 
 
Mayor Breman asked the Council members if they had any specific concerns about Metro Districts and 
Metro District Policies.  
 
Councilor Purser expressed concern that the Metro District Policy and Ordinance allow residential uses 
in the Downtown Mixed -Use District. Kelley clarified that at least 90% of development must be

Mayor Breman pointed out that future Councils could always adjust the Policy if they saw fit  while

Vassen said it is just another tool  to encourage development and if Metro Districts can make 
development possible to achieve Council’s goals, staff recommends that the City allow them. Caris 
pointed out that since there’s nothing prohibiting people from submitting a Model Service Plan for a 
Metro District, he likes the idea of having a roadmap rather than not having one.  He noted that Fruita 
does have some properties that have infrastructure challenges and Metro Districts could be an 
incredibly helpful tool to diversify development in the City. 
 
Kelley added that in the current interest rate environment, the ability to access a tax-exempt financing 
tool for infrastructure might mean the difference between a project being built and not being built.

OTHER ITEMS (9:30 P.M.) 
 
Mayor Matthew Breman  noted that he had spoken with City Manager Shannon Vassen about the 
possibility of having Fruita City Council minutes transition ing to “ action” minutes from that of 
“summary” minutes to help simplify th e task and significantly reduce the amount of time spent in 
preparation of meeting minutes. Vassen emailed the Mayor and City Councilors an example of action 
minutes, which do not include all the discussion, but simply the following: 
 
• Meeting type, date, time, and location 
• Attendance and quorum 
• Each motion's wording, the mover and seconder, and the vote (including a roll-call breakdown 
when it isn't unanimous) 
• Resolution and ordinance numbers, dollar amounts, and contract parties 
• Start and end times for agenda items including public hearings and executive sessions 
• Adjournment time and the clerk's signature line 
 
Mayor Breman noted that with the current technology, the Council meetings are video/audio recorded 
and YouTube can transcribe them. He noted that the minutes could include links to the time-stamped 
agenda sections and agenda items that people could access if they wanted to hear all the discussion. He 
also pointed out that it would be a policy/procedure change only that wouldn’t require an Ordinance or 
a Resolution. 
 
City Manager Vassen stated that staff is also going to add a new feature called “Otter AI” that will 
produce transcripts from the YouTube video. He said that staff was recommending to Council that the 
City switch to action minutes because all the technology can now be accessed should anyone want 
further details about a Council meeting. 
 
The City Clerk estimated spending at least 25 hours on producing the minutes. It was also estimated 
that action minutes would only take the City Clerk approximately an hour to complete.  
 
Assistant City Manager Dan Caris suggested that the Planning Department switch to action minutes as 
well.  
 
Mayor Breman directed staff to include a statement at the beginning of the action minutes that explains 
where and how to access video recordings, agenda sections and agenda items of meetings. 
 
Vassen explained that Executive Sessions will still be recorded separately and held by staff for  the 
required 90-day period, meaning there won’t be any changes to them.
