4. Update from Barnhart Communications
a. Ms. Frederking reported that writer Laura Burseri has committed to a story for Travel 
Boulder on Fruita as a family-friendly Colorado destination, which will incorporate 
sensory-friendly ways to experience the museum and the community. Carrie Wilbanks 
has confirmed a trip to Fruita and will focus on Fruita as a family adventure destination. 
Additional journalists interested in visiting include freelance writer Jennifer Nied who 
plans a fall visit and will pitch Fruita as a destination off I-70, Olivia Dodson, who is 
working on fall dates, and Colin Wren who is interested in a winter or early-spring visit, 
which would support coverage outside the summer and fall seasons. Earned media 
placements so far in 2026 include Mountain Town Magazine, The Colorado Sun, 
Westword, and 9News, and Fruita events have been featured on 24 event calendars across 
Colorado.
b. All paid media was paused for 10 days in July due to the wildfire and has since 
returned to the full schedule. Paid social on Facebook and Instagram and Google Search 
continue year-round, and CTO is running a Fruita video on its site as a make-good for the 
paused period.
c. Website sessions declined in July, which was expected given the pause. Most website 
traffic comes from metro Denver, with Salt Lake City also significant. Phoenix continues 
to appear among the top markets for reasons not yet identified. Google Search 
engagement held steady despite lower spend. The branded campaign had a higher click-
through while the unbranded campaign had a more efficient cost per click and captures 
users earlier in trip planning, so it receives approximately 70% of the budget. General 
hiking terms have delivered the most clicks, followed closely by biking.
d. On Meta July ads included mountain biking, water, Dino Dig, the town video, and the 
rodeo event ad. Lower spend combined with a slightly higher cost per thousand 
impressions resulted in fewer impressions, but click-through rates held. The mountain 
bike and rodeo static ads exceeded the 1% click-through benchmark at 2.6%, and the 
video achieved a click-through rate above 4% at a higher cost. The rodeo ad was removed 
about a week and a half earlier because Rimrock Rodeo is now advertising the event 
itself, and those dollars were shifted to other ads.
e. For September, Barnhart plans to run the fall festival, mountain biking, Dino Dig, and 
outdoor video ads, all directed to the Fall Adventures landing page. Barnhart is working 
with staff to resolve an issue with that page before September 1. Barnhart will proceed 
with these ads unless the Council requests otherwise.
f. Barnhart has built three landing pages this year: Mike the Headless Chicken, Summer 
Adventures, and Fall Adventures. Leftover budget from creative shifts was used for 
website improvements, including meta descriptions, content structure, and broken link 
repairs. The blog and mountain biking were added to the main navigation, and the blog 
section has a new look.
g. Photographer/videographer Ross Bernards continues to provide photography, video, 
and organic social content. A new static ad using one of his images is pending approval 
from Ms. Amann and Mr. Pierce and, if approved, will begin running in September.
h. Barnhart has written four new blog posts this year, the current post is "Colorado's Best 
Kept Secret," and a variation of it will be used for the October newsletter. The newsletter 
has 7,853 subscribers, a decline of less than 1%. The resend feature has been successful, 
with an overall delivery rate of 98.4%; since resends began in June, the initial send 
reached 8.1% and the resend an additional 2.2%, and click-through rates have been 
steady or improving.
i. Barnhart's next marketing status meeting with Ms. Amann and Mr. Pierce is September 
2. Upcoming priorities include approval of the new static ad, confirmation of fall ads, and 
October and November newsletter topics and copy.
j. Ms. Shepardson asked that the presentation be shared with the Council, Ms. Frederking 
will send it to Ms. Amann for distribution. Ms. Frederking noted that reporters would be 
interested in covering the co-op tower project and will share the upcoming celebration 
with Barnhart's earned media team. Ms. Shepardson suggested the project would also be 
a good fit for a blog post, and Ms. Frederking noted it could be included in the newsletter, 
which reaches both residents and visitors.
5. General Reports/Staff Updates
a. Ms. Amann reported that installation of the co-op tower banner is scheduled for 
September 21 and 22, with a celebration tentatively planned during Fourth Friday on 
September 25 in Mulberry Plaza. Mr. Pierce added that the City purchased a refurbished 
vintage telescope, previously located atop the Empire State Building and at Rockefeller 
Center, which will be permanently installed in Mulberry Plaza before the celebration so 
visitors can view the tower's flower designs. It will be free to use.
b. The new artwork on the Aspen Street restroom has been installed. It is printed on a 
specialty outdoor metal that holds up better to weather and is easier to clean if 
vandalized, and carries a seven-year warranty.
c. Staff applied for a tourism marketing grant for next year to fund an earned media 
campaign pitched by Ann Keller focused on local stories and events, with the goal of 
securing major earned media placements. 
d. Staff are cleaning up the GoFruita website, including repairing broken links such as a 
link to the Veterans Memorial whose domain had been hijacked, while Barnhart updates 
the events page and landing pages. Council members asked for direct URLs to the 
seasonal landing pages, which are not linked from the main menu, so they can view them 
without clicking on paid ads; staff will provide them.
e. Staff will begin scheduling presentations from mini-grant recipients at upcoming 
meetings. Shannon has been providing training to City boards and commissions on 
meeting best practices and will present to FTAC at a future meeting.
f. Because staff will be attending the Colorado Governor's Tourism Conference and the 
September 24 regular meeting falls during Fall Fest, the Council agreed to reschedule the 
September meeting to Thursday, October 1.
g. Ms. Shepardson announced that the Museums of Western Colorado board voted earlier 
this month to consolidate its three museum locations onto the Fruita campus where 
Dinosaur Journey is located. The museum is working on a capital campaign to place a 
Brachiosaurus at Grand Junction Regional Airport directing visitors to the museum in 
Fruita, and has a long-term goal of building a larger, multidisciplinary natural history 
campus in Fruita covering paleontology, geology, cultural, transportation, and 
agricultural history. Ms. van Waveren noted the consolidation will be an added draw for 
visitors to Fruita.
6. Adjourn
The meeting was adjourned at 11:26 a.m.
