7. PUBLIC HEARINGS AND ACTION ITEMS 
Public hearings are held to obtain input from the public on various items.  Public hearings are either 
Legislative in nature or Quasi-Judicial in nature.  Public Input is limited to three minutes per person.  
People speaking should step up to the microphon e and state their name and address.  Each is 
described as follows: 
LEGISLATIVE – Legislative public hearings are held when the City Council is considering an item 
that establishes legislation such as an ordinance amending or establishing laws of the city.  
 
QUASI-JUDICIAL – Quasi-judicial public hearings are held when the City Council is acting in a 
judicial or judge like manner and a person with a legitimate interest is entitled to an impartial 
decision made on the basis of information presented and laws in effect. Quasi-judicial hearings are 
Fruita City Council Minutes 4                         September 1, 2026 
 
commonly held for land use hearings and liquor license hearings. The City Council must limit its 
consideration to matters which are placed into evidence and are part of the public record.  Quasi -
judicial hearings are held in the following manner: 
 
1. Staff presentation - Staff will present the comments and reports received from review agencies and offer a recommendation. 
2. Applicant Presentation - The petitioner  is asked to present the proposal.  Presentations should be  
brief and to the point and cover all of the main points of the project.   
3. Public Comment - (limit of 3 minutes per person) Speakers must step up to the microphone and state their name and address. Speakers 
should be to the point and try not to repeat the points others have made. 
4. Staff and/or applicant(s) respond to public comment. 
5. Council will ask questions of staff and applicant. 
6. The public hearing is closed to public comments. 
7. Make a motion.  A member of the City Council will make a motion on the issue. 
8. Following a second to the motion, the City Council may discuss the motion. 
9. Vote.  The City Council will then vote on the motion. 
ORDINANCES - After introduction of an Ordinance (First Reading), a public hearing date is set and 
notice of the hearing is published in the newspaper.  Staff presents the ordinance on Second Reading 
and the hearing is opened to the public for public input.  After comments from the public, the Mayor 
will close the hearing and bring the Ordinance back to the City Council for discussion and potential 
action.  The Council will make a motion to approve the Ordinance or take no action.  In the event the 
ordinance is approved, it will become effective 30 days after adoption. 
ACTION ITEMS – Action items are items that require Council action and have public comment.

Hemphill explained that a facilitator is a licensed clinical pract itioner that has met certain requirements 
and who are regulated by the State of Colorado to administer Natural Medicine or regulated natural 
medicine products. He noted that there are currently seven (7) licensed facilitators in the Grand Valley, 
mainly located in the City of Grand Junction. 
 
Hemphill also explained that a participant that receives natural medicine services is under supervision of 
the facilitator the entire time and there are certain steps in the process in which a facilitator can administer 
the natural medicine products. 
 
Hemphill advised that municipalities cannot unreasonably regulate natural medicine types of land uses or 
natural medicine businesses. The state has given municipalities the discretion to regulate time, place and 
manner through their Municipal/Land Use Codes. The proposed Land Use Table reflects that a Natural 
Medicine Business can be conditionally allowed in the Commercial-1 (C-1) and Commercial-2 (C-2) Zone 
Districts and allowed in the I ndustrial (I) Zone District. This aligns with current provisions in the Land 
Use Table for the manufacture of other products. It is proposed that City of Fruita staff and Planning 
Commission administratively review any Conditional Use Permit  (CUP) application for a Natural 
Fruita City Council Minutes 6                         September 1, 2026 
 
Medicine Business in the C-1 and C-2 Zone Districts and apply any additional conditions if the business 
is not compatible with surrounding land uses.   
 
Hemphill further stated that a Natural Medicine Healing Center would be like a dental clinic or medical 
office that operates in a similar regulatory framework and as proposed, would be conditionally allowed in 
the Downtown Mixed -Use (DMU), C-1 and Neighborhood Commercial Overlay  (NCO) Zones, but 
allowed as a use “by right” in the C-2 and I Zone Districts.  
 
Hemphill explained that staff looked at other Municipal Codes within the State of Colorado and they have 
the same allowed and conditionally allowed uses as what was being proposed to Council.  Those Codes 
treat allowed uses for a Natural Medicine Healing Center as very similar to dental and medical clinics.  
 
The City of Grand Junction’s Municipal Code places manufacturing or production type of businesses in 
their I Zone District, therefore, staff feels that the City of Fruita should stay consistent  with other Codes 
in the Grand Valley. 
 
Councilor Hancey asked Hemphill to explain the difference between the C-1, C-2 and NCO Zone Districts. 
Hemphill stated that the C-1 Zone District is located along Highway 6 & 50 while the C-2 Zone District 
is located in south Fruita, which contains hotels, medical offices and fast-food restaurants. The C-2 Zone 
is very flexible in nature; they allow many different commercial land uses while the C-1 contains more of 
a residential component.   
 
Hemphill also explained that the NCO  allows for some additional flexibility  in an area that’s primarily 
focused on Fruita’s downtown with lighter land uses such as realtors’ offices (like Lori Chesnick’s office 
on Peach Street) and coffee shops (such as Bestslope Coffee Company on Peach Street). These uses may 
be a little more compatible with a residential land use, but staff wants to see downtown Aspen Avenue be 
more pedestrian-oriented. 
 
Hemphill continued that a CUP allows staff to go through a land use process to apply conditions in order 
to meet compatibility requirements that would otherwise not be seen as more of an allowed use. A relevant 
example would be a childcare facility such as Little Imaginations on Aspen Avenue and Maple Street that 
required a CUP because a childcare facility may or may not be an adequate land use in that specific area.  
CUPs allow staff to analyze compatibility issues that may or may not come up and are unforeseeable and 
staff can apply conditions on those land uses to add in more control of the area and minimize impacts on 
the public. 
 
Hemphill explained that many of the text amendments included in Section 5 were copied from other 
municipalities. He noted for the record that pursuant to state statute, no Natural Medicine Business  or 
Natural Medicine Healing Center  shall operate out of a building that is within 1,000 feet of a school or 
childcare facility. He added that all storage for a Natural Medicine Business or Natural Medicine Healing 
Center must be located inside of a permanent building  and not within sheds, outbuildings, cars, tents or 
trailers to ensure more of a higher level of security for the products themselves. 
 
Hemphill added that Natural Medicine Businesses shall provide secure disposal of any natural medicine 
or natural medicine products or byproducts; they cannot just put them inside a trash bag and throw it out. 
The State of Colorado provided some language with regards to that which can be used in municipal 
regulations.

A. RESOLUTION FHA 2026-02 – APPROVING AND RATIFYING AMENDMENTS TO 
THE BYLAWS OF THE FRUITA HOUSING AUTHORITY  – SPECIAL COUNSEL  
DALTON KELLEY 
Special Counsel to the Fruita Housing Authority (FHA) Dalton Kelley (in attendance virtually) with Butler 
Snow reviewed the proposed amendments to the Authority’s Bylaws, the purpose of which were to provide 
for the continued effective administration and operation of the Authority . He explained that when the 
Housing Authority was first established, it was with a limited purpose as the Authority began to participate 
in abatement programs for property tax  and sales tax exemptions  through Low -Income Housing Tax 
Credit (LIHTC). Since then, the Housing Authority has begun to participate with the City of Fruita in 
different types of housing projects to help the City meet its affordable housing needs.   
The proposed amendments revise Article I, Section 2 (Purpose) of the Bylaws to align the Authority's 
stated purposes with the City  of Fruita’s adopted Housing Action Plan and Housing Needs Assessment. 
The amendments broaden the Authority's tax -abatement and partnership language, add a purpose clause 
authorizing the Authority to assist in the planning, development, and redevelopment of the City's housing 
stock, and add a general clause authorizing the Authority to do those things authorized by City Council 
and the Colorado Housing Authorities Law. There were no other updates aside from those in the Purpose 
Section of the Bylaws.

B. RESOLUTION FHA 2026 -03 – APPROVING THE FIRST AMENDMENT TO THE 
GROUND LEASE OPTION  AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE FRUITA HOUSING 
AUTHORITY AND INDIBUILD, LLC – SPECIAL COUNSEL DALTON KELLEY 
Special Counsel to the Fruita Housing Authority (FHA) Dalton Kelley (in attendance virtually) with Butler 
Snow stated that the Resolution before the Authority was a follow -up item to a request that the board 
heard last month from IndiBuild, LLC, which was to be granted an extension of their Ground Lease Option 
Fruita City Council Minutes 16                         September 1, 2026 
 
for two more grant cycles. The Resolution would approve an amendment to the Option Agreement by 
changing the “Condition Precedent” from the 2026 LIHTC Cycle to accommodate an application for 9% 
LIHTC in February of 2027 and February of 2028. 
Kelley explained that if IndiBuild, LLC does not receive a 9% credit in February of 2028, once that news 
is received, the Ground Lease Option automatically terminates.  
Kelley further explained that the way the current agreement works is that after IndiBuild didn’t receive 
the LITHC award, they could come back to the FHA and present some alternative structures to maintain 
their Option. With the amendment, IndiBuild would have the opportunity to apply in February of 2027 
and February of 2028, but if they want to propose any alternatives, they would need to do it simultaneously 
with those applications because it does terminate once IndiBuild receives that news. 
Kelley stated that the Housing Authority retains the discretion if there is an alternative presented that it 
wants to pursue to extend the Ground Lease Option, but the big change is that the Option  Agreement 
would automatically terminate if IndiBuild doesn’t receive a LIHTC award by February of 2028. 
Kelley noted that there was an initial $2,000 payment for the initial Option  and for the first Amendment, 
there would be a payment for the extension for the February 2027 9% LIHTC Cycle and if IndiBuild does 
not receive it in the 2027 Cycle, they would have to pay an Option payment to basically get the second 
year through the 2028 Cycle. He point ed out that those amounts have not yet been set unless the Vice 
Chair had a suggestion, so it would need to be filled in in the Agreement. 
If IndiBuild were to receive a LIHTC award in February of 2028 but needed to meet the other “Conditions 
Precedent” (things like getting their easements and Planning and Development approval), they could pay 
the extension  of $2,000 to basically buy the time to get the other “Conditions Precedent” done. That 
assumes they have the financing in place  through the 9% LIHTC program and are working on their 
development approvals. Kelley advised that this will make the date correspond with the 2028 Cycle. 
Commissioner Purser asked for confirmation that the Agreement wouldn’t terminate in February of 2028, 
it will terminate once they receive the decision on their application of February 2028. Kelley responded 
that that was correct and recalled that the award announcement occurred in May last year, so it should be 
around that same time again this year when IndiBuild will learn of the decision.  
Commissioner Prochnow  referred to Section 4 of the First Amendment to the Ground Lease Option 
Agreement where it relates to the Option Payment and asked who, when and how the amount  of the 
payment is determined. 
Assistant Executive Director Dan Caris stated that in the original Agreement,  the payment in the event 
that IndiBuild was awarded a 9% LIHTC was $150,000 , so he thinks part of the reason Kelley left the 
amounts blank for 2027 and for 2028 is that it was set up as if it were a menu because the automatic one-
year extension was actionable based off the $2,000 payment.  He said the concern has always been if it 
has gone too far into the timeline, whether the Housing Authority will be able to  pivot and find another 
partner to move the project forward. Caris stated that it was up to the Authority as far as that’s concerned 
and he thinks that the way that it worked before is that the Option Payments were set up to get more 
escalatory based off the fact that the real estate is being tied up for longer. 
Kelley responded that there are two things: the extension of the Option to keep it open and then the actual 
payment for the exercise of the Option. The exercise of the Option is the $150,000 that Caris mentioned 
Fruita City Council Minutes 17                         September 1, 2026 
 
that is basically IndiBuild paying rent under the Ground Lease upfront. He said the extension of the Option 
is basically IndiBuild’s payment as consideration for the opportunity to have the additional cycles in which 
to apply and because the property was purchased with a land banking grant, the Authority has ten years to 
meet the requirements of the grant or there is a claw back of the funds. Kelley said understanding that as 
time goes by, the City/Authority is starting to get more exposure on the claw back and so, the thought 
process was that there should be something to pay for keeping the Option open; there’s value to the 
Authority’s ability to feel comfortable keeping it open for two years while losing time if it isn’t successful. 
He reassured the Authority that the $150,000 (10 years for rent for 15 years) IndiBuild paid to exercise 
the Option hasn’t changed and the sooner the Authority can get it, the more it helps to deploy it to other 
projects.
