SPEAKER’S PROTOCOL 
PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD & PUBLIC HEARINGS 
 
The Spring Hill Governing Body encourages citizen participation in the local governance process and wants 
to ensure that all interested parties have an opportunity to be heard at public meetings. To that end, the 
Governing Body has determined that procedur es are needed to ensure fair and efficient meetings are 
conducted in an ethical, civil, and cordial matter. The purpose of the Public Comment period is to provide 
an opportunity for members of the public to provide information to the Governing Body; the Pu blic 
Comment Period is not an open public forum, but rather a limited forum for citizens to provide 
information to the Governing Body. Members of the Governing Body should not engage in discussion or 
debate with speakers offering Public Comment.  
 
Therefore, the Governing Body adopts the following procedures regarding the conduct of public meetings. 
 
Persons desiring to address the Governing Body, or board, commission, or committee, during the Public 
Comment Period shall place their name on the speaker’s list via telephone, e-mail, or by personally signing 
it prior to the public meeting.  The speaker’s  list(s) shall be maintained by the Office of the City Clerk or 
Board Secretary and shall be posted no less than one-half (1/2) hour prior to the meeting. 
 
Speakers will be called in the order they appear on the list for each topic.  Persons unable to place their 
name on the speaker’s list may be allowed to address the Governing Body or Board after recognition by 
the meeting chairperson. 
 
Always speak from the lectern, using the microphone. This ensures that all remarks will be accurately 
recorded. Speakers are requested to state their full name, address, and group affiliation if related to their 
comments or if acting as a representative of an entity, organization, or group, if any, before delivering any 
remarks. Speakers should restate their name each time subsequent remarks are made. If unable to speak 
from the lectern, accommodations can be provided. A speaker granted a current Protection  from Abuse 
or Protection from Stalking Order shall not have to state his or her address.     
  
Persons wishing to address the Governing Body or Board for general issues or concerns during Citizen 
Participation on items not under consideration within the agenda shall be limited to a maximum of five 
(5) minutes, provided an extension of time may be granted, by formal motion. If a large number of people 
seek to make comments, the Governing Body or other Board may limit the time to three (3) minutes per 
speaker to ensure that all those who wish to speak are provided an opportunity. Speakers may not give,  
donate, or yield their time to speak to extend another person’s time. Comments during Citizen 
Participation are to be directed to the Chair. Staff will respond to specific issues if so  directed by the 
Mayor. 
 
Speakers are expected to conduct themselves in a civil manner. The following topics and types of 
comments are prohibited: 
 
Political campaigning; 
Commercial advertisement or hawking; 
Personal attacks, threats, or slanderous remarks that are unrelated to official conduct or qualifications; 
Profanity; 
 
Disclosure of identifying, locating, or sharing of private information whether concerning elected or 
appointed officials, City employees, or members of the public. 
 
To protect privacy interests of all parties involved, the Governing Body shall not hear personal complaints 
concerning an employee during Public Comment on any matter related to his or her employment with the 
City. Individual personnel matters should be re ferred to City administration who may report to the 
Governing Body in executive session, as appropriate. Policy matters regarding employment shall be 
addressed in open session.  
 
If a number of  persons are aligned on an agenda topic, they may select one (1) spokesperson for an 
applicant, party request or proposing the item, or on behalf of an agenda topic and one (1) spokesperson 
for the opposition who shall be allotted ten (10) minutes. Other s peakers, including unaligned or 
otherwise unrepresented persons, may speak for up to five (5) minutes each as determined by the 
Governing Body or Board depending upon the number of speakers.  
 
The Governing Body and City Boards conduct public hearings from time to time as required by law or as 
part of consideration of certain matters prior to passage, adoption, or approval of an item. Persons who 
wish to address the Governing Body or a Board dur ing a Public Hearing are not required to sign up or to 
provide advance notice of the intent to appear and comment on the subject of the Public Hearing.   
 
Persons wishing to address the Governing Body in a public hearing may make comments for five (5) 
minutes; provided that, a spokesperson for an applicant or group of applicants or petitioners may speak 
for up to ten (10) minutes, and a spokesperson for an a ligned group in opposition to the application or 
petition under consideration, if so designated, shall also be permitted to speak for up to ten (10) minutes. 
Speakers may not otherwise give, donate, or yield their time to speak to extend another person’s time.  
 
If you have documentation or other materials to present to the Governing Body or Board, please provide 
them to the City Clerk or Board Secretary for distribution and filing with the official record of the meeting. 
If possible, a total of eight (8) copies of any document should be provided. 
 
The City of Spring Hill does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, gender, sexual 
orientation, religion, age, disability, genetic information, membership or service in the military, or other 
characteristics prohibited by federal, state, or local law, rule, or regulation, including Title II of the 
Americans with Disabilities Act. The City will provide reasonable accommodation to individual’s possessing 
a disability upon request. A request for reasonable accommodation for disability, or other assistance, 
should be made, if possible, at least three (3) days in advance, to the Office of the City Clerk at (913) 592-
3664 or cityclerk@springhillks.gov.  
 
The Governing Body welcomes your participation and appreciates your cooperation. Meeting agendas 
and minutes are available via the City website at www.springhillks.gov or by contacting the Office of the 
City Clerk. Meetings are also available for viewing v ia live streaming on YouTube. Additional details are 
available on the City of Spring Hill website.

DISCUSSION ITEM 
10. Development Applications Public Feedback 
Mr. Mallon proposed implementing modifications to the planning processes to provide additional 
clarity and transparency.  
 
The modifications discussed included: 
• Improving access to public records request and development applications. 
• Establishing a policy highly encouraging developers to hold neighborhood meetings with 
surrounding property owners prior to Planning Commission public hearings or City Council 
meetings.  
• Creating additional avenues for the public to provide feedback on proposed development 
applications to better reach all audiences, including residents and businesses, in order to 
gather more well-rounded input. 
• Working with developers and consultants to provide clearer, easier to read plans as part of 
application materials and presentations to the Planning Commission and City Council. 
 
After discussion, the Governing Body reached consensus to move forward with this direction to 
enhance transparency and directed staff to prepare any necessary policies to implement these 
changes.

The City previously hosted a public meeting in February 2026 and a work session in July 2026.  The purpose 
of tonight’s meeting is to provide a status  of the wastewater treatment facility , provide a financial 
overview, and impact to rate pa yers.  At the conclusion of the presentation, the public will have an 
opportunity to ask questions and provide comments. 
 
As staff previously presented in the July work session, the existing wastewater treatment plant has a 
capacity of 1.25 million gallons per day (mgd) and is currently running at 71% capacity.  With future growth 
projections, the existing plant will be at capacity in 3 years.

In 2024, Lamp Rynearson completed a service area study to review alternatives related to the wastewater 
plant.  Option 1 includes an expansion to the existing plant from 1.25 to 2.5 mgd.   Option 2 includes the 
construction of a new plant at the confluence of Ten Mile and Sweetwater Creeks.  Option 3 includes the 
construction of a new plant at a location 8 miles south of the existing plant .  In October 2025, t he City 
purchased the 160-acre property in the approximate location of Option 2. 
 
In 2025, Lamp Rynearson completed an Anti-Degradation Study to determine the treatment limits for a 
new treatment plant.  As the conceptual plan is finalized, the Anti-Degradation Study will be updated and 
submitted to the Kansas Department of Health & Environment for final approval. 
 
Growth projections charts shows a reasonable growth in future years.  Th e flow projections show the 
anticipated flows from future sewer mains that are currently under design and construction.  This also 
includes the additional in Bucyrus and Olathe  residents that will be served with these future 
improvements.   
 
In October 2025, the City engaged a design-build team of Crossland Heavy and Black & Veatch.  The  City 
staff and project team toured several wastewater treatment plants in the region, the information 
collected we could be used for the design of our future facility.   
 
During the first design workshop, the City and project team developed a list of goals associated with this 
project.  These goals include “Get it Built” and providing expandability.  These goals are reviewed regularly.   
 
The initial design started with a 2.5 mgd plant, with the ability to expand up to 40 mgd.  Once the 
workshops concluded, the contractor provided cost estimates for the conceptual design.  The cost of the 
conceptual design was outside the budget available.  In subsequent discussions, there were several 
elements to the plant that were value engineered to bring the project within the project budget. 
 
With the value engineering exercise, the City staff and project team looked at several alternatives.  Option 
1 revisited expanding the existing plant.  That option was quickly dismissed due to the limited capacity .  
Option 2 included the construction of a 2.5 mgd plant with a future expansion to 5 mgd.  Option 3 included 
the construction of a 5 mgd plant all at once.  The costs are more economical with Option 3, there would 
be additional costs and inflation in the future  with Options 1 and 2 .  Staff recommends proceeding with 
Option 3. 
 
This is a progressive design build process.  The design team is currently working under a professional 
services agreement.  There will be future actions for City Council as we move through to a design build 
contract with at least two guaranteed maximum pricing targets.  City Council will have several 
opportunities through the life of this project for discussions and approvals.

The State of Kansas has State Revolving Fund (SRF) loans available to assist with our project.  There is $30 
million this year and $30 million next year in SRF loans available for our project .  The current SRF rate is 
3.31% and spans 20 -30 years, with the ability to defer the initial payment  until on e year after final 
completion. 
 
The federal government has Water Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act (WIFIA) program loans 
available.  The current WIFIA rate is 5% and spans 30 years, with the ability to defer the initial payment 
for 5 years.  The loan can be tailored to our needs.   
 
To date, the City has spent $4 million in cash to cover the initial engineering fees and real estate 
acquisition.  The City will issue bonds to repay the City for this debt.  The current rate for revenue bonds 
is 5.2%.  There is currently $7 million available in the sewer fund.   The anticipated project cost is $140 
million, with an anticipated payback of $333 million in principal and interest payments.   
 
Ms. Dunn presented a chart showing the current sewer rates for surrounding communities.  Compared to 
other communities, Spring Hill’s rate are in line.  The City is preparing to engage a consultant to perform 
a rate study to review user fees. 
 
It is anticipated that sewer users will pay more compared to JCW, to have a treatment plan in the first 15 
years.  After Year 23 and above, JCW’s rates are projected be above ours.

Interim City Administrator Mallon explained the purpose of the Concept Review discussion, noting that it 
provides the Governing Body with a n opportunity to respond to an initial idea or concept from a 
developer. He presented the general guidelines for the discussion, emphasizing that it is strictly informal 
and does not constitute a final decision or formal approval. The City Attorney reiterated that the City 
Council is not making any binding decisions during this process.

Mr. Mallon introduced Matt Adams  of Matt Adams Development Company, the d eveloper for the 
proposed project at 191st & Woodland. Mr. Adams presented a conceptual plan for the development, 
which includes dividing the area into two subdivisions. He reviewed the proposed lot sizes, home price 
points, landscaping plans, and subdivision amenities. 
 
His attorney, Curt Petersen, outlined potential financial incentive options for the project and stated 
that establishing an RHID would be the most suitable mechanism for elevating the quality of the 
development. 
 
18. Property at the Southwest Corner of 199th and Ridgeview – Arise Homes 
On behalf of Arise Homes, Brian Hamm presented the conceptual plan for the proposed project at the 
southwest corner of 199 th Street and Ridgeview Road. The Master Plan includes four acres of 
commercial development, 60 attached homes (duplex -six-plex), and 100 detached homes, and is 
holistically designed without a pool. He noted that the homes are slab-on-grade construction and use 
real brick or stone to help reduce long-term maintenance costs. 
 
His attorney, Curt Petersen, outlined the proposed financial incentives for the project , including an  
Industrial Revenue Bond, 50% property tax abatement for 10 years, and a Community Improvement 
District for the commercial site that would add a 1% sales tax. 
 
19. Solicitors

enforcement, permits, restrictions, and designated hours for soliciting. The Chief explained that if a 
violation occurs, a reporting party is required to testif y in court. Following discussion, the Chief will 
contact neighboring communities regarding their processes  and will present the information at a 
future meeting.

Project Understanding 
Each year in which annexations occur, K.S.A. 12-518 requires the City to adopt and file a resolution describing its 
corporate limits by December 31. In 2026 the City annexed land under Ordinance Nos. 2026 -07, 2026-08, 2026-09, 
2026-10, 2026-14 and 2026-15, together with the ordinance annexing the Mills property in Section 5, Township 15 
South, Range 24 East. Lamp Rynearson will incorporate these annexations into the corporate limits description 
adopted by Resolution No. 2025-R-27 and prepare the 2026 resolution for adoption. 
Scope of Services 
1. Review the 2026 annexation ordinances, their recorded exhibits, and Resolution No. 2025 -R-27. 
2. Prepare a revised legal description of the corporate limits in resolution form, incorporating the 2026 
annexations, prepared and sealed by a Kansas Professional Surveyor.  
3. Prepare an exhibit map of the corporate limits identifying the 2026 annexations by ordinance 
number. 
4. Address one round of consolidated comments from the City Attorney and the Johnson County and 
Miami County tax roll reviewers. 
5. Coordinate with City staff on the filing package required under K.S.A. 12-518. 
Assumptions and Exclusions 
• All 2026 annexation ordinances, are adopted and recorded and will be furnished by the City.  
• Where an ordinance annexes “adjacent right-of-way” without describing it, the City will confirm the 
intended extent before the description is finalized. 
• No field survey is included; the description is a record retracement. 
• Additional revision cycles, or descriptions for annexations adopted after Notice to Proceed, are 
additional services billed at the hourly rates below. 
 
 
 

 
Schedule 
Draft resolution and exhibit to the City within three weeks of receipt of all recorded ordinances; final 
documents within one week of receipt of consolidated comments. The schedule allows for adoption at a 
regular Council meeting before December 31, 2026.
