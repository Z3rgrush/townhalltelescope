Mayor Chad Young opened the hearing. 
 
City Engineer Alysen Abel provided an overview of the agenda.   
 
The City previously hosted a public meeting in February 2026 and a work session in July 2026.  The purpose 
of tonight’s meeting is to provide a status of the wastewater treatment facility, provide a financial 
overview, and impact to rate payers.  At the co nclusion of the presentation, the public will have an 
opportunity to ask questions and provide comments. 
 
As staff previously presented in the July work session, the existing wastewater treatment plant has a 
capacity of 1.25 million gallons per day (mgd) and is currently running at 71% capacity.  With future growth 
projections, the existing plant will be at capacity in 3 years.

In 2024, Lamp Rynearson completed a service area study to review alternatives related to the wastewater 
plant.  Option 1 includes an expansion to the existing plant from 1.25 to 2.5 mgd.  Option 2 includes the 
construction of a new plant at the confluence of Ten Mile and Sweetwater Creeks.  Option 3 includes the 
construction of a new plant at a location 8 miles south of the existing plant.  In October 2025, the City 
purchased the 160-acre property in the approximate location of Option 2. 
 
In 2025, Lamp Rynearson completed an Anti -Degradation Study to determine the treatment limits for a 
new treatment plant.  As the conceptual plan is finalized, the Anti-Degradation Study will be updated and 
submitted to the Kansas Department of Health & Environment for final approval. 
 
Growth projections charts shows a reasonable growth in future years.  The flow projections show the 
anticipated flows from future sewer mains that are currently under design and construction.  This also 
includes the additional in Bucyrus and Olathe residen ts that will be served with these future 
improvements.   
 
In October 2025, the City engaged a design-build team of Crossland Heavy and Black & Veatch.  The City 
staff and project team toured several wastewater treatment plants in the region, the information 
collected we could be used for the design of our future facility.   
 
During the first design workshop, the City and project team developed a list of goals associated with this 
project.  These goals include “Get it Built” and providing expandability.  These goals are reviewed regularly.   
 
The initial design started with a 2.5 mgd plant, with the ability to expand up to 40 mgd.  Once the 
workshops concluded, the contractor provided cost estimates for the conceptual design.  The cost of the 
conceptual design was outside the budget available.  In subsequent discussions, there were several 
elements to the plant that were value engineered to bring the project within the project budget. 
 
With the value engineering exercise, the City staff and project team looked at several alternatives.  Option 
1 revisited expanding the existing plant.  That option was quickly dismissed due to the limited capacity.  
Option 2 included the construction of a 2.5 mgd plant with a future expansion to 5 mgd.  Option 3 included 
the construction of a 5 mgd plant all at once.  The costs are more economical with Option 3, there would 
be additional costs and inflation in the future with Options 1 and 2.  Staff recomme nds proceeding with 
Option 3. 
 
This is a progressive design build process.  The design team is currently working under a professional 
services agreement.  There will be future actions for City Council as we move through to a design build 
contract with at least two guaranteed maximum pricing targets.  City Council will have several 
opportunities through the life of this project for discussions and approvals. 
 
Finance Director Rhonda Dunn provided background with the loans available.   
 
The State of Kansas has State Revolving Fund (SRF) loans available to assist with our project.  There is $30 
million this year and $30 million next year in SRF loans available for our project.  The current SRF rate is

3.31% and spans 20 -30 years, with the ability to defer the initial payment until one year after final 
completion. 
 
The federal government has Water Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act (WIFIA) program loans 
available.  The current WIFIA rate is 5% and spans 30 years, with the ability to defer the initial payment 
for 5 years.  The loan can be tailored to our needs.   
 
To date, the City has spent $4 million in cash to cover the initial engineering fees and real estate 
acquisition.  The City will issue bonds to repay the City for this debt.  The current rate for revenue bonds 
is 5.2%.  There is currently $7 million available in the sewer fund.  The anticipated project cost is $140 
million, with an anticipated payback of $333 million in principal and interest payments.   
 
Ms. Dunn presented a chart showing the current sewer rates for surrounding communities.  Compared to 
other communities, Spring Hill’s rate are in line.  The City is preparing to engage a consultant to perform 
a rate study to review user fees. 
 
It is anticipated that sewer users will pay more compared to JCW, to have a treatment plan in the first 15 
years.  After Year 23 and above, JCW’s rates are projected be above ours. 
 
The purpose of tonight’s meeting is to provide the public an opportunity to speak.   
 
Mayor Young opened the floor for public comment. 
 
Bill Peterman – Mr. Peterman expressed concern that the City does not have enough revenue to pay the 
interest.  He suggested constructing the smaller plant.

Jason O’Kane – Are we taking on additional customers?

DISCUSSION 
Interim City Administrator Mallon explained the purpose of the Concept Review discussion, noting that it 
provides the Governing Body with a n opportunity to respond to an initial idea or concept from a 
developer. He presented the general guidelines for the discussion, emphasizing that it is strictly informal 
and does not constitute a final decision or formal approval. The City Attorney reiterated that the City 
Council is not making any binding decisions during this process.  
 
17. Development Concept Review – 191st and Woodland 
Mr. Mallon introduced Matt Adams  of Matt Adams Development Company, the d eveloper for the 
proposed project at 191st & Woodland. Mr. Adams presented a conceptual plan for the development, 
which includes dividing the area into two subdivisions. He reviewed the proposed lot sizes, home price 
points, landscaping plans, and subdivision amenities. 
 
His attorney, Curt Petersen, outlined potential financial incentive options for the project and stated 
that establishing an RHID would be the most suitable mechanism for elevating the quality of the 
development. 
 
18. Property at the Southwest Corner of 199th and Ridgeview – Arise Homes 
On behalf of Arise Homes, Brian Hamm presented the conceptual plan for the proposed project at the 
southwest corner of 199 th Street and Ridgeview Road. The Master Plan includes four acres of 
commercial development, 60 attached homes (duplex -six-plex), and 100 detached homes, and is 
holistically designed without a pool. He noted that the homes are slab-on-grade construction and use 
real brick or stone to help reduce long-term maintenance costs. 
 
His attorney, Curt Petersen, outlined the proposed financial incentives for the project , including an  
Industrial Revenue Bond, 50% property tax abatement for 10 years, and a Community Improvement 
District for the commercial site that would add a 1% sales tax. 
 
19. Solicitors 
Mr. Mallon provided an overview of the regulations for solicitors within Spring Hill and discussed 
enforcement, permits, restrictions, and designated hours for soliciting. The Chief explained that if a 
violation occurs, a reporting party is required to testif y in court. Following discussion, the Chief will 
contact neighboring communities regarding their processes  and will present the information at a 
future meeting.
