2027 Budget Notes 
November 4, 2026 
 
General Reminder: Some expenditure estimates may go up and some may go down slightly between 
this final draft of the budget and the final budget for adoption. The same applies to revenue. This is 
simply because we want the 2026 estimates to be as accurate as possible. If expenditure estimates 
exceed the 2026 budget, a supplemental budget (budget amendment) is required.  
Regardless of slight increases or decreases, a supplemental budget is not needed for the 2026 budget. 
Morrison operates under a fund-level budgetary control, meaning a formal budget amendment is only 
necessary if total expenditures are expected to exceed the fund’s authorized appropriation.  
Townwide Compensation & Benefits  
• After looking into this further, I realized that the cost of the Board's proposed additional 1% 
retirement match would be the same as increasing the Town's contribution by 1% across the board 
if all eligible employees participate. The actual cost will depend on how many employees elect to 
contribute enough to receive the additional match. For budgeting purposes, I used a 3.5% employer 
contribution rate for all eligible employees as a middle-ground estimate rather than assuming full 
participation at 4%. 
• The important thing will be coordinating with CRA to update the contribution structure correctly, 
ensuring the additional 1% is treated as a match rather than an automatic increase for all 
employees. 
All Departments 
• I reviewed the workers’ compensation insurance inconsistencies in the 2026 estimated budget. I 
prepared a recode list so it can be fixed in the system. Those changes are reflected in this draft.  
• We should have an agreement for the Town Attorney to review for changing our Xpress Bill Way 
structure. The change will require customers to pay the fees rather than the Town. We will send out 
ample notice before it goes into effect. 
• When we send out those communications, we will include the mailing fee and encourage customers 
to sign up for online billing. 
GENERAL FUND 
General Fund Revenue 
• There are a few cells in the general fund revenue spreadsheet that are red. This is because the 
revenue was coded to a different line item than in the approved budget. I have started a list of 
recodes so this can be adjusted for the final document.  
Admin 
• The wages were updated, which resulted in a large increase for the Admin budget.  
• I took the bank fees from the Court budget and split $13,000 between Water and Sewer, since the 
majority is from utility bills. The remainder was put in admin to house planning, building, permit, 
etc. bank fees. 
Board 
• I looked into the large CIRSA increase for property and casualty insurance. The operating expenses 
went up substantially for the amount covered. In the past we used the operating expenses for both 
funds. However, it is supposed to be for operational and capital expenses.  
• CIRSA does an end of year audit to look at actual expenses vs what was budgeted for. The Town 
receives a credits for any overpayment.   
Court 
• I removed the bank fees, as noted in the Admin section. 
Planning 
• I created a new line item in planning for all consultant expenses for the 16 acres/ East Planning Area. 
I put $50,000 in 2027. Accordingly, I removed this from the capital budget. 
Museum 
• MNHM is looking into several changes, including their point-of-sale system. That could include a 
better payment structure with improved functionality. They are incredibly mindful of their 
expenditures and want to limit their fiscal impact. I recommend the proposed amount for bank fees 
remain while they explore other options.  
General Fund Capital Projects / Expenses 
• I updated the Town Hall security improvements to $5,000. This may not be a capital expense, but I 
don’t think there is any harm having it on the list.  
• I reduced the Town maintenance shed relocation to $25,000.  
UTILITY FUND

Honorable Mayor, and Trustees,  
 
Pursuant to the Town of Morrison Home Rule Charter and applicable Colorado law, I submit the 
proposed (draft) 2027 budget for the Board of Trustees' review and consideration.  
 
The budget outlines Town operations and capital improvements for the 2027 calendar year. The 
budget, as presented, will allow the Town to achieve many of its goals and priorities in a cost-
effective and efficient manner.  
 
Budget Basis  
All governmental fund budgets are prepared using the modified accrual basis of accounting. 
Under this method, revenues are recognized when susceptible to accrual, that is, measurable and 
available. Available means collectible in the current period or soon enough thereafter to be used 
to pay liabilities of the current period. Expenditures, other than interest on long-term obligations 
that are recorded when due, are recognized when they are expected to be liquidated with 
expendable available resources.   All proprietary fund budgets are also prepared using the 
modified accrual basis of accounting. Although this basis is not consistent with GAAP for 
enterprise funds, it is common practice among municipalities and is acceptable to our auditors.  
 
The budget encompasses two funds, the General Fund, and the Utility Enterprise Fund. The 
revenue and expenditure summaries for both funds, as well as budget highlights, are summarized 
below.  
 
General Fund  
Revenues in the General Fund are projected at $4,421,440 for FY27. Revenues in the General 
Fund estimates for FY26 are $4,329,672. General Fund revenues come from a variety of sources, 
including property tax, sales taxes, use taxes, fees, and charges for services.  
 
▪ Sales tax is the single largest source of revenue for the Town. General Fund sales tax 
collections are projected at $2,200,000. This is a zero-growth projection from 2026.   
▪ Traditionally, the second largest source of tax revenue for the Town is property tax. 
The mill levy rate for Morrison is 6.746. In 2026, Morrison is projecting property tax 
revenues of $110,901. This is a decrease from the prior year. 
▪ It is anticipated that in 2027, the second highest source of tax revenue will become 
lodging tax, with an estimate of $200,000. 
 
Expenditures in the General Fund are projected at $2,534,767 for FY27. Expenditures in the 
approved FY26 budget were $4,028,156, with estimated actual of $2,349,083. The Expenditures 
pay for basic governmental services. Basic services include:  
▪ Capital Improvements 
▪ Administration 
▪ Streets, Grounds, and Buildings 

 
 
 
▪ Museum

Capital projects. The Town has budgeted $175,000 for capital projects in FY27.  Capital projects 
budgeted in the 2027 General Fund include: 
▪ Museum Trailer

▪ Relocating the Town Maintenance Shed 
 
 
 
Utility Fund 
Revenues in the Utility Fund are derived through service charges and fund reserves and are 
projected at $12,762,500 for FY27. Revenues in the Utility Fund estimates for FY26 are 
$4,085,405. It is important to note that $8,700,000 of the FY27 projected revenue is a pass-
through expense. Accordingly, the expenditures also reflect the $8,700,000 expense. The FY27

Expenditures in the Utility Fund are projected at $20,121,036 for FY27. Expenditures in the
