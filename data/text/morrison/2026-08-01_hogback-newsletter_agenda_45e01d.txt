Due to drought conditions all Morrison water service users are hereby noticed of
MANDATORY watering restrictions, effective APRIL 2, 2026. Restrictions shall remain in
effect until notice lifting restrictions is provided.
To ensure a reliable water supply and promote responsible water use, the following outdoor
watering restrictions shall apply to ALL water customers. This includes residents, out of town
residents, in-town businesses, and out of town businesses.
Outdoor Watering Restrictions & Conservation Guidelines
Watering Schedule Requirements & Water Use Regulations
Outdoor watering is limited to no more than two (2) days per week
NO watering is allowed between 10:00 AM and 6:00 PM 
Waste Prohibited: It is unlawful for any user to waste water including to permit
water to run down hard surfaces or leak in any manner.
Efficient Water Use & System Maintenance Requirements
Adjust sprinkler heads to ensure proper coverage of landscaped areas only
Use shut-off nozzles on hoses when not actively watering
All leaks in irrigation systems must be repaired within ten (10) days
Regularly inspect systems to ensure proper operation and minimize waste
Additional Conservation Measures 
Limit watering on windy days to prevent drift and evaporation
Consider drought-tolerant or native landscaping options
Sweep driveways and sidewalks instead of washing them down
Wash vehicles using a bucket or hose with a shut-off nozzle
Compliance
These restrictions are in place to protect the Morrison’s water supply and ensure
equitable distribution among all users. Failure to comply with these restrictions may
result in penalties as outlined in the Morrison Municipal Code, including fines, loss of
services, and municipal violations.

The warm temperatures and winds of spring and summer in the foothills create perfect conditions for wild fires. As
Hogback fires in recent years have demonstrated, fire emergencies can and do escalate in an instant. We want all
Town residents to be informed, prepared, and safe in the event of any emergency. As Tom Lehrer, a pundit of the
1960s advised: “don’t be nervous, don’t be flustered, don’t be scared, be prepared.” If you are always prepared,
there will be no cause for panic if an emergency situation arises.
The first step in being prepared is to create a Family Emergency Action Plan that is known and available to every
member of your household. This plan should include an out-of-state contact, phone numbers for all household
members, evacuation routes, a designated meeting site, location of emergency supply kit, and any other
information that is important to your family situation.
Assemble an emergency ready-to-go kit and make sure that all family members know its location. This kit should
include enough supplies to get you, your family, and your pets through at least 72 hours if you are unable to return
to your home. Start with the 5 Ps: people, pets, pills, personal items, and important papers. Communication is the
key to staying safe. Make sure you have signed up with Lookout Alert, the emergency notification system in
Jefferson County. Through this system, subscribers are notified of natural or man-made disasters, evacuation or
shelter-in-place orders, law enforcement activities, and other incidents that require public awareness. Visit
www.smart911.com to sign up for this service.
A red flag warning issued by the National Weather means that warm temperatures, very low humidity and strong
winds are expected to combine to produce an increased risk of fire danger.
A pre-evacuation warning is issued by officials if a situation has the potential of escalating. If a pre-evacuation
order is issued, this is the time to put all of your preparation steps into action. Make sure that family members, pets,
medications, important papers and necessary personal items are ready to go into your vehicle. Point the front of
your car facing the street to make it easier to leave rapidly if necessary. If you have large animals, it might be time
to move them to a safe location; you might want to check and see if facilities such as the Jeffco Fairgrounds are
able to take your animals. If you feel that you are in an unsafe situation, now is the time to leave.
 If you receive the order to evacuate, gather all family members and pets and leave immediately. Tune into local
news outlets for instructions on evacuating safely. Follow the instructions regarding evacuation routes. Remember,
your normal route might not be accessible or safe. As you leave, post visible notification so that emergency crews
will know that you have evacuated. A white cloth or sign with the word “evacuated” will work.

STAGE 2 FIRE BAN 
A Stage 2 Fire Ban is in effect for all areas of unincorporated Jefferson County and will
remain in effect until further notice.
The ban prohibits all fires and campfires, including those in grates, fire pits and
developed fire rings. It also prohibits charcoal grills, open burning, fireworks, sky lanterns
and other activities that may create a fire risk.

Be aware of possible flash flooding hazards. If there is any possibility of a flash flood, move
immediately to higher ground. Do not wait for instructions to move.
Listen to radio or television stations for local information. 
Be aware of streams, drainage channels, canyons or other areas known to flood suddenly. Flash
floods can occur in these areas without such typical warning signs as rain clouds or heavy rain. 
If local authorities issue a flood watch, prepare to evacuate. 
Secure your home. If you have time, tie down or bring outdoor equipment and lawn furniture
inside. Move essential items to the upper floors. 
If instructed, turn off utilities at the main switches or valves. Disconnect electrical appliances.
Do not touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water. 
Fill bathtubs with water in case water becomes contaminated or services cut off. Before
filling the tub, sterilize it with a diluted bleach solution. 
Do not walk through moving water. Six inches of moving water can knock you off your feet. If you
must walk in a flooded area, walk where the water is not moving. Use a stick to check the firmness
of the ground in front of you. 
Do not drive into flooded areas. Six inches of water will reach the bottom of most passenger cars
causing loss of control and possible stalling. A foot of water will float many vehicles. Two feet of
water will wash away almost all vehicles. If floodwaters rise around your car, abandon the car and
move to higher ground, if you can do so safely. You and your vehicle can be quickly swept away as
floodwaters rise.
