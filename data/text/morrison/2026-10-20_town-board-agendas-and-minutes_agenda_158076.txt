2027 Budget Notes 
October 20, 2026 
 
General Reminder: Estimates are updated in each iteration of the budget. For 2027, unfortunately that 
is singular. The 2027 budget draft that will be presented with the first of two hearings on November 4 
will have more refined numbers. Some expenditure estimates will go down and currently use the 2026 
approved numbers as placeholders until estimates are more refined.   
Town – Wide Compensation & Benefits  
• The Town’s health insurance renewal rate is a 15.85% increase. This is a substantial increase. 
•  I explored trimming some other benefits to assess fiscal impact (savings) to counter the insurance 
increase. With such a small staff the fiscal impact of the reductions would be minimal. For example, 
removing accidental death/dismemberment would save $768 annually. Cutting life insurance from 
$100,000 to $50,000 would save $1,706 annually. The savings from those reductions would have no 
meaningful impact. Looking at those reductions in the framework of accommodating a 1% 
retirement contribution increase, the impact would be minimal.  The reductions would only equate 
to $2,492 (town-wide) and the retirement increase would be between $9,000 - $10,000 for 1% 
(townwide). 
 
• The draft budget reflects the proposed COLA (3.6%) and longevity (2% / pro rated). It also reflects 
the 15.85% health insurance increase. It DOESN’T reflect a 1% retirement contribution increase. 
o Based on the substantial health insurance increase, I am not sure if it is something the Board 
still wants to consider.  
o Speaking with department heads, the consensus was that the priority should be getting 
employees COLAs over an increased retirement contribution. 
o If the Board is uncomfortable with the insurance expenses, I could speak with department 
heads about insurance changes. For example, the Town could go with a less favorable plan, 
with the option for employees to opt for another plan and pay the difference in their 
deductible.  
GENERAL FUND 
General Fund Revenue 
• Lodging tax revenue is somewhat of a guesstimate. However, I believe it will be the second 
highest source of tax revenue in 2027, surpassing property tax. 
• Property tax revenue will decrease in 2027. The reduction is $2,640, and is minimal but worth 
noting. 
• When the hotel is online, we will see the lodging tax and some sales tax increase. I did not 
increase the sales tax estimate for 2027 because I suspect 2026 will come in under $2,300,000. 
• The hotel utilized the Enhanced Sales Tax Incentive Program (ESTIP) tool offered by the town. 
Essentially, it provides a share back of the sales tax generated for the hotel from the town to the 
developer. I was unsure of the best way to reflect that in the budget because it must be shown 
as both revenue and an expenditure. I elected to put it as a separate line item and note that it is 
negative. In accounting, a negative is shown as (_). 
• There are a few cells in the general fund revenue spreadsheet that are red. This is because the 
revenue was coded to a different line item than in the approved budget. Red cells may also 
reflect something I want to verify and/or review line item details.  
Admin 
• There is a substantial increase in the Admin wages. There are two reasons for this: 
1. Consideration of additional FTE, which is included in the draft budget numbers. 
2. 2027 Wage increase is tied to FTE allocation change: 
▪ Town Manager 2026 = 0.4 → 2027 = 0.45 
▪ Town Clerk 2026 = 0.68 → 2027 = 0.7 
▪ Admin Assistant 2026 = 0.5 → 2027 = 0.5 
▪ Potential new FTE 2026 = 0 → 1 
▪ The remaining FTE portions are in court, water, and sewer 
 
• The Utility Fund is an enterprise fund, meaning it is funded by fees vs. property and sales tax. Think 
of it as a government run “business.” For that reason, portions of Admin position wages are paid by 
the Utility Fund because they perform duties for the Utility Fund.  
1. I contemplated not allocating a portion of the FTEs to the Utility Fund given that they will 
have a few turbulent years. However, you want to preserve your General Fund vs funding 
Utility Fund staffing. Building the General Fund back up would take a considerable amount 
of time. The Utility Fund on the other hand will recover.  
2. If you pay for Utility Fund expenses with tax revenue, the Utility Fund will lose the 
enterprise fund designation. However, it is my understanding that you could loan the Utility 
Fund money from the General Fund and keep the enterprise status. To do this, it would 
have to be structured as a legitimate loan with interest. Legal counsel and financial advisors 
would need to confirm. Given that the Utility Fund will recover and could repay the loan, it 
makes sense to keep proper FTE allocations. 
▪ Stating this doesn’t mean it is the solution for the Utility Fund. It could be one tool 
but the financial advisors should determine the extent and if appropriate. 
 
• The 2026 Admin wage expenditures estimate is the same as the approved 2026 amount. There are 
cost savings due to vacancies. However, that has resulted in an employee taking on the duties of a 
separate full-time position. That employee will be fairly compensated.  
• If a new FTE is approved, I would like the Board’s approval to post the position in November. With 
the holidays, the position might not start until 2027. If someone is hired and can start in 2026, I 
believe there is enough in the 2026 budgeted wages to cover the pay without requiring a budget 
amendment. This would be verified by the November 4th budget reading, assuming the Board elects 
to proceed with the new FTE and authorizes hiring in 2026.

Pursuant to the Town of Morrison Home Rule Charter and applicable Colorado law, I submit the 
proposed (draft) 2027 budget for the Board of Trustees' review and consideration.  
 
The budget outlines Town operations and capital improvements for the 2027 calendar year. The 
budget, as presented, will allow the Town to achieve many of its goals and priorities in a cost-
effective and efficient manner.  
 
Budget Basis  
All governmental fund budgets are prepared using the modified accrual basis of accounting. 
Under this method, revenues are recognized when susceptible to accrual, that is, measurable and 
available. Available means collectible in the current period or soon enough thereafter to be used 
to pay liabilities of the current period. Expenditures, other than interest on long-term obligations 
that are recorded when due, are recognized when they are expected to be liquidated with 
expendable available resources.   All proprietary fund budgets are also prepared using the 
modified accrual basis of accounting. Although this basis is not consistent with GAAP for 
enterprise funds, it is common practice among municipalities and is acceptable to our auditors.  
 
The budget encompasses two funds, the General Fund, and the Utility Enterprise Fund. The 
revenue and expenditure summaries for both funds, as well as budget highlights, are summarized 
below.  
 
General Fund  
Revenues in the General Fund are projected at $4,534,514 for FY27. Revenues in the General 
Fund estimates for FY26 are $4,323,288. General Fund revenues come from a variety of sources, 
including property tax, sales taxes, use taxes, fees, and charges for services.  
 
▪ Sales tax is the single largest source of revenue for the Town. General Fund sales tax 
collections are projected at $2,300,000. This is a zero-growth projection from 2026.   
▪ Traditionally, the second largest source of tax revenue for the Town is property tax. 
The mill levy rate for Morrison is 6.746. In 2026, Morrison is projecting property tax 
revenues of $110,901. This is a decrease from the prior year. 
▪ It is anticipated that in 2027, the second highest source of tax revenue will become 
lodging tax, with an estimate of $200,000. 
 
Expenditures in the General Fund are projected at $2,607,548 for FY27. Expenditures in the 
approved FY26 budget were $4,028,156, with estimated actual of $2,392,989. The Expenditures

Town of Morrison 
Board of Trustees Rules of Procedure 
 
1. Relationship to Town Charter and Code . Additional requirements applicable to the 
Board of Trustees (the "Board") are found in Articles 2 and 3 of the Town Charter. In the event of 
a conflict between these Rules and the Town Charter or Code, the Town Charter and Code shall 
control. 
2. Bob's Rules of Order. Bob's Rules of Order are adopted as the parliamentary rules for 
Board meetings. In the event of a conflict between these Rules and Bob's Rules, these Rules shall 
control. 
3. Meetings. 
A. Regular meetings of the Board shall be held on the first and third Tuesdays of each 
month at 6:00PM unless noticed otherwise.  
B. Special meetings shall be called by the Town Clerk upon the request of the Mayor, 
any two Members, or the Town Manager. Notice of special meetings shall be provided to the Board 
at least forty-eight (48) hours prior to the meeting.  
4. Agendas, Staff Meetings, and Information Requests.  
A. All Member communications related to Town business  shall be directed to the 
Town Manager. 
B. Agendas shall be prepared by the Town Manager  in consultation with the Mayor . 
The Mayor and Town Manager shall meet weekly, generally for no more than thirty (30) minutes, 
or every other week  generally for no more than sixty (60) minutes, to coordinate upcoming 
agendas.  
C. The Town Manager shall hold office hours twice a month for one-on-one meetings 
with Trustees. Each Trustee shall be allotted a maximum of thirty (30) minutes and sign up in 
advance. Office hours shall not be used as a substitute for public meetings or collective deliberation 
on matters required to be discussed in public. 
D. Any Member may request that an item be placed on a future agenda during a public 
meeting. If at least one additional Member supports the request, the item shall be placed on a future 
agenda. Requests received outside a public meeting shall be presented at the next public meeting 
for consideration under this subsection. 
E. To the maximum extent practicable, M embers shall submit  questions related to 
agenda items to the Town Manager prior to Board meetings. Responses will be given at the Board 
meeting or at office hours as determined by the Town Manager.  
F. Requests for information shall be treated as a future agenda item request unless 
such information was previously provided by Staff and can be provided again without  additional 
Staff time. 
5. Rule Suspension. The Board, by majority vote, may temporarily suspend any of these 
Rules for the duration of the meeting. A motion to suspend a Rule must specify the Rule to be 
suspended and the purpose of the suspension.

6. Duties of the Mayor.  
A. The Mayor, or the Mayor Pro Tem in the Mayor's  absence, shall chair all Board 
meetings. If, at the time of the meeting, the Mayor and the Mayor Pro Tem are absent, then the 
Town Clerk shall call the Board meeting to order,  and the first order of business shall be the 
election of a Member to serve as the acting Chair.  
B. The Mayor shall have the right to speak and vote as any other Trustee and is  
considered a Member for the purposes of these Rules. The duties of the Mayor as the Chair include:   
i. Open the meeting at the appointed time and call the meeting to order. 
ii. Announce the business coming before the Board in accordance with the 
agenda and introduce items of business. 
iii. Control the proceedings of the meeting and recognize M embers, Staff and 
the public who are entitled to the floor. 
iv. State or cause to be stated and put to a vote all questions that legitimately 
come before the Board and announce the results of the vote. 
v. Enforce these Rules. 
vi. Decide all questions of order. 
vii. Adjourn the meeting.

BOARD ACTION FORM 
SUBJECT: Discussion and Possible Action on Additional Full-Time Employee 
SUGGESTED MOTION: I move to approve the creation of the ___________ position. 
BACKGROUND: 
The Board is being asked to consider and provide direction regarding the potential addition of one 
full-time equivalent position. There are two positions suggested, a Deputy Town Manager or a 
Management Analyst (or another title). There are pros and cons to both. 
The cost will vary depending on the position selected and will include salary, benefits, payroll 
expenses, and related employment costs. 
Deputy Town Manager vs. Management Analyst Considerations  
 
Key distinction. Both positions increase the Town's ability to complete important work, but they 
address different needs. A Management Analyst adds professional workload capacity within the 
existing management structure. A Deputy Town Manager provides that workload support while also 
adding delegated executive authority, organization-wide coordination, and succession depth. 
Deputy Town Manager Benefits 
Succession planning and organizational continuity. In a small organization, the loss or extended 
absence of one employee can disrupt an entire function. A deputy provides planned leadership 
coverage, preserves institutional knowledge, and can serve as acting manager when formally 
designated. 
Workload. The manager can delegate defined operational decisions, sensitive assignments, and major 
initiatives rather than remaining the sole point of responsibility for nearly every escalation. 
Cross-department and consultant coordination. The position has the organizational standing to 
clarify responsibilities, reconcile competing priorities, monitor performance, and keep complex 
work moving across departments and outside service providers. 
Ownership of major priorities. A deputy can lead development partnerships, capital projects, 
procurement, grants, community engagement, intergovernmental work, and other initiatives that 
require sustained management attention. 
External representation and problem resolution. The deputy can represent the Town in substantive 
meetings and address difficult resident, consultant, or operational matters within delegated 
authority. 
Stronger organizational systems. The role can improve procedures, project tracking, internal 
communication, cross-training, management coverage, and other practices that strengthen the 
organization as a whole. 
Deputy Town Manager Potential Drawbacks and Considerations 
Higher personnel cost. A deputy generally requires higher salary and benefit costs than an analyst 
because the position carries broader authority, leadership expectations, and accountability. 
Requires sustained executive-level work. The position provides the most value when the Town has 
enough complex operational, supervisory, and strategic work to justify a second executive role. 
Importance of leadership fit. The deputy must exercise sound judgment, maintain trust with the 
manager and Board, and work effectively with department heads. A poor fit can create 
organizational friction at a senior level. 
Management Analyst Benefits 
Direct workload assistance. An analyst can independently complete defined research, reports, agenda 
materials, and project assignments, allowing existing staff to focus on other priorities. 
Hands-on program and project support. The position can manage schedules, work plans, 
documentation, budgets, and follow-through for assigned initiatives. 
Support for high-demand functions. An analyst can coordinate grants, procurement, consultants, 
community engagement, resident inquiries, and community development administration. 
Improved research and documentation. The role adds capacity for policy analysis, data review, 
written procedures, process improvement, recordkeeping, and Board materials. 
Lower-cost staffing option. The position generally provides professional capacity at a lower 
compensation level than a deputy. 
Management Analyst Potential Drawbacks and Considerations 
Limited delegated authority. An analyst can research, coordinate, and recommend, but significant 
operational, personnel, policy, and organizational decisions ordinarily remain with the manager. 
Limited management coverage. The position does not ordinarily provide department supervision, 
executive decision-making, or Acting Town Manager coverage. 
Less succession depth. An analyst strengthens technical continuity and documentation but does not 
create the same ready leadership coverage for a manager absence, vacancy, or transition. 
Coordination without executive standing. The analyst may organize cross-department or consultant 
work but may need the manager to resolve competing priorities, performance concerns, or 
resistance. 
Risk of an overly broad workload. If too many unrelated functions are assigned, urgent 
administrative needs may crowd out analytical and project work. Priorities and decision boundaries 
must remain clear.

Position Summary 
Under the general direction of the Town Manager, the Management Analyst performs professional analytical, 
administrative, program, and project management work in support of Town services and priorities. The 
position is intended to take ownership of defined work that would otherwise remain with the Town Manager or 
department staff. The Analyst independently plans and completes assignments, coordinates participants and 
resources, monitors schedules and deliverables, prepares recommendations, and implements approved 
actions. 
Assignments may include community engagement, resident and business inquiries, grants, procurement and 
contract administration, consultant coordination, community development processes, and special projects. 
The Analyst exercises initiative and independent judgment within established policies and assigned authority.  
Essential Duties and Responsibilities 
• Conduct research and analysis on municipal policies, programs, services, regulations, and emerging 
issues; develop options and practical recommendations and carry out approved actions within assigned 
responsibility. 
• Independently manage assigned projects and programs, including objectives, work plans, milestones, 
communication, documentation, stakeholder coordination, and progress reporting; identify decisions or 
obstacles requiring management attention. 
• Develop or coordinate community outreach and engagement for assigned programs and projects, 
including informational materials, meetings, surveys, participation opportunities, documentation of 
feedback, and timely follow-up. 
• Serve as a professional point of contact for resident and business inquiries, requests, complaints, and 
service concerns; research issues, coordinate responses, facilitate resolution within established policies, 
and elevate sensitive, legal, policy, or unresolved matters. 
• Lead or coordinate the grant lifecycle for assigned funding opportunities, including research, project 
development, application preparation, award documentation, budgets, compliance, reimbursements, 
performance monitoring, reporting, closeout, and coordination with responsible employees and partners. 
• Lead or coordinate assigned procurement and contract-administration activities, including needs 
assessment, scopes of work, solicitation documents, evaluation processes, selection recommendations, 
negotiation support, agreement routing, invoice and change review, performance tracking, renewal, and 
closeout. 
• Coordinate assigned consultants and service providers. 
• Prepare analytical reports, recommendations, correspondence, presentations, agenda materials, data 
summaries, and other documents for management and the Board of Trustees; present information and 
answer questions when assigned. 
• Evaluate assigned administrative procedures, forms, workflows, and service processes; recommend and 
implement approved improvements that strengthen customer service, documentation, coordination, 
efficiency, and follow-through. 
• Coordinate multidisciplinary project teams and special assignments, communicate approved expectations 
and deadlines, facilitate collaboration, and maintain accountability for assigned deliverables without 
exercising departmental or personnel authority.

Supervision and Authority 
The Management Analyst is a professional individual contributor with responsibility for independently 
managing assigned projects, programs, and administrative processes. Within an approved scope of work, the 
position may coordinate participants, establish project-level tasks and schedules, request information, 
monitor deliverables, make routine administrative determinations under established policies, provide 
functional direction for assigned work, and represent the Town Manager or Town when designated. The 
Analyst does not ordinarily directly supervise, evaluate, or discipline employees; direct department 
operations; establish Town policy; exercise executive or organization-wide personnel authority; or serve as 
Acting Town Manager. Technical, legal, policy, personnel, and other discretionary decisions remain with the 
officials, employees, or professional advisors assigned that authority.

Minimum Qualifications 
• Bachelor's degree in public administration, business administration, public policy, or a closely related field; 
equivalent education and directly relevant experience may be considered consistent with Town policy. 
• At least four years of progressively responsible experience in government administration involving 
substantial responsibility for programs, projects, or operations. 
• Minimum of two years of progressively responsible supervisory experience. 
Required Knowledge and Skills 
• Broad understanding of municipal administration, public accountability, governance, budgets, 
procurement, and applicable legal and policy frameworks. 
• Sound judgment, discretion, and diplomacy in addressing sensitive operational, personnel, and 
community issues. 
• Strong leadership, communication, collaboration, problem-solving, and project management skills. 
• Ability to lead complex work through both direct supervision and collaboration with individuals or teams 
outside the position's reporting line. 
Supervision and Authority 
The Deputy Town Manager may serve without any direct departmental reports, but may be assigned 
supervision duties. Supervisory assignments are determined by the Town Manager based on organizational 
needs and may change over time. The position retains senior management responsibilities regardless of the 
number of direct reports. Acting Town Manager assignments and other delegated authorities must be 
expressly authorized and exercised in accordance with applicable Town law and policy.
