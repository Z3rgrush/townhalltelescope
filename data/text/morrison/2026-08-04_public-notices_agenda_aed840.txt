TOWN OF MORRISON, COLORADO
BOARD OF TRUSTEES
ORDINANCE NO.  ____
AN ORDINANCE REPEALING AND REENACTING TITLE 8 OF THE MORRISON
MUNICIPAL CODE REGARDING WATER AND SEWER REGULATIONS
WHEREAS, the Town of Morrison is a Colorado home rule municipality operating under
a Home Rule Charter approved by the electorate pursuant to Article XX of the Colorado
Constitution and governed by its elected Board of Trustees;
WHEREAS, the Board has authority pursuant to the Home Rule Charter and C.R.S. §
31-16-101, et seq. to adopt and enforce all ordinances;
WHEREAS, the Board recently amended Titles 8 and 10 to include water conservation
standards and prohibit certain landscaping standards in accordance with C.R.S. § 37-99-101, et
seq.; and
WHEREAS, the Board now desires to repeal and replace Title 8 to clarify all water and
sewer requirements.
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Board of Trustees of the Town of
Morrison, Colorado:
Section 1. Title 8 of the Morrison Municipal Code is hereby repealed in its entirety
and reenacted as follows:
TITLE 8 WATER AND SEWER
CHAPTER 1 GENERAL PROVISIONS
8-1-1: PURPOSE AND APPLICABILITY
This Chapter establishes general provisions for all Town utilities and applies to all Town
provided water and sewer service.
8-1-2: DEFINITIONS:
AIR GAP: A physical separation between the free-flowing discharge end of a potable water
supply pipeline and an open or non-pressure receiving vessel installed in accordance with
standard ASME A112.1.2.
BACKFLOW: The undesirable reversal of flow of water or mixtures of water and other
liquids, gases or other substances into the Town water system from any source other than
its intended source.
BACKFLOW CONTAMINATION EVENT: Backflow into the Town water system from
an uncontrolled cross connection such that the water quality no longer meets the Colorado
Primary Drinking Water Regulations or presents an immediate health or safety risk to the
public.

BACKFLOW PREVENTION METHOD: Any method or non-testable device installed at

CHAPTER 2 WATER SYSTEM
8-2-1: PURPOSE AND APPLICABILITY:
A. Purpose: The purpose of this Chapter is to promote the public health, safety, and
welfare by requiring water conservation while facilitating orderly growth and exceptional
water service and protecting the Town's water system from contaminants or pollutants that
could enter the distribution system by backflow from a user's water supply system through
the service connection.
B. Applicability: This Chapter shall apply to all water users, whether inside or outside
the Town, unless specifically exempted herein or pursuant to a written agreement approved
by the Board of Trustees.
8-2-2: WATER USE REQUIREMENTS:
A. Authorized Use: All water use shall be limited to the specific property and user to
which it was authorized by the Town. 
B. Cross Connections: Cross connections between the Town water system and other
lines shall be prohibited. 
C. Pressure Systems: No pumps or storage tanks, except a hot water storage tank no
larger than three hundred twenty (320) gallons, shall be installed in plumbing systems
except with prior written approval of the Town and the payment of a 10 percent system
development fee surcharge. The Town may require a user to have a pressure regulating
valve, a backflow prevention valve, or both, the cost and installation of which shall be the
responsibility of the user.
D. Yard Hydrant Connections: No outside faucet or yard hydrant shall connect directly
to any service line. A curb stop must be installed in the line immediately upstream of the
hydrant. All yard hydrants must be a certified freeze-proof design.
E. Fire Hydrants: No user shall use water from fire plugs except for the extinguishment
of fires by the fire department or upon prior written approval of the Town.
F. Water Waste: No user shall waste water. Watering of hard surfaces in any manner
shall be prohibited.
G. Irrigation Taps: The Town shall not issue any irrigation taps except as expressly
approved by the Board of Trustees in a water service agreement and when the user proposes

substantial benefits to the Town not otherwise required by this Code including additional
water conservation measures.
H. Irrigation Hours: Irrigation shall be prohibited between the hours of 10 a.m. and 6
p.m. daily to reduce evaporation loss.
I. Consumptive Limits: Maximum water consumption limits shall be established by

K. System Protection and Tampering: No person shall trespass upon, contaminate,
introduce any contaminant into, damage, tamper with, bypass, or otherwise interfere with
the operation of any of the Town’s water facilities, sources, treatment works, distribution
system, meters, monitoring devices, testing equipment, backflow prevention assemblies,
or related appurtenances. No person shall knowingly make any false statement,
representation, or certification relating to utility service, compliance data, or any required
report. Any violation of this section is a violation of this Title. Such acts may also constitute
a federal felony under the Safe Drinking Water Act, 42 U.S.C. § 300i-1. The Town may
immediately suspend or discontinue utility service and will aggressively pursue any
remedy available at law or in equity, including referring violators to federal and state law
enforcement agencies.

8-2-5: RAW WATER SYSTEMS:
A. Policy: The Town encourages the use of raw water systems to service outdoor
watering needs.
B. Requirements:
1. Raw water irrigation systems shall be completely separate and independent
from the Town’s potable water system. Under no circumstances shall the raw water system
be physically connected, cross-connected, valved, or otherwise capable of interconnection
with the potable water system.
2. Raw water piping, valves, appurtenances, and service lines shall be entirely
separate from potable water infrastructure and shall not share piping, fittings, or service
connections.
3. All raw water lines and appurtenances shall be clearly identified and
color-coded (bright purple) in accordance with industry standards and labeled “NON-
POTABLE – DO NOT DRINK.”
4. Where potable water is used to supplement irrigation or where any potential
hazard exists, the two systems must remain physically separated either by physical air gap
meeting state criteria or a Reduced Pressure Zone (RP) Assembly shall be installed as
required by the Town.
C. Emergency Enforcement and Termination: Any physical connection, valve
interconnection, or cross-connection discovered between the Town's potable water system
and a raw water system constitutes an immediate threat to public health and safety. Upon
discovery of such a violation, the Town is authorized to immediately terminate water
service to the property without prior notice. Service shall not be restored until the illegal
connection is physically cut out and capped, the potable system is flushed and tested for
contamination, and the owner pays all associated emergency disconnection and inspection
costs.

CHAPTER 3 SEWER SYSTEM
8-3-1: PURPOSE AND APPLICABILITY:
A. Purpose: The purpose of this Chapter is to promote the public health, safety, and
welfare by facilitating orderly growth, protecting the Town’s wastewater collection and
treatment systems, preventing the introduction of pollutants or prohibited discharges that
may interfere with the sewer system, and protecting the environment and public
infrastructure from damage or contamination.
B. Applicability: This Chapter shall apply to all sewer users, whether inside or outside
the Town, unless specifically exempted herein or pursuant to a written agreement approved
by the Board of Trustees.
8-3-2: SEWER USE REQUIREMENTS:
A. Prohibited Discharges to System:
1. No person shall discharge or cause to be discharged any stormwater, surface
water, ground water, or unpolluted industrial process water to any sanitary sewer.
2. No person shall discharge or cause to be discharged into any public sewer,
any harmful waters or wastes, whether liquid, solid, or gas, capable of causing obstruction
to the flow in sewers, damage or hazard to structures, equipment and personnel of the
sewage works, or other interference with the proper operation of the sewer system
including substances that:
a. Creates a fire or explosion hazard;
b. Has a pH less than 5.0 or greater than 12.0, or is otherwise corrosive;
7/30/2026

c. Contains solid or viscous substances in amounts capable of causing
obstruction;
d. Contains toxic gases, vapors, or fumes creating acute worker safety hazards;
e. Causes excessive heat such that the wastewater treatment process is
inhibited;
f. Causes pass through, interference, or operational upset at the treatment
works;
g. Contains petroleum oil, nonbiodegradable cutting oils, or products of
mineral oil origin in amounts causing interference;
h. Contains pollutants resulting in noncompliance with sludge use or disposal
requirements.
3. The admission into the public sewer of any waters or wastes having harmful
or objectionable characteristics shall be subject to the review and approval of the Town,
who may prescribe limits on the strength and character of these substances. Where
necessary, at the discretion of the Town, the owner shall provide at their expense,
preliminary treatment mechanisms to treat wastes prior to discharge to the public sewer as
approved by the Director of Public Works.
4. All industrial, commercial, and non-domestic users discharging to the
Town’s wastewater system shall comply with all applicable federal pretreatment
requirements, including 40 C.F.R. Part 403, all applicable categorical pretreatment
standards, the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment Pretreatment
Regulations (5 CCR 1002-63), and all other applicable federal, state, or local laws.
5. Where the Town determines that a discharge may adversely affect the
Town’s sewer system, treatment works, receiving waters, or compliance obligations, the
Town may impose additional monitoring, pretreatment, operational, or discharge
requirements as reasonably necessary to protect the public health, safety, welfare,
environment, and utility infrastructure.
6. The Town may immediately suspend any industrial discharge permit or
sewer service whenever such suspension is necessary to stop an actual or threatened
discharge presenting imminent danger to persons, the environment, the sewer system, or
treatment facilities.
B. Control Manhole Required:
1. When required by the Town, any industrial or commercial user shall install
and maintain, at their sole expense, a suitable control manhole or monitoring facility to
facilitate the inspection, sampling, and flow measurement of wastewater discharges. The
control manhole must be located on the user’s property, downstream of all pretreatment
systems, and upstream of any point where the industrial wastewater mixes with domestic
sanitary waste or other building discharges. The facility shall be designed and constructed
in accordance with Town Engineering Standards, shall provide safe and unrestricted access
to Town inspectors at all times, and shall serve as the official compliance point for
industrial pretreatment permit sampling.

2. All wastewater sampling must adhere to the most current edition Standard

C. Interceptors Required:

8-3-3: SEWER SERVICE CONNECTIONS:
A. Cost Responsibility: All costs and fees related to the installation, connection, and
service of sewer shall be borne by the user, including all costs necessary to establish sewer
service to the property.
B.  Individual Service Lines:
1. Each user shall own and be responsible for construction, maintenance,
repair, and replacement of service lines.
2. Leaks or breaks in the service line shall be repaired by the property owner
within twenty-four (24) hours from notification by the Town unless the Town grants an
extension due to exigent circumstances. The Town may undertake emergency repairs
necessary to protect public health, safety, and welfare, or the sewer system and the property
owner shall be responsible for the payment of all associated costs.
3. Every primary use in the Town shall be supplied with sewer through a single
service line connected to the Town’s sewer system, except for a hotel, motel, multifamily
dwelling, or other multi-tenant use under single ownership and operated as an integrated
unit as determined by the Director of Public Works. Accessory dwelling units may share a
service line with the primary dwelling on the property; provided, however, that the
applicant or property owner shall submit design documentation demonstrating to the
satisfaction of the Director of Public Works that the existing sewer service line has
adequate capacity to serve the primary dwelling and the accessory dwelling unit in
accordance with the Town Engineering Standards and all applicable code requirements.
4. In the event that any premises receiving sewer from the Town is
subsequently divided into separate ownership, each owner shall have a separate sewer
service line and each additional user shall pay all fees established herein. 
C. Specifications: All materials and installations shall be in accordance with the Town
Engineering Standards and all applicable federal, state, and local laws, rules, regulations,
codes, and permit requirements. The sewer tap or connection shall consist of the physical
connection from the Town’s sewer main to the user’s sewer service line, including the
service wye or other approved connection to the sewer main, fittings, cleanouts, risers,
transitions, and any other appurtenances required to convey sewage from the user’s
premises to the Town’s sewer system. All such work shall be inspected and approved by
the Director of Public Works prior to backfill, use, and final acceptance.

A. Fee Established: There is hereby established a sewer system development fee,

regardless of the occupancy status of the property.

when such disconnection will not adversely affect the sewer system. Any user who is
disconnected from the Town sewer system and seeks to reconnect must apply for new
sewer service in accordance with this Title.
F. Property Owner Responsible: The property owner shall be liable for all charges for
sewer service to the property.
