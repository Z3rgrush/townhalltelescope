  BRIEF SUMMARY 
The draft Comprehensive Plan updates and builds upon the 2020 Comprehensive Plan.  It was 
developed through extensive community outreach and input so that it reflects our local priorities and 
values.  It provides a clear roadmap for our future while preserving the character that makes 
Severance special.  Together, this shared vision, goals, and strategies will guide decisions and 
investments and support responsible growth for generations to come.

Community Snapshot 
[design note: pair this opening with a regional context map showing the relationship to

Severance is located in Northern Colorado, approximately 50 miles north of the Denver 
metropolitan area and between the regional centers of Fort Collins, Loveland, and Greeley. 
This location provides access to employment, services, education, and recreation 
throughout the region while allowing Severance to retain a distinct small-town identity. The 
Town encompasses nearly 4,300 acres. 
Severance began as a small agricultural settlement around 1894 when a post office was 
established to serve a community of approximately 50 families. Agriculture, the sugar-beet 
industry, and the railroad shaped the community’s early development, and Severance was 
formally incorporated as a statutory town in 1920 following a unanimous 40–0 vote by 
residents. 
For much of the twentieth century, Severance remained a small agricultural and railroad 
community. Our population declined from 1940 to 1970 as railway activity diminished and 
nearby communities attracted regional employment, commercial development, and new 
residents. The closure of Severance’s sugar-beet receiving station in 1985 marked the end 
of an approximately 80-year period in which beet agriculture and rail service had played 
central roles in the local economy. 
Severance’s modern period of residential growth began during the 1990s as Northern 
Colorado’s expanding economy and housing market attracted residents seeking newer 
homes, relative affordability, and a small-town setting near regional employment centers. 
Growth accelerated after 2010, transforming Severance from a small agricultural town into 
a rapidly growing community.

Key Historical Eras  
[design note: Integrate the ﬁve historical eras with the population-growth graphic using 
corresponding colors. Keep each era visually connected to its portion of the growth curve.] 
The shape of Severance’s population growth reﬂects several distinct periods in the 
community’s history. Together, these eras show how the Town evolved from a small 
agricultural settlement into one of Northern Colorado’s fastest-growing communities. 
• 1894–1905: Agricultural Beginnings 
The post office, sugar-beet cultivation, railroad access, and Windsor sugar factory 
created the foundation for the community. 
• 1906–1920: Becoming a Town 
The townsite was founded and later incorporated as an organized municipality. 
• 1920s–1980s: Agricultural Continuity and Low Growth 
Severance remained a small farming and railroad community while neighboring 
cities attracted most regional commercial and industrial growth. 
• 1990–2010: Residential Transition 
Front Range economic growth and housing demand turned Severance into a 
commuter-oriented community. 
• 2010–present: Rapid Growth and Expansion 
Population growth accelerated, prompting updated land-use plans and major 
investments in transportation, utilities, parks, schools, and municipal services. 
Population growth tells only part of Severance’s story. The following moments highlight the 
local and regional events that helped shape the community we know today.
