In-person and remote meeting public attendance and participation instructions: 
Participation 
• To provide scheduled public comment, either in person or remotely, please fill out 
and return the Public Comment Form on the City's website. All requests must be 
submitted to the City Clerk (cityclerk@idahospringsco.com) by 6 p.m. (Six) the 
Wednesday before the scheduled meeting. 
• To provide unscheduled public comment, please join the Zoom Meeting, identify 
yourself with your full first and last name, and use the “Raise Hand” feature to 
indicate your desire to speak. 
General Guidelines 
• Each public comment, whether scheduled or unscheduled, is limited to three (3) 
minutes. 
• The Board typically does not provide feedback during public comment sessions.  
• If you would like to provide materials for the Board to review along with your 
Comment, please sign up for Scheduled Public Comment and provide those 
materials to the City Clerk by the Wednesday Deadline.

  Dear Members of the Business and Community Promotion Board and City Council, 
I am writing to express my strong interest in serving on the Idaho Springs Business and 
Community Promotion Board (BCPB). As a local resident, business owner, and active community 
member who grew up in Idaho Springs, I understand both the town’s heritage and the 
importance of thoughtful growth. I am deeply invested in supporting local businesses, 
strengthening community promotion, and helping preserve the historic character that makes 
Idaho Springs unique. 
Idaho Springs occupies a unique position as both a close-knit mountain community and a major 
gateway for Colorado tourism. I believe that thoughtful, strategic promotion is key to supporting 
our local businesses year-round while balancing the needs of our local residents. 
Through my background in custom design, fabrication, managerial and accounting experience, 
I’ve developed a hands-on understanding of what it takes to capture attention and create 
impactful community events. Whether it’s designing effective signage, conceptualizing physical 
installations for events, or streamlining project logistics, I thrive on bringing creative concepts 
into practical reality. I look forward to bringing this collaborative, detail-oriented perspective to 
board discussions regarding downtown vitality, visual wayfinding, local marketing campaigns, 
and seasonal events. 
As Co-Owner of Smithrock LLC, manager and accountant of Squatch Store and Mt. Evans Trout 
Fishing, and co-founder of our new venture, Squatch Fabrication & Welding I bring a deep, 
practical understanding of business strategy, operations, and community engagement. 
Managing these enterprises from the ground up has given me valuable insight into budgeting, 
marketing, project management, and what it takes for local businesses to adapt and thrive in 
Clear Creek County. Beyond my entrepreneurial background, I am actively invested in the future 
of our community, serving on the District Accountability Committee (DAC) for Clear Creek 
School District and participating on the Carlson Elementary PTA. 
 I admire the work the BCPB does to foster vibrant community spaces and support local 
enterprise. I welcome the opportunity to contribute my time, skills, and creative energy to help 
guide these efforts forward. 
Thank you for your time, consideration, and dedication to Idaho Springs. I look forward to the 
opportunity to discuss how I can best serve on the board. 
Sincerely,

VII. Director Report 
a. Marketing & Tourism Updates: The Director provided an update on Visit Idaho Springs 
marketing efforts, including the conclusion of the $12,000 fall marketing campaign. Placer.ai 
reported 325,900 out-of-market visitors during the campaign period, a 1% increase year over 
year. Social media audiences and website engagement continue to grow. Updates were also 
provided on the Wayfinding Grant, Historic Multimedia Tour, Economic Impact Dashboard, and 
Trails to Towns projects. 
b. Community Events & Holiday Planning: The Director reviewed the inaugural Miner’s Feast, 
which sold out at 150 tickets and raised over $4,000 for Charlie’s Place. Updates were provided 
on upcoming Halloween activities, including Trunk or Treat and the Zombie Party on Miner 
Street. The Board also discussed Holiday Ramble planning, including a possible holiday hayride, 
a Sips of the Season experience, and opportunities for Board members to assist with event 
planning and execution. 
c. Miner Street Market – Transition to Vehicle Traffic: The Director reviewed the seasonal 
closure of Miner Street Market. Following the Board's September discussion and feedback from

participating businesses, cleanup of outdoor activation areas may begin November 2, with Miner 
Street scheduled to reopen to vehicle traffic on November 5, 2026. A post meeting discission 
with Public works operations they have requested we return to a two-way street.  
d. 2027 BCPB Budget & Parking Enterprise Fund: The Director reviewed the proposed 
$273,109 BCPB budget for 2027 and its presentation to City Council. Mayor Chuck Harmon and 
City Administrator Andy proposed funding the BCPB budget through the Parking Enterprise 
Fund as part of the City's 2027 budget discussions. The Board discussed the potential change in 
funding allocation and its implications for supporting business promotion, destination marketing, 
and community programming. Final funding decisions remain subject to City Council's budget 
process. 
e. CODA Standards & Downtown Improvements: Dylan Graves, City Planner presented 
proposed updates to the City's Communal Outdoor Dining Area (CODA) standards for the 2027 
season. Key considerations included stronger requirements for public safety, commercial-grade 
and weather-resistant materials, cleanliness, emergency access, and historically appropriate 
design. The proposed approach would allow the BCPB to adopt and periodically update 
activation standards. Discussion topics included preferred versus prohibited materials, signage 
and lighting, inflatable or moving displays, and an enforcement process involving warnings and 
potential revocation of encroachment approval. The proposals remain under review and are not 
yet adopted standards. 
VIII. Round Table Updates  
a. Idaho Springs AWARED Recreational Occupation Tax: The Board received an update on the 
awarded Recreational Rural Technical Assistance Program (RTAP), a collaborative initiative 
involving CU Boulder, the Colorado Outdoor Recreation Industry Office, and CSU Extension. 
The program helps rural communities strengthen their outdoor recreation economies while 
supporting downtown businesses, economic development, and Main Street revitalization. 
Through this program, graduate students will work alongside community stakeholders to identify 
opportunities, challenges, and priorities, ultimately developing a Community Action Plan for the 
next 12–24 months. The process includes a two-day community workshop planned for March 
2027, with the final action plan expected in May 2027. CSU Extension will then provide two 
years of implementation support.
