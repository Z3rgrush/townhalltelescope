Proposed 2027 Budget Highlights 
October 12, 2026 
 
Budget Summary 
• The proposed 2027 budget has been extremely difficult to prepare, particularly for the General 
Fund.  Through additional cost allocations to other funds, moving additional business and 
community promotion costs to the Parking Fund, and pausing certain employee benefits, the 
General Fund has a year-end fund balance for 2027 sufficient to cover the required 3% Tabor 
reserve.  General Fund revenues in 2027 are projected to be about only $100,000 higher than 
they were in 2024, and 2027 expenditures are budgeted to be about the same as they were in 
2024.  Even so, expenditures exceed revenues by $265,703 in 2027. 
• The negative fund balance in the Sales Tax Improvement Fund will be reduced significantly by 
the end of 2027. 
• All other funds have positive fund balances, and the combined fund balances are projected to 
increase by more than a million dollars. 
• The proposed 2027 budget will be finalized in the upcoming weeks and is scheduled to be 
adopted at the City Council meeting on December 14. 
 
General Fund Revenues 
• Property Tax – Based on the preliminary certification from the Clear Creek County Assessor’s 
Office, the City’s share of property tax will decrease from $282,237 for 2026 to $267,546 for 
2027 at the same mill levy rate of 6.310. 
• Sales Tax – The primary revenue source for the General Fund is sales tax, and a 2% City sales tax 
is dedicated to the General Fund.  Sales tax for 2026 is estimated to be $2,000,000, 
approximately the same amount as the actuals for 2025 and 2024.  The significant sales tax 
increases that were anticipated this year due to the opening of the Argo gondola, the Fieldhouse 
apartments, and increased marketing have not materialized.  However, sales tax revenue is 
projected to increase 5% in the proposed budget for 2027. 
• Business Licenses – A new $25 fee for each 2027 business license is proposed to help fund the 
cost of business and community promotion.  With the current 224 business licenses, an 
additional $5,600 in revenue would be generated.  City Council confirmation of this new fee is 
requested this evening so that business license annual renewal letters may be sent. 
• Severance Tax – While severance tax revenue has doubled to approximately $35,000 for 2026 
from the actual amount of $16,000 in 2025, the revenue has not rebounded as anticipated to 
the $71,000 actual amount in 2024. 
• Marijuana Special Sales Tax – As of July 1, 2027, the State will no longer share marijuana sales 
tax with local governments, so the City is projected to receive no more than $6,000 in marijuana 
revenue from the State in the first half of 2027.  Marijuana revenues have declined significantly 
from the 2024 actual of $55,423 to the estimated actual of $12,000 in 2026. 
• Electric Shuttle Planning Grant – The City has received a $75,000 grant award to plan for an 
electric shuttle in the City. 
• Communications Tower Ground Lease - The City Council approved a grant of easement and 
assignment of lease for the communications tower at the fire station for a one-time lump sum 
payment in the amount of $1,018,132.33 that was received on September 30, 2026.

General Fund Expenditures 
• Senior Affordable Housing Project - The City has identified a possible development partner and 
is working with the Clear Creek Regional Housing Authority regarding next steps for this project 
on property that the City now owns next to the Idaho Springs Cemetery. 
• Housing Authority Contribution – The City’s $57,000 contribution to the Clear Creek Regional 
Housing Authority has been moved to the Improvement Fund. 
• Community Promotion – Business and community promotion expenses have been moved to the 
Parking Fund. 
• Business & Community Promotion Director – This contract position will be funded entirely from 
the Parking Fund in 2027. 
• Clear Creek Fire Authority (CCFA) – The City’s contribution to the CCFA will decrease slightly 
from $396,275 in 2026 to $381,600 in 2027 for a 9-mil equivalent based on the 2026 property 
valuation. 
 
Conservation Trust Fund 
• Courtney Ryley Cooper Park Playground Improvements - Limited improvements are under 
consideration using up to $100,000 that has accumulated in the Conservation Trust Fund until 
additional funding can be secured for more comprehensive capital improvements based on the 
master plan that was completed. 
• Park Maintenance - $30,000 is allocated to the General Fund this year to help offset the cost of 
park maintenance. 
 
1% Street Sales Tax Fund 
 
• A 1% City sales tax is dedicated to the Street Fund. 
• Streets Milling and Overlay is budgeted at $500,000. 
• Debt service in the amount of $684,612 for the bonds issued in 2018 for street projects. 
 
Sales Tax Improvement Fund 
• A 1% City sales tax is dedicated to the Improvement Fund.  The negative fund balance is 
projected to be reduced significantly by the end of 2027. 
• Engine 60 Restoration – The City is re-applying for a $250,000 State Historical Fund competitive 
grant for the restoration of Engine 60, with a 25% cash match required, for a total project cost of 
$312,500.    
• Shelly Quinn Park Improvements Phase 2 – The City was awarded a $750,000 Greater Outdoors 
Colorado (GOCO) grant and will use the remaining $395,448 grant funds in the Land and Water 
Conservation Fund award as match for the construction of a tennis/pickleball court and 
playground next to the skatepark that was completed as part of phase 1 improvements.  The 
total phase 2 project cost is $1,145,448. 
• Police Simulator – The Police Department will be receiving a $25,000 grant from the State for 
the purchase of a training simulator at the new police station.  The City’s match is expected to 
be approximately $9,000.

• Fingerprint Machine – The Police Department is requesting a fingerprint machine at a cost of 
$22,500 and is seeking possible grant funding. 
• Train Shelter - $100,000 has been budgeted for a train shelter so the restored Coach 70 may be 
returned to Idaho Springs. 
• Central Hose House and Carnegie Library - $20,000 has been budgeted for exterior repairs and 
painting. 
Water Enterprise Fund 
• A 3% water rate increase and 2% wastewater rate increase as recommended by the rate 
study. 
• A 1% City sales tax is dedicated to water and sewer improvements projects and to help 
mitigate the size of rate increases.  
• Water Treatment Plant Upgrades – Replacement water filters at $120,000, and an intake 
shelter at $15,000. 
• Chicago Creek Water Main Replacement Project Phase 1 at $3,600,000.  The City has received a 
$1,000,000 EIAF grant to help fund this project.  The project is budgeted to begin in July 2027 
and to be completed in 2028. 
• Sewer Maintenance and Inspection Program at $75,000 – Contract for inspection and 
maintenance of a third of the City’s sewer mains every year as recommended by the City’s 
insurance carrier. 
• Mini-Excavator @ $30,000 – Cost split between water and wastewater. 
Parking Enterprise Fund 
• While parking fees are well below what was budgeted for 2026, they are about $70,000 higher 
than the actual for 2025 and about the same as the actual for 2024. 
• Mobility Hub Project Phase 1 – This $6,300,000 project funded by a grant from the State 
involves improvements at I-70 Exit 240 including bus stops with slip lanes on the eastbound and 
westbound on and off ramps, a transit parking lot, and a public parking lot.  Construction is 
planned to begin in the fall of 2027 and to be completed in 2028. 
• Colorado Boulevard Public Parking Lot – The final lease/purchase payments in the amount of 
$239,925 on the new public parking lot at 1856 Colorado Boulevard will be made in 2027. 
• Pedestrian Bridge over I-70 at Exit 240.  The City submitted in March a Congressionally Directed 
Spending request to Senators Hickenlooper and Bennet and to Representative Neguse for 
assistance with funding the design of a pedestrian bridge over I-70 just west of Exit 240.  Our 
request is still actively under consideration and proceeding through the federal legislative 
process. 
All Departments 
• Vacant Positions – The City currently has the following vacant positions which are not budgeted 
to be filled in 2027: 
o Deputy Treasurer/Court Clerk – The financial duties of this position have been assumed 
by the Assistant City Administrator, with assistance from the Administrative Clerk and 
Deputy City Clerk.  The court duties of this position have been assigned to the Police

Department Administrative Assistant, with assistance from the Administrative Clerk and 
the Deputy City Clerk. 
o Parks Maintenance Worker – One of these two positions has remained vacant during 
2026.  Some of the duties of this position were performed through the summer by a full-
time seasonal employee and part-time seasonal employee. 
o Police Officer – One of these positions was intended to remain vacant in 2026 to help 
fund additional pay raises for incumbent police officers in 2026. 
o Water Facilities Supervisor/Lead Operator – Due to difficulty recruiting for this position, 
the City contracted with a firm during 2026 to serve as the operator in responsible 
charge (ORC) and to perform many of the duties of this position. 
• Employee Compensation - Given that sales tax revenue and overall General Fund revenue has 
remained about the same since 2024 and given that expenses continue to increase and exceed 
revenues, no employee wage increases are proposed for 2027.  The greater Denver Area 
Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased 3.9% from July 2025 to July 2026.  Last year, the CPI was 
2.1%, and the City Council approved a 2.1% cost of living adjustment for employees. 
• Employee Benefits - Employee health insurance premiums will increase by an average of 11.5% 
in 2027.  Dental premiums will increase by 5% in 2027.  There will be no increase in 2027 for 
vision and life insurance premiums.  The City pays 80% of the employee health and dental 
premiums and 100% of the employee life insurance premiums.  The City does not share the cost 
of vision insurance. 
• Employee Retirement - In 2024, City Council approved a new retirement program that requires 
a 3% employee contribution that is matched by the City.  Employee voluntary contributions up 
to 10% are matched by the City.  To reduce costs, the proposed budget for 2026 suspends the 
City’s match above the required 3% match, a savings of $19,801 in the General Fund. 
• Long Term Disability Insurance – Several years ago, City Council approved funding a long-term 
disability insurance program for all employees, except police officers who have a separate 
program through the Fire and Police Pension Association.  To reduce costs, the proposed budget 
suspends the long-term disability insurance program for 2027, a savings of $6,170 in the General 
Fund. 
• Property/Casualty and Workers’ Compensation Insurance – After several years of dramatic 
increases in the City’s insurance costs, little change is expected with insurance premiums for 
2027.
