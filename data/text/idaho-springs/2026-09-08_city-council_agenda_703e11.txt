In-person and remote meeting public attendance and participation instructions: 
Participation 
• To provide scheduled public comment, either in person or remotely, please fill out 
and return the Public Comment Form on the City's website. All requests must be 
submitted to the City Clerk (cityclerk@idahospringsco.com) by 6 p.m. (Six) the 
Wednesday before the scheduled meeting. 
• To provide unscheduled public comment, please join the Zoom Meeting, identify 
yourself with your full first and last name, and use the “Raise Hand” feature to 
indicate your desire to speak. 
General Guidelines 
• Each public comment, whether scheduled or unscheduled, is limited to three (3) 
minutes. 
• Council typically does not provide feedback during public comment sessions.  
• If you would like to provide materials for Council to review along with your 
Comment, please sign up for Scheduled Public Comment and provide those 
materials to the City Clerk by the Wednesday Deadline.

SUBJECT:  Potential Contributions or Incentives for CCCAE Development of Roberts Brothers’ Garage 
MEETING DATE:  September 8, 2026 
 
BACKGROUND 
 
The Clear Creek Center for Arts and Education (CCCAE) is a local nonprofit organization that seeks to sponsor and bolster 
opportunities for local, regional, and national artists, performers, and craftsmen to enhance and promote visual and performing arts; 
and develop a community focused educational arts and event center as a family-oriented hub for the community. 
 
The CCCAE was awarded a $1.538 million grant from Colorado Creative Industries (CCI) for construction and capital 
improvements. They have a lease to occupy the Roberts Brothers Garage building long-term and develop arts and arts education. 
 
The CCI grant requires a 1:1 match for their $1.538 million grant award. CCCAE currently has approximately $700,000 in existing 
cash and in-kind contributions towards the match, meaning that there is an existing gap of approximately $800,000. CCCAE has 
requested that Idaho Springs help to bridge that gap through in-kind contributions, fee waivers or reductions, and other investments 
in the project that would assist in unlocking additional grant money. The maximum value of requested contributions is $729,307.  
 
This project is unlikely to set a precedent for future contributions because this is a relatively unique ask. The applicant has a grant 
for capital improvements and construction costs, which requires a cash or in-kind match. They are requesting contributions that 
would function as a match for the grant that they already received. They are not requesting a monetary donation; rather, they are 
requesting that certain fees be waived, which counts towards their required match. In many ways, this request can be seen as a 
municipal investment in the arts and in art education for local students and adults.  
 
POTENTIAL CONTRIBUTIONS 
 
A full list of potential contributions is included as an attachment to this staff memo. Contributions are divided into four main 
categories: miscellaneous in-kind contributions; utility offsets; impact offsets; and parking offsets and collaboration. The CCCAE 
has discussed the benefits that the CCCAE would provide to the city and that the city’s contributions could bring. This report lays 
out the potential costs that providing each potential benefit might have for the city. 
 
1. Miscellaneous In-Kind Contributions 
 
The CCCAE is requesting a number of miscellaneous in-kind contributions, including the donation of city staff time (e.g. time 
reviewing applications and materials), reductions in the city’s application fees, and reductions or waivers of other review, application, 
or planning fees. The cost to the city would be staff time associated with any application submitted, as the city would be donating 
staff time and waiving application fees that are met to offset staff time at meetings, completing reviews, etc. The total cost of 
contributions would be approximately $16,487, at maximum. 
 
2. Utility Offsets 
 
These water and sewer tap fees are plant investment fees paid for the maintenance and construction of water and sewer facilities in 
Idaho Springs. Currently, the city’s water and sewer plants are in good working order, but these plant investment fees would be used 
when maintenance becomes necessary.

2 
 
Based on the estimated size of water and sewer taps needed, they are estimating a total value of approximately $277,821 for this 
contribution.  
 
3. Impact Fees Offsets 
 
The city charges $5,000 per parking space required for commercial uses. These fees go towards growth-related improvements, 
facilities and equipment in the general functional area of parks, fire, police, municipal facilities, recreation, transportation, and storm 
water management. 
 
In the past, the city has been willing to apply the impact fees to public improvements associated with the project. For example, the 
Fieldhouse Apartments project applied their impact fees to roadway, sidewalk, and utility improvements. CCCAE has suggested that 
there will be substantial public benefits from the project, both in terms of providing arts and education services to the community 
and in terms of infrastructure investment in the vicinity of the building.  
 
Potential value: Based on the size of the Roberts Brothers’ Garage and the occupancy anticipated, the anticipated number of spaces 
required is 16. This would equal an impact fee of $80,000, plus an application would require an impact study that is estimated to 
cost $3,000. As such, an impact fee offset would be worth a total of $83,000 for the project. 
 
4. Parking Considerations 
 
Sec. 21-134 of the ISMC allows for a voluntary fee-in-lieu to be considered for the downtown Miner Street area. This allows for an 
organization to pay a fee in lieu of providing an off-street parking space. The fee for a Restaurant/Retail development is $20,000 per 
space, which is the closest use to the proposed CCCAE use of the building. There is not a specific fee-in-lieu amount for nonprofits 
or for event venues.  
 
Sec. 21-134 of the ISMC requires an applicant to apply for fee-in-lieu that would then be considered and acted upon by the Planning 
Commission. The CCCAE would be required to apply for fee-in-lieu to the Planning Commission. If City Council agrees to waive 
any of the fees associated with a parking fee-in-lieu approval, and if the Planning Commission approves a fee-in-lieu application, 
the applicant would not have to pay the fees associated with the approval.  
 
The CCCAE estimates that the use of the Roberts Brothers’ Garage will result in a need for 16 parking spaces. This is based on a 
calculation that the maximum occupancy for events would be ~64 occupants and the city’s parking table requires 1 parking space / 
4 occupants. When events are not scheduled, the parking demand will be lower and will be limited to staff, instructors, and 
parents/students who will occupy the space for day-to-day business.  
 
Much of the use’s parking demand will be after 6:00 PM when the city’s managed parking program is no longer in operation. The 
city currently charges for parking from 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM. Because much of their programming is expected to take place outside 
of these hours, the overall impact on the city’s parking resources is likely to be less than for other uses in the downtown area.  
 
The maximum fee-in-lieu request would be $320,000 (16 spaces at a rate of $20,000/space). Some spaces may be able to be provided 
on-site. However, they are requesting that no off-street parking be required.

• What is the total amount of contributions that City Council wants to provide to CCCAE for this project, and from what 
source(s)? 
• Does City Council want city staff to draft a policy to address future requests of this kind from nonprofits and other 
community organizations? 
 
NEXT STEPS: 
 
If City Council agrees to contribute to the project with some or all the requested considerations, city staff will draft a resolution for 
review and action at the next City Council meeting on September 15th.

Economic Impact of Rural Arts Centers 
Narrative Summary 
Across the Mountain West, rural arts centers consistently generate between $1 million and $6 million in 
annual economic activity, supporting 20 to 80 local jobs depending on scale, programming, and tourism flow. 
Communities such as Creede, Leadville, Paonia, Mancos, and Telluride demonstrate how activating a historic 
structure as an arts hub increases downtown foot traffic, strengthens restaurant and retail revenue, and 
anchors long-term economic revitalization. These outcomes are documented patterns in towns far smaller and 
less traveled than Idaho Springs. Given 29 million cars passing annually, even a modest visitor capture rate 
positions us to achieve economic gains at the upper end of the spectrum, making the transformation an arts 
center one of the most defensible and high-return investments available.

I published the draft Comprehensive Plan to solicit public comment from June 15th to July 31st. I received a few comments during 
the public comment period and made some edits to attempt to address their concerns and suggestions. I then presented an updated 
draft to the Planning Commission on August 5th for their review and edits. Some edits were suggested, which I again incorporated. 
Today’s work session is meant to get City Council feedback before the October 2nd, 2026, Planning Commission meeting, which is 
the tentative date for the Planning Commission to adopt the updated plan. City Council would finalize the process with a resolution 
at the first October meeting. From there, implementation can begin.   
 
If there are significant requests for changes, however, we can push the adoption further. For tonight’s meeting, the goal is to 
address any City Council comments or edits. 
 
This report goes over the major changes that are included in the Plan since 2017. This is a long document, so I am not including 
the entire plan in the Council packet. The entire plan document is available here: https://www.idahospringsco.com/community-
development/page/city-idaho-springs-comprehensive-plan-update. The updated objectives are included as an attachment.  
 
Background and Introduction: 
The key updates to this section are as follows: 
1. New section (pg. 2-5): Guiding Principles for the Plan 
2. New section (pg. 6): How to Use the Plan 
3. New section (pg. 10-14): Achievements Since 2017 
4. Updated all the demographic and economic information. 
5. Chapter 3: Updated the vision statements to match what City Council adopted in 2025. 
 
Land Use and Community Character:  
The key updates to this section are as follows: 
1. Adding a “principles” section that talks about architectural design and preserving community character (pg. 1-3) 
2. Updates to land use categories 
a. Kept all the same categories but created more flexibility to make it more about mass and scale of development, 
rather than the use itself.  
b. The 2017 Plan seemed to largely match the city’s land use recommendation map with the existing zoning, 
whereas now I am trying to be more proactive at allowing more flexibility where it makes sense while protecting 
areas that are largely residential and have historic characteristics we want to protect. 
3. Updates to the objectives for this section. 
 
Housing: 
There have been no major changes since 2017. We simply updated goals to reflect the fact that Proposition 123 now exists, and we 
have updated Housing Needs Assessment for the area. The major implementation item for this section is to work collaboratively 
with the County and adjacent municipalities to put together a Housing Action Plan (HAP) in the next ~12 months. Another goal is 
to maintain community character and focus on maintaining affordability within existing homes, helping current and future property 
owners keep their existing properties in good condition so that they can remain in the community for the foreseeable future.  
 
Transportation: 
There have been substantial improvements to public transit along the I-70 corridor since 2017. We now have Bustang and Pegasus 
buses running through town at least 20 times per day in each direction. We also have the County’s Roundabout bus service. The

major change for automobile travel is the Floyd Hill project that is anticipated to be complete in early 2029. The focus of 
objectives for this section is to continue growing access to public transit to and from the city, increase parking solutions, and 
overall improve connectivity within the city and along the I-70 corridor.  
 
Economic Development and Tourism: 
There are a few major updates since 2017. The Miner Street Marketplace has changed the downtown area and made it much more 
pedestrian-friendly, which is good for business. The Mighty Argo Cable Car and the Virginia Canyon Mountain Park have 
increased visitation significantly on the east end of town. With these changes in mind, there is a lot of room for additional 
improvements, and we must think about how to build upon these new draws without creating negative outcomes for local 
residents.  
 
 Environment: 
The biggest immediate concerns to be addressed are wildfire risk, drought, and water supply. These are key focus areas of the 
updated Plan for this section. These concerns existed in 2017 and remain to this day. Wildfire risk has expanded since 2017 and we 
are seeing issues with fire insurance, which is a need to be addressed with local and regional partners.  
 
Water Supply: 
This is a new state-required section that we used to estimate current water supply and demand, risks to the city’s water system, 
ways to improve sustainability and conservation, and create plans to accommodate future development without putting existing 
residents at risk. The city’s water and wastewater plants have adequate capacity for the foreseeable future and could have 
expanded use, and the Idaho Springs reservoir and other storage areas have adequate capacity, as well, but existing and probable 
future drought conditions have made water supply more of a concern for the near future. With that in mind, while the city has 
adequate capacity now and into the future, making intelligent water decisions and being considerate about growth will be 
important.  
 
Parks, Recreation, Open Space, and Trails: 
There have been a few major achievements since 2017, notably the skatepark project at the ballfields complex and the Virginia 
Canyon Mountain Park. There are desires to keep building trails – especially hiking trails – and improve access to Clear Creek. A 
long-range goal is to connect the Clear Creek Trail all the way to Golden, working with Clear Creek County and Jefferson County 
to accomplish this.  
 
Historic Resources: 
This section acknowledges the importance that history has for Idaho Springs now and going forward. This section seeks to expand 
the city’s historic protections through new historic districts that protect residential areas; to promote additional heritage tourism; 
protect the Historic District; and partner with the Historical Society to continue to promote history in the community.  
 
Public Facilities and Services: 
This is always a key focus for the city, as water and wastewater systems, the city’s street and sidewalk network, and other capital 
improvements are always in need of maintenance to keep the city’s quality of life high for residents and visitors.  
 
Strategic Growth Element: 
This is another new section that the state is requiring jurisdictions to adopt. The purpose of the section is to get communities to 
think about where growth and development could and should be promoted. The goal is to get communities to identify areas that are 
appropriate for development and areas that are not appropriate for development. While the state is broadly pro-housing and wants 
to encourage as much development as possible, they do not want to promote sprawl developments that use undeveloped lands 
(open space, forested lands, etc.) when there are redevelopment and infill development opportunities. The goal is to prioritize infill 
development in the existing city that is close to existing city infrastructure and only push for greenfield development if appropriate.

I analyzed all the lots in the city based on a few different metrics. First, where are the vacant and underutilized “opportunity” sites 
that could be redeveloped within city limits. These opportunity sites are those that could accept additional density or intensity of 
uses, e.g. a single-family home on a commercially zoned property.  
 
Second, I looked at how close these lots were to existing infrastructure. If adequate water and sewer facilities and other utilities 
were available nearby, I identified them as a higher-potential property for redevelopment. Other properties would require 
infrastructure investment, increasing development costs. Here is the map breaking those sites up into those with infrastructure and 
those with improvements needed: 
 
This map shows us a few things. 
 
1. Red properties: These are the most common opportunities identified for new development and redevelopment but because 
they have existing uses on-site, the timeframe for redevelopment on these parcels is probably longer term. If a property 
owner wanted to redevelop their properties, they certainly could because there is adequate water and sewer infrastructure 
in the vicinity, but it would require demolition or rehabilitation of existing buildings on-site to accommodate additional 
density or more intense uses. Over time, these properties may be redeveloped and if they are, they could accommodate 
more density.  
a. Note that many of these parcels are small (about 50% are under 5,000 square feet) and would be unlikely to 
accommodate substantial density increases.  
b. The east end of town has the largest concentration of larger properties that could be redeveloped and are clearly 
underutilized. This is a key area identified for infill and redevelopment, as shown with the East End Overlay

processes, like a rezoning.

City of Idaho Springs Comprehensive Plan Objectives

The Comprehensive Plan lays out 37 objectives that the city will seek to accomplish over the duration of the plan. Within these 38 objectives, there are

LAND USE AND COMMUNITY CHARACTER OBJECTIVES 
Objective: Ensure that new developments are compatible with surrounding developments, promote the most efficient use of 
land, and meet the city’s strategic growth and water supply objectives. 
a. New development or redevelopment should follow the land use recommendations in the City of Idaho Springs Land Use and Character Area Map.  
b. New development in a neighborhood should complement the existing neighborhood’s mass and scale.  
c. The city will support infill development that takes advantage of existing infrastructure and services by considering incentive programs to encourage 
appropriate redevelopment.  
d. The city will seek public / private partnerships to foster more efficient use of land and existing structures, such as adaptive reuse of the previous 
middle and high school building for housing and community uses.  
Objective: Improve neighborhood livability and quality of life. 
a. The city will work on regulations that require thoughtful landscaping standards to require new developments to beautify the adjacent public realm 
and right-of-way areas. 
b. The city will evaluate locations that would benefit from public infrastructure (trash cans, public benches, etc.) that promote walkability and 
opportunities for social interaction.  
Objective: Beautify, preserve, and activate the Historic District. 
a. The city will encourage residential units above 
b.  ground floor commercial uses by auditing current regulations and considering potential revisions to stimulate demand, including parking 
reductions, reduction in tap fees or building permit fees, or other potential incentive programs. 
c. The city will implement wayfinding infrastructure to better identity the location of less well-known areas of the city, including the Charlie Tayler 
Water Wheel Park and other off-the-beaten path locations around the city. 
d. The city will implement the adopted Downtown Plan designs into Miner Street to better activate the Miner Street Marketplace to improve the 
pedestrian experience in the historic downtown.  
Objective: Upgrade the appearance of Idaho Springs as viewed from I-70.

a. New development with significant visibility from I-70 should be designed with the appearance of facing the Interstate rather than backing to it.  
b. The city will work to activate the Idaho Mall as a pedestrian-oriented space fronting I-70 and rear parking lots using parklets, informational or 
vendor kiosks, landscaping and public realm space (seating, signage, historical artifacts) to create a barrier and sense of pedestrian enclosure 
between the paved walkway at the south perimeter of the Historic District between 15th and 17th Avenues and the large parking area adjacent to

c. The city will work to enhance the aesthetics of surface parking lots at the south side of town as viewed from I-70 through landscaping, art 
installations, and other enhancements will be a priority in the next two years 
Objective: Create welcoming, active public spaces through public art installations at city-owned properties. 
a. The city will work with local artists and art organizations to establish a public art plan for Idaho Springs. 
b. The city will promote art installations at city-owned historic sites and facilities to further activate these sites and bring additional visitors to these 
areas. 
c. Using art, the city will work to connect different areas of Idaho Springs through a coordinated public art network. This could include art activations 
along the Clear Creek Trail connecting downtown Miner Street to the East End, murals in the downtown area, and other installations as people 
travel through town.  
HOUSING OBJECTIVES 
Objective: Maintain a diverse housing stock. 
a. The city will address housing needs at all AMIs through partnership with Clear Creek County, Georgetown, Silver Plume, Empire, the Clear Creek 
Regional Housing Authority, DRCOG, nonprofit groups, and other relevant groups. 
b. The city will research and consider alternative homeownership opportunities that create shared equity, such as cohousing, co-living, community 
land trusts, rent-to-own, community REITs, and other opportunities. 
c. The city will explore regulations that incentivize the construction of Accessory Dwelling Units through fee waivers, water tap reductions, expedited 
permitting, etc. and will adopt regulations that are most likely to result in more ADU development. 
d. The city will continue to monitor and enforce STR regulations in the City to ensure that housing stock remains available for long-term occupancy.  
e. The city will focus on a mix of housing types – single-family detached, single-family attached, lower density multifamily, and higher density 
multifamily units to provide a diversity of options to current and future residents. 
f. The city will consider programs to improve existing mobile homes in the city to create better quality housing for residents of these types of units. 
We will consider allowing new mobile homes to replace existing mobile homes or programs to replace existing mobile homes with new factory-
built or manufactured housing.  
g. The city will encourage a diverse range of housing types within residential neighborhoods while ensuring that new development is compatible with 
surrounding properties through appropriate regulation of building scale, height, setbacks, massing, parking, landscaping, access, and architectural 
character.

Objective: Increase the availability of housing in Idaho Springs to build or preserve at least 109 new affordable housing units 
to the city by 2045 to meet the city’s Housing Needs Assessment goals.  
a. Updates to the city’s Inclusionary Housing requirements will be considered that either require a certain number of units to be affordable or require 
fees-in-lieu of housing units with new development proposals.  
b. The city will use the adopted DRCOG Regional Housing Needs Assessment to promote construction of dwelling units that meet the needs of the 
community. 
c. The city will work with regional partners to create a Housing Action Plan (HAP) that addresses the community’s housing needs by January 1, 
2028.  
d. The city will prioritize the preservation of Naturally Occurring Affordable Housing (NOAH) to ensure that existing homes that are affordable to 
residents remain affordable into the future while maintaining the quality of existing housing stock. The city will explore ways to fund the 
preservation and rehabilitation of NOAH units, with the goal of preserving at least one unit per year, on average.  
Objective: Preserve and renovate existing housing stock. 
a. The city will work to promote neighborhood historic districts and designation of existing historic homes in the city and work with property owners 
of historic properties to secure funding for rehabilitation and preservation efforts.  
b. City staff will explore ways to promote the rehabilitation and renovation of legally existing non-conforming multifamily units in appropriate 
locations within single family neighborhoods.  
c. Where existing homes need to be rehabilitated, the city will promote redevelopment that is consistent with the neighborhood’s existing scale, 
massing, and architectural design. 
d. The city will continue to regularly update and actively enforce the building codes, as may be amended which requires the application of minimum 
health, safety, and welfare standards to all new construction; reconstruction; historic preservation/ renovation; housing rehabilitation/adaptive 
reuse, and/or the removal of unsafe, unsanitary substandard structures. 
Objective: Pursue programs to allow seniors the opportunity to “age in place.” 
a. The city will participate in efforts to locate and develop adequate, affordable facilities for seniors, including independent adult housing, assisted 
living facilities, and elderly care facilities and programs.  
b. The city will create incentives and opportunities for the development of senior housing or assisted living so that there are more age-in-place 
opportunities.

Objective: Develop and maintain safe, multimodal, attractive and efficient roadways. 
a. The city will ensure that streets effectively accommodate transit, pedestrian, bicycle and other modes of transportation safely.  
b. For future transportation projects, the city will require that traffic calming techniques be considered to create a multimodal streetscape that promotes 
cycling, walking, and transit in addition to vehicular travel.  
c. Recreational access and oversized vehicle parking at State Highway 103 will be improved and optimized in accordance with the city’s Parking

d. The city will continue to support advances in transportation technology, such as Autonomous Vehicles and Smart Cars.

Objective: Make it feasible to visit or live in Idaho Springs without a car.  
a. The city will partner with other agencies and governments to develop, operate and maintain public transportation options within Idaho Springs and 
the I-70 corridor. 
b. The city will work with CDOT to construct the regional mobility hub at Exit 240 to optimize transportation infrastructure in the city. 
c. Strategies to encourage additional transit use by residents, employees, and visitors of the city to reduce car-dependency will be prioritized. The 
city will promote transit ridership to the city by offering incentives or discounts to visitors arriving to the city without a car.  
d. The city will continue its partnership with CDOT to create a program for free rides for locals and commuters to increase local ridership by at least 
10% over the next ten years.  
e. To encourage car-free travel along the I-70 corridor and promote overnight stays during the winter months, the city will coordinate with CDOT to 
create a Snowstang stop in Idaho Springs so locals and visitors can stay in Idaho Springs and access nearby ski resorts without a car. 
f. The city will work with Clear Creek County to optimize the Roundabout bus service to increase ridership and increase the frequency of buses into 
Idaho Springs, making it a more viable option for local residents.  
g. In coordination with the Housing Element, the city will work to site new housing developments within ¼-mile of existing transit and bring 
additional transit options to higher-density areas of the city.  
Objective: Manage the supply, operations, and demand for parking to encourage economic vitality, safety, and livability. 
a. The city will consider reduced parking requirements for new or expanded uses in the Historic District, expanding on the existing parking fee-in-
lieu program.  
b. The city will continue working toward a parking structure in the vicinity of the Downtown area and elsewhere in the city. 
c. In conjunction with a circulator shuttle, the city will identify satellite lot locations that could accommodate parking without impacting existing 
community character in the city’s core areas.

Objective: Support tourism and special event activities. 
a. The city will work with neighboring communities to promote tourism and other events in cooperation with Clear Creek County, Georgetown, 
Central City, and others to create a sustainable regional economy and increase visitation across the County. 
b. The city will promote, develop and market the use of Idaho Springs’ historic and recreational assets. 
c. The city will leverage digital marketing and storytelling through Visit Idaho Springs, social media, and partner channels to strengthen destination

d. The city will conduct a study of the impact of various industries on the city’s local economy, including heritage tourism, mountain biking, rafting,

Objective: Incentivize economic development and supporting local businesses while protecting the Idaho Springs quality of life. 
a. The city will put together an updated Capital Improvements Plan (CIP), which will include an inventory of infrastructure improvements needed to 
redevelop vacant and available properties.  
b. City staff will review and modify zoning and subdivision regulations to support home-based and small business development, including home-
based childcare solutions. 
c. The city will work with community organizations, nonprofits, and other entities to design and implement childcare improvements to support local

d. The city will promote more overnight stays through coordination with the Clear Creek Tourism Bureau, Loveland Ski Area, CDOT’s Bustang and 
Snowstang services, and local businesses to position Idaho Springs as an ideal weekend or vacation destination near the Front Range.  
e. The city will focus on workforce retention and job creation to increase the number of skilled trades, local employment opportunities, and increase 
the number of residents who are able to work locally. 
 
Objective: Support continued redevelopment of the East End business district. 
a. The city will prioritize and strategize how to encourage development in the East End Overlay District (EEOD). This includes formation of an

of an available properties database. 
The city will promote activated storefronts and additional housing opportunities on the East End of the city to build upon existing and new businesses 
coming into the area. 
ENVIRONMENT OBJECTIVES 
Objective: Ensure that development does not aggravate, accelerate, or increase the level of risk from natural hazards. 
a. Working with FEMA and the Army Corps of Engineers, the city will work to create clearer floodplain development regulations to ensure that 
property owners within a flood zone understand what is required to develop in those areas. 
Page 33 of 62
 
b. The city will work to create wetland and navigable water buffers to ensure that development near sensitive waterways and wetland areas does not 
cause environmental harm. 
c. The city will work to create regulations that limit or mitigate construction on slopes of greater than 30% and other areas identified as having 
hazardous slopes or soil conditions 
d. The city will work to participate in the National Flood Insurance Program’s Community Rating System (CRS) - this is a voluntary, incentive-based 
community program that recognizes, encourages, and rewards local floodplain management activities that exceed minimum standards.  
e. The city will coordinate with first responders to inform and educate citizens on accomplishing defensible space on private properties. This can be 
done through meetings, special “wildfire days” involving fire department and forest service officials, mailings, or other events.  
f. The city will create a hazard mapping/GIS database by coordinating with USGS, Clear Creek County and CDOT to further study and map 
vulnerable areas. 
 
Objective: Encourage effective management of air quality and the impacts of light and noise. 
a. The city will work with property owners and CDOT to create a plan to reduce the impacts of noise coming from activities along I-70. This may 
include the installation of berms, sound attenuating structures or fencing.  
b. Stronger noise regulations will be pursued to align with state noise requirements and reduce noise impacts within city limits. 
c. The city will develop “dark sky” lighting regulations that would apply to the height of light poles, intensity of the luminaries, types of bulbs, light 
shielding, and maximum allowed foot-candles from property. 
d. New city infrastructure will be implemented to reduce noise, air, and light pollution on city properties and within the city’s vehicle fleet. 
Objective: Minimize and/or mitigate impacts on wildlife, their habitats and migration corridors. 
a. The city will work to develop regulations that require new developments to consider the existing species found within the immediate area and take 
actions to mitigate any potential negative impacts to wildlife.  
b. The city will encourage the use of bear-resistant containers, especially in human-bear conflict areas, working with local trash and recycling 
companies to provide solutions that better address wildlife interactions in the community.  
Objective: Work to reduce wildfire risk and increase wildfire resiliency in Idaho Springs. 
a. The city will put together resources for local homeowners to improve defensible space, home hardening, and other wildfire resiliency actions on a 
home-by-home basis by establishing an incentive and education program to help homeowners improve their property’s resiliency against wildfires. 
b. The city will work with the Clear Creek Fire Authority and other agencies to promote and implement roadside mitigation actions to improve 
residents’ ability to evacuate effectively. 
c. Working with the Clear Creek Fire Authority, Clear Creek County, USFS, and other local land management agencies, the city will perform 
landscape-scale mitigation projects to link existing, mitigated areas together in alignment with USFS risk reduction goals.  
d. The city will work to adopt updated landscaping regulations that require native plant species and species that are fire/drought tolerant to reduce the 
risk of wildfire within the built environment.

WATER SUPPLY OBJECTIVES AND CONSERVATION POLICIES 
Objective: Protect, preserve, and conserve the city’s surface water and groundwater resources. 
a. The city will continue to actively participate with the Upper Clear Creek Watershed Association, and the Clear Creek Watershed Foundation.  
b. The city will develop an education and information campaign to help the public to reduce point-source pollution and pollution from stormwater 
runoff. 
c. The city will review the recommendations of the Idaho Springs Source Water Protection Plan and consider action to implement recommendations. 
These include establishing procedures regarding spills, updating floodplain regulations, and providing maps of the source water protection area to 
adjacent municipalities with requests for notification of development activities occurring within this area.  
d. The city will develop best management practices (BMP) manuals for water and groundwater management and will pursue inclusion of critical 
BMPs into the Municipal Code over the next three years. 
e. In the next five years, the city will update the city’s water and sewer line CIP rating assessment to maintain an accurate and up-to-date list of 
necessary infrastructure improvements around the city.  
f. The city will continue to work with CDOT to limit and mitigate water quality impacts from Interstate 70.  
Objective: Implement water efficiency planning in the city to ensure that current Idaho Springs water rights and capacities can 
continue to support current and future developments.  
a. The city will continue to develop and implement drought restrictions to create a set of conditions under which water restrictions will be required. 
This is a high priority goal for the first-year post-adoption to build upon existing Phase One voluntary strategies to reduce water use.  
b. The city will work with the City Council to consider updates to the city’s water restriction ordinance that can be considered to reduce per capita 
demand for water through irrigation limitations, low water landscaping requirements, and other factors that can reduce water use.  
c. The city will begin work on a water efficiency plan to assess the ways that the city, businesses, and residents can reduce water demand through 
infrastructure investments (low flow / high efficiency appliances, demand management, etc.). 
d. In concert with the above strategies, the city will put together a factsheet/guide that helps inform water users on voluntary measures that can help 
reduce water demand and improve efficiency within the city’s system.

HISTORIC RESOURCES ELEMENT OBJECTIVES 
Objective: Coordinate with local and regional stakeholders to ensure that historic preservation work in Idaho Springs is 
prioritized. 
a. The city will continue to work with the Historic Society of Idaho Springs and other entities to preserve, protect and improve the historic resources 
of the community. 
b. The city will coordinate with other governmental entities such as Clear Creek County, CDOT, EPA, FHWA so that the historic character of Idaho 
Springs is understood and accounted for in the design of public projects.  
 
Objective: Continue to protect & preserve the historic resources of the Idaho Springs area, increasing the number of designated 
properties by at least 10% over the next 10 years. 
a. The city will evaluate additional historic areas and individual landmarks for potential designation by City Council. We will identify funding sources 
to undertake an update of existing historic resources survey data and prepare designation proposals. 
b. The city will engage property owners in the process of designating additional historic districts and landmarks.  
c. The city will establish and publish clear guidance for property owners and project developers in buffer areas surrounding historic districts and 
landmarks so that the historic qualities of Idaho Springs are enhanced rather than harmed by physical changes in the City. 
d. The city will work on the creation of materials that can assist property owners in assessing whether designation would be beneficial to them, 
including process guidelines, toolkits explaining tax credits, sample/template residential historic district documents, etc. 
e. The city will prioritize continued maintenance of city-owned sites and facilities, relying on the Historic Sites and Facilities Board to provide 
recommendations and expertise. 
 
Objective: Promote the benefits of historic preservation through education and outreach. 
a. The city will identify and promote incentives for local property owners to assist with preservation efforts. 
b. The city will create educational materials for property owners and project developers which clearly explain the benefits and the process for 
obtaining tax credits and grants associated with renovation, preservation and upkeep of historic properties.  
c. With the Sites and Facilities Board as the lead, the city will work to promote increased visitation and activation of city-owned historic sites and 
facilities to highlight historic resources citywide through the creation of wayfinding, signage, and educational materials.  
d. The city will prepare point of contact materials for visitors to Idaho Springs highlighting the historic qualities of the city.  Expand the content of 
interpretive materials beyond architectural history to include information on the individuals and groups of past residents who have left their marks 
on Idaho Springs. 
Page 37 of 62
 
e. The city will provide educational opportunities to local property owners, realtors, mortgage companies, and other key stakeholders to ensure that 
current and future property owners are aware of and understand the restrictions – and opportunities – that come with owning a historic property.  
 
Objective: Balance growth and economic development with historic preservation to ensure that future growth does not come at 
the expense of historic character, historic resources, or historic sites and facilities. 
a. The city will perform a code audit to determine regulations and requirements that can be strengthened to ensure that historic resources are 
considered as part of all development applications.  
b. Development adjacent to historic districts shall provide a compatible transition in scale, massing, site design, and architectural character while 
allowing contemporary architectural expression. The city will work to address this goal by clarifying and strengthening existing codes.  
c. Strengthening building permit and zoning processes to require property owners to consider the historic character of Idaho Springs will be 
prioritized. 
d. The city will work with the state and other potential entities to create an incentive program for architecturally compatible design and/or 
rehabilitation projects that take into account the city’s Design Guidelines. 
e. The city will implement the eight (8) recommendations and priorities from the 2022 Historic Resources Survey Plan. 
PUBLIC FACILITIES AND SERVICES OBJECTIVES 
Objective: Provide an effective and balanced system of community facilities, services and utilities to meet the current and future 
needs of the community and all its citizens. 
a. The city will evaluate existing services that determine areas that have the capacity for future development and continue to upgrade public 
infrastructure systems as needed to allow redevelopment in areas of the city that currently suffer from a lack of adequate infrastructure.  
b. The city will consider regulations that encourage renewal energy such as hydroelectric power on Chicago Creek, solar panels on city-owned 
properties, and wind power on hilltops.  
c. To assess the future potential for annexations and developments outside the city’s historic core, the city will work with Xcel Energy to evaluate 
utility line service extensions in terms of long-term costs including maintenance and operations.  
d. The city will monitor water and wastewater enterprise funds to ensure adequate revenues are generated to address debt service, operational and 
maintenance expenses.  
e. The city will work to finish paving all city-maintained streets and roads to create a uniform street system with similar maintenance needs.  
f. The city will conduct an updated Capital Improvements Plan (CIP) to assess the current condition of city streets, water and sewer lines, and other 
utility infrastructure.  
g. The city will assess the city’s street and sidewalk system to determine compliance with Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) guidance and best 
practices. 
 
Objective: Locate and design new public facilities and utilities to decrease cost, increase efficiency and reduce impacts on the 
surrounding community. 
a. The city will construct utilities and communication equipment to minimize visual obtrusiveness by utilizing stealth technology, coloring equipment, 
locating away from residential areas, and/or screening to blend with the surroundings.  
Page 38 of 62
 
b. The city will coordinate with the County, Special Districts and other quasi-governmental agencies in relocation, siting and possible co-location of 
public facilities such as the County bus maintenance facility, the police department and public works buildings and combination of the Clear Creek 
Fire and EMS Stations. 
 
Objective: Consider the role that special districts play in providing services and infrastructure necessary for future growth, 
development, and programming within City Limits. 
a. The city will work with local special districts to ensure that both city and district goals are met, with a goal of furthering partnerships in a way that 
improves the delivery of services from both parties. 
b. The city will consider regulations and policies regarding metropolitan districts to ensure that necessary infrastructure associated with new 
developments does not cause an undue financial burden on existing residents outside the development’s boundaries or for future residents of said 
developments. Regulations may include a cap on the maximum operating and debt mill levies, limits on the types of costs eligible for 
reimbursement, a requirement for plain-language disclosures to homebuyers, and other programs meant to ensure that costs are not unreasonably 
levied on current or future city residents.  
c. The city will ensure that new development pays an equitable share of the infrastructure needed to support growth while protecting the City's long-
term financial health and will support development that provides high-quality public infrastructure concurrent with new residential growth. 
d. The city will consider creating standards for the establishment of new metropolitan districts for residential development, based on updated research 
and data collection.  
 
Objective: Improve the city’s resiliency through crisis and emergency management planning. 
a. The city will work with the other jurisdictions of Clear Creek County, the Clear Creek Fire Authority, Clear Creek EMS, and other stakeholders 
to implement the strategies from the 2022 Hazard Mitigation Plan and 2025 Clear Creek County Community Wildfire Protection Plan to improve 
the city’s resilience against various hazards.  
b. The city will provide public outreach of local hazards to educate local residents and homeowners on ways to mitigate against hazards, promoting 
specific actions owners can take to reduce the impact of a natural disaster.  
 
STRATEGIC GROWTH ELEMENT OBJECTIVES 
Objective: The city will increase density within City Limits by redeveloping underutilized and vacant properties through infill 
development that have the infrastructure and size necessary for redevelopment. 
a. Growth, when it occurs, should be prioritized first through infill and redevelopment.  
b. Existing infrastructure should be used before extending new infrastructure.  
c. Development should be concentrated in walkable areas.  
d. Infrastructure costs should be borne by growth, rather than paid for by existing residents or properties.  
e. Underutilized or vacant city-owned properties will be prioritized for redevelopment when not needed for a municipal purpose. Affordable 
workforce housing will be the top priority for these types of properties. 
f. The city will explore the development of an incentive program that encourages redevelopment of underutilized and vacant properties already in 
city limits to encourage property owners and developers to consider projects that meet the city’s goals for strategic growth. 
Page 39 of 62
 
g. The city will continue to promote redevelopment of the East End Overlay District in accordance with Chapter 26 of the Idaho Springs Municipal 
Code. If redevelopment is slow to progress, the city will further changes to the EEOD to encourage additional investment in this area. 
 
Objective: Consider ways to discourage growth that does not meet the state’s definition of strategic. 
a. The city will work to put together regulations and requirements that consider environmental constraints to growth, such as steep slopes, wetland 
or floodplain areas, or other environmentally sensitive conditions. 
b. The city will work with the Planning Commission and City Council to create a framework or guide that identifies appropriate site characteristics 
that are consistent with strategic growth to bring more clarity to developers and residents about the sorts of projects that meet the city’s strategic 
growth goals.  
c. The city will promote infill development in areas that have already experienced infrastructure investment (roads, water and sewer infrastructure, 
etc.) and can handle additional capacity without needing infrastructure upgrades. 
d. The city will work with Clear Creek County to identify areas outside of existing city limits that could accommodate additional growth while 
meeting both City and County goals for development.  
e. The city will only consider development of undeveloped lands outside city limits where existing city infrastructure is already available or could be 
extended without substantial city costs.  
f. The city will consider new regulations related to metropolitan districts to ensure that any infrastructure required with future developments are 
properly funded to avoid future financial strain or responsibility on the city or its residents.  
g. In concert with the water supply element, the city will identify areas with suitable water and wastewater infrastructure and prioritize redevelopment 
in these areas.  
h. Future development outside the city’s core that requires infrastructure investment will need to be funded by developers to avoid spending city 
resources on undeveloped or greenfield properties.  
i. The city will consider incentive programs for infill and redevelopment projects that are in areas that do not require additional infrastructure 
investment. 
ANNEXATIONS 
Objective: Develop, update and implement an annexation policy 
a. Update or review the Three Mile Plan annually.  
b. Incorporate open, public meetings early in the annexation process to identify and address issues of concern.  
c. Ensure that the analysis of any annexation considers the long-term costs and benefits of annexation.  
d. Support the annexation of developments that provide a net long-term benefit to the community.  
e. Ensure that all agreements and commitments between the City and applicants clearly identify the responsibilities and roles of each party.
