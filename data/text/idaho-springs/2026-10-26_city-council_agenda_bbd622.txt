Re: Request for Consideration of Parking Arrangement Associated with Roberts Brothers Garage 
 
Dear Mayor, Council Members, and City Staff: 
 
On behalf of Clear Creek Center for Arts & Education (CCCAE), we would like to express our appreciation for 
the City's ongoing partnership and the thoughtful conversations regarding the future of the Roberts 
Brothers Garage property. 
 
CCCAE exists to expand access to arts, culture, creativity, and lifelong learning opportunities for residents of 
all ages throughout Clear Creek County. Through educational programming, performances, exhibitions, 
artist residencies, workshops, youth engagement, and community gatherings, CCCAE works to strengthen 
the social, cultural, and economic vitality of our mountain communities. Our vision for the Roberts Brothers 
Garage is not simply the renovation of a historic building, but the creation of a year-round community asset 
that serves local residents, supports artists and educators, attracts visitors, and contributes to the vibrancy 
of downtown Idaho Springs. 
 
The donation of the Roberts Brothers Garage by Four Bairs, Inc. represents a rare and transformational 
investment in the future of our community. Combined with the grant funding CCCAE has already secured, 
this project presents an opportunity to leverage private philanthropy, public investment, and community 
partnerships. It will create a lasting civic resource that would be difficult to achieve through any single 
funding source or individual actions alone . 
 
Previous discussions with Four Bairs Inc. explored the possibility of exchanging or relocating onsite parking 
spaces located on the south side of the Roberts Brothers Garage property in anticipation of a future 
municipal parking structure that might eventually occupy adjacent City-owned property. Since those 
discussions occurred, circumstances have changed. Due to federal NEPA-related requirements associated 
with the parking structure project, as well as the donation of the property to CCCAE, the original concept of 
a future parking exchange tied to redevelopment of the site is no longer feasible. 
 
While that specific arrangement is no longer available, we believe there remains a unique opportunity for a 
partnership that provides substantial benefit to both the City and the broader community. 
As part of the donation agreement with Four Bairs, Inc., CCCAE must provide a specified number of parking 
spaces through a long-term covenant. If the City were willing to allow CCCAE to satisfy a portion of that 
parking obligation through a swap of space in the adjacent City parking lot, CCCAE would be able to 
repurpose a portion of the Roberts Brothers Garage site for community-serving uses rather than dedicate

This additional space would be used to support arts, educational, and cultural programming that directly 
benefits residents and visitors. Potential uses include: 
 Outdoor workshop and garden (think Central City Opera outdoor space). 
 Flexible indoor/outdoor teaching artist workspace. 
 Secure onsite storage and staging area.  
 
Equally important, this arrangement would maximize the impact of the grant funding already secured for 
the project. The planned renovation of the Roberts Brothers Garage represents only a portion of the total 
investment CCCAE is positioned to make in the property. By reducing the amount of land that must be 
dedicated to parking infrastructure, more of our available resources can be directed toward facilities, 
programming, and community amenities that provide lasting public benefit. 
 
This request creates an exceptional public return on a relatively small contribution. The space could 
transform into active community use that supports arts education, cultural programming, workforce 
creativity, tourism, and public engagement. The City would not only help satisfy a covenant obligation 
associated with the property's donation, but would help unlock the highest and best community use of a 
once-in-a-generation charitable gift. Every square foot made available for programming translates into 
greater community access, more educational opportunities, and increased utilization of grant funds. 
 
The City's consideration of this request unlocks the full potential of a significant philanthropic donation, 
leverage substantial grant investments already awarded for the benefit of Idaho Springs and Clear Creek 
County residents and this historic property. CCCAE believes this represents the type of public-private 
partnership that can create lasting value for generations.  
 
The return on this collaboration extends well beyond parking; it results in a more active, vibrant, and 
accessible community asset for residents and visitors alike. 
 
We welcome the opportunity to work collaboratively toward a solution that advances both the City's long-
term goals and CCCAE's mission to expand access to arts, culture, and education throughout our 
community. 
 
Thank you for your consideration and continued partnership.

1. Public Hearing: Ordinance #20, Series 2026 An Ordinance designating property known as 121 15th 
Avenue (Roberts Brothers Garage) as a local historic preservation site. 
Councilmember Manifold left Council chambers for this hearing.   
Mayor Harmon opened the public hearing at 7:15 pm. 
Staff: Community Development Planner Dylan Graves went over his staff report with council.    The 
Clear Creek Center for Arts and Education (CCCAE) and the owners of 121 15th Avenue (Four Bairs 
Inc) have requested historic designation of the property at 121 15th Avenue (otherwise known as the 
Roberts Brothers Garage) and has petitioned the City to consider designation. The garage is intended to 
be rehabilitated into a community arts and education venue, and designation will open additional 
funding sources, including historic tax credits and grant opportunities. 
 
The ISMC Sec. 22-22(A) states that a property may be designated if it: 
1. Is associated with events that have made a significant contribution to the broad patterns of the 
City's history; 
2. Is associated with the lives of persons significant in the City's past; 
3. Embodies the distinctive characteristics of a type, period or method of construction; 
4. Represents the work of a master; 
5. Possesses high artistic values; 
6. Represents a significant and distinguishable entity whose components may lack individual 
distinction; or 
7. Has yielded, or may be likely to yield, information important in prehistory or history. 
 
The Roberts Brothers Garage appears to meet Criterion #1 and #3. The City’s Historic Preservation 
Review Commission (HPRC) reviewed this request at their most recent meeting and unanimously 
recommended that 121 15th Avenue be designated as a local historic site. 
 
APPLICANT: The applicant did not provide any testimony. 
PUBLIC COMMENT: There was no in person or virtual public comment for this hearing.   
Mayor Harmon closed the public hearing at 7:17 pm 
DISCUSSION: There was no discussion on this hearing. 
MOTION: Councilmember Mariani moved to approve Ordinance #20, Series 2026 An Ordinance 
designating property known as 121 15th Avenue (Roberts Brothers Garage) as a local historic 
preservation site.  Councilmember Reagon seconded.  Second followed by an all in favor roll call vote 
(Manifold not voting).
