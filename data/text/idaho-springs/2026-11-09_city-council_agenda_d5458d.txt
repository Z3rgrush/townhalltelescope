 Subject:  Rural Technical Assistance Program (RTAP) Update 
The City applied and was recently accepted into the Rural Technical Assistance Program (RTAP) for 
2026-2027 that is part of the Colorado Outdoor Recreation Industry Office of the Colorado Office of 
Economic Development and International Trade (OEDIT).  Following is the program summary: 
The program aims to revitalize Colorado’s rural economies by leveraging their local outdoor 
recreation opportunities. 
RTAP partners with graduate students from the Masters of the Environment (MENV) 
program at CU Boulder, CSU Extension, and the U.S. EPA’s Recreation Economy for Rural 
Communities (RERC) program to provide planning and strategy assistance for outdoor 
recreation and Main Street development in selected rural communities. 
Graduate students take on the role of community consultants and walk their partner 
communities through a facilitated planning assistance process that is based upon the RERC 
program, to develop a Community Action Plan for the next 12-24 months. CSU Extension 
provides support for the subsequent 2-year period to assist partner communities with the 
implementation of the Community Action Plan and to evaluate program deliverables.  
All three phases are intended to create a solid framework for communities to respond to the 
opportunities and challenges presented by outdoor recreation by prioritizing common 
goals, connecting stakeholders, and identifying resources to support the implementation of 
the Community Action Plan. 
Staff had a kick-off meeting with RTAP representatives on October 15.  One of the first tasks in the 
process is to form a Community Steering Committee with 12 – 15 members including 
representation from elected and appointed government officials, community groups, institutions, 
environmental and recreation groups, and local businesses. 
Attached is the proposed RTAP Steering Committee for Idaho Springs for your review and 
consideration of approval as an action item during the regular meeting this evening.

SUBJECT:  Idaho Springs CODA Standards 
MEETING DATE:  November 9, 2026 
 
BACKGROUND 
Sadie Schultz, Director of Business and Community Promotion, and I have met twice with the Business and 
Community Promotions Board (BCPB) concerning the existing Communal Outdoor Dining Area (CODA) 
standards found in Chapter 9 of the Idaho Springs Municipal Code (ISMC). We discussed whether the BCPB 
would be interested in potential changes to the City’s current system and if there is interest in updated standards 
and/or regulations to improve safety, durability, and aesthetics within the Miner Street Marketplace area. The 
BCPB is interested in pursuing some updates, so Sadie and I wanted to run this past City Council for direction 
before proceeding. 
 
We have discussed adding the following: 
1. Safety standards  
2. Durability standards 
3. Cleanliness standards 
4. Emergency access standards 
5. Potential aesthetic standards for historically compatible materials and other activation items 
 
I spoke with the City’s attorneys, who indicated that a code amendment will not be necessary. The City can 
require standards by updating the City’s existing encroachment permit agreement. If the Council wants the 
BCPB to have some role in developing the standards, Council can direct the BCPB to do so by motion. The 
BCPB could then take that direction and draft suggested Encroachment Standards for CODA encroachments. 
Once reviewed by City staff, the attorneys, etc., the updated Standards would be added to the encroachment 
agreement template. 
 
The current encroachment agreement permit application is included as an attachment. The current application 
requires that a site plan be attached, including as applicable, length, width, height, placement and location, 
including distances from other improvements upon the abutting property and adjacent properties and distance 
from existing established travelled street and sidewalk, lighting, illumination, trash plan, ADA Compliance, 
description of external barrier (if applicable) and type of construction. The current application requires that the 
area “be shown to keep with the historic theme and use historic elements,” but otherwise does not set standards 
for encroachment elements.  
 
The following standards below are recommended based on past conversations. These would be included as an 
addendum to the encroachment agreement permit application, and applicants will be required to agree to meet 
these standards. 
 
SAFETY / DURABILITY 
These are suggested mandatory standards that any encroachment area would need to meet.  
 
All CODA components shall be: 
• Commercial-grade  
• Weather-resistant

CLEANLINESS 
All vendors with an encroachment agreement agree to keep their area clean and free of trash, debris, 
construction equipment, or other non-approved materials. Vendors further agree that any materials that are 
damaged, moved due to weather or other forces, or are otherwise altered be addressed in a timely manner.  
 
EMERGENCY ACCESS 
The Clear Creek Fire Authority (CCFA) has already established their standards for emergency access. 
Formalizing this in the policy document adopted by the BCPB would be all the City needs to do to ensure that 
this is enforceable and continues to be met.  
 
CURB CLEAR ZONE REQUIREMENTS 
All activations in an encroachment area must agree to maintain clear zone near the existing street curbs to 
ensure that drainage is maintained and avoid ponding or icing. The encroachment agreement shall require that 
an approved encroachment keep a 12” high by 12” wide clear zone directly adjacent to the curb that is not 
blocked with any activation items to allow for drainage. Any encroachments that do not maintain this one 
square foot clear zone will be considered to be in violation of their encroachment agreement permit. No tent, 
table, canopy, sign, A-frame, merchandise, or stanchion shall be placed, staked, or anchored within the clear 
zone.  
 
START AND END DATE COMMITMENT 
Anyone establishing an encroachment agreement on Miner Street must agree to keep their activation through 
the entire season, which is determined by the BCPB. The BCPB will establish an opening date for the Miner 
Page 15 of 133
4 
 
Street Marketplace each year, and an end date. All activations must be in place by the opening date, and may 
not be taken down until the designated end date. This will be determined in the fall before the upcoming season 
by the BCPB to give businesses time to sign on for their encroachment agreements.  
 
ENCROACHMENT AGREEMENTS FOR EVENTS ONLY 
To encourage people who don’t want activations in front of their business to participate, the City will allow a 
business to acquire an events-only encroachment agreement that commits them to activate for events, even if 
they aren’t activated most of the time. This would be a way for a business to ensure that another vendor is not 
placed in front of their business on those days. If a business does not agree to activate for public event days, 
then the City will reserve the right to situate an outside vendor in the encroachment area for that event day. 
The BCPB will want to establish what a minimum “activation” is so that businesses have a clear understanding 
of what is expected of them.  
 
 
SIGNAGE 
City staff would like to create additional flexibility in signage for encroachment areas, to allow for additional 
signage options to keep up "temporary" signs for the entire season and allow additional allowances for 
freestanding signage.  
  
Currently, Chapter 20 of the ISMC only allows freestanding temporary signs up to 6 square feet in area. 
Movable signs are only allowed to be up to 10 square feet in area. Chapter 20 also requires that only one 
temporary sign be placed at any one time. It finally requires that temporary signs only remain in place for up 
to 60 days.  
 
City staff think it would be reasonable to allow additional business signage in encroachment areas. Staff would 
support allowing temporary signage to be installed for the duration of the encroachment period, rather than a 
60-day time limit. Staff would also support an allowance for additional temporary signage in the encroachment 
area, up to a certain total square footage. Staff would also support larger temporary signs if evidence is shown 
that the signs are durable and will not blow away, fall over, or otherwise pose risks to public health or safety.  
 
ENFORCEMENT 
 
Enforcement can be done through the existing encroachment agreement process, without the need for citations 
or tickets. If someone allegedly violates the encroachment agreement, the City could issue them a notice of 
breach letter right away through the BCPB or Code Enforcement. The recipient has 30 days to cure the alleged 
problem/breach. If they do, great! If they don’t, we give one additional warning of up to 30 days. At that point, 
if they are still not in compliance, the City revokes the encroachment permit. At that point, if they are still out 
of compliance, then a citation with the Police Department would likely be necessary.  
 
They could reapply but they would need to show that they are in compliance before a new permit could be 
issued.  
 
REQUEST FOR DIRECTION: Does City Council authorize City Staff and the Business and Community 
Promotions Board to pursue updates to the City’s CODA encroachment agreement standards that any 
business or property owner would need to adhere to if they wished to establish an encroachment on City 
property?

incorporated by this reference, is hereby adopted and approved.

of the Fee Schedule that are posted on the City’s website, on file with the Clerk’s Office or 
otherwise made available to the public for viewing and copying.

Resolution No. 22, Series 2026: AN ORDINANCE AMENDING SECTION 21-12 OF THE IDAHO SPRINGS 
MUNICIPAL CODE TO AUTHORIZE ALTERATIONS TO NON-CONFORMING BUILDINGS WHEN THE 
ALTERATIONS ARE LEGALLY CONFORMING 
 
BACKGROUND 
 
Section 21-12 of the Idaho Springs Municipal Code (ISMC) regulates nonconforming buildings and nonconforming 
uses. This section defines these nonconforming buildings and uses and determines what can or cannot be done with said 
properties containing said uses. 
 
Currently, the ISMC would allow a nonconforming building to be restored to its precalamity condition if damaged by 
fire or other causes. It also allows a building to be strengthened or restored to a safe condition if declared unsafe by the 
city’s Building Official. 
 
What the ISMC does not currently authorize is the expansion of a nonconforming building, even if that expansion is 
compliant with current ISMC requirements. For nonconforming buildings, city staff would not currently be able to 
authorize a compliant expansion/addition to the building. This limits a property owner’s ability to modify the exterior of 
their buildings. This has implications for properties around the city, since there are a substantial number of properties 
that have nonconforming buildings (likely in the hundreds). 
 
Other communities allow nonconforming buildings to be modified as long as those modifications are compliant with 
current code requirements. For example, in Summit County, their codes allow additions, expansions, or enlargements to 
a legal nonconforming structure as long as that expansion meets all applicable Zoning Regulations and other regulations 
of their county codes and the Building Code. If someone has a building that is built in a required setback area, they 
cannot expand that portion of their building or increase the encroachment into the setback, but they can expand the 
portions of their building that are outside of the setback and are compliant with the applicable codes. 
 
The following code amendment is proposed to add a Sec. 21-12(H) to the ISMC to address this issue: 
 
Notwithstanding any other provision of this Section, a nonconforming building may be enlarged or altered, provided 
that the enlargement or alteration complies with all underlying zoning standards and applicable requirements of this 
Code and does not increase the existing nonconformity. 
 
This would ensure that the city does not allow additional encroachments that increase the nonconformity, but allow for 
reasonable additions or expansions to be completed. 
 
STAFF ANALYSIS: This code amendment would allow for property owners to make improvements and other 
changes to their existing structures, without expanding any existing encroachments or nonconformities. As such, it 
provides a real benefit to property owners while avoiding additional adverse on neighboring properties. Staff are in 
support of this amendment as it increases opportunities and flexibility for Idaho Springs property owners who wish to 
maintain and improve their existing structures.

PROJECT UNDERSTANDING
COMMUNITY CONTEXT
The City of Idaho Springs sits at the intersection of transportation, economic, and environmental challenges 
that make investment in sustainable, low-emission mobility solutions increasingly important. Located along 
the Interstate 70 mountain corridor approximately 35 miles west of Denver, Idaho Springs serves as a gateway to some 
of Colorado’s most popular mountain recreation destinations. While the City is home to approximately 1,800 residents, it 
welcomes a large number of visitors annually who travel through or stop in the community to access its historic downtown, 
area attractions, public lands, outdoor recreation, and cultural attractions, including the new Mighty Argo and Virginia 
Canyon Mountain Park. This substantial visitor-driven demand places considerable pressure on the City’s transportation 
network, infrastructure, and limited parking resources, a pressure that can be minimized through e-mobility solutions.
E-mobility options, including shuttles, scooters, bikes, and vehicles create low emission, low decibel transportation 
solutions. Idaho Springs’ historic development pattern and mountainous terrain create unique constraints that complicate 
traditional transportation solutions. The historic downtown, steep slopes, narrow rights-of-way, gaps in pedestrian 
infrastructure, and surrounding natural features limit opportunities to widen roads or develop additional parking. 
Combined with seasonal congestion, aging infrastructure, and increasing visitation, these constraints affect the daily 
experiences of residents, workers, businesses, and visitors. The City needs transportation solutions that improve access 
and connectivity while preserving its historic character, protecting its natural environment, and making the most of existing 
infrastructure and regional transportation investments.
PROJECT GOALS AND APPROACH
Antero understands that the City is seeking to evaluate the feasibility of a set of e-mobility options, including 
an electric shuttle system, that provides a safe, accessible, and low-emission transportation option for 
residents, workers, and visitors. A successful system could improve access to historic downtown Idaho Springs, strengthen 
connections to the Idaho Springs Mobility Hub, and create more convenient first- and last-mile connections to the City’s 
cultural, commercial, and recreational destinations. The CAMP Technical Readiness Plan will establish the foundation 
for informed decision-making by evaluating the feasibility, infrastructure requirements, operational considerations, and 
potential community impacts of an e-mobility system. Key considerations include electric shuttle procurement, operations 
and maintenance, charging infrastructure, service needs, and connections between the Mobility Hub and key destinations 
throughout the community.
Equally important, Antero recognizes that the proposed e-shuttle program must complement existing transportation 
services, including Bustang and Pegasus services and Clear Creek County’s RoundAbout service. The Plan will therefore 
consider how a locally focused e-mobility system can build on existing investments, address unmet mobility needs, improve 
connectivity, and expand access for residents and visitors, including individuals with disabilities and those who face 
transportation cost barriers. Through a combination of technical analysis, stakeholder and community engagement, and 
equity and impact assessment, the Project will identify practical, scalable strategies that reflect Idaho Springs’ unique needs 
and position the City to advance implementation over time.
