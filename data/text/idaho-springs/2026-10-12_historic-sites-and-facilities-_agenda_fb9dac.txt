Work Plan:  
 The HSFB discussed the types of projects that require additional review on top of it being approved in the Work 
Plan.  
  
Vice Chair Davenport mentioned that city staff could bring a middle-ground to the chair to see if these are things that 
should be elevated to the level of a full board review.  
  
Chair Adams mentioned that for a project like library doors that are on the Work Plan, but require a level of expertise, 
the board should be informed in advance and be given a chance to provide input. The board would want to see what 
their qualifications are to do the work and can do it with respect to the historic needs. 
  
If city staff discover something that goes beyond the work plan, bring it to the board. If it is in the Work Plan but 
requires specific expertise, bring it to bring it to the board. Otherwise, there is no need to bring it to the board. If there 
is emergency work needed, Public Works should do the work (or hire someone to do the work) to stabilize the site 
and/or fix the problem, and then bring it to the board for a debrief to ensure that no additional work is needed.  
  
The board will start talking about projects that are needed in July, with finalization of budget talks / needs in October. 
July agendas in future years may have a "project status review" meeting where the board discusses where the city is at 
with various projects for the sites and facilities under the board’s purview. This will be an opportunity to talk through 
what has been done so far and what has not and whether city staff can make it happen in that given year. 
  
The board needs to schedule a work session in the near future with City Council prior to budget season so they know 
what has been achieved, what hasn't, what goes into each project, etc. City staff will add sites and facilities updates to 
monthly staff reports to City Council going forward that details what has been done regarding Work Plan goals.

Library: 
  
Mr. Sigward presented a proposal for repair work at the library to include re-sealing the gutters around the building to 
protect them from leaks that are slowly deteriorating the exterior brick. A quote from a professional also included the 
addition of a new downspout on the northern façade of the building, where no downspout currently exists and where 
water appears to be settling.  
 
The board questioned why no downspout included in the rehabilitation of the library on the northern façade of the 
building? Dylan will reach out to Hoehn architects to see why that was not included. If the downspout should be 
included, it needs to go to HPRC for review/approval and the downspout needs to match other downspouts.  
  
There may be a specification sheet for the property that discusses how the doors need to be maintained with particular 
materials, stains, treatments, etc. Look into this to determine how it should be done and then hire a firm on who is 
qualified to do the work. 
  
With respect to the library, the State Historic Fund funded a large portion of the project, so they have a say in how it 
should be maintained for at least 15 years per the grant agreements. Additionally, you cannot make changes to the 
building to install new gutters (for example) if it does not comply with the NPS standards for rehabilitation. HPRC 
would review whether the change to the gutters would impact the overall historical integrity of the exterior of the 
building. The city should not be changing the historical appearance of the building. If the gutters have to be 
completely replaced, they should be replaced like-for-like as much as possible.  
  
An NPS rule of thumb is: if you are on the sidewalk, can you see if there is a seam? The answer is probably not, if 
you are talking about a two-story building. The downspouts will be a bigger issue since downspout technology today 
is very different than what it was like 100+ years ago.  
  
Cemetery: 
  
In Lue Howard's report about the cemetery work plan for the summer, there were a significant number of projects 
included to be done. Now that the Sites and Facilities Board exists under the city's umbrella, does the board need to 
approve / agree to the work plan for the cemetery, or is it to be left up to Lue as it always has been?

Many of the items in the report Lue presented appear to largely be maintenance that is needed on an annual and/or as-
needed basis, rather than new construction. Maintenance is not something needed in the work plan generally.  
  
Lue does a work order through Deputy City Clerk Wonder Martell to get Public Works staff to do the work. Lue 
believes that getting this all approved with the full board would be difficult timing-wise because there is a small 
window in which work can be done (summer season), and it is not known when issues will come up, so having to put 
it on a work plan is difficult to do without slowing things down.  
  
The opinion of the board was that there is a big difference between paying someone to fix the headstones, which costs 
the city money versus conducting routine dead tree/weed eating maintenance or having city staff rebuild walls. 
Ultimately, should the board review and approve or recommend changes? The board have been kind of doing it now 
since the board is given an extensive report and have been agreeing to it. Unless the board members see something 
that needs to be changed, they could just endorse the report and agree to the maintenance things that need to be done. 
The board agreed that they were satisfied with this course of action when the work is largely maintenance, but if there 
is something needed that goes beyond general maintenance (for example, a contractor coming out to fix a drainage 
issue that is causing flooding / erosion), then it should be brought to the board.  
  
In January, Lue presented a proposed work plan for the year, but this does not necessarily reflect what is ultimately 
needed for the year. This is why there was an update in July to talk through the things that are being ultimately 
furthered in 2026. The board decided that Lue should continue to put together that report in January for review and 
discussion. When changes come up in the summer that affect that plan / adds additional clarification or scope, that can 
come back later on to the board.   
  
There was historically a $5,000 cemetery fund for the maintenance / rehabilitation work in the cemetery. This was 
pulled from the budget in 2026 because of budgeting constraints. There are some projects that do not come out of the 
cemetery budget - for example, when 75 trees were removed in 2025 because of beetle kill, that came out of a city 
budget for tree removal and other similar items. 
  
The concern is not about Lue continuing to do great work; however, eventually Lue will not be able to take leadership 
over the cemetery anymore and the board will need to have an SOP / maintenance plan for the property in the future.  
  
Coach: 
 
The next discussion item was about the future of the coach, which remains covered and protected in Silver Plume.   
 
The city needs a 3-5 year plan for bringing the coach back to town. What is this going to take to do this and what will 
it cost to do it? The city needs a long-term goal for getting it back, rather than just putting it off year after year 
because the city cannot afford it. 
  
What is the city's goal to get the coach back? 3 years? 5 years? If we know when we want it back by, then we can 
budget to get it covered ahead of that date.  
 
This is a good reason to have a work session with City Council. Chair Adams agreed to put together a report to solicit 
direction from City Council on their goals and objectives for the coach. Once direction is received, the board can 
work on an implementation plan that can be used to facilitate the construction of a shelter to protect the train engine 
and the coach and allow the city to bring the coach back to Idaho Springs.  
 
The train shelter was discussed. There may be a few options to get this off the ground. It is recognized that the initial 
plans provided by Hoehn Architects are outside of the city’s budget. However, less expensive options may exist that

still look reasonably nice and would protect the site. For example, a simple, all metal structure could be constructed. 
This could even potentially be built in the train’s current location if the existing overhead power lines are 
undergrounded or relocated. While a viewing platform is an important long-term goal, the most important short-term 
goal is having a protective cover for the train. Additional improvements could be phased over the 3-5 year plan for

engine and tender are restored. A variety of options exist that would help the city start moving forward, which is 
preferred over simply letting the project get put off indefinitely into the future.  
 
Once a discussion with City Council occurs, the board will work on an implementation plan for the train shelter, 
coach relocation to Idaho Springs, and other elements surrounding the train.  
  
Potential Future of Running the Train Coach on the Georgetown Loop: 
  
City staff requested direction from the board on whether the city should consider running the coach for another 
excursion along the Georgetown Loop railroad.  
 
One of the things that the city did to help fund the restoration of the interior of the library was to run a dinner train, 
which was fully booked. Would this work as well if the city was doing it instead of the library district and the 
fundraising was for the shelter? 
  
Board member Smith does not think it is a good idea because it does not seem like the city will get much money from 
fundraising, especially compared to the potential costs that would be incurred if something was damaged.  
  
The board shared the concern of wear and tear if the coach was run for special events, especially since a fundraiser is

an overall plan and the city can properly project how much funding might be able to earned, then it is not worth the 
risk.  
  
City staff will include a report to Council that the board does not recommend running the coach, even for a one-time 
fundraiser.

engine up to Silver Plume to complete the restoration, as was suggested in the city’s grant ask. City staff will confirm

possibility of performing the restoration on the train at its current location.

General Updates: 
 
• Brass Plaques 
The brass plaques have not yet been installed. The Public Works team has indicated that installation is on their to-do list for 
before the end of this year.  
 
• State Historic Fund Grant for Engine No. 70 Rehabilitation 
The City submitted a second application to the State Historic Fund for the Engine No. 70 rehabilitation on October 1st. We 
should hear about the success of our application by the end of the year, if awarded. Hopefully this updated application will have 
addressed reviewer concerns, one of which was the need to build a shelter to protect the resource from the weather.  
 
The grant scope of work would include transporting the engine and tender to Silver Plume for the Historic Rail Adventures team 
to complete the work. They calculated out anticipated costs of transporting the train to Silver Plume against rehabilitating it in 
place behind City Hall. They ultimately determined that it would end up costing a similar amount to rehabilitate the engine in 
place versus transporting it to Silver Plume once transportation of staff and materials, security, and staging were taken into 
consideration. City staff agreed that transporting it to Silver Plume is acceptable given that it will be protected from the 
weather and kept in a secure environment while being rehabilitated.  
 
There is an opportunity to transport the Coach back to City Hall at the same time that the engine and tender are transported to 
Silver Plume. Discussion will be needed about whether this is a good idea before a shelter is built for long-term protection. A 
key consideration would be whether a shelter could be built around the Coach. The Coach has a secure cover that could remain 
in place with it at City Hall, which could be removed from time to time if there was reason to do so.  
 
• Train Shelter 
Since the last meeting, City staff have made some progress regarding the train shelter. City Administrator Andy Marsh met with 
representatives from Xcel about the potential to underground the power lines in the vicinity of City Hall. The City has funds for 
undergrounding that could cover this work. Undergrounding the power lines will allow a shelter to be built in the train’s current 
location, greatly reducing infrastructure and site work costs. This is scheduled to occur in late 2026 or early 2027. The City will 
need to eventually underground all the lines behind the downtown buildings along Idaho Mall, so this is part of a wider plan for 
this area. I have been talking with metal building manufacturers to receive quotes on what a metal, open-sided structure built 
in that location might cost, and I believe that this could be accomplished for $100-150k in total once the lines are 
undergrounded. I am not sure if the City will be able to budget this in 2027, though that is the goal in the current budget. I will 
update the Board once the budget is finalized to confirm if at least a portion of the project will be budgeted for 2027 (for 
example, the shelter could be built in phases, which would spread the cost over several budget cycles).  
 
• Hose House #2 
Ed Sigward and I went down to the Hose House #2 in mid-September to look at the moisture issues that are ongoing. The 
wettest area of the building’s interior is the Northeast corner, adjacent to a downspout. The downspout did not appear to be 
draining, as it had an upward bend in the pipe and was clogged and filled with dirt. Ed slightly shortened the downspout pipe to 
remove the upward bend and we cleaned out the pipe thoroughly. While this likely is not the cause of the internal moisture 
issues, we are hoping that it will somewhat address the problem. With that in mind, we will be monitoring the building over the 
next few months as it rains to see if anything dries out. If not, then the next step is likely bringing an expert back in to consider 
other ways of addressing the moisture concerns.

• Central Hose House 
I reached out to Jan Bowland from the Historical Society of Idaho Springs to see what updates, if any, had occurred with the 
painting and maintenance project at the Central Hose House. Jan indicated that she had met with Hoehn Architects on-site and 
fortunately they didn’t view the hose house as needed as drastic of repairs as perhaps anticipated. Jan is in the process of 
contacting and getting bids from contractors to evaluate the cost of these repairs, with a goal of having another update in the 
next week or so.  
 
Tim and Kris Hoehn reviewed the current condition and observed the following items that require attention: 
1. The building needs to be completely repainted, including the application of a primer.  The wood is cracked in various 
locations, especially where subject to moisture infiltration.  Those areas include the tops of the rafter tails, the 
window sills, the bottoms of the corner boards, and the bottoms of doors.  Those areas should be repaired with epoxy 
filler and then sanded where deep cracks are present, prior to the application of a primer and paint.  The painted 
signage above the large entry doors has faded and requires repainting.  
2. The large pair of doors at the main entry have sagged and are no longer able to be secured at the top.  The 
installation of diagonal tie rods with turnbuckles on the interior sides of the doors was suggested as a way to 
eliminate the sagging.  Some reconstruction may be required.   
3. The exterior light fixtures need to be repainted, which has chalked and faded.   
4. Weeds should be removed.  Regrade the site along east side to reintroduce the drainage swale in the center and to 
drain away from building.  Provide barriers outside (to be selected) along both sides of the hyphen to prevent the 
homeless from camping in these areas.   
5. Inside the hose tower, the water stains on the original wood siding were pre-existing during the rehabilitation project 
18 years ago.  A layer of building paper and new novelty style wood siding matching the profile and exposure of the 
original were installed as part of the rehabilitation project.  A white powdery substance was observed in limited areas 
on the timber sill plates and at the bottom of some of the wood framing, mostly at the northeast and northwest 
corners.  The substance has no odor and upon misting it with a spray bottle followed with scrubbing, it was 
determined to be efflorescence, which is a dry, powdery dust that can appear on wood when water moves through it 
and deposits dissolved minerals on it.  The timber sill plates rest on grade so when the surrounding grade gets wet, 
the wood will absorb some of the moisture, causing efflorescence.  It can be easily removed with a spray mist and 
brush.  No significant wood rot was observed.

No wayfinding signage has been installed in this area yet, though Public Works informed me that they would be looking to see if

sometime this autumn to discuss the idea and see what concerns they have, discuss plans to eventually move the gate, lighting 
and security needs, and other elements.  
 
New Business: 
 
• Miner Street Bridge 
The Miner Street Bridge, located just east of Riverside Drive along Miner Street in the downtown Historic District, is both a 
National Historic Landmark site and is located within the city’s National Historic District. However, when the HSFB was created, 
it was not included as an official city site or facility. This seems strange, since it is a nationally registered structure and is owned 
by the city.  
 
The ordinance allows for additional sites and facilities to be designated by the City Council from time to time. Staff think it could 
be beneficial to add the bridge to the sites and facilities list. However, before designating it, we wanted to mention this to the 
HSFB for feedback. If it is added to the Board’s purview, then the Preservation Plan will need to be updated to include the 
bridge. We will also want to put together language for a brass plaque to include on the bridge at a convenient location.
