Director’s Report-9.21.2026 
Business and Community Promotions Board Meeting 
Overview & Key Updates 
Since the September meeting, efforts have focused on transitioning from fall event execution into holiday programming, 
winter destination marketing, and strategic planning for 2027. Over the last 30 days, work has included the inaugural 
Miner’s Feast – Taste of Idaho Springs & Music Festival, continued promotion of upcoming Halloween activities, 
preparation for the seasonal transition of Miner Street Market, and advancement of several grant-funded projects. 
As we approach the end of 2026, the focus is on assessing the effectiveness of our programs, identifying opportunities for 
improvement, and ensuring that our investments in marketing, events, infrastructure, and partnerships continue to 
produce measurable beneﬁts for Idaho Springs. 
Marketing & Branding Initiatives 
a. Visit Idaho Springs  
Website: Over the past month, our focus has included maintaining current event information, supporting 
fall programming, and strengthening connections between destination marketing and individual business 
visibility. The dedicated Miner’s Feast event page provided a central landing page for promotional efforts 
and helped connect advertising, social media, and event information in one location. 
 
  
b. Instagram & Facebook: Visit Idaho Springs social channels continue to beneﬁt from a much more 
consistent content strategy, collaborative posts, reels, event promotion, and business storytelling. 
At our last meeting we had 2844 followers on Instagram, and 2500 followers on Facebook. As of today we 
have 2976 on Instagram and 2600 on Facebook 
c. Fall Paid Marketing Campaign: Our $12,000 fall marketing campaign concluded October 3rd, promoting 
Idaho Springs as an easy mountain getaway just 30 miles from Denver under the theme “So Much to Do. So 
Close to Home. ” Using Placer.ai and visitor spending data, we can begin to understand how our destination 
performed during the campaign. 
i. Key Takeaways: 
1. 325,900 visitors came from outside Idaho Springs, a 1% increase over the same period last 
year. 
2. 445,300 nonresident visits were recorded, remaining relatively consistent with 2025. 
3. Weekends continue to drive the strongest visitation, while midweek travel remains an 
opportunity for growth. 
4. Food, beverage, and regional day-trip experiences remain important opportunities for 
encouraging visitors to spend more time and money in Idaho Springs. 
ii. What Worked & What We Learned: 
1. Our focus on promoting Idaho Springs as an accessible destination close to Denver aligns 
well with our visitor market. We maintained strong visitation during the fall shoulder season, 
with a slight increase in unique visitors. 
2. The data also reinforces the importance of promoting the entire Idaho Springs experience, 
not just individual attractions, including dining, shopping, recreation, and overnight stays. 
iii. Final Thrive4 advertising performance results will provide additional insight into the effectiveness of 
our investment.

FROM:   Dylan Graves, Community Development Planner 
SUBJECT:  Idaho Springs CODA Standards 
MEETING DATE:  TBD 
 
SCOPE OF MEMO 
 
A continued discussion of potential standards for the City of Idaho Springs’ Downtown Communal Outdoor Dining Area (CODA) 
Standards and Regulations for encroachment areas. 
 
The goal is to get feedback about a few topics, discussed later in this memo.  
 
BACKGROUND 
We met a few months ago to discuss the existing CODA area standards and regulations and request direction on whether the BCPB 
would be interested in potential changes to the City’s current system and if there is interest in updated standards and/or regulations. 
 
At that meeting, the BCPB identified a few priority areas for City staff to consider. These included: 
1. Safety standards  
2. Durability standards 
3. Cleanliness standards 
4. Emergency access standards 
5. Potential aesthetic standards for historically compatible materials and other activation items 
 
The BCPB wanted to see a code amendment that establishes CODA activation standards that are adopted by the BCPB and updated 
from time to time. The Idaho Springs Municipal Code (ISMC) would establish a protocol for creating the activation policies, with 
the BCPB being in charge of adoption of standards and updates to said standards. This would make for an easy code amendment and 
give the BCPB and the City flexibility to make changes when unanticipated issues arise or priorities/needs change.  
 
SAFETY / DURABILITY 
Here are my thoughts for addressing this section: 
 
All CODA components be: 
• Commercial-grade  
• Weather-resistant  
• UV-resistant  
• Corrosion-resistant  
• Capable of withstanding normal mountain wind/weather  
• Maintained in good repair  
• Free from peeling, cracking, rust, broken components, etc. 
  
Prohibit: 
• Household furniture that isn't designed for outdoor use  
• Deteriorated pallets  
• Unfinished plywood  
• Carpet or indoor rugs that become saturated  
• Duct-taped components  
• Makeshift fasteners  
• Plastic tarps  
• Temporary construction fencing and other construction materials 
• At-grade electrical cords and outlets that could be a trip hazard 
• Damaged or visibly deteriorated furniture 
  
Items must be weighted or otherwise stabilized to maintain durability and avoid potential safety hazards.

This speaks a bit to aesthetics, as well, but foremost ensures that businesses are installing durable items that will continue to look 
nice and remain functional so as not to create hazards.  
 
Another thing we could consider is barrier/fencing related standards: 
• Be stable and resistant to tipping.  
• Have a finished appearance on all sides.  
• Be designed for repeated seasonal use.  
• Have no sharp edges or protrusions.  
• Not create trip hazards.  
• Not obstruct visibility at intersections or driveways.  
• Be removable without damaging the street.  
• Be capable of being secured against wind.  
• Not be attached to historic buildings without approval.  
• Not be anchored by drilling into sidewalks or historic masonry unless specifically approved. 
 
CLEANLINESS 
This seems relatively straightforward. Language around cleaning up the areas, maintaining them to be free from trash, junk, etc., 
would be pretty simple to put together if the BCPB wanted to add cleanliness language.  
 
EMERGENCY ACCESS 
The Clear Creek Fire Authority (CCFA) has already established their standards for emergency access. Formalizing this in the policy 
document adopted by the BCPB would be all the City needs to do to ensure that this is enforceable and continues to be met.  
 
AESTHETICS 
he BCPB expressed a desire to allow creativity and artistic freedom. Different businesses have different sized encroachment areas, 
different needs, and different financial capacities to work with, so requiring certain designs could have a negative impact on 
businesses.  
 
That said, establishing a list of preferred materials and discouraged materials, appropriate and prohibited items (if relevant), and 
signage standards would ensure that a minimum level of aesthetic consideration is preserved throughout the CODA area. My question 
on this would be whether to just create guidelines / preferences or whether it would be better to establish approved and prohibited 
materials and make these standards mandatory. Here are some of my thoughts: 
 
Preferred materials: 
 
Wood 
• Natural wood  
• Painted wood  
• Stained wood  
Metal 
• Black or dark bronze powder-coated steel  
• Wrought-iron-style elements  
• Galvanized steel where appropriately finished  
Masonry 
• Brick  
• Stone  
• Masonry planters  
Fabric 
• Canvas  
• High-quality outdoor fabric 
 
Discouraged Materials (may be approved as part of an encroachment application): 
 
• Brightly colored plastic fencing  
• Chain-link fencing

3 
 
• Vinyl lattice  
• Corrugated plastic  
• Raw plywood  
• Artificial turf  
• Inexpensive plastic privacy screens  
• Highly reflective materials  
• Inflatable decorations  
• Tarps  
• Advertising banners used as walls 
 
Some of these are subjective, so may require some additional discussion. However, the goal would be to avoid materials that do not 
hold up well over a long summer season or could pose a health or safety risk. 
 
Some questions to consider: 
• Is there an interest in regulating lighting within encroachment areas? 
• What do you think about items that move to attract attention (inflatable tube men, flags, flashing lights, pinwheels, etc.)? 
• Are there color schemes that the BCPB wants to consider to maintain certain aesthetics? 
• Does the BCPB want to consider tent/canopy standards? 
 
SIGNAGE 
Do you want to allow additional signage in the CODA area under the City's temporary signage requirements, to allow for additional 
signage options / flexibility to keep up "temporary" signs for the entire season without additional permits? There could be reasons 
to install signs in the encroachment area that would not fit cleanly within the City’s sign regulations. Addressing it through the 
CODA may be easier than amending the sign code.  
  
Thoughts: 
• Signage on approved furniture/tents/umbrellas do not need permits 
• Freestanding signs need to show evidence that they won't blow away 
• Do freestanding signs need to be brought inside outside of business hours? 
• Do you want to regulate "inflatable advertising" aka tube men and other kinds of inflatables? 
• Should encroachment area signage meet the size and material standards found in Chapter 20 of the ISMC, or should there 
be more leniency since these are temporary encroachment areas? 
  
ENFORCEMENT 
Do you want to establish an enforcement protocol for encroachments where approval can be revoked if the standards are not met? 
What is the preferred mechanism? Warning - Fine - Revocation? No fine, just revocation? I am not sure what the BCPB’s thoughts 
are on this. My research shows that most places offer at least one warning and a timeline to get into compliance. My suggestion 
would be a three strikes rule – you get two warnings and if you are not in compliance after two warnings, revocation of the 
encroachment agreement would be the next remedy. 
 
REQUESTS FOR DIRECTION: 
Sadie and I thought the following areas are the biggest places where direction is needed, though direction on any or all the above is 
appreciated: 
 
1. Does the Board like having preferred and discouraged materials in the standards? Would the Board prefer these to be 
mandatory standards, rather than suggested? 
2. How does the Board want to handle enforcement? 
3. What are the Board’s thoughts on lighting, moving, and other attention-grabbing devices (strobe lights, pinwheels, 
inflatables, etc.)?
