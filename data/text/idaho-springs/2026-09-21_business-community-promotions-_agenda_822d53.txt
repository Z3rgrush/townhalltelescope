Director’s Report-9.21.2026 
Business and Community Promotions Board Meeting 
Overview & Key Updates 
Since the August meeting, efforts have shifted from peak summer operations into fall marketing, shoulder-season 
visitation, event execution, and planning for 2027. Over the last 30 days, work has focused on launching the fall Visit Idaho 
Springs paid marketing campaign, preparing for the Taste of Idaho Springs & Music Festival, coordinating upcoming 
community events, continuing grant-funded projects, and advancing long-term visitor management and economic 
development initiatives. 
As we transition into fall, the focus remains on converting strong awareness and visitation into increased local spending, 
longer stays, and greater engagement with businesses and attractions throughout Idaho Springs. At the same time, we are 
beginning to evaluate what worked during the 2026 season and identify priorities, budgets, and opportunities for 2027. 
1. Marketing & Branding Initiatives 
a. Visit Idaho Springs Launch 
Website: Website development and optimization continue as we build upon the new Visit Idaho Springs 
platform. Over the last month, we have made several graphic and visual updates throughout the site and 
continued reﬁning content to improve the visitor experience and strengthen our search presence. 
We also created a dedicated Miner’s Feast – Taste of Idaho Springs event page, which will serve as the 
primary landing page for the event and support our fall paid and organic marketing efforts. 
SEO continues to show positive growth. As of September 3, the website is tracking 69 keywords across 
three search engines with 36% overall search visibility. Google visibility increased to 30% (+3%), while 
Google mobile visibility increased to 31% (+1%). This is an encouraging indication that our continued 
investment in content development and SEO is beginning to strengthen the organic visibility of Visit Idaho 
Springs. 
Our next focus will be continuing to build relevant destination and event content, improving keyword 
rankings, and using the website to convert increased search visibility into trip planning, event attendance, 
and engagement with local businesses. 
 
  
b. Instagram & Facebook: Visit Idaho Springs social channels continue to beneﬁt from a much more 
consistent content strategy, collaborative posts, reels, event promotion, and business storytelling. 
At our last meeting we had 2564 followers on Instagram, and 2400 followers on Facebook. As of today we 
have 2844 on Instagram and 2500 on Facebook 
c. Social Media Co-Op 
d. Following the success of the summer Social Media Co-Op, recruitment is underway for the Fall/Winter 
Social Media Co-Op running October through January. The program is designed to provide local businesses 
with an affordable and easy way to access professional social media content while beneﬁting from the 
broader reach of Visit Idaho Springs. Participating businesses receive professionally captured photo and 
video assets, collaborative posts through Visit Idaho Springs, and access to all content for use on their own 
channels. Businesses can participate $500 per month for 4-reels, with a seasonal commitment. Once 
enrolled, businesses receive a dedicated content folder and scheduled content capture. The next season 
will allow us to build a strong library of fall, holiday, and winter content while continuing to amplify 
individual businesses through the Visit Idaho Springs platform and maintain consistent destination 
marketing throughout the shoulder and winter seasons. 
e. Fall Paid Marketing Campaign: The $12,000 fall streaming campaign launched August 24 and runs 
through October 3 under the theme “Idaho Springs: So Much to Do. So Close to Home. ”

The campaign is now actively promoting Idaho Springs across streaming platforms as an easy fall mountain 
getaway just 30 miles from Denver, highlighting fall colors, historic downtown, the Mighty ARGO, hot 
springs, outdoor recreation, local dining and shopping, and overnight stays. 
Thrive4 continues to monitor campaign performance, placement, and bonus inventory throughout the 
ﬂight. A complete performance report will be provided following the conclusion of the campaign, including 
reach, impressions, completion rates, and other key results. 
f. Brand Asset Rollout 
i. Trash Can Beautification: are almost finalized. We are working on the design for the front that will 
call out the Historical Society of Idaho Springs for photo credit as well as a QR code to the Visit 
Idaho Springs Website.  
  
2. Grants & Projects 
a. Wayﬁnding Grant: The FY26 Wayfinding & Branding Strategy is officially underway following receipt of the 
Colorado Tourism Office grant funding.  
b. Digital Multimedia Historic Tour Grant: This project is officially in process  
c. Economic Impact Dashboard: We were awarded a second Colorado School of Mines Field Session and 
have begun working with a new student team to continue development of the Idaho Springs Economic 
Impact Dashboard. This next phase will focus on making the dashboard fully functional, easier to update, 
and more sustainable for long-term use, including improving data integration and simplifying the ongoing 
update process. This is an exciting opportunity to build upon the work completed by the summer team and 
move closer to having a reliable, user-friendly tool for tracking visitation, economic trends, and community 
impacts. I look forward to sharing additional progress with the Board at our next meeting. 
d. Trails to Towns: The Trails to Towns grant has moved fully into the design and content development phase. 
We have seen our 1st draft and are starting to work on capturing content and images for the book.  
3. Event Planning & Activations  
a. Idaho Springs Police Department Community Open House: A reminder for this week’s community 
celebration marking the opening of the new Idaho Springs Police Department on Wednesday, September 
23. The Community Open House will begin at 2:00 PM, followed by welcome remarks and a ribbon cutting 
at 3:00 PM, with the event concluding around 4:00 PM.

b. Taste of Idaho Springs & Music Festival: Planning is in the final stages for the inaugural Taste of Idaho 
Springs & Music Festival, now branded as Miner’s Feast, taking place October 2–3 on Miner Street. 
We currently have 10 participating restaurants, creating a strong opportunity to showcase Idaho Springs’ 
local food and beverage community. Tommyknocker Brewery continues to support coordination of the 
Taste experience, and our partnership with Shamrock Foods has helped support restaurant and vendor 
participation. The event will also benefit Charlie’s Place, adding an important community component to 
the weekend. 
Live music and entertainment are being finalized alongside event logistics. I will need Board members to 
volunteer during the event and assist with event operations, and I will be sending out specific shifts and 
needs as we get closer. 
Marketing is now a major focus, with the new Miner’s Feast event page live on Visit Idaho Springs and 
promotion incorporated into our fall destination marketing efforts. I am also asking all Board members and 
community partners to help spread the word. Please share Visit Idaho Springs’ Miner’s Feast posts through 
your social channels and business pages. I have printed flyers available and would appreciate help 
distributing them throughout the community and surrounding areas. 
The goal remains to establish Miner’s Feast as a signature shoulder-season event that drives visitation 
while directly supporting our restaurants, businesses, and community partners. 
c. Halloween Programming: Planning has begun for an expanded Halloween season downtown. 
Current programming includes: 
i. October 29 – Community Trunk or Treat  
ii. October 31 – Zombie Fun Run with CCMRD & Zombie Party on Miner Street The Zombie Party on 
Miner Street will build upon our existing Halloween traditions while creating more opportunities for 
downtown businesses to participate and benefit from increased activity throughout the day. 
Businesses will be encouraged to get involved by offering trick-or-treating, treats or specials for 
customers who come in costume, themed food and drinks, decorations, or other Halloween 
activities. The goal is to make the entire downtown feel like part of the celebration and encourage 
families to move up and down Miner Street and visit our local businesses. 
We are also planning an activity area around 16th Street with vendors, prizes, and live music, 
adding another gathering point and extending the Halloween experience beyond traditional trick-
or-treating. This is intended to be a fun, relatively easy activation for businesses to participate in at 
whatever level works for them while creating a stronger downtown-wide Halloween experience for 
residents and visitors. 
d. Miner Street Market – Fall Operations & Holiday Ramble: The Board previously agreed to keep Miner 
Street Market closed through the Monday after Thanksgiving. As we move into fall, however, I have received 
feedback from several of the primary businesses and activations within the Market that they may not be 
able or willing to maintain their outdoor setups that late into the season. This creates a larger question 
about the experience we are trying to create. If most businesses remove their patios, tents, and 
activations, keeping Miner Street closed with limited activity could make the Market feel empty rather than 
activated. I need Board guidance and a decision at this meeting on how we would like to proceed. I see 
several options: 
i. Maintain the current plan: Keep Miner Street closed through Thanksgiving and work with 
businesses to encourage them to maintain meaningful outdoor activations through the season.  
ii. Reopen after Halloween: Keep the Market activated through the Zombie Party on October 31, then 
reopen Miner Street to vehicles for November. We could close the street again beginning Friday 
after Thanksgiving specifically for Holiday Ramble and ask businesses to create outdoor 
activations for the event.  
iii. Keep Miner Street open for Holiday Ramble: Return to a format similar to last year, with Holiday 
Ramble activities hosted inside participating businesses and locations throughout downtown.  
iv. Create a concentrated outdoor Holiday Ramble activation: Keep Miner Street generally open but 
consider closing 16th Street for the event, installing a large tent, and creating a central outdoor 
gathering space for entertainment and activities.  
Page 8 of 36
My recommendation is that we consider quality of activation over simply maintaining the closure. If 
businesses are committed to staying set up and participating, there is value in extending Miner Street 
Market. If most activations will be removed after Halloween, reopening the street and creating a more 
intentional, concentrated Holiday Ramble activation may provide a stronger experience for both 
businesses and the community. Board Action Requested: Determine the operating timeline for Miner 
Street Market after October 31 and provide direction for the 2026 Holiday Ramble format. 
e. Duck Out DV 2027 – New Event Partnership Opportunity 
I recently met with Clear Creek Advocates to discuss Duck Out DV 2027, a proposed new fall event 
combining a Jeep show and mountain festival, local vendors, family activities, and the Jeep “ducking” 
tradition to raise awareness for domestic violence while bringing visitors into Clear Creek County.  
The Year 1 concept targets 75+ registered Jeeps, 500–1,000 attendees, and 20–30 participating local 
vendors and businesses, with the goal of creating a signature annual shoulder-season event that supports 
local restaurant and retail spending.  
I see strong alignment with our goals around fall visitation, community partnerships, local business 
engagement, and developing signature events. Clear Creek Advocates is interested in partnering with 
Idaho Springs and other communities and businesses throughout the county as the concept develops.  
Board Guidance Requested: I would like direction from the Board on whether to continue working with 
Clear Creek Advocates to explore incorporating Duck Out DV into our 2027 event calendar and planning 
process, including potential City/BCPB support, location, logistics, marketing, and partnership 
opportunities. 
4. Community Engagement 
a. Historic District Encroachment & Design Standards: Dylan and I will be working on this in the coming 
months to make some recommendations prior to next year. 
b. Parking Program Review & Mobility Strategy: Parking continues to be one of the most common concerns 
raised by residents and businesses. During peak periods, downtown lots are regularly reaching capacity, 
while changes to parking areas and regulations have created some confusion for residents. 
We had a parking roundtable with City staff and Interstate Parking to review current conditions and identify 
opportunities to simplify communication and improve resident access. 
5. Budget: 2027 BCPB Budget 
The proposed 2027 BCPB budget will be presented to City Council during the September 22 Work Session.  
The baseline request is $211,109, consistent with the 2026 budget. 
I am also recommending that we continue allocating 77% of the Director position to the Parking Enterprise Fund 
and 23% to BCPB, reﬂecting the signiﬁcant portion of the Director role dedicated to parking operations, 
communications, planning, and administration. 
 
Two items will be highlighted for Council discussion: 
Marketing: If funding is available, I recommend a 5% increase to Website & Marketing, increasing the allocation 
from $70,000 to $73,500. With the Visit Idaho Springs website and destination brand now established, the 
additional funding would allow us to build on that investment through expanded destination marketing, increased 
visitation, and continued support for local businesses. If the increase is not feasible, the recommendation is to 
maintain the existing $70,000 allocation.

Wayﬁnding: Stage 2 of the grant-funded Wayﬁnding project is currently underway and will provide phased 
implementation recommendations and more reliable cost estimates. Because that work is not yet complete, I do 
not recommend assigning an implementation amount at this time. However, Council should begin discussing a 
potential funding source or placeholder for 2027. There is also a strong case for considering Parking Enterprise 
funding for portions of wayﬁnding, as improved directional signage can directly address parking circulation, help 
visitors locate available parking, and improve movement between parking areas, downtown, and community 
assets. 
The overall recommendation is to maintain the 2026 budget as the 2027 baseline, with the 5% marketing increase 
as the immediate adjustment for consideration and wayﬁnding funding to be revisited once Stage 2 costs are 
available. If this budget is approved we will start working on the full breakdown of each category.

Duck Out DV
Duck Out DV is a new community event and 
awareness campaign designed by Clear Creek 
Advocates to unite the Jeep community, local 
businesses, residents, and visitors in support of 
survivors & Domestic Violence Awareness Month.
 The event combines a Jeep show, festival, local 
vendors, family-friendly activities, and the popular 
Jeep “ducking” tradition to create a fun and 
meaningful way to raise awareness for domestic 
violence and promote Clear Creek County. 
Unlike traditional events that end when attendees go 
home, Duck Out DV has the potential to continue 
generating awareness long after October as Collector 
Ducks travel around Colorado and beyond. Through 
the Jeep community, each duck becomes an 
ambassador for the cause while showcasing our 
community and local businesses to new audiences. 
Duck Out DV aims to become a Clear Creek County 
tradition, attracting visitors, and extending 
awareness... one duck at a time.
