Town of Berthoud Historical Preservation Advisory Committee Regular Meeting 
807 Mountain Avenue, Berthoud, CO 80513 
Monday, September 14, 2026, at 5:30 PM 
 
This was an in-person meeting at the location noted above; it was called to order at 5:37 PM 
 
1. Roll Call 
HPAC members present were Leslie Moore, Carri Grimditch, and Joan Cullen. Staff 
liaisons Anne Johnson and Lakin Dickson were present, as was Andrew Wayland, Berthoud 
Historical Society Museum Director. HPAC members John Balcastro and Lee Hardies were 
all absent, as was Trustee Karen Anderson. Due to not having enough members present for 
quorum, this was an informal meeting with no formal motions made. 
2. Discussion Items 
A. Water Tower Grant Update 
Anne updated HPAC that funding was received for the elevated water tank interpretive 
sign. We are now waiting to hear back about next steps. Leslie mentioned that History 
Colorado, the granters, are understaffed right now so updates may move slower than 
usual. 
B. Survey Grant Update 
Lakin provided an update that the Town is working with John Schuttler to conduct a cost 
estimate that will be provided to History Colorado when applying for this survey plan grant. 
This should be coming in the next couple weeks. Leslie expressed hope for thematic or 
geographical recommendations to come out of the survey plan. 
C. October 3rd Walking Tour Updates 
It was discussed that Andrew and Lakin will tackle the opening remarks and welcomes at 
the beginning of the tour. Carri, Leslie, Lee, and Joan will all have a stop along the tour to 
provide information. Each stop will be approximately 7 minutes, and the tour will total to 1 
hour, starting at 11 AM. Anne confirmed that Bella did provide an update to property 
owners along the tour to limit confusion. There will be no map to print or follow, the group 
will walk together. A reminder will be sent to HPAC members a few days before the walking 
tour to remind everyone of the time and place. Lastly, Anne confirmed that Leslie and 
Andrew will be assisting with the Berthoud Surveyor interview about the historic walking 
tour. 
D. Membership Ordinance and Recruitment Update 
Anne confirmed she will bring this ordinance to the next meeting so that all HPAC 
members can hear it. There will be potential for 4 new appointments to the committee, and 
applications will be received a week prior to interviews. 
E. Section 106 Update

Resolution No. 26 
Series 2026 
A Resolution of the Town of Berthoud Approving the Issuance of a Notice of Concern Regarding 
Proposed Revisions to 36 Code of Federal Regulations 800, Implementing Section 106 of the 
National Historic Preservation Act 
Whereas, Section 106 is part of the National Historic Preservation Act passed by Congress in 
1966;   
Whereas, Section 106 requires that each federal agency identify and assess the effects its actions 
may have on historical buildings, districts, archaeological sites and cultural landscapes, and to 
consider local concerns when making decisions about projects affecting a community;  
Whereas, the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation (ACHP) issues regulations to guide the 
Section 106 review process, specifying actions federal agencies must take to meet their legal 
obligations to local governments; 
Whereas, on July 24, 2026, the ACHP voted to change Section 106 regulations to make it optional 
for federal agencies to consider local public views; and 
Whereas, the ACHP will provide 30 days to accept public comments to the Notice of Proposed 
Rulemaking in the Federal Register. 
Now Therefore be it Resolved by the Board of Trustees of the Town of Berthoud as follows: 
Section 1.  The Board of Trustees approves the issuance of the attached Notice and 
authorizes the Mayor to execute the Notice on behalf of the Town.  
Introduced, approved and adopted this 13th day of October, 2026.

Subject: Advisory Council on Historic Preservation RIN# XXXX-XXXX 
(TBD) Protection of Historic Properties 
The Board of Trustees of the Town of Berthoud, along with its Berthoud Historic Preservation Advisory 
Committee (HPAC), a Certified Local Government (CLG) in Colorado, is writing to express concern with 
the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation's proposed revisions to 36 Code of Federal Regulations 
800, implementing regulations of Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA). 
The proposed changes do not recognize that representatives of local governments, such as CLGs, are 
entitled to participate as a consulting party throughout the Section 106 process.  The proposed 
regulations will strip the Town’s authority to participate in this review process.  The Town is concerned 
with the proposed language as it does not guarantee the local government to be notified let alone 
participate in the review.  Berthoud’s agricultural roots mean that many of our historical buildings are 
unassuming and vernacular in style and are associated with important local historical trends and 
individuals.  Our HPAC is familiar with these places that may not be noticed by others.  
The Town and HPAC know the history of our buildings, neighborhoods, landscapes, and archaeological 
sites better than a federal agency.  Local participation in decision-making is best.  As a consulting party, 
the HPAC can share our knowledge with a federal agency to improve the design of a project and avoid 
potential conflicts.  We do not have interest in stopping federal projects and want to preserve a process 
where the people who are most familiar with our local heritage have a meaningful opportunity to 
inform federal decision-making.  
The Town and HPAC do not believe that ACHP should accept these regulation changes without engaging 
with local communities like CLGs who have participated as Section 106 consulting parties and have 
insight into the process.  
We respectfully ask the ACHP to reconsider these sweeping changes and follow the collaborative and 
balanced framework that has long been the foundation of Section 106. 
Sincerely, 
 
William Karspek, Mayor
