Monday, July 13, 2026, at 5:30 PM 
 
This was an in-person meeting at the location noted above; it was called to order at 5:32 PM 
 
1. Roll Call  
HPAC members present were Leslie Moore, John Balcastro, Lee Hardies, Joan Cullen, Courtney 
VanHooser, and Carri Grimditch. Staff liaison Bella Manzo was present, as was Trustee Karen Anderson, 
and Andrew Wayland, Berthoud Historical Society Museum Director. HPAC member Ryan Durfee was 
absent. 
2. Approval of Meeting Minutes 
June 8, 2026 Meeting Minutes 
Motion made by Lee Hardies, seconded by Courney VanHooser; passed unanimously 
3. Public Comment Period 
There was no public comment 
4. Discussion Items 
B. Work Planning for 2027 – Historic Preservation Plan Goals and Strategies 
Bella Manzo reviewed plans that HPAC members devised at the June meeting before moving on to the next 
goals. Leslie explained the difference between a survey plan and a reconnaissance survey; survey plans 
are more commonly done first. Bella added that grant funds are more readily available for survey plans 
than reconnaissance surveys. 
The committee agreed to pursue the survey plan grant. Bella will let the HPAC know what help the town 
may need, but she doesn’t anticipate there being much. 
The committee discussed how the Education goal of the preservation plan is something that it will carry 
through every year. Andrew Wayland suggested a history fair, where students could present topics they 
researched, and he also suggested that the HPAC do more with high school students. Carri Grimditch 
suggested the high school students could do something with the historic trades. Chairwoman Moore 
created an ad-hoc committee to pursue these goals. 
Carri and Bella are going to coordinate with the Berthoud Historical Society to present something at one of 
the Society’s speaker series talks at the Rec Center. 
Re: Strategy 1.6 in the preservation plan, the committee discussed how the town website should make the 
HPAC Walking Tour easier to access. 
Re: Strategy 1.5 – It will remain a goal for HPAC members to attend the annual Colorado Saving Places 
preservation conference. 
Re: Strategy 2.1 – Bella mentioned that it’s paused for now, but she will need an HPAC member to assist 
her with establishing relationships with owners of historic properties. 
Re: Strategy 4.6 – The walking tour. The HPAC will continue to do this; the Berthoud Historical Society 
would like to help. The committee discussed various ideas for future tours, such as a fall-themed one or 
Craftsmen homes. Courtney VanHooser said the fall tour could be promoted at Oktoberfest. The 
committee decided to do the arts-and-crafts/art-themed. Bella will consult with other staff and return next 
month with ideas. HPAC members should email Bella with additional tour ideas. 
Chairwoman Moore asked the committee for other ideas for 2027. She suggested bumping up the creation 
of historical guidelines as a priority, so new businesses coming to town know how to proceed with their 
buildings. Bella said having a survey plan first could help with winning grants to help with establishing 
design guidelines, so Leslie said she’ll communicate to the town’s Main Street group that the design 
guidelines are a long-term goal. She will attend the next Main Street meeting to establish a connection 
between HPAC and Main Street. 
C. Walking Tour re-cap and next steps 
Bella thanked HPAC members and shared photos. Andrew said the turnout (over 50) was great and that 
people are already asking when there will be another tour. Carri asked if people could share their scripts

Water in Berthoud
Water in Berthoud
Listed on Berthoud's Local Landmark Register in 2006, this elevated water tank can be
seen from most points in town and is a familiar sight along Highway 287. More than a
century after Berthoud's first water company was formed, it remains a beloved and
iconic symbol of the town's history.
On October 13, 1959, former Berthoud mayor John Bunyan donated
1,000 shares of Bethlehem Steel stock, worth roughly $67,000, to
fund construction of an elevated water tank for the town.
Completed in April 1960, the 100-foot-tall tower held 100,000
gallons and served Berthoud's growing population for decades.
Bunyan, whose father Thomas Chalmers Bunyan founded the Bank
of Berthoud, was also an amateur astronomer whose backyard
observatory is now reconstructed and operating at the Little
Thompson Valley Pioneer Museum.
A Gift That Built a Landmark
A Gift That Built a Landmark
In 1886, the Berthoud Water Company organized to replace the town's original water source, a
small pond near the train depot. The company secured storage rights on Samuel Dobbins
Reservoir and built a wooden pipe system to carry water into town. By 1907, a mechanical
filtration plant had been added, and in 1946 the Town of Berthoud purchased the reservoir
outright, replacing the old wooden pipes with galvanized ones two years later.
From Pond to Pipes
From Pond to Pipes
As Berthoud grew, the water tower was joined by additional infrastructure, but it remained
in service as backup storage and pressure support. In 1982 it received new paint, an epoxy
coating, and equipment upgrades to keep it working for years to come. The land beneath it
was annexed into the Town of Berthoud in 1995.
