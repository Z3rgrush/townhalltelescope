BACKGROUND 
Data centers include those rooms within buildings such as IT centers at Town Hall, Larimer County, 
and Colorado State University, for example as well as large 50+ acre campuses for companies like 
Google or Microsoft.   
 
According to a Google AI search, as of August 2026, there are “4,713 tracked data center facilities, 
3,567 operational, 307 under construction and 751 planned or approved.  The United States data 
center industry is the largest in the world… Texas leads with 578 facilities, followed by Virgina with 
484 and California with 393.”  The same search revealed that Colorado has 90 tracked centers with 
78 being operational, 8 under construction and 4 in the planning phase. The following graphic was 
obtained from the website, Colorado Data Center Map: 90 Sites, 1.7 GW  on September 1, 2026.  
This graphic illustrates the quantity and county name for the greatest number of Data Centers.

In the Town of Berthoud, a Data Center is defined as, “…a use consisting of buildings or structures 
specifically designed or modified to house networked computers and data and transaction 
processing equipment and related infrastructure support equipment, including power and cooling 
equipment. A data center may be used to provide data and transaction processing services, 
outsource information technology services and/or computer equipment colocation services. A data 
center may also include buildings or structures that support the operation of the data center 
including retrofit buildings, warehousing for logistics, independent network buildings, buildings for 
support staff, and emergency generators. A Data Center shall not include activities which 
consistently produce noise discernable at the property line. Should a Data Center produce noise 
discernable at the property line, it shall be classified as an Industrial use.” 
 
The use, “Data Center” for the purposes of this document shall mean an independent center vs. a 
room in a building that is used for data processing to support a specific business (Town Hall, 
Schools, etc.). 
 
Data Centers are only allowed in the M-2, Industrial Zone District in Berthoud.  The use is one 
contemplated through the Special Use Permit process which requires a public hearing before the 
Planning Commission and the Board of Trustees.  Data centers have few occupants and can 
consume a lot of power and water, primarily for use in cooling the servers.   
 
Development standards required at the time of an application submittal include how proposed uses 
will address the following site-specific issues in accordance with Municipal Code Section 30-5-101: 
• Architectural Guidelines 
• Landscape Guidelines 
• Sufficient utilities – water, electrical power, stormwater, traffic, fire protection and 
emergency response, for example.  
• Mitigating negative impacts of the development on the surrounding property: 
o Light 
o Air quality 
o Sensitive natural, historic and environmental areas and properties

There are 66 parcels zoned M2 in Berthoud and all but 5 are less than 5 acres in size.  The average 
parcel size in acres is 3.94 inclusive of those greater than 5 acres and 0.72 excluding the parcels 
greater than 5 acres in size.  The typical data center requires 10 – 40 acres according to a Google 
AI search and this is consistent with staff’s findings contained within this memo.   
 
The information below is a summary of findings after discussing the subject of Data Centers with 
Cheyenne, Wyoming; Weld County; Larimer County; and educational sessions with the American 
Planning Association.  The summary below is arranged by topic. 
 
Land Size 
• Cheyenne:  Seven (7) acres is the minimum; larger and more common centers used by 
companies such as Google or Microsoft require more than 100 acres per use 
• Weld County:  One center is currently being reviewed at the former 438-acre Kodak site.  
This location is surrounded by the Great Western Industrial Park which is in Windsor 
• Larimer County:  No comment on acreage 
 
Water Requirements 
• Cheyenne and Weld County:  Most new centers require water for restrooms in the amount 
of 2 standard residential structures.  Water is needed for exterior landscaping. 
• Cheyenne, Weld and Larimer Counties: Close-loop systems are efficient however waste 
water requires special treatment.  
• Cheyenne:  If evaporative cooling is not used, there needs to be regulations in place to 
require the water to be held and disposed of in a certified hazardous waste disposal site.   
 
Traffic 
• Weld County:  Addresses construction traffic vs. low-volume traffic during operation through 
the Development Agreement process.   
 
Air Quality from Weld County, Larimer County and Cheyenne 
• All roads leading to the facility need to be paved 
• All work is conducted inside a building 
• A Battery(s) and chiller(s) are sometimes located outside and the concerns can be 
addressed through noise and visual mitigation requirements 
 
Energy Provision from Weld County, Larimer County and Cheyenne 
• Noise can be an issue with smaller, less established companies (bitcoin mining). 
• The process of approval through Xcel Energy is 4 – 6 years. 
• Poudre Valley REA is limited in their ability to supply power. 
• Alternative power sources include solid oxide fuel cells.  These are efficient and generate 
heat with little noise. 
• Many facilities include back-up generators for emergency uses only. 
• Noise mitigation is the biggest concern and Weld County will share their contact for a 
scientist at CU Boulder.

• Larimer County: Considering requirements for proper disposal of eWaste and site 
reclamation with surety posting.   
 
Larimer County has a moratorium in place that started in early 2026 and will be continued until early 
to mid-2027. Larimer County is reviewing the following operational standards: 
• Noise – d(B)A and d(B)C 
• Setbacks and reverse setbacks 
• Energy provision 
• Water consumption and disposal 
• Heat 
• Size thresholds and location specifications 
 
The American Planning Association hosted a continuing education course on Data Centers on 
August 7, 2026.  Representatives from Louden County, Virginia were present.  According to the 
presenter, as a County, Louden County has the greatest number of data centers in the country and 
provided best practices to the attendees.  These are listed below along with commentary by 
Berthoud Town staff.  
 
Basic Rules 
• Make sure the Comprehensive Plan and the Development Code list performance standards 
and are aligned. 
o Berthoud staff comment:  The Town Development Code has performance standards 
and bulk standards inclusive of architecture and landscaping control, for example.  
• Never allow a data center to be a use by right.  Require local jurisdiction approval. 
o Berthoud staff comment: A data center is a use only allowed in the M-2 Industrial 
Zone District through the Use by Special Review process.  
• Never sign a Non-Disclosure Agreement 
o Berthoud staff comment:  Agreed, would be discussed with legal counsel. 
 
Noise 
• Conduct a pre- and post-construction noise study (dBA and dBC).  For reference,  dBA is 
audible noise; dBC is Tonal/Narrow band low-frequency humming.  Of the two, dBC has the 
worst health effects and usually occurs in a single, narrow band. 
o Berthoud staff comment: We are proposing to retain a noise expert in 2027 to 
develop noise standards. 
• Adopt a noise ordinance limiting dBA and dBC levels to background noise levels. 
o Berthoud staff comment: We are proposing to retain a noise expert in 2027 to 
develop noise standards. 
 
Power 
• Require utilities to specify, in writing, the maximum power requested by the data center; how 
the utility plans to deliver power (routing, overhead, substations, etc.); and require local 
approval to exceed the reported maximum. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide 
• Build data centers near existing power lines and no closer than 2x the right-of-way width. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be a development standard and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide 
• Require substations to be onsite or adjacent and included in the application; never allow “by 
right” separate substation development or to call them “accessory use.” 
o Berthoud staff comment: This is how our Code is currently written and there are no 
plans to change.

• For energy storage, use long duration storage systems (10 hours of storage minimum), not 
lithium-ion batteries which are not long duration systems.  
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide 
 
Water 
• Require the data center and water utility to specify in writing how much water the data center 
will use annually. 
o Berthoud staff comment: A hydrozone analysis is a required element of a Special 
Use Permit today and would be required.  
• Determine whether the data center will use air cooling, open loop water cooling or closed 
loop water cooling. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide 
• Determine whether the data center will use potable or reclaimed water. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide 
• Determine how the data center will purify its wastewater and whether the local water 
treatment facility can adequately process the wastewater. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide 
 
Emissions 
• Require Tier IV backup generators or Tier II generators with Selective Catalytic Conversion 
(SRC) attached.  If using natural gas turbines, require SCR using ammonia as the catalyst.  
Require no PM2.5 emissions. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide.   
• Require strict noise limits. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide 
 
Setback 
• Minimum 500’ setback from residential; 1000’ preferred 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide as a development standard.  
 
Appearance 
• Require natural screening, berms, fenestration on principal facades. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This is already a requirement. 
• Limit building height, Floor to Area (FAR) ratio to ensure compatibility with surrounding 
structures. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide. 
• Encourage microgrid design within the data center campus for future use.  A microgrid 
includes large power consumer, baseload power source, backup power source, 
long0duration storage, grid connection. 
o Berthoud staff comment: This would be an application requirement and listed in the 
Technical Criteria Guide

I. PURPOSE  
Establish a consistent process for planning, evaluating, designing, approving, bidding, and constructing 
capital improvement projects of an estimated value greater than $2M that: 
 
• Aligns projects with Town strategic goals, master plans, and improvement plans. 
• Improves budget accuracy and schedule predictability. 
• Incorporates value engineering prior to bidding. 
• Incorporates Advisory Committee recommendations and public input. 
• Provides Town Board oversight at key decision intervals. 
• Ensure project scopes are e:ectively developed prior to design development.

VII. GLOSSARY 
ADP – Alternative Delivery Program. This contracting method o:ers a structured approach in which a 
project owner uses non-traditional procurement and contracting methods to plan, design, construct, and 
sometimes operate a project, in contrast to the conventional design-bid-build process.  
A/E – Architect / Engineer. The team contracted for design of the project. Usually consists of a minimum 
of an Architectural ﬁrm and an Engineering ﬁrm.  
As-built Drawings – Drawings of the ﬁnal revised construction documents that show the project exactly 
as it was actually built, capturing all ﬁeld changes, modiﬁcations, and deviations from the original 
design.  
Design-Build – This alternative contracting method integrates design and construction, enabling 
innovation through proposer-driven solutions. E:ective use requires clearly deﬁned project requirements 
and performance outcomes. One team is contracted to perform the design and construction phases of 
the project. 
DBB – Design-Bid-Build. Also known as “Hard Bid” . A traditional construction project delivery method 
where design and construction are contracted separately in a sequential process. 
CMAR – Construction Manager At Risk. Alternate name for CM/GC contracting method. See CM/GC  
CM/GC – Construction Manager / General Contractor. This alternative contracting method supports 
collaboration among the Owner, designer, and contractor during preconstruction to address complex 
technical challenges. Design teams and Construction teams are contracted separately, though these 
contracts can be executed simultaneously. 
Contract – Any written agreement, regardless of title, for the acquisition of supplies or equipment, 
including related incidental services. 
Construction Drawings – Detailed, scaled technical documents that depict exactly what is to be built, 
including dimensions, materials, and methods, and serve as o:icial contract documents guiding 
construction. 
Design Alternatives – Multiple viable methods of achieving the project goals. Owners typically select 
desired alternative based on price, performance, or conformance with stated objectives. 
Feasibility – The likelihood that any alternative or stage of the project can be easily or conveniently 
completed. 
GMP – Guaranteed Maximum Price. This contract sets a maximum price for a construction project, 
beyond which the contractor absorbs additional costs. 
Hard Bid -  See Design-Bid-Build . 
P3 – Public-Private Partnership.  This alternative contracting method provides an arrangement where 
public and private organizations collaborate to design, build, ﬁnance, operate, and maintain 
infrastructure or deliver public services to reduce the individual cost or liability on parties involved. 
Page 29 of 62
15 
 
 Preconstruction Services – These are services o:ered before any actual construction begins. These 
services are part of the preconstruction planning stages of a construction project including Value 
Engineering  and Feasibility  exercises.  
Project Scope – This is a written narrative of the goals and constraints for the project. The scope deﬁnes 
what is and is not included in the project. 
Punch list – This is a register of outstanding project tasks that have yet to be satisfactorily completed 
under the construction contract. These items are typically not substantive in scope. 
Risk Register – A forward-looking list of risks that could happen with their owners, scores, controls, and 
actions enumerated and updated throughout the project life. 
Signiﬁcant Risk – A multitude of potential conditions that have HIGH likelihoods of occurring and can 
have HIGH impacts on the success of the project.  
Value Engineering – A systematic analysis of the functions of various project components and materials 
to lower the cost of materials and services with a tolerable loss of performance or functionality.

I. BASIC PURCHASING POLICY 
 
This policy applies to every department, agency, commission, bureau or other unit of the Town. 
A purchase order shall be obtained from the Town Administrator or any person(s) designated by the Town 
Administrator to act on behalf of the Administrator, on all purchases $2,500.00 or greater before making 
a purchase.  Purchases of produ cts and services of less than $2 ,500.00 shall be pre -approved by the 
applicable department head.  A detailed receipt of the purchase shall be signed by the employee and 
department head and filed with the Finance Department within one business day after the purchase. 
On items over $5,000 but under $50,000.00  a telephone solicitation shall be done to obtain at least three 
(3) bids.  The bid amounts and bidders shall be listed on a solicitation sheet (see Appendix A) and filed 
with the purchase order for the item(s) concerned. 
On items over $50,000.00 at least three (3) written bids shall be obtained.  The written bids, along with 
the results of the bids shall be filed with the purchase order for the item(s) concerned. 
Unsuccessful bidders may view information on the outcome of the bid as listed on the solicitation sheet 
by contacting the applicable Department Head. 
Purchase Orders are not required if a contract for services, product, or project has been signed by the 
Town Administrator.  The contract will serve as the Purchase Order. 
Section IX “Conflict of Interest” provisions shall apply to this section. 
 
II. NORMAL BIDDING POLICY & GUIDELINES 
The Town of Berthoud requires formal sealed bidding on projec ts and equipment expected to cost  
$50,000.00 or more.  In these cases, bid packages shall be prepared and posted on the Town’s website as 
well as other online resources .  In cases where competitive bidding is required by grant or loan terms, 
those rules shall take priority over these. 
Each bid package shall contain complete instructions for submission of the bid.  Bidders will be directed 
to complete the bid in its entirety.  Bidders will also be required to identify any flaws in the bid, to the 
Town Administrator or Department Head ove rseeing the bidding.  All bids must be clearly identified by 
bid number and title on the face of the envelope contain ing the bid.  Bid package fees, if applicable, are 
not refundable unless all bids are rejected; in this case fees may be refunded. 
Bids shall customarily be publicly opened at the time and date specified in the bid packages.  NO late bids 
or telephone bids will be accepted.  Openings are generally held at Town Hall located at 807 Mountain 
Ave in Berthoud Colorado. 
After bids are opened, there will be an evaluation of the bids.  The successful bidder shall be required to 
furnish certificates of insurance and Workers Compensation, and may be required to furnish bid bonds 
and performance-payment bonds, as specified in the bid documents. 
The bid documents shall state that the Town of Berthoud reserves the right to reject any and all bids.  In 
the case of rejection of all bids on construction projects, a staff recommendation will be made to the 
Board of Trustees to reject all bids by motion, which, if rejected, will be made a part of the public record. 
In normal circumstances, after the bid opening and evaluation of bids, a contract will be issued to the 
successful bidder.  All bids awarded shall be in the best interest of the Town and the lowest bid may not 
be selected in all situations.  (See definitions 2 & 11 in the section titled “Glossary”) 
Page 46 of 62
4 
 
Progress payments for construction projects shall be set forth in the contract and shall be approved in 
advance by the Town Administrator, or, in the case of Public Works projects, the Director of Public Works.  
The average payment period by the Town of Berthoud Finance Department is thirty (30) days after review 
and approval of the invoice. 
The Town of Berthoud contract documents shall require a minimum warranty on all work performed  of 
two (2) years, unless otherwise specified in the bid package documents. 
Once a contract is awarded, there shall be no changes to that contract without the written authorization 
of a change order by the Town Project Supervisor and the Town Administ rator.  The contract shall state 
that no verbal changes or agreements shall be binding upon the Town or its representatives.  The official 
Town of Berthoud Change Order will be the only acceptable instrument for execution of changes to a 
contract.  (See Appendix B) 
The Board of Trustees reserves the right to waive competitive bidding under circumstances that it may 
find to be in the best interest of the Town. 
The Board of Trustees will designate, in the resolution approving the annual Town budget, projects and 
purchases which will require board approval before acceptance of bids.  All other projects and equipment 
purchases will be approved by the Town Administrator unless otherwise directed by the Board of Trustees 
during the budget process. 
 
III. EXCEPTIONS 
The bid policy shall apply to every department, agency, commission, bureau or other unit of the Town 
except as set forth, all or in part, in this section: 
a. When a bid project or purchase involves the expenditure of State or Federal assistance or contract 
funds, the Town shall follow any mandatory requirements of applicable State of Federal law and 
implementing regulations. 
b. Nothing in this policy shall prevent the Town from complying with the terms and conditions of 
any grant, gift bequest or cooperative agreement.  Competitive procedures and public disclosure 
shall be followed whenever possible. 
c. Procurement of professional services is excepted from competitive bid regulations.  Requests for 
competitive proposals may be accepted for such services. 
d. When supplies or equipment are purchased using the State of Colorado contracts available to 
local units of government. 
e. When supplies, equipment or services are so specialized that th ere are a limited number of 
known, documented sources, or a single source. 
f. When supplies or equipment are purchased in large quantities that are required for the basic 
operaton of Town facilities (i.e. chemicals, road sand, and aggregates). 
g. When advertising for bids constitutes no advantage in quality, price, or service to the Town, or 
when the “best interest of the Town” is not served by advertising for bids. 
h. This policy shall not be applicable to the leasing, rental, acquisition or disposition of real property. 
i. Any exceptions taken under this section shall be documented with a copy provided to the Town 
Administrator.

IV. LOCAL BUSINESS PREFERENCE 
No provision is made in this policy for preferential consideration of local vendors or contractors.  It is 
however, the policy of the Town to solicit bids from local suppliers whenever and wherever such 
competitive local sources exist, and where no sacrifice or loss in price or quality would result. 
 
V. SOLICITATION TIME 
The minimum time for solicitation of bids shall be 14 calendar days.  When special requirements exist, the 
Town may lengthen or shorten the bid time, but in no case, shall the time cycle be shortened if it will 
reduce competition.  Complicated bids may have a time period which is longer than the standard 14 
calendar days.  Solicitation times of less than 14 days shall be documented by the Town Administrator or 
appointed representative as to why a reduced bid period was required. 
 
VI. RECEIPT OF BIDS 
Upon receipt, each bid shall be time-stamped by machine or by hand and shall be stored in a secure place 
until bid opening time.  Bids shall not be opened upon receipt except for unidentified bids, which may be 
opened for purposes of identification and resealed in the presence of 2 witnesses. 
 
VII. EMERGENCY PURCHASING – DEFINITION OF SITUATION AND GUIDELINES 
An emergency situation is one which creates a threat to public health, welfare or safety such as may arise 
by reason of flooding, utility failures, epidemics, riots, equipment failures or other such reason as may be 
proclaimed by the Town Administrator or duly appointed representative.  The existence of such condition 
creates an immediate and serious need for supplies, equipment, materials or services that cannot be met 
through normal bidding or purchasing methods and the lack of which would threaten, inhibit or impair 
the function of town government or its programs. 
Emergency purchase shall be limited to those supplies, equipment materials or services necessary to meet 
the emergency. 
Any department may make emergency purchases when an emergency condition arises and the need 
cannot be met through normal bidding methods.  In the event an emergency arises after normal working 
hours, the department head shall notify the Town Administrator immediately.  Documentation shall be 
filed listing the situation and action taken. 
 
VIII. CONFLICT OF INTEREST 
Any Town Board member desiring to bid on Town of Berthoud projects shall contact the Secretary of 
State’s Office and submit statements as required by that office.  A copy of these statements shall remain 
on file at the Town Hall during the board member’s term of office.  Should the board member be a bidder, 
contractor, sub-contractor, supplier or consultant on a project, he or she shall withdraw from the Board 
Room, refrain from discussions about the project with any other Trustee or Staff Member, and  shall not 
vote on the proposed contract.

IX. GLOSSARY 
 
1. AWARD – The acceptance of a bid or proposal; the presentation of a purchase agreement or 
contract to a bidder. 
2. BEST INTEREST OF THE TOWN – A term frequently used in granting a Town official the authority 
to use discretion to take action felt to be the most advantageous to the public interest, peace, 
health, or safety. 
3. BID – An offer, as a price whether for payment or acceptance.  A tender given specifically to a 
prospective purchaser upon request, usually in competition with other bidders. 
4. BID BOND – An insurance agreement in which a third part y agrees to be liable to pay a certain 
amount of money in the event that a bid is accepted and the bidder fails to accept the contract as 
awarded. 
5. BIDDER’S LIST – A list maintained by any department of the Town, setting out the names and 
addresses of suppliers of various goods and services from whom bids, proposals and quotations 
may be solicited. 
6. CHANGE ORDER – A fully executed change to an awarded and accepted bid, usually in progress.  
See Appendix B 
7. CONTRACT – Any type of Town agreement, regardless of what it may be called, for the 
procurement or disposal of supplies, equipment, materials or services. 
8. EMERGENCY PURCHASE – A purchase made without following the normal purchasing procedures 
in order to obtain goods or services to meet an urgent and unexpected requirement. 
9. EVALUATION OF BID – The process of examining a bid after opening to determine the bidder’s 
responsibility, responsiveness to requirements and to ascertain other characteristics of the bid 
and bidder that relate to selection of the successful bidder. 
10. LEGAL NOTICE – Notice of a proposed purchase as required by law.  Depending on the legal 
requirement, notice may be satisfied by posting an announcement of the purchase in a public 
place, notification of the appropriate bidders from the bidders list, formal advertisement in a 
newspaper of general circulation, or a combination of these methods. 
11. LOWEST RESPONSIBLE BIDDER – Originally the bidder submitting the lowest initial price and 
capable of performing the proposed contract.  Under current purchasing concepts, generally the 
same as the lowest responsive and responsible bidder, or the bidder who submits the lowest and 
best bid considered to be in the best interest of the Town. 
12. PERFORMANCE BOND – A contract of guaranty executed subsequent to award by a successful 
bidder to protect the Town from loss due to contractor’s inability to complete the contract as 
agreed. 
13. PROPOSAL – An offer made by one party to another as a basis for negotiations for entering into a 
contract. 
14. PUBLIC WORKS DIRECTOR – The director of Public Works department or any person designated 
by the Town Administrator to act on behalf of the director. 
15. RESPONSIBLE BIDDER – That bidder who has the capability in all respects to perform in full the 
contract requirements, and the integrity and reliability that will assure the complete and timely 
compliance with the bid specifications and contract. 
16. SEALED BID – A bid which has been submitted in a sealed envelope, to prevent its contents from 
being revealed or known before the deadline for the submission of all bids. 
17. SPECIFICATIONS – Any description of the physical, functional or performance characteristics, or 
of the nature of a supply, service or construction item.  A specification includes as appropriate, 
requirements of inspecting, testing or preparing a supply, service or construction item for 
delivery.

18. TOWN – The Town of Berthoud, including any department, agency, commission, bureau or unit 
of the Town government. 
19. TOWN ADMINISTRATOR – Chief Administrative Official on all town business appointed to that 
position by the town board, to see that all business conducted is in the best interest of the Town. 
20. WAIVER OF BIDS – A process authorized by the Town Administrator to purchase items without 
competitive bidding procedures, due to unique situations or emergency situations.

I. PURPOSE AND SCOPE 
The purpose of this policy is to establish consistent, transparent, and efficient 
procedures for the acquisition of supplies and equipment by the Town of Berthoud, while 
promoting competition, accountability, responsible stewardship of public funds, and the best 
interest of the Town. 
This policy applies to every department, agency, commission, and other unit of the 
Town. It applies to supplies and equipment acquired through purchase, rental, lease, lease-
purchase, or similar arrangement. It also applies to delivery, installation, warranties, 
maintenance, training, and other services that are incidental to the acquisition of supplies or 
equipment. 
 
Excluded Procurements 
a) Capital projects are outside the scope of this policy. Capital projects include 
construction, reconstruction, renovation, repair, or improvement of Town-owned or 
Town-controlled real property or infrastructure, together with contracts and change 
orders primarily related to that work. Capital projects shall be procured under separate 
procedures approved by the Town and in accordance with applicable law, funding 
requirements, and contract documents. 
b) Professional services are exempt from the competitive requirements of this policy. The 
Town may use requests for qualifications, requests for proposals, direct negotiation, or 
another appropriate selection method for professional services, at the discretion of the 
Town Administrator. 
c) The leasing, rental, acquisition, or disposition of real property is not governed by this 
policy. 
 
When a procurement includes both equipment and construction or other services, the 
Town Administrator shall determine which policy, or procedure applies based on the principal 
purpose, relative cost, and risk of the procurement. 
 
 
II. PURCHASING AUTHORITY AND GENERAL REQUIREMENTS 
1) Budget and Appropriation. No purchase may be made unless sufficient funds have 
been budgeted and appropriated, except as otherwise authorized by law and the Board 
of Trustees. 
2) Town Administrator Authority. The Town Administrator may approve purchases and 
execute contracts within the approved annual budget. The Board of Trustees may 
identify, through the annual budget resolution or other formal action, specific 
purchases that require Board approval before award or commitment. 
Page 55 of 62
4 
3) Department Responsibility. Department heads are responsible for planning 
purchases, confirming budget availability, preparing specifications, obtaining required 
competition, documenting the selection, and ensuring that employees do not commit 
the Town without proper authorization. 
4) Purchase Orders. A purchase order shall be obtained from the Town Administrator or 
designee before committing the Town to a purchase of $2,500 or more. A contract 
signed by the Town Administrator may serve in place of a purchase order. Purchases 
below $2,500 require department-head approval and supporting receipts shall be 
submitted to the Finance Department in accordance with Finance procedures. 
5) Total Purchase Value. Thresholds shall be based on the reasonably anticipated total 
value of the purchase, including freight, delivery, installation, accessories, options, 
renewals, and related charges. Trade-in value, rebates, or credits shall not be used to 
reduce the purchase below an applicable threshold. 
6) Best Interest of the Town. Awards shall be made to the responsible and responsive 
vendor whose offer provides the best value and is in the best interest of the Town. Price 
is important, but the Town may also consider quality, delivery, operating cost, warranty, 
service, compatibility, vendor performance, useful life, and other relevant factors, 
including giving preference to local vendors within the town limits. 
 
 
III. PURCHASING THRESHOLDS 
1) Purchases of $10,000 or Less. Competitive quotations are not required. The 
department shall use reasonable judgment to confirm that the price and terms are fair 
and reasonable. The purchase-order requirement in Section II applies to purchases of 
$2,500 or more. 
2) Purchases Over $10,000 and Under $50,000. The department shall obtain at least 
three competitive quotations. Quotations should be written or electronic whenever 
practicable; telephone quotations may be accepted when documented. Vendor names, 
quoted amounts, material terms, and the basis for selection shall be recorded on the 
Quote Summary and Procurement Justification Form in Appendix A. If three quotations 
cannot reasonably be obtained, the department shall document its good-faith efforts 
and the reason fewer quotations were available. 
3) Purchases of $50,000 or More. The Town shall use a formal competitive solicitation 
unless an exception in Section V applies. The solicitation may be an invitation for bids, 
request for proposals, or another written competitive process approved by the Town 
Administrator. 
 
 
Page 56 of 62
5 
IV. FORMAL COMPETITIVE PROCUREMENT 
1) Solicitation Method. An invitation for bids is generally appropriate when specifications 
are clear and award can be based primarily on price. A request for proposals or other 
best-value process may be used when quality, technical approach, service, operating 
cost, compatibility, delivery, or other factors are material to the selection. 
2) Notice and Solicitation Period. Formal solicitations shall be posted on the Town 
website and may be posted through other electronic procurement resources. The 
normal solicitation period is at least 14 days. The Town Administrator or designee may 
approve a shorter period when operational needs require it and competition will not be 
unreasonably restricted; the reason shall be documented. 
3) Submission and Security. Each solicitation shall state the submission method, 
deadline, required information, evaluation criteria, and Town rights. Electronic or sealed 
submissions may be used. Late submissions shall not be accepted unless the 
solicitation expressly provides otherwise. Submissions shall be secured and not 
disclosed before the deadline. 
4) Opening and Evaluation. Bids may be publicly opened when specified in the 
solicitation. Proposals and other best-value submissions may be evaluated without a 
public opening. The Town may seek clarification, verify qualifications and references, 
inspect equipment, request demonstrations, conduct interviews, or negotiate as 
permitted by the solicitation and at the discretion of the Town. 
5) Town Rights. Each solicitation shall reserve the Town's right to reject any or all 
submissions, waive minor informalities, cancel or reissue the solicitation, request 
additional information, negotiate, and make the award that serves the best interest of 
the Town. The lowest price is not required to be selected when another offer provides 
better overall value. 
6) Award and Contract. The selected vendor shall provide required insurance, licenses, 
warranties, and other documentation before award. The award shall be documented 
through a purchase order, contract, notice of award, or other written instrument 
approved by the Town Administrator.  
 
 
V. EXCEPTIONS AND ALTERNATIVE PROCUREMENT METHODS 
1) Funding Requirements. When state, federal, grant, loan, gift, bequest, cooperative 
agreement, or other funding terms impose procurement requirements, those 
requirements shall control to the extent they are mandatory. 
2) Cooperative Purchasing. Supplies or equipment may be purchased without a 
solicitation process when purchased through a competitive contract established by the 
State of Colorado, another governmental entity, a public procurement cooperative, or 
another cooperative purchasing program available to the Town.   
Page 57 of 62
6 
 
3) Sole Source or Limited Source. The solicitation process may be waived when only one 
source, or a limited number of sources, can reasonably meet the Town's requirements 
because of specialized features, compatibility, standardization, warranty restrictions, 
availability, proprietary technology, or similar documented circumstances. 
4) Used, Demonstration, or Unique-Opportunity Equipment. The solicitation process 
may be modified or waived when the Town has an opportunity to acquire used, 
demonstration, surplus, auction, or otherwise uniquely available equipment and delay 
would likely cause the opportunity to be lost. The department shall document condition, 
market comparison, and value. 
5) Bulk and Market-Priced Operational Supplies. Recurring operational commodities, 
including chemicals, fuel, road sand, aggregates, and similar supplies, may be 
purchased through unit-price agreements, cooperative contracts, documented market 
quotations, or another method approved by the Town Administrator when formal 
bidding would not provide a practical advantage. 
All exceptions shall be documented in writing and retained with the purchase record. The 
Town Administrator may require additional competition, market research, legal review, or 
Board of Trustees approval when warranted by the size, complexity, risk, or public interest 
associated with a purchase. 
 
 
VI. LOCAL BUSINESSES 
This policy does not establish a guaranteed price preference for local vendors. The Town 
should solicit qualified local suppliers whenever competitive local sources exist and doing so 
will not reduce competition or result in an unreasonable sacrifice in price, quality, availability, 
or service. 
 
VII. EMERGENCY PURCHASING 
An emergency is a condition that creates an immediate threat to public health, welfare, 
safety, property, essential services, or the continuity of Town operations, including without 
limitation flooding, fire, utility failure, epidemic, pandemic civil disturbance, equipment failure, 
cybersecurity incident, or a similar urgent and unexpected event. 
Emergency purchases shall be limited to supplies, equipment, materials, and incidental 
services reasonably necessary to address the emergency. A department may make an 
emergency purchase when normal procedures cannot be followed without unacceptable 
delay.   All filings and documentation will still be required within a reasonable timeframe.  The 
department head shall notify the Town Administrator as soon as practicable and shall submit 
written documentation describing the emergency, the purchase, the vendor selection, and the 
cost.

7 
VIII. RECORDS AND ADMINISTRATION 
1) Procurement File. The responsible department shall provide the Finance Department 
with the purchase order or contract, and other records needed to support the purchase 
and payment. 
2) Public Records. Procurement records are Town records and may be subject to 
inspection and disclosure under applicable law. Confidential or proprietary information 
shall be handled in accordance with law and the solicitation documents. 
3) Administrative Procedures. The Town Administrator and Finance Director may issue 
forms, procedures, and guidance consistent with this policy, including procedures for 
purchasing cards, purchase orders, electronic solicitations, vendor onboarding, 
insurance, asset tracking, surplus property, and records retention. 
4) Interpretation. The Town Administrator is authorized to interpret and administer this 
policy. Questions involving legal requirements, conflicts of interest, or the classification 
of a procurement may be referred to the Town Attorney. 
 
 
IX. GLOSSARY 
AWARD - The Town's acceptance of an offer and authorization to proceed through a purchase 
order, contract, notice of award, or similar written instrument. 
BEST INTEREST OF THE TOWN - The use of reasonable discretion to select the action or offer 
that provides the greatest overall public value after considering price, quality, risk, service, 
timing, and other relevant factors. 
CONTRACT - Any written agreement, regardless of title, for the acquisition of supplies or 
equipment, including related incidental services. 
EQUIPMENT - Tangible personal property of a durable nature, including vehicles, machinery, 
tools, furnishings, technology, and operational equipment. 
PROFESSIONAL SERVICES - Services requiring specialized knowledge, judgment, licensure, 
or expertise, including legal, engineering, architectural, financial, consulting, and similar 
services. 
SUPPLIES - Consumable or expendable goods, materials, parts, commodities, and other 
tangible items used in Town operations.
