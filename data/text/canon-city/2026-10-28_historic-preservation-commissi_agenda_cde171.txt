To Whom It May Concern:  
 
The Historic Preservation Commission of Cañon City, Colorado has major concerns 
regarding the proposed changes to Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act of 
1966 (36 C.F.R. Part 800). For nearly sixty years, the protections provided by Section 106 have 
ensured small communities the right to have a voice before – not after – historic places are 
permanently affected.  
 
The current Federal Administration has proposed regulations to weaken public participation 
and consultation with informed local historians. Effective communication between all 
stakeholders, including the public, prevents conflicts, reduces project costs, and decreases the 
potential for lawsuits.  
 
Small communities deserve the right to have their voices heard when it comes to protecting our 
shared history and the places that matter to our local communities before these historic 
resources are disastrously altered or demolished.  
 
Our Historic Preservation Commission feels every American has a place that tells their 
family’s story, and people deserve the right to help shape the decisions affecting those places 
that matter to them since their communities will live with the consequences.  
 
Historic resources support local tourism, create jobs, attract local investment, strengthen our 
historic downtowns, tell America’s story, increase property values, and preserve the overall 
character that makes our communities an attractive place to live and work.  
 
Our historic landmarks go beyond buildings; they include neighborhoods, our Main Street, 
parks, and cultural landscapes that surround our community. The proposed changes to Section 
106 could put all these resources at significant risk.  
 
Please help us preserve our history by allowing us to keep our voice in the decision-making 
process. Thank you for your consideration.  
 
Sincerely,
