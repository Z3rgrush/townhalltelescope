Section 4. 
Analysis: 
 
Evaluation of variance requests is regulated in  § 17.10.050(B) of the CCMC.   
 
Variance Review Criteria.  In determining whether a variance should be granted, the Board of Adjustment 
must consider whether the practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship claimed by the applicant was created 
by the applicant and take into account the nature of the hardship. N o variance shall be granted unless the 
Board of Adjustment makes all of the following findings: 
 
1. That the strict application of this UDC would result in exceptional practical difficulty or undue hardship, 
unique to the applicant's property, inconsistent with the objectives of this UDC. 
 
The applicant notes that the existing GC zoning regulations would typically require fence to be at least 
50% transparent and restrict the use of barbed wire.  These restrictions  present a challenge for the 
impound lot in that the site will be used to house sensitive evidence and requires an extra layer of security 
to prevent attracting unwanted attention to the area.  
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. The strict application of standard commercial fencing requirements 
to an active law enforcement evidence and vehicle impound yard creates an exceptional operational 
difficulty. Unlike typical retail or commercial uses envisioned in the GC District, securing police evidence 
requires high physical security and visual screening (opacity) to prevent theft, vandalism, and 
unauthorized access or observation. Requiring 50% transparency and restricting necessary perimeter 
security wire would undermine the primary security and evidentiary preservation objectives of the facility. 
 
2. That either: 
 
a. There are unique or unusual circumstances or conditions applicable to the site of the variance, 
such as unusual lot size or shape, topography, or other physical conditions peculiar to the affected 
property, that do not exist throughout the neighborhood or district in which the property is located, 
or 
b. There is a physical disability affecting an owner of the property or any member of the family of an 
owner who resides on the property, which impairs the ability of the disabled person to utilize or 
access the property. 
 
The applicants’ proposed variance is requested to integrate and match with the existing fencing on site.  
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. The unique physical condition applicable to this property is its 
specific functional site layout and governmental land use as a municipal public safety headquarters. 
The existing impound yard already utilizes security fencing features along its original footprint. 
Expanding the yard to the west within parcel boundaries requires matching the established security 
installation to maintain a consistent, uninterrupted security envelope.  
 
Criterion 2b is not relevant in the evaluation of the variance request.

3. That the unique or unusual circumstances or conditions of the property will not allow a reasonable use 
of the property, comparable with other property in the general neighborhood of the site, in the absence 
of relief. 
 
The applicant maintains that the Police Department would not be able to utilize this space in a fashion 
consistent with law enforcement operations without the variance.  The sensitive and secure nature of 
the work conducted at this site requires these variances to function. 
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. A primary reasonable use of municipal public safety grounds is 
the secure storage of impounded vehicles and physical evidence gathered by the CCPD, Fremont 
County Sheriff’s Office, and Colorado State Patrol. Without the requested relief to allow full visual 
opacity and enhanced perimeter security barriers, the City cannot reasonably or safely utilize this 
portion of the property for its intended public safety purpose. 
 
4. That the variance to be granted is the minimum variance needed to allow for a reasonable use of the 
land, building, buildings and/or other structure or structures involved. 
 
The proposed 6-foot fence with privacy inserts, overhead barbed wire, and internal razor wire 
represents the standard security barrier configuration utilized for law enforcement impound facilities. 
The facility also utilizes closed-circuit security cameras to monitor the yard, and additional cameras will 
be integrated to cover the expanded footprint. 
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. The requested variances represent the minimum opacity 
requirements and material relief necessary to establish a fully secure impound facility. The proposed 6-
foot overall fence height aligns with typical non-residential standards, while the addition of privacy slats 
and interior/overhead security wire provides the minimum physical and visual deterrent required for 
multi-agency law enforcement evidence storage. Electronic surveillance measures (security cameras) 
complement, but cannot replace, the required physical perimeter barriers. 
 
5. That the variance, if granted, will not alter the essential character of the neighborhood or district in which 
the property is located, nor substantially or permanently impair the appropriate use or development of 
adjacent property. 
 
The surrounding Justice Center Road corridor contains existing properties with security fencing 
configurations comparable to the proposed design, including nearby civic and institutional facilities such 
as Loaves & Fishes Ministries.

The applicant maintains that the police department property poses a unique security obligation that is 
distinct from most typical functions within the GC zone district.    
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. Granting the variance does not confer a special commercial

RE: Zoning Variance Request – 161 Justice Center 
 
 
The city is seeking a variance from portions of Sections 17.06.050.A and 17.06.050.C of the municipal code in relation to 
installation of additional fencing at the Cañon City Police Department.  The CCPD has a need to expand their existing 
impound lot yard at 161 Justice Center Drive to accommodate secure storage of materials that are impounded in the 
course of their work.  In addition to providing impound service to CCPD the lot is also utilized by Fremont County Sheriff 
and Colorado State Patrol for impound purposes.  The existing lot is undersized and requires expansion within the existing 
parcel boundaries. 
 
Within Section 17.06.050.A the CCPD is seeking a variance to allow for barbed wire in the GC zone district.  Currently 
barbed wire is only allowed in the I district and on larger lots in the R-L and R-1 district.   
 
Within Section 17.06.050.C the CCPD is seeking a variance from the requirements related to material in three separate 
areas.  First, seeking a request to not require 50% transparency in the fencing.  Second, seeking a variance to allow chain 
link inserts, and third seeking a variance to allow for the installation of razor wire on the interior face of the fence.    
 
The proposed fence will be a 6 FT high, galvanized chain link fence with inserts, three strands of barbed wire at the top, 
and three internal strands of razor wire per the details in the drawing.  The proposed fence will expand the impound 
storage by approximately 100 FT to the west as shown in the attached, and the proposed fencing material will be as near 
identical as possible to the existing fenced perimeter of the impound lot.   
 
We believe that this use is consistent with the existing use on the site and meets the requirements listed for consideration 
of a variance to allow the expansion of the impound lot to proceed.
