Established in 2020, Cañon City Area for Reinvestment Expansion (C-CARE)’s mission is to mitigate
blight in Cañon City’s commercial core and planned development areas, encouraging investment and
reinvestment within targeted areas of the community. The ultimate goal is to strengthen the citywide
tax base and expand the area’s job base. 
C-CARE provides gap-financing incentives to developers pursuing projects that remediate blight, 
improve aginginfrastructure, and redevelop key historic structures. These incentives leverage future
tax revenues known as Tax Increment Financing (TIF) on a reimbursement or grant basis. TIF is a
mechanism that captures net new, incremental tax revenues (sales or property tax above an established
base that does not exist until development occurs) that are created when blighted, vacant or
underutilized properties are redeveloped, and channels those revenues into grants or cost
reimbursements for developers. This tool helps relieve the financial “gap” of development that results
from the added costs of construction caused by the need to create new infrastructure or replace aging, 
blighted infrastructure and modernizing historic structures. 
Past Projects
Since its inception in 2020, C-CARE has assisted four development projects: two historic renovations, 
one project addressing blight in key areas of the community, and one that addresses Colorado
Department of Transportation (CDOT) infrastructure requirements along the high-traffic east US 50
corridor: 
Renovation of the historic Hotel St. Cloud - Main Street
Phase 1 was completed on December 31, 2022, with the opening of Fremont Public House.  
Phase 2 was completed in early 2024 with the completion and opening of the main lobby, 1887
Historic Eatery and the War Room speakeasy. 
PROJECT COMPLETE - Phase 3, the completion of the guest rooms and complete grand
opening, was completedin 2025 with a soft opening in early May 2025 and a grand openingin July
2025. 
Renovation of the historic Fremont County National Bank building - Main Street
Project is complete, and was awarded the Governor’s Award for Downtown Excellence in the
Place” category, presented by Downtown Colorado, Inc.

Faricy Boys Ford redevelopment project - US 50 East Access Corridor
Project is complete. Faricy moved into its new building in October 2022, followed immediately

Holiday Inn Express development project - US 50/Mackenzie Avenue Median Project

Four Mile Ranch Planned Development District & New TIF Area Proposed
C-CARE continues to work with the Four Mile Ranch development team on the creation of a new
25-year TIF area cover approximately 740 acres within the 1,563-acre Four Mile Ranch Planned
Development District on the east side of Cañon City. Unlike C-CARE's existing reimbursement-based
projects, this would be a project-specific urban renewal area in which tax increment is used to support
bonds and other debt financing needed to convert a decades-dormant development that remains vacant,
underutilized land, into the major infrastructure required to initiate development. The proposed TIF
area represents only a portion of the overall Four Mile Ranch development.
At full buildout, scheduled to occur over more than 20 years, the 1,563-acrea Four Mile Ranch plan
provides for approximately 2,700 housing units, with most of the residential development
approximately 75%) located outside the proposed TIF area. This distinction is important because
growth outside the TIF boundary will generate new property tax revenue directly for all taxing entities, 
while the TIF area helps provide the infrastructure necessary to make the larger development possible. 
The plan also includes significant acreage for future commercial development, helping address Cañon
City's limited inventory of development-ready commercial land and the substantial retail spending that
currently occurs outside the community. Four Mile Ranch has also proposed providing land to the City
for future workforce-housing opportunities. Together, the residential, commercial, and infrastructure
components make Four Mile Ranch one of the community's most significant long-term opportunities
for expanding the tax base and accommodating the future planned growth needed to regain economic
self-sufficiency. 
C-CARE Grants Program
C-CARE continues to provide Façade Improvement, Life Safety and Accessibility, and Building
Systems grants to help property and business owners overcome some of the higher costs associated
with renovating and modernizing older commercial buildings. Eligible improvements include façades;
fire suppression and alarm systems; electrical and HVAC upgrades; energy-efficiency improvements;
accessibility improvements; and other building-system upgrades. Initiated in 2026, the program will
expand in 2027 to include a new, more flexible grant structure that provides support for a broader
scope of building improvements.
Grant activity remained strong in 2026, with projects occurring primarily along Main Street and
elsewhere within the urban renewal area. During the year, C-CARE supported façade improvements; 
sprinkler and fire-safety projects; electrical and energy-efficiency upgrades; and other building and
infrastructure improvements. The 2026 register includes projects at Sally's Bakery/5th Street Market, 
Yarned n Dangerous, Unbridled Holdings, several Main Street properties, Palace Drug, Royal
Pharmacy Consultants, the Abbey Event Center and others.

improving the long-term usability of Cañon City's commercial properties.

C-CAREGrant Program Feasibility Gap Survey
Overview of Findings
Per C-CAREboard request, C-CAREstaff conducted an online survey of former URA
grant recipients and others familiar with development and renovation in Cañon City, 
principally in the downtown and URAarea. Invitations were sent to 36 email addresses
with 7 responses to the survey.  
Executive Summary
The C-CAREGrant Program Feasibility Gap Survey provides an early but fairly consistent picture
of the financial challenges associated with rehabilitating older commercial buildings in downtown
Cañon City. 
The survey received eight responses, with seven respondents providing substantive information
about completed or proposed building projects. Allseven project respondents identified projects
located in the downtown area between 1st and 9th Streets. 
Although the sample size is limited, several themes were repeated across respondents: older-
building conditions are difficult to predict, code and construction requirements can significantly
increase project costs, and the completed value or income potential of a property often does not
support the amount that must be invested in rehabilitation. 
The responses also indicate that C-CAREassistance is doing more than improving appearances. 
Respondents reported that projects increased property values, improved building safety and
functionality, created usable or leasable space, retained businesses, attracted tenants, and
returned vacant spaces to productive use. 
The principal finding is that the feasibility problem is not simply the cost of construction. It is the
gap between what must be invested in an older building and what the property can reasonably
support through value, rent, or business income after the work is completed. 
Project Size and Financial Feasibility
Projects represented a wide range of investment levels, from less than $ 10,000 to more than
250,000. Several respondents reported projects in the $ 100,000 to $250, 000 range or higher. 
26
When asked whether their projects would have been financially feasible without C-CARE
assistance, five of the seven substantive respondents indicated that the project probably or
definitely would not have been feasible, or selected a response indicating that feasibility was
doubtful. Several also indicated that they would have reduced the scope of their project without
assistance. 
This suggests that grant assistance is not simply reimbursing projects that would have occurred
unchanged. In many cases, assistance appears to affect whether a project proceeds, how much
work is completed, or whether additional improvements can be incorporated. 
The importance of assistance was also reflected in the percentage of project cost covered by C-
CARE. Five of the seven project respondents reported that C-CAREassistance represented 21% 
or more of project cost, including respondents who reported assistance exceeding 30%. 
The Feasibility Gap
The estimated financial gap varied considerably by project. 
Among respondents able to estimate a specific amount: 
Two reported gaps of approximately $ 5,000–$9,999. 
One reported a gap of $10,000–$ 24,999. 
Two reported gaps of $25,000–$ 49,999. 
One reported a gap of $100,000 or more. 
One respondent indicated that the gap was simply too difficult to estimate. 
The range is significant because it suggests there may not be a single appropriate grant amount
for every rehabilitation project. Smaller façade or building improvements may face relatively
modest gaps, while major rehabilitation projects can encounter feasibility gaps measured in tens
or even hundreds of thousands of dollars. 
Primary Barriers to Rehabilitation
The most frequently identified challenges were: 
Building age and condition — identified by five of seven project respondents. 
Construction costs — identified by four respondents. 
Building code requirements — identified by four respondents. 
Other recurring concerns included structural repairs, electrical/ plumbing/ mechanical
upgrades, fire and life-safety requirements, architectural and engineering expenses, 
unexpected construction conditions, and the inability of rents, business income, or
appraised property value to support the required investment. 
These responses point toward two related problems. 
27
The first is the cost of rehabilitating the building itself. Older buildings frequently require
work that is not immediately visible before demolition or construction begins. 
The second is the economic performance of the completed property. Even when
rehabilitation is technically possible, the resulting property may not generate enough rent, 
business income, or appraised value to justify the total investment. 
One respondent summarized the problem particularly well, noting that rehabilitation of historic
buildings can become a “labor of love” rather than a sound business decision because
completed projects seldom appraise for an amount approaching the actual construction cost. 
Another respondent described the difficulty as the inability to know what will be discovered once
work begins, particularly when opening walls or removing existing improvements triggers
additional code requirements. 
Unexpected Costs and Risk
Three of seven project respondents reported significant unexpected costs after construction
began. Allthree reported unexpected costs of at least $ 10,000, and two reported unexpected
costs exceeding $ 25,000. Even respondents who did not report a specific unexpected- cost
amount repeatedly described uncertainty itself as a major feasibility problem. 
For an owner or developer, this uncertainty creates risk before a project even begins. Financing
may be based on an initial construction estimate, but concealed structural, electrical, plumbing, 
environmental, or code issues can materially change the project after financing has already been
arranged. This suggests that project feasibility should be evaluated not only according to the
known construction budget but also according to the owner' s capacity to absorb unforeseen
costs. 
Benefits of C-CARE-Assisted Projects
The survey also provides strong evidence of the broader benefits generated by rehabilitation
projects. Among the seven substantive project respondents: 
7 of 7 reported improved appearance. 
5 of 7 reported increased property value. 
5 of 7 reported improved building safety or functionality. 
3 of 7 reported increased usable or leasable space. 
3 of 7 reported helping retain an existing business. 
2 of 7 reported that a vacant building or space became occupied. 
2 of 7 reported helping attract a tenant. 
One reported that the project allowed an existing business to expand. 
These findings are important because the public benefit extends beyond the individual property
owner. Rehabilitation can contribute to occupancy, business retention, property values, public
safety, neighborhood appearance and additional private investment. 
28
One respondent specifically noted that improvements to their building appeared to encourage
investment by other property owners in the same downtown block and generated positive
comments from residents about the improvement of that portion of Main Street. 
Assistance Property Owners Would Find Most Useful
Respondents were also asked which forms of assistance would be most helpful for future
projects. The strongest recurring requests were: 
Code compliance assistance and larger grants for major rehabilitation projects. 
There was also significant interest in electrical/ plumbing/ mechanical assistance, façade
grants, interior renovation assistance, assistance for vacant buildings, low-interest loans, 
architectural or engineering assistance, environmental remediation and pre- development
or feasibility assistance. 
The responses support maintaining relatively simple assistance for smaller projects while
providing the C-CAREBoard with greater flexibilityfor larger or more complicated projects. 
In particular, the results support the concept behind the Special Projects Grant: rather than
attempting to anticipate every possible building problem with a separate grant program, C-CARE
can evaluate larger projects according to the actual feasibility gap and the public benefit
produced by the investment. 
Overall Conclusion
While the number of responses remains limited, the survey provides a consistent initial message. 
Renovating older downtown buildings in Cañon City is often economically difficult because
rehabilitation costs can exceed what the property' s eventual value, rent, or business income can
reasonably support. That challenge is compounded by uncertainty about hidden conditions and
code requirements that may not become apparent until construction begins. 
Atthe same time, the survey demonstrates that successful rehabilitation projects create benefits
extending beyond the individual property owner. They improve appearance and safety, increase
property value, create usable commercial space, retain businesses, attract tenants, return
vacant properties to productive use and can encourage additional investment nearby. 
The results therefore support continuing C-CARE's smaller, easily accessible grant programs

ExecutiveSummary
The survey provides a small but consistent picture of the feasibility challenge associated with rehabilitating
older commercial buildings in downtown Cañon City. Seven respondents supplied substantive information
about completed or proposed projects, all in the downtown area between 1st and 9th Streets. The sample is not
large enough to be statistically representative, but the responses show recurring themes that are useful for
program design and board discussion. 
The central finding is that the feasibility problem is not simply that construction is expensive. Respondents
described a recurring gap between the amount that must be invested in older buildings and what the completed
property can support through appraisal, rent, or business income. That gap is compounded by uncertainty: 
hidden conditions, structural needs and code requirements may not become known until construction is
underway. 
Keyfindings
Building age and condition was the most frequently identified feasibility barrier, cited by 5 of 7 project
respondents; construction costs and building code requirements were each cited by 4 of 7. 
Three respondents reported significant unexpected costs after work began; all three reported at least
10, 000 in unexpected costs, and two reported more than $ 25, 000. 
The estimated feasibility gap ranged from $5,000-$9,999 to $100,000 or more, showing that the size of
the gap varies substantially by project. 
Every project respondent reported improved property appearance; 5 of 7 reported increased property
value and 5 of 7 reported improved safety or functionality. 
Code compliance assistance and larger grants for major rehabilitation were the most frequently
selected forms of future assistance among substantive project respondents. 
SurveyMethod:  
The workbook contains eight responses. Seven respondents provided project- specific information; one
respondent answered only the future- assistance question and meeting- related questions. Unless otherwise
noted, project findings and the charts in this report use the seven substantive project responses. Several survey
questions allowed multiple selections, so category totals can exceed the number of respondents.

1. Barriers to Rehabilitation Feasibility
Finding. The strongest concentration is around the physical condition of older buildings, construction cost and
code compliance. Respondents also identified structural, building- systems, fire/ life-safety, appraisal and
income limitations. Together, these responses indicate that the feasibility gap can be driven by both the cost
side of a project and the limited economic value created after rehabilitation. 
2. Benefits Produced by Completed Projects
Finding. The reported benefits extend beyond cosmetic improvement. Respondents identified increases in
property value, building safety and functionality, usable or leasable space, business retention, tenant attraction
and occupancy of previously vacant space. Thisprovides evidence that rehabilitation assistance can generate
broader redevelopment and economic- development outcomes.
