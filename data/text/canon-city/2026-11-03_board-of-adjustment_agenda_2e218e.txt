Section 4. 
Analysis: 
 
Evaluation of variance requests is regulated in  § 17.10.050(B) of the CCMC.   
 
Variance Review Criteria.  In determining whether a variance should be granted, the Board of Adjustment 
must consider whether the practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship claimed by the applicant was created 
by the applicant and take into account the nature of the hardship. N o variance shall be granted unless the 
Board of Adjustment makes all of the following findings: 
 
1. That the strict application of this UDC would result in exceptional practical difficulty or undue hardship, 
unique to the applicant's property, inconsistent with the objectives of this UDC. 
 
The applicant notes that the existing GC zoning regulations would typically require fence to be at least 
50% transparent and restrict the use of barbed wire.  These restrictions  present a challenge for the 
impound lot in that the site will be used to house sensitive evidence and requires an extra layer of security 
to prevent attracting unwanted attention to the area.  
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. The strict application of standard commercial fencing requirements 
to an active law enforcement evidence and vehicle impound yard creates an exceptional operational 
difficulty. Unlike typical retail or commercial uses envisioned in the GC District, securing police evidence 
requires high physical security and visual screening (opacity) to prevent theft, vandalism, and 
unauthorized access or observation. Requiring 50% transparency and restricting necessary perimeter 
security wire would undermine the primary security and evidentiary preservation objectives of the facility. 
 
2. That either: 
 
a. There are unique or unusual circumstances or conditions applicable to the site of the variance, 
such as unusual lot size or shape, topography, or other physical conditions peculiar to the affected 
property, that do not exist throughout the neighborhood or district in which the property is located, 
or 
b. There is a physical disability affecting an owner of the property or any member of the family of an 
owner who resides on the property, which impairs the ability of the disabled person to utilize or 
access the property. 
 
The applicants’ proposed variance is requested to integrate and match with the existing fencing on site.  
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. The unique physical condition applicable to this property is its 
specific functional site layout and governmental land use as a municipal public safety headquarters. 
The existing impound yard already utilizes security fencing features along its original footprint. 
Expanding the yard to the west within parcel boundaries requires matching the established security 
installation to maintain a consistent, uninterrupted security envelope.  
 
Criterion 2b is not relevant in the evaluation of the variance request.

3. That the unique or unusual circumstances or conditions of the property will not allow a reasonable use 
of the property, comparable with other property in the general neighborhood of the site, in the absence 
of relief. 
 
The applicant maintains that the Police Department would not be able to utilize this space in a fashion 
consistent with law enforcement operations without the variance.  The sensitive and secure nature of 
the work conducted at this site requires these variances to function. 
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. A primary reasonable use of municipal public safety grounds is 
the secure storage of impounded vehicles and physical evidence gathered by the CCPD, Fremont 
County Sheriff’s Office, and Colorado State Patrol. Without the requested relief to allow full visual 
opacity and enhanced perimeter security barriers, the City cannot reasonably or safely utilize this 
portion of the property for its intended public safety purpose. 
 
4. That the variance to be granted is the minimum variance needed to allow for a reasonable use of the 
land, building, buildings and/or other structure or structures involved. 
 
The proposed 6-foot fence with privacy inserts, overhead barbed wire, and internal razor wire 
represents the standard security barrier configuration utilized for law enforcement impound facilities. 
The facility also utilizes closed-circuit security cameras to monitor the yard, and additional cameras will 
be integrated to cover the expanded footprint. 
 
Staff finds that this criterion is met. The requested variances represent the minimum opacity 
requirements and material relief necessary to establish a fully secure impound facility. The proposed 6-
foot overall fence height aligns with typical non-residential standards, while the addition of privacy slats 
and interior/overhead security wire provides the minimum physical and visual deterrent required for 
multi-agency law enforcement evidence storage. Electronic surveillance measures (security cameras) 
complement, but cannot replace, the required physical perimeter barriers. 
 
5. That the variance, if granted, will not alter the essential character of the neighborhood or district in which 
the property is located, nor substantially or permanently impair the appropriate use or development of 
adjacent property. 
 
The surrounding Justice Center Road corridor contains existing properties with security fencing 
configurations comparable to the proposed design, including nearby civic and institutional facilities such 
as Loaves & Fishes Ministries.

Section 2.  
Issue: 
 
Setback requirements for Accessory Structures in residential zones are described in Sec. 17.05.140(C): 
 
C. Accessory structure. 
 1. An accessory structure shall not exceed two hundred (200) square feet. 
 2. An accessory structure shall not have a permanent foundation.  
 3. An accessory structure shall have a maximum height of twelve (12) feet. 
 4. An accessory structure shall be located either: 
 a. Completely within the required rear setback and a minimum of five (5) feet from rear lot lines, or 
 b. Completely within the buildable area of the lot and to the interior side or rear of the principal 
building. 
 
Note that this placement regulation is in addition to a standard 25-foot front yard setback for all structures on 
a residential lot (whether principal or accessory .)  The zoning conflict arises due to the fact that the lot at 
2101 N. 8th Street is undersized according to lot standards for the R-1 zone district.

The lot at 2101 N 8 th Street is a remnant undersized lot. In the R -1, Low Density Residential Zone District, 
the minimum lot size is 6,000 square feet. 2101 N. 8th Street is approximately 4,600 square feet.   
 
It should be noted that the property was previously addressed as 731 Beech Avenue. It contained a single -
family home which was damaged in a fire and eventually demolished in 2008. In 2024, a building permit was 
obtained to construct a new home on the property. As it neared compl etion in 2025, the property owners 
sought to change the address to N. 8th Street, since street access could not be provided on Beech Avenue- 
the width of the lot along Beech Avenue is approximately 35 feet, which does not meet the minimum 60-feet 
width for R-1 zoned lots. The 8th Street side of the property is also the side of the property the front door is 
oriented toward.     
 
As the pictures in Figures 2 and 3 show, this property is disadvantaged under zoning because of its size. The 
lot pre-dates the zoning, which is why it is considered legal, non-conforming under the City’s land use code.

B. Variance Review Criteria. In determining whether a variance should be granted, the Board of Adjustment 
must consider whether the practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship claimed by the applicant was 
created by the applicant and take into account the nature of the hardship. No variance shall be granted 
unless the Board of Adjustment makes all of the following findings:  
 1. That the strict application of this UDC  would res ult in exceptional practical difficulty or undue hardship, 
unique to the applicant’s property, inconsistent with the objectives of this UDC , 
 2. That: 
 a. There are unique or unusual circumstances or conditions applicable to the site of the variance, 
such as unusual lot size or shape, topography, or other physical conditions peculiar to the affected 
property, that do not exist throughout the neighborhood or district in which the property is located, 
or 
 b. There is a physical disability affecting an owner of the property or any member of the family of an 
owner who resides on the property, which impairs the ability of the disabled person to utilize or 
access the property, 
 c. That the unique or unusual circumstances or conditions of the property will not allow a reasonable 
use of the property, comparable with other property in the general neighborhood of the site, in the 
absence of relief, 
 d. That the variance to be granted is the minimum variance needed to allow for a reasonable use of 
the land, building, buildings and/or other structure or structures involved, 
 e. That the variance, if granted, will not alter the essential character of the neighborhood or district in 
which the property is located, nor substantially or permanently impair the appropriate use or 
development of adjacent property, and 
 f. That the granting of the variance will not constitute a grant of special privilege inconsistent with the 
limitations on other properties classified in the same district.

Staff concurs that the lot is simply not wide enough to accommodate a 20- foot carport and meet front yard 
setbacks. In order to meet the required 25-foot setback, the carport would have to be shrunk to 13 feet, which 
is not a sufficient depth to allow most passenger vehicles to be fully parked under the structure.

Staff therefore finds that criteria 1, 2a, c, d, and e are relevant in the evaluation of the variance and the 
consideration of the existing precedent on the same block. 
1. In the immediate vicinity of the subject property, every lot contains a garage or carport. This lot does not 
contain a structure to shelter vehicles. Strict compliance with the City’s Land Use Ordinance would result 
in an accessory structure too small to do the job of sheltering vehicles adequately in a manner consistent 
the other residences in the immediate vicinity. 
2a. There exists an unusual condition on site at 2101 N 8th in that it is an undersized residential lot within the 
R-1, Low Density Residential District (4,300 square feet as opposed to the minimum required 6,000 
square feet) and has insufficient lot width ( 35 feet as opposed to the minimum required 60 feet) . This 
represents a physical hardship that exists on the site itself, irrespective of any action the property owner 
has taken. 
2c. As stated in Criteria 1, in order for an accessory carport structure to meet the front yard setback, it would 
have insufficient depth to properly shelter a passenger vehicle. 
2d. The nature of the vari ance would be to allow the ac cessory carport structure to meet a 13.5 foot front 
yard setback, which would be consistent with the setback for both the house (the principal structure) and 
the intermodal shipping container (another accessory structure), both of which were placed on site prior 
to the address being changed to North 8th Street. 
2e. Staff believes not granting this variance request would create the unusual condition this criteria seeks to 
avoid. Every other residential lot in the vicinity of the subject property has garages (either attached to the 
principal structure or as stand-alone accessory structures) or carports. Not granting this variance would 
make this lot the exception to the existing condition in this neighborhood.
