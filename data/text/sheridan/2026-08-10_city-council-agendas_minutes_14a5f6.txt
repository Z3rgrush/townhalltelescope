10. Public Hearing  
To consider an application to rezone property located at 3600 S. Federal Blvd. from the 
Business (BUS-1) and Commercial (COM-C) district to the Public (P) district. 
Public Hearing - 3600 S. Federal Blvd.pdf 
Mayor Daigle opened the public hearing at 7:23 p.m. on August 10, 2026, to consider a request to rezone 
property located at 3600 South Federal Boulevard from Business-1 (B-1) and Commercial C (Com-C) to the 
Public (P) District. No disclosures or recusals were stated. 
The applicant, Ana Claudia Magalhaes, Planning Supervisor, and Melissa Reese-Thacker, Director of Planning 
and Construction, introduced themselves and confirmed they were present on behalf of South Suburban 
Parks and Recreation District. 
Mayor Daigle entered Exhibits A through I into the record, including the rezoning application, authorization 
documents, conceptual site plan, Planning and Zoning Commission materials, site photographs, and public 
notice affidavits. 
Planner Ari Snow presented the staff report. He explained that the property consists of two parcels located at 
the southeast corner of Federal Boulevard and U.S. 285, currently zoned B-1 and Com-C. The City acquired 
the property in January 2024 as part of a partnership with the Mile High Flood District. The proposed rezoning 
would facilitate development of a public trailhead and park space. Mr. Snow reviewed the surrounding land 
uses, public outreach efforts, and the applicable rezoning criteria. He noted that the proposal promotes 
public health, safety, and welfare through increased recreational opportunities, improved trail circulation, 
enhanced site visibility, and replacement of a vacant property with a maintained public space. He further 
stated the proposal is consistent with multiple goals of the City's Comprehensive Plan related to recreation, 
open space, trail connectivity, environmental stewardship, and neighborhood revitalization. Staff reported 
receiving no objections from reviewing agencies, no letters of support or opposition, and noted that the 
Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval of the rezoning request through 
Resolution No. 4-2026. 
In response to a question from the audience, staff confirmed the intent is to develop the property as a park 
and trailhead facility. 
Applicant Representative Ana Claudia Magalhaes presented the proposed Bear Creek Trailhead project. She 
described plans for reconstruction of the vehicular and pedestrian bridge, trail realignment to improve safety 
and circulation, additional trailhead amenities, and small park features. She noted that extensive community 
engagement informed the design and highlighted ongoing coordination with CDOT and City staff. The 
applicant also discussed future way finding signage, potential incorporation of a "Welcome to Sheridan" 
monument sign, and anticipated project funding efforts, including grant opportunities. No questions were 
posed by Council following the presentation. 
Mayor Daigle opened the public comment portion of the hearing. No members of the public came forward to 
provide testimony. 
With no additional testimony or evidence presented, Mayor Daigle closed the public hearing at 7:44 p.m. on 
August 10, 2026.

Following the close of testimony, the City Attorney reminded Council of the quasi-judicial standards for 
deliberation and encouraged members to reference the applicable findings contained in the Municipal Code 
and staff report when discussing the rezoning request. 
Council members expressed support for the proposed rezoning and commended staff for their work 
throughout the planning process. Mayor Pro Tem Moreno noted the project had been well planned and 
appreciated the opportunity to participate in community engagement efforts, including the neighborhood 
outreach process, which helped shape the project's vision. Council Member Whittaker stated that the 
proposed park and trailhead represented a much better use of the property and fulfilled a community need. 
Mayor Daigle expressed appreciation for the project, noting that efforts to connect trailheads and improve 
way finding in the area had been discussed for many years through previous planning initiatives. She reflected 
on the history of the former Carrol’s Corner Pub property and suggested that the site's history and community 
significance be considered during future discussions regarding naming of the park. 
In discussing the required findings, Council members agreed with staff's analysis that the application met the 
rezoning criteria outlined in the Municipal Code and was consistent with the goals and policies of the City’s 
Comprehensive Plan. Council concurred that the proposed public use would be an appropriate use of the 
property and would provide a benefit to the community.
