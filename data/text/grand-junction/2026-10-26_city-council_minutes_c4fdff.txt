1. Discussion Topics  
 
a. 2027 City Manager's Recommended Budget   
City Manager Mike Bennett and staff continued their presentation of the proposed 2027 
budget, focusing on enterprise and internal service funds, utility rates, capital improvements, 
and one-time funding requests. 
Key Points: 
Budget Overview and Fund Balance 
• Staff reviewed efforts to address the structural deficit through strategic expenditure 
reductions, personnel adjustments, cost reallocations, and use of fund balance. 
• The proposed budget includes 20 additional positions, primarily supporting public safety 
and expanded recycling operations. 
• The projected General Fund balance is $42.8 million, approximately $13 million above 
the City's minimum reserve requirement. 
• Fund balance will continue to support the First Responder Fund expenses while the City 
pursues a long-term funding solution through a potential fire district. 
Internal Service and Special Revenue Funds 
• Staff reviewed the Visit Grand Junction, Information Technology, Equipment, Facilities, 
and Communications Center funds. 
   
 
 
• Visit Grand Junction's budget includes deferred marketing expenditures from 2026 to 
2027 to maximize their economic impact. 
• The Equipment Fund includes $4.87 million for fleet replacement. Council discussed the 
aging fleet, replacement priorities, and opportunities for sharing equipment with 
neighboring jurisdictions. 
• Staff reviewed facility maintenance needs, regional emergency communications 
funding, investment earnings, and changes to the City's financial partnership with 
Colorado Mesa University. 
Enterprise Funds and Utility Rates 
• Staff presented proposed budgets and rates for water, wastewater, Ridges irrigation, 
solid waste and recycling, golf, and parking operations. 
• Council discussed proposed utility rate increases, water conservation, infrastructure 
investments, and the long-term sustainability of utility funding. 
• Staff highlighted improvements planned for the Juniata Ditch, which experiences 
approximately 1,400 acre-feet of water loss annually, and discussed the proposed dam 
expansion. 
• Council discussed utility availability charges, tap fees, and their relationship to housing 
affordability. 
• Staff reviewed the expansion of citywide recycling and optional seasonal yard waste 
collection, including associated rate adjustments. 
• Council discussed proposed golf course fee increases, particularly their impact on 
affordability at Lincoln Park Golf Course. 
• Staff reported that the Parking Fund has stabilized following operational adjustments 
and public outreach, although reserves remain limited for future infrastructure 
expansion. 
Capital Improvement Program 
• Staff presented the proposed $93.5 million capital improvement program, including 
approximately $19.1 million from the dedicated capital sales tax fund and $8.1 million 
for transportation capacity projects. 
• Major projects include the next phase of the Persigo wastewater treatment facility 
expansion, water infrastructure improvements, street maintenance, bridge repairs, and 
transportation safety projects. 
• Council discussed project prioritization, maintenance of existing infrastructure, and 
opportunities to leverage outside funding. 
• Projects reviewed included North Avenue improvements, the C 1/2 Road Riverfront Trail 
connection, school safety routes, intersection improvements, and storm drainage 
infrastructure. 
• Staff explained the reallocation of approximately $2.1 million in personnel expenses to 
capital projects to more accurately reflect project costs. 
 
   
 
 
One-Time Funding Requests 
• Council reviewed funding requests from organizations serving individuals experiencing 
homelessness, including Joseph Center, Catholic Outreach, Hilltop, Homeward Bound, 
and Mutual Aid Partners. 
• Discussion focused on the importance of these services, available City resources, 
accountability, and the sustainability of using one-time funding for ongoing operations. 
• Council expressed differing perspectives regarding additional funding outside the 
established request-for-proposals process. 
• Council requested that Homeward Bound submit a revised written funding request with 
updated financial information before further consideration. 
• Staff noted that additional funding would require Council direction and would come from 
available one-time funds. 
Budget Adoption Schedule 
- Staff reviewed the remaining budget process, with the first reading scheduled for November 
4, 2026, and public hearings and final adoption anticipated for November 18, 2026.
