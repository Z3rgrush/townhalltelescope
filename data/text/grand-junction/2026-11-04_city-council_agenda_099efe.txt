7.a.    Authorize Contract Amendment with AE2S for the Persigo 
Wastewater Treatment Plant Phase 2 Expansion Project 
Recent failures of secondary clarification infrastructure at the Persigo Wastewater 
Treatment Plant (Persigo) have significantly reduced operational reliability and 
eliminated redundancy within this essential step of the treatment process. Although 
Secondary Clarifier #1 has been returned to service following emergency repairs, 
Secondary Clarifier #4 remains out of service, leaving Persigo dependent on Secondary 
Clarifiers #1 and #2 without a standby unit. 
 
The City's 2020 Wastewater Treatment Facilities Master Plan identified construction of 
Secondary Clarifier #3 as a future improvement. However, the original Phase 2 
Expansion Project anticipated rehabilitation of the three existing clarifiers rather than 
construction of an additional system. Recent mechanical failures and potential structural 
deficiencies at Clarifier #4 have raised concerns about whether rehabilitation remains a 
viable long-term solution. 
 
Staff recommended amending the existing AE2S engineering services contract in an 
amount not to exceed $950,000 to accelerate design and permitting of Clarifier #3 while 
assessing the condition of Clarifier #4. Construction of Clarifier #3 will be accelerated as 
part of the Phase 2 Expansion Project to restore operational redundancy and allow for 
the planned rehabilitation of Clarifiers #1 and #2 while maintaining treatment capacity. 
Enviro Comp Sustain Manager Ashley Firl gave a presentation on this contract 
amendment and was available to answer questions from Council. 
Councilmember Van Dyke moved, and Councilmember Nguyen seconded, to authorize 
the City Purchasing Division to execute a contract amendment with Advanced 
Engineering and Environmental Services, LLC (AE2S) in an amount not to exceed 
$950,000 for secondary clarifier assessment, design, and permitting services as part of 
the Persigo Wastewater Treatment Plant Phase 2 Expansion Project. Motion carried by 
a unanimous voice vote.

BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
BACKGROUND 
WinCo Foods LLC requests an amendment to the Zoning and Development Code to 
remove two parcels totaling approximately 39.3 acres from the area depicted at Figure 
04.04-1 “Prohibited Restaurant and Retail Drive-Through Areas.” The effect would be to 
allow drive-throughs associated with restaurant and retail sales uses on the two parcels 
comprising the applicant’s properties presently within the prohibition area (“affected 
Packet Page 21
property”). 
 
The prohibition on restaurant and retail drive-throughs has evolved across multiple 
codes and plans. 
 
The affected property was a portion of the Neighbors Annexation of 1985 (Ordinance 
No. 2239). Upon annexation, it was zoned Planned Recreation (PREC, Ordinance No. 
2240).  
 
Early conceptual planning for 24 Road development and infrastructure was 
incorporated into the City’s first Growth Plan in 1996 (Resolution No. 91-96). Action 
Items 38 and 39 in the Growth Plan called for “corridor development regulations […] 
addressing appropriate uses and development design” and for 24 Road to be 
constructed “as an urban parkway with a trail system.”  
 
An updated Zoning and Development Code was adopted March 7, 2000 by Ordinance 
No. 3240 (“2000 Code”). With this adoption, Planned Development zone districts 
formerly identified by their type and addressed within prior codes, including the affected 
property’s PREC zone district, were collapsed into a single Planned Development zone 
district. 
 
During and after the 2000 Code adoption process, the City was undertaking a study of 
the greater 24 Road area, which culminated in the adoption of the 24 Road Corridor 
Subarea Plan in November 2000 (Resolution No. 109-00). It introduced the “Preferred 
Plan” for the 24 Road Corridor, which included the assignment of subarea-specific land 
use categories to different parts of the subarea. The Commercial designation was 
assigned to a “node in the north east corner of the I-70 interchange, as well as 
expansion of commercial uses along U.S. Highway 6/50.” The affected property of this 
proposed amendment, on the other hand, was assigned the Mixed Use Development 
designation, which applied to approximately 41.6% of the subarea, and which limits the 
extent of retail uses: 
 
Mixed use development is encouraged in the remaining areas to include 
employment, residential and open space. Retail commercial may be appropriate as a 
secondary use, integral to other uses and structures or as a small (eight to 10 acres) 
nodal development at 24 Road and G Road intersection. 
 
The Mixed Use (M-U) zone district was also introduced in November (Ordinance No. 
3303) explicitly for the purpose of implementing 24 Road Corridor Subarea Plan 
recommendations, and it was applied to properties including the affected property by 
Ordinance No. 3304. 
 
The M-U zone district in the 2000 Code (as amended) provided for “a mix of light 
manufacturing and office park centers, limited retail, service and multifamily residential 
uses,” where those limitations on retail included a maximum floor-area ratio (0.5), a 
maximum building size for retail (30,000 square feet), and a minimum of 20% of parcels 
Packet Page 22
or projects exceeding 5 acres developed as residential. Restaurant and retail drive-
throughs were not allowed in the M-U district, and office drive-through uses required a 
conditional use permit. 
 
Ordinance No. 3305, also adopted in November 2000, established the 24 Road 
Corridor Design Standards and Guidelines. Among other requirements, “Auto-Oriented 
Uses” were addressed. Following minor revisions in 2016, those standards provide that 
“drive-up and drive-through facilities (order stations, pick-up windows, bank teller 
windows, money machines, car drop-off areas for auto service or rental, etc.) shall be 
located on the side or rear of a building and away from residential uses,” with a limited 
exception for buildings greater than 100 feet from the street when an “adequate 
landscaping buffer from the right-of-way line” is provided. 
 
February and April 2010 saw a new Comprehensive Plan (Ordinance No. 4406, 
replacing the 1996 Growth Plan) and a new Zoning and Development Code (Ordinance 
No. 4419, “2010 Code”) adopted. The 2010 Comprehensive Plan assigned new land 
use designations across much of the City, which included splitting the affected property 
into the Commercial Industrial and Village Center – Mixed Use (7 DU/acre) 
designations. At the time of the 2010 Code’s adoption, the M-U zone district was an 
implementing district for the Commercial Industrial land use designation, but it was not 
an implementing district for the VC-MU designation. 
 
The 2010 Code retained the two principal uses, “Drive-Through Uses (Restaurants)” 
and “Drive-Through Uses (Retail),” which were not allowed in the Mixed Use (M-U) 
zone district (among other districts). “Office with Drive-Through,” however, was allowed 
in M-U. Under the 2010 Code, the affected property remained zoned Mixed Use (M-U); 
therefore, drive-through restaurants and retail were not allowed on the affected 
property.  
 
The One Grand Junction Comprehensive Plan (“2020 Comprehensive Plan”) replaced 
the 2010 Comprehensive Plan upon adoption on December 16, 2020. The 2020 
Comprehensive Plan again assigned new land use designations across the City. The 
Commercial designation supplanted the previously split designations for the entire 
affected property. The Commercial designation is characterized by primary uses 
including “commercial, offices, retail, employment, medical campus/hospitals, and 
service-oriented uses.” 
 
Following the adoption of the 2023 Zoning and Development Code, as described below, 
and a corresponding Comprehensive Plan amendment to update its Implementing Zone 
Districts, the Commercial designation is implemented by Mixed-Use Light Commercial 
(the present zone district of the affected property), Commercial General, Public, Civic, 
and Institutional Campus, and Planned Development. 
 
The 2020 Comprehensive Plan also incorporated existing neighborhood and subarea 
plans, including, for example, those previously adopted for Pear Park and Orchard 
Mesa. Notably, as will be discussed further under the analysis below, the 24 Road 
Packet Page 23
Corridor Subarea Plan was not similarly incorporated and was instead explicitly sunset 
by the adoption of the 2020 Comprehensive Plan in favor of the latter’s treatment of the 
subarea. 
 
Ordinance No. 5190, adopted December 20, 2023, repealed the 2010 Code and 
adopted its replacement (“2023 Code”). The 2023 Code introduced a new 
organizational structure and new content across most sections. The 2023 Code also 
introduced new zone districts, which resulted in the consolidation of several zone 
districts established under the 2010 Code into fewer zone districts under the 2023 
Code. The 2023 Code became effective on January 21, 2024. The Official Zoning Map 
was updated on the same date from the 2010 Code’s zone districts to the 2023 Code’s 
zone districts by Ordinance No. 5191. 
 
The 2010 Code’s Mixed Use (M-U), Light Commercial (C-1), Business Park Mixed-Use 
(BP), and some among that code’s form-based districts were consolidated into the 2023 
Code’s Mixed-Use Light Commercial (MU-2) zone district. At the time of the 2023 
Code’s adoption, there were 1,114.22 acres of land zoned C-1 (71.6%), 366.26 acres 
zoned M-U (23.5%), and 75.76 acres zoned BP (4.9%), for a total of 1,556.24 acres. 
Today, following the district consolidation and a few annexations and zone changes 
throughout the City, there are 1,635.44 acres of land zoned MU-2. Beginning January 
21, 2024, and continuing through the present, the affected property has been and is 
zoned Mixed-Use Light Commercial (MU-2).  
 
Combining these zone districts (“legacy districts” in the 2023 Code) with disparate 
development standards (dimensions, uses, etc.) entailed selecting for preservation only 
those standards from each legacy district which would cohere to meet the intent of the 
MU-2 district. Whereas M-U had not allowed “Drive-Through Uses (Restaurants)” and 
“Drive-Through Uses (Retail),” these uses were allowed in the 2010 Code’s C-1 and BP 
districts. 
 
Given that the preponderance of the land to be zoned MU-2 had been zoned C-1, it 
would have represented a significant expansion of the prohibition on restaurant and 
retail drive-throughs to apply the M-U’s restriction. Instead of doing so, a new approach 
was adopted. First, drive-throughs from principal uses paired with one of three business 
types to accessory uses (“Drive-Through Facility”), while preserving the tripartite 
division into Office, Restaurant, or Retail. Each of these is allowed in MU-2. Second, the 
prohibition was partially preserved by shifting it from being zone district-based to 
geographically bound, as represented by Figure 04.04-1 at § 21.04.040(e)(2)(i)(E) of 
the 2023 Code. This had the effect of maintaining the prohibition for 233.07 acres of 
land, or 63.6% of the total land that had been zoned M-U. 
 
More specifically, both the 2010 Code’s M-U prohibition and the 2023 Code’s 
geographic prohibition area were and remain of greatest impact on the 24 Road 
Corridor. Of all land previously zoned M-U, 264.73 acres (72.3%) were located within 
the 24 Road Corridor, as previously defined by Title 33, 24 Road Corridor Subarea 
Plan, and implemented by Title 25, 24 Road Corridor Design Standards. The 
Packet Page 24
geographic prohibition area is contained entirely within the 24 Road Corridor. The 
233.07 acres subject to the geographic prohibition represent 88% of those lands 
previously zoned M-U in the 24 Road Corridor. 
 
Altogether, the focus of the restaurant and retail drive-through prohibition across time 
and varying means has historically been to ensure that development in the 24 Road 
Corridor Subarea is compatible with adopted plans and the vision for the subarea. The 
applicant argues, however, that amending the area subject to the prohibition is 
compatible with the Comprehensive Plan and is more consistent with the Zoning and 
Development Code than the present extent. 
 
PROPOSED AMENDMENT 
The applicant proposes to amend Figure 04.04-1 at Section § 21.04.040(e)(2)(i)(E) to 
allow drive-throughs associated with restaurant and retail sales uses on the two parcels 
comprising the applicant’s properties presently within the prohibition area. The existing 
Figure 04.04-1 and the amended figure proposed by the applicant are attached to this 
staff report and shown in the proposed ordinance.

Code Amendment Narrative 
  
WinCo Foods, LLC (“Applicant”), submits this narrative regarding its request to amend 
the Grand Junction Zoning & Development Code (the “Code”) §21.04.040(e)(2)(i)(F) and 
Figure 04.04-1. 
 
This request is made in connection with anticipated supermarket and related shopping 
center development (the “Project”) on Parcels No. 2701 -324-00-113 and No. 2701-324-00-
114 in Grand Junction, Colorado 81505 (collectively, the “Property”).  The Property is a 
combined 39.3-acre parcel (approximately) and is currently designated as Mixed -Use Light 
Commercial (“MU-2”) subject to the 24 Road Corridor Design Standards under Title 25 of the 
Code. Drive through uses are generally allowed by right in the MU -2 zone district. However, 
drive-through restrictions under § 21.04.040(e)(2)(i)(F) and Figure 04.04 -1 of the Code  
preclude retail and restaurant drive through uses on the Property, as well as certain surrounding 
lands which are common uses on major corridors, particularly in proximity to a freeway 
interchange. This prohibition makes development of the Project economically infeasible.

RECOMMENDATION: 
  
Staff recommends approval for the City Purchasing Division to enter into a contract with 
QP Services, LLC for the amount of $2,534,894.00 for cured-in-place-pipe lining 
rehabilitation of sanitary sewers for the 2026-2027 CIPP Lining Rehabilitation Project. 
  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: 
  
The purpose of this contract is to hire a contractor who specializes in Cured-In-Place-
Pipe Lining and Rehabilitation to line existing sanitary sewer lines that are nearing the 
end of their useful life. The work will also include lining a water main under the Union 
Pacific Railroad. The scope of this project includes lining approximately 23,309 linear 
feet of sanitary sewer pipe and 263 linear feet of water pipe. 
  
BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
The City of Grand Junction is responsible for maintaining over 588 miles of sanitary 
sewer lines throughout the Persigo 201 service area and all water infrastrusture, 
including water mains, associated with the City's Water Services. The condition of the 
sanitary sewer collection system is continually inventoried as part of the regular 
cleaning and inspection program. The City's asset management program prioritizes 
repair of sanitary sewer lines based on factors such as age of infrastructure, condition 
of pipe, maintenance history, and consequences of failure. Water main lines are 
categorized in a similar way through the City's asset management program and critical 
locations, such as under railroads, help identify areas where lining can be 
advantageous. The sanitary sewer lines and water main included in this contract will be 
rehabilitated through lining to extend their useful life and restore pipe integrity without 
Packet Page 46
requiring full replacement. 
 
The sanitary sewer pipes to be lined in this project consist of segments that were noted 
as structurally depreciating, holes in the pipe wall, and visible soil from within the pipe. 
Pipe sizes range from 6-inches to 20-inches in diameter. The water main included in 
this project is an aging 8-inch cast iron pipe located beneath a railroad crossing. 
Although previously identified for full replacement, inspection confirmed that the pipe 
can be cost-effectively rehabilitated through lining instead. The parses identified for 
CIPP lining will be completed in 2026 and early 2027.

agencies to post solicitations), posted on the City's Purchasing website, sent to the

A Resolution Supporting the Application for the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice

RECOMMENDATION: 
  
Staff recommends adoption of the resolution authorizing application for the 2026 BJA 
JAG grant. 
  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: 
  
The Grand Junction Police Department has been solicited by the Bureau of Justice 
Assistance (BJA) program of the US Department of Justice (DOJ) to apply for an 
annual, formula grant in the amount of $42,582. As part of the application process, the 
Bureau of Justice Assistance requires that City Council review/authorize receipt of the 
grant and provide an opportunity for public comment (30 days minimum). Therefore, a 
public comment opportunity is requested for the purpose of satisfying this requirement. 
  
BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
The Grand Junction Police Department continues to focus on initiatives that will further 
strengthen our community policing and officer safety initiatives. The Grand 
Junction Police Department will use these funds to purchase one additional TAC-WALL 
48 Modular Defense System for the Grand Junction Police Department. The system 
includes three 20 x 48 inch ballistic shield panels, Level III ballistic protection, a 
crossbar, and a carrying case. The project will expand mobile ballistic protection 
available through patrol supervisors' vehicles for response to incidents involving 
firearms, protection of exposed persons, and rescue operations. The equipment will be 
compatible with the Department's existing TAC-WALL platform, supporting consistent 
training and interoperable defensive configurations. These grants are specifically

designed for law enforcement and public safety improvements, making them an ideal 
source for this critical investment. 
  
FISCAL IMPACT: 
  
An application will be submitted for $42,582, to be shared with the Mesa County 
Sheriff’s Department. The Grand Junction Police Department’s portion will be used to 
purchase drug field-testing equipment for patrol officers.

I move to adopt Resolution 98-26, a resolution authorizing the City Manager to submit a

Recitals: 
The City Council supports an application for the Fiscal Year 2026 Edward Byrne 
Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG), administered by the United States 
Department of Justice (DOJ), Office of Justice Programs, Bureau of Justice 
Assistance (BJA), to provide financial assistance to the Grand Junction Police 
Department (GJPD). 
The 2026 Colorado Local JAG Allocations identify a joint award of $42,582 for 
the City of Grand Junction and Mesa County, consisting of $29,892 allocated to 
the City and $12,690 allocated to Mesa County.  
 
The City proposes to use its $29,892 portion for mobile ballistic shields. 
The proposed purchase is one additional Special Ops Bunker TAC-WALL 48 
Modular Defense System, for deployment within the patrol supervisor vehicle 
fleet. The estimated total project cost for the complete system is $38,500. The 
City will request $29,892 in JAG funds and fund the remaining $8,608 from the 
Police Department budget. 
 
GJPD has been awarded this grant annually and would like to apply for the 
current cycle, which requires assurance of community priority. Applications 
cannot be submitted unless approved by the City Council. As part of the 
application process, the Bureau of Justice Assistance requires that the City 
Council review/authorize receipt of the grant and provide an opportunity for 
public comment (30 days minimum). Therefore, a public comment opportunity 
will be provided for the purpose of satisfying this requirement.

Manager is authorized and directed to finalize and submit such DOJ BJA 
grant application; and,  
2. If the grant is awarded, the City Council supports the use of the funds as 
provided in the grant agreement which the City Manager is authorized to 
sign on behalf of the City as grantee of the DOJ BJA grant.
