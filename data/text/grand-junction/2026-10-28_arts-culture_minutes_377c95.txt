MINUTES 
Grand Junction Commission on Arts and Culture (GJCAC)

GJ Community Recreation Center – Sycamore Community Room  
 
Commissioners Present: Robbie Breaux (Chair), Julie Matthews (Vice Chair), Libbie Early, Bill Lelbach, 
Gilberto Ramirez Macias, TJ Smith, Cynthia Zaitz 
Commissioners Absent: Pamela Blythe, Hank Braxtan, Mayor Laurel Lutz, Porcia Silverberg, Amy Wall

Chair Robbie Breaux called the meeting to order at 4:35 p.m. and brief introductions were made. Kalen 
McCain with the Daily Sentinel was sitting in to hear about the Firefighter Memorial agenda item, and 
Abigail Mangum and Connie Monroe with Grand Junction Casting were sitting in to learn more about the 
GJCAC. 
Libbie Early moved to approve the August 26, 2026 minutes, Cynthia Zaitz seconded the motion, and it 
passed unanimously. Cynthia moved to approve an amendment to the July 22, 2026 minutes to 
document the previous consensus that organizations participating in the funding program must be 
located in Mesa County, in addition to the discussion regarding the location of funded activities. Bill 
Lelbach seconded the motion, and it passed unanimously. 
BUSINESS 
Staff presented two proposed options for the 2027–2028 Arts and Culture Funding Program based on 
discussions from the July and August GJCAC meetings. Option 1 would allow Colorado organizations to 
apply, with priority given to Mesa County organizations and additional location requirements for 
organizations outside Mesa County. Option 2 would limit eligibility to Mesa County organizations.  
Commission members discussed the value of supporting local organizations while retaining 
opportunities to bring high-quality arts and cultural programming into the community. Following 
discussion, Cynthia moved to approve moving forward with Option 1, TJ Smith seconded, and it passed 
unanimously. The Commission then proceeded to review the proposed Criteria and Goals document for 
the Funding Program.  
The Commission continued the discussion on expanding the program to allow organizations to request 
General Operating Support (GOS) rather than requiring funding to be tied exclusively to a specific 
project or event that was discussed during the September meeting. It was mentioned that the removal 
of the previous “Does Not Fund” section from the Criteria and Goals document that was approved 
during the September meeting needed clarification that this meant organizations may apply for GOS. 
Cynthia moved to approve the removal of the previous “Does Not Fund” section from the Criteria and 
Goals document, clarifying that organizations may apply for GOS. Bill Lelbach seconded, and the motion 
passed unanimously. 
Additional discussion included: maintaining a maximum funding request of $4,500; giving priority to 
expenditures that compensate artists; continuing to require performing arts organizations to submit a 
performance video; retaining the requirement that organizations sit out one funding cycle after 
receiving funding for three consecutive years, (one commissioner expressed opposition to this 
requirement but indicated it would not prevent support of the overall document); improving and 
standardizing the annual budget information submitted by applicants to make financial comparisons 
between organizations clearer and more consistent; clarifying required documents and eligibility 
requirements within the application materials.  
Cynthia moved to approve the revised Funding Program Criteria and Goals document, Libbie seconded, 
and the motion passed unanimously.   
Staff presented proposed revisions to the Funding Program Application to align it with the approved 
Criteria and Goals and the transition to General Operating Support. Commissioners discussed the need 
to clearly communicate to applicants that they may request operating support rather than funding for a 
specific project or event. Members also discussed how project location, audience/attendance, 
collaborations, and other information should be collected when applicable.  
The Commission emphasized that the application and associated outreach should clearly explain the 
significant change in the funding model so that organizations understand their options. Staff noted that 
workshop sessions for the Funding Program will continue to be offered and that program changes can 
also be communicated through outreach materials.  
There was a consensus to table approval of the funding application until the October meeting to allow 
additional revisions and discussion. Commissioners were asked to submit comments or concerns to staff 
prior to the meeting. Further discussion regarding whether priority should be given to activities 
occurring within Grand Junction will also continue in October. 
Staff provided information regarding a proposed memorial at Las Colonias honoring firefighters who lost 
their lives in a recent fire. The proposed concept includes a monument with a compass pointing toward 
the location of the fire. Staff requested feedback regarding the possibility of incorporating a Native 
American element into the memorial.  
Commission members generally expressed that the memorial should remain focused on honoring the 
firefighters. Members noted that if Native American elements were incorporated, consultation with 
appropriate tribes would be important. Other suggestions included incorporating geographic or 
geological references to the area. 
CITY STAFF REPORTS/UPDATES 
Staff provided brief updates on upcoming events and ongoing public art projects, including completed 
murals along the Riverfront Trail and an update on Superbloom 2.0 which will be installed later in the 
fall at 24 ½ Road and Four Canyons Parkway. The Strategic Cultural Plan Subcommittee has also met and 
is developing a project timeline and working on facilitation training. 
COMMISSIONER UPDATES 
A commissioner requested a future agenda discussion regarding protocols for providing constructive 
and supportive feedback following event evaluations. Members discussed current evaluation practices 
and the potential need to revisit evaluation and final-report processes as the funding program 
transitions toward organizational operating support.  
Upcoming funded events were noted, including the Grand Valley Community Orchestra fall concert, Día 
de los Muertos activities, and the Junktown Film Festival. 
As there was no further business, Bill moved to adjourn the meeting at 6:07 p.m., Libbie seconded, and 
it passed unanimously.
