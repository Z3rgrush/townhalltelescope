What is the purpose of a Workshop? 
 
The purpose of the Workshop is to facilitate City Council discussion through analyzing 
information, studying issues, and clarifying problems. The less formal setting of the Workshop 
promotes conversation regarding items and topics that may be considered at a future City 
Council meeting.

Dear Grand Junction City Council Members and City Manager Bennett,  
On behalf of HomewardBound of the Grand Valley (HBGV), we respectfully submit this funding 
request to support the sustainment of HBGV in our community. 
HBGV requests a Pay for Performance model to provide assurance to the City Council and 
Taxpayers on meaningful use of funds. With this request:  
• $50,000 upfront funding 
• $1,500 for every individual moved into non-shelter housing from HBGV 
With a Pay for Performance model, the base award supports the shelter capacity, staffing, food, 
utilities, safety, and case-management infrastructure needed to serve guests consistently. The 
outcome payment rewards verified movement from shelter into housing – with a cap of 
$150,000/year. This structure gives the City a defined service commitment, transparent reporting, 
and a direct connection between public investment and housing outcomes.  
 Current State 
Since the HBGV in February 2026, HBGV has seen increased success in meeting the needs of our 
vulnerable community members while maintaining responsible financial

to improve sustainability while increasing the organization’s focus on housing progression and

HBGV in the Homeless Care Continuum 
HBGV functions as both an emergency safety-net provider and a bridge to the housing and service 
systems that follow shelter. It does not replace affordable housing, permanent supportive housing, 
behavioral-health treatment, or prevention services. The shelter’s role is to provide a safe, stable, 
and supportive environment for individuals experiencing homelessness, helping them address 
immediate needs, regain stability, and connect with appropriate resources and a clear pathway 
toward permanent housing or other suitable long-term support. HBGV is one of the key service 
providers that allow access to other community care providers including Hilltop, Catholic 
Outreach, the Joseph Center, and more.

outlines the Council's agreed-upon guiding principles and conduct standards.

Risk Sharing Agency (CIRSA). Throughout that discussion, Councilmembers provided

behaviors. Based on those conversations, staff developed a draft Norms of Conduct 
attached to this staff report for Council discussion. This is a Council document and the

any changes.

The Grand Junction City Council adopts these Norms of Conduct to guide how members work

Our guiding principles describe who we intend to be as a governing body. Our norms describe

SUBJECT: 
  
Boards and Commissions Bylaws 
  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: 
  
The City Council and staff have expressed a desire to review and update the bylaws of 
boards and commissions to create a higher level of consistency, clarity, and efficiency 
in their management. 
  
BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
Staff proposes that each board review and adopt a model board and commission bylaw. 
In doing so, the model bylaw will create clarity and consistency in several key areas. 
The model bylaw is intended to solidify, across all advisory boards, key policy decisions 
of the City Council, including required board member qualifications, the role of Council 
liaisons as voting/nonvoting members, board member terms and vacancies, and 
Council ratification of future board and commission bylaw changes. The draft model 
bylaw is attached and has sections in red that require further City Council discussion 
and feedback. 
 
The model bylaw template also creates consistent, clear language related to the 
following: 
- purpose and authority of boards 
- ethical conduct, conflicts 
- compensation and expenses 
- contracts and expenditures 
- attendance and removal procedures 
- officers, staff liaison roles 
- open meeting and public notice requirements 
- public access and recording requirements. 
Packet Page 15
 
The purpose of this discussion is for staff to receive direction on certain policy decisions 
that must be determined by the City Council, as well as to discuss the adoption of a 
model bylaw for advisory boards and commissions. If this approach is taken, and after 
Council has provided direction on the policy-level elements of the model bylaws, each 
advisory board will receive a copy to review. The board will be expected to fill in all 
board-specific elements of the template (number of board members, membership 
requirements, purpose and authority, etc.). Upon the board's review and approval, the 
completed bylaws will be submitted to the City Council for review and ratification.

properly noticed meeting.

Article XV. Legal Advice, Finances, and Insurance 
STANDARD ARTICLE: same text for all advisory boards (Boilerplate tab) 
The City Attorney is the legal advisor to the Board. Staff designated by the City Manager shall 
serve as treasurer for the Board. The City's insurance provides coverage for members as City 
volunteers and will defend members against losses, costs, and expenses, including legal fees, 
reasonably incurred because they are or were members of the Board, so long as the member 
acts in accordance with law and policy. A member shall contact the City Attorney immediately if 
a claim arises or if the member has questions about coverage.
