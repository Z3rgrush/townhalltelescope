SUBJECT: 
  
2027 Recommended Budget for the Persigo Sewer System 
  
RECOMMENDATION: 
  
This presentation and discussion is intended for informational purposes. 
  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: 
  
The City manages, operates, and maintains the Persigo Wastewater System for the 
benefit of users of sewer service in the Persigo 201 Service Area. The City and Mesa 
County participate jointly as the Persigo Board to provide policy direction for operation 
and maintenance of the system. The purpose of this item is to present the 2027 
Recommended Budget for the Persigo Wastewater System. 
  
BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
The Persigo Board meets on an annual basis to review the status of the overall system 
and the recommended budget for the Persigo Wastewater System for the forthcoming 
fiscal year. The purpose of this item is to present the 2027 Recommended Budget for 
the Persigo Wastewater System.

Recommended 2027 Budget information is included in this presentation.

September 17, 2026 
To the Honorable Members of the Joint Persigo Board, 
It is our pleasure to present the Recommended Persigo Budget for 2027. The 2027 budget totals 
$69.9 million, of which approximately $49.7 million is associated with capital projects. The 
proposed budget represents the allocation of resources necessary to operate and maintain the 
regional wastewater system while continuing significant investment in aging infrastructure, 
system reliability, and future treatment needs. 
The 2027 Recommende d Budget is informed by the 2025 Wastewater Cost of Service, Plant 
Investment Fee, and Rate Study, the 2020 Comprehensive Wastewater Basin and Wastewater 
Treatment Facilities Master Plan, and subsequent updates to the 10 -Year Capital Improvement 
Plan. These planning efforts provide the basis for the rates, capital investments, reserves, and 
debt financing necessary to maintain the long -term financial and operational stability of the 
Persigo system. The 2026 budget similarly relied upon these planning docume nts and the 
financial recommendations of the independent rate study.  
 
The combined funds for the Joint Persigo Sewer System are managed in two separate accounting 
funds: the operating fund and the sewer capacity fund. The operating fund supports operation 
and maintenance of the sewer collection and treatment system as well as planned infrastructure 
rehabilitation and replacement. The sewer capacity fund receives revenue from plant investment 
fees (PIFs) that are accumulated to fund eligible system capacity improvements. 
Fund Balance and Long -Range Financial Planning  - Persigo is currently in a period of 
significant capital investment. During 2026, over $20  million in planned capital expenditures 
were deferred or rescheduled into future years as project schedules and construction timing 
Packet Page 4
 
      
 
evolved. Associated debt financing for those projects has consequently been delayed.  At the 
same time, approximately $4.5 million in unplanned and accelerated infrastructure needs were 
identified during the year. As a result, the projected 2026 ending fund balance and subsequent 
years are being updated to reflect anticipated actual expenditures rather than budgeted 
appropriations. 
The 2027–2029 financial plan is also being refined to align the timing of Phase 2 and other major 
capital projects with available reserves, Plant Investment Fee revenues, and planned debt 
financing. Based on the current long -range financial model, Persigo is expected to draw upon 
accumulated reserves during this period of substantial capital investment  while maintaining cash 
reserves above established targets. The projected 2027 ending fund balance is $14.46 million, 
well above the reserve target of $3.9 million (30 percent of annual operations and maintenance 
costs). 
Revenue - Revenues reflect the proposed increase in the monthly wastewater service charge 
from $30.89 to $34.60 per EQU, consistent with the 12% annual adjustment identified in the 
2025 rate study. Industrial pretreatment and hauled -waste rates are also proposed to increase by 
12%. Plant In vestment Fee revenue is projected at $4.1 million, based on anticipated 
development activity and a modest increase related to construction inflation as recommended in 
the 2025 rate study . The 2027 budget also anticipates approximately $31.5 million in debt 
proceeds to support planned capital investment. 
Operations, Maintenance, and Reliability - The 2027 Recommended Budget includes 
approximately $15.1 million for labor, operating expenses, and internal support services 
necessary to operate and maintain the wastewater collection and treatment systems. Some 
operational efficiencies have been realized with the start-up of Phase 1 equipmen t. Significant 
operating expenditures include biosolids hauling and disposal ($650,000), chemicals ($730,000), 
and electricity ($700,000).  The budget also continues investments in asset management, 
preventive maintenance, regulatory compliance, and operational reliability. 
Infrastructure Rehabilitation and Replacement - The 2027 Recommended Budget includes 
approximately $17.2 million for rehabilitation and replacement of existing collection and 
treatment infrastructure. Major investments include $4.88 million for sewer line rehabilitation 
and replacement, $1.0 million for sewer improvement districts, $810,000 for wastewater 
treatment plant improvements and asset replacement, approximately $9.5 million for lift station 
elimination and rehabilitation, and $1.0 million for condition assessment of the Orchard Mesa 
River S yphon. The lift station program includes work associated with th e Lake Road, Grand 
Valley Byproducts, and El Poso Lift Stations. These investments address aging infrastructure, 
improve reliability and maintainability, and support continued operation of the regional 
collection system. 
Staff is also preparing for the next phase of master planning. The 2020 Master Plan identified 
limited collection system flow data as a constraint to accurately modeling the system. Capital 
funding is included for strategic property acquisition, with the 2027 budget specifically investing 
in expanded flow monitoring and sewer system modeling. This work will improve understanding 
of existing system capacity, identify hydraulic constraints and future infrastructure needs, and 
Packet Page 5
 
      
 
provide the technical foundation for the next phase of master planning focused on the Persigo 
collection system. 
Future Wastewater Treatment System Expansion - Construction of the Phase 1 Wastewater 
Treatment Plant Expansion  has reached substantial  completion, marking the culmination of a 
significant multi -year investment in Persigo's treatment capacity and reliability. The 2027 
Recommended Budget includes $20.3 million for Phase 2 and $12.0 million for construction of 
the new Administration and Maintenance Building , both of which will be multi -year projects. 
The new building will be completed in 2028 with additional budgeted funds in that calendar 
year, and Phase 2 is currently projected for completion in 2030, subject to final design and 
construction scheduling. 
Phase 2 is the next major treatment plant investment identifi ed in the 2020 Wastewater 
Treatment Facilities Master Plan and includes replacement of the anaerobic digesters  and 
improvements to the  renewable natural gas conversion infrastructure, grease receiving site, raw 
sewage pump station, and secondary clarification systems.  The 2027 budget supports continued 
design, early procurement, and construction of priority components, including work being 
advanced to address recently identified reliability concerns within the secondary clarification 
systems. The Administra tion and Maintenance Building was also identified through the 2020 
Master Plan as part of Phase 2 and is being delivered as a separate project. 
In summary, the 2027 Recommended Budget reflects the continuation of a period of substantial 
reinvestment in the Persigo Sewer System.  Staff continues to refine project schedules and the 
multi-year funding strategy to ensure that necessary improvements can be completed while 
maintaining appropriate reserves and long -term financial stability. The budget supports the City 
and County's joint goal of providing reliable wastewater service throughout the 201 Service Area 
in a sustainable and fiscally responsible manner.

2027 Recommended Joint Sewer Fund 
Capital Project Descriptions 
September 17th, 2026
1. Sewer Line Rehabilitation and Replacement Projects - $ 4,880,000 
This project provides for the design, rehabilitation, and replacement of sewer mains within the collection 
system. Annual condition assessments are used to prioritize projects, with a  combination of cured-in-place 
pipe (CIPP) lining and open-trench replacement used to maximize the investment. This project is funded by 
the Sewer enterprise fund. 
2. Orchard Mesa River Syphon - $1,000,000 
This project provides for condition assessment of the Orchard Mesa River Siphon, which serves as the primary 
means of conveying Orchard Mesa wastewater flows across the Colorado River. The redundant pipelines cross the 
river near the 5th Street bridge and require specialized cleaning and inspection to evaluate their condition and 
identify future rehabilitation needs. This project is funded by the Sewer enterprise fund. 
3. Property Acquisition - $250,000 
The next phase of Persigo master planning will focus on the collection system and the infrastructure necessary to 
support long-term system needs. Lift station elimination and rehabilitation, interceptor capacity improvements, 
and future development require advance planning and may require acquisition of real property, easements, and 
rights-of-way. This funding will allow the utility to strategically secure property interests needed to preserve 
options for future collection system improvements. This project is funded by the Sewer enterprise fund. 
4. Sewer Improvement Districts - $ 1,000,000 
In 2000, the City and County established the Septic System Elimination Program to incentivize connection of 
properties served by septic systems  to sanitary sewer. Approximately 1,550 properties remain on septic 
systems within the Persigo 201 service area. In 2026, the Persigo Board increased the Joint Sewer Fund cost 
share from 30% to 50%. At least two districts have since progressed toward formation, and staff will continue 
pursuing additional districts. This project is funded by the Sewer enterprise fund. 
5. Wastewater Treatment Plant Improvements and Asset Replacement - $ 810,000 
This annual program provides for improvements and replacement of aging infrastructure and equipment at the 
Persigo Wastewater Treatment Plant to maintain reliable treatment operations.  This project is funded by the 
Sewer enterprise fund. 
6. Lift Station Elimination and Rehabilitation- $ 9,492,795 
Construction and replacement of the Lake Road, Grand Valley Byproducts, and El Poso Lift Stations are planned 
to commence in 2027. Lake Road will replace the aging Ridges 1 and Brachs Market Lift Stations; Grand Valley 
Byproducts will be relocated outside the floodplain to a recently acquired site with improved maintenance 
access; and El Poso will be rehabilitated with submersible pumps and infrastructure better suited to the site. This 
project is funded by the Sewer enterprise fund. 
7. Phase 2 Wastewater Treatment Plant Expansion - $ 20,300,000 
Phase 2 is the next major investment identified in the 2020 Wastewater Treatment Facilities Master Plan

following completion of Phase 1. Improvements include replacement of the anaerobic digesters and 
improvements to the raw sewage pump station and secondary clarification system. The 2027 budget 
supports continued design, early procurement, and construction of priority components, including work 
being advanced to address recently identified reliability concerns within the secondary clarification systems. 
This project is funded by debt proceeds in the Sewer enterprise fund. 
8. Wastewater Treatment Administrative Building - $ 12,000,000 
The Administration and Maintenance Building was identified through the 2020 Wastewater Treatment 
Facilities Master Plan as part of Phase 2 and is being delivered as a separate project. A CM/GC was selected 
in 2026 and design has progressed to approximately 60%. Construction is anticipated to begin in the second 
quarter of 2027. Additional capital is budgeted for completion of the building in 2028. This project is funded 
by debt proceeds in the Sewer enterprise fund.
