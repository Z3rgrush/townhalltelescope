New Business 
 
Sara Kinkade; GJ Water Conservation Specialist 
Sara Kinkade, the City's new Water Conservation Specialist, introduced herself to the Board. Board 
members also introduced themselves. 
Sara discussed challenges with the City's turf conversion program, noting that many completed projects 
have raised concerns about maintaining the health of existing trees as irrigated turf is removed. Most 
projects are located in the downtown area making it difficult to balance water conservation goals with 
preserving mature trees. Ongoing discussions with City staff have focused on identifying ways to 
maintain tree health while continuing to promote turf conversions. 
Packet Page 3
 
Sara noted she is exploring a variety of water conservation strategies beyond landscaping, including 
consulting with staff at the water treatment plants, and is seeking as many ideas as possible to reduce 
water use.  
Sara Kinkade asked about the potential damage sod cutting can cause to tree roots during turf 
conversion projects and whether there are alternative removal methods. Members noted that removing 
turf with sod cutters can damage tree roots, and alternatives such as cutting turf low, covering it, and 
using mulch were discussed. 
Board members emphasized the importance of providing irrigation across the full tree root zone rather 
than only near the trunk, as mature tree roots often extend well beyond the canopy. Proper mulch, 
avoiding weed fabric near trees, and using appropriate irrigation methods were identified as key factors 
in maintaining tree health. 
The discussion also highlighted that water conservation does not always require removing all turf. 
Alternatives such as native grasses, drought-tolerant groundcovers, and improved watering practices 
can reduce water use while preserving trees and creating more diverse landscapes. 
The Board discussed the value of evaluating water savings from turf conversion projects by comparing 
changes in landscape type and water use. Staff noted that some previous conversions have not resulted 
in significant water reductions but have improved landscape health and maintenance needs. The 
discussion highlighted the importance of understanding the overall impact of conservation efforts and 
continuing to evaluate best practices for sustainable landscapes. 
The Board discussed concerns about property owners discontinuing irrigation after turf conversions and 
the potential impact on mature trees. Staff noted that City code requires adjacent property owners to 
maintain watering of trees within the public right-of-way and suggested using conservation programs 
and available funding to help residents maintain tree health. 
Anna expressed interest in developing recommendations for Council that strengthen protections for City 
trees, including potential updates to ordinances and enforcement practices. Board members 
emphasized the need for a balance between water conservation and preserving the community’s urban 
tree canopy through education, outreach, and consistent enforcement. They also noted the importance 
of coordinating outreach so residents understand both their responsibility to maintain trees and 
available resources to help with irrigation improvements.

Volunteer Program; Tanya and Dan 
Dan informed the Board that he had been speaking with Tanya about developing a volunteer program to 
support public outreach and forestry-related efforts. Potential volunteer activities could include assisting 
with Arbor Fest, seedling organization and delivery, IRA grant efforts, data collection and inventory 
updates, tree-watering issues, school presentations, neighborhood canvassing, flyer distribution, public 
education, and pruning. 
Packet Page 7
Dan outlined several steps to establish a volunteer program: 
• The Board would need to define the program’s mission and goals. 
• Volunteer roles and tasks would need to be identified. 
• Position descriptions would need to be developed outlining responsibilities, required skills, and 
time commitments. 
• Volunteers would then need to be recruited and provided with appropriate training. 
 
Dan asked the Board for feedback on whether they felt a volunteer program would be useful and 
whether there would be enough variety in tasks to keep volunteers interested and engaged. 
The Board discussed how volunteer forestry programs in Fort Collins and Denver are structured and 
shared contacts who may be able to provide additional information and guidance. 
The Board also discussed the feasibility of developing a local program. Members noted that tasks such as 
inventory inspection updates, flyer distribution, and public education would be well suited for 
volunteers. Pruning was identified as a more challenging activity due to the level of training and 
oversight that would be required. 
The Board discussed whether the benefits of adding volunteers would outweigh the additional time and 
effort required to recruit, train, and manage the volunteer program. 
 
Forestry Updates  
Bylaw Update 
Jamie Beard discussed the proposed bylaws updates with the Board. 
Jamie then informed the Board that the hearing regarding proposed changes to the significant tree 
section of the City Code is scheduled for September 2. The proposed change would remove the 
“preservation of significant trees” section from the City Code, which has been noted as a potential factor 
increasing costs for affordable housing projects. 
The Board discussed the effectiveness of the current code, noting concerns that trees are being removed 
before development applications are submitted. Members discussed the importance of identifying and 
considering significant trees early in the development process and emphasized the need for Forestry and 
Planning to work together. A general meeting or pre-application meeting may provide an appropriate 
opportunity to identify trees that should be considered for preservation at the beginning of a project. 
A board member asked whether a letter from the Board to City Council would be beneficial in 
communicating that, while the current significant tree provisions may not be working effectively, the 
intent behind them is important and the language should be updated rather than removed entirely. 
Jamie Beard suggested that the Board consider developing an updated version of the significant tree 
provisions and presenting it to City Council. 
 
Water Conservation and Tree Health  
Rob informed the Board that he compiled a list of trees that are currently struggling and plans to send 
flyers to affected homeowners reminding them of their responsibility to water ROW trees. The Board 
discussed a more formal approach involving official warning letters and potential fines if watering issues 
are not addressed. A two-step approach was suggested, beginning with an educational reminder and 
followed by a formal warning if there is no improvement.

The Board also discussed the importance of educating homeowners about the importance of watering 
trees during the drought and the long-term benefits trees provide. The Board also highlighted the 
importance of educating homeowners about the need to continue watering trees during the winter 
months.

New Business 
Discussion and development of Forestry Board recommendations regarding the potential elimination 
of the Significant Trees section of the Grand Junction Development Code 
Rob Davis explained that he used AI to assist in drafting a letter to City Council with recommendations 
regarding the potential elimination of the Significant Trees section of the Grand Junction Development 
Code. The prompts incorporated comments from Board members as well as relevant information from 
City strategic documents, including the 2021 Comprehensive Plan, Parks and Recreation Open Space 
Master Plan, and Forestry Management Plan. 
Board members then started discussing recommendations for city council and talked about guiding 
documents adopted by the City and their emphasis on the importance of trees and the need to protect 
the community’s tree canopy. Strong public support for protecting trees within the community was also 
noted. 
A board member asked whether data exists demonstrating the financial impact of significant trees on 
affordable housing and development. Anna Stout stated that she was not aware of data showing that 
significant trees have had a measurable impact on construction costs or affordable housing. She noted 
that the proposed elimination of the ordinance appears to be primarily focused on deregulation. 
 
 
 
Packet Page 10
 
 
Anna Stout suggested that the Board consider proposing a middle-ground approach rather than simply 
opposing elimination of the Significant Tree Ordinance. She also suggested requesting that the matter 
be postponed and scheduled for discussion at a City Council Workshop before being considered on a 
regular Council agenda. A workshop would provide an opportunity for stakeholders and subject-matter 
experts, including the Forestry Board, to provide input and answer questions from Council members. 
Anna Stout noted that this approach would also be consistent with the City’s strategic priority of 
transparency. 
The numerous benefits provided by trees, including soil-moisture retention, improved physical and 
mental well-being, reduction of urban temperatures, and other environmental and community benefits 
were discussed and why these benefits support maintaining protections for significant trees. 
The Board expressed the belief that the Significant Tree Ordinance can be improved to better 
accommodate affordable housing and development while continuing to protect significant trees. The 
Board’s recommendation is to ask City Council to retain the Significant Tree Ordinance while its 
effectiveness is evaluated, additional data is collected, and potential alternatives or improvements are 
considered. 
 
Drafting of a letter with Forestry Board recommendations to be included in the City Council Packet for 
the 9/2/2026 meeting
