Bylaws 
Ken Sherbenou reported that the City Attorney’s Office has been working with the City Clerk to 
update the bylaws to ensure they are current. He noted that a change to the City ordinance 
regarding term limits requires the bylaws to be updated to align with the City Code. 
Ken Sherbenou reviewed the proposed bylaw amendments with the Board.  
 
Packet Page 3
The first amendment, under Article VI – Members, Section 1, changes the City Council 
representative to a voting member of the Board. 
The second amendment, under Article VI – Members, Section 2, updates the term limit 
provision to state that members may serve up to four terms in total. 
The third amendment to Article XI – Committees, Sections 4 and 5, adds the phrase "working 
with City staff" to the requirement that the Committee Chair provide written reports of 
committee meeting proceedings to all Board members. 
The final amendment updates the latest amendment date to July 9, 2026. 
 
Kyle Gardner made a motion to approve the proposed bylaws as presented. Lisa Whalin 
seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously. 
 
Yes –6   No - 0 
 
Botanical Gardens Greenhouse 
Ken Sherbenou said that the expansion of the botanical garden campus has been part of the long-
term vision for some time. 
The botanical gardens are owned by the City and operated by Strive. The first phase of the 
campus expansion is the construction of a new greenhouse, which will be a significant 
improvement over the current facility. The existing greenhouse is located on the far east side of 
town off Highway 50, approximately 20 minutes from the Parks Operations office. Because the 
current greenhouse is rented and costly to operate, relocating it closer to the Parks Operations 
office will reduce travel time and provide long-term cost savings. 
Funding has been secured for the new greenhouse, and it has already been ordered. It will be 
constructed on the vacant lot between the botanical gardens and WestCo Brewing. Since 
additional greenhouses and other amenities are envisioned for the future, the site is being 
planned to ensure the greenhouse is positioned to accommodate future expansion. Plans also 
include installing fencing and security cameras to protect the facility and deter vandalism. The 
greenhouse will be accessible only to staff and will be used to grow flowers during the winter for 
spring planting. 
The possibility of adding a community garden in the future was also discussed. Nancy noted that 
the library gardens have experienced theft and vandalism at night, which could present similar 
challenges for a community garden at the botanic gardens. Ken emphasized that no decisions 
have been made regarding a community garden and that, if one is developed, public access 
would likely be limited to designated hours. For now, the priority is ensuring the site is designed 
to support future growth and that the greenhouse is located to allow for additional expansion. 
Future phases may include infrastructure improvements, a shelter, maintenance facilities, and 
food production opportunities such as a farm-to-table program. 
 
CRC Update 
Ken reported that staff are close to selecting a grand opening date for the Community Recreation 
Center (CRC). The goal is to announce the date in the Fall Recreation Guide, which will be 
available to residents in mid-August. 
Packet Page 4
The guide will also outline the transition of staff from the Lincoln Park Recreation Office to the 
CRC. RSRs are expected to transition to the CRC approximately six weeks before the grand 
opening, at which time membership sales will move from the on-site trailer into the new facility. 
Tours of the CRC are also planned to help promote memberships. Membership sales continue to 
be strong, with approximately 1,400 memberships sold to date. Ken Sherbenou noted that 
participation through the SilverSneakers program has been especially strong, and some 
individuals have even changed insurance providers to take advantage of the benefit. 
A board member asked about plans to address concerns raised by private gym owners following 
a recent Daily Sentinel article regarding the CRC's opening. Ken felt the article presented the 
issue fairly and acknowledged that while there will be some overlap between the CRC and 
private fitness facilities, extensive discussions have been held with private business owners 
throughout the planning process. He explained that compromises were made regarding the 
services and amenities offered at the CRC to minimize direct competition with private gyms. 
While some short-term impacts are expected, Ken believes the CRC and private fitness 
businesses will complement one another over the long term. Studies indicate that the long-term 
impact on private gyms should be minimal, and he emphasized the importance of communicating 
that these concerns have been carefully considered. 
Anna Stout noted that City Council is sensitive to public comments and the concerns of private 
businesses. She asked whether additional steps could be taken to proactively address the issue 
and avoid future concerns. Ken Sherbenou asked the Board for ideas beyond promotional 
materials that could help support local fitness businesses. A board member suggested that 
individuals who begin exercising at the CRC may eventually transition to private gyms as their 
fitness goals evolve. Anna Stout proposed partnering with private gyms by offering promotions, 
such as a free trial membership for CRC members, to capitalize on increased community interest 
in fitness. 
Ken Sherbenou reported that construction of the CRC continues to progress very well. The 
recent punch walk for the dry side of the building went smoothly, and the facility looked 
excellent. The pools are currently being filled. 
Staffing efforts are also progressing well. Ali Little has been hiring guest service representatives, 
lifeguards, fitness instructors, climbing wall staff, and other recreation personnel. Approximately 
90 employees have been onboarded so far, with a goal of hiring 150 to 175 staff members. 
Lifeguards are currently completing their required training. 
Bill Findlay asked whether any project funds might remain after construction is complete. Ken 
responded that several alternates, including slide timers and additional fitness equipment, have 
already been added to the project. However, some desired items, such as shade structures and 
signage, remain unfunded. 
Ken stated that PRAB will be notified as soon as the grand opening date has been finalized. 
 
Magpie Island Damage Update  
Ken Sherbenou reviewed the history of fire mitigation efforts on Magpie Island. He explained 
that, through grants administered by the Bureau of Land Management (BLM), the Fire 
Department previously completed vegetation management work that opened up large areas of the 
island by removing primarily tamarisk. In subsequent years, goats were brought in to help 
prevent regrowth. 
Packet Page 5
Nancy Strippel asked whether goats would be used again this year by the parks department. Ken 
Sherbenou replied that grazing would occur at only one location because the dry conditions have 
resulted in very little vegetation growth. He added that reducing the grazing program this year 
will also help meet the current budget. 
Ken Sherbenou reported that, based on the Parks staff's initial assessment of the recent fire, the 
burned area reached the sections where vegetation had previously been cleared but did not spread 
significantly beyond them. While these observations have not yet been reviewed with the Fire 
Department, they suggest that the previous fire mitigation work was effective in slowing the 
fire's spread. 
Ken Sherbenou also discussed ongoing conversations with RiversEdge West about pursuing 
grants through Great Outdoors Colorado (GOCO) and the Gates Family Foundation to support 
additional fire mitigation and habitat restoration efforts. Potential grant funding would be used to 
continue removing tamarisk and reestablish native vegetation, including cottonwood trees. 
Future plans for Magpie Island were also discussed. Ken said there has been interest in pursuing 
passive recreational improvements, such as soft-surface walking and biking trails, while 
preserving the island's natural character.
