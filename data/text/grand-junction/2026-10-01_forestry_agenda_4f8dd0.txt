New Business 
 
Review of significant tree status 
Rob Davis notified the board that City Council voted at the previous night’s meeting to remove the 
Significant Tree Ordinance from the Development Code. The Board discussed the Council’s decision and 
potential next steps. 
Kamie Long reported that no supporting evidence was provided to support claims that removing the 
Significant Tree Ordinance would reduce costs associated with affordable housing and expressed 
Packet Page 3
   
 
   
 
disappointment about City Council deciding not to move the topic to a Council workshop for further 
discussion. She also noted that there had been strong public support for retaining the ordinance and 
that City staff had also supported keeping it in place.  
There is currently no plan to immediately replace the Significant Tree Ordinance. However, a consultant 
is expected to review the City’s Development Code from September through December and identify 
areas for potential revision. A replacement or alternative to the Significant Tree Ordinance is expected 
to be considered as part of that process, with results anticipated to be presented to City Council during 
the first quarter of the following year. 
A Board member noted that an incentive-based approach, rather than the previous punitive measures, 
was discussed during the Council meeting. The Board discussed ways to move forward with an approach 
that could more successfully encourage tree preservation. It was also pointed out that the removal of 
the Significant Tree Ordinance does not align with the City’s Comprehensive Plan, which supports the 
retention of established trees. 
Rob discussed the challenges associated with protecting trees on private property, noting that trees can 
be removed before a development application is submitted if protections exist only within the 
Development Code. He suggested that the Board consider focusing its efforts on the protection and 
preservation of trees on public property. A board member added that consideration should also be given 
to publicly owned trees located on private property. 
Rob noted that the Board has been working on updates to Chapter 8.32 regarding public trees, street 
trees, and park trees and suggested that this may be an appropriate time to focus the Board’s efforts on 
those updates. 
 
 
Volunteer program

provide an update at the next meeting. 
 
IRA Irrigation Grant 
Rob provided an update on irrigation installations and showed the Board examples of current projects. 
He explained the scoring system used to prioritize properties for participation in the program. The 14th 
to 17th blocks of Main Street are a focus of the program and properties located in that area are given 
priority. Properties with existing backflow devices receive a higher score because installation costs are 
significantly lower. Existing canopy cover and changes in canopy over time are also considered when 
determining which properties to prioritize. Rob noted that lower-scoring properties may still move 
forward when no other interested properties are available. 
A Board member shared that a homeowner who participated in the program the previous year had 
questions about operating the backflow device. The Board discussed the importance of following up 
with participating homeowners and providing instructions on tree care as well as operation and 
winterization of backflow devices. 
The Board discussed applying for additional funding through the State Forest Service. Members 
expressed concern about the possibility of property owners not continuing to water or care for trees

after installation, potentially resulting in a loss of the program’s investment. Rob acknowledged that 
there is some risk regarding long-term tree care but stated that the property owners currently 
participating in the program have been enthusiastic about having irrigation and trees installed and are 
motivated to keep trees watered. 
The Board discussed seeking approximately $50,000 to $70,000 in supplemental funding for the existing 
program. 
 
Motion 
Tanya Travis made a motion to apply for the IRA grant again and request an additional $50,000 to 
$70,000 in funding. Dan Komlo seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. 
    Yes – 5     No - 0 
 
Rob suggested gathering cost data from properties where irrigation has already been installed to 
determine the percentage increase in water costs associated with the program. Sara stated that she 
could obtain information regarding changes in overall water consumption for participating properties. 
 
Water Conservation and Tree Health 
Rob discussed outreach efforts involving distributing flyers to residences with visibly stressed trees. He 
noted that notices have not been sent to rental property owners and asked whether it would be 
worthwhile to pursue additional outreach this year given the recent rainfall. He also discussed 
developing a cyclical outreach program to provide ongoing education and reminders related to tree 
health and watering and noted it might be easier to start the program in the spring. 
Sara reported that she is working with the Communications Department on a winter watering campaign 
and would like to partner with Forestry on outreach efforts. 
 
2026 Licensed Contractor Day of Service 
Rob informed the Board that the 2026 Licensed Contractor Day of Service is scheduled for September 22 
and will take place in Sherwood Park, as it did the previous year. 
The Board discussed setting up an informational forestry table at the event but decided not to pursue it 
due to limited interest the previous year.
