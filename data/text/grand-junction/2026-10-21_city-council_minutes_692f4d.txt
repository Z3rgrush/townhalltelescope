6.a.i. An Ordinance for Supplemental Appropriation  
The budget is adopted by City Council through an appropriation ordinance to authorize 
spending at a fund level based on the line-item budget. Supplemental appropriations 
are also adopted by ordinance and are required when the adopted budget is increased 
to re-appropriate funds for capital projects that begin in one year and need to be carried 
forward to the current year to complete. Supplemental appropriations are also required 
to approve new projects or expenditures.  
 
This supplemental appropriation is for the authorization to spend grant funding and 
other outside revenues not anticipated in the original 2026 budget. It also includes 
spending authority for health insurance claims, as well as the reinvestment of funds into 
the self-insurance plan.  
Chief Financial Officer Jay Valentine gave a presentation on this supplemental 
appropriation and was available to answer questions from Council. 
Comments were heard from Councilmembers Beilfuss, Kennedy, Nguyen, and Van 
Dyke. 
The public hearing opened at 7:19 p.m. 
No comments were heard. 
The public hearing closed at 7:19 p.m. 
Councilmember Nguyen moved, and Councilmember Stout seconded, to adopt 
Ordinance No. 5344, an ordinance making supplemental appropriations to the 2026 
Budget of the City of Grand Junction, Colorado, for the year beginning January 1, 2026, 
and ending December 31, 2026, and on final passage and ordered final publication in 
pamphlet form. Motion carried by a unanimous roll call vote. 
6.a.ii.   An Ordinance Enacting a Temporary Moratorium on the Acceptance, 
Processing, and Approval of Applications for Data Centers Within 
the City of Grand Junction; Providing that the Moratorium Shall 
Terminate at the Earliest of the City's Adoption of Amendment(s) to 
Title 21 of the Grand Junction Municipal Code Addressing Data 
Centers or the Expiration of Three Hundred Sixty-Five (365) Days 
from the Effective Date of this Ordinance, Providing for Findings, 
Intent and Purpose; Providing for Definitions, Directing a Code 
Study; and Providing for Severability 
The City of Grand Junction received inquiries regarding Data Centers and the City's 
current approach, regulations, or planned initiatives related to the approval and 
management of data centers within city limits. Communities considering data-center 
development often evaluate whether their land-use regulations adequately address the 
facilities’ distinctive operational and infrastructure needs. A temporary moratorium is 
narrowly tailored, is temporary in nature, is necessary to protect the public health, safety 
and welfare, and is adopted in good faith for the purpose of completing needed planning 
and regulatory work. The moratorium will allow the City Manager, by and through the 
Community Development Department, in conjunction with the City Attorney and such 
other departments as the City Manager designates, to study the impacts of data 
centers, to evaluate appropriate zone districts, use classifications, thresholds and use-
specific standards, and to present recommended amendments to Title 21 to the City 
Council. The moratorium is limited in duration and terminates automatically upon the 
earlier of the City Council's adoption of such amendments or the expiration of 365 days. 
Interim Community Development Director Thomas Lloyd gave a presentation on this 
ordinance and was available to answer questions from Council. 
Comments were heard from Councilmembers Kennedy, Nguyen, Beilfuss, and Stout. 
The public hearing opened at 7:33 p.m. 
Comments were heard from Daria Emir, Eric Reckel, Kimmy Ward, Stephanie Hall and 
Jack Byron. 
The public hearing closed at 7:42 p.m. 
Councilmember Kennedy moved, and Councilmember Van Dyke seconded, to adopt 
Ordinance No. 5345, an ordinance enacting a temporary moratorium on the 
acceptance, processing, and approval of applications for data centers within the City of 
Grand Junction; providing that the moratorium shall terminate at the earliest of the City's 
Adoption of Amendment(s) to Title 21 of the Grand Junction Municipal Code addressing 
data centers or the expiration of three hundred sixty-five (365) days from the effective 
date of this ordinance, providing for findings, intent and purpose; providing for 
definitions, directing a code study; and providing for severability, on final passage and 
ordered final publication in pamphlet form.  Motion carried by a unanimous roll call vote.

7.a.    Authorize Contract Amendment with AE2S for the Persigo 
Wastewater Treatment Plant Phase 2 Expansion Project 
Recent failures of secondary clarification infrastructure at the Persigo Wastewater 
Treatment Plant (Persigo) have significantly reduced operational reliability and 
eliminated redundancy within this essential step of the treatment process. Although 
Secondary Clarifier #1 has been returned to service following emergency repairs, 
Secondary Clarifier #4 remains out of service, leaving Persigo dependent on Secondary 
Clarifiers #1 and #2 without a standby unit. 
 
The City's 2020 Wastewater Treatment Facilities Master Plan identified construction of 
Secondary Clarifier #3 as a future improvement. However, the original Phase 2 
Expansion Project anticipated rehabilitation of the three existing clarifiers rather than 
construction of an additional system. Recent mechanical failures and potential structural 
deficiencies at Clarifier #4 have raised concerns about whether rehabilitation remains a 
viable long-term solution. 
 
Staff recommended amending the existing AE2S engineering services contract in an 
amount not to exceed $950,000 to accelerate design and permitting of Clarifier #3 while 
assessing the condition of Clarifier #4. Construction of Clarifier #3 will be accelerated as 
part of the Phase 2 Expansion Project to restore operational redundancy and allow for 
the planned rehabilitation of Clarifiers #1 and #2 while maintaining treatment capacity. 
Enviro Comp Sustain Manager Ashley Firl gave a presentation on this contract 
amendment and was available to answer questions from Council. 
Councilmember Van Dyke moved, and Councilmember Nguyen seconded, to authorize 
the City Purchasing Division to execute a contract amendment with Advanced 
Engineering and Environmental Services, LLC (AE2S) in an amount not to exceed 
$950,000 for secondary clarifier assessment, design, and permitting services as part of 
the Persigo Wastewater Treatment Plant Phase 2 Expansion Project. Motion carried by 
a unanimous voice vote.
