Facility Naming Policy Resolution 
Ken Sherbenou informed the Board that he had incorporated suggested changes from the Legal 
Department and Josh into the resolution draft distributed prior to the meeting. He explained that, 
with the continued need to name new trails, a formal policy is necessary to establish a consistent 
framework for reviewing and processing naming requests. Ken reviewed the proposed revisions 
with the Board. 
The Board discussed several aspects of the policy, including obtaining written consent from 
relatives when naming trails after deceased individuals and establishing a timeline for the

naming process. 
A board member asked whether information about the naming process would be available on the 
City's website. Ken expressed concern that actively advertising the process could generate an 
overwhelming number of requests but noted that interested individuals would be directed to the 
appropriate process. 
The Board also discussed concerns about naming trails after living individuals, including 
whether stricter criteria should apply. Members noted the potential costs associated with 
renaming a trail should circumstances arise that warrant a name change. 
 
Motion 
Cindy Enos-Martinez made a motion to approve the Facility Naming Policy resolution with 
corrections. Nancy Strippel seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

Columbine Park Improvements 
Ken presented the original Columbine Park plan from the 2021 PROS Plan, noting that it has 
been refined over time. He explained that improvements have been completed incrementally 
based on available funding and staff resources, with Community Development Block Grant 
(CDBG) funding playing a significant role. 
Anna Stout explained that CDBG funding is provided by the federal government and allocated 
based on census data and income levels. In the past, the City distributed these funds among 
various nonprofits, which required considerable administrative effort and resulted in relatively 
small allocations. A few years ago, the City shifted its approach to focus funding on eligible City 
projects and infrastructure improvements, allowing for a greater overall impact. Funds must still 
be used in CDBG-eligible neighborhoods. 
Ken highlighted improvements completed at Columbine Park since 2021, including new 
dugouts, scoreboard updates, infield repairs, protective fencing and netting, additional shade 
trees, a restroom enclosure, a parking lot gate, volleyball court renovations, and field rolling to 
improve playability. 
Ken also reported that the Parks Department received a $500,000 Safe Streets grant to install 
pedestrian lighting at Columbine Park and Hawthorne Park to improve safety. 
Looking ahead to 2027, Ken outlined plans to convert the concrete slab beneath the existing 
shelter into a pickleball court and install two smaller, dispersed shelters near the pickleball and 
volleyball courts. 
During the discussion, a Board member suggested enclosing the dugouts to prevent spectators 
from approaching players. Another member asked about replacing the playground. Ken 
acknowledged that replacement is still needed and expressed hope that funding would become 
available in the near future. He also mentioned the possibility of adding more pickleball courts. 
 
Motion 
Bill Findlay made a motion to support the installation of two dispersed shelters and the 
conversion of the existing shelter area into a pickleball court at Columbine Park in 2027. Lisa 
Whalin seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

CRC Soft Openings and Grand Opening Review 
The Board discussed the successful opening of the CRC, noting the positive feedback received 
from the community. Emily Krause reported that nearly 5,900 people visited the facility during 
its first four days of operation. 
Ken recognized the Projects Team and Parks and Recreation staff for their efforts in completing 
the additional south overflow parking lot ahead of opening day. A split-rail fence was also 
installed around the lot to protect newly planted native seed. Ken noted that the CRC now has a 
total of 520 parking spaces, including 300 in the main lot, 76 in the north overflow lot, and 144 
in the south overflow lot. He also reported that approximately 4,000 people participated in 
facility tours over a four-month period. 
A board member asked about opportunities to tour the CRC. Emily explained that visitors are 
welcome to stop by throughout the day, with informational flyers available and staff-led tours 
offered when staffing allows. She also reported strong participation in fitness classes, with many 
reaching capacity. While personal training is not offered, group introductory fitness classes are 
available. Ken explained that the decision not to offer personal training was a compromise 
intended to differentiate the CRC from private gyms. 
A Board member asked about accessibility for low-income families. Ken explained that GJ Plays 
is developing a fundraising campaign to support scholarships. Bill added that GJ Plays is 
working with the City to establish a sliding-scale assistance program, with a focus on 20-visit 
punch cards. 
 
Parking Lot Maintenance Plan 
Ken informed the Board that several Parks parking lots are in poor condition and in need of 
repaving. The department has identified seven priority locations based on their condition. West 
Lake will be the first to receive improvements, with work expected to begin within the next 
couple of months. Other priority locations include Blue Heron, Duck Pond Open Space, Junior 
Service League, Pine Ridge, Eagle Rim, and the Botanic Gardens, which currently has an 
unpaved parking lot. 
Ken noted that $100,000 has been budgeted for parking lot improvements in 2027. The 
department will work with the Projects Team to complete as many of the priority locations as 
funding allows.
