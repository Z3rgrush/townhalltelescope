IRA Irrigation Grant 
Rob Davis informed the Board that 2 of the 13 properties scheduled for irrigation installation were 
removed from the program due to lead pipes in their private water lines, which contractors are unable 
to work on. 
He reviewed ongoing projects and discussed challenges with one homeowner regarding landscaping 
concerns that resulted in increased project costs. He also mentioned that they were able to add more 
properties along Main Street within the targeted area. 
 
Packet Page 3
 
Irrigation installations will wrap up soon, with any remaining funds carrying over into 2027. Rob also 
announced that the department applied for an additional $75,000 in funding for 2027 and 2028 which is 
very similar to the existing grant. 
Rob reviewed potential properties for future installations, explaining that selection criteria include 
location, available planting spaces, existing trees, canopy coverage, and canopy decline. He discussed 
targeting outreach to high-ranking properties to manage application volume. 
Rob asked the Board for suggestions on additional locations that would be good candidates for the 
program, particularly areas with low canopy coverage and opportunities for responsible land 
stewardship. 
 
2026 Licensed Contractor Day of Service 
Rob reported that six companies participated in the September 22 event: At Ease Tree Services, 
Asplundh, Bighorn, Miller’s, Mission Ready, and SavATree. The companies worked alongside City 
Forestry staff, CSU Extension, and the Colorado State Forest Service. 
A total of 23 large trees and 50 small trees were pruned. Rob estimated that contracting out the work on 
the large trees alone would have cost approximately $19,000. 
Overall, the event was a great success. 
 
Farmers Market Recap 
Board members reported steady participation throughout the season and great engagement with 
passersby. Having a Forestry staff member present was especially helpful. Rob estimated approximately 
80–100 contacts per event, totaling around 1,000 contacts for the season. 
Board members noted that questions varied throughout the season, with tree planting being a popular 
topic early on and tree maintenance becoming more common during the summer. Questions about 
controlling Tree of Heaven were also frequent. 
The Board discussed opportunities to expand public education on tree care, particularly regarding heat 
stress, fall overwatering, and the importance of winter watering. Suggestions included targeted mailers 
and online educational materials. Rob mentioned coordinating with Sara, who is already developing 
watering guidelines materials. 
Mollie also suggested using the CSU Extension radio show to share winter watering information. Rob 
agreed to meet with Sara and Mollie to discuss outreach materials and the radio opportunity. 
The Board also discussed coordinating the timing of City mailers to avoid overwhelming residents with 
information, potentially postponing some outreach efforts until early next year. 
 
Western Slope Urban Forestry Conference 
Mollie reported that the conference will take place on October 29th in Montrose at the event center. The 
event can be attended either virtually or in person. Topics include champion tree genetics, soil and 
street trees, trees for the apocalypse, aerial rescue and urban wood. 
 
Notable Tree Tours 
Rob shared that he and Kamie had discussed moving the Notable Tree Tour away from Arbor Day due to 
the challenges of coordinating multiple activities and asked the Board for feedback on offering it as a 
standalone event. 
Board members discussed several options, including hosting tours during Market on Main or offering 
separate guided walks at Lincoln Park or along Main Street. Anna Stout suggested advertising a few

tours in the Activity Guide to gauge public interest. Cameron noted that information would need to be 
finalized by mid-November to meet the publication deadline. 
Additional suggestions included scheduling a tour around Arbor Day with a seedling giveaway, charging a

The Board also discussed offering educational classes for children, potentially partnering with Eureka, as

Urban Wood Network Update 
The Board discussed the Urban Wood Utilization Network, which focuses on repurposing urban wood 
and connecting wood users with tree service companies and other potential partners. 
Rob explained that logs and mulch previously stored near the Parks Operations office have been 
relocated to a secured area near the cemetery, eliminating public access to the free mulch previously 
offered. With logs now being discarded, the department is looking for a more accessible location where 
residents could obtain free mulch and wood. 
Board members discussed the challenges of preserving usable wood during tree work, connecting 
suppliers with potential users, and distributing free wood to the public.  
Potential storage and distribution locations were also discussed, including the new Material Recovery 
Facility and the parking lot near the new greenhouse site at the Botanical Gardens.

Forestry Updates 
Developing Tree Maintenance Rotation Areas 
Rob presented a proposed seven-year pruning and inspection rotation aimed at creating a more 
proactive and strategic approach to tree maintenance rather than responding primarily to immediate 
needs. 
Using an AI model, the City was divided into seven areas, each containing approximately 2,000 trees, 
with a relatively even distribution of large, medium, and small trees. Rob explained that the goal is to 
inspect and, when needed, prune all trees within one area each year. 
Rob explained that he hopes to use a significant portion of the contract services budget (which is 
currently approximately $187,000) to hire contractors for pruning, with the City's two Forestry crews 
handling the remaining work. 
He also discussed potential challenges due to budget constraints, which could reduce contract funding in 
2027. Additionally, a recently vacated Forestry position may be frozen. Rob explained that most tree 
work requires two employees per crew, meaning the department may be limited to operating only one 
crew if the position remains unfilled. 
Mollie Freilicher asked for an update on the Forestry brochure. Rob reported that Welcome Home has 
begun distributing the brochures to new homeowners and additional copies are being printed to meet 
demand. 
Rob also complimented the Board's ongoing public education efforts through events such as Market on 
Main and Arbor Day.
