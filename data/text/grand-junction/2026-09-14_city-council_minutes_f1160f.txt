   
 
 
GRAND JUNCTION CITY COUNCIL WORKSHOP SUMMARY 
September 14, 2026

Room, 625 Ute Avenue, and live-streamed via GoTo Webinar.

Staff present: City Manager Mike Bennett, Interim City Attorney Jeremiah Boies, Deputy City

1. Discussion Topics  
 
a. Water Supply Element – Comprehensive Plan Amendment  
Key Points: 
• Staff presented the new Water Supply Element required by Colorado Senate Bill 24-
174. The element will be incorporated into the Comprehensive Plan and must be 
adopted by the Planning Commission by December 31, 2026. 
• The document draws from existing regional water-efficiency and drought-response 
plans and has also been aligned with the Colorado Water Plan. 
• Staff reported that current water supplies are sufficient for current and projected 
needs. Conservation efforts and declining water use per tap have improved the 
City's long-term supply outlook. 
• Council discussed the effects of population growth and demographics on demand, 
coordination among area water providers, drought and increasingly arid conditions, 
and benchmarking Grand Junction's water use against other communities. 
• Staff noted that water-supply projections incorporate drought and worst-case 
conditions and currently indicate supplies could extend to approximately 2049 
without significant infrastructure investments. 
• Staff will consult with local water providers and irrigation districts, incorporate 
feedback, and take the amendment to the Planning Commission. 
 
b. Temporary Moratorium on Data Centers 
Key Points: 
• Staff proposed bringing forward an ordinance establishing a temporary moratorium 
on large data centers while the City develops appropriate land-use regulations. 
   
 
 
• The City's current code does not specifically address data centers, creating 
uncertainty regarding how applications would be evaluated, particularly given 
potentially significant electricity and water demands. 
• Staff emphasized that no applications are currently pending or known to be 
forthcoming, although the City has received inquiries. 
• The proposed moratorium would last up to 365 days or until Council adopts 
applicable code amendments, whichever occurs first. 
• Council discussed coordinating with Mesa County, neighboring jurisdictions, water 
providers, and energy providers because a data center outside city limits could still 
have regional infrastructure impacts. 
• Council also raised whether the review should consider other high-water or high-
energy industrial uses, such as water bottling or chip manufacturing. 
• Staff will refine the draft moratorium ordinance and use the moratorium period to 
evaluate best practices and potential development standards. 
 
c. Local Preference for Procurements  
Key Points: 
• Staff reviewed options for giving local businesses an advantage in City procurement. 
• Staff identified examples of local-preference programs in Colorado but noted 
concerns including potentially higher costs, reduced competition, administrative 
complexity, reciprocal disadvantages for Grand Junction businesses bidding 
elsewhere, and conflicts with some state or federal grant requirements. 
• Councilmembers expressed differing perspectives. Some emphasized the economic 
benefit of keeping City contracts and employment local, particularly when competing 
against out-of-state firms; others were concerned that preference policies could 
discourage bidders and reduce competition. 
• The possibility of a broader Colorado-based preference rather than strictly local 
preference was raised, although legal staff noted that interstate-commerce and 
constitutional issues would need review. 
• There was substantial interest in gathering better information before considering a 
policy change. 
• Staff will begin tracking local versus non-local participation and contract awards, bid-
price differences, local subcontracting, and reasons local businesses may not bid. 
Staff will also continue outreach and education on how local businesses can 
compete for City contracts. 
 
2. Council Communication 
 
• Council discussed information from the Colorado Basin Roundtable concerning 
water conservation and statewide water issues. 
• Staff clarified that regular meetings among local domestic water-provider managers 
primarily address operational issues, regulations, supply, and short-term planning. 
   
 
 
• Council revisited drought-related water rates. Staff indicated proposed 2027 rates 
will address the rate structure and potential future drought-rate adjustments.  
• Council also discussed an opportunity to advocate for additional Mountain Rail 
passenger service to Grand Junction. A potential resolution would demonstrate City 
support without committing funding, and Council expressed interest in coordinating 
support with regional partners. A resolution demonstrating support will be placed on 
the September 16, 2026, regular agenda. 
 
3. Next Workshop Topics 
City Manager Bennett summarized list of next workshop topics. 
 
• The next three workshops will focus heavily on the City budget. 
• October workshops were confirmed for October 5 and October 26 at 4:00 p.m., 
rather than the usual first and third Monday schedule. 
• Staff is preparing a comprehensive review of boards and commissions, including 
voting structures, bylaws, ordinances, charter provisions, and the roles of Council 
and staff liaisons. 
• Council's norms of conduct are also expected to return for discussion following the 
budget process, with draft information anticipated beforehand. 
 
4. Other Business

Childhood Hunger.
