SUBJECT: 
  
Consider a request by Davenport and Company and Anthony Properties, LLC, for a 
Comprehensive Plan Amendment for approximately 14.88 acres from Residential

2913 D 1/2 Road, Grand Junction, CO 81504. The parcel of land abuts 2911 D 1/2 Rd

RECOMMENDATION: 
  
Staff recommends approval of the Comprehensive Plan Amendment request. 
  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: 
  
The applicants, Davenport and Company and Anthony Properties, LLC, are requesting 
a Comprehensive Plan Amendment for approximately 6.78 acres to change the Future 
Land Use designation of Tax ID #: 2943-173-00-094 from Residential Medium to Mixed-
Use. This amendment would establish a consistent Mixed-Use designation across both 
properties, aligning the Future Land Use designations to support the anticipated 
development pattern. 
 
The request is being processed in coordination with two additional applications to 
facilitate the potential development of a multi-unit residential apartment complex: 
Packet Page 3
1. Annexation: Annexation of the combined 14.88 acres into the City of Grand Junction. 
2. Zone of Annexation: Establishment of City MU-2 (Mixed-Use Light Commercial) 
zoning for the properties. 
 
A separate Circulation Plan Amendment is anticipated to be submitted and processed 
at a later date to address the relocation of planned future roadways identified in the 
2018 Grand Junction Circulation Plan to better accommodate the anticipated 
development pattern. 
 
The proposed Comprehensive Plan Amendment is being considered by the Planning 
Commission prior to the associated Zone of Annexation request. If the Planning 
Commission recommends approval of the Comprehensive Plan Amendment, the Zone 
of Annexation request will proceed for consideration. The requested amendment would 
establish a consistent Mixed-Use Future Land Use designation across the combined 
project area and provide the land-use foundation for the requested MU-2 zoning. 
 
 
  
BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
PROJECT DESCRIPTION & SURROUNDING AREA 
 
Existing Conditions 
The project site consists of approximately 14.88 acres of vacant land with no existing 
structures. The Mesa County Ditch crosses the western parcel, and the Odelberg Drain 
Ditch runs along the eastern property boundary adjacent to 29 ¼ Road. 
 
Infrastructure and Growth Tiers 
The subject properties are located within Tier 1 (Urban Infill) of the Comprehensive 
Plan’s Intensification and Growth Tiers Map. Urban infrastructure is available or can be 
extended to serve the site. Domestic water is provided by the Ute Water District, and 
sanitary sewer service is available through the Persigo system.  
 
Surrounding Zoning and Land Uses 
• North: Mesa County Residential Single-Family Rural (RSF-R) / Single-Family 
residential. 
• South: Mesa County Residential Single-Family Rural (RSF-R) / Single-Family 
residential. 
• East: City Residential Medium 8 (RM-8) / Developed as a Solar Farm. 
• West: Mesa County Planned Unit Development (PUD) / Single-Family Residential.  
 
Existing and Proposed Future Land Use Designations 
The current Future Land Use designations for the subject properties, as shown on the 
Future Land Use Map, are Mixed-Use for 2911 D ½ Road (Tax ID #: 2943-173-00-268) 
and Residential Medium for the adjacent eastern parcel (Tax ID #: 2943-173-00-094). 
The applicants are requesting to amend the Future Land Use designation of the eastern 
parcel from Residential Medium to Mixed-Use, resulting in a consistent Future Land 
Packet Page 4
Use designation across the combined 14.88-acre project area. This amendment would 
establish a unified land-use designation for the properties in support of the anticipated 
development pattern.   
  
COMPREHENSIVE PLAN ANALYSIS 
Review Criteria for Comprehensive Plan Amendments (Non-Administrative) are set 
forth in Section 21.02.050(e)(4)(iii) of the Zoning and Development Code. The Planning 
Commission and City Council shall review a Comprehensive Plan Amendment request 
in light of the following criteria: 
 
(A). The existing Comprehensive Plan and/or any related element thereof requires 
the proposed amendment. 
The proposed amendment would address the inconsistency in Future Land Use 
designations across the combined project area. The existing Residential Medium 
designation on Tax ID #: 2943-173-00-094 differs from the Mixed-Use designation 
assigned to the adjacent parcel at 2911 D ½ Road. Amending the eastern parcel to 
Mixed-Use would establish a consistent land-use framework for evaluating future 
development across the combined properties and better align the Future Land Use Map 
with the anticipated development pattern and requested MU-2 zoning. 
 
(B). The community or area will derive benefits from the proposed amendment. 
The proposed amendment would support coordinated development of the 
approximately 14.88-acre project area and provide opportunities for additional housing 
within an area designated for urban infill. Establishing a consistent Mixed-Use 
designation would support coordinated site planning, efficient use of available 
infrastructure, and opportunities for complementary commercial uses that could serve 
the surrounding neighborhood. 
 
(C). The amendment will be consistent with the vision, goals, principles, and 
policies of the Comprehensive Plan and the elements thereof. 
The proposed Comprehensive Plan Amendment is consistent with the Grand Junction 
Comprehensive Plan by encouraging infill development, promoting a mix of land uses 
and housing opportunities, and supporting the efficient use of existing and planned 
infrastructure. The change from Residential Medium to Mixed-Use would establish a 
consistent future land-use designation across both properties and provide flexibility for 
future development, including multifamily residential and complementary commercial 
uses. Located within the Tier 1 Urban Infill area, the amendment supports the City's 
goals for coordinated and sustainable growth. A separate Circulation Plan Amendment 
is anticipated at a later date to address future transportation access and connectivity 
needs. This amendment establishes a future land-use framework and does not approve 
a specific development proposal.

A) Consistency. The proposed zoning is generally consistent with applicable

• Growth and Infill Goals: The properties are located within the Tier 1 Urban Infill 
area and are adjacent to existing City development. The area is served by 
existing urban infrastructure, including Ute Water and the Persigo sanitary sewer 
system. Establishing City zoning would allow future development to occur under 
the City's development standards and support efficient growth within the 
established urban area. 
• Housing Choices (Plan Principle 5): The MU-2 district allows multi-unit 
residential development and provides opportunities to expand the City's housing 
options. Although the applicant has submitted a conceptual plan for a 288-unit 
apartment complex, approval of the Zone of Annexation establishes the zoning 
regulations applicable to the property and does not constitute approval of a 
specific development plan. Future development will remain subject to applicable 
subdivision, site plan, utility, and other development review requirements. 
 
Supported Comprehensive Plan Principles, Goals, and Policies 
• Policy 3.2 (Encourage Infill): 
Directs growth to vacant parcels with direct access to existing transportation and utility 
networks. 
• Policy 3.6 (Mixed-Use Development): 
Establishes a flexible development framework that allows neighborhood-scale 
commercial and multi-unit residential uses to blend over time. 
Packet Page 26
• Policy 5.1 & 5.3 (Housing Options & Infrastructure Investment): 
Increases multi-unit housing capacity within the urban core where city services are 
already maintained, avoiding the costly expansion of infrastructure into undeveloped 
areas. 
• Policy 5.4 & Principle 6 (Transportation Integration & Connectivity): 
Concentrates density along existing Major Collector and Principal Arterial roads, 
optimizing the efficiency of existing multimodal networks and fostering walkable 
neighborhood patterns.  
 
B) Development Patterns. The proposed zoning will result in logical and orderly 
development pattern(s).      
 
The surrounding area consists primarily of single-family residential development, with 
City RM-8 zoning and an existing solar farm to the east. The proposed MU-2 zoning 
would establish a framework for mixed-use development, including multi-unit residential 
and complementary commercial uses, within an area identified for urban infill. The 
proposed zoning provides opportunities for a range of development types while allowing 
future development to be evaluated through the City's applicable site design, access, 
and infrastructure requirements. This supports a logical and orderly development 
pattern within the surrounding area. 
 
C) Benefits. The community or area, as decided by the reviewing body, derives an 
overall benefit(s) from the proposed zoning.        
 
The community would benefit from bringing the vacant properties into the City and 
establishing City zoning that supports coordinated urban development within a Tier 1 
Urban Infill area. The proposed MU-2 zoning provides opportunities for additional 
housing and complementary commercial uses while requiring future development to 
comply with applicable City zoning, site design, utility, and access requirements. The 
properties are located in an area where urban infrastructure and services are available 
or can be provided, supporting the efficient use of existing and planned infrastructure.

BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
BACKGROUND 
WinCo Foods LLC requests an amendment to the Zoning and Development Code to 
remove two parcels totaling approximately 39.3 acres from the area depicted at Figure 
04.04-1 “Prohibited Restaurant and Retail Drive-Through Areas.” The effect would be to 
allow drive-throughs associated with restaurant and retail sales uses on the two parcels 
comprising the applicant’s properties presently within the prohibition area (“affected 
property”). 
 
The prohibition on restaurant and retail drive-throughs has evolved across multiple 
Packet Page 51
codes and plans. 
 
The affected property was a portion of the Neighbors Annexation of 1985 (Ordinance 
No. 2239). Upon annexation, it was zoned Planned Recreation (PREC, Ordinance No. 
2240).  
 
Early conceptual planning for 24 Road development and infrastructure was 
incorporated into the City’s first Growth Plan in 1996 (Resolution No. 91-96). Action 
Items 38 and 39 in the Growth Plan called for “corridor development regulations […] 
addressing appropriate uses and development design” and for 24 Road to be 
constructed “as an urban parkway with a trail system.”  
 
An updated Zoning and Development Code was adopted March 7, 2000 by Ordinance 
No. 3240 (“2000 Code”). With this adoption, Planned Development zone districts 
formerly identified by their type and addressed within prior codes, including the affected 
property’s PREC zone district, were collapsed into a single Planned Development zone 
district. 
 
During and after the 2000 Code adoption process, the City was undertaking a study of 
the greater 24 Road area, which culminated in the adoption of the 24 Road Corridor 
Subarea Plan in November 2000 (Resolution No. 109-00). It introduced the “Preferred 
Plan” for the 24 Road Corridor, which included the assignment of subarea-specific land 
use categories to different parts of the subarea. The Commercial designation was 
assigned to a “node in the north east corner of the I-70 interchange, as well as 
expansion of commercial uses along U.S. Highway 6/50.” The affected property of this 
proposed amendment, on the other hand, was assigned the Mixed Use Development 
designation, which applied to approximately 41.6% of the subarea, and which limits the 
extent of retail uses: 
 
Mixed use development is encouraged in the remaining areas to include 
employment, residential and open space. Retail commercial may be appropriate as a 
secondary use, integral to other uses and structures or as a small (eight to 10 acres) 
nodal development at 24 Road and G Road intersection. 
 
The Mixed Use (M-U) zone district was also introduced in November (Ordinance No. 
3303) explicitly for the purpose of implementing 24 Road Corridor Subarea Plan 
recommendations, and it was applied to properties including the affected property by 
Ordinance No. 3304. 
 
The M-U zone district in the 2000 Code (as amended) provided for “a mix of light 
manufacturing and office park centers, limited retail, service and multifamily residential 
uses,” where those limitations on retail included a maximum floor-area ratio (0.5), a 
maximum building size for retail (30,000 square feet), and a minimum of 20% of parcels 
or projects exceeding 5 acres developed as residential. Restaurant and retail drive-
throughs were not allowed in the M-U district, and office drive-through uses required a 
conditional use permit. 
Packet Page 52
 
Ordinance No. 3305, also adopted in November 2000, established the 24 Road 
Corridor Design Standards and Guidelines. Among other requirements, “Auto-Oriented 
Uses” were addressed. Following minor revisions in 2016, those standards provide that 
“drive-up and drive-through facilities (order stations, pick-up windows, bank teller 
windows, money machines, car drop-off areas for auto service or rental, etc.) shall be 
located on the side or rear of a building and away from residential uses,” with a limited 
exception for buildings greater than 100 feet from the street when an “adequate 
landscaping buffer from the right-of-way line” is provided. 
 
February and April 2010 saw a new Comprehensive Plan (Ordinance No. 4406, 
replacing the 1996 Growth Plan) and a new Zoning and Development Code (Ordinance 
No. 4419, “2010 Code”) adopted. The 2010 Comprehensive Plan assigned new land 
use designations across much of the City, which included splitting the affected property 
into the Commercial Industrial and Village Center – Mixed Use (7 DU/acre) 
designations. At the time of the 2010 Code’s adoption, the M-U zone district was an 
implementing district for the Commercial Industrial land use designation, but it was not 
an implementing district for the VC-MU designation. 
 
The 2010 Code retained the two principal uses, “Drive-Through Uses (Restaurants)” 
and “Drive-Through Uses (Retail),” which were not allowed in the Mixed Use (M-U) 
zone district (among other districts). “Office with Drive-Through,” however, was allowed 
in M-U. Under the 2010 Code, the affected property remained zoned Mixed Use (M-U); 
therefore, drive-through restaurants and retail were not allowed on the affected 
property.  
 
The One Grand Junction Comprehensive Plan (“2020 Comprehensive Plan”) replaced 
the 2010 Comprehensive Plan upon adoption on December 16, 2020. The 2020 
Comprehensive Plan again assigned new land use designations across the City. The 
Commercial designation supplanted the previously split designations for the entire 
affected property. The Commercial designation is characterized by primary uses 
including “commercial, offices, retail, employment, medical campus/hospitals, and 
service-oriented uses.” 
 
Following the adoption of the 2023 Zoning and Development Code, as described below, 
and a corresponding Comprehensive Plan amendment to update its Implementing Zone 
Districts, the Commercial designation is implemented by Mixed-Use Light Commercial 
(the present zone district of the affected property), Commercial General, Public, Civic, 
and Institutional Campus, and Planned Development. 
 
The 2020 Comprehensive Plan also incorporated existing neighborhood and subarea 
plans, including, for example, those previously adopted for Pear Park and Orchard 
Mesa. Notably, as will be discussed further under the analysis below, the 24 Road 
Corridor Subarea Plan was not similarly incorporated and was instead explicitly sunset 
by the adoption of the 2020 Comprehensive Plan in favor of the latter’s treatment of the 
subarea. 
Packet Page 53
 
Ordinance No. 5190, adopted December 20, 2023, repealed the 2010 Code and 
adopted its replacement (“2023 Code”). The 2023 Code introduced a new 
organizational structure and new content across most sections. The 2023 Code also 
introduced new zone districts, which resulted in the consolidation of several zone 
districts established under the 2010 Code into fewer zone districts under the 2023 
Code. The 2023 Code became effective on January 21, 2024. The Official Zoning Map 
was updated on the same date from the 2010 Code’s zone districts to the 2023 Code’s 
zone districts by Ordinance No. 5191. 
 
The 2010 Code’s Mixed Use (M-U), Light Commercial (C-1), Business Park Mixed-Use 
(BP), and some among that code’s form-based districts were consolidated into the 2023 
Code’s Mixed-Use Light Commercial (MU-2) zone district. At the time of the 2023 
Code’s adoption, there were 1,114.22 acres of land zoned C-1 (71.6%), 366.26 acres 
zoned M-U (23.5%), and 75.76 acres zoned BP (4.9%), for a total of 1,556.24 acres. 
Today, following the district consolidation and a few annexations and zone changes 
throughout the City, there are 1,635.44 acres of land zoned MU-2. Beginning January 
21, 2024, and continuing through the present, the affected property has been and is 
zoned Mixed-Use Light Commercial (MU-2).  
 
Combining these zone districts (“legacy districts” in the 2023 Code) with disparate 
development standards (dimensions, uses, etc.) entailed selecting for preservation only 
those standards from each legacy district which would cohere to meet the intent of the 
MU-2 district. Whereas M-U had not allowed “Drive-Through Uses (Restaurants)” and 
“Drive-Through Uses (Retail),” these uses were allowed in the 2010 Code’s C-1 and BP 
districts. 
 
Given that the preponderance of the land to be zoned MU-2 had been zoned C-1, it 
would have represented a significant expansion of the prohibition on restaurant and 
retail drive-throughs to apply the M-U’s restriction. Instead of doing so, a new approach 
was adopted. First, drive-throughs from principal uses paired with one of three business 
types to accessory uses (“Drive-Through Facility”), while preserving the tripartite 
division into Office, Restaurant, or Retail. Each of these is allowed in MU-2. Second, the 
prohibition was partially preserved by shifting it from being zone district-based to 
geographically bound, as represented by Figure 04.04-1 at § 21.04.040(e)(2)(i)(E) of 
the 2023 Code. This had the effect of maintaining the prohibition for 233.07 acres of 
land, or 63.6% of the total land that had been zoned M-U. 
 
More specifically, both the 2010 Code’s M-U prohibition and the 2023 Code’s 
geographic prohibition area were and remain of greatest impact on the 24 Road 
Corridor. Of all land previously zoned M-U, 264.73 acres (72.3%) were located within 
the 24 Road Corridor, as previously defined by Title 33, 24 Road Corridor Subarea 
Plan, and implemented by Title 25, 24 Road Corridor Design Standards. The 
geographic prohibition area is contained entirely within the 24 Road Corridor. The 
233.07 acres subject to the geographic prohibition represent 88% of those lands 
previously zoned M-U in the 24 Road Corridor. 
Packet Page 54
 
Altogether, the focus of the restaurant and retail drive-through prohibition across time 
and varying means has historically been to ensure that development in the 24 Road 
Corridor Subarea is compatible with adopted plans and the vision for the subarea. The 
applicant argues, however, that amending the area subject to the prohibition is 
compatible with the Comprehensive Plan and is more consistent with the Zoning and 
Development Code than the present extent. 
 
PROPOSED AMENDMENT 
The applicant proposes to amend Figure 04.04-1 at Section § 21.04.040(e)(2)(i)(E) to 
allow drive-throughs associated with restaurant and retail sales uses on the two parcels 
comprising the applicant’s properties presently within the prohibition area. The existing 
Figure 04.04-1 and the amended figure proposed by the applicant are shown in the 
proposed ordinance. 
 
NOTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS 
Notice was completed as required by GJMC § 21.02.030(g). Notice of the public 
hearing was published on October 31, 2026, in the Grand Junction Daily Sentinel. 
 
ANALYSIS   
The criteria for review are set forth in GJMC § 21.02.050(d) of the Zoning and 
Development Code, which provides that the City may approve an amendment to the 
text of the Code if the applicant can demonstrate evidence proving each of the following 
criteria: 
 
(A) Consistency with Comprehensive Plan 
The proposed Code Text Amendment is generally consistent with applicable 
provisions of the Comprehensive Plan. 
 
As outlined above, the intersection of the 24 Road Corridor, the former M-U zone 
district, and the current restaurant and retail drive-through prohibition area is 
complex. Evaluating the applicant’s proposal and argument in favor of the 
amendment requires analyzing the evolution of each and their relationships with the 
Comprehensive Plans of 2010 and 2020. 
 
As described above, the 24 Road Corridor Subarea Plan was adopted in 2000 and 
informs the origin of the drive-through prohibition subject to this proposed 
amendment. 
 
The 2010 Comprehensive Plan and the 2010 Code were developed in tandem and 
adopted on February 17 (Ordinance No. 4406) and April 5, 2010 (Ordinance No. 
4419), respectively. Ordinance No. 4406 includes in its recitals that “[t]he 
Comprehensive Plan will control when area plans, adopted prior to the 
Comprehensive Plan, are inconsistent with the Comprehensive Plan.” 
 
Packet Page 55
Likewise, Ordinance No. 4971, which adopted the 2020 Comprehensive Plan on 
December 16, 2020, includes this same language in its recitals. Moreover, 
irrespective of the genesis and evolution of the restaurant and retail drive-through 
prohibition to implement the purposes of the 24 Road Corridor Subarea Plan, that 
connection is rendered obsolete by the sunsetting and supersession of that plan by 
the 2020 Comprehensive Plan’s Area-Specific Policies. Therefore, the 2020 
Comprehensive Plan stands as the lone remaining adopted plan addressing land 
uses—and specifically drive-throughs—in the affected area. 
 
The applicant’s narrative attests to the proposed amendment’s consistency with 
Chapter 2, Plan Principles 2 and 3. Staff finds that weighing the relationships 
between the existing area subject to the drive-through prohibition, on one hand, and 
the proposed amendment to remove the affected property, on the other, is 
inconclusive. More compelling evidence to support the amendment is found in 
Chapters 3 and 4. 
 
CHAPTER 2: PLAN PRINCIPLES 
Although staff does not find that the Plan Principles cited by the applicant alone 
compel the proposed amendment, the resulting geographic area may be equally 
compatible with those Plan Principles. 
 
Plan Principle 2: Resilient and Diverse Economy 
The applicant attests that the amendment is necessary to allow appropriate 
development on the affected property to support Goal 1, Strategies a (Economic 
Diversity), b (Employment Base), and g (Sales and Use Tax Revenue). 
 
The applicant links the prospective development by the owner of their grocery store 
to economic diversity, yet it is unclear from the applicant’s narrative why that 
business (a Retail, Big Box use as defined by the Zoning and Development Code) is 
precluded by the absence of complementary restaurant and retail drive-through 
uses. 
 
Further, even being deferential to the applicant’s industry expertise on the business 
mix necessary for the intended grocery store, the purported result of the amendment, 
to “give consumers in the City more choices for their shopping needs, provide new 
shopping opportunities and other commercial offerings, help make the local economy 
more resilient, and facilitate competition among existing business operations to 
improve pricing for local consumers,” is not demonstrably promoted by reducing 
barriers (if indeed the existing extent of the prohibition constitutes one) to the formula 
businesses characteristic of big box retail and drive-through restaurants. 
 
Increased employment and tax revenue are additional claimed benefits of the 
amendment. Anecdotally, the extended vacancy of the affected property and the 
applicant’s purchase of and intent to develop it support the claim that the prohibition 
inhibits the potential job and tax growth represented specifically by the applicant’s 
potential development. Certainly, the addition of multiple businesses spanning tens 
Packet Page 56
of thousands of square feet of sales-generating floor area would contribute both. 
This is compatible, especially, with the call in Strategy g for “increasing retail sales 
inflow and reducing retail sales leakage.” 
 
Staff notes that these arguments are premised on enabling the specific development 
intended by the applicant on the affected property, which the applicant owns. A case 
in favor of revenue- and employment-generating regulation generally is credible 
when those impacts can be demonstrated causally, but staff advises keeping that 
broader perspective, rather than relying on one development scenario for which a 
development application has not been received—and therefore has not been 
reviewed or approved—when deciding whether a code provision should be modified. 
Staff further acknowledges that the applicant’s arguments could be advanced for 
virtually any merchant mix, and so it should not be taken as tantamount to 
establishing that only the applicant’s development optimally fulfills these goals and 
strategies. It is plausible, although far from guaranteed, that continued inclusion of 
the affected property in the drive-through prohibition area would accomplish a more 
efficient use of land with higher sales and employment density over the long run. 
 
The applicant also references Goal 3, Strategies b, e, and f. However, Strategies b 
and e do not apply; rather, Strategy b directs the City to “[P]roactively invest in 
infrastructure and amenity projects,” which, the applicant observes, the City has 
already done through improvements to 24 Road and Canyon View Park, while 
Strategy e pertains to support for economic development partners. Staff has held 
that Strategy f, which calls on the City to “reduce barriers to entry for new 
businesses,” may be construed as consistent with a variety of amendments intended 
to reduce perceived regulatory burdens increasing the cost or complexity of business 
starts or viability. Here again, the specificity of the applicant’s reasoning strains the 
evidential relevance of the Strategy. The referents for “barriers” and “new 
businesses” as used in Strategy f are underdefined. The same regulation may 
present a barrier to one business and create an advantage for another within the 
same or a different use. If there does exist a set of barriers that inhibit new 
businesses broadly, pursuing reductions of those barriers depends on first identifying 
which businesses are meant to be classified as new. The Comprehensive Plan 
provides no such taxonomy, leaving open the question as to whether a new location 
of a business founded in 1967, which operates more than 140 stores and does not 
offer franchises, would qualify. 
 
Finally, the existing conditions study, summarized as “Where We Are Now,” to inform 
Plan Principle 2 cautions that “the future of retail is unknown due to a national 
decline in sales from brick and mortar stores and increasing online sales.” 
Accordingly, “[t]he City is concerned with the viability of traditional retail areas such 
as shopping malls and recognizes the importance of reprogramming key areas into 
mixed use areas and the needed evolution to include more residential and 
experiential uses.” The proposed amendment is for the express purpose of allowing 
a traditional retail area by reducing the scope of a restriction adopted twenty-six 
years ago and maintained to the present as one among a set of regulations which 
Packet Page 57
function as tools to promote the very reprogramming contemplated by earlier policies 
and echoed by the extant Comprehensive Plan. 
 
Plan Principle 3: Responsible and Managed Growth 
Goal 1, Strategy b prioritizes Urban Infill development to make efficient use of 
existing public facilities and services. Any development on the affected property will 
make use of existing, high-quality facilities in 24 Road and Canyon View Park. 
Strategy e is to “[e]nsure zoning and development regulations are consistent with the 
Comprehensive Plan.” To invoke this Strategy to argue for the proposed 
amendment’s consistency with the Comprehensive Plan is circular. Fulfilling the 
Strategy—along with satisfying this companion review criterion for a Zoning Code 
Amendment—requires that this or any proposed amendment be consistent with the 
Comprehensive Plan by virtue of other elements and the plan altogether. 
 
Goal 2, Strategy a concerns enabling infill and redevelopment, especially the reuse 
of existing buildings, but also the consolidation of lots to increase development 
potential. To the extent that the amendment facilitates development that would 
otherwise be precluded, as the applicant purports, it is supported by this Strategy.  
 
Goal 6, Strategy a directs the City to “support higher intensity employment uses 
through land use policies and investment in infrastructure that support these 
employment areas.” As with Goal 2, Strategy a, the amendment accords with this 
Strategy to the extent that it stimulates higher intensity employment uses on the 
affected properties. Neither the Comprehensive Plan nor the Zoning and 
Development Code defines “intensity.” Within the context of both, intensity, as 
applied to nonresidential uses and development, appears to be closely related to 
what density means for residential. Density refers to the number of dwelling units per 
acre of land, the increase of which concentrates more people in smaller areas. 
Intensity, similarly, connotes the density of nonresidential activity, which may be 
measured by the number of employees or consumers, or both, occupying a given 
area, by the volume or variety of sales transactions or other business occurring 
within a development, or by the degree of traffic, noise, visual, or other impacts 
caused by a development. In some cases, the Comprehensive Plan associates

stakeholders and policymakers.

Finally, the applicant argues that the amendment implements Goal 7, Strategy a to

Staff advises careful consideration of each Goal and Strategy in the context of the

by Chapter 2. Therefore, it is necessary to consult other, more narrowly applicable,

CHAPTER 4: AREA-SPECIFIC POLICIES 
Chapter 4, Area-Specific Policies, addresses the affected property both by its 
inclusion within an identified Commercial Area Regional Center and by its 
identification as a portion of the I-70 Regional Commercial Character Area within the 
24 Road Corridor Subarea.  
 
Regional Centers “are large commercial nodes that support the needs of Grand 
Junction residents as well as the surrounding communities […] well-served by the 
region’s transportation network and serve as the hub for services.” They “should 
include a broad mix of activity-generating uses such as retail stores of various sizes, 
restaurants, lodging, and entertainment” with compatible residential. Circulation and 
Access, Building Form and Character, and Streetscape policies promote safe and 
efficient multimodal movement in the context of major transportation corridors. 
 
Subarea Plans for Lower Downtown and 24 Road Corridor are presented. They 
“should inform development decisions within these key areas of Grand Junction and 
guide the vision for these areas.” The 24 Road Corridor Subarea is divided into five 
Character Areas. The affected property is within the I-70 Regional Commercial 
Character Area, described as follows: 
 
This major node offers potential for the development of new regional-serving 
commercial areas including uses such as office, commercial, light industrial, and 
residential. New restaurants and entertainment are desirable, especially as 
complementary to the regular activity of both local and regional sporting events that 
regularly occur at Canyon View Park. Commercial development in this character 
area should be developed with building oriented towards 24 Road. Development 
should also orient an architectural façade, signage, and landscaping towards

Area-Specific Policies for the 24 Road Corridor Subarea address the 24 Road

(B) Consistency with Zoning and Development Code Standards 
The proposed Code Text Amendment is consistent with and does not conflict with or 
contradict other provisions of this Code. 
 
The intent of the MU-2 zone district is provided at GJMC § 21.03.060(d)(1) as 
follows: 
 
The MU-2 district is intended to accommodate commercial, employment, multi-
unit, and other uses along transportation corridors to promote development that is 
comfortably accessible via all modes of transportation, including motor vehicles, 
bicycles, and walking. Commercial uses in the MU-2 district may be somewhat 
larger in scale and more flexible than the MU-1 district, including more auto-
oriented uses such as gas stations. While ground stories along streets are 
intended for commercial uses, the upper stories are appropriate for residential 
and/or office uses. MU-2 is appropriate as indicated in Table 21.03-2: 
Comprehensive Plan Implementation. 
 
The flexibility in commercial scale and auto-oriented uses is consistent with the 
allowance for drive-throughs (for all three specified types—office, restaurant, and 
retail) as an accessory use in the MU-2 district. 
 
Modifying the boundary of the geographic restriction on drive-throughs for restaurant 
and retail does not affect other provisions of the Zoning and Development Code. Site 
development standards pertaining to drive-throughs and other auto-oriented uses in 
the 24 Road Corridor Design Standards (GJMC § 25.02.090) would remain in effect 
and are likewise not affected by the proposed amendment. 
 
Staff finds this criterion is met.
