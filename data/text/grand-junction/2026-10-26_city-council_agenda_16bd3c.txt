What is the purpose of a Workshop? 
 
The purpose of the Workshop is to facilitate City Council discussion through analyzing 
information, studying issues, and clarifying problems. The less formal setting of the Workshop 
promotes conversation regarding items and topics that may be considered at a future City 
Council meeting. 
 
How can I provide my input about a topic on tonight’s Workshop agenda? 
Individuals wishing to provide input about Workshop topics can: 
 
1.  Send input by emailing a City Council member (Council email addresses) or call one or more 
members of City Council (970-244-1504)

SUBJECT: 
  
Continuation of 2027 City Manager's Proposed Budget 
  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: 
  
The purpose of this workshop item is to continue with presenting to City Council the 
recommended balanced 2027 Annual Budget, focusing on Internal Service, Enterprise 
and Capital funds. 
  
BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
The 2027 budget was developed in a significantly more constrained financial 
environment than in recent years, requiring meaningful adjustments to address the 
structural imbalance between recurring revenues and expenditures. As discussed 
during the previous budget workshops, staff has developed a recommended balanced 
budget that prioritizes core municipal services while seeking to maintain the City's long-
term financial stability. 
 
During the October 5 workshop, City Council reviewed the recommended 2027 budget, 
with an emphasis on the General Fund and the strategies used to achieve a balanced 
budget. This workshop will continue the review of the recommended budget, focusing 
on Internal Service, Enterprise, and Capital funds. Staff will discuss the financial 
outlook, significant budget considerations, and planned capital investments associated 
with these funds. 
 
Staff will also provide updates on refinements made to the recommended budget since 
the previous workshop and address any outstanding questions from City Council. 
Additionally, the workshop will include a discussion of proposed contributions to outside 
agencies and organizations. 
 
This workshop provides City Council with an opportunity to complete its review of the 
Packet Page 3
major components of the recommended 2027 Annual Budget and provide additional 
direction to staff before the budget is brought forward for formal consideration and 
adoption. 
  
FISCAL IMPACT: 
  
There is no direct financial impact associated with the workshop discussion itself. 
However, the recommended 2027 Annual Budget establishes the proposed financial 
plan for City operations, capital investments, personnel, and other expenditures for the 
upcoming fiscal year. 
 
Final fiscal impacts will be established through City Council's subsequent consideration 
and adoption of the 2027 Annual Budget and associated appropriations. 
  
SUGGESTED ACTION: 
  
This item is for discussion and direction to staff regarding the recommended 2027 
Annual Budget.

2. Traffic Signal Upgrades - $ 308,700 The City currently owns 52 traffic signals with 
electronic controllers of varying age and functionality and operates the 46 state highway 
signals inside the City limits under a maintenance contract. Upgrades to signal 
equipment are required for safety and compliance with Federal requirements. This is 
an ongoing replacement/upgrade program for traffic signal controllers and other 
equipment. Maintaining a replacement cycle for signal controllers and equipment is 
necessary, primarily because of the limited service life of the equipment which is 
exposed to in -the-field conditions. It is also necessary to keep pace with current 
technology supporting traffic signal coordination, vehicle detection, and emergency pre-
emption systems; all of which contribute to safer and less congested roadways. 
 
3. Contract Street Maintenance - $ 3,475,100 Street maintenance requires an ongoing 
annual investment to preserve the City’s $266 million street network. The City uses a 
pavement management system to evaluate pavement conditions and prioritize 
maintenance based on ride quality, structural adequacy, and surface distress.  The City 
is divided into 12 Street Maintenance Areas (SMAs), with one area generally scheduled 
for maintenance each year. Streets within each SMA are evaluated individually to 
determine the most appropriate treatment, which may include fog seal, chip seal, 
microsurfacing, asphalt overlay, or reconstruction. Contracted Street Maintenance 
typically includes a variety of competitively bid and contracted work such as hot -mix 
asphalt overlays, asphalt patching, high- density mineral bond (HA5) treatments, and 
street reconstruction. In 2027, the primary projects for the Contracted Street 
Maintenance program will focus on asphalt overlays on Patterson Road, replacement 
of the 12th Street & Horizon Drive Roundabout, and completing an updated assessment 
of the street network’s pavement condition. The City will separately self -perform chip 
seal and crackfill work with a budget of $1,463,250, which is not included in the Contract 
Street Maintenance amount. Crackfill helps prevent water intrusion and slows 
pavement deterioration, while chip seal provides a moisture -resistant surface and 
improves skid resistance. This work will continue to be performed by City crews.

Information Technology – Capital   
12. City Hall UPS Replacement Project - $ 800,000 This project replaces the end-of-life 
uninterruptible power supply (UPS) systems in the City Hall data center, which houses 
the City's core technology infrastructure, including systems that support public safety. 
The UPS keeps servers, network and phone systems running through power outages, 
surges and fluctuations, bridging the gap until generator power takes over. A failure of 
the current equipment could interrupt City and public safety operations, damage 
hardware and corrupt data.  The project covers design, installation and the supporting 
data center systems needed to operate the new UPS safely and reliably: 
 
• UPS equipment: Replacement units sized for current load and future growth, with 
better energy efficiency than the existing systems. 
• Electrical: Upgrades to power distribution serving the data center. 
• HVAC: Cooling improvements to maintain proper operating conditions for critical 
equipment. 
• Fire suppression: Protection for data center equipment and the building. 
 
Replacing this equipment on a planned schedule avoids the higher cost and service 
disruption of an emergency failure. It also protects the City's technology investments 
and keeps essential services, including public safety, available to residents.

The following provides follow-up information on questions raised at the Oct. 5 budget workshop, 
including the proposed 2027 rate increases and the impact of ending the affordable housing 
incentive fee backfill. 
Proposed 2027 rate increases — A household using 3,000 gallons of water a month, with 
single-family sewer and a 64-gallon trash cart, would pay $86.02 a month in 2027, up $9.74 
(12.8%) from $76.28.

City water — Base charges (availability and minimum) would rise 24%, and usage rates 12%. 
The 2025 Water Rate Study set a new structure for 2026: an availability fee by meter size, a 
minimum charge for the first 2,000 gallons and tiered usage rates. Even at those higher rates, 
summer 2026 water sales revenue was about 9% below 2025 because customers used less 
water. Raising base charges faster puts more of the revenue the system needs on charges that 
do not swing with usage. The proposed base charges are close to the levels of the city’s 
consultant (Raftelis) projected for 2028; moving them up one year keeps planned operations 
and capital projects on schedule instead of deferring them.

Sewer and Kannah Creek water — Both follow the 2025 rate studies. Persigo sewer rises 12%, 
from $30.89 to $34.60 a month for a single-family home, and industrial pretreatment, septage 
and grease fees rise the same 12%. Kannah Creek base rates rise 12% and its capital 
surcharge 4%. 
Ridges irrigation and connection fees — Ridges irrigation rises 6%; the planned Motor Control 
Center replacement may affect future rates. Water tap and Persigo plant investment fees rise 
3.6% with the ENR Construction Cost Index (2.5% in 2026), reflecting construction costs rather 
than any change in service or method. 
Other City services — Solid waste rates will not increase in 2027, and the monthly rate for the 
48-gallon cart drops from $16 to $14 to align with its cost of service. Following a successful pilot 
program, seasonal yard waste collection will be fully implemented in 2027. The optional service 
will be available April through October at a cost of $16 per month, charged only to participating

households. Recycling will be free under Colorado’s producer responsibility program, and

Right-of-way permits — Communication boring projects would pay a new $0.50 per linear foot 
of boring for permits issued on or after Jan. 1, 2027, in addition to the $60 right-of-way permit 
application fee, which does not change. Boring projects vary widely in length, and the per-foot 
fee ties the charge to the staff time spent administering and inspecting them. The proposed rate 
is comparable to what other communities charge. It does not include a street impact or 
degradation fee. No right-of-way permits will be issued Dec. 18 through 31 while the City 
updates its permit and billing systems. 
Development CCTV inspections — Beginning in 2027, Utilities will move closed-circuit 
television (CCTV) inspections of new sewer lines from City crews to qualified third-party 
contractors. These inspections are required before the City accepts new infrastructure. The City 
has performed them at no charge, including re-inspections. However, its two CCTV vans and 
crew are also needed for system maintenance, capital projects and emergency response, so 
development inspections can wait a week or longer. Under the preferred hybrid approach, 
developers can hire their own qualified contractor or ask the City to arrange one, with the 
contractor’s cost passed through to the developer. Either way, developers can schedule 
inspections and re-inspections on their own timeline instead of waiting for City crews, which will 
speed up the acceptance process. The City keeps review and acceptance authority. Staff will 
develop inspection standards, coordinate with the development community, and return to 
Council if a fee structure is needed. 
Affordable housing incentive fee backfill — Ending the backfill would leave $1,366,601 in 
2026 and $732,603 in 2027 of waived water and sewer fees unreimbursed. Today, the General 
Fund reimburses the Water and Sewer funds for City water and sewer fees waived for qualifying 
affordable housing projects. Without the backfill, those funds absorb the waived fees, and the 
budgeted backfill stays in General Fund balance. The new units will also add water and sewer 
customers, providing ongoing rate revenue to those funds over time.
