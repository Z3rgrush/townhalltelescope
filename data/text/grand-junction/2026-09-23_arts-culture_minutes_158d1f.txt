BUSINESS 
The GJCAC reviewed the results of 38 artist submissions for the Riverfront Trail mural project. A total of 
$9,000 was available for nine projects, with one project consisting of two smaller murals, requiring 
selection of ten designs.  
For the bridge location, the GJCAC reviewed four proposals and discussed design quality, durability, 
educational components, the artists’ demonstrated abilities, and how well the proposed artwork could 
be executed in the designated space. Following discussion, Cynthia moved to select Karrie Steely’s 
salamander design as the primary selection and Melissa Piazza’s floral design with a black background as 
the secondary selection. TJ seconded the motion, and it passed unanimously.  
For the remaining mural locations, there was discussion about whether to prioritize a greater number of 
individual artists or strictly follow the scoring results, including whether artists should be permitted to 
complete multiple murals. The Commission ultimately approved using the scoring matrix in order of 
average score. The motion was moved by Cynthia, seconded by Porcia Silverberg, and it passed 
unanimously. If an artist declines an additional location, or if a design is no longer eligible because the 
bridge location has already been filled, staff will proceed to the next highest-rated submission. 
There was continued discussion regarding revisions to the funding program and application process. 
Existing restrictions included scholarships, fundraisers, certain capital expenses, general operating 
expenditures, previously incurred deficits, and organizations receiving City Council funding for the same 
project. There was discussion moving toward a less restrictive funding model that would provide 
organizations greater flexibility in using awarded funds, including the possibility of supporting general 
operating expenses.  
There was broad support for evaluating organizations based more on their mission, credibility, history, 
and ability to carry out their work rather than requiring organizations to develop specific projects solely 
to qualify for funding. Staff noted that this change would require substantial revisions to the application 
but could be incorporated into the broader application overhaul already planned.  
The Commission reached consensus to remove the existing section identifying categories the 
Commission does not fund outlined in the Criteria and Goals document. The discussion indicated that 
the primary remaining geographic stipulation would be that funded events, projects, or programs take 
place within Grand Junction city limits.  
There was also discussion on whether applicant presentations should continue. Because the overall 
application process will be substantially revised, the consensus was to retain presentations for the 
upcoming funding cycle and revisit the requirement next year. Commissioners discussed concerns that 
presentation skills can influence funding decisions independently of the quality of an organization or 
project. 
CITY STAFF REPORTS/UPDATES 
Staff presented a preliminary concept for the greenhouse project and discussed approximately $1,600 in 
available 1% for Art funding. The greenhouse is envisioned as the first component of a larger project 
that could expand with additional greenhouses as future funding becomes available. Future phases have 
not yet been approved.  
Commissioners discussed possible artwork including stained glass, metalwork, outdoor sculpture, and 
interactive or educational art. The Commission favored issuing a broad call to artists rather than 
prescribing a specific medium, allowing artists to propose work appropriate for the space and available 
budget. Staff noted the limited timeline because the funds must be expended and the final product 
received by the end of December. Direct purchase of existing artwork was also identified as an available 
alternative if the call does not produce a suitable proposal.  
 
Staff shared completed artwork updates, including the Story Wall and Recognition Wall pieces at the 
Community Recreation Center. Staff also reported concerns with the execution and quality of the 
Community Room mural, including differences from the approved proposal and issues with paint 
consistency and finishing. Following discussions with the artist and project staff, the hallway mural was 
retained after touch-ups, while other portions were painted over. Staff noted that the experience 
reinforces the importance of evaluating an artist’s previous work and ability to execute the proposed 
project when reviewing future applications.  
Artwork installation at the Convention Center was scheduled for September 3 at 11:00 a.m., with 
commissioners invited to attend. Staff also reported that work was underway to schedule the first 
Strategic Cultural Plan subcommittee meeting and that Third Sector Innovations was being engaged to 
assist with facilitation and community engagement.  
 
Staff noted Commissioners will receive a ranking survey to assist in selecting local photography for the 
City calendar. More than 900 photographs were submitted, with City Communications staff narrowing 
the submissions before Commission review. Commissioner rankings were requested by September 8.
