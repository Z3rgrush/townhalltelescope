BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
The bylaws of the Historic Preservation Board specify that at the first meeting of the 
board each calendar year, the members of the board shall select three officers: a 
chairperson, a vice-chairperson, and a secretary. The secretary of the Board is 
understood to be the staff liaison to the Board. 
 
These officers have not been appointed for 2026. Staff recommends appointing a new 
chairperson and vice chairperson for the remainder of 2026.

CALL TO ORDER/ANNOUNCEMENTS 
The meeting was called to order at 4:01 p.m. by Vice Chair Dustin Anzures. 
 
APPROVAL OF MINUTES 
The minutes of the June 2, 2026, July 7, 2026, and August 28, 2026 meetings were 
reviewed and considered for approval. A motion to amend to the July 7, 2026 minutes 
to include the names of the members of the public who spoke was made by Dave 
Fishell, seconded by Kevin Tinsley, and approved by voice vote. 
 
Members of the public introduced themselves. 
 
A motion to approve the minutes with the amendment was made by Tinsley, seconded 
by Fishell, and unanimously approved by voice vote. 
 
DISCUSSION ITEMS 
1. Discussion concerning Certified Local Government 
a. Tim Lehrbach, Zoning Supervisor, introduced the item. 
b. Lindsey Flewelling, Preservation Planner and Certified Local Government 
(CLG) Coordinator for History Colorado, provided an overview of features, 
benefits, and requirements of the CLG program, the steps to become 
one, and elements of local preservation programs. 
c. Sidney Wood asked questions regarding tax credits for historic 
properties. Flewelling provided clarification. 
d. Flewelling addressed design guidelines for designated historic properties, 
their relationship to the Secretary of the Interior’s (SOI) Standards for 
Rehabilitation, mandatory Board review, and voluntary compliance. 
Later, Anzures, Wood, Lehrbach, and Adam Nikirk asked follow-up 
questions on subject properties, review authorities and criteria, and tax 
credits.

e. Fishell asked about proposed changes to national historic designation. 
Flewelling clarified that proposed changes are limited to Section 106. 
f. Bruce Noble commented that if Colorado Mesa University would commit 
to abiding by the SOI Standards for Rehabilitation, concerns over 
treatments to the Lincoln Park Barn would be resolved. 
g. Bennett Boeschenstein noted that the City has some required elements 
of a CLG and asked whether these would be folded into the program as a

guidance. The Guidelines do not anticipate all projects.

discussed the process for adopting standards for districts without them, 
particularly outreach to affected property owners; time commitment for 
City staff; the design standards previously developed, recommended by 
the Historic Preservation Board, but not adopted by the residents within 
the districts, for the districts without them; the necessary steps prior to 
becoming a CLG. 
j. Elizabeth Rowan indicated she would send the previously-developed 
district design standards to Lehrbach by email.

OTHER BUSINESS/PUBLIC COMMENT 
1. Public comments concerned the Lincoln Park Barn. 
a. Boeschenstein asked the Board to recommend that the Lincoln Park Barn 
be added to the local registry of historic structures. 
b. Tinsley and Lehrbach discussed the process by which the Board could 
initiate its own application to nominate the Lincoln Park Barn. 
c. Mari Shawcroft noted that the Barn had been a regular item for 
discussion on Board meetings prior to the transition in staff liaison in 
May. Lehrbach responded that there are no standing items continued on 
the agenda. 
d. Shawcroft and Noble stated that the City has not been responsive to the 
application submitted by the Friends of the Lincoln Park Barn. Lehrbach 
reiterated that the application was determined to be incomplete without 
an authorized signature of the property owner (City). 
e. Noble reminded the Board that History Colorado determined the Barn is 
eligible for nomination as a state designated historic structure, but the 
City and Colorado Mesa University would need to support the 
nomination. 
f. Members of the Board discussed holding a special meeting to consider 
the Board’s consideration of nominating the Barn for designation and 
requested that staff schedule such special meeting. Lehrbach responded 
that this could occur the following week.

adjourned at 5:45 p.m.

SUBJECT: 
  
Update regarding the Plaza Urrutia Fronton structural assessment 
  
RECOMMENDATION: 
   
  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: 
  
In June 2025, the City accepted a State Historical Fund grant from History Colorado to 
complete a structural assessment of the Plaza Urrutia Fronton at Canyon View Park. 
On September 23, 2026, City staff and contractors from Chamberlin Architects and 
Lindaur Dunn made the first of two field visits to complete ground-level investigations 
and measurements. Examination of the upper walls will be completed on a second field 
visit. 
  
BACKGROUND OR DETAILED INFORMATION: 
  
In June 2025, the City accepted a State Historical Fund grant from History Colorado to 
complete a structural assessment of the Plaza Urrutia Fronton at Canyon View Park. 
On September 23, 2026, City staff and contractors from Chamberlin Architects and 
Lindaur Dunn made the first of two field visits to complete ground-level investigations 
and measurements. Examination of the upper walls will be completed on a second field 
visit. 
Packet Page 11
 
The Plaza Urrutia Fronton is a pelota (handball) court constructed in 1978 on Jean 
Urruty's farm property. Jean Urrutia was a founding member of the North American 
Basque Organization. The structure's construction details and embellishments are 
characteristic of Basque pelotas throughout the world. In addition to being a popular 
recreational amenity, the Plaza Urrutia has become an important site for Basque 
gatherings and celebrating the historic and continuing presence of the Basque 
community and culture in Grand Junction and Western Colorado.
