New Business 
 
Sara Kinkade; GJ Water Conservation Specialist 
Sara Kinkade, the City's new Water Conservation Specialist, introduced herself to the Board. Board 
members also introduced themselves. 
Sara discussed challenges with the City's turf conversion program, noting that many completed projects 
have raised concerns about maintaining the health of existing trees as irrigated turf is removed. Most 
projects are located in the downtown area making it difficult to balance water conservation goals with 
preserving mature trees. Ongoing discussions with City staff have focused on identifying ways to 
maintain tree health while continuing to promote turf conversions. 
Packet Page 3
 
Sara noted she is exploring a variety of water conservation strategies beyond landscaping, including 
consulting with staff at the water treatment plants, and is seeking as many ideas as possible to reduce 
water use.  
Sara Kinkade asked about the potential damage sod cutting can cause to tree roots during turf 
conversion projects and whether there are alternative removal methods. Members noted that removing 
turf with sod cutters can damage tree roots, and alternatives such as cutting turf low, covering it, and 
using mulch were discussed. 
Board members emphasized the importance of providing irrigation across the full tree root zone rather 
than only near the trunk, as mature tree roots often extend well beyond the canopy. Proper mulch, 
avoiding weed fabric near trees, and using appropriate irrigation methods were identified as key factors 
in maintaining tree health. 
The discussion also highlighted that water conservation does not always require removing all turf. 
Alternatives such as native grasses, drought-tolerant groundcovers, and improved watering practices 
can reduce water use while preserving trees and creating more diverse landscapes. 
The Board discussed the value of evaluating water savings from turf conversion projects by comparing 
changes in landscape type and water use. Staff noted that some previous conversions have not resulted 
in significant water reductions but have improved landscape health and maintenance needs. The 
discussion highlighted the importance of understanding the overall impact of conservation efforts and 
continuing to evaluate best practices for sustainable landscapes. 
The Board discussed concerns about property owners discontinuing irrigation after turf conversions and 
the potential impact on mature trees. Staff noted that City code requires adjacent property owners to 
maintain watering of trees within the public right-of-way and suggested using conservation programs 
and available funding to help residents maintain tree health. 
Anna expressed interest in developing recommendations for Council that strengthen protections for City 
trees, including potential updates to ordinances and enforcement practices. Board members 
emphasized the need for a balance between water conservation and preserving the community’s urban 
tree canopy through education, outreach, and consistent enforcement. They also noted the importance 
of coordinating outreach so residents understand both their responsibility to maintain trees and 
available resources to help with irrigation improvements. 
Tri-fold Follow-up 
The Board revisited the proposal to distribute the forestry board’s trifold informational brochure 
through a third-party marketing group that provides welcome packages to new homeowners. 
Previously, the Board had discussed moving forward if at least 70% of the addresses reached were 
within city limits. Rob Davis reported that GIS review showed the percentage was significantly lower 
than expected, a little over 50% 
Overall, approximately 5,000 households had received welcome packages since 2023, with about 2,663 
located within city limits. The marketing group’s nonprofit rate would be approximately $150 per 
month, compared with their typical rate of $450–$500 per month. 
Packet Page 4
The Board discussed whether city funds should be used for outreach that also reaches residents outside 
city limits. Members noted concerns about potential confusion for non-city residents and possible 
impacts on staff time, but also recognized the value of broad tree education and the efficiency of using 
an existing outreach program. 
Members agreed that the program could serve as one component of a larger public outreach strategy 
and discussed the importance of evaluating its effectiveness before making a long-term commitment. 
Motion 
Tanya Travis made a motion to conduct a three-month trial of the welcome package program and 
monitor results, including public response and any impacts on staff time. If August is unavailable, the 
trial will begin in September. Dan Komlo seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

2026 Licensed Contractor Day of Service 
Rob reported that discussions had taken place with Tom Ziola regarding another Licensed Contractor 
Day of Service in 2026. The current plan is to hold the event at Sherwood Park to continue improvement 
efforts in that area, although a date has not yet been selected. 
Board members expressed support for following the same format as the previous year's event, noting 
that it had been successful and generated positive feedback. Staff indicated they were tentatively 
considering September.  
IRA Irrigation Grant 
Rob Davis and Tanya Travis will coordinate outreach to potential participants, including reviewing the list 
of interested property owners from the previous year and conducting additional neighborhood outreach 
as needed. Kamie Long offered to assist with outreach if needed. 
Kamie Long reported that the next round of grant applications will open at the end of the month. While 
federal agency restructuring is ongoing, there have been no indications that the grant funding will be 
withdrawn. The program is now being referred to as the Urban and Community Forestry (UCF) grant 
rather than the IRA grant. 
 
Market on Main Starts Tonight 
Board members reviewed preparations for the first event. Molly, Tanya, and Jermaine were assigned to 
staff the booth, with setup scheduled for 4:45 p.m. at the storage facility before the event begins at 5:30 
p.m. Staff discussed booth logistics, including equipment, the misting fan, water station access, and 
volunteer support. Members emphasized the importance of adequate setup assistance and agreed to 
evaluate the first event to identify any needed improvements for future markets. 
 
Four Canyons Parkway 
Rob Davis provided an update on the Four Corners Parkway/25 Road project, noting concerns with tree 
protection, project coordination, and the loss of several trees. He is working with the City's

Transportation Engineering and Parks and Recreation departments to improve tree protection practices 
and ensure forestry staff are involved earlier in future projects. 
Rob Davis discussed opportunities to restore canopy by planting additional trees in nearby city right-of-
way and emphasized the need for better maintenance of young trees. He also highlighted the potential 
value of a dedicated young tree maintenance crew and trained volunteers to support pruning and long-
term tree care. 
 
Other Items to Discuss
