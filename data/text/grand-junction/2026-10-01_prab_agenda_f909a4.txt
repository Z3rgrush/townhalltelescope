Jonathan Wheatley Supervisor Presentation 
Ken Sherbenou introduced Jonathan Wheatley, the Recreation Supervisor. He oversees Haley 
Van Camp (Arts, Culture and Senior Programming), Janelle Bjornestad (Childcare), and Shon 
Birch (summer camps, youth/adult leagues and contracted programs). 
 
Summer Camps and programming 
Jonathan informed the board about the summer camps the Recreation Department offers. Little 
Sunshines, STARS and SCORE camps usually fill quickly, while Tween Camp is less popular. 
Overall enrollment is about 95%, serving more than 700 children with approximately 65 seasonal 
staff. 
The department partners with D51, CMU and community organizations for facilities and 
programming. The department also offers contracted camps and programs which include 
wilderness survival, climbing, Challenge Island STEAM Camp and fencing, yoga, dancing and 
more. The rec department handles enrollment for contracted programs and generally uses an 
80/20 revenue split with providers. Jonathan clarified that scholarships are available for summer 
camps run by the rec department not for contracted camps, CCAP is also accepted. 
Another popular program is youth basketball which serves approximately 700 boys and 250 girls 
in grades 1–8 and relies heavily on volunteer coaches.  
D51 schools provide gym space for basketball, summer camps and adult volleyball and school 
gyms will still be needed after the CRC opens. Practices will continue at schools, with the goal 
of hosting one game per team each season at the CRC. 
 
Arts and Culture 
Jonathan shared that Haley serves as the liaison for the Commission on Arts and Culture and 
talked about the implementation of the Strategic Cultural Plan which they are preparing to update 
for 2028. An economic impact study is also completed every five years to highlight the social 
and economic value of the arts. 
The Commission on Arts and Culture provides $70,000 in funding for local arts and culture 
initiatives such as mural programs like art on the Riverfront Trail, murals on signal boxes, skate 
parks, and Parks Department connex equipment storage. 
Jonathan called out some of the 1% for the arts program projects, which are funded through 
qualifying capital improvement projects. They include a lot of roundabout installations as well as 
various CRC installations. The CRC will feature murals, interior design enhancements, a story 
wall, recognition wall, and rotating art. 
The department also puts on the annual Take Part in the Arts event at Lincoln Park which 
promotes local arts by encouraging visitors to participate in and experiencing art activities. 
For Senior Programming Jonathan highlighted the 25th annual Western Slope Senior Games, a 
week-long event with more than 50 events and 175 participants. 
 
Child Care center 
Jonathan discussed Little Learners, a Level 4 Colorado Shines childcare facility serving children 
6 weeks to 5 years old. Originally created for City employees, the program has recently 
expanded to the broader community and now offers UPK (Universal Preschool). 
Packet Page 4
Although enrollment fluctuates, infant rooms are typically at capacity, while preschool has some 
openings that they hope to fill soon. Community rates are higher, while City employees receive a 
discount. 
The City established the program to help attract and retain employees, and Ken noted that it has 
had a positive impact. However, Ken also noted that the cost of subsidizing the program has been 
challenging, particularly as benefit costs continue to rise. The department is working toward a 
more sustainable subsidy, with updated fees planned for the new year. 
 
Matchett Bike Park 
Ken Sherbenou discussed the future of Matchett Bike Park and the survey from the previous 
meeting. He introduced Faith Rodriguez, Executive Director of COPMOBA, who provided an 
update on the Lunch Loop Bike Park, located on City property and leased to COPMOBA. 
Faith informed the board that COPMOBA has raised $170,000 toward a $225,000 goal for the 
Lunch Loop project. Professional trail builder Spencer Rugland of Thunderbird Trail Works is 
expected to begin construction around December. Faith clarified that COPMOBA will also 
continue maintaining the park. 
The City had considered partnering with COPMOBA to formalize Matchett due to liability 
concerns. Recent improvements have addressed safety concerns and conflicts with the disc golf 
course, reducing the urgency for formalization. Due to COPMOBA’s current focus on Lunch 
Loop and the City’s budget challenges, Ken recommended holding off on further development at 
Matchett. 
The board also discussed the community’s interest in more beginner-friendly trails. Faith 
Rodriguez expressed that COPMOBA is interested in developing more accessible trails on City 
property in the future. 
 
Motion 
Cayman Haltiner made a motion to hold off on moving forward with further development and 
formalization of Matchett Bike Park. Cindy Enos-Martinez seconded the motion. The motion 
passed unanimously.

RESOLUTION NO. 02-26 
A RESOLUTION CREATING A PARKS AND RECREATION FACILITY NAMING PROCESS AND POLICY 
Purpose: 
The purpose of this policy is to establish a consistent and transparent process for the official naming and 
renaming of parks and recreation amenities and facilities under the jurisdiction of the City of Grand 
Junction. 
Names should help the public easily identify and locate facilities while reflecting the geographic, natural, 
historical, and cultural character of Grand Junction and the surrounding community. 
This policy is intended to: 
• Establish clear criteria for selecting names for parks and recreation facilities and amenities, including 
trails; 
• Provide a consistent process for considering naming and renaming requests; 
• Encourage names that are meaningful and relevant to the facility and surrounding area; 
• Preserve appropriate historical and community connections; and 
• Minimize confusion within the City parks and recreation system. 
Scope: 
This policy applies to City-owned or City-managed parks and recreation facilities and amenities, 
including but not limited to parks, trails, open spaces, recreation centers, buildings, sports fields, courts, 
shelters, playgrounds, and similar improvements (each referred to in this policy as a "facility"). 
The policy may also be applied to significant segments, loops, connectors of trails, shelters, fields, 
amenities or other identifiable components of the City parks and recreation system when the City 
determines that a separate name would improve identification, navigation, or public understanding. 
Existing names established through deed restrictions, agreements, easements, grants, or other legally 
binding documents shall be honored unless otherwise permitted by the applicable agreement. 
General Naming Principles: 
Naming should be undertaken thoughtfully and with the expectation that an approved name will remain 
in place for an extended period. 
Preference should generally be given to names that establish a meaningful relationship between the 
facility and its location, surroundings, history, or community. 
Names should be distinctive, reasonably concise, easy to understand, and appropriate for use on maps, 
signs, emergency response systems, and other City materials. 
Names that duplicate or are substantially similar to existing City parks, trails, recreation facilities, streets, 
or other prominent public places should generally be avoided when the similarity could create 
confusion. 
Facility Naming Criteria: 
Packet Page 7
When considering a name for a facility, preference should be given to names associated with one or 
more of the following: 
A. Geographic Location: A neighborhood, geographic area, destination, corridor, street, landmark, or 
other location associated with the facility. 
B. Natural or Geological Features: A prominent natural feature associated with the facility or 
surrounding area, including waterways, landforms, geology, native vegetation, wildlife, or other 
distinguishing environmental characteristics. 
C. Historical or Cultural Significance: A person, place, event, activity, or other subject with documented 
historical or cultural significance to Grand Junction or the surrounding area. 
D. Common Usage: A name by which the facility, corridor, property, or geographic feature is already 
commonly known when continued use of that name would promote public recognition and minimize 
confusion. 
E. Community Identity: A name that reflects the identity, character, heritage, or other distinguishing 
characteristics of the neighborhood or community served by the parks and recreation facility. 
F. Significant Contribution: An individual, family, organization, or group that has made a significant and 
documented contribution to the community, neighborhood, the City parks and recreation system, 
conservation, outdoor recreation, or the Grand Junction community. 
Naming Facilities After Individuals: 
Naming a facility after an individual should be considered a significant form of recognition and should 
occur selectively. 
An individual considered for recognition should have a demonstrated and significant connection to 
Grand Junction or have made a substantial contribution to the community, parks and recreation, trails, 
conservation, public lands, or another area directly relevant to the proposed recognition. 
A proposal to name a facility after an individual should include documentation describing the 
individual's accomplishments, contributions, and connection to the community or facility. 
If the individual is living, written consent from the individual should be obtained before the name is 
formally approved. If the individual is deceased, the City shall obtain written consent from the family or 
estate before the name is formally approved. 
Naming a facility after an individual is honorary recognition only and does not create any contractual, 
commercial, or property right in the individual's name, image, or likeness. Any consent required by this 
policy shall be given in writing and without compensation, and shall authorize the City to use the name, 
and any associated image or likeness, on signage, maps, GIS data, websites, and other City materials 
related to the facility. The City shall not pay or give any compensation, royalty, fee, or other 
consideration to any individual, family, estate, or other person for the use of a name, image, or likeness 
under this policy. Except as provided in an approved agreement under the Donations and Naming Rights 
section of this policy, no person shall pay or give any consideration to the City to obtain or keep the use 
of a name. Consent, once given, shall be irrevocable and shall not limit the City's authority to rename a 
Packet Page 8
facility under this policy. The City's use of an approved name does not imply endorsement by the City or 
the individual of any product, service, or cause. 
Naming Requests: 
6. Naming Requests 
Names may be proposed by City staff, City boards or commissions including the Parks and Recreation 
Advisory Board (PRAB) and City Council members. Requests for rename, other than direct City Council 
action, shall be submitted to the Parks and Recreation Department in writing and should include, at 
minimum: 
• The proposed name; 
• Identification of the facility to be named; 
• The reason and justification for the proposed name; 
• An explanation of how the proposal satisfies the criteria contained in this policy; 
• Relevant historical or supporting documentation; 
• Documentation of the individual’s contributions when an individual is proposed; 
• A description of any community outreach or public support associated with the proposal; and 
• For a renaming request, an explanation of why changing the established name is warranted. 
This policy is not intended to provide a general process for members of the public to submit applications 
for naming or renaming. Any member of the public may contact a City Council member, member of 
PRAB, or City staff with naming ideas. Members of Council, PRAB, or City staff may then submit an 
application for naming or renaming under this policy. 
Review and Approval Process: 
Upon receipt of a complete naming or renaming request, the following process shall apply: 
1. Staff Review. Parks and Recreation staff shall review the proposal for completeness, compliance 
with this policy, potential duplication or confusion, historical accuracy when applicable, and any 
operational considerations associated with the proposed name. 
2. Advisory Board Review. Qualifying proposals related to parks and recreation shall be presented to 
the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board (PRAB) for consideration at a public meeting. Public 
comment may be considered as part of the review. 
3. Final Approval. PRAB shall make a final decision regarding the proposed name based upon the 
criteria established in this policy.  City Council, as the City’s governing board, may reverse any 
decision made by PRAB when City Council believes such action to be warranted.  For major assets, 
PRAB’s function will be advisory to City Council, who has ultimate decision-making authority. For the 
purpose of this policy, a major asset is a facility, improvement, amenity, or trail that meets one or 
more of the following: (a) an initial construction cost, acquisition cost, or current replacement value 
exceeding $2,000,000; (b) a park as a whole, a recreation center, or another facility that serves a 
citywide or regional population; (c) a primary trail corridor or trail system, as distinguished from a 
segment, loop, or connector; or (d) as determined by the Parks and Recreation Director, significant 
cultural, historical, geographic, or other community value such that its naming warrants City Council 
consideration.  When a naming request or recommendation is made for a major asset, PRAB shall, 
Packet Page 9
by resolution, recommend or not recommend the name along with their reasons. The application 
and PRAB recommendation shall be sent to City Council for consideration of the naming request. 
4. Implementation. Following approval, Parks and Recreation staff shall coordinate changes to signs, 
maps, websites, GIS information, public information materials, and other City records as 
appropriate. 
Renaming Existing Parks and Recreation Facilities: 
Facility names are intended to provide continuity and long-term community identification. Accordingly, 
renaming an established facility should be undertaken only after careful consideration. 
A request to rename a facility shall follow the same review and approval process as a request to name a 
facility and shall include a clear justification for changing the established name. 
Renaming may be considered when: 
• The existing name creates substantial confusion or is no longer effective for identifying the facility; 
• The existing name duplicates or conflicts with another public facility or geographic feature; 
• New historical information demonstrates that the existing name is inaccurate or inappropriate; 
• Continued use of a commemorative name is determined, based on documented information, to no 
longer appropriately represent the community; 
• A change would improve consistency within the City parks and recreation system; or 
• Other compelling circumstances warrant reconsideration of the established name. 
Names Not Eligible for Consideration: 
Proposed names shall not: 
• Duplicate or closely resemble an existing City trail, park, facility, or other public place when doing so 
would create confusion; 
• Contain obscene, vulgar, discriminatory, or threatening language; 
• Promote or encourage unlawful activity or violence; 
• Conflict with applicable federal, state, or local law; 
• Conflict with requirements associated with grants, deeds, easements, agreements, or other 
restrictions applicable to the property; or 
• Be primarily commercial or promotional in nature unless considered separately under an approved 
City sponsorship, donation, or naming-rights agreement. 
Donations and Naming Rights: 
A donation of money, property, materials, or services does not automatically entitle a donor to naming 
rights. 
Any naming rights associated with a significant donation, capital campaign, property acquisition, or 
development agreement shall be considered separately by the City and documented through an 
appropriate agreement. The City Council and City Manager retain authority to enter into any naming 
rights agreement for any facility subject to this policy. Such agreement shall not be subject to this policy 
and shall not require PRAB approval or review. 
Packet Page 10
Where a legally binding deed, donation agreement, or other instrument establishes naming 
requirements, those requirements shall be honored consistent with applicable law. 
Signage and Implementation: 
Official facility names shall be incorporated into City signage, maps, GIS data, websites, and other public 
information as appropriate. 
Facility signs, including trail signs, shall conform to applicable City signage and wayfinding standards. 
Approval of a facility name does not necessarily require immediate replacement of existing signage. Sign 
installation or replacement may occur as part of normal maintenance, capital improvements, or as 
funding becomes available unless otherwise directed by the City. 
Exceptions and Final Authority: 
The City Council or the City Manager may approve an exception to this policy when unusual or 
compelling circumstances warrant an exception and doing so is consistent with applicable law and the 
public interest. 
Final authority for the official naming or renaming of City parks and recreation assets rests with PRAB, 
except that final authority for major assets, as defined in this policy, rests with the City Council.  
City Council retains ultimate authority to exercise any and all naming rights for any facility subject to this 
policy and may circumvent this policy through exercise of its authority. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT HEREBY RESOLVED BY THE PARKS AND RECREATION ADVISORY BOARD OF THE 
CITY OF GRAND JUNCTION THAT: 
1. The Parks and Recreation Advisory Board adopts this policy as described above.
