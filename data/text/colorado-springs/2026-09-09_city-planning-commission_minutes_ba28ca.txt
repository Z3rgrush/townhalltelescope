Allison Stocker, Senior Planner, explained that the proposal consisted of 
two application types, a Zone Map Amendment and a Land Use Plan 
Amendment for approximately 23 acres located southeast of Platte Avenue 
and Wooten Road. She noted that the property is currently zoned as a 
mixture of Business Park and Light Industrial, and included the Airport 
Overlay, Accident Prevention Zones 1 and 2, and the Streamside Overlay. 
She added that the existing zoning carried Conditions of Record.
Ms. Stocker stated that the applicant’s request was to consolidate the 
entire subject area under the Light Industrial (LI) zone district, remove the 
Conditions of Record, and retain all existing overlays. She explained that 
the intent of the application was to allow future development of heavy 
vehicle sales, rental, and repair through a later development plan review, 
that are not currently allowed under Business Park. Light Industrial, she 
said, was the next higher intensity zoning option available without 
pursuing an even higher industrial classification. Ms. Stocker clarified 
that the project did not include the entirety of the parcels involved, 
noting that the larger holdings were approximately 60 acres, and that the 
southern portion would remain unchanged. 
Ms. Stocker stated that the plan was last amended in 2009, and at that 
time the property carried a mix of future land use designations including 
Business Services and Neighborhood Commercial. She pointed out 
Page 7City of Colorado Springs Printed on 11/3/2026

that the plan illustrated access points from Wooten Road and East Platte 
Avenue, and that the overlay would nonetheless be triggered if future 
development occurred within the hatched area shown on the plan. Ms. 
Stocker stated that keeping higher intensity uses closer to major 
intersections and highways was desirable, especially considering the 
heavy vehicle related uses proposed by the applicant, and would help 
keep heavy vehicles off residential streets and lower volume routes. 
Ms. Stocker said the project had undergone two public notices and no 
comments were received. She stated that both applications were found 
to be in compliance with PlanCOS and met their respective review 
criteria.
Applicant Presentation
William Guman, representative for the applicant, stated that the request 
was a rezone to Light Industrial, which they believed was compatible with

repair, small office space, and other service uses permitted by code. Mr. 
Guman noted that Wagner Equipment, a distributor for Caterpillar 
construction equipment, considered the site ideal for the intended 
operations.
Mr. Guman described surrounding land uses, including RV sales and 
service, warehouse storage, and light industrial operations west of the site, 
and RV and boat storage to the east. He stated that staff’s report and the 
project statement indicated no anticipated adverse impacts to adjacent 
neighborhoods, and that future development would include standard 
exterior improvements and on-site parking. 
Mr. Guman reiterated that the Towne East Master Plan was adopted in 
1985, and that the subject parcel was one of the last undeveloped parcels 
in the plan area. He said the property is currently vacant, and the request

restrictive streamside buffers. He said nearby uses include an RV 
sales/service site, a storage warehouse, and a boat storage facility. Mr. 
Guman said the 23.37 acre site was accessible from both the East 
Platte frontage road and Wooten Road, with additional access 
requirements to be addressed during development plan review. 
Mr. Guman summarized the project’s alignment with Colorado Springs 
master plan goals, including diversification of the local economy and

William Gray, Senior Planner, presented the Lowell Zone Map Amendment 
and associated Major Modification. He explained that the request involved 
1.20 acres located at 202, 251, and 252 East Las Animas Street, part of 
The Poets Lofts Subdivision. He noted that the existing PDZ allowed 15 
dwelling units per acre, 60 feet of maximum height, and a mix of residential 
and commercial uses, while the proposed PDZ revision, would increase 
density to 184 dwelling units per acre, allow 950 square feet of commercial 
space, and raise the height allowance to 65 feet, expanding permitted uses 
to include apartments, condominiums, live/work units, commercial retail, 
office, and service uses.
Mr. Gray referenced the submitted exhibits, including the vicinity map, Poet 
Lofts subdivision documents, site views, and previously approved Lowell 
Neighborhood Land Use Plan materials. He stated that the change was 
intended to update development expectations for this portion of Lowell and 
Page 12City of Colorado Springs Printed on 11/3/2026
September 9, 2026City Planning Commission Meeting Minutes - Final
align zoning with the applicant’s desired configuration for the project.
Mr. Gray explained that the Major Modification would remove Lot 2, Lot 3, 
and Lot 4 from the existing Lowell Neighborhood Concept Plan, clarifying 
that this adjustment was needed to accommodate the revised density and 
development envelope proposed under the updated PDZ. He said the 
project complies with PlanCOS and the review criteria for the applications. 
Commissioner Cecil asked how making alteration to an application is 
different to placing conditions on it. Mr. Gray said they should be reviewed 
as conditions, just like any other application. 
Commissioner Willoughby asked how they came up with the max density. 
Mr. Gray said the densities are based on the 220 units that were part of the 
original application. 
Applicant’s Presentation
John Olson, Urban Landscapes, representing the applicant, noted that the 
site is part of the Lowell Neighborhood, located on the south side of 
downtown, originally planned in the 1980s. He explained that the first Poets 
Loft building was constructed around 2001, during a time when downtown 
residential was still uncommon, with only early projects like CityWalk 
beginning to appear. He referenced other early downtown-area 
developments from that period, including Palmer Village / Park Union, 
noting that the area has evolved significantly. 
Mr. Olson explained that the conceptual proposal includes approximately 
220 units. He said, because the request is a PDZ, the plan includes a 
required maximum density figure, though they prefer flexibility. He stated 
that the units would be distributed between the north and south portions of 
the site, with structured parking serving each building, and that the existing 
plaza would remain. Mr. Olson noted that the Lowell neighborhood 
originally included live/work units and townhomes, followed by a long 
stagnant period due to market conditions including 9/11, the Great 
Recession, and impacts to urban renewal timelines.
Mr. Olson stated the project process began in 2024, including review of the 
urban renewal clock and affordable housing financing requirements. He 
pointed out that the capital structure for affordable housing is complex and 
that the project’s land-use request is closely tied to meeting those financing 
needs. Mr. Olson noted that the project hosted a community meeting in 
October 2025 at the City Administration Building, attended by downtown 
advocates, nonprofit partners, and groups such as Concrete Couch, who 
expressed support. He said presented the regional housing-need data, 
Page 13City of Colorado Springs Printed on 11/3/2026
September 9, 2026City Planning Commission Meeting Minutes - Final
including the 13,815 homes estimated as needed in the Pikes Peak.
Mr. Olson clarified that access to the site would come from Las Animas 
Street, with an exit-only movement to Weber Street. He highlighted key 
PlanCOS alignment themes for the project, such as infill development, 
affordable housing, public art integration and efficient use of existing 
infrastructure. 
Commissioner Willoughby verified if the parking will be a structured 
parking garage. Mr. Olson said yes, it would be below grade and maybe 
one above, and with HUD financing they are looking at a one to one ratio. 
He said they are proposing combining the count between the garage and 
adjacent on-street parking.
Commissioner Engel asked if they need on-street parking to accomplish 
their goal of providing one parking per dwelling unit. Mr. Olson said with 
HUD financing they must provide one to one parking, that can be achieved 
through a combination of on-street parking and spaces within a structured 
parking facility, similar to typical downtown apartment projects. He 
explained that if the parking structure can be designed more efficiently, 
additional parking may be incorporated, but the goal remains a full 
one-to-one ratio. Mr. Olson also noted that much of the zoning language in 
the request comes directly from the former form-based code applied to the 
property across the street, particularly the Transition Sector 1 standards, 
which previously guided this area. He stated that building height is the main 
condition they seek flexibility on due to financing requirements; the 
conceptual image shows a six-story building, which is allowed under 
existing height conditions, but financing agencies look more favorably on 
projects that allow for additional height capacity, even if the final building 
does not exceed six stories. 
Commissioner Willoughby asked if they decided how much percentage of 
parking would be on or off-street. Mr. Olson said it would probably be 80% 
parking in the structure, and the adjacent parking would be fully adjacent, 
not a few blocks over.
Commissioner Robbins asked if this would be the tallest building in the 
area. Mr. Olson said yes. He said, related to density, it is around the same 
range of the surrounding area in downtown. 
Commissioner Robbins asked for the average square footage and number 
of bedrooms for the project. Mr. Olson said they will generally have 
one-bedroom unit, maybe few two-bedroom units.
Vice Chair Case asked Mr. Olson to expand on the vision for the mixed 
Page 14City of Colorado Springs Printed on 11/3/2026
September 9, 2026City Planning Commission Meeting Minutes - Final
use project. Mr. Olson explained that the commercial square footage 
shown in the plan represents maximum allowance, intended to preserve 
flexibility rather than commit the project to specific commercial uses. He 
said the idea was simply to allow for the possibility of a small neighborhood 
serving use, such as a coffee shop, studio, or fitness space, but 
emphasized that the project remains primarily an affordable housing 
development, not a commercial one.
Commissioner Willoughby asked if the HUD financing requires a minimum 
number of units per acre to grant funding. Toby Gannett clarified that the 
project does not require a minimum number of units but emphasized that 
the financing model is highly complex and drives many of the project’s 
essential components. He explained that the funding provides more 
favorable rates than market financing and is what enables the units to 
be delivered at below market rents. Mr. Gannett said, because of high 
construction, land, and financing costs, especially downtown, higher 
density improves financial efficiency, and that efficiency is critical to 
achieving long term affordability.
