Commissioner Willoughby requested that this item be pulled off the

Ethan Shafer, Urban Planner II, presented a request for a non-use variance 
to allow an existing six-foot ornamental wrought iron fence within the front 
setback area of the property at 411 West Bijou Street, where City Code 
permits a maximum height of four feet. He explained that the property 
consists of approximately 9,500 square feet, zoned R-2, and contains two 
single-family residences. He stated that the fence had been installed 
approximately two years earlier, was the subject of an active code 
enforcement case, and remained the focus of the variance request.
Mr. Shafer described the property’s context, noting that it sits between a 
law office and a church and fronts West Bijou Street, a corridor with 
significant pedestrian traffic between downtown and the West side. He 
reported that the owner documented years of vandalism, theft, break-ins, 
porch occupation, trespassing, and property damage. He further explained 
that previous security measures included lighting, cameras, alarms, and 
contracted security patrols, none of which fully resolved the issues. Staff 
received two public comments and a statement from the property manager, 
all supporting the request. Staff found the application met the criteria for a 
non-use variance and is compliant with PlanCOS.
Applicant’s Presentation
Dennis Lowe, participating remotely, described his family's ownership of 
the property since 2022 and discussed extensive renovations completed 
after acquisition. He detailed repeated criminal activity, including broken 
windows, graffiti, theft of appliances and air-conditioning equipment,

previously terminated its lease because of ongoing safety concerns. 
According to Mr. Lowe, installation of the fence substantially reduced those 
incidents. He acknowledged that the fence had been installed based on a

the variance process should have occurred before installation.
Mr. Lowe argued that the property occupied a unique location, surrounded 
largely by commercial and institutional uses rather than neighboring 
residences. He presented maps showing the surrounding zoning and land 
uses and explained that the property lacked the natural surveillance 
typically found in residential neighborhoods. He asserted that the site’s 
former use as a halfway house may also have contributed to recurring

intention of replacing it with an opaque fence.

Allison Stocker, Senior Planner, presented a Development Standards 
Adjustment request for the Kettle Creek development located at 2060 Old 
Ranch Road. The request sought approval of a seven-foot front 
street-loaded garage setback for approximately half of the “Unit B” product 
type dwellings rather than the standard twenty-foot setback required by 
code. The property consists of approximately 19.9 acres zoned R-Flex 
Medium and is planned for single-family detached and attached housing.
Ms. Stocker explained that no parking would be permitted in the shortened 
driveway aprons and that each dwelling would provide a two-car garage, 
along with development-wide guest parking exceeding minimum 
requirements. She identified two compensating benefits offered by the 
applicant: preservation of approximately 1.7 acres of open space 
containing flood plain, streamside overlay, and Preble’s meadow jumping 
mouse habitat, and enhanced architectural variation through housing 
design and building articulation. Staff determined that the application met 
the applicable review criteria and recommended approval, subject to 
documentation from the Metropolitan District demonstrating enforceable 
parking restrictions prior to final plat approval. 
Applicant’s Presentation
Andrew Rivadeneira, applicant representative, NES, explained that the 
development had previously received annexation, zoning, and land use 
approvals and that the setback issue arose because the current code limits 
flexibility for front-loaded garage configurations in the R-Flex Medium 
district. He described prior discussions regarding planned development 
zoning and explained why the project ultimately proceeded through the 
Development Standards Adjustment process. 
Mr. Rivadeneira argued that the proposed design furthered the intent of the 
R-Flex Medium district by providing diverse housing types, encouraging 
architectural variety, creating opportunities for enhanced neighborhood 
interaction, preserving environmentally sensitive areas, and increasing 
attainable housing choices. He also noted that the project significantly 
exceeded minimum guest parking and open-space requirements.

Johnny Malpica, Senior Comprehensive Planner, presented a citywide 
UDC text amendment concerning height allowances for accessory dwelling 
units (ADUs). He explained that the proposal was intended to correct an 
unintended consequence of the 2025 ADU ordinance update. Under 
current regulations, detached ADUs could reach twenty-five feet in height 
only when associated with a converted garage; otherwise, detached ADUs 
were limited to sixteen feet in height. 
Mr. Malpica explained that the proposed amendment would allow a 
detached ADU attached to a detached garage to utilize the twenty-five-foot 
height allowance without requiring a formal garage conversion. He stated 
that the change would eliminate unnecessary permitting complications 
while preserving the original policy intent. He further explained that the 
proposal affected only detached ADUs and did not otherwise change 
broader ADU regulations.
Public Comment 
Page 10City of Colorado Springs Printed on 11/13/2026
October 14, 2026City Planning Commission Meeting Minutes - Final
Jill Gaebler, Executive Director, Pikes Peak Housing Network, said she 
supports the amendment, describing it as a reasonable compromise that 
would reduce costs, streamline construction, increase housing options, 
support multigenerational living, and expand attainable housing 
opportunities. She cited housing affordability challenges and maintained 
that ADUs could help address local housing shortages without materially 
altering neighborhood character. 
Daniel Rennich, resident of the area, addressed the Commission with 
clarifying questions regarding ADU regulations, including detached ADUs 
in portions of the Westside. Staff responded by explaining current 
restrictions associated with the Wildland Urban Interface overlay and 
discussing the history of recent state and local regulatory changes.
Commission Discussion
Commissioner Cecil referenced comments from the Historic 
Neighborhoods Partnership and asked staff to clarify how the phrase 
"attached to a detached garage" would be interpreted. She questioned 
whether the language could allow configurations other than an ADU located 
above a garage and sought clarification on how the provision would be 
administered. Mr. Malpica explained that staff intentionally avoided 
requiring an ADU to be located specifically above a garage. He stated that 
the proposed language was intended to provide reasonable design 
flexibility while still requiring the ADU to be physically connected to the 
detached garage. He noted that the Code contains definitions for detached 
structures and attached ADUs that would guide staff interpretation during 
the review process.
Chair Hensler commented that the terminology could be difficult to follow 
but stated that the amendment appropriately addressed an unintended 
consequence of the previous ADU ordinance and would provide additional 
flexibility for homeowners seeking to construct detached ADUs associated 
with garages.
Motion by Commissioner Willoughby, seconded by Commissioner 
Clements, to recommend approval to City Council the adoption of an 
Ordinance amending Chapter 7 (Unified Development Code) of the 
Code of the City of Colorado Springs 2001, as amended, as related to 
height allowances for accessory dwelling units.
The motion passed unanimously.

requested variances would allow development of a single-family detached 
residence while complying with all other applicable standards. Staff 
reviewed comments addressing drainage, access, safety, and 
neighborhood character concerns and coordinated with emergency service
