Nadine Hensler - Planning Commission Chair
Kevin Walker - Planning Director
Kevin Walker, Planning Director, announced that Parks staff member Lana 
Thielen would join later in the morning to present the new Parks Master 
Plan, depending on the timing of her Parks Board hearing. He also 
reported on his recent trip to Huntsville with the Chamber of Commerce 
and City Council representatives, describing the city’s development culture, 
tax structure, space-related industries, and economic ecosystem. He 
contrasted Huntsville’s approach to infrastructure investment and tax 
authority with Colorado Springs’ more limited fiscal tools. Commissioners 
asked questions regarding zoning and development practices observed on 
the trip.
Mr. Walker addressed the recent postponement of the Project Taurus 
appeal at City Council, stating that Council had delayed action until April 
13. He noted ongoing uncertainty around the specific follow-up items 
requested by Council and clarified that, because the appeal remained 
pending, Commissioners should refrain from commenting on substantive 
aspects of the project. He described Council’s interest in examining 
citywide data-center regulations, including noise standards, definitions, and

Colorado Springs New Parks Master Plan
7.A. Colorado Springs New Parks Master Plan
  Presenter:
Lonna Thelen, Parks Development Manager
CPC 2752
Parks PresentationAttachments:
Lana Thielen, Design and Development Manager for Parks, Recreation, 
and Cultural Services, delivered an extensive overview of the newly 
completed Parks System Master Plan, joined by Skyler Rorabaugh, Parks 
Director. Thielen noted that the Parks Advisory Board had just 
recommended approval and that the plan would proceed to City Council in 
October. She described the park system as more than 150 years old and 
central to community identity, health and wellness, environmental 
stewardship, and the region’s tourism economy. The system includes 
19,000 acres of parkland, 326 miles of trails, 152 developed parks, and 
more than 4 million annual visitors to cultural destinations. She emphasized 
that only 242 employees maintain the system citywide
Ms. Thielen explained the necessity of a new plan, noting significant 
population growth since the last major update in 2014, escalating 
operations and construction costs, aging infrastructure, and evolving

recreation needs, including shifting trends such as the rise of pickleball. 
She highlighted a deferred capital reinvestment backlog of approximately 
$125 million and capital replacement needs exceeding $837 million. She 
said staff had calculated a $52.2 million annual funding gap when 
accounting for current assets, deferred maintenance, and future needs. 
She also pointed out that while costs have risen, parks’ share of the 
general fund has decreased over time, with staffing levels lower today than 
in 2008 despite added responsibilities.
Ms. Thielen described the 20-month public engagement process, during 
which staff gathered more than 6,000 written comments, 3,000 survey 
responses, and extensive feedback from district-by-district statistically 
valid surveys, stakeholder groups, online mapping exercises, and pop-up 
events. She highlighted the resulting four guiding principles: preserving 
natural and cultural resources, strengthening community connections, 
creating exceptional visitor experiences, and investing in the system’s 
long-term sustainability. These principles informed seven recommended 
“first steps” for implementation, including developing a strategic funding 
plan, modernizing park classifications and PLDO, establishing an asset 
management program, and advancing key visionary projects. The plan’s 
phased approach includes setting up for success, stabilizing the system, 
and strengthening and growing, followed by an extended horizon beyond 
2040 to ensure the system remains sustainable.
Commissioners asked about restroom closures, trash receptacle 
limitations, homelessness impacts, and long-term maintenance costs. Ms. 
Thielen and Mr. Rorabaugh explained that restrooms had been closed in 
some locations due to budget constraints and confirmed that restroom 
access and comfort amenities were a major theme in public feedback. 
They noted that 25-30 percent of the Parks budget now supports 
homelessness-related cleanups. Staff described ongoing efforts to reduce 
water use through native landscape conversions, synthetic turf installation, 
and operational adjustments across golf courses, medians, and open 
spaces.
 
Commissioners also inquired about PLDO and the implications for land 
dedication in new developments. Ms. Thielen discussed level-of-service 
metrics for neighborhood and community parks, existing targets (2.5 acres 
per 1,000 residents for neighborhood parks; 3 acres per 1,000 for 
community parks), current shortfalls, and new approaches to evaluating 
experiential access, such as proximity to amenities like basketball courts 
or playgrounds. She explained that Parks staff frequently negotiate with 
developers to secure usable park land rather than leftover undevelopable 
parcels and that Commission support is sometimes needed when 
applicants resist necessary site adjustments.

Commissioner Cecil asked about alignment with the COS Creek Plan. Ms. 
Thielen confirmed that the creek corridor is represented in multiple 
sections of the master plan, including as a guiding document, a strategic 
goal, and a major visionary project developed jointly with the engineering 
division and waterway partners. She acknowledged that long-term 
maintenance obligations will increase as improvements are constructed 
and stated that the City intends to incorporate these costs into future 
funding strategies. Staff described the next phase as hiring a consultant to 
develop a comprehensive funding strategy, likely requiring a diversified 
approach across multiple revenue sources.
