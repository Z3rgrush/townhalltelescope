other power resources. Mr. Gearhart provided a brief overview of CSU’s 
resources.

utilities in Colorado. Mr. Gearhart stated CSU rates are approximately 
fifteen percent lower than other Front Range utilities.

Mr. Bidlack explained the differences in rates are not discriminatory if 
the rate differences are supported.
Councilmember Donelson asked if $.30 per kilowatt hour is during peak 
hours. Mr. Gearhart stated for Option 1, off peak is $.07 per kilowatt 
hour, peak hours are $.29 per kilowatt hour in the summer, and $.14 per 
kilowatt hour in the winter and for Option 2, it would be $.13 per kilowatt 
hour for all times. 
Councilmember Henjum expressed appreciation for the work of CSU to 
engage citizens and make compromises with the new rate case. 
Councilmember Henjum asked if the $38 dollar figure derived from cost 
causation under each rate option or is it from dividing a revenue target 
by the number of solar customers. Mr. Gearhart stated they priced to the 
average customer and they used demand charges as one vehicle to 
achieve that and they used the grid access fee for the other option.
Councilmember Henjum stated that a battery solution is needed and 
should be addressed concurrently with net metering changes. Mr. 
Gearhart stated they are already starting from a $4.5 million deficit so 
they could not evaluate the potential ability to financially support a 
battery program for solar customers until the need to collect the full cost 
of the rate was addressed.
Councilmember Donelson asked why there will be an increase for 
customers who produce more than they use. Mr. Gearhart stated the 
production happens in the daytime hours, but there is use after the sun 
goes down.
Councilmember Donelson asked why installation of solar has declined. 
Mr. Gearhart stated it may be due to the incentives which have changed 
over time.
Councilmember Donelson asked if CSU tries to address costs of service 
from all different types of customers. Mr. Gearhart confirmed they do 
implement different price structures such as Energy Wise rates across 
the board depending on customer usage.
Councilmember Leinweber asked if the Option 1 rates are due to the 
Page 12City of Colorado Springs Printed on 9/25/2026

connection to power during the peak time.
Councilmember Leinweber stated he agrees with the $1 per day for

smaller number of solar panels because they will also have to pay the
