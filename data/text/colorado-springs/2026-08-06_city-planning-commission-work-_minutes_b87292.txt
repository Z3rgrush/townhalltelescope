7.4.304 STREET IMPROVEMENTS
Attachments:
Allison Stocker explained that the request was to change the street name 
from Sugarbowl Grove to Sugarbowl Point. The road, located at 1670 
Sugar Grove northwest of Chelton Road and Delta Drive, has not yet been 
constructed and no buildings are addressed from it, meaning the name 
change carries no practical impacts. She stated the future intent of the site 
is to apply for development plans for senior affordable housing. During 
early marketing and project preparation, the name “Sugarbowl Grove” 
became associated with the future development itself, prompting the desire 
to avoid confusion, particularly for emergency services, by renaming the 
street. She confirmed that the City’s street-naming reviewers checked for 
similar or duplicate names per section 7.4.304(F) and found no conflicts. 
Ms. Stocker concluded by asking for any questions before departing the 
meeting.

7.5.525 DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS ADJUSTMENT
Attachments:
Ethan Shafer, Planner II, presented the request for a Development 
Standards Adjustment at 501 N. Wahsatch Avenue. The application seeks 
approval to allow a residential density of 13.33 dwelling units per acre in 
the R-4 zone district, where approximately 8 dwelling units per acre is 
normally permitted. The adjustment is needed to convert an existing 
commercial structure, formerly a laundromat that ceased operation around 
2017, into a residential unit. The site also includes 503 N. Wahsatch, which 
already contains a residential unit.
Mr. Shafer explained that because the prior commercial use ended more 
than a year ago, the property lost its legal nonconforming commercial 
status, and commercial uses can no longer be continued under the R-4 
zone. At least four public comments were received, primarily concerning 
parking and opposition to multifamily use in favor of retaining commercial 
activity. Mr. Shafer noted that the property currently has six parking spaces, 
which meet single-family parking standards and would be considered 
over-parked if evaluated as multifamily.
In response to commissioner questions, Mr. Shafer stated that while a 
mixed-use rezoning could theoretically resolve some issues, a rezoning 
would allow a broader range of uses that may not align with neighborhood 
preferences and would require a lengthier City Council process. Therefore, 
the development standards adjustment is the more appropriate path given 
existing conditions and public feedback.

7.5.527 USE VARIANCE
Presentation_Woodmen Valley Chapel Use Variance
CPC Minutes WVC Coffee Shop Excerpt
Attachments:
Ethan Shafer, Planner II explained that Woodmen Valley Chapel was 
seeking a use variance to allow a second principal use within their existing 
structure, a public-facing coffee shop. He stated that the chapel currently 
operates as a religious institution within the R-5 zone district, where that 
use is permitted, but introducing a commercial use requires a use variance 
or rezoning. He noted that some of the chapel’s other campuses already 
incorporate coffee shops into their ministry practices, but those are located 
in zone districts that allow such uses. In this case, approximately 1,600 
square feet of the building would be converted into the coffee shop, and no 
public comments had been received on the application.
Mr. Shafer said the planned location for the coffee shop is a portion of the 
building near the north parking lot on the first floor. The proposal includes 
an ADA ramp, a small patio, and minor exterior storefront changes, which 
would be reviewed administratively during development-plan review.
Commissioner Cecil asked whether the coffee shop would operate during 
service hours or have additional public hours. Mr. Shafer indicated that the 
applicant had not yet provided those details.
Mr. Tefertiller further noted that the site is downtown and directly adjacent to 
the form-based zone (FBZ). If it were within the FBZ, applications would 
instead be reviewed by the Downtown Review Board. He referenced 
previous discussions about rezoning the site into the FBZ and explained 
that the staff report includes additional context on why a use variance is the 
more appropriate path. Chair Hensler requested clarification about what 
uses would be allowed under the FBZ compared to the requested variance,
