Colorado City Downtown Development Authority (OCC DDA).
Citizens Chuck Murphy, Beth Salinas, Gary Salinas, and Christine VanWert 
spoke in support of the Ordinance.
Citizens Megan Morris, Timothy Trell, Adam Stephan, Chair, OCC DDA, 
Jim Richardson, member OCC DDA, Melanie Richardson, Ryan Lloyd,

Citizen Ignacio Ruiz requested citizens be more informed regarding these

tool for OCC and she will continue to work toward the right answer and 
requested information regarding what other tools are options. Kevin 
Walker, Director, Planning Department, spoke about the differences 
between DDA’s and Business Improvement District (BID).

that residential could also be turned into a business.

Attorney, City Attorney’s Office, stated there was no timely challenge of the

Councilmember Leinweber asked if there is any remaining avenue to 
contest the 2024 election results. Mr. Gloss stated not to his knowledge.

dissolve if the mill levy is not approved is a voluntary agreement or a

a binding agreement with the City.

Development while deferring the Tax Incremental Financing (TIF) start date.

Page 14City of Colorado Springs Printed on 10/20/2026

developments that are planned.

some OCC residents did not get to vote on the 2024 election. Mr. Gloss 
stated the City did not run the election, Spencer Fane did, and the ballots 
went out to qualified electors.
Councilmember Henjum stated she does not see the urgency to dissolve 
the OCC DDA and believes they should be able to put the mill levy to a vote 
so she will be voting against the proposed dissolution.
President Crow-Iverson stated she is concerned with the statistics 
regarding the 2024 election, the incorrect boundaries, and the failure of the 
mill levy in the first election. Jariah Walker, Executive Director, Colorado 
Springs Urban Renewal Authority (CSURA), provided an overview of the 
implications of the OCC DDA on the City, the data from the 2024 election, 
and the CSURA and other funding sources which were in support of a BID, 
not a DDA. He stated he reviewed the data indicating the district includes 
approximately 425 businesses, 350 commercial properties, and 700 
registered voters, concerns were noted regarding low attendance at focus 
groups and limited public awareness of the election process, further 
analysis showed that roughly eighty-five percent of ballots cast originated 
from a concentrated four‑block area within OCC, raising questions about 
the representativeness and reach of the outreach efforts. He stated that it 
was reported that only 216 ballots were returned out of 830 issued, despite 
records indicating nearly double that number of eligible electors, multiple 
property owners and businesses stated they had not received ballots or
