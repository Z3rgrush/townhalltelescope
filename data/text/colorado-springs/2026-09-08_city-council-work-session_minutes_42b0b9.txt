  Presenter:

Homelessness Response Action Plan - City Council BriefAttachments:
Aimee Cox, Chief Housing and Homelessness Officer, Housing and 
Homelessness Response Department, introduced the Homelessness 
Response Action Plan (HRAP) and provided an overview of HRAP, what 
they do, three-time horizon, foundation phase, expansion phase, 
homelessness response team, transformation and City Council 
partnership. 
Councilmember Leinweber asked if every department is on the same 
system in order to obtain the data and how City Council could assist with 
the data collection efforts. Ms. Cox stated they are not, so they have to

collect the data from each individual department and they need a person 
dedicated to these efforts which they will be requesting during the 
Budget planning.
Councilmember Henjum expressed appreciation for the leadership Ms. 
Cox has brought to the Housing and Homeless Response team. She 
also asked for an update on the efforts of the Housing and Homeless 
Response team after the passing of the Ordinance banning sleeping in 
your car. Ms. Cox gave an update on the Safe Parking program model. 
Councilmember Henjum asked for more information about HOPE COS 
best practices regarding drug use. Ms. Cox gave an overview on best 
practices and how the program has updated its operating process to 
boost safety. 
Councilmember Donelson asked when housing and homelessness were 
combined. Ms. Cox stated it has been combined since her time in this 
position. Councilmember Donelson stated he feels they should be 
separated.
Councilmember Donelson asked if the position of Chief Housing and 
Homelessness Officer should be split into two separate positions. 
Councilmember Donelson asked about the enforcement of the 
Ordinance banning living in cars. Ryan Tepley, Lieutenant (Lt.) 
Homeless Operations Team (HOT), Colorado Springs Police 
Department, stated they have been enforcing since March 24, 2026, 
when the Ordinance went into effect and there has been 335 vehicles 
camps and 28 people cited which had no families with young kids. 
Councilmember Donelson asked if the court system has been 
overwhelmed by the number of citations. Shantel Withrow, Division 
Chief, City Attorney’s Office, stated that the data takes time and they do 
not have the most updated take, but so far, the court system has not 
been overrun. 
Councilmember Leinweber asked about the status of housing vouchers. 
Ms. Cox stated there are about three thousand people on the waitlist for 
housing vouchers. 
Councilmember Leinweber asked about citing people for illegal camping. 
Ms. Withrow stated the maximum potential penalty is ten days in jail or a 
$2,500 fine.
Councilmember Leinweber asked why some people are released after 
only spending two hours in jail. Ms. Withrow stated it may be due to 
Truth in Sentencing or bond.

Agenda Planner Review 9-8-26Attachments:
Councilmember Donelson requested additional information regarding 
the Resolution of the City approving the request to accept the 
designation as the approving authority of the Southern Colorado 
Railyard Metropolitan Districts Nos 1-3 which has been annexed into the 
City limits. Kevin Walker, Director, Planning Department, explained that 
those Districts in the railyard were formed under El Paso County’s 
jurisdiction and when those parcels are annexed into the City, the City 
needs to be the managing authority.
Councilmember Donelson requested additional information regarding 
the zone change consisting of 203 acres known as portions of Ute 
Valley Park from R-E / HS-O / SS-O (Residential Estate with Hillside 
Overlay and Streamside Overlay) and BP cr / R-E / HS-O / SS-O 
(Business Park with Conditions of Record and Residential Estate with 
Hillside Overlay and Streamside Overlay) and BP cr / HS-O/ SS-O 
(Business Park with Conditions of Record  with Hillside Overlay and 
Streamside Overlay) to PK / HS-O / SS-O / AF-O (Public Park with 
Hillside Overlay, Streamside Overlay, and United States Air Force 
Academy Overlay) located at 1179 Golden Hills Road. Mr. Walker stated 
this rezone is to clean up the zoning of properties gifted to the City which 
need to be rezoned to Public Park (PK).

Caitlin Moldenhauer, Associate Attorney, City Attorney’s Office, 
presented the Ordinance amending multiple Sections of Article 3 (Code 
of Ethics) of Chapter 1 (Administration, Personnel and Finance) of the 
City Code pertaining to the Code of Ethics. She provided an overview of 
the proposed Ordinance and City Council actions.
Councilmember Donelson asked for clarification on serving on the board 
of a 501c3 as a Councilmember. 
Ms. Moldenhauer the intent of the Code and stated the change came at 
the request of City Council. 
President Crow-Iverson, asked for clarification on why only 501c3 are in 
this code change. Ms. Moldenhauer,  stated she is open to looking at all 
non-profits to be included at the request of council. 
Councilmember Casey, asked for clarification on the definition of 
“participate”. Ms. Moldenhauer expounded and clarified on the definition 
as a “direct official action”. 
Wayne Williams, Chief of Staff, asked if the proposed changes would 
apply to spouses of City covered people when the spouse is a member 
of another Board or Commission. Ms. Moldenhauer clarified that the 
provision only applies to City covered personnel.
Councilmember Leinweber asked if there would be a violation of the 
Code of Ethics if the cost of event tickets for both a City Councilmember 
and their spouse are over the $175 limit. Ms. Moldenhauer identified that 
would fall under the reasonable cost and frequency of meals and event 
tickets for elected officials.
