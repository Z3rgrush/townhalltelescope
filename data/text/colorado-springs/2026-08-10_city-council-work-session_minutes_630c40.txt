Councilmember Donelson asked who the survey went out to and how many

the management fee is a four percent fee used to pay for their time in 
managing the program.
Councilmember Henjum asked how many individuals experiencing 
homelessness are no longer in the downtown corridor. Steve Wyant, 
Homeward Pikes Peak, stated there are 221 individuals experiencing 
homelessness in the downtown area which fluctuates and there are 
approximately 100 individuals which are consistently in the downtown area.
Councilmember Henjum requested additional information regarding the 
success of the application which was made available to people for 
reporting. Mr. Wyant stated it has been working very well to this point.
Councilmember Henjum requested additional information regarding the 
costs versus returns on the Program. Ms. Gondeck stated the original 
launch costs have been approximately thirty percent of the program, but 
they anticipate future costs will be four percent.
Councilmember Leinweber asked how many people experiencing 
homelessness do not go to the Springs Rescue Mission to sleep at night. 
Mr. Rigdon stated there are twenty-five to thirty people who sleep 
downtown in doorways at night.

with mental health issues to help prevent them from going to jail. Amy Cox, 
Chief Housing and Homeless Response Officer, Housing and 
Homelessness Department, identified how they work with Colorado

7.  Staff and Appointee Reports
7.A. Overview of the City Parking Enterprise
  Presenter:  
Richard Mulledy, Public Works Director, City of Colorado Springs 
Alyssa Alt, Public Works Division Manager - Parking System Enterprise, 
City of Colorado Springs
26-306
Parking Enterprise_City Council_081026Attachments:
Alyssa Alt, Public Works Division Manager - Parking System Enterprise, 
provided an overview of the City Parking Enterprise, their mission, division 
responsibilities, budget, occupancy, 2026 Capital Improvement Projects 
(CIP), projects, and marketing.
Councilmember Donelson asked if parking enforcement and Americans 
with Disabilities Act (ADA) parking enforcement is paid out of the Parking 
System Enterprise budget. Ms. Alt confirmed parking enforcement is, but 
ADA parking enforcement is funded by Colorado Springs Police 
Department.
Councilmember Henjum requested additional information regarding the 
decrease in the 2026 budget. Richard Mulledy, Public Works Director, 
stated they changed the range of chargeable parking hours which 
decreased revenue significantly. Councilmember Henjum asked if the 
businesses support this change in hours. Ms. Alt confirmed they appear to 
support the change.
Councilmember Leinweber stated the parking space numbers are difficult 
to read from the vehicle. Ms. Alt stated they will be adding signage to the 
spaces.
Councilmember Donelson asked if there are any plans to change the 
pricing or hours this year. Ms. Alt stated there are no plans to change them 
this year, and they are collecting data to determine if any changes need to 
be made next year.

Councilmember Donelson asked if there has been an increase in 
occupancy in the parking garages. Ms. Alt stated it has remained relatively 
the same.
Councilmember Donelson requested information regarding the labor costs. 
Ms. Alt stated she will provide that information.
Councilmember Donelson asked if money is being put aside for updating 
the parking garages. Mr. Mulledy stated they have the final say where they 
are investing the revenue, they have previously invested in streetscapes, 
but they are also putting money aside for capital projects.
Councilmember Donelson asked what the cost is for building a new 
parking garage. Mr. Mulledy stated it is approximately $40 million.
Councilmember Gold asked if the electric vehicle (EV) charging stations 
are leased. Ms. Alt stated Colorado Springs Utilities (CSU) owns the City 
Administration Building parking garage EV charging stations and the City 
owns the Kiowa parking garage EV charging stations.
Councilmember Henjum asked if the Parking Enterprise would invest in 
construction of a new transit center. Mr. Mulledy stated that in order for 
them to enter into a cooperative agreement for a transit center, it would 
have to be financially sound and make revenue for the Parking Enterprise.
 
Councilmember Donelson asked if they have the data regarding how many 
people are parking downtown. Ms. Alt stated they have the data, but they 
need to find a way to analyze it.
Councilmember Donelson asked how full the garages are on a regular 
basis. Ms. Alt stated it depends on the day of the week, but generally they 
are eighty percent full.
Councilmember Casey asked if the EV charging stations are paying into 
the Parking Enterprise. Ms. Alt confirmed they are.
