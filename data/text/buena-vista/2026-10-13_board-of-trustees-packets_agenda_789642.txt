Work Session at 5:00 PM – Urban Land Institute Water Efficiency Presentation 
Mayor Libby Fay, Trustees Andrew Rice, Devin Rowe, Chris Sturm, and Cindie Swisher, Town 
Administrator Brian Berger, Planning Director Marika Kopp, Town Clerk Ed Barkowski, and Deputy 
Town Clerk Catherine Kelly were present for the work session. 
Planning Director Kopp introduced the Urban Land Institute (ULI) panel, which spent two days in Buena 
Vista reviewing local development and landscaping practices and gathering community input. Institute 
Director Victoria Oestreich explained that ULI’s statewide initiative, supported by a Colorado Water 
Plan grant, brings together public- and private-sector professionals to develop recommendations for 
water-wise land use. The panel focused on implementing the Town’s Water Conservation and 
Efficiency Plan, updating landscape regulations and the planting guide, and identifying water-
management strategies. 
The panel recommended a locally tailored landscaping approach that balances aesthetics, ecological 
function, and reduced irrigation. Suggestions included partnering with local nurseries, CSU Extension, 
and native-plant organizations; developing a “BV Scape” campaign and demonstration gardens; 
training landscape professionals; and considering a conservation coordinator. Panelists also 
emphasized accessible public education through online resources, social media, videos, and schools, 
along with clearer guidance for developers on water supply, allocation, expectations, and compliance 
options. 
Drawing on Castle Rock’s experience, panelists discussed consistent and flexible development 
standards, meaningful incentives, and comprehensive landscape and irrigation criteria. Potential tools 
included smart controllers, flow sensors, and tailored plant lists. They also addressed conservation-
based water rates and tap fees, drought-response planning with defined stages and restrictions, and 
the staff capacity needed for implementation and enforcement. 
Panelists said community members expressed uncertainty about Buena Vista’s water sources, storage, 
supply, and the reasons for conservation. Trustees discussed how to communicate the value of water 
efficiency, measure progress, and provide practical turf-replacement resources. Suggestions included 
Resource Central’s Garden in a Box and lawn-removal programs, CSU Extension, Master Gardener 
programs, and locally tailored landscape templates. Panelists advised that incentives should be paired 
with guidance on successful replacement and maintenance. Kopp emphasized connecting residents, 
businesses, and developers with practical guidance alongside regulatory changes. Trustees and staff

Co-Response Update (Audio Time: 00:11:47) 
Sarah Briggs, Program Manager for the Chaffee County Co-Response Program, reported that since 
launching in September 2025, the program has served 159 clients through 152 dispatches and 
documented 591 contacts. Follow-up and case management accounted for 64% of calls. The program 
diverted six individuals from jail, 20 from the emergency department, and 15 from other formal 
interventions. Of 30 clients with case-management goals, 24 achieved them. 
A second clinician-deputy team is expected to begin operating by year-end. The program is expanding 
partner training through a 30-hour Crisis Response Intervention Team course in November and 
developing a community threat assessment team focused on prevention and coordinated responses. 
Coverage will expand to six days per week with longer daily hours, while the program works toward 
seven-day coverage. 
Funding includes approximately $301,000 in recurring state support and a one-time $562,000 Attorney 
General’s Office grant for the second team and two years of startup costs. Briggs cautioned that state 
funding may decline and encouraged municipalities to plan for long-term sustainability, noting that 
state support covers only part of salaries and operating costs. Town Administrator Berger reported 
2026 contributions of $32,000 from Buena Vista, $25,000 from Salida, and $30,000 from Poncha 
Springs; the County contribution was unconfirmed. Trustees praised the program and discussed the 
funding gap. Trustee Rice noted Buena Vista’s proposed 2027 contribution of $32,500 and suggested 
considering the program as a municipal service. 
Water Rate Study Update (Audio Time: 00:35:24) 
Paul Young, Vice President and Principal Owner of Swiftwater Solutions, LLC, provided an update on 
the Town’s water rate study. The firm has delivered five technical memoranda, with a sixth expected 
by week’s end. The financial tool is nearing completion, with a demonstration planned for an October 
Board meeting, and the final report is expected around October 9. Young explained that the Town has 
22 customer classifications but uses one rate structure, so revenue from each group may not reflect its 
water use or the costs it places on the system. The study will evaluate alternative, conservation-based 
rates and possible consolidation of customer classes. It distinguishes recurring revenue from grants, 
loans, and tap fees. Young noted that conservation and drought have affected water use and revenue, 
and agreed to clarify why audited figures show a decline in recurring revenue from 2024 to 2025 
compared with the Town’s budget. User charges totaled approximately $1.5 million in 2025. 
Young said current rates generally cover near-term operations but may not support reserve allocations, 
debt service, planned capital improvements, and desired fund balances. The Water Enterprise Fund 
has approximately $3 million in reserves, which could decline due to operating deficits and future 
capital needs. He also reviewed the Town’s three water-system loans and their outstanding balances. 
The financial tool will help staff evaluate rates, budgets, capital needs, growth, and water use. Young 
demonstrated an illustrative scenario in which a conservation-based rate structure reduced projected 
user-charge revenue from approximately $1.46 million to $1.3 million, emphasizing that it was not the

Prop 123 Commitment (Audio Time: 02:05:03) 
Planning Director Marika Kopp presented Resolution No. 65, which would close out the Town’s current 
three-year Proposition 123 commitment and authorize sharing excess housing credits regionally. 
Buena Vista committed to 26 qualifying units and achieved 47 through The Crossing apartments (33 
units) and subdivision (14 units). Because credits cannot carry forward, Kopp recommended 
transferring six excess credits to Salida, which is six units short. The proposed MOU would establish a 
credit-sharing framework among Buena Vista, Salida, Poncha Springs, Chaffee County, and the Chaffee 
Housing Authority. Kopp emphasized that the transfer would not affect Buena Vista’s compliance. She 
also explained that recent DOLA funding supported development review software, with additional 
funds for a local housing action plan and consultant assistance to identify ways to advance affordable 
housing.

Planning Department Monthly Report 
 
The Planning and Zoning Department continues to see steady development activity across a variety of 
application types, reflecting ongoing investment and interest in Buena Vista. During the reporting 
period of September 2 through October 7, 2026 (attached for the Board’s reference), the Department 
received 19 planning and zoning application reviews, including residential and commercial building 
authorizations, subdivisions, site plans, zoning applications, and sign permits. Of those, six have been

the project cost is also required.
