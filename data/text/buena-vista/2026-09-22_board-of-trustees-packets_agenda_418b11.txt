Urban Land Institute Water Wise Technical Assistance Panel Presentation 
Summary: The purpose of this worksession item is to provide the Board of Trustees with the 
recommendations of the Urban Land Institute (ULI) Water Wise Technical Assistance Panel following its 
work with the Town of Buena Vista on September 21–22, 2026. 
The Town is partnering with ULI to receive outside technical guidance on practical strategies to 
strengthen water conservation and water-efficient land use practices in Buena Vista. The effort builds 
upon the Town’s ongoing Waterwise Landscape planning, the recent adoption of the Water 
Conservation Efficiency Plan and broader goal of reducing community water use by 15% over the next 
ten years while accounting for continued community growth.

ackground: The ULI Technical Assistance Roundtable brings together professionals with expertise in 
development, sustainable landscaping, landscape architecture, municipal water conservation, public 
education, and water utility planning. The panel includes: 
• Amy Cara, Managing Principal, East West Partners – development and large-scale/resort
development
• Deryn Davidson, Extension State Specialist for Sustainable Landscapes, Colorado State
University – sustainable landscaping and planting guidance
• Brian Levitt, CEO, NAVA Real Estate – development and sustainability

• Rick Schultz, Water Manager, City of Castle Rock – municipal water conservation and public

• Suzanne Serna, Senior Associate/Landscape Architect, Stantec – landscape architecture and
planting guidance
Prior to arriving in Buena Vista, the panelists were provided background information regarding the 
Town, its water system and development context, existing conservation efforts, and the key questions 
the Town is seeking assistance in addressing. See the attached briefing book for more information.  
On September 21, Town staff and Swiftwater will brief the panel on Buena Vista’s water system, 
planning and development context, existing conservation initiatives, and implementation challenges. 
The panel will also participate in a guided community tour highlighting examples of existing 
development and landscaping conditions, successful practices, and areas of opportunity. 
On September 22, the panel will lead a Water Wise Technical Assistance Roundtable with Town staff 
and a broad group of local and regional stakeholders, including development and real estate 
professionals, business owners, advisory board members, residents, community partners, and 
technical experts. The roundtable is intended to provide the panel with local perspectives on both 
opportunities and practical barriers to implementation. 
Following the roundtable, the ULI panel will hold a working session to synthesize what it has learned 
and develop recommendations for the Town. 
Areas of Focus: The Town has asked the panel to consider practical strategies that could help advance 
Buena Vista’s water conservation goals, with particular attention to: 
• Water-efficient landscaping and irrigation practices;
• Potential updates to development standards and the Town’s planting guide;
• Education and technical assistance for residents, developers, builders, and landscape 
professionals;
• Consider local capacity and implementation challenges, including limited staff, plant availability 
and access to qualified landscape professionals that can support large scale development; and
• Strategies that improve the community’s long-term resilience to drought, changing snowpack, 
and future growth. 
T

steps. They will help inform future staff work and Board discussions regarding water conservation 
policies, programs, development standards, and potential code amendments. The presentation itself 
does not constitute adoption of any new Town policy or regulation.

Nestled in the Upper Arkansas River Valley at the foot of Colorado's Collegiate Peaks,
Buena Vista is a vibrant mountain community where natural beauty, outdoor
recreation, and small-town character define daily life. Cottonwood Creek flows
through the center of town before joining the Arkansas River, creating the landscapes,
recreational opportunities, and water resources that have shaped Buena Vista's
history and continue to support its future.
Originally established as a ranching and railroad community, Buena Vista has evolved
into one of Colorado's premier outdoor recreation destinations. Residents and visitors
are drawn to the area's whitewater, hiking, mountain biking, fishing, camping, and
scenic beauty, making tourism a cornerstone of the local economy. At the same time,
Buena Vista remains committed to preserving the welcoming, connected character
that makes it a desirable place to live, work, and raise a family.
Like many mountain communities, Buena Vista has experienced steady growth over
the past decade. New residents, businesses, and housing have strengthened the local
economy while increasing demand for public infrastructure and finite natural
resources. Recognizing these pressures, the Town has made water stewardship a
central component of its long-term planning, ensuring that future growth supports
both the community and the natural environment on which it depends. Water is not
simply a utility, it is fundamental to Buena Vista's economy, recreation, community
character, and quality of life.

About Buena Vista
Community Snapshot
Although Cottonwood Creek flows through the heart of town and the Arkansas
River borders the community, the amount of water available for municipal use is
finite. Colorado's water rights system, combined with continued population
growth, increasing development, and seasonal irrigation demands, requires the
Town to carefully balance today's needs with tomorrow's opportunities.
Over the past several years, Buena Vista has invested significantly in its water
future by expanding treatment capacity, developing additional groundwater
supplies, acquiring new water rights, and strengthening system reliability. These
investments provide a solid foundation, but infrastructure alone cannot meet
future needs. Long-term water security will also depend on reducing demand
through thoughtful planning, efficient landscapes, and responsible water use.
Outdoor irrigation remains the largest driver of seasonal water demand, making
landscape design and water-efficient development some of the most effective
opportunities for conservation. By reducing peak demand today, Buena Vista
can better preserve existing water supplies, support responsible growth, and
delay the need for costly future infrastructure investments.
Our Water Challenge
Buena Vista’s water demand has shown a steady
increase to date, with water service tap growth
exceeding population growth. This pattern may
be linked to factors such as second-home
ownership, tourism, and post-pandemic
migration, though it is unclear whether the trend
will continue. Current water rights and the
upgraded WTP can reliably meet present day
demands, but high summer irrigation use, system
water losses, and peak day demands underscore
ongoing vulnerability. Without implementation of
conservation measures, evaluation of water
rights, and expanded supply planning, demand
could exceed firm water rights by the 2050s,
making proactive management essential to
sustain future growth and reliability.

Top Priorities of WCEP
Buena Vista's Water Allocation Policy establishes a framework for allocating available
water resources in a manner that aligns with the Town's Comprehensive Plan, Water
Resources Master Plan, and community priorities. The policy is reviewed annually to
respond to changing water resources, development trends, and community needs while
promoting measured, sustainable growth.
Water Allocation Policy 
Reduce Outdoor Water Demand
 Landscape water budgets, water-wise planting, efficient irrigation, and reducing high-water-
use/nonfunctional turf.
Make Water Efficiency Part of Development
 Update codes and development standards so conservation and water-efficient design are
incorporated from the beginning rather than addressed after development occurs.
Make Conservation Practical & Understandable
 Give residents, HOAs, developers, and staff usable tools, including an updated Planting
Guide, clear standards, education, and incentives to actually implement water-wise
practices.
Buena Vista already has several water-wise principles embedded in its landscaping
code. The Town's standards are intended in part to conserve water and currently limit
high-water-use landscaping to 50% of landscaped area and high-water-use turf to 30%.
The Code also restricts nonfunctional turf in commercial, institutional, industrial,
HOA/common areas, rights-of-way and other areas consistent with recent State
requirements. Existing healthy vegetation can count toward landscaping requirements,
invasive species are prohibited, and drip or other low-volume irrigation is required for
non-turf areas when an irrigation system is installed.
Building on this foundation, the Town is looking to update its Buena Vista Planting
Guide, which is incorporated into the development standards and helps determine which
plant materials meet Town landscaping requirements. The goal is to create a more
comprehensive and practical resource that better reflects Buena Vista's climate, water
conservation goals, and wildfire resilience; aligns with current Colorado State University
Extension recommendations and evolving State landscaping and wildfire requirements;
and provides clear guidance for both residents and development applicants. As the
Town implements its 2025 Water Conservation and Efficiency Plan and works toward a
15% reduction in overall water demand over the next decade, the Town is seeking
recommendations from ULI panelists on effective approaches, partnerships, and
implementation strategies for integrating the Planting Guide, landscaping code,
irrigation standards, and educational resources into a cohesive water-wise framework
that can be effectively implemented and maintained over time.

Sangre de Cristo Electric Association Tree Permit Presentation (Audio Time: 00:41:42) 
Jon Beyer, CEO of Sangre de Cristo Electric Association (SDCEA), presented a phased vegetation-
management plan addressing wildfire and electrical-line risks within Town limits. The revised 
plan narrows the proposed clearance from 15 feet to eight feet on either side of primary lines, 
focuses on trees in the public right-of-way and high-risk trees on private property, and begins 
with a pilot project on South Pleasant Avenue involving approximately 15 trees. Work is 
planned for November, followed by additional work in the southwest portion of Town this 
winter, with affected property owners receiving notice and SDCEA conducting community 
outreach. 
 
Trustees discussed public communication, tree replacement opportunities, stump removal, 
trimming practices, landscaping impacts, and coordination with the Tree Advisory Board. Beyer 
stated that SDCEA would provide tree-selection information, continue working with the Tree 
Advisory Board, grind stumps below grade and reseed affected areas, and prioritize trimming 
over removal for high-risk trees outside the eight-foot clearance. He noted that SDCEA’s tree 
inventory was completed with a licensed arborist and includes species-specific information. 
 
Trustees generally viewed the revised proposal as more narrowly tailored than previous plans 
and supported using the South Pleasant project as a pilot before expanding the work. Beyer 
stated that Town staff would inspect the pilot project after completion, after which SDCEA 
would seek approval for the remainder of the southwest section and subsequently evaluate 
other areas of Town.

Public Hearing – 2027 Town of Buena Vista Budget (Audio Time: 00:58:48) 
Mayor Fay opened the Public Hearing, and Treasurer Puckett reviewed updates to the proposed 
2027 budget. Property and liability insurance increased 5%, below the projected 8%, but the 
savings were offset by the State’s discontinuation of marijuana tax distributions, reducing Town 
revenue by approximately $14,000. The General, Capital Improvement, Water, and Airport 
funds remain in deficit at this stage, but Puckett emphasized that the Town will adopt a 
structurally balanced budget. 
 
Puckett clarified that the proposed Community Center window and exterior door replacement 
addresses security, functionality, and aging infrastructure, noting that more than $43,000 had 
been previously invested in ADA improvements to the entrances. Trustees also discussed 
employee health benefits, with the Town covering 90% of employee-only premiums and 75% 
when dependents are included. Administrator Berger noted the 2025 insurance provider 
change saved approximately $180,000. Trustee Rosenoer requested utilization data for the 
proposed $35,000 outdoor fitness equipment; Recreation Director Basford reported significant 
anecdotal use but no hard usage data and noted that grants may supplement the project. 
 
Puckett emphasized maintaining the $400,000 annual General Fund transfer to the Capital 
Improvement Fund. Trustee Rice stressed validating project cost estimates so speculative 
amounts do not displace higher-priority projects. Puckett noted that capital requests exceed 
available funding by approximately $53,000 and priorities may be revisited as the budget is 
finalized. 
 
Trustees discussed funding for the Chaffee County Co-Response Program. Puckett confirmed 
Buena Vista’s proposed contribution remains $32,500, alongside contributions from other 
municipalities and a $500,000 grant. Rosenoer suggested treating the program as a municipal 
service and requested information on other municipalities’ contributions. Staff will provide 
additional information at the next meeting.

Law Enforcement Intergovernmental Agreements (Audio Time: 01:22:41) 
Police Chief Morgan reviewed proposed updates to Intergovernmental Agreements for the 
regional combined tactical team and mutual aid with the City of Salida and Chaffee County. The 
tactical team agreement is being restructured following changes within the Sheriff’s Office and 
Salida’s developing their own response team; Buena Vista will remain a participating agency. 
The agreements establish legal protections, mutual-aid procedures, and cost-sharing 
responsibilities while providing access to specialized equipment and personnel. 
 
Morgan noted that Buena Vista currently has no officer assigned to the tactical team and 
incurred little or no expense last year. The Town previously contributed approximately $40,000 
toward the armored vehicle, which will be owned by Chaffee County but remain available to 
participating agencies. Trustees discussed the benefits of the regional arrangement given the 
Town’s limited staffing and the costs and training required to operate an independent team. 
Trustee Rowe asked about concerns regarding police militarization. Morgan explained that the 
team provides specialized non-lethal resources, including armored vehicles, chemical 
munitions, robots, and specialty impact munitions, to help resolve barricade and hostage 
situations while reducing risks to officers and the public. The proposed resolutions address 
separate agreements with the Chaffee County Sheriff’s Office and City of Salida.
