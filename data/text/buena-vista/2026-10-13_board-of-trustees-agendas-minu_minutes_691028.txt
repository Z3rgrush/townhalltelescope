Supplemental Memo, BVHS Irrigation Well - (audio: 48:40:00) 
Trustee Sturm recused himself because his employer awarded the project grant. Town Administrator 
Berger presented a proposed contribution of up to $83,000 from the Water Enterprise Fund toward 
the approximately $232,120 irrigation well project. The Colorado Water Conservation Board awarded a 
$106,560 grant, leaving $125,560 in matching funds, proposed to be split between the School District 
(approximately $42,700) and the Town (up to $83,000). The project would reduce demand on the 
Town’s treated water system and could provide an emergency backup water source. Trustee Rice 
emphasized that the contribution would advance planning, with final design and construction returning 
to the Board. 
 
Superintendent Lisa Yates said the School District remained interested but had not formally approved 
its share. Trustees discussed a formal agreement for emergency backup use, potential utility savings, 
and the need for state review and testing before the well could be used as a potable backup. Treasurer 
Puckett reported a projected year-end 2027 Water Enterprise Fund balance of nearly $2.8 million.

Dogs Running-At-Large Violation Fine Increase - (audio: 1:31:19) 
Police Chief Morgan reviewed the proposed ordinance increasing fines for dogs running at large. 
Following public concerns and a June work session with Code Enforcement, staff recommended fines 
of $50 for a first violation, $100 for a second, and $150 for a third or subsequent violation within a 
three-year period. Morgan said Code Enforcement Officers Bryans and Taylor had compared fines in 
other municipalities and that higher penalties could deter violations. Berger noted that the $25 fine 
had been in place since 1998. 
 
Trustees discussed citation procedures and dog impoundment. Morgan explained that stray dogs are 
typically impounded, while impoundment from private property requires meeting specific legal criteria. 
The proposed ordinance specifies minimum fines for running-at-large violations and allows the 
Municipal Judge to impose higher penalties.

Chaffee County Fire Protection District Local Ballot Issue 6A Support - (audio: 01:35:32) 
Treasurer Puckett presented Resolution No. 71 supporting Local Ballot Issue 6A, which would include 
all Town property in the Chaffee County Fire Protection District. Town Attorney Parker explained that 
the Colorado Fair Campaign Practices Act permits the Board to express its position through one 
resolution, publicized through the Town’s usual means for publishing resolutions. 
 
Puckett explained that inclusion would give Town residents a voice in District governance and fire 
protection decisions. To offset the District’s current 3.936-mill levy, the Town had previously adopted 
Resolution No. 51, committing to reduce its own mill levy by the same amount. Trustees supported 
giving residents a say in District fees and taxes. Puckett clarified that impact fees vary by project and 
include exemptions for low- and moderate-income housing and affordable employee housing. Berger 
noted that the fee policy was relatively new and that, absent the ballot measure, the Town would likely 
be discussing a request from the District to incorporate the fee. 
 
MOTION NO. 08: 
MOVE TO ADOPT RESOLUTION NO. 2026-71, SUPPORTING LOCAL BALLOT ISSUE 6A, INCLUSION OF 
ALL PROPERTY IN THE TOWN INTO THE CHAFFEE COUNTY FIRE PROTECTION DISTRICT
