A. Public Hearing: Roof replacement - Historic St. Rose of Lima/ Shining Mountains Montessori (343 
Hwy 24)  
 
Carly gave a staff presentation. The church is not locally landmarked but it is designated on the State 
Register of Historic Places, which made it automatically eligible for local landmarking. Thus, alterations 
are subject to HPC review.  
 
Ben Buschur, Town of BV Public Works Department, presented on the need for the shingle’s 
replacement due to wear, water leaking and pest issues.  
 
John O’Brien asked if the new building code would prohibit replacement with cedar shake shingles and 
Marika Kopp confirmed that cedar shake shingles are now prohibited.  
 
Melanie Roth shared that she had spoken with the State Historical Fund (History Colorado) who 
recommended suitable replacement materials. Melanie will share that information with Ben via email. 
She offered to provide other forms of assistance.   
 
Bill made and Jo seconded a motion to deem that the property is eligible for local landmarking based 
upon the following criteria:  
 
Section 19-7(b) – the property is deemed to qualify because it is listed on the state register for historic 
places 
 
Section 19-7: 
 
(a) – The property exemplifies specific elements of an architectural style or period; and 
(i) – The property exemplifies cultural, political, economic, or social heritage of the community. 
 
The motion carried unanimously. 
 
The HPC then discussed the appropriateness of the alterations. Melanie suggested that the Town 
explore the composite cedar replacement material with architect Barb Darden. John and Marika 
expressed concern that if we delay the project by too long, the roof may not be replaced at all due to 
the fact that the cost is currently budgeted but could be removed from the budget in the coming year. 
Katy referred to the Secretary of Interior Standards, which would discourage removal of the cedar shake 
shingles; however, since the code prohibits replacement with like materials, the SOI Standards say that 
the shingles should attempt to match the old shingles in design, color, texture, and other visual 
qualities. Rick feels that the asphalt shingles can satisfy those elements.  
 
John made and Jo seconded a motion to approve the alteration with the following recommendations: i) 
consider an alternate shingle product that would more closely match the existing cedar, ii) consult with 
a structural engineer and preservation architect, iii) pursue a State Historical Fund grant to cover the 
cost of the replacement. The motion carried unanimously. 
 
B. Pearl Update from Grant Bryans 
Grant gave an update on the shoring/stabilizing efforts for the Pearl. There is a lot of water damage on 
the theatre side of the building where the building goes from three to two stories. The parapet wall may 
be beyond repair. Depending on the approach to shoring (e.g., through the roof), more hazardous 
materials abatement may be necessary. The Town has expended very little cash on the project (only to 
pay for fencing) but has devoted considerable staff time. Grant is optimistic that the building will be 
stabilized and the Town will step away from the project by end of the year. Mary Therese asked the HPC 
to consider spearheading a working group to support the Groy’s in their ongoing effort to rehabilitate 
The Pearl.
