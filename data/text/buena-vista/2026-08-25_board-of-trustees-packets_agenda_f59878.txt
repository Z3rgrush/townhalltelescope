RE:   Work session: Housing Needs Assessment  
 
 
Background: Senate Bill 24-174 requires subject jurisdictions, including the Town of Buena Vista, to 
complete a Housing Needs Assessment (HNA) by the end of 2026. The HNA evaluates existing and 
projected housing needs by analyzing demographic and economic trends, housing inventory, market 
conditions, development constraints, and community input. Rather than simply estimating future 
housing demand, the HNA identifies where housing needs are currently unmet and estimates the types 
and quantities of housing needed to support residents and employees across a range of income levels 
and life stages. 
 
The HNA is being completed as a regional effort in partnership with Chaffee County, the City of Salida, 
and the Town of Poncha Springs. Earlier this year, the regional review committee selected Root Policy 
Research to prepare the assessment. Since then, the project team has held monthly coordination 
meetings and conducted extensive public outreach, including community events, participation at 
existing local events, and a regional survey that received approximately 850 responses. 
 
Root Policy Research has now completed its initial analysis and will present the preliminary findings to 
the Board of Trustees. As part of the state-required process, the draft HNA must be presented to each 
participating governing body for review and feedback before it is finalized and submitted to the 
Colorado Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) for compliance review and approval. 
 
The HNA is intended to provide a shared understanding of the region's housing needs and will help 
inform future housing policy, programs, and implementation strategies. 
 
Requested Direction from the Board: 
Staff is requesting that the Board provide feedback on the preliminary findings and identify any 
questions, areas requiring clarification, or topics the consultant should further evaluate or address 
prior to finalization of the HNA. 
 
No formal action is requested at this time.

Methodology and Terminology
• Data sources: The report (and presentation) rely on the U.S. Census Bureau’s American Community Survey (ACS) 
data and state demographer estimates as well as market sources such as CoStar, Zillow Analytics, and Assessor sale 
reports. 
• Geography: participating jurisdictions are Chaffee County, Buena Vista, Poncha Springs, and Salida. 
• Affordability: Industry standards consider housing 
affordable when the housing payment—the rent or 
mortgage payment plus taxes and utilities—consumes 
30% or less of a household’s gross income. 
• Area Median Income (AMI): The HNA uses both household income ranges and “Area Median Income (AMI).” AMI is 
a measure of income based on the median, or middle income household. Housing programs base program 
eligibility on income limits that are represented as percentages of AMI (published by HUD by region and household 
size). The 2026 100% AMI for a 2-person household is $88,900.

Work Session at 6:00 PM – Budget Presentation, Department Heads 
Mayor Fay and Town Treasurer Phillip Puckett introduced the work session, noting that 
department heads would present their priorities and budget implications for 2027 in 
preparation for the Town’s draft budget. 
 
Planning Director Marika Kopp began, noting that the department is currently structured with 
three full-time positions and shared administrative support with Recreation, but would benefit 
significantly from adding a Planner II to address workload associated with major development 
reviews, long-range planning, housing initiatives, state requirements, and process 
improvements. Kopp highlighted major development projects, a total of $194,000 in DOLA 
grants, GovWell implementation, regional partnerships, and identified staffing capacity as the 
department’s primary challenge. 
 
Public Works Director Shawn Williams reviewed the department’s 2026 accomplishments and 
2027 priorities, highlighting the water plant project, well work, completion of the water 
conservation and efficiency plan, updated master drainage plan, and ongoing water-rate 
analysis. Priorities include infrastructure, sidewalks and streets, parks and irrigation, and 
incorporating water conservation into capital planning. 
 
Recreation Director Shane Basford reviewed programs, events, facilities, and capital projects, 
highlighting Whipple Bridge, river safety and Rodeo Grounds improvements, strong youth-
program participation, expanded culinary programming, and numerous special events. He 
identified staff turnover as the department’s primary challenge and cited sustainable funding 
for capital projects, increased facility demand, water needs, staff retention and training, and 
facility maintenance as priorities. He also requested a staff vehicle and noted that no new 
facilities were proposed for 2027.

operations and 17,000 visitors and its role in wildland fire, medevac, and search-and-rescue 
response. He emphasized that continued aircraft activity and hangar development could 
support future FAA and CDOT investment and aviation-related economic activity. Airport 
Manager Chandra Swanson presented the airport’s 2027 priorities of protecting existing assets,

As the new school year begins, drivers will notice new SCHOOL ZONE signs and 
ﬂashing lights along US Highway 24 through Buena Vista. 
Following a traffic and school zone study conducted by the Colorado Department of 
Transportation (CDOT), a section of US 24 through town has officially been designated 
as a school zone. 
When the Lights Are Flashing, the Speed Limit is 25 MPH 
The normal speed limit on US 24 through town is 35 mph. When the school zone lights 
are ﬂashing, the speed limit is reduced to 25 mph. 
The lights will be activated during times when students are most likely to be walking and 
crossing US 24, including: 
• Before school 
• During the school lunch period 
• When school lets out 
Local law enforcement will be conducting RADAR and LiDAR speed 
enforcement within the school zone and will take enforcement action when necessary. 
Where is the School Zone? 
  
57
 
• Northbound US 24: The reduced-speed school zone begins near Brown Dog 
Coffee and ends near The Oasis, across from McPhelemy Park. 
• Southbound US 24: The reduced-speed school zone begins near The Oasis, 
across from McPhelemy Park, and ends near Oak Street. 
Drivers should watch for the ﬂashing school zone signs rather than relying solely on 
these landmarks. 
Improving Safety Along US 24 
The new school zone is part of a larger effort by the Town, CDOT, local schools, and law 
enforcement to improve pedestrian safety along the US 24 corridor. 
The recent study also evaluated pedestrian crossings, signage, and other potential 
safety improvements. Additional work under consideration includes improvements near 
the Oak Street crossing, seasonal pedestrian-safety measures during busy summer 
months and festivals, and longer-term planning for the US 24 corridor through 
downtown Buena Vista. 
These improvements will take time, but the new school zone is an important step that 
can make a difference immediately. 
Please Slow Down and Help Keep Our Kids Safe 
Many Buena Vista students walk, bike, and cross US 24 as part of their school day. As 
everyone adjusts to the new school zone, please pay close attention to the ﬂashing 
signs and remember: 
FLASHING LIGHTS = 25 MPH 
A few extra seconds behind the wheel can help make the route to and from school safer 
for everyone. 
Questions about the new school zone may be directed to the Town of Buena Vista.
