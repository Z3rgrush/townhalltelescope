Work Session at 6:00 PM - Short-Term Rentals, Cap Discussion 
Planning Director Marika Kopp opened a work session on proposed short-term rental (STR) 
license cap percentages by zoning district, following the Board's previous review of the STR 
Committee's recommendations. She presented updated housing unit counts by zoning district, 
refined to better account for accessory dwelling units (ADUs) and multifamily properties, and 
explained revisions to the zoning analysis, including separate Old Town overlay categories and 
clarification that the Highway Commercial district contains no active residential zoning and 
currently allows no STRs. 
 
The Board discussed methodologies for establishing percentage-based STR caps, including 
whether caps should be based solely on zoning districts or the Town's overall housing stock. 
Staff clarified that the proposed caps would apply only to non-primary residence STRs, while 
owner-occupied STRs would remain exempt. Trustees also supported maintaining a 0% STR cap 
within the Highway Commercial district and requested additional analysis showing how various 
cap scenarios would affect the Town's overall STR inventory. 
 
Trustees debated appropriate STR caps for residential zoning districts, weighing neighborhood 
character, housing availability, tourism, and private property rights. Discussion included 
whether R1 neighborhoods should have little or no future non-owner-occupied STRs and 
whether Old Town and mixed-use districts should accommodate higher concentrations because 
of their proximity to downtown amenities. Individual trustees expressed support for a range of 
cap percentages, including R1 limits between 0% and 6%. 
 
Staff recommended preparing comparison tables illustrating multiple STR cap options for 
continued discussion. Trustee Sturm encouraged fellow Trustees to review the Town's zoning 
map in advance of future STR discussions. Kopp concluded by noting that the Comprehensive 
Plan reflects significant public concern regarding STR concentration and housing availability and 
echoed Town Treasurer Phillip Puckett's observation that additional public input would occur 
during the ordinance review process. The Board directed staff to prepare revised cap scenarios 
for subsequent meetings, after which Mayor Fay called a brief recess.

assumed management of the Midland Apartments in late April, with lease-up efforts on track to 
achieve 95% occupancy by year-end. 
 
Curgus reported that the regional housing inventory for Colorado's Proposition 123 indicates 
participating communities are expected to exceed their required housing commitment by 
approximately 53 units, crediting strong collaboration among local governments and housing 
partners. She also discussed housing navigation services for vulnerable populations and noted 
that declining grant funding for emergency and transitional housing is prompting the Housing 
Authority to pursue new funding sources and expand temporary housing options. 
 
Responding to Board questions, Curgus said the Midland Apartments remain on track to meet 
state occupancy requirements and that the Crossing Apartments are expected to see strong 
demand due to their range of affordability levels. She added that increased housing availability 
has begun placing modest downward pressure on rents and home prices, but emphasized that 
the county continues to face a significant shortage of affordable housing, particularly for 
households earning below 80% of Area Median Income.

2027 Budget Presentation (Audio Time: 02:49:55) 
Finance Director Phillip Puckett kicked off the Town's 2027 budget process by outlining the 
budget schedule through anticipated adoption in October. He reiterated the Town's 
commitment to a structurally balanced budget, with recurring revenues funding operations and 
one-time fund balance reserved for capital and approved carryover projects. 
 
Puckett presented preliminary revenue assumptions, including 1% sales tax growth and 5% 
property tax growth, while assuming the current fire protection funding model remains in place 
pending the Chaffee Fire District ballot measure. He also cautioned against overreacting to 
monthly sales tax fluctuations, noting that inflation has largely offset recent revenue growth 
and kept the Town's purchasing power relatively flat. 
 
Staff priorities for 2027 include maintaining competitive employee compensation, funding 
capital maintenance, limiting staffing increases while addressing targeted operational needs, 
and continuing community support funding subject to Board direction. Puckett also reviewed 
the differing financial structures of the General Fund and Enterprise Funds, highlighting the 
Airport Enterprise Fund's unique financial constraints and the Water Enterprise Fund's ability to 
adjust rates to support operations and capital improvements. 
 
Trustees discussed the Airport Enterprise Fund's long-term financial outlook, requested future 
comparisons with peer communities, and asked about the Town's remote sales tax reporting, 
which Puckett explained includes taxes remitted by businesses located outside Town limits. The 
Board also discussed the revised budget calendar, with Puckett recommending the earlier 
schedule continue in future years if successful.

them and really helps our schedule.

team. We met with the CCSO yesterday on this topic. We are still fully involved in the tactical

joint press release about this with us to alleviate any confusion. All that was done was a review 
and re-signing of the IGA related to the tactical team. The old one was signed in 2023, and now 
there are some new individuals involved, to include a new sheriff, so it was just time to update 
and review the agreement. We also initiated a similar process with our IGA with the Colorado 
Department of Corrections, as discussed at our tabletop exercise. 
 
Training: 
• We are working on partnering with co-response to build a regional threat assessment team. As 
part of this, we hosted a training taught by the University of Boulder, Violence Prevention 
Project, on targeted violence—lethality assessments. At the state level, all local law enforcement 
will be implementing assessments like this on all domestic violence cases beginning in July of 
2027.  
• We hosted a school violence tabletop exercise on July 15th and had great participation from our 
community partners, including EMS, Department of Corrections, CCSO, the school district, etc. 
• We joined the school district on August 4th to prepare for the upcoming school year and our law 
enforcement partnership with the school. We also participated with a tabletop exercise with the 
school. 
• Upcoming: Conducting continuing skills and mandated POST trainings. Officers will be going 
through some reality-based training exercises in the coming weeks.

Data Center Moratorium  
Staff continues to evaluate potential approaches for regulating data centers following the adoption of 
the temporary moratorium. Staff has coordinated with regional partners to understand how 
neighboring jurisdictions are responding to this emerging land use. Chaffee County currently prohibits 
data centers in all zoning districts, while the City of Salida adopted a 12-month moratorium on August 
4, 2026, to allow time to develop regulations. Salida anticipates relying heavily on any state legislation 
adopted during the next legislative session as the foundation for its regulations, supplemented by local 
standards where appropriate. Staff has also contacted Gunnison County and the City of Leadville for 
information on their current approaches and plans to attend the Colorado Chapter of the American

pressure in Buena Vista, staff's objective is to develop a proactive regulatory framework that addresses 
potential land use, infrastructure, utility, and community compatibility impacts before proposals are

Drought Monitoring: 
The Public Works and Water Department continues to actively monitor drought conditions and key water 
system indicators to ensure a reliable and sustainable water supply for the Town. In the month of July, river, 
creek and stream calls showed vulnerabilities clearly, identifying water rights stream calls as a clear trigger for 
restricting outside use water. July also showed how a few days of summer rain provides increased basin, 
stream and river flows allowing less restrictive calls. 
Current monitoring activities include: 
• Stream Calls: Staff continuously monitor stream calls, river conditions, and water rights administration 
to evaluate potential impacts on the Town's available water supplies and operational flexibility. 
• Water Production: Daily water production and customer demand are tracked to identify consumption 
trends, evaluate conservation effectiveness, and support proactive water supply management. 
• Water Quality: Routine water quality sampling and compliance monitoring continue throughout the 
treatment and distribution systems to ensure all state and federal drinking water standards are 
maintained while monitoring drought-related water quality changes. 
• Well Data: Groundwater well levels, pumping rates, are being monitored to assess aquifer conditions, 
verify system capacity, and identify any changes that may indicate drought-related impacts.

Consideration: 
A consideration of the Board of Trustees to accept the resignation of Trustee Huyck and direct staff on 
next steps. 
 
Background: 
Trustee Huyck submitted the attached resignation letter due to her recent move outside of municipal 
limits.  Staff is requesting the Board 1) formally accept the resignation of Trustee Huyck; and 2) direct 
staff to either initiate the solicitation and appointment process or identify the 2026 Regular Election as 
mechanism to fill the vacancy. 
 
Relevant portions of the Code: 
 
• State Statute, C.R.S. 31-4-401: 
o The board of trustees has the power, by appointment, to fill all vacancies in the board or 
any other office, and the person so appointed shall hold office until the next regular 
election and until a successor is elected and has complied with section 31-4-401. The 
board also has the power to fill a vacancy in the board or in any other elective office of 
the town by ordering an election to fill the vacancy until the next regular election and 
until a successor has been elected and has complied with section 31-4-401. 
 
o (2) If the board, at the time a vacancy occurs or within sixty days thereafter, lacks a 
sufficient number of members to establish a quorum to fill the vacancy as provided in 
subsection (1) of this section, the town clerk shall, without further action by the town 
board, call a special election to be held as soon as practicable to fill the vacancy until the 
term of office of a successor elected at the next regular election has commenced as 
provided in section 31-4-301. The board may cancel such election if the board is able to 
fill the vacancy prior to the date of the special election. 
 
• Municipal Code, Chapter 2, Article II, Sec. 2-21.b & c

o b. Qualifications. Each member of the Board of Trustees shall be a registered elector and 
shall have resided within the limits of the Town for a period of at least twelve (12) 
consecutive months immediately preceding the date of the election. If any Trustee shall 
move from or become, during the term of this office, a nonresident of the Town, he or 
she shall be deemed thereby to have vacated his or her office. 
 
o c. Vacancies. The Board of Trustees has the power, by appointment, to fill all vacancies 
in the Board of Trustees, and the person so appointed shall hold his or her office until 
the next regular election and until his or her successor is elected and has complied with 
Section 31-4-401, C.R.S. The Board of Trustees also has the power to fill a vacancy in the 
Board of Trustees by ordering an election to fill the vacancy until the next regular 
election and until a successor has been elected and has complied with Section 31-4-401, 
C.R.S. If a vacancy in the Board of Trustees is not filled by appointment or an election is 
not ordered within sixty (60) days after the vacancy occurs, the Board of Trustees shall 
order an election, subject to the Municipal Election Code, to be held as soon as 
practicable to fill the vacancy until the next regular election and until a successor has 
been elected and has complied with Section 31-4-401, C.R.S. If the term of the person 
creating the vacancy was to extend beyond the next regular election, at the next regular 
election the three (3) candidates for Trustee receiving the highest number of votes shall 
be elected to four (4) year terms and the candidate or candidates receiving the next 
highest number of votes, in descending order, shall be elected to fill the remainder of 
the unexpired term of the person or persons who created the vacancy on the Board. 
 
Per State Statutes there is no express requirement for a statutory town to appoint a person to fill a 
vacancy unless it has resulted in a lack of quorum (Trustee Huyck’s resignation does not create lack of 
quorum).  The Buena Vista Municipal Code identifies that if a vacancy in the Board of Trustees is not 
filled by appointment or an election is not ordered within sixty (60) days after the vacancy occurs, the 
Board of Trustees shall order an election, subject to the Municipal Election Code, to be held as soon as 
practicable to fill the vacancy (60 days from the acceptance of the resignation would be October 10th, 
2026).  Since the 2026 Regular Election has already been initiated with Candidate Petition Packets 
available for pick up starting August 4th, 2026 it would satisfy the requirement of “the Board of Trustees 
shall order an election, subject to the Municipal Election Code, to be held as soon as practicable”. 
 
Based on prior appointment processes, it typically encompasses the full 60 days, meaning the appointed 
individual would potentially attend 2-3 meetings “until the next regular election and until his or her 
successor is elected” 
  
Board of Trustees Action: 
1. Motion to accept the resignation of Trustee Huyck 
2. Motion to: 
a. Direct staff to initiate and facilitate the solicitation and appointment process; or 
b. Confirm that the vacancy shall be filled through the 2026 Regular Election process
