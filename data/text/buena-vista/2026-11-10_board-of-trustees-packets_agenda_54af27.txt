RE:   Chaffee County Regional Housing Needs Assessment – Presentation and Public Review 
 
 
Purpose: The purpose of this work session is to present the Chaffee County Regional Housing Needs 
Assessment (HNA), receive public input, and provide the Board of Trustees an opportunity to review 
and comment on the findings before the assessment is submitted to the Colorado Department of Local 
Affairs (DOLA). 
 
Pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-32-3704, participating local governments must review the Regional HNA at a 
public meeting and submit comments to the regional entity coordinating the assessment. Formal 
adoption of the HNA is not required by the State. 
 
Background: In 2024, Colorado adopted Senate Bill 24-174, requiring local governments to evaluate 
their current and future housing needs. The legislation allows communities to work together on a 
regional assessment rather than conducting separate studies. 
 
Chaffee County, Buena Vista, Poncha Springs, and Salida partnered with Root Policy Research to 
prepare the Regional HNA. 
 
What is a Housing Needs Assessment (HNA)? Simply put, an HNA is a study that helps communities 
understand what housing is available, what residents can afford, and where housing shortages exist. 
 
The assessment looks at population changes, household incomes, employment, housing prices, rental 
costs, existing housing supply, and projected growth. It also incorporates feedback from residents, 
employers, and community stakeholders to better understand housing challenges that may not be fully 
reflected in available data. 
 
The purpose is to establish a shared understanding of housing needs across the region and provide a 
foundation for future housing policies and programs. 
 
Importantly, the HNA is an informational document. It does not change zoning, approve new 
development, require the Town to construct housing, or commit the Town to adopting specific housing 
policies. The assessment identifies needs, while future planning efforts will determine how the Town 
may choose to respond. 
 
3
     
Key Findings for Buena Vista: While the assessment evaluates housing conditions across Chaffee 
County, several findings are particularly relevant to Buena Vista: 
• Housing costs have increased significantly. Between 2019 and 2024, Buena Vista's estimated 
median rent increased from $627 to $2,020 per month, while median home values increased 
approximately 66%, from $303,700 to $505,000. 
• Housing tenure is shifting. The estimated homeownership rate in Buena Vista declined from 
72% in 2010 to 46% in 2024, indicating that renters may now outnumber homeowners. 
However, the report notes a substantial margin of error in this estimate. 
• Local incomes remain below regional housing costs. Buena Vista's median household income 
was approximately $78,323 in 2024, illustrating the challenges many local households face 
when seeking homeownership or affordable rental options. 
• Additional housing is needed across income levels. The assessment estimates a need for 
approximately 307 additional housing units in Buena Vista through 2036, including 162 
ownership units and 145 rental units. Of those, approximately 232 units are estimated to 
require some form of public support to achieve affordability. 
• Housing needs extend beyond lower-income households. The assessment identifies challenges 
for moderate-income households seeking to purchase homes, including households that may 
earn too much to qualify for traditional affordable housing programs but cannot afford market-
rate home prices. 
 
These findings provide a clearer picture of the challenges facing Buena Vista residents and employers. 
The housing production estimates are intended to inform future planning and are not mandatory 
development targets. 
 
Understanding Common Housing Terms: The assessment uses several terms that may be unfamiliar 
and staff have provided the below definitions to help support clarity and understanding. 
• Area Median Income (AMI): A measure used to compare household income to the median 
income for an area, adjusted for household size. For example, in Chaffee County, 100% AMI for 
a two-person household in 2026 is $88,900. A two-person household earning approximately 
$71,120 would be at 80% AMI. Housing programs commonly use AMI to determine eligibility. 
• Affordable Housing: Generally, housing is considered affordable when a household spends no 
more than 30% of its gross income on housing costs, including utilities. Affordable housing does 
not necessarily mean government-subsidized or income-restricted housing. 
• Income-Restricted Housing: Housing with legal restrictions that limit occupancy or pricing based 
on household income. These restrictions are commonly established through recorded 
agreements or deed restrictions. 
• Cost-Burdened Household: A household spending more than 30% of its gross income on 
housing costs. Households spending more than 50% are considered severely cost-burdened. 
• Housing Affordability Gap: The difference between the number of households that need 
housing at a particular price point and the number of homes available and affordable to those 
households. 
• Housing Production Needs: Estimates of additional housing units needed to address current 
shortages (catch-up needs) and accommodate anticipated future households (keep-up needs). 
 
Next Steps: Completion of the Regional HNA represents the first step in a broader housing planning 
process. 
 
4
     
Following public review and consideration of comments from participating jurisdictions, the regional 
assessment will be submitted to DOLA for review. 
 
The Town will then begin work on its Housing Action Plan (HAP), which will evaluate potential 
strategies to address the needs identified in the assessment. This process will include further 
evaluation of the Town's existing land use regulations, development opportunities, available resources, 
and potential housing programs. 
 
Unlike the HNA, the Housing Action Plan will involve consideration of specific strategies and priorities 
for Buena Vista. Those decisions will be brought forward through a separate public process and will 
require Board consideration and approval. 
 
Board Consideration: Staff requests that the Board of Trustees receive the presentation from Root 
Policy Research, provide an opportunity for public comment, and discuss any questions or comments 
regarding the Regional Housing Needs Assessment. 
 
No formal motion or adoption is requested. Board comments will be documented and transmitted to 
the regional project coordinator for inclusion in the final submission to DOLA.

29
Continue 
Regional 
Collaboration
RECOMMENDATION 1
Because housing needs extend across 
jurisdictional boundaries, Chaffee County, 
Buena Vista, Poncha Springs, and Salida 
should continue to participate in and support 
regional housing efforts and funding. 
Continued regional collaboration allows 
jurisdictions to present a unified approach to 
the housing issues identified in the Housing 
Needs Assessment, define common goals, 
and promote efficient resource allocation.
RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
• Continue current collaboration among the municipalities 
and county, including data sharing and resource 
alignment, such as in Prop 123 commitment agreements. 
• Set up regular meetings to coordinate project pipelines 
and plan for competitive funding well in advance.
• Develop jurisdictional Housing Action Plans in 
collaboration so local strategies, such as inclusionary 
zoning, incentives, and land use changes, support 
regional goals and don’t compete with each other. 
30
Explore local 
recurring funding 
sources
RECOMMENDATION 2
Dedicated local funds for affordable housing 
production, preservation, and programs can 
have a high impact on housing needs. 
Revenue from local sources varies widely but 
can include general obligation bonds, 
commercial and/or residential linkage fees, 
sales tax, property tax, general fund 
allocations, and other taxes directly tied to 
housing demand. 
RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
• Develop a long-term regional funding strategy that 
identifies and compares options. The strategy should 
examine potential local funding tools and identify the 
most appropriate, politically viable funding source. This 
could include evaluating funding streams for housing 
projects in peer cities/communities. County examples of 
other dedicated tax streams for housing include 
dedicated property tax in Denver and Boulder counties 
and dedicated sales tax in Denver, Pitkin, Summit, and 
San Miguel counties. 
• Time any ballot measure to favorable economic 
conditions and work to build public support ahead of 
time.

Increase capacity 
to build on 
current successes
RECOMMENDATION 3
Chaffee County has built an effective set of 
housing programs, and the Housing Authority 
continues to grow its rental inventory. The 
Housing Trust offers permanently affordable 
homeownership for households at or below 
80% AMI, and Salida's inclusionary housing 
program is producing new deed-restricted 
units. Continued efforts to connect eligible 
households with these homes can further 
strengthen and optimize the impact of these 
programs.
RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
• Expand housing navigation capacity by building on the Housing 
Trust’s existing one-on-one model rather than creating new 
programs from scratch. Scale services as units come online.  
• Expand navigation services to offer homebuyer readiness and 
credit-building support that provides hands-on assistance with 
document preparation, credit repair, and guidance on program 
requirements.  
• Explore down payment assistance programs. A countywide 
program could reduce reliance on a single lender, and a soft-
second mortgage option could double as a light affordability 
restriction.
• Explore interest rate relief. Monitor CHFA’s Drive it Home program, 
which pairs below-market construction loans with reduced-rate 
mortgages for deed-restricted homes for sale. Advocate for such 
programs and for them to work for smaller rural projects. 
• Fund staff capacity at the Housing Authority and the Housing 
Trust and partner organizations so programs can be 
administered, marketed, and monitored effectively as the 
portfolio of units grows.

Assist and 
stabilize existing 
households 
through program 
support 
RECOMMENDATION 5
Chaffee County's housing challenges affect 
households that are housed but struggling to 
stay housed. Single parents, single-income 
households, older residents on fixed incomes, 
and other non-family households face the 
greatest stress. Economic and job insecurity 
add to that pressure. 
RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
• Support improvements in employer-provided housing. 
Collaborate with employers on basic quality standards and fair 
housing education, and help affected households access 
navigation services and alternative housing.
• Strengthen tenant and landlord resources. Provide clear 
information on tenants' and landlords' rights and responsibilities. 
Evaluate a landlord or rental registry to better understand the 
rental stock and pricing trends.
• Work with partners to expand homelessness prevention and 
response efforts. Coordinate with service providers and regional 
partners to support people experiencing homelessness.
• Expand housing navigation beyond homeownership. Build on 
existing navigation efforts so renters, seniors, and households in 
unstable situations receive one-on-one help finding and keeping 
housing, not just buying it.
• Plan for aging in place and senior housing. Support home 
modification and repair assistance so older adults can stay in their 
homes. Continue working to close the gap in independent and 
assisted living units.

DEFINING AFFORDABILITY AND AMI 
The most common definition of housing affordability is linked to 
industry standards, which consider housing affordable when housing 
costs—rent or mortgage payment plus taxes and utilities—consume 
30% or less of a household’s gross income. Households paying more 
than 30% are “cost burdened” and have less money to spend on other 
essentials like healthcare, education, groceries, and transportation—
adversely affecting their well-being, limiting economic growth 
potential, and constraining local spending.  
The HNA uses both household income ranges and “Area Median 
Income (AMI).” AMI is a measure of income based on the median, or 
middle income family. Housing programs base program eligibility on 
income limits that are represented as percentages of AMI (published 
by HUD by region and household size).

WHY WORK TO ADDRESS HOUSING NEEDS? 
A balanced housing stock accommodates a full “life cycle community”—where there are affordable housing options for residents 
of all stages of life from young adults to centenarians—which in turn supports the local economy and contributes to Chaffee 
County’s culture and community. 
 Research shows that a constrained housing market negatively 
impacts economic growth while stable and affordable housing is 
central to the health of individuals, families, and communities. 
 Households living in stable housing are more likely to spend their 
incomes in the local economy through direct spending on goods 
and services. 
 Housing investments that allow workers to live near their place of 
employment can reduce the impacts of traffic and commuting, and 
attract new businesses by increasing recruitment, productivity, and 
retention of talent.  
 Affordable housing is key to providing high quality public services 
as many essential workers (e.g., first responders, medical 
professionals, and teachers) often leave communities that do not 
have an adequate supply of housing in their price range. 
 Affordable housing also plays a key role in preventing and resolving 
homelessness. 
 Generational wealth from affordable homeownership is a major 
contributor to positive outcomes for children and youth. As 
housing and equity are passed down, young adults have the option 
to remain in their community and have families of their own. 
 Housing investments and stable housing environments bolster 
local revenue, increase job readiness, help renters transition to 
homeownership, lower public costs of eviction and foreclosure, 
and increase the economic and educational opportunities for 
children.

community.  
As a mountain resort community, Chaffee County faces a distinct mix 
of housing pressures. Its economy relies on lower-wage tourism and 
service jobs, while housing demand is driven by higher-income in-
migrants, second-home buyers, and remote workers. Development 
costs are also higher than in other parts of the state. Over the past five 
years, affordable multifamily development in Colorado’s mountain 
rural/resort regions averaged $527,734 per unit, 22% above the

modular products. The result is unmet need across the entire income

Poncha Springs, and Salida should continue to participate in and 
support efforts to advance regional housing efforts and funding. 
Continued regional collaboration allows jurisdictions to present a 
unified approach to the housing issues identified in the Housing 
Needs Assessment, define common goals, and promote efficient 
resource allocation. 
Much of the county’s recent progress comes from this kind of 
partnership. The Chaffee Housing Authority and the Chaffee Housing 
Trust have brought significant state funding into the county and 
turned it into successful developments.  
Recommended actions:  
 Continue current collaboration among the municipalities and 
county, including data sharing and resource alignment, such as in 
Prop 123 commitment agreements.  
 Set up regular meetings to coordinate project pipelines and plan 
for competitive funding well in advance. 
 Develop jurisdictional Housing Action Plans in collaboration so 
local strategies, such as inclusionary zoning, incentives, and land 
use changes, support regional goals and don’t compete with each 
other.  
2. Explore local recurring funding sources. Dedicated local 
funds for affordable housing production, preservation, and programs 
can have a high impact on housing needs. Revenue from local sources 
varies widely but can include General Obligation Bonds, commercial 
and/or residential linkage fees, sales tax, property tax, general fund 
allocations, and other taxes directly tied to housing demand.  
Local funds are particularly effective for affordable housing 
development projects because they provide a sustainable, flexible 
funding source with fewer federal or state regulations. Fee revenue can 
be used for gap financing of low-income housing projects, land 
banking, development incentives or subsidies (such as fee or tax 
rebates), and/or to leverage state and federal funding that requires a 
local match. Dedicated funds—as opposed to discretionary 
allocations—have the added benefit of dependable future revenue, 
which protects funding from shifting politics and allows a jurisdiction

to bond against the revenue, supporting the up-front costs of major

 Develop a long-term regional funding strategy that identifies and

funding tools and identify the most appropriate, politically viable 
funding source. This could include evaluating funding streams for 
housing projects in peer cities/communities. County examples of 
other dedicated tax streams for housing include: dedicated 
property tax in Denver and Boulder counties; and dedicated sales 
tax in Denver, Pitkin, Summit, and San Miguel counties.  
 Time any ballot measure to favorable economic conditions and 
work to build public support ahead of time.    
3. Increase capacity to build on current successes. 
Chaffee County has built an effective set of housing programs, and the 
Housing Authority continues to grow its rental inventory. The Housing 
Trust offers permanently affordable homeownership for households 
at or below 80% AMI, and Salida's inclusionary housing program is 
producing new deed-restricted units. Continued efforts to connect 
eligible households with these homes can further strengthen and 
optimize the impact of these programs. 
 
 
 
 
Recommended actions: 
 Expand housing navigation capacity by building on the Housing 
Trust’s existing one-on-one model rather than creating new 
programs from scratch. Scale services as units come online.   
 Expand navigation services to offer homebuyer readiness and 
credit-building support that provides hands-on assistance with 
document preparation, credit repair, and guidance on program 
requirements.   
 Explore down payment assistance programs. A countywide 
program could reduce reliance on a single lender, and a soft-
second mortgage option could double as a light affordability 
restriction. 
 Explore interest rate relief. Monitor CHFA’s Drive it Home program, 
which pairs below-market construction loans with reduced-rate 
mortgages for deed-restricted homes for sale. Advocate for such 
programs and for them to work for smaller rural projects.  
 Fund staff capacity at the Housing Authority and the Housing Trust 
and partner organizations so programs can be administered, 
marketed, and monitored effectively as the portfolio of units grows.    
4. Preserve existing housing serving low- and 
moderate-income households. There are two different 
submarkets of rental properties serving existing low- and moderate-
income households: income-restricted properties and naturally 
occurring affordable housing, or NOAH. Preservation efforts are 
needed when the affordability contracts on income-restricted units are 
set to expire and when NOAH is under market pressure to raise rents 
beyond what low-income households can afford or when NOAH is for 
sale (especially to corporate investors).

Preservation is an attractive strategy because it utilizes existing 
housing stock and is not bound by the timeline and cost of new 
construction. However, preserving NOAH can be challenging because 
it is driven by market opportunities and owned by private landlords—
in other words, Cities/Counties have no control over rents. 
Much of the county's most affordable housing already exists in mobile 
home parks, older rentals, motels and hotels used as housing, and 
employer-provided units. Rising prices, conversion to short-term 
rentals, deferred maintenance, and poor conditions often threaten this 
housing.   
Recommended actions: 
 Partner with local non-profits, housing providers, affordable 
developers, and other entities that could acquire expiring income-
restricted properties and units.  
 Contingent on available resources, dedicate funds to support 
acquisition/rehabilitation that renews affordability contracts (e.g., 
PAB funds paired with 4% LIHTC could be a source for this). 
 Preserve and improve mobile home parks. Support the modular-
home-on-foundation model as a pathway to affordable 
homeownership. Survey park residents to understand their needs 
and what low-cost solutions work for them. Continue efforts to 
support resident or nonprofit ownership when parks come up for 
sale. 
 Identify properties for acquisition and rehabilitation, including 
hotels and motels already being used as housing and older rental 
properties at risk of conversion or deterioration. Prioritize 
acquisitions that can be deed-restricted for long-term affordability. 
 Fund repair and rehabilitation assistance for low-income 
homeowners and small landlords, so aging units stay safe and 
affordable rather than being lost or sold. 
 Continue to monitor expiring affordability restrictions on deed-
restricted and subsidized units, and plan in advance to extend or 
replace them. 
5. Assist and stabilize existing households through 
program support. Chaffee County's housing challenges impact 
households that are housed but struggling to remain so. Single 
parents, single-income households, older residents on fixed income, 
and other non-family households face the greatest stress. Economic 
and job insecurity add to that pressure.  
Recommended actions: 
 Improve conditions in employer-provided housing. Work with 
employers to establish basic quality standards. Provide education 
on fair housing obligations and connect affected households with 
navigation services and alternative housing options. 
 Strengthen tenant and landlord resources. Provide clear 
information on the rights and responsibilities of both tenants and 
landlords. Evaluate a landlord or rental registry to better 
understand the rental stock and pricing trends. 
 Work with partners to expand homelessness prevention and 
response efforts. Coordinate with service providers and regional 
partners to support people experiencing homelessness in securing 
stable housing. 
 Expand housing navigation beyond homeownership. Build on 
existing navigation efforts so renters, seniors, and households in 
unstable situations receive one-on-one help finding and keeping 
housing, not just buying it.

 Plan for aging in place and senior housing. Support home 
modification and repair assistance so older adults can stay in their 
homes and continue working to close the gap in independent and 
assisted living units. 
6. Lower barriers and costs to build the housing the 
community needs. Increasing the supply of housing at a range of 
price points was the most common solution stakeholders raised. Local 
regulations and development costs can work against that goal. 
Developers said zoning and development standards sometimes 
undermine the outcomes communities want: more density, smaller 
units, accessory dwelling units (ADUs), and attached housing. They also 
noted that program requirements (income qualification, deed 
restrictions, compliance paperwork), parking minimums, and design 
standards add cost.  
Recommended actions: 
 Review zoning and development codes to identify and remove 
barriers to desired housing types. Allow greater density where 
appropriate, smaller lot sizes, and by-right approval of ADUs, 
duplexes, townhomes, and cottage developments. 
 Right-size parking requirements. Review minimum parking 
requirements for affordable, senior, small-unit, and infill housing. 
 Explore streamlined approvals with predictable timelines, pre-
approved plans (for example, for ADUs), and expedited review for 
other desirable projects in addition to affordable projects under 
the fast-track process tied to Prop 123 commitments. 
 Use public and donated land strategically. Make publicly owned 
land available for affordable and mixed-income housing. Continue 
to allow land donations to satisfy inclusionary obligations, and 
define desired outcomes rather than over-prescribing the process. 
 Promote counter-cyclical homebuilding. Use slower market 
periods to acquire or bank land, get projects entitled and ready to 
build, and secure below-market construction financing.  
Next steps. This Housing Needs Assessment is intended as a 
foundation for future policy discussions, not as a commitment to adopt 
any particular strategy. The production estimates and 
recommendations are a guide for planning and prioritization rather 
than mandated targets. 
The next step is the development of Housing Action Plans, through 
which each jurisdiction will determine how to respond to the needs 
identified here. Each plan will reflect local priorities, resources, and 
community input, and will identify the specific strategies best suited to 
that community.  
The average need of about 105 units per year is within the county’s 
recent construction pace: since 2021, the county has permitted about 
300 units annually. The central task, then, is not simply to build more 
housing, but to ensure that more of it reaches the households who live 
and work here. Chaffee County is well positioned to meet that 
challenge. Its jurisdictions have exceeded their initial Proposition 123 
commitments, the Housing Authority and Housing Trust have attracted 
significant state investment, and affordable units are already 
planned/under construction. Building on this momentum gives the 
community a strong foundation to expand housing opportunities for 
current and future residents.

WHAT IS A HOUSING NEEDS 
ASSESSMENT? 
EXCERPT FROM COLORADO STATE GUIDANCE ON HNAS1 
A Housing Needs Assessment (HNA) identifies the type and amount of 
housing needed in a community or region to ensure current and future 
resident and employee households at all income levels have access to 
quality housing that they can afford. As a result, an HNA does more than 
estimate the number of housing units needed to accommodate projected 
population or job growth. An HNA evaluates demographic trends, economic 
characteristics, housing unit inventory, housing market conditions, 
development opportunities and constraints, and existing housing 
programs and services, combined with input from stakeholders and 
residents, to identify where the market is providing suitable housing and 
where it is not. The HNA uses this information to estimate how much 
additional housing is needed to address current housing shortfalls and 
keep up with future growth. The objective is to estimate the housing needed 
to ensure quality housing is available and affordable for resident and 
employee households at all income levels and at different life stages to 
sustain community and economic health and vibrancy. 
It is important to understand that an HNA is an informational tool. While 
the state of Colorado requires each local government to complete an HNA, 
the state does not require jurisdictions to build a certain number of housing 
units or produce a certain type of affordable housing. Rather, completed 
HNAs are to be used for state agency planning, select grant programs, and 
regions and local governments to develop housing action plans tailored to 
the unique constraints, resources, and capacity of a community or region.

By identifying the current housing challenges and future housing needs in 
a community, an HNA serves many purposes, such as: 
 Informing Housing Policy and Programs: An HNA guides local 
governments, housing service organizations, and other partners in 
crafting policies, programs, and partnerships to address housing 
shortages and affordability challenges. 
 Planning for Change: HNAs help communities plan for evolving 
demographic and economic conditions by estimating how many 
housing units will be needed as households, housing markets, and 
employment levels change. 
 Accessing Funding: HNAs can be used to inform applications for state 
or federal housing grants and financing. This includes Colorado's State 
Affordable Housing Fund (or Proposition 123). 
 Increasing Affordability and Housing Stability: By identifying the 
housing needs of households at different income levels, local 
governments can create programs to improve the availability of 
housing that is secure and affordable for residents. 
 Sustaining Community and Economic Vibrancy: HNAs identify the 
housing needs of resident and employee households at all life stages, 
helping jurisdictions develop housing strategies that support local 
businesses, economic development, and a generally diverse and 
thriving community.  
 Educating and Building Support: HNAs can be used to educate 
community members and leaders on housing problems, telling the 
data-driven "housing story" and providing robust information for 
decision-making, which is necessary to build program support within 
communities and address housing needs. 
HNA STRUCTURE 
The HNA is organized by the following sections, following the general 
structure recommended by state guidance on HNA components:  
I. Demographic Trends evaluates demographic changes, which 
drive housing demand.  
II. Economic Trends analyzes jobs, wages, and commuting to 
show whether housing meets workforce needs. 
III. Housing Inventory evaluates existing housing stock by type. 
IV. Housing Market Trends assesses how market factors like 
rents, home prices, and supply impact housing affordability. 
V. Housing Problems and Displacement Risk provides data on 
cost burden, overcrowding, homelessness, and housing 
instability, including displacement risk indicators. 
VI. Community Engagement Summary shares input from 
residents and stakeholders on housing challenges and solutions. 
VII. Community Survey Analysis provides a detailed analysis of 
the 2026 Chaffee County Resident Housing Survey. 
VIII. Current and Projected Housing Needs ties together data 
from previous sections to estimate current and future housing 
needs across all income levels. 
IX. Barriers, Opportunities, and Recommendations discusses 
development challenges, identifies available resources to 
support housing programs, and offers recommended 
strategies to address needs and improve affordability.
