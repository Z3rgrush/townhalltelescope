1. SUMMARY OF REQUEST 
The Planning Department received a building permit application for the replacement of windows, 
doors, and siding at the home at 213 N Colorado Avenue. This home has been determined to have 
been built in approximately 1947, and therefore required review by the Commission as a non-
designated structure facing alteration. 
 
Attachment A: Staff Presentation 
Attachment B: Site Photographs 
Attachment C: HPC Correspondence Requesting Public Hearing 
 
2. PROCESS 
When the completed Building Permit application was submitted to the Planning Department, it 
triggered a review by the Commission to determine 
 1) appropriateness of the window, door, and siding replacement, and; 
 2) eligibility of the structure for local landmark designation.  
 
In a majority vote, the Commissioners determined that the structure had the potential for eligibility 
and that a public hearing would need to be held to discuss the local landmarking potential as is 
procedure for review of alteration or demolition of non-designated structures.  
 
Commissioners discussed eligibility potential under Criteria A and E through email correspondence 
(Attachment D).  
 
The applicant provided public notice of this hearing via a sign on the property where the structure 
is located for 15 days prior to the hearing. The intent of this scheduled hearing is to determine 
whether the structure meets the criteria for designation as specified in Chapter 19 of the Buena 
Vista Municipal Code. 
 
3. ANALYSIS OF CRITERIA FOR DESIGNATION 
The criteria for eligibility to designate a structure as a local landmark are as follows.  
Properties receiving historic designations shall be at least fifty (50) years old, except as otherwise 
provided herein, and possess architectural, social, or geographical/environmental importance by 
meeting at least one (1) or more of the following: 
a. Exemplifies specific elements of an architectural style or period 
     
b. Is an example of the work of an architect or builder who is recognized for expertise nationally, 
statewide, regionally, or locally 
c. Demonstrates superior craftsmanship or high artistic value 
d. Represents an innovation in construction, materials, or design 
e. Represents a style particularly associated with the Buena Vista/Chaffee County area 
f. Represents a built environment of a group of people in an era of history 
g. Represents a pattern or grouping of elements representing at least one (1) of the above 
criteria 
h. Is the site of a historic event that had an effect upon society 
i. Exemplifies cultural, political, economic, or social heritage of the community 
j. Represents an association with a notable person or the work of a notable person 
k. Represents a typical example/association with a particular ethnic group 
l. Represents a unique example of an event in Buena Vista's history 
m. Enhances sense of identity of the community 
n. Is an established and familiar natural setting or visual feature of the community 
o. Properties that have yielded or may be likely to yield, information important in history or 
prehistory 
p. If the property is less than fifty (50) years old, must demonstrate overwhelming historic 
significance. 
Commissioners are to provide evidence for eligibility for designation by making justifications rooted 
in these criteria.  
 
4. EFFECT OF COMMISSION DETERMINATION 
Following the close of the public hearing, the Historic Preservation Commission is requested to 
make a determination regarding the eligibility of the subject structure for local landmark 
designation. Pursuant to the criteria set forth in Section 19-7 of the Buena Vista Municipal Code, 
the Commission may determine that the structure is eligible for designation or not eligible for 
designation.  
 
If the Commission determines that the non-designated structure meets the criteria for local 
landmark designation, the application for a certificate to alter, demolish, or relocate the structure 
is suspended for a period of up to 180 days, as determined by the Commission. The suspension 
period shall have a duration based on the recommended action deemed necessary to preserve or 
document the structure.  
 
The Historic Preservation Commission shall recommend appropriate action items, to the Town 
Administrator, to preserve the structure. Such actions include, but are not limited to: 
• Consulting civic groups, public agencies, and interested citizens; or 
• Require the opportunity to document the building photographically prior to alteration, 
demolition, or relocation of the structure; or 
• Exploring alternative designs that would preserve the historic character of the architectural 
features of the structure; or 
• Looking for alternatives to the proposed alteration which will not have any adverse impact 
on the historic significance of the structure 
 
The Commission’s determination to impose a suspension period of up to one hundred eighty (180) 
days is binding and is intended to provide time to explore preservation alternatives and encourage 
     
voluntary solutions. While the suspension itself is binding, any recommendations made by the 
Commission are nonbinding and subject to further review and determination at the discretion of 
the Town Administrator. During the suspension period, the Town Administrator may seek support 
and additional guidance from the Historic Preservation Commission and may consider or 
incorporate the Commission’s recommendations as appropriate. The applicant will work with 
Planning Department staff to obtain final direction from the Town Administrator. Staff will update 
the Commission at future meetings on the determination and the status of the Town 
Administrator’s recommendations. 
 
If the applicant chooses to move forward with designation if the structure is deemed eligible and 
no longer wishes to alter, demolish, or relocate the structure, a public hearing will be set before the 
Board of Trustees in which further determination will be made about local landmarking status.

Architectural Styles & Characteristics
Pre-WWII block homes generally fall into a few distinctive aesthetic categories depending on the exact decade they
were built:
The Rock-Face "Cast Block" Era (1900–1920s): The earliest pre-war block homes utilized hand-operated
casting machines. The blocks were molded to mimic natural, rusticated stone on the exterior, giving
modest Bungalow or American Foursquare cottages a heavy, castle-like appearance. [1]
The Minimal Traditional Box (1930s): Emerging during the Great Depression and promoted by early Federal
Housing Administration (FHA) principles, these homes shifted to smooth-faced blocks. They were tight, square,
one-story structures with low-pitched roofs, tight eaves, and very little decorative ornamentation. [1, 2, 3, 4]
Early Modern & International Style (1930s): In some regions, pioneering architects utilized smooth concrete
blocks to build ultra-affordable, flat-roofed modern homes. These featured stark grid mortar joints and clean
geometric profiles. [1]
Common Interior Layouts
Because these homes prioritized efficiency and affordability, their internal configurations were highly standardized:
The "Core" Floor Plan: Usually consisting of a living room, a small kitchen with an adjacent dining nook, one or
two small bedrooms, and a single bathroom. [1]

Compact Footprint: Often built on a tight 24x28 or 30x30 foot footprint to maximize space while minimizing the

Centralized Utilities: To save on plumbing costs, the kitchen and bathroom were almost always situated back-
to-back or sharing a plumbing wall. [1, 2]
Modern Trade-offs & Structural Realities
If you are looking at buying or renovating one of these properties today, they come with a unique set of pros and
cons: [1]
Feature Advantage Modern Challenge
Durability Highly fire-safe, rot-resistant, and structurally sound compared to wood-frame peers. Early blocks can suffer from moisture retention or crumbling if not sealed properly.
Insulation The dense block provides a natural thermal mass. True pre-war block walls lack interior insulation cavities, making them hard to heat or cool efficiently without furring out the walls.
Renovations The compact layout makes them relatively affordable to update cosmetically. Running new electrical wiring or plumbing is difficult because the walls are solid, core-filled concrete block rather than hollow drywall studs.

3. ANALYSIS OF CRITERIA FOR DESIGNATION
The criteria for eligibility to designate a structure as a local landmark are as follows.
Properties receiving historic designations shall be at least fifty (50) years old, except as otherwise
provided herein, and possess architectural, social, or geographical/environmental importance by
meeting at least one (1) or more of the following:
a. Exemplifies specific elements of an architectural style or period

b. Is an example of the work of an architect or builder who is recognized for expertise nationally,
statewide, regionally, or locally
c. Demonstrates superior craftsmanship or high artistic value
d. Represents an innovation in construction, materials, or design
e. Represents a style particularly associated with the Buena Vista/Chaffee County area
f. Represents a built environment of a group of people in an era of history
g. Represents a pattern or grouping of elements representing at least one (1) of the above
criteria
h. Is the site of a historic event that had an effect upon society
i. Exemplifies cultural, political, economic, or social heritage of the community
j. Represents an association with a notable person or the work of a notable person
k. Represents a typical example/association with a particular ethnic group
l. Represents a unique example of an event in Buena Vista's history
m. Enhances sense of identity of the community
n. Is an established and familiar natural setting or visual feature of the community
o. Properties that have yielded or may be likely to yield, information important in history or
prehistory
p. If the property is less than fifty (50) years old, must demonstrate overwhelming historic
significance.

4. EFFECT OF COMMISSION DETERMINATION
Following the close of the public hearing, the Historic Preservation Commission is requested to
make a determination regarding the eligibility of the subject structure for local landmark
designation. Pursuant to the criteria set forth in Section 19-7 of the Buena Vista Municipal Code,
the Commission may determine that the structure is eligible for designation or not eligible for
designation.
If the Commission determines that the non-designated structure meets the criteria for local
landmark designation, the application for a certificate to alter, demolish, or relocate the structure
is suspended for a period of up to 180 days, as determined by the Commission. The suspension
period shall have a duration based on the recommended action deemed necessary to preserve or
document the structure.
The Historic Preservation Commission shall recommend appropriate action items, to the Town
Administrator, to preserve the structure. Such actions include, but are not limited to:
• Consulting civic groups, public agencies, and interested citizens; or
• Require the opportunity to document the building photographically prior to alteration,
demolition, or relocation of the structure; or 
• Exploring alternative designs that would preserve the historic character of the architectural
features of the structure; or
• Looking for alternatives to the proposed alteration which will not have any adverse impact
on the historic significance of the structure
The Commission’s determination to impose a suspension period of up to one hundred eighty (180) 
days is binding and is intended to provide time to explore preservation alternatives and encourage 
voluntary solutions. While the suspension itself is binding, any recommendations made by the 
Commission are nonbinding and subject to further review and determination at the discretion of 
the Town Administrator. During the suspension period, the Town Administrator may seek support 
and additional guidance from the Historic Preservation Commission and may consider or 
incorporate the Commission’s recommendations as appropriate. The applicant will work with 
Planning Department staff to obtain final direction from the Town Administrator. Staff will update 
the Commission at future meetings on the determination and the status of the Town 
Administrator’s recommendations. 
If the applicant chooses to move forward with designation if the structure is deemed eligible and 
no longer wishes to alter, demolish, or relocate the structure, a public hearing will be set before the 
Board of Trustees in which further determination will be made about local landmarking status.
