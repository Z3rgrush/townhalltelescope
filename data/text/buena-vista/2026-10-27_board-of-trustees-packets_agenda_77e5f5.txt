PROCLAMATION 
VETERANS AWARENESS MONTH 
WHEREAS, The Town of Buena Vista recognizes the tremendous sacrifice our Veterans have 
made throughout the history of our Country; and 
 WHEREAS, In recognition of this sacrifice and to honor our Veterans, The Town of Buena Vista 
is declaring the month of November 2026 (the 250th year of our country) as VETERANS AWARENESS 
month. 
 WHEREAS, The first 11 days of November will be identified as “11 DAYS OF 
REMEMBERANCE” 
November 1 will be the history of Veterans Day.   
November 2 will be a day to remember the Mothers and Wives of our Veterans while they served 
in the armed forces. 
November 3 will be a day to recognize medical personnel that were committed to aid our 
military personnel. 
November 4 will be a day to identify the reason our Country’s flag is folded in a triangle and 
what each fold signifies. 
November 5 will give the history of the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier. 
November 6 will recognize all Buena Vista military members and their families who paid the 
ultimate sacrifice in service to our country. 
November 7 will recognize each branch of our country’s military service. 
November 8 will recognize the integral part women played in civilian service to our country. 
November 9 will recognize the part women played in the military service to our country. 
November 10 will recognize our local veterans and the service they continue to provide to our 
country. 
November 11 will have an assembly for high school and middle school students and encourage 
the community to attend.  At 11:00 AM play taps and ring the school victory bell 11 times followed by 
a moment of silence in recognition of those that made the Ultimate Sacrifice in defense of our country. 
 WHEREAS, Red, White and Blue Wrist Bands will be available for all in our community.  The 
Blue wrist bands will be for all Veterans, The Red wrist bands will be for family members or loved ones 
of those that have or are serving in the armed forces.  The White wrist  band will be for any family 
member whose loved one paid the ultimate sacrifice in the defense of our country.  
 NOW, THEREFORE, I, Libby Fay, Mayor of the Town of Buena Vista, do hereby proclaim the 
month of NOVEMBER 2026, a month to remember and honor our Veterans, as   
VETERANS AWARENESS MONTH 
All citizens are encouraged to recognize the service Veterans have given to our country for the last 250 
years.

Dogs Running-At-Large Violation Fine Increase - (audio: 1:31:19) 
Police Chief Morgan reviewed the proposed ordinance increasing fines for dogs running at large. 
Following public concerns and a June work session with Code Enforcement, staff recommended fines 
of $50 for a first violation, $100 for a second, and $150 for a third or subsequent violation within a 
three-year period. Morgan said Code Enforcement Officers Bryans and Taylor had compared fines in 
other municipalities and that higher penalties could deter violations. Berger noted that the $25 fine 
had been in place since 1998. 
 
Trustees discussed citation procedures and dog impoundment. Morgan explained that stray dogs are 
typically impounded, while impoundment from private property requires meeting specific legal criteria. 
The proposed ordinance specifies minimum fines for running-at-large violations and allows the 
Municipal Judge to impose higher penalties.

Party wall standards & Related dimensional cleanup 
The Commission discussed party wall standards and side-yard setbacks, including the applicant’s 
desire for greater flexibility to improve building articulation, visual appeal, and interior 
functionality. While a larger side yard could improve marketability, commissioners noted the 
need to consider impacts on garages, carports, parking, and access. The applicant also 
emphasized that setback flexibility would allow better use of interior space and reduce visual 
bulk. 
Commissioners discussed the relationship between the proposed 3-foot side-yard setback and 
amassing in other R-2 OT areas. B. Bennetts generally favored maintaining the 3-foot setback for 
consistency, while T. Bennetts expressed concern about amassing and suggested requiring a 6-
foot front setback while allowing 3-foot side setbacks. Staff confirmed this could be addressed 
through the party wall standards, potentially using a percentage-based approach. Lane 
supported a percentage limitation, noting the potential cost implications for future 
homeowners. 
13
 
The Commission also discussed articulation requirements, with the goal that each duplex appear 
as a single-family home and that two attached duplexes have distinct appearances from one 
another. Commissioners considered allowing additional flexibility where two duplexes come 
together while still requiring each duplex to read as one cohesive structure. 
LaGreca raised concerns about larger or unusual lot configurations, including wider lots and 
properties spanning multiple lots. Kopp will evaluate how the proposed standards would apply 
to these scenarios, including an example on South San Juan, and bring potential code 
amendments back to the Commission at a future meeting. The possibility of requiring a Special 
Use Permit for larger lots or other outlier proposals was also discussed as a preferred option, 
which could provide an opportunity for neighboring property owners to comment. Minimum lot 
width and related dimensional standards will also be addressed in the upcoming code 
amendments.

Water Wise BV: Protecting Our Water Today and Planning for Tomorrow 
Water is one of Buena Vista’s most important resources, and planning for its future 
involves much more than any single project. 
Across the Town, work is underway to strengthen our water supply, invest in 
infrastructure, plan responsibly for growth, increase conservation, and help our 
community better understand how Buena Vista’s water system works. 
This special Water Wise BV update brings those efforts together in one place. From 
long-term water planning and infrastructure investments to regional storage and the 
recent Water Wise Convening, each project is part of a larger goal: maintaining a 
reliable and resilient water system for the community we serve today and the Buena 
Vista of tomorrow. 
������� Understanding Buena Vista’s Water 
Before talking about conservation, infrastructure or future growth, it helps to understand 
the bigger picture: Where does our water come from? How do we access and store it? 
What affects supply and demand? And how do the decisions we make today influence 
our water future? 
Building that shared understanding was one of the major recommendations from Buena 
Vista’s recent Water Wise Convening. The panel encouraged the Town to make 
information about our water system easier to find and understand and to provide 
regular updates about water supply, projects and progress toward conservation goals.

Water planning isn’t simply about finding more water. It is about making the most 
responsible use of the resources Buena Vista already has while building flexibility and 
resilience into the system for the future.

Ahead with Water-Wise Strategies 
Water Wise Convening brings local leaders and water, land-use, and development 
experts together to explore practical strategies for Buena Vista’s future. 
On September 21 and 22, Buena Vista hosted a Water Wise Convening in partnership 
with the Urban Land Institute (ULI) Colorado and its Water Wise Initiative. The two-day 
event brought together community members, Town representatives, developers, 
landscape professionals and water conservation experts to discuss how Buena Vista 
can continue to grow while using its water resources wisely. 
33
 
 
The convening focused on three key questions: how the Town can advance its Water 
Conservation & Efficiency Plan through development practices, how landscaping 
standards and the Town’s Plant Guide could become more water-wise, and which 
conservation tools have proven successful in other communities. 
🌼🌼 A Water-Wise Landscape Can Still Be a Beautiful Landscape 
One of the strongest themes from the discussion was that water conservation does not 
have to mean sacrificing the character and beauty of Buena Vista. 
Thoughtful landscaping can reduce irrigation demand while supporting pollinators and 
birds, improving habitat and site ecology, and incorporating flowers, trees and shrubs 
appropriate for our environment.  BV Water Wise Roundtable 
The panel recommended working with local and regional partners—including nurseries, 
growers, CSU Extension and other plant experts—to develop a plant list specifically 
suited to Buena Vista and the surrounding region.  
🌻🌻 Introducing the Idea of “BVScapes” 
One idea emerging from the discussion was “BVScapes”: a locally focused approach to 
designing, planting and maintaining resilient landscapes in Buena Vista. 
Rather than simply removing turf or reducing irrigation, BVScapes could help property 
owners, developers and land managers create landscapes that use water efficiently 
while remaining beautiful, functional, ecologically resilient and reflective of Buena 
Vista’s unique community character.  BV Water Wise Roundtable 
Potential strategies include a locally appropriate plant guide, demonstration gardens, 
irrigation and maintenance resources, workforce training, and partnerships with local 
and regional experts.

�������� Water + Growth: Planning Them Together

Every conversation about Buena Vista’s future growth is also a conversation about 
water. 
The Water Wise Convening explored how development standards can support 
conservation while recognizing that every project is different. The panel emphasized the 
importance of transparency, consistency, flexibility and choice. 
Clear standards can establish baseline expectations, while incentives can encourage 
developers and property owners to go further. Ideas discussed included conservation-
based tap fees, landscape and irrigation standards, incentives for exceeding minimum 
requirements, and recognition for projects that demonstrate exceptional water 
efficiency and sustainable design. 
These are recommendations and ideas for consideration—not predetermined policy 
changes. They provide a starting point for continued conversations about how water 
conservation and responsible development can work together.

The recent Water Wise Convening is one part of putting that larger vision into action. 
Future work may include community education, water-wise landscaping resources, 
demonstration projects, development and landscape standards, conservation 
incentives, partnerships and other strategies. 
The goal is not simply to use less water. It is to use the water we have wisely while

Buena Vista’s approach is about understanding and caring for the whole system: 
securing reliable supplies, investing in infrastructure, using water efficiently, planning 
responsibly for growth, and giving our community the information and tools needed to 
participate.
