Work Session at 6:00 PM – Housing Needs Assessment Update 
Avilia Bueno, Director of Root Policy Research, presented preliminary findings from the regional 
Chaffee County Housing Needs Assessment, a collaborative effort involving Chaffee County, 
Buena Vista, Salida, and Poncha Springs. She explained that the assessment is intended to 
better understand current and future housing needs and will inform required Housing Action 
Plans. Bueno reviewed demographic and economic trends, including continued population 
growth driven primarily by net migration, an aging population, and rising incomes alongside 
declining homeownership. 
Bueno also discussed housing market and affordability trends, including home prices and rents 
outpacing income growth, elevated housing cost burdens among renters, and the impacts of 
second-home ownership and short-term rentals on housing available to year-round residents. 
The assessment estimates a countywide need for approximately 1,070 additional housing units 
over the next 10 years, including units needed to address existing shortages and accommodate 
projected population and job growth; based on its share of the county population, Buena Vista 
could account for approximately 293 of those units. She also presented preliminary resident 
survey findings related to housing conditions, displacement, housing stability, barriers to 
homeownership, and community preferences for future housing programs and housing types. 
Trustees and staff discussed several Buena Vista-specific findings and requested additional 
clarification and context regarding the data, including federally assisted housing units, the 
relationship between population growth and housing production, second-home ownership and 
short-term rentals, and renter cost burdens. Trustees expressed interest in more localized 
short-term rental data and information regarding changes in short-term rental activity over 
time. Bueno stated that Root Policy Research is incorporating staff comments and will prepare 
a full draft report and recommendations for a future Board presentation and required public 
hearing before submission to the State, after which the Town will begin developing its Housing 
Action Plan.

Chaffee County Community Foundation Update (Audio Time: 02:03:00) 
Anna Hendricks, Program Director for the Chaffee County Community Foundation, provided an 
update on the Community Grants Partnership between the Town and the Foundation. She 
reviewed the process, including Board approval of funding, applicant assistance, volunteer 
grant committee review, Board approval of recommendations, and ongoing reporting and 
nonprofit support. Hendricks emphasized the program's role in promoting transparent, 
community-driven funding decisions and leveraging public and private resources. Hendricks 
noted that the Foundation has partnered with Buena Vista on the grant process since 2020 and 
has contributed more than $270,000 in services and support to local nonprofits. 
Mayor Fay noted her and former Trustee Huyck’s past service on the grant review committee. 
Administrator Berger stated that applications are expected to open in January, and Hendricks 
plans to return in November to discuss the upcoming grant cycle and Board participation. 
 
Transportation Master Plan Update (Audio Time: 02:13:10) 
Ben Manibog, KLJ’s Transportation Planning Team Leader, presented the first phase of the 
Transportation Master Plan, including the existing conditions analysis and community 
engagement. He explained that the Plan will update the 2004 Transportation Plan, align with 
the Comprehensive and Three-Mile Plans, and provide a 10- to 20-year outlook for 
transportation needs. The analysis identified limited pedestrian and bicycle infrastructure 
outside the Town core and a concentration of crashes along U.S. 24, particularly near 
intersections. Engagement with staff, stakeholders, community organizations, and 
approximately 65 open house attendees identified West Main Street, East Main Street/U.S. 24, 
Arizona Street, an additional railroad crossing, and improved pedestrian and bicycle safety and 
connectivity as priorities. 
Manibog reviewed draft goals emphasizing multimodal transportation, connectivity, 
seasonality, sense of place, and attainable projects. Trustees and staff identified additional 
considerations, including Crossman Avenue, future development and City Market impacts, peak 
summer traffic conditions, multimodal trail safety, the Steel Drive-Antero Circle connection, and 
potential shuttle and park-and-ride options. 
The next phase will identify projects, policies, missing connections, and system needs; establish 
prioritization criteria; and develop short-, medium-, and long-term recommendations with 
preliminary costs. A second public engagement round is anticipated in January, followed by 
Planning and Zoning Commission and Board consideration of the completed Plan in late March 
or April.

Data Center Moratorium  
Staff continues to evaluate potential approaches for regulating data centers following adoption of the 
temporary moratorium. Over the next month, staff will coordinate with Sangre de Cristo Electric 
53
     
Page 2 of 2 
Association and the Chaffee County Economic Development Corporation to better understand 
potential utility, infrastructure, and economic development considerations associated with this land 
use. 
 
Given the uncertainty regarding the timing and substance of future State regulations, staff is currently 
leaning toward recommending that data centers be prohibited within Town limits as an interim 
regulatory approach. The temporary moratorium requires the Town to actively evaluate and advance 
potential regulations during the moratorium period. Because it is unclear how long it may take for the 
State to establish a regulatory framework, staff believes it is important to establish a clear local 
standard rather than delay action indefinitely while awaiting State direction. 
 
A local prohibition would not preclude the Town from reconsidering this use in the future. Once State 
regulations or legislative direction are established, the Board of Trustees could choose to revisit the 
issue and direct staff to amend the Unified Development Code consistent with the State's framework 
and the community's needs. Staff anticipates returning to the Board with a more specific 
recommendation following coordination with Sangre de Cristo Electric Association and the EDC.

OP 123 
Staff is currently reviewing a Regional Housing MOU and unit allocation letter drafted by the Chaffee 
Housing Authority with direction from the Colorado Department of Local Affairs (DOLA). Buena Vista 
has met its current housing commitment of 26 units; however, the City of Salida remains a few units 
short of its commitment. As previously discussed with the Board, regional partners are exploring a 
countywide approach to allocating qualifying housing units to help ensure that each participating 
jurisdiction meets its respective commitment. 
 
Buena Vista currently has qualifying units in excess of its commitment. Staff confirmed with DOLA that 
excess units from the current commitment period cannot be carried forward or credited toward the 
Town's 2027–2029 commitment. As a result, there is no benefit to the Town in retaining those excess 
credits for its own future use. Staff is therefore reviewing the proposed MOU and allocation letter to 
determine how Buena Vista's excess units may be applied regionally to assist Salida in meeting its 
current commitment while establishing a coordinated framework for future regional reporting. 
 
Separately, the Town must submit a new three-year housing commitment for the 2027–2029 reporting 
cycle by N ovember 1, 2026. House Bill 26-1313 revised the methodology used to calculate 
jurisdictional housing commitments. Staff is working through the updated calculation and will return to 
the Board with the Town's new commitment and any actions necessary to meet the November 1 filing 
deadline.
