2. General Discussion: Data Center Moratorium 
Kopp provided an update on the current data center moratorium, elaborating that 
mid-December is the deadline for when any code amendments would need to be adopted. Kopp 
has reached out to area municipalities to understand how Data Centers are managed in their 
code. Shortly after BV initiated the moratorium, Salida adopted a one year moratorium. Salida 
staff shared they are waiting on direction from the State legislator before pursuing code 
amendments. The County code prohibits data centers. 
 The Town’s moratorium may only remain in effect if work is being completed. Therefore, after 
discussing with the Town Administrator, Kopp would like to recommend adopting code 
prohibiting data centers, which can be amended once the State adopts code or sets forth a 
statute. Legal is hesitant to propose a moratorium that would extend beyond six months without 
active work being done in relation to the moratorium item.  
B. Bennetts asked whether “data centers” are defined in the Town Code. Kopp confirmed that a 
definition was established through the moratorium, but not yet captured in code. T. Bennetts 
asked Kopp to clarify what feedback staff was seeking from the Commission. Kopp explained that 
staff was seeking general feedback on the proposed approach to prohibit data center uses, with 
the opportunity to revisit or amend the regulations in the future. Commissioner Lane asked 
whether the prohibition would apply to all data centers or whether smaller, dispersed facilities 
would be considered separately. Kopp clarified that the proposed prohibition would apply to all 
data centers, with careful wording to avoid inadvertently prohibiting accessory technology uses 
associated with other businesses.

TO: Planning and Zoning Commission  
 
FROM: Marika Kopp, Planning Director 
   
RE:  Public Hearing: Proposed Code Amendment Concerning Two-Family Dwelling Setbacks in 
the R-2 Old Town Overlay District 
 
 
PURPOSE: This memorandum summarizes the proposed R -2 Old Town Overlay District code 
amendment, previous Commission discussions, and public feedback. Staff is not presenting a draft 
ordinance at this time and seeks Commission direction on whether to defer further consideration to the 
Town's upcoming housing planning and code audit efforts or continue pursuing the proposal as a 
standalone amendment.  
ATTACHMENTS: 
• Staff Presentation 
• Public Engagement Postcard 
• Postcard Responses 
• Public Engagement Survey 
• Survey Responses 
• Email Responses 
 
BACKGROUND: At the August 5, 2026, Planning & Zoning Commission meeting, a local development 
team requested that the Town consider amending the R-2 Old Town Overlay District regulations to allow 
small multifamily residential development. 
Under the Town's current Unified Development Code, the R -2 Old Town Overlay District permits a 
variety of residential housing types, including single -family detached dwellings, two -family dwellings 
(duplexes), and single -family attached dwellings (rowhouse s or townhomes). However, small 
multifamily dwellings are not permitted within the district, even through a Special Use Permit. 
In comparison, small multifamily dwellings are allowed through a Special Use Permit in both the R -2 
District and the R-1 Old Town Overlay District, with the latter limited to properties east of Highway 24. 
The rationale for excluding small multifamily dwellings specifically from the R -2 Old Town Overlay 
District was not readily apparent during staff's initial review of the Code. 
The development team's request raised questions about this distinction, particularly given that the 
district already permits attached residential building forms. The development team expressed interest 
in providing greater flexibility for residential devel opment, including the ability to construct two over -
under duplexes connected along a shared property line. This configuration would accommodate four 
dwelling units within a single attached building form while allowing the underlying lots to remain under 
separate ownership. 
Page 2 of 4 
The Commission discussed the potential benefits and impacts of allowing small multifamily 
development within the district,  and directed staff to further investigate potential regulatory 
approaches but did not make a recommendation supporting or opposing a code amendment. 
 
PREVIOUS COMMISSION DISCUSSIONS: 
August 19, 2026 – Initial Alternatives 
At the August 19 meeting, staff presented three potential approaches for consideration: 
1. Option 1 –  Maintain Existing Standards: Retain the existing R -2 Old Town Overlay District 
regulations without modification, continuing to allow over- under duplexes subject to existing 
dimensional standards and setbacks. 
2. Option 2 – Allow Party -Wall Connections Between Two -Family Dwellings: Allow two adjoining 
over-under duplexes to share a common wall along a property line, eliminating the interior side 
setback between buildings while establishing corresponding setback req uirements along the 
opposite sides. This approach would provide greater flexibility in building configuration without 
changing the number of dwelling units permitted within an individual duplex. 
3. Option 3 – Allow Small Multifamily Development: Expand permitted residential uses within the R-
2 Old Town Overlay District to include small multifamily development, with consideration of 
whether the use should be permitted by right or subject to Special Us e Permit approval and 
additional use-specific standards. 
 
Following discussion, the Commission expressed interest in further exploring Option 2, as a more 
narrowly tailored amendment than allowing small multifamily development throughout the district. 
Commissioners also discussed building articulation, side setbacks, parking accommodations, and 
whether additional standards would be necessary to address larger or unusual lot configurations. Staff 
was directed to further evaluate the potential amendment and return with additional information. 
 
October 21, 2026 – Continued Discussion 
At the October 21 meeting, several members of the Historic Preservation Commission expressed 
concerns regarding the proposed amendment, particularly its potential impacts on Old Town's historic 
character, existing relatively affordable housing, and neighborhood building mass and scale. Additional 
concerns included floodplain constraints, infrastructure capacity, and stormwater management. 
Suggestions included limiting the allowance to specific locations where construction cost savings could 
be demonstrated and considering affordability requirements targeting households earning 60 –80% of 
Area Median Income (AMI). 
 
PUBLIC ENGAGEMENT: The Town satisfied the Unified Development Code's newspaper publication 
requirement for the November 4, 2026, public hearing. In addition, staff mailed 156 informational 
postcards to R -2 Old Town Overlay District property owners describing the proposal and  providing 
opportunities to participate through an online survey, email, written comments, an October 27 staff 
drop-in session, and the November 4 public hearing. 
At the time of this report, staff received 25 responses through returned postcards, online surveys, and 
email correspondence, with some potential duplication among respondents.

Feedback was overwhelmingly opposed to the proposed amendment, with the most common concerns 
involving building size, mass and scale, and neighborhood character. Some respondents expressed 
support for additional housing opportunities and construction flexibility. 
Although these responses are not statistically representative of all property owners, they provide 
valuable insight into community concerns regarding the proposed amendment. 
 
PROPOSED CODE AMENDMENT 
Based on the Commission's previous direction to explore Option 2, staff initially evaluated a limited 
amendment involving three provisions of the Unified Development Code: 
• Table 2.2: Summary of Residential District Dimensional Standards 
Add a footnote to the R -2 Old Town Overlay District's minimum side setback requirement 
referencing applicable use-specific standards for two-family dwellings. 
• Table 2.7: R-2 District Dimensional Standards 
Provide a corresponding footnote as stated above. 
• Section 16.04.3.2.1.k – Dwelling, Two-Family Use Standards 
Add a provision allowing vertically stacked two -family dwellings on adjoining lots to share a 
common wall along a property line with a zero -foot interior side setback, provided a minimum 
five-foot side setback is maintained along the opposite side of each dwelling. 
 
STAFF ANALYSIS: Staff recognizes the potential benefits of greater residential development flexibility, 
including expanded housing options, construction efficiencies, and potential cost savings. However, 
public feedback and Commission discussions have identified concerns regarding neighborhood 
compatibility, building mass and scale, infrastructure, and preservation of existing housing. 
Further evaluation is also warranted regarding the relationship between two -family and small 
multifamily use classifications, particularly where four dwelling units are connected across separately 
owned lots. Although the proposal would connect two individ ually permitted duplexes across a shared 
property line, the resulting four-unit building may more appropriately be classified as small multifamily 
development. The Code's existing ownership requirements for small multifamily dwellings introduce 
considerations that extend beyond a setback adjustment and warrant a more comprehensive evaluation 
of the applicable use classifications and development standards.  
Moreover, t he Town is completing the Chaffee County Regional Housing Needs Assessment (HNA), 
scheduled for presentation to the Board of Trustees on November 10, 2026. The HNA will inform the 
Town's forthcoming Housing Action Plan, and DOLA grant funding will support a broader Unified 
Development Code audit focused on opportunities to incentivize income -aligned and affordable 
housing. These initiatives provide an opportunity to evaluate alternative residential building forms 
alongside neighborhood compatibility, appropriate development standards, ownership configurations, 
and the Town's broader housing goals. 
Page 4 of 4 
Staff recommends pausing advancement of the proposed amendment and incorporating further 
evaluation into these upcoming housing planning and code audit efforts. This recommendation is not a 
determination that the proposed building form is inappropriate, bu t rather an opportunity to evaluate 
it within a more comprehensive framework informed by the HNA, public feedback, and legal 
considerations. 
REQUESTED COMMISSION DIRECTION:  
Staff requests that the Planning & Zoning Commission conduct the publicly noticed hearing, receive 
public testimony, and provide direction on one of the following approaches: 
1. Defer further consideration: Defer further evaluation of the proposal into the Town's upcoming 
housing planning and code audit efforts. Any future hearing would require new public notice. 
2. Continue as a standalone amendment: Continue the public hearing to a date certain, at which 
time staff would return with a draft ordinance for the Commission's consideration. 
 
No draft ordinance is presented, and staff is not requesting a recommendation of approval or denial at 
this time.

We are vehemently opposed to the proposed code amendment to R-2 Old Town zoning code,
which would allow an over-under duplex on one lot to be constructed with a zero-foot side setback
when attached to another on an adjoining lot.  The current Old Town setback, height, and building
requirements ought to be maintained in order for R-2 OT zoning district to remain as is and not
packed in by allowing larger building masses on properties.  There are so many of the over-under
duplexes in Town already that it is quickly losing its charming & small-town feel.  We believe the
current R-2 OT ought to remain a transition between the high-density and low-density
neighborhoods.  Whatever entity, whether it be developers or   out-of-county land grabbing groups,
to benefit from this amendment, ought to respect how residents in R-2 OT want the neighborhood
to look like.  The current zoning code for R-2 OT protects those of us to wish to live as it permits.
Permitting large building masses in this zoning district is simply inappropriate.  Please, please do
not amend the zoning code for this district.
