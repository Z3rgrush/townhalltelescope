Mayor Richards called the meeting to order at 5:00 p.m. with Councilors Benedetti, Rose and Doyle 
present. Councilor Guth was absent.  
PUBLIC COMMENT:  
Deacon Harrington – He said he is the Ministry Coordinator at St. Mary’s and said he is here regarding 
the sound stage in Conner Park. They have 500 or so weekly participants at the church and most seem of 
the opinion that amplified sound would denigrate the Armory Hall redevelopment. It is our intention to 
hold meetings at St. Mary’s on this subject. Thank you in advance for the continued conversation on this 
matter. We are a community within a community who desire to be a good neighbor and will continue to 
do so.   
Ward Hauenstein – Mr. Hauenstein said he is here asking them to consider getting involved in e-bikes 
and e-motos. He would like for Council to consider alignment up and down the valley of usage. Required 
licensing and permitting. He would like to see an age limit of 16 years of age to be able to use. You see 
kids all the time on these moving at high speeds. If they are under 18, there should be a helmet 
requirement as well.  
Mayor Richards said other towns are discussing this too and are having the same issues. Locals no longer 
feel comfortable walking on the Rio Grande Trail. We are taking this very seriously.  
Mr. Hauenstein said the only way to enforce this is to change the speed limits on the bike paths.

PUBLIC HEARINGS:  
Ordinance #17, Series of 2026 – Proposed Ordinances to Amend the Home Rule Charter to Change the 
City of Aspen’s General Election from March to November – Alissa Farrell, Administrative Services 
Director and James R. True, Interim City Attorney   
Mayor Richards said they have number of memos and attachments. The Clerk has concerns with the 
timing of the runoff and how it is conducted. She has been asked if they should put this on the March 
election for time to gather more information. Putting something on this year’s November ballot would 
not affect anything and a delay to March would not delay anything, but it may not be fully baked to go 
on the ballot this November if we reach that consensus. She would expect some serious work and 
outreach to be done between now and then. She said she feels very uncomfortable moving it to 
November without a runoff and explained why.  
James R. True introduced himself and said he appreciates the comment on his retirement party. He said 
this has been discussed a great deal since the August 11th meeting, and we do not have a significant 
presentation to make today. A runoff has significant issues that we have outlined in the memo. No one 
can debate this. We presented this data and issues, so it comes down to a determination you need to 
make if you’d like to put something on the ballot for November or for something to be considered for 
March.  
Ms. Farrell introduced herself and she mentioned the conversations which staff has had. Further 
recognizing the community survey and that the question it asked was limited and elevator starting point, 
if you will. Engagement with the community would be important for us making an informed decision. 
Gathering election staff and judges during the holidays and staff for a transparent process is a concern. 
Holding an election over Thanksgiving is an important note and may be difficult and place additional 
responsibilities on the Clerks staff and team. Giving us time to consider more conversations with Pitkin 
County is also important as we don’t know how they feel about assisting in our election while they are 
running their own election. We take election timing very seriously. Providing pros and cons is the 
ultimate goal.  
Mayor Richards opened public comment:  
Elizabeth Siegel – Ms. Siegel said she supports moving the election date to November to increase the 
voter turnout. Why does she care? She has studied American government for years. She lectures 
annually on important issues. Voter apathy is bad and should be reversed. It’s the foundation of our 
democracy and it begins at the local level. This is a very informed community and opinionated one. It’s 
easy and safe to cast a vote. Combining the elections is an important step. The results can only be 
positive. She would like for the question to be on the November ballot.

Linda Manning – Ms. Manning mentioned the weight given to the community survey, but it didn’t ask 
the right question. It was only about coordinating, and her issue is with the runoff. The majority of 
people who are voting will not be reading the ordinance beforehand. She has talked with the County 
election staff multiple times, and she brought up the conflict of the County canvassing while we are 
running our own runoff. It’s bad optics and what if a recount is initiated. Ballot Trax is a great tool that 
the Clerk also mentioned. She spoke about voter confusion and people not knowing where to drop their 
ballots off. She mentioned coordinating with the County for the primary if they insist on a runoff.  
Ward Hauenstein – Mr. Hauenstein is here to encourage the November election on even years. He 
doesn’t need to rehash, but the option that is first in the memo is the way to go. Elections have become 
a mess. He thinks the Aspen electorate has enough bandwidth to focus on national and local issues at 
the same time. November gives the best turnout and should be put on the ballot for this November.  
Neil Siegel – Mr. Siegel said he also is here to support the change for the election to be in November. 
Let’s put the matter on the ballot in November. He supports maintaining the runoff as well. A mandate 
is very important. Deferring this matter to March is a very bad mistake. There are two issues here – 
voter education so the local issues don’t get pushed to the back. The character of the local election is 
lost in November. There are traits we should continue and the Clerk and the whiteboard in City Hall is a 
tradition we could continue.  
Mayor Richards closed the public hearing.  
Mr. True mentioned the comments by Ms. Manning. He said it’s a separate difficult problem to wait for 
the county to certify.  
Councilor Doyle said he is very interested in increasing voter turnout and coordinating with the County 
in November. But with all comments considered, there are unintended consequences with a December 
runoff. We should avoid voter confusion. There will be a voter absence because of Thanksgiving and 
Christmas holiday. It’s important to have a runoff but the one we are thinking of doesn’t seem to be the 
way and putting this on the November ballot is rushing. We should take our time and do this right. 
Councilor Benedetti said she is with John. She was on the committee to change the election from May to 
March instead of November. We didn’t consider November because it’s off season here. March, August 
and July are when most people are in town. For her tonight, we moved too quickly and there are too 
many unknowns. Linda was right in that the survey question wasn’t complete. We haven’t weighed 
through the nuances thoroughly enough yet.  
Councilor Rose said November has a higher turnout and less voter fatigue and has Ballot Trax, but the 
cons are more nuanced. He is so in the weeds of this; he can’t think of what it’s like to fill out a mail in 
ballot. He can’t explain people’s behavior and doesn’t feel like people will do the research to know what 
to vote on. He is looking for feedback on this. The local issues will be lost in federal election. If it 
becomes more partisan and less locally focused. The runoff would be very confusing. He talked to the 
City Clerk. You need election judges and during Thanksgiving is questionable. You would still need to 
have all of the equipment to maintain. Listening to Aspen Public Radio this morning, there will be up to 
14 ballot initiatives on this ballot in the state of Colorado this November. There is so much research 
required here. He talked about issues with certification, and getting voter roll updates and information 
from the County while they are running their own election. There is a tenuous situation with the
