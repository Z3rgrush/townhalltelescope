REGULAR MEETING         ASPEN CITY COUNCIL      AUGUST 11, 2026 
Mayor Richards called the meeting to order at 5:00 p.m. with Councilors Benedetti and Rose present. 
Councilor Guth was present via Zoom and Councilor Doyle was absent.  
PUBLIC COMMENT:  
John Kelleher – Mr. Kelleher said has been a longtime member with the St. Mary’s parish. He spoke 
about a stage that is being built and that amplified music will be going on during church activities. He 
also spoke about the historic properties surrounding Connor Park. They are in favor of renovating the 
Armory but at present, the park provides a pleasant and quiet respite to the busy commercial core. The 
performance deck is their greatest concern. We very much appreciate your concern and review of the 
potential intrusion into St. Mary’s peaceful environment.  
Deacon Harrington – Deacon Harrington provided a letter provided by the Arch Diocese of Denver. They 
thanked Jen Phelan and the asset department for providing communication with them. They are 
generally supportive of the project, but they are worried about loud amplified music at the performance 
deck. Please reconsider your approval for the park use. The city of Aspen has a long history of preserving 
noise. Please do not place a music venue so close to a place of peace and sanctity and prayer. They hope 
to continue the conversation in mutual respect and appreciation.  
Mayor Richards said we hope for continued dialogue in hope of continued appreciation.  
Mary Wolf – Ms. Wolf said she is the business manager at St. Mary’s and has been asked by Suzanne 
Caskey to read her statement, who is one of their longtime parishioners. This community values history 
and the city has gone to great lengths to honor the church over the years and now we beg for that 
respect again. Intensity has become a monster. We leave no space for a quiet bench to sit on or a place 
to listen for our better angels. We will have lost an irreplaceable gift from those who were here before 
us.  
Francis Stuckens – Mr. Stuckens said he appreciates Connor Park too. Maybe we can do unplugged. He 
has been here for 40 years. Thank you for APCHA for all the checks and balances they do. He is glad to 
see Mayor Richards at Climate Curve and repping the community and taking it seriously. He is grateful to 
the Aspen Daily News for all of their commentary. He gives sincere thanks to Aspen Community Voice. 
He mentioned bike safety again.  
Linda Manning – Ms. Manning said she is for the election date change to November. She is not in favor 
of a runoff. The runoff creates flips. The goal is to have the largest number voter turnout. If the city was 
to stick with a runoff, there could be a scenario where the clerk’s office is out of practice because they 
don’t have to do it one year and then two years later, they do. This can produce a not ideal situation. 
Folks are used to long ballots with a lot of questions.  
COUNCIL COMMENTS:  
Mayor Richards said Councilor Doyle is taking his daughter to college this week, and Councilor Guth is 
online.

FIRST READING OF ORDINANCES:  
Ordinance #17, Series of 2026 – Proposed Ordinances to Amend Charter to Change City of Aspen’s 
Election from March to November – James R. True, Special Counsel  
Mr. True asked if they have any questions about ranked choice voting, which he previously mentioned in 
the work session. Colorado has adopted language for ranked choice voting.  
Mr. True said the first question is regarding if council is in favor of coordinating the election in 
November and if they would like the runoff in December.  
Councilor Guth said yes for coordinating. Councilor Rose said yes. Councilor Benedetti has reservations 
but will move forward because of the community survey and let the public decide. 
Mr. Strecker said Councilor Doyle got in touch with him and said he was supportive of moving to 
November. 
Mr. True asked about including a runoff in December.  
City Attorney, Katharine Johnson, said you could move both ordinances to second reading and have 
public hearings on both options so people can speak.  
Councilor Guth said he does not want a runoff.  
Councilor Rose is perplexed.   
Mayor Richards said she supports a runoff.  
Councilor Benedetti would like to see both moved forward to the public discussion.  
Mr. Strecker said Councilor Doyle does support a December runoff.

Mr. True said there isn’t a lot of downside to approving both ordinances at this point. Mr. True said City 
Clerk, Nicole Henning, has some additional comments to make on the runoff.  
Ms. Henning reminded the council that by having a runoff, it would not lessen the financial and 
administrative burden on the city. Also, with a county run election and then a city run election, it would 
cause a lot of public confusion with where to drop off ballots and with the county using Ballot Trax and 
the city not having access to that program.  
Councilor Benedetti motioned to read both ordinances; Councilor Rose seconded. Roll call vote: 
Benedetti, yes; Guth, yes; Rose, yes; Richards, yes. 4-0, motion carried.  
Ms. Henning read the ordinances.  
Councilor Guth motioned to approve for second reading; Councilor Rose seconded. Roll call vote: 
Benedetti, yes; Guth, yes; Rose, yes; Richards, yes. 4-0, motion carried.   
Mayor Richards called for a five-minute break at 6:03 p.m.
