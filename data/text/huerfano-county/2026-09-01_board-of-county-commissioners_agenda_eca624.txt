THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS 
OF HUERFANO COUNTY, COLORADO 
 
A RESOLUTION MAKING CERTAIN LEGISLATIVE 
FINDINGS AND APPROVING THE GARDNER MAIN 
STREET REVITALIZATION PLAN 
 
WHEREAS, by Resolution No. 26-09, the Board of County Commissioners of Huerfano 
County, Colorado (the “County”) established and organized the Huerfano County Revitalization 
Authority (the “Authority”) to exercise the powers granted in the Colorado Revitalization Law, 
Part 1 of Article 31 of Title 30 of the Colorado Revised Statute, C.R.S., (the “Act”); and 
 
WHEREAS, the purpose of the Act is to provide for revitalization of certain unincorporated 
areas that would benefit from economic investment and development and redevelopment by 
approval of revitalization plans for such areas; and  
 
WHEREAS, the County has commissioned the preparation of a county revitalization plan 
for an area in Gardner (the “Area”); and 
 
WHEREAS, there has been presented to the Board of County Commissioners, the Gardner 
Main Street Revitalization Plan, a copy of which has been approved as to form by the County 
Attorney and is attached hereto and incorporated herein; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Plan is a matter of public record in the custody of the County Clerk and 
Recorder is available for public inspection during business hours of the County; and  
 
WHEREAS, notice of the public hearing on the Plan was published as required by Section 
30-31-109(5) of the Act, at least thirty days prior to the public hearing; and  
 
WHEREAS, written notice of the public hearing was not required to be mailed or delivered 
to each property owner, business, and resident of the area included in the Plan because the County 
is the sole owner of the Area and there are no businesses or residents in the Area; and 
  
WHEREAS, the County Planning Commission has determined that the Plan complies with 
the Huerfano County Comprehensive Plan Update 2025 (the “Comprehensive Plan”), which is the 
general plan for the development of the County as a whole; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Plan contains provisions that allocate property tax increment revenues 
derived from the levies of Taxing Entities that levy property taxes in the Area as provided in 
Section 30-31-109(13) of the Act; and  
 
WHEREAS, on May 26, 2026, the Board of County Commissioners conducted a public 
hearing and reviewed said Plan pursuant to the procedural and notice requirements of the Act, and 
the Board of County Commissioners having considered the evidence presented in support of and

II. Introduction 
 
This Plan is intended to apply the powers and provisions of the 2024 County Revitalization 
Law (the “Act”) to an area in the County that would benefit from revitalization and 
economic investment. As stated in the Act, the County finds that revitalization will not 
occur without additional funding and public investment.  The Act incorporates many 
concepts and powers included in the Urban Renewal Law and Downtown Development 
Law, both of which apply to deteriorated or deteriorated municipal areas, including tax 
increment financing.  While those laws are designed to operate in areas that are blighted 
and found to contain specific statutory factors, the County Revitalization Law is designed 
for unincorporated areas of counties that are characterized by one or more “opportunity 
Factors” listed in Section IV.   
The Area included in the Plan has been studied and designated for development and 
redevelopment in the Comprehensive Plan, the 2023 Housing Needs Assessment, the 
2024 Innovative Housing Opportunities Plan, and the Gardner Village Revitalization Main 
Street Master Plan. The primary purpose of this Plan is to implement the provisions of the 
Comprehensive Plan and other guiding documents by providing additional financial and 
legal benefits contained in the Act to the Revitalization Area. The Comprehensive Plan

IV. Statutory Criteria 
 
The Act defines ”Revitalization area“ as an area that, upon the implementation of a County 
Revitalization Plan, substantially promotes the sound growth of the county, improves 
economic and social conditions, and furthers the health, safety, and well-being of the 
public by the actualization of one of the following opportunity factors: 
 (a)  Investment in critical infrastructure, including water, sanitary sewer and storm 
water systems and management, electricity, and other public utilities to achieve desired 
levels of residential density and employment growth; 
 (b) Improvement of mobility and increased access to transportation corridors and 
multimodal transportation options; 
 (c)  Development of affordable housing proximate to enhanced transportation hubs 
and corridors; 
 (d) Development of economic opportunities for job creation and growth in 
entrepreneurship and successful location of existing businesses; 
 (e)  Expansion of access to healthy food systems, community medical services, 
public parks, or public education opportunities; 
 (f)  Improvement of circulation patterns and enhancement of safe and reliable 
public transportation systems; 
 (g)  Remediation of contaminated soils or water; 
 (h)  Clearance, abatement, or rehabilitation of structurally unsound, deteriorating, 
or otherwise unsafe structures; or 
 (i)  Redevelopment of former landfills, floodplains, or other areas challenged by 
topography that, in their present condition, pose a threat to public health and safety.

VI. Project Activities, Goals, and Priorities 
 
To preserve Huerfano County’s rural character while promoting growth, this Plan will 
concentrate   development in the Gardner village center as prioritized in the 
Comprehensive Plan.  
 (a) Conformity with and Implementation of the Comprehensive Plan.  
 Developments will emphasize pedestrian-friendly design, resilience against natural 
hazards, promote compact growth, and preserve surrounding open spaces. 
The Comprehensive Plan also recommends that the County establish a new village 
zoning district that would allow a wide range of uses, promote compact development and 
community-oriented businesses and services. This type of district would enable orderly 
growth to occur in and around  Gardner. Such zoning would lessen the need for a PUD 
process to facilitate compact, village-style development in the Area.

(b)  The Innovative Housing Opportunities Plan  
 
This Plan will also implement the Innovative Housing Opportunities Plan (IHOP) by 
locating compact development in Gardner.  The Area is a site with proximity to existing 
infrastructure and employment hubs and is designated for affordable and workforce 
housing. This Plan incorporates IHOP’s design strategies, such as mixed-use zoning and 
diverse housing types, to maximize affordable and sustainable housing. 
 
(c)  Economic Development 
 
A goal of this Plan is to encourage the retention and expansion of existing businesses 
and the attraction of new businesses that will benefit the Area by addressing barriers to 
business formation (e.g., affordable housing, transportation, worker education and skills 
development) and target needed business types. The Plan will also support existing 
businesses and encourage the development of local entrepreneurs. 
 
(d)  Traffic, Utilities, Community Facilities, and other Public Improvements 
 
Driving is currently the main mode of transport in the County. The Plan will encourage 
and assist in development of multi-modal facilities, public transportation and on-demand 
services for commuting purposes. The Plan will provide access and options for walking 
and bicycling and connectivity and transportation options for all ages, abilities, and 
income levels, including  opportunities for recreational bicycling and hiking.  
 
This Plan encourages uses providing access to medical care, daycare and childcare 
opportunities, arts and community-building programming, education, and opportunities for 
all age groups in the Area. Activities may include improvement of streetscapes,

II. Introduction 
 
This Plan is intended to apply the powers and provisions of the 2024 County Revitalization 
Law (the “Act”) to an area in the County that would benefit from revitalization and 
economic investment. As stated in the Act, the County finds that revitalization will not 
occur without additional funding and public investment. The Act incorporates many 
concepts and powers included in the Urban Renewal Law and Downtown Development 
Law, both of which apply to deteriorated or deteriorated municipal areas, including tax 
increment financing.  While those laws are designed to operate in areas that are blighted 
and found to contain specific statutory conditions, the County Revitalization Law is 
designed for unincorporated areas of counties that are characterized by one or more 
“opportunity Factors” listed in Section IV.   
The Area included in the Plan has been studied and designated for development and 
redevelopment in the Comprehensive Plan, the 2023 Housing Needs Assessment, the 
2024 Innovative Housing Opportunities Plan, and the Multi-Hazard Mitigation Plan. The 
primary purpose of this Plan is to implement the provisions of the Comprehensive Plan 
and other guiding documents by providing additional financial and legal benefits 
contained in the Act to the Revitalization Area.  The Area is included in the Wellness Hub, 
Page 36 of 169
EXHIBIT B 
Page 4 of 13 
 
one of two defined areas where the Comprehensive Plan designates for compact, village-
style development, distinct from Agricultural and Conservation/Public lands. The intent is 
to concentrate growth in small, well-served areas with existing infrastructure and services, 
reducing pressure on agricultural lands and open space. The Wellness Hub serves and 
benefits from nearby important medical centers. 
This Plan has been proposed for consideration by the Board of County Commissioners 
pursuant to the provisions of the Act. The administration of the Project and the 
implementation of the Plan shall be the responsibility of the Authority.

IV. Statutory Criteria 
 
The Act defines “Revitalization area“ as an area that, upon the implementation of a County 
Revitalization Plan, substantially promotes the sound growth of the county, improves 
economic and social conditions, and furthers the health, safety, and well-being of the 
public by the actualization of one of the following opportunity factors: 
 (a)  Investment in critical infrastructure, including water, sanitary sewer and storm 
water systems and management, electricity, and other public utilities to achieve desired 
levels of residential density and employment growth; 
 (b) Improvement of mobility and increased access to transportation corridors and 
multimodal transportation options; 
 (c)  Development of affordable housing proximate to enhanced transportation hubs 
and corridors; 
 (d) Development of economic opportunities for job creation and growth in 
entrepreneurship and successful location of existing businesses; 
 (e)  Expansion of access to healthy food systems, community medical services, 
public parks, or public education opportunities; 
 (f)  Improvement of circulation patterns and enhancement of safe and reliable 
public transportation systems; 
 (g)  Remediation of contaminated soils or water; 
 (h)  Clearance, abatement, or rehabilitation of structurally unsound, deteriorating, 
or otherwise unsafe structures; or 
 (i)  Redevelopment of former landfills, floodplains, or other areas challenged by

Constitution and Colorado Revised Statutes and such further incidental implied powers as are 
reasonably necessary to carry out the express powers; 
 
WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of Huerfano County, Colorado 
(“Board”) finds that certain changes to the Land Use Code will help to facilitate development 
and improve the processes for obtaining land use permits; 
 
WHEREAS, CRS 30-28-106 (III) requires master plans to include strategies to promote 
strategic growth and prevent rural sprawl and to limit the need to develop natural and agricultural 
lands for residential uses and the following updates help to bring the land use code into 
alignment with the recent update to the Comprehensive Plan; 
 
WHEREAS, existing land use patterns in Huerfano County require the creation of the 
Homesteading Zone to recognize the residential nature of certain large-acre subdivisions and 
protect and distinguish these areas from the agricultural nature of the Agricultural Zone and from 
the residential character of the Rural Residential Zone; 
 
Page 53 of 169
WHEREAS, there are currently sufficient development rights on vacant County parcels 
to increase the population or number of dwelling units in the County by over 65%. Of the 
County’s 12,529 non-exempt parcels, 8,217 are vacant. If a home were to be built on each vacant 
parcel that exists today, it would require a vast extension of roads and electric wires that would 
cut across the landscape, it would impact the water table and wildlife and the low-density, 
spread-out character of development would make it more costly and challenging to provide 
services such as police and fire protection, road maintenance, and school busing. By ceasing to 
create new development rights with the creation of each new parcel, and the ability to transfer 
development rights, the intent of the County is to direct future development to areas of the 
County that are already close to services, jobs, infrastructure and historical development and to 
protect from development those areas of the County where vast open ranges and wild landscapes 
persist; 
 
WHEREAS, the purpose of Planned Unit Developments (PUDs) is to allow a developer 
flexibility to carry out development that would otherwise conflict with existing zoning, the 
sunsetting of unique zoning provisions applied to each PUD removes an unnecessary 
administrative burden and challenge for future owners or prospective owners to be able to access 
and understand the regulations unique to each PUD; 
 
WHEREAS, it is in the public interest to recognize all existing parcels in the County as 
conforming based on their acreage, regardless of their zoning district;
