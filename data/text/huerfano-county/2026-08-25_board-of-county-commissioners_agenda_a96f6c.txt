Key Takeaways & Opportunities
4Huerfano County 2026 Ballot Measure Survey, July-August 2026, 303n, +/-5.5% MoE
Voters see significant needs in the County, but have concerns about how existing resources 
are managed: Half of voters (50%) approve of the County's performance, compared with 38% 
who disapprove. At the same time, 62% believe the County does not currently have the 
financial resources needed to provide an acceptable level of services. However, voters are 
skeptical about fiscal management: 52% disagree that the County is fiscally responsible and 
spends taxpayer money wisely, compared with only 35% who agree. 
The proposed 4-mill property tax increase falls short of majority support: On the initial ballot 
test, 42% would vote yes and 46% would vote no, with 12% undecided. After voters receive 
additional information, the final ballot test moves slightly—to 45% yes and 49% no, and the 
undecided percentage declines to 6%. This suggests the measure has a potential path to 
passage, but significant education would be necessary, and the information tested does not 
significantly change the overall yes/no balance. 
Page 23 of 105
Key Takeaways & Opportunities
5Huerfano County 2026 Ballot Measure Survey, July-August 2026, 303n, +/-5.5% MoE
The modest household cost is the strongest argument for the measure: The most persuasive 
information is the actual tax impact: a home valued at the County median of $242,900 would 
pay approximately $66 more per year. After hearing this, 55% say they are more likely to 
approve the measure, compared with 34% who are more likely to reject it. 
Recreation generates interest, but including Wild Waters also creates a significant 
vulnerability: Recreation figures prominently among supporters of the measure, who 
emphasize the pool, parks, youth activities, and quality-of-life benefits. However, Wild Waters 
is simultaneously a major objection among opponents, who question its previous problems, 
future maintenance costs, and particularly the wisdom of investing in a water-intensive facility 
when water supply, quality, and infrastructure are identified as one of the County's most 
prominent issues.

Please describe the top two or three issues or problems that you think 
Huerfano County should be addressing the most. 
7
Water Supply, Quality & Infrastructure: This is the most prominent concern. Respondents 
repeatedly mention  unreliable or unsafe water, aging/broken water lines, water storage and 
rights, conservation, high water costs, and the need for better coordination between Huerfano 
County and Walsenburg. Concerns about protecting limited water resources from future 
development, particularly data centers, also appear frequently. 
Crime, Drugs & Public Safety: Many respondents identify crime, theft, drug use, 
homelessness/squatting, and inadequate law enforcement as major problems. There are calls 
for more sheriff's deputies, better law enforcement training and equipment, stronger prosecution 
of repeat offenders, and improved emergency/fire services. 
Economic Development, Jobs & Community Revitalization:  Respondents frequently want the 
County to attract businesses and employers, create better -paying jobs, support small businesses, 
and expand workforce opportunities. This is often connected with concerns about Walsenburg's 
appearance—abandoned or dilapidated buildings, poorly maintained properties, and the need for 
code enforcement and downtown/community revitalization.

T20. Please describe the reasons why you would vote yes and approve a 4 -mill property tax 
increase.	
	
Parks, Recreation & Youth Amenities: The strongest theme is support for improving 
recreational facilities, particularly reopening/replacing the Wild Waters pool, improving parks, 
and creating a year-round recreation center. Many respondents emphasize providing children 
and families with more activities and see these amenities as important to quality of life.  
 
Law Enforcement, Jail & Public Safety Improvements: Many respondents support additional 
funding for the Sheriff's Office, more deputies/police presence, better equipment and training, 
and improvements to the jail. However, there is a notable subgroup that supports the tax 
despite opposing jail expansion or additional Sheriff's Office spending, preferring the money go 
toward recreation and other services.

T21. Please describe the reasons why you would vote no and reject a 4 -mill property tax 
increase.	
	
Taxes Are Already Too High / Affordability Concerns: Many respondents say property taxes 
have recently increased substantially and that another increase would be difficult to afford. 
This concern is especially pronounced among seniors and people on fixed incomes, as well as 
respondents who describe Huerfano County as a low-income community already facing rising 
utilities and other living costs.  
 
Distrust of Government Spending & Financial Management: Respondents also express a lack 
of confidence that the County or City will manage additional tax revenue responsibly. They 
frequently cite perceived waste, mismanagement, corruption, lack of transparency, poor 
maintenance, and failure to deliver on previous projects. Many argue that existing funds should 
be audited and managed better before voters are asked for more money.  
 
Opposition to the Proposed Projects, Especially the Water Park — Many respondents question 
whether the proposed projects are the right priorities. The Wild Waters/water park receives 
particularly strong criticism, with concerns about its previous failure, ongoing maintenance 
costs, and the wisdom of investing in a water-intensive amenity while the community faces 
serious water problems. Others question spending on a new jail or sheriff substations and 
would prefer funding basic infrastructure, water, roads, or other essential services.
