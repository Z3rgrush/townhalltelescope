Budget Overview 
• The transition to Tyler Technologies ERP Pro system is in the final stretches.  Already this new 
system, and the procedures the Finance office is building around the system, has transformed 
how the County does business.  Once it is fully operational, we expect that Board, Elected 
Officials, and Department Heads will be able to track spending and manage the financial aspects 
of projects with a precision not previously possible.  I also believe that we will be able to offer 
the public new levels of transparency into the County’s finances.  This Preliminary Budget is the 
first step towards that management and transparency and there are several important notes about 
how this transition impacts this budget: 
o Chart of Accounts: This budget includes a revised Chart of Accounts, that renumbers 
funds, departments, and some line items.  Each line item is assigned a category and 
subcategory, and each line item has a place for a program code.  A necessary addition in 
order to have one general ledger for the entire County, the program code will eventually 
allow us to organize our grants and other areas of special reporting in a way that we hope 
is easier for the public to follow.  That said, we still need to finish cleaning up the new 
chart of accounts and resolve new and pre-existing issues. I hope to finish this work 
before we adopt the final budget. 
o Payroll Related Changes: Payroll is part of this new system and with a new payroll 
system comes new challenges and opportunities.  This payroll system cannot easily 
separate the employee salaries from elected officials.  In this budget, and going forward, 
the salaries of elected officials will appear in the same lines as their employees.  
Eventually, we may fold the historical data for elected officials into the employee lines. 
o Fund Balances: Unlike in previous years this preliminary budget does not show fund 
balances or expected current year spending. Unfortunately, there is no way to display this 
information directly from the system.  This presents a challenge and I have been working 
with our accountants at Redlands to make the necessary calculations to ensure the budget 
is balanced.  We will be able to display the balances in the final budget. 
• The County General Fund (“County General”) continues to struggle, legal bills and expenses 
related to housing prisoners in other Counties have drained an already tight budget for this fund.  
We are fortunate that, in general, most of our revenues are holding.  However, we cannot say the 
same things about costs.  Our County covers 1591 square miles and roughly 700 miles of roads.

We have an obligation to provide services across this territory, which means that the County is 
particularly sensitive to increases in fuel prices.  This is why we operate our own fuel farm.  The 
inflation we have seen over the last several years has taken its toll on our budget, particularly 
County General and Road and Bridge.  Going into next year we will have to find ways to cut 
costs, particularly in County General.  This preliminary budget floats County General for 2027, 
we should not expect to be able to replicate that in future years. 
• Our local economy has been relatively resilient despite major shocks this year from water main 
breaks to fires to skyrocketing fuel costs.  We should anticipate County revenues will remain 
stable over the next year.  Sales tax revenues are generally more subject to fluctuation based on 
economic conditions and we should be prepared for potential weakness in collections, 
particularly if consumer confidence drops. 
• Earlier this year the County conducted a market compensation study to review the 
competitiveness of employee salaries.  This study covered 76 jobs held by 94 employees.  Of 
those 94 employees, 44 were found to be compensated below the minimum range for our job 
market and 4 employees were found to be above the maximum.  Our comparison ratio at 86.67% 
means that our employees are generally paid at the low end of the market.  A 100% ratio would 
mean the County pays our employees at the market rate.  However, there is a wide gap between 
departments with the Assessor’s Office at the low end at 67.84% and the Human Services 
Department at the high end at 97.35%. This preliminary budget attempts to begin to close this 
gap, but the financial reality of County General massively limits our ability to address the issue. 
o This Preliminary Budget includes a 3.6% cost of living increase for all employees.  
Matching the increase proposed by the Colorado Department of Labor for minimum 
wage and exempt employees. 
o In the Human Services and Road and Bridge Departments, I have targeted bringing 
employee salaries to market rates in addition to the cost-of-living increase.  In these 
departments, I have capped the increase at $10,000. 
o In the Emergency Management Department, I have targeted bringing employee salaries 
to the minimum of the salary range in addition to the cost-of-living increase.  Similarly, I 
have capped the combined increase at $10,000. 
o For Departments in County General, I have targeted bringing employee salaries to the 
minimum of the salary range in addition to the cost-of-living increase.  For these 
departments I have capped the combined increase at $4,000. This preliminary budget 
contains no new positions in County General. 
Budget Calendar 
By statute, Huerfano County operates on a fiscal year that begins January 1st and ends on December 
31st.  The County’s Budget Officer is required to submit a balanced preliminary budget to the Board 
of County Commissioners by October 15th of each year and the Commissioners are required to adopt 
the budget by December 15th.  The calendar below outlines the key dates and deadlines for this 
year’s budget process.

Fund Balance and Operating Reserves 
In 2022 the County adopted a fund balance policy, this preliminary budget complies with that policy.  
The primary TABOR reserve is budgeted at $350K in County General.   
 
Working Capital Reserve 
Certain Funds (County General, Road and Bridge, Lodging Tax Tourism, Public Welfare, and 
Emergency Services) are subject to a working capital reserve that is equal to 10% of the budgeted 
operating expenditures and which cannot be used to balance the budget.  In this preliminary budget 
each of these funds meet their reserve requirement.

General Fund Targeted Operating Reserve 
In addition, the General Fund has a target operational reserve of 25% of budget operating 
expenditures for the entire County.  For this preliminary budget the target reserve is $4,027,576.   
This reserve includes the TABOR Reserve, General Fund Working Capital Reserve, and the General 
Fund Unassigned Fund Balance.  The current General Fund Operating Reserve is $1,206,024, which 
is $2,821,552 below the target.

The Huerfano County Works and Temporary Aid to Needy Families (TANF) programs were
implemented July 1, 1997.  These programs were implemented statewide pursuant to the federal 
Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act, commonly known as Welfare Reform
legislation.  The County share is an MOE derived from expenditures from a year prior to 1997.
The focus of this program is to assist participants to become self-sufficient by providing or
purchasing services to eliminate barriers participants may have which prevent them
from being self-sufficient.  These services include, but are not limited to, basic job readiness
training, GED courses, subsidized and unsubsidized on the job training, community work experience 
placements, college courses limited to 12 months, substance abuse avoidance counseling, mental
health counseling, domestic violence elimination counseling, etc.  The services available to
participants are based on an assessment which allows the local flexibility needed to design 
effective plans.  The temporary benefits provided to participants, Temporary Assistance to Needy 
Families or "TANF," are no longer an entitlement.  These benefits are made available only after
the participant agrees to and signs a Road map  and participates as
set forth in that contract.
 The MOE is found in that portion of the budget that deals with the authorized benefits provided to
Huerfano County Works participants and in the Child Welfare 80% State funded sections (pg. 5)
