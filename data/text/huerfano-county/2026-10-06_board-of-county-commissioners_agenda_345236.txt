RESOLUTION NO. 26-46 
 
THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS 
OF HUERFANO COUNTY, COLORADO 
 
A RESOLUTION NAMING COUNTY ROAD 620.1 OF HUERFANO COUNTY, 
COLORADO MALACHITE TRAIL 
 
WHEREAS, the Huerfano County Commissioners responsibilities include the 
health, safety and welfare of the residents of the County; and  
 
WHEREAS there are roads within Huerfano County with similar names or names 
that could cause confusion in life threatening situations, the delivery of goods or services, 
or other purposes; and  
 
WHEREAS, the residents and owners of a certain road in Huerfano County have 
asked that County Road 620.1 be changed to Malachite Trail for the benefit of the citizens; 
and,  
 
WHEREAS, no other existing roads in Huerfano County would be impacted or 
have a conflict with changing County Road 620.1 to Malachite Trail; and  
 
WHEREAS, no responses were received from notified entities opposed to said 
change therefore no public hearing is necessary; and  
 
WHEREAS, the County Commissioners have reviewed the request, and find said 
amendment is consistent with the responsibility for the protection of our citizen’s health, 
safety, and general welfare.  
 
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners 
of Huerfano County Colorado that the request to give County Road 620.1 the name 
Malachite Trail is hereby approved.  
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners that said 
resolution is effective immediately to all property and structures in Huerfano County that 
are the subject of said resolution.  
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that staff is hereby directed to take the necessary 
actions to ensure notification and recording of this Road Name Change, and that it is 
recognized by all affected parties and agencies.

MODIFIED VEHICLE SAFETY
Counties often extend the useful life of vehicles and equipment by adapting them for new purposes. This can be a practical, cost-effective way to 
meet operational needs, especially when budgets and resources are limited. However, when a vehicle is modified for a significantly different use, 
its weight, handling, and other operating characteristics may also change.
Taking a proactive approach to vehicle modifications can help prevent accidents and protect employees. Before placing modified equipment 
into service, counties should carefully evaluate whether the vehicle is safe and appropriate for its new intended use.
WHEN A VEHICLE’S PURPOSE CHANGES
One example of a significant vehicle conversion is a truck originally designed to pull a load that is repurposed as a water tender by removing 
the fifth wheel and installing a large water tank. While this conversion may meet a new operational need, carrying a substantial liquid load can 
change vehicle performance and may require modifications to support its new load-carrying function.
Converting a vehicle in this way can affect its load, center of gravity, suspension, axles, braking, 
steering, tires, and overall handling. A vehicle originally designed to pull a load may perform 
differently when carrying a substantial liquid load. For this reason, evaluate significant modifications 
as a new vehicle configuration—not simply the original vehicle with new  
equipment added. 
CONSIDER THE VEHICLE’S CONDITION
Before investing in a vehicle conversion, consider the condition and service history of the original 
equipment. A vehicle that is no longer suitable for its previous role may not automatically be a 
good candidate for a new one. Age, mileage, prior repairs, existing mechanical concerns, and 
expected service demands should all factor into the decision. Counties should consider whether 
modifying the vehicle is appropriate for its condition and whether the completed vehicle can safely 
and reliably perform the work expected of it.
BUILD SAFETY INTO THE CONVERSION
Repurposing equipment can be appropriate, but counties should make sure the modified 
vehicle is suitable for the work it will perform. Before converted or specialized equipment is 
placed into service:
• Have significant modifications evaluated by a qualified manufacturer, retrofit specialist, or 
other appropriately qualified professional.
• Verify that weight ratings, suspension, brakes, steering, tires, frame, and other affected systems 
are appropriate for the new configuration.
• Maintain maintenance and inspection records for each individual vehicle or piece of 
equipment.
• Develop pre-use inspections specific to the modified equipment and its hazards.
• Provide documented, task-specific training for employees who will operate the equipment.
• Establish written operating procedures addressing loading, speed, braking, roadway 
conditions, and emergency operations.
These controls can help identify potential problems before an employee is placed behind the wheel 
and provide documentation that the county has evaluated the equipment for its intended use.
