RESOLUTION NO. 26-49 
 
THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS 
OF HUERFANO COUNTY, COLORADO 
 
A RESOLUTION APPOINTING CAROL DUNN TO THE FEDERAL MINERAL 
LEASE DISTRICT BOARD FOR A TERM EXPIRING ON DECEMBER 31, 2029 
 
 
 WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners serve as the governing body of 
Huerfano County and are vested with administering the affairs of the County pursuant to state 
statutes; and,  
 
 WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners established the Huerfano County, 
Colorado Federal Mineral Lease District in Resolution 15-03 as an independent district under the 
Federal Mineral Lease District Act, § 30-20-1301, et seq, C.R.S.; and, 
  
WHEREAS, by that same resolution the Board of County Commissioners established the 
membership structure of the Federal Mineral Lease District Board; and, 
 
WHEREAS, Carol Dunn has submitted a letter of interest and has demonstrated her 
qualifications to serve on the Federal Mineral Lease District Board for Huerfano County and 
agrees to serve as a member of the Board while adhering to the principles applicable to 
governmental units and other requirements of law; and, 
 
WHEREAS, the Board of Huerfano County Commissioners desires to appoint Carol 
Dunn to serve as a member of the Federal Mineral Lease District Board as a reflection of the 
values of the Huerfano County Board of County Commissioners. 
 
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Huerfano County Board of County 
Commissioners hereby appoints Carol Dunn to serve as a member of the Federal Mineral Lease 
District Board until December 31, 2029, and a successor has been appointed. 
 
INTRODUCED, READ, APPROVED AND ADOPTED ON THIS 10th day of 
NOVEMBER 2026.

IRS RAISES AFFORDABILITY THRESHOLD FOR 2027
The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has announced an increase in the Affordable Care Act (ACA) affordability threshold for 2027. The percentage 
will rise to 10.22%, up from 9.96% in 2026. This adjustment matters most for Applicable Large Employers (ALEs), including qualifying Colorado 
counties, that must offer affordable health coverage to full-time employees or risk ACA penalties.
WHAT IS THE AFFORDABILITY PERCENTAGE?
Under the ACA’s employer mandate, ALEs are generally employers with 50 or more full-time 
employees, including full-time equivalent employees. To avoid potential penalties, they must offer 
qualifying health coverage that meets minimum value and affordability requirements.
For 2027, an employee’s required contribution toward the lowest-cost, self-only coverage that provides 
minimum value cannot exceed 10.22% of household income to meet the affordability standard. 
Because employers typically do not know an employee’s household income, the IRS provides three 
affordability safe harbors: 
• Form W-2 Wages: Based on the employee’s Form W-2 wages.
• Rate of Pay: Calculated using the employee’s hourly or monthly pay rate.
• Federal Poverty Line: Based on the federal poverty guidelines for a single individual.
 These safe harbors help employers evaluate affordability using information available to them. 
IMPACT ON EMPLOYERS 
The higher affordability percentage gives ALEs more flexibility in setting employee health insur-
ance contributions. Employers may be able to increase employee contributions while remaining 
ACA-compliant, provided their coverage meets all applicable requirements.
However, counties should carefully evaluate any adjustments to ensure coverage remains 
affordable for employees across different positions and pay levels.
SAFE HARBOR CONSIDERATIONS 
Many ALEs use the Federal Poverty Line Safe Harbor to simplify affordability calculations. For 2027 
calendar-year plans in Colorado, the maximum employee contribution for the lowest-cost, self-only 
coverage providing minimum value is $135.92 per month, compared with $129.89 in 2026. This 
gives employers modest flexibility while maintaining affordability protections for employees. 
Different calculations may apply to noncalendar-year plans. 
NEXT STEPS FOR EMPLOYERS 
Counties should begin reviewing their health coverage contribution structures before the 2027 
plan year. Although the higher threshold offers additional flexibility, employers must ensure they 
continue to meet ACA requirements. Key considerations include:
• Reviewing Contribution Models: Evaluate employee premium contributions against the new 
10.22% affordability threshold.
• Using Safe Harbors: Determine whether Form W-2 wages, rate of pay, or federal poverty line 
calculations are most appropriate for the workforce.
• Consulting Advisors: Work with benefits, financial, or legal advisors to verify compliance 
before making changes.
