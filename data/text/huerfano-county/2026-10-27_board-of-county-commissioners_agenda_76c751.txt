 Staff Report  
 
The Planning Commission held a public hearing on Sept. 10, which was continued twice to October 8 
and 22. On October 22, The Commission voted to recommend denial of this application. 
 
Project Location: Parcel 16978. Address: 1112 CR 628, Gardner, CO 81040. 
Legal: N1/2 S1/2 SW1/4 Section 15, Township 25 South, Range 69 West, 6th P.M. 
Parcel Size: 40 ac. 
 
1) Summary of Proposed Project 
GrowAway LLC (Applicant) proposes a project to operate greenhouses, aquaponics and mushroom 
farming powered by biogas and syngas produced on site in a biodigester and industrial pyrolyzer. The 
on-site gas production will be used to heat the greenhouses in order to provide a year-round growing 
season. In addition to heating the agricultural operation in the winter, the energy produced will also 
power six homes, a co-housing building with up to 12 bedrooms, and run some of the machinery 
involved in the operation.  
 
The project will use an anaerobic digester used to produce biogas, fertilizer in the form of liquid 
digestate and biochar from organic waste, including food waste and agricultural byproducts, sourced 
on-site and off-site sources. The projects anticipates receiving waste on a regional scale including from 
Gardner, La Veta and Walsenburg-area residential and small-business accounts. The project also 
proposes an industrial, two-stage pyrolyzer which can process contaminated wastes to produce syngas 
and biochar. The pyrolyzer would be used to treat cat waste from the proposed cat sanctuary, to treat 
any organic waste intended for the biodigester that is found to be contaminated, or waste designated as 
contaminated. The pyrolyzer can neutralize compounds such as PFAS, pharmaceuticals, paint residues, 
residual chemicals, and pathogens or biological contaminants). Without the pyrolyzer, if the biodigester 
were to become contaminated with PFAS, it would require that its contents be filtered for solids, which 
would be trucked away to be disposed of at the Deer Trail facility, and the liquids would be filtered and 
cleaned on site. No proposed testing or of sharing records of such testing of digestate for contaminants 
is included in the application. Presumably, the majority of digestate will be applied to greenhouse crops, 
which means contamination could eventually reach the water table or permanently impact the soil on 
site. The combined biodigester and pyrolyzer constitute a biorefinery within the meaning of 7 U.S.C. 
8103. The application gives some detail on how biogas will be stored, compressed and used, but does 
not explain how syngas will be stored, compressed or used, nor on the scale of production. 
 
The biorefinery will provide year-round energy sufficient to heat seven greenhouses, including 
aquaponics operations, a mushroom cultivation operation, on-site housing and pyrolyzer. In the event 
that excess biogas is produced, the applications request conditional permission to pressurize such gass 
for off-site sale, all syngas will be used on site.

Determining the scale of the proposed project is a challenge. There appears to be a disconnect between 
the stated intended intensity of use and the potential intensity of use and request to set ceilings on 
capacity at or near the full potential of the equipment. The applicant has stated that the biorefinery 
would be primarily used for on-site power needs, and that any export of gas would be incidental, but 
the capacity of the equipment to be installed, the caps on the volume of material to be delivered, and 
the volume of gas to be exported give the impression that, once approved, the project could become 
primarily a commercial gas producing/exporting operation (see discussion of the maximum capacity of 
biodigester and pyrolyzer in traffic impact section).  
 
At the public hearing, at least 23 people showed up to speak out against the project, and one person, 
who was a family member of the applicants spoke in favor. 22 letters were received, 21 of which were 
in opposition with one letter signed by 19 members of Libre and another a petition signed by 53 people. 
Primary concerns include the appropriateness of this location for this type of operation; impact on 
water, including on water table, surface water in Turkey Creek and Victor Ditch, and impacts on Good 
Kharma Spring and impact of project on available water during drought conditions; noise and its 
impact on neighbors and wildlife; fire risk, and the challenge of fighting a fire in that part of the county; 
hazardous waste, including tars, particulate matter, and contaminated biochar from pyrolyzer; fumes 
and odors from waste disposal and pyrolyzer operations; impact on roads, including the narrowness of 
CR 628, safety of pedestrians on county roads, road conditions when icy or muddy, damage to roads by 
volumes of truck traffic; concern about lack of detail on veterinary expertise/service for cats in 
sanctuary; safety of gas compression and storage; erosion risk from earthworks required by the steep 
topology; emergency access if ground is wet or roads not plowed.

2)Site Description 
 
Within the 40-acre parcel. The ownership of the parcel has changed since the application was submitted. 
The proposed development will be concentrated in a 15.7-acre area on the west side of the parcel along 
CR 628, about 7.8 miles north of Hwy 69 accessed via CR 630 and 627. It abuts National Forest to the 
west, and there are about three parcels (¾ mile) between this parcel and National Forest to the north, 
and one to the west (½ mile). This area is heavily wooded with piñon-juniper forest. Part of the project 
area near CR 628 is mildly sloped, but the project area drops off with slopes of around 40% between 
the road and Turkey Creek, which includes the area where greenhouses and homes are proposed. The 
septic leach line is on a slope of around 25%. 
 
Applicant proposes a dedicating a portion of the property to grazing/wildlife, and conserving a 50-ft 
riparian no-build buffer and a 100-ft OWTS/well setback along a watercourse. The County received a 
letter from CPW after the closure of the public hearing that recommended a 500-foot buffer around 
Turkey Creek. The project is in an ecologically diverse part of the County. CPW listed 34 Species of 
Greatest Conservation Need documented in the area, and recommends no human activity during May 
15-June 30 in the western portion of the property, which intersects with Elk Production Area, and no 
human activity in the Elk Severe Winter Range from Dec. 1 to April 30. In the southeast corner of the 
project, which is part of the mule deer winter range, CPW requests no human activities from Dec. 1 to 
April 30. 
 
The waste receiving area will be located at a second entrance to the property (north of current driveway) 
off of CR 628. There will be a parking area near the site of the Barndominium which will minimize on-
site vehicle circulation and serve all of the anticipated dwelling units. The existing home and 
greenhouse are accessed through an existing driveway. The site plan appears to indicate that there will 
be no internal roads connecting directly to the additional dwelling units, greenhouse, aquaponics and 
mushroom stand alone, and the helipad. All structures will be connected by an underground utility 
corridor. The water tower will be directly adjacent the biogas storage and energy pavilion. 
 
The camping area will be on the west side of CR 628, adjacent to National Forest land. 
 
Surrounding uses are rural-residential homesteads, undeveloped agricultural and grazing land, and 
small-acreage residential parcels. There are around 13 residential structures within approximately ¼

3) Impacts at Full Build-Out 
The following is an overview of the estimated impacts of the project at full build-out based on what 
was stated in the application.  
1) Noise: Significant sources of noise from proposed operations include: 
(a) Generators: Backup CHP (combined heat and power) generator: a 75 kWe generator 
that would be used 50 – 200 hrs per year during grid outages, when operations demand 
high loads, when the low pressure dome and medium pressure gas storage are depleted, 
and for testing. The application also describes this unit as having integrated heat 
recovery of 460,000 BTU/hr. Page 16/37 of the application lists the applications of 
recovered heat as part of ongoing operations, raising doubt on the claim that its use 
would only be intermittent.  
• At the public hearing, applicant stated that the project would include another 50 kWe 
CHP generator for constant 24-hr/day operation. The application only made mention 
of a backup generator that would have minimal and occasional use. The constant 24-
hr generator is consistent with descriptions of how the heat exchange would be used 
in daily operations, and this was likely an oversight. 
(b) Gas processing and compression: Gas would pass through three stages of compression, 
with most on-site use being at less-compressed stages, and high-pressure compressed 
natural gas prepared for the vehicle fueling dispenser and for export.  
(c)  On-parcel CNG vehicle-refueling dispenser  ~55 dBA at 10 ft. 
(d) Truck arrivals (15-30 min each). Up to 35 arrivals for deliveries per day. Additional 
truck arrivals for exports. The number of truck trips is discussed in greater detail in the 
traffic section below.  
(e) Traffic (see below) 
(f) Thermal Distribution Loop & heating: Centralized heating system for all operations, 
plus forced air heating in greenhouses. To be heated with backup CHP unit. 
(Application states this is to be done with the backup CHP unit, but these operations do 
not seem to be backup operations lasting 50-200 hrs per year). 
(g) Pyrolyzer: can be kept at 50-55 dBA at the property line. Application limits operation 
to daytime hours only – not clear if this includes startup/cooldown cycles.

Is the Biodigester a Conditional Use (.55) or a Use By Right? 
In the staff report to the planning Commission, the biodigester was presented as a use by right based on 
the reasoning that it is essentially a composting operation, and composting is accessory to agriculture, 
and therefore an agricultural by-right use. The referral agency letter from CDPHE stated that the State 
views this as a solid waste facility requiring a Certificate of Designation from the County because not 
all of the inputs are agricultural inputs. If the County is going to issue a certificate of designation to the 
biodigester and pyrolyzer as solid waste treatment facilities, it would be internally consistent to 
consider them as such in the use table (as .25: Sewage disposal areas, sludge, septage, landfills for non-
hazardous substances and waste water treatment plants, which is a conditional use in the Agricultural 
district).

Does this project trigger a 1041 review? 
A 1041 review, rather than a conditional use permit, is required when: 
 
• Establishing a New Community (defined as the least of either 25 dwelling units, 100 residents, 
or one percent of the county's most recent U.S. Census population numbers). 
• Constructing a water distribution system serving more than 15 service connections or 25 people 
• A project proposes to use over 3 acre-feet of water in a water distribution or wastewater system.

• A water-related project will cause a depletion of more than one acre foot to the water basin of 
origin within the county. 
 
The applicant proposes to use 5,760 gallons of water/day (described as “in-building demand”), which 
amounts to 6.45 acre-feet/year, which would be considered a water-related activity. A water or 
wastewater project that uses more than 3 acre-feet per year would require a 1041 review rather than a 
CUP review. While this exceeds 3 acre-feet per year, it would not be considered a New Community, as 
it includes only six homes. But depending on the scale of the combined residential, commercial, 
industrial water distribution and wastewater treatment, which is not described in the application, it may 
qualify for 1041 review as a water related activity. In considering this application, the Planning 
Commission assumed that the majority of water use would be for agricultural use in the greenhouses, 
and therefore would not trigger a 1041, however the applicant has not provided information on the 
amount of water that will be treated in the OWTS or broken down how much of the proposed water use 
is for agricultural/non-agricultural uses. If 3 or more acre feet are being used to support non-
agricultural uses, or are being treated as wastewater, or if it causes a depletion of more than one acre 
foot to the water basin of origin within the county, it should trigger a 1041 review (See 11-338 and 11-
336(j)). 
The application states that the OWTS will receive ~3,500 gallons per day, but that some of this will be 
produced water (not coming from commercial well, but from water extracted from feedstock)(p. 27/37). 
This would amount to ~3.9 acre-feet per year being processed as wastewater.

WHEREAS, the residents of Huerfano County have great respect, admiration and the utmost 
gratitude for all the men and women who have selflessly served our country and this community 
in the Armed Forces; and

Foreign Wars, religious groups, civil service, and as County Veterans Service Officers in 29 states 
to help fellow former service members access more than $52 billion in federal health, disability 
and compensation benefits each year; and

annually; and

declares November 4, 2026 through November 11, 2026, as a time to salute and honor the service 
and sacrifices of those transitioning from active service; and

INTRODUCTION: 
This Memorandum of Understanding (“MOU”) is entered into by and between Prowers County, 
Colorado body corporate and politic by and through its Board of County Commissioner (“Prowers 
County’), and Huerfano County, Colorado (“Huerfano County”) a body corporate and politic by and 
through its Board of County Commissioners.  Prowers County and Huerfano County shall jointly be 
referred to as the “Parties.” 
PURPOSE: 
1. This MOU is developed in partnership between Prowers County and Huerfano County, with 
confirmation by the State of Colorado (“State”), for Prowers County to manage and administer calls to 
the hotline regarding persons that reside in Huerfano County, Colorado. 
2. Prowers County, through its Hotline County Connection Center (“HCCC”), agrees to answer and 
process Child Welfare (“CW”) related and APS related hotline calls on behalf of Huerfano County 
(“Call Coverage Services”).  Prowers County will perform the tasks outlined in this MOU as they relate 
to Call Coverage Services.  Huerfano County will perform the tasks identified in the MOU to assist the 
HCCC with successfully receiving the Hotline calls so Huerfano County can complete the final 
disposition of each call.

2027.

extension.

agreement of the Parties.

instrument, executed and signed by the Parties, and will be effective in accordance with the terms 
and conditions contained herein.
