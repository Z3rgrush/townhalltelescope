Connecting Land, People & Place 
A Regional Geoheritage Story Map 
A Concept Paper for Prospective Coalition Partners 
Initiated by La Veta Trails | October 2026 
 
The vision 
La Veta Trails (LVT) proposes to convene a regional coalition to develop a professionally 
designed, interactive story map connecting the geological features of Huerfano and Las Animas 
counties with their ecological, historical, and cultural heritage. 
 
The story map would bring together scientific research, professional cartography, historical 
resources, community stories, photography, and video to create an accessible public resource. 
It would help residents, students, and visitors explore how the landscape formed, how people 
have related to it over time, and why these places matter today. 
 
Why now? 
LVT was invited to present at the Geological Society of America's 2026 Annual Meeting in 
Denver. The presentation, Connecting People to Place: A Community Engagement Model for 
Advancing Geoheritage Stewardship, highlights LVT's partnership with CU TRaIL and the 
importance of community partnerships in making geology accessible beyond academic settings. 
This recognition provides a timely opportunity to build on LVT's existing work and bring 
together organizations with complementary expertise, resources, and connections to the 
region. 
 
What we're building on 
LVT has established a foundation for community-centered geoheritage through: 
• Scientific partnerships: Collaboration with Lon Abbott, Sabrina Kainz, and the University 
of Colorado Boulder Thermochronology Research and Instrumentation Laboratory (CU 
TRaIL). Since 2024, Lon and Sabrina have participated in community talks and hikes, 
including Hiking Through Time in July 2026, documented by Blacktimber Media. Another 
community talk and hike is planned for July 2027. 
• Indigenous perspectives: Donald Whyte, a retired National Park Service ranger, is 
scheduled to present Legacy of the Land: A Ute Perspective on May 7, 2027, in La Veta. 
• Cultural heritage partnership: LVT and the Huerfano County Historical Society have 
established a partnership to share and promote local history and cultural heritage. 
• Public resources: The LVT geoheritage website and three geoheritage trail guides 
developed by Lon Abbott and Sabrina Kainz. 
Page 23 of 61
2 
 
• Stories of Land, People & Time: A separate $30,000 project to produce approximately 
6–10 professional video stories featuring people whose lives, work, and experiences 
connect them to the land. The Robert Hoag Rawlings Foundation has awarded $10,000, 
leaving $20,000 to be raised. Blacktimber Media is the videography partner. 
• Community engagement: Eleven years of LVT's work connecting people with trails and 
public lands through stewardship, volunteerism, education, and community 
partnerships. 
 
Together, these efforts demonstrate an established commitment to connecting scientific 
knowledge, community experience, and stewardship. The videos being developed through 
Stories of Land, People & Time will provide an initial collection of stories of the people whose 
lives are shaped by the landscape. 
 
Opportunities ahead 
A regional story map would create opportunities to: 
• Interpret the landscape: Connect distinctive geological features with ecological 
processes and the region's natural history. 
• Connect geology and cultural heritage: Explore Indigenous and Hispanic relationships to 
place, regional history, historic sites, and the people and communities shaped by the 
landscape. 
• Share community voices: Incorporate video, photographs, oral histories, and personal 
stories that deepen understanding of the land. 
• Make knowledge accessible: Create a free, interactive resource for education, outdoor 
discovery, heritage interpretation, and responsible visitation. 
• Build regional collaboration: Connect scientific, historical, cultural, educational, 
tourism, and outdoor-recreation organizations through a shared initiative. 
 
Professional cartography 
LVT has reached out to Anna Risling, an award-winning cartographer, geologist, and story map 
creator who owns Four Corners Mapping and GIS in Durango, Colorado. Anna has expressed 
strong interest in the project. Her expertise could help translate the region's geological and 
cultural stories into a compelling, geographically connected public experience. Examples of her 
work are available at Four Corners Mapping and GIS. 
 
An emerging coalition 
Building on its existing partnerships, LVT invites additional organizations and individuals to help 
shape the vision, scope, and development of the story map. Prospective additional coalition 
members include:

• Huerfano County Tourism Board 
• Spanish Peaks Outdoor Coalition 
• Pueblo History Museum and other appropriate cultural heritage organizations and

The coalition would collaboratively identify priorities, responsibilities, resources, and 
opportunities for participation. 
 
Potential funding sources 
• Humanities and public-history funding, particularly National Endowment for the 
Humanities (NEH) and Colorado Humanities, for cultural interpretation, historical 
research, and community stories. 
• Tourism and heritage funding, working with the Huerfano County Tourism Board and 
other eligible partners to explore visitor education and regional heritage opportunities. 
• Colorado foundations and individual donors, seeking support for the cartography, digital 
development, and regional coordination that make the story map possible. 
 
The opportunity to collaborate 
The regional story map is a proposed, currently unfunded initiative. It will require its own 
project plan, budget, and funding strategy. The remaining $20,000 needed for Stories of Land, 
People & Time represents a separate but complementary funding opportunity. 
 
Together, we can create a lasting public resource that connects land, people, and time—and 
deepens understanding of the landscapes and cultural heritage of southern Colorado.

General Copy Tips
 › Clear, concise communications usually beat clever 
concepts. However, sometimes a clever concept 
can reinforce your brand perception.
 › Always articulate the “why” and “how” you want 
your users to engage with your content.
 › Focus on one message—when you give your users 
too many messages and links, they typically won’t 
take any action at all.
 › Keep instructions on how to respond simple  
and scanable. 
 › Identify one main selling point and make sure you 
communicate it at every touchpoint.
 › Try not to generalize your audience. Focus on the 
user’s individual needs.
 › Prioritize main benefits, starting with the most 
important, to establish a clear message hierarchy. 
 › Break up long sentences that are more than  
10 words.
 › Use simple English and common language
Email Recommendations
Subject/From Lines
 › Subject lines can make or break an email 
campaign.
 › Continually test subject lines to maximize 
response.
 › Try to keep your subject line around 45 characters, 
if possible.
 › Test your “from” line—it can significantly impact 
response.
Design
 › Use web-safe fonts whenever possible (or provide 
an appropriate fallback stack).
 › Code buttons and images with “alt” text so that  
a text description will appear with images off.
 › Design for a width of less than 600 pixels for 
optimum mobile viewing (don’t worry about this if 
your email is responsive).
Copy
 › Keep copy as succinct as possible and avoid long 
paragraphs.
 › Test copy: minimal, billboard style versus longer, 
print ad style copy. 
 › Include pre-header text that summarizes offer and 
includes a link. Pre-headers can be used as a 1–2 
punch with your subject line.
 › Not every email has to include an offer or a sales 
tactic. Less promotional emails often perform well 
among current customers.
