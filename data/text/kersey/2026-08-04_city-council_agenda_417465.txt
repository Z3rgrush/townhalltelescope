The site is located along the U.S. Highway 34 corridor and the Union Pacific Railroad and is framed by 
an existing pattern of rural residential, intensive agricultural, and industrial uses.  Surrounding land uses include rural 
residential properties to the north and northeast, the Kohler Farms industrial subdivision immediately south of Highway 
34, Chevron oil and gas facilities to the east, and the Five Rivers cattle feedlot nearby, all of which support the 
suitability of the site for employment-oriented industrial development. 
 
The subject property was formerly irrigated farmland, but the previous landowner sold the water rights to Aurora, and 
the property is subject to a dry-up agreement.  Staff understands that, as a condition of converting the existing 
residential well to an industrial well, the State of Colorado will not allow irrigation, which substantially limits the viability 
of conventional landscape screening for the site. This context is relevant because Chapter 20 includes both fencing 
standards and landscape/buffering standards intended to shape development character and mitigate impacts. 
 
Staff also notes that the site design approach and the limited-landscaping context were addressed in the approved 
annexation agreement for the property.  The Town's annexation provisions require an annexation agreement to outline 
responsibilities and other matters related to the impacts of annexation, and the Code contemplates that annexed areas 
must develop in an orderly manner consistent with Town standards and approved agreements. 
APPLICABLE CODE PROVISIONS 
Section 20-18 states the intent of the fence standards is: "To ensure that walls and fences are attractive and in 
character with the neighborhood. Recognizing that fences are used to create privacy, Kersey encourages privacy 
fences be located close to the house and not alongside and rear property lines."  Section 20-18 also provides that 
fences in interior side and rear yards shall not exceed a height of six feet. 
Section 22-114 establishes the exception process for Community Design Principles and Development Standards. 
Under that section, the applicant must submit a request letter to the Town Manager, and the Planning Commission and 
Board of Trustees are to evaluate the request under the review criteria for "Justification for Exception" and "Alternate 
Design Quality." 
The approval criteria in Section 22-114 require findings that either special circumstances prevent full compliance or that 
the proposed alternative better achieves the intent of the standard, that strict application would not achieve the 
intended outcome in this context, that the modification may improve the application of other design standards, and that 
the exception will remain consistent with the Comprehensive Plan and Municipal Code, protect public safety, avoid 
significant adverse environmental impacts, maintain neighborhood character, and include reasonable mitigation for any 
potential adverse impacts. 
ANALYSIS 
1. Justification for Exception 
Staff finds there is a reasonable basis for the Planning Commission to conclude that special circumstances and 
site-specific context justify consideration of the exception.  The property is large, with the fenced portion limited 
to approximately 6.5 acres of a 49.5-acre tract, while the balance remains open prairie grassland, which 
reduces the visual intensity of development across the site as a whole. 
More importantly, the site lacks the practical ability to rely on conventional irrigated landscaping as the primary 
screening method.  The property was formerly irrigated farmland, but the prior sale of water rights to Aurora 
and the associated dry-up conditions, together with the limitation on exterior irrigation tied to the industrial well, 
create a site constraint that is not based on financial hardship or owner preference.  Under Section 22-114, 
financial hardship and maintenance concerns are excluded factors, but site-specific constraints that make strict 
 
Page | 3 
 
adherence impractical may support an exception. 
Here, the inability to provide irrigated perimeter landscaping at a scale that would meaningfully 
screen a 6.5-acre industrial compound supports a finding that strict adherence to the six-foot fence standard 
may not achieve the intended design outcome at this location.  Staff therefore finds the applicant has 
presented a credible code-based justification for an exception request. 
2. Better Achievement of Section 20-18 Intent 
Although Section 20-18 discourages privacy fences along side and rear property lines in typical neighborhood 
settings, the proposed fence is not intended to create residential-style separation between neighboring homes. 
Instead, it is proposed as part of an industrial development pattern on a large annexed property where the 
primary objective is to screen business operations and outdoor storage in a manner that is orderly, durable, 
and compatible with the proposed building design. 
Staff finds the proposed seven-foot metal fence can reasonably be viewed as better achieving the stated intent 
of Section 20-18 than a standard six-foot fence in this specific context.  Because the fence will match the 
proposed metal building, it will create a coordinated design treatment rather than an improvised or visually 
inconsistent screening element, which directly supports the requirement that fences be attractive and in 
character with the surrounding development pattern for the site. 
A taller fence also better screens trailers, equipment, and business operations from public view, especially 
given the limited ability to supplement the fence with landscaping.  On a large industrial parcel with outdoor 
storage, a six-foot fence may not sufficiently screen stored materials, trailers, and associated activity, whereas 
a seven-foot fence provides additional visual buffering while remaining modest in scale when compared to 
industrial fencing commonly used for operational screening and security. 
3. Impacts on Adjacent Properties and Public Realm 
Staff finds the request is also supportable because the proposed fence is designed to limit impacts on adjacent 
properties and the public realm rather than intensify them.  The applicant has identified several functional 
purposes for the additional fence height: screening of business operations, acting as a sound barrier, helping 
prevent fugitive dust from leaving the site, providing additional security, and screening trailers from public view. 
These functions align with the broader purposes of Chapter 20, which seeks to ensure development does not 
create avoidable adverse impacts and that site design contributes to an orderly and well-designed community. 
In this setting, the additional one foot of height is a targeted mitigation tool that may reduce off-site views, noise 
perception, and wind-driven dust movement more effectively than a code-compliant six-foot fence. 
The surrounding context also matters. Much of the property will remain in prairie grassland, and the site is not 
described as a compact residential neighborhood where a tall privacy fence along lot lines would undermine 
streetscape character.  As a result, staff finds the requested increase to seven feet is more likely to maintain 
compatibility with the site's industrial and open-land context than to disrupt neighborhood character. 
4. Relationship to Other Standards and Prior Approvals 
Section 22-114 allows approval where the modification improves the application of other design standards or 
where strict adherence is impractical due to site-specific features.  Staff finds both concepts are relevant here 
because the taller fence compensates, in part, for the limited ability to install irrigated landscape screening and 
therefore improves the site's overall buffering and screening performance under Chapter 20. 
Staff also finds it relevant that the limited-landscaping situation and overall site development approach were 
addressed in the approved annexation agreement. While the annexation agreement does not itself replace the 
need for a formal exception decision, it provides additional context showing that the Town has already 
recognized the unusual water and site conditions affecting how the property can be developed.

STAFF FINDINGS 
Based on the application materials and the standards in Section 22-114, staff finds the Planning Commission may 
recommend approval of the requested exception based on the following findings: 
• Special circumstances exist due to the property's former irrigated agricultural condition, the sale of water to 
Aurora, the dry-up agreement, and the prohibition on exterior irrigation associated with conversion of the well to 
industrial use, which collectively limit the feasibility of conventional landscape screening. 
• Strict application of the six-foot maximum fence height would not fully achieve the intended outcome of Section 
20-18 in this context because a six-foot fence would provide less effective screening of business operations, 
outdoor storage, and trailers on a large industrial site with minimal landscaping opportunities. 
• The proposed seven-foot metal fence better achieves the intent of Section 20-18 by creating an attractive, 
coordinated, and site-appropriate screening element that matches the proposed metal building and is in 
character with the industrial development proposed on the site. 
• The proposed exception improves the application of related buffering and screening standards by providing 
additional visual screening, some sound attenuation, and assistance with controlling fugitive dust leaving the 
site. 
• The request is not based on excluded factors such as financial hardship, loss of prospective profits, or 
maintenance concerns. 
• The exception can be approved with conditions that ensure the fence materials, color, location, and 
maintenance remain consistent with the approved site plan and do not create adverse impacts on adjacent 
properties or the public realm. 
RECOMMENDATION 
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission recommend approval with conditions of the requested exception to 
allow a seven-foot-tall metal fence around the proposed building and outdoor storage area for 6Ps, Limited. 
Recommended conditions of approval are as follows: 
1. The exception shall apply only to the fenced area shown on the approved site plan, generally encompassing 
the proposed building and outdoor storage area of approximately 6.5 acres. 
2. The fence shall be constructed of metal materials substantially matching the color, finish, and design character 
of the approved principal building. 
3. The maximum fence height authorized by this exception shall be seven feet. 
4. Any gates shall be constructed of materials and finishes consistent with the fence and principal building. 
5. Outdoor storage, trailers, and operational areas shall be maintained within the fenced area as shown on the 
approved site plan. 
6. The remainder of the property outside the approved development area shall remain in dryland grass except as 
otherwise authorized by Town approval. 
7. Any future modification to the fence design, height, location, or materials shall require Town review and 
approval in accordance with the Land Use Code.

LANDSCAPE DESIGN INTENTIONS
THE LANDSCAPE DESIGN FOR THE SITE HAS BEEN THOUGHTFULLY FOCUSED AROUND THE
PROPOSED BUILDING, PARKING AREAS, AND OUTDOOR STORAGE YARD IN ORDER TO
PROVIDE ENHANCED AESTHETICS AND VISUAL SOFTENING FOR THE OPERATIONAL
COMPONENTS OF THE DEVELOPMENT.
LANDSCAPING ALONG THE ROADWAY FRONTAGES IS ALSO INTENDED TO HELP SCREEN
VIEWS OF THE PARKING, STORAGE, AND INDUSTRIAL ACTIVITIES FROM ADJACENT PUBLIC
STREETS WHILE MAINTAINING AN ATTRACTIVE STREETSCAPE PRESENCE.
PLANT MATERIALS AND OVERALL DESIGN INTENT EMPHASIZE LOW WATER USE, DURABILITY,
AND REDUCED MAINTENANCE REQUIREMENTS. GIVEN THE OVERALL SIZE OF THE
PROPERTY, THE LANDSCAPING ASSOCIATED WITH THIS INITIAL PHASE HAS BEEN
INTENTIONALLY CONCENTRATED WITHIN AND AROUND THE CURRENTLY DEVELOPED
PORTIONS OF THE SITE WHILE ALLOWING FLEXIBILITY FOR FUTURE EXPANSION AND
CONTINUED SITE IMPROVEMENTS.
