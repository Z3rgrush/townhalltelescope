OFFICIAL PROCLAMATION 
 
WHEREAS,  Code Enforcement Officers provide an essential service to the residents, 
businesses, and visitors of the Town of Kersey by promoting the health, safety, and 
quality of life of our community through the fair and consistent application of local 
codes and ordinances; and 
WHEREAS,  Code Enforcement Officers work with property owners, residents, businesses, and 
other Town departments to identify concerns, encourage voluntary compliance, 
resolve neighborhood issues, and maintain the standards established by the 
community; and 
WHEREAS,  the work of Code Enforcement Officers often requires patience, professionalism, 
sound judgment, effective communication, and a commitment to ﬁnding practical 
solutions while balancing individual circumstances with the needs of the 
community; and 
WHEREAS,  Code Enforcement Officers serve an important role in protecting property values, 
maintaining safe and attractive neighborhoods, addressing nuisances and unsafe 
conditions, and helping make Kersey a welcoming place to live, work, and visit; and 
WHEREAS,  the Town of Kersey recognizes that successful code enforcement is not simply 
about enforcement, but about education, cooperation, problem-solving, and 
building positive relationships within the community; and 
WHEREAS,  Code Enforcement Officer Appreciation Week provides an opportunity to recognize 
the dedication and valuable contributions of the professionals who perform this 
important and often challenging public service. 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Nathan Roth, Mayor of the Town of Kersey, Colorado, on behalf of the Board of 
Trustees, do hereby proclaim October 11–17, 2026, as 
CODE ENFORCEMENT OFFICER APPRECIATION WEEK 
in the Town of Kersey and encourage our residents, businesses, and community partners to join us 
in recognizing and expressing appreciation for the professionalism, dedication, and service of our 
Community Service Technician.  
 
PROCLAIMED this 13th day of October, 2026. 
      
 
Nathan Roth, Mayor

New Business 
1. Consideration of Resolution 2026-0022 Awarding the Contract for the Kersey Clark Street 
Storm Sewer Project to Mountain Constructors, Inc.; and Authorizing the Mayor to Execute the 
Contract 
 
Town Engineer, Gene MacDonald, stated that the Town received 13 bids for the project. 
MacDonald and Scott Larson with Benchmark reviewed the bids and compared the two lowest 
bidders. Staff recommended awarding the contract to the second-lowest bidder, Mountain 
Constructors, Inc., after identifying concerns with the lowest bidder, including negative 
references and issues with certain bid line items. 
 
MacDonald stated that Mountain Constructors received excellent references and that both he 
and Larson have previously worked successfully with the company. He also noted that 
Mountain Constructors agreed to hold its bid pricing for 180 days while the Town awaits 
availability of FEMA funding. The bid came in well below the engineer’s estimate. MacDonald 
clarified that the contract is for construction and that additional project-related costs will be 
incurred separately. 
There was no public comment and/or comments from the Board of Trustees. 
Motion made by Trustee Joens, seconded by Trustee Dusin to approve Resolution 2026-0022 
Awarding the Contract for the Kersey Clark Street Storm Sewer Project to Mountain 
Constructors, Inc.; and Authorizing the Mayor to Execute the Contract. Motion carried with a 4-
0 vote.

3. Town Manager 
Stacy Brown, Town Manager, provided several project and community updates.  
 
The Quality Assurance Project Plan (QAPP) for the EPA and CDPHE remains under review. 
Staff will return to the Board with options for pigeon control. 
 
The Town held a stakeholder meeting regarding the Water Blueprint. Due to State 
requirements and the project timeline, additional discussions are anticipated at the November 
and December work sessions, with adoption anticipated in December. Central Weld County 
Water District, Northern Water, and Latham Reservoir were among the stakeholders 
discussed. 
 
Brown reported that staff met with the Colorado Department of Local Affairs regarding EIAF 
grant funding and discussed the possibility of receiving approximately 8% reimbursement. 
Letters regarding the Town’s FEMA funding were also sent to representatives in the offices of 
Senator Hickenlooper, Senator Bennet, and Congressman Evans. FEMA funding remains 
pending. 
 
Brown attended Highway 34 transportation meetings and provided an update regarding 
ongoing fire watch requirements at the Penske property. The Town sent a certified letter to Mr. 
Sparrow regarding responsibility for the property, and an inspection is scheduled for 
September 10. 
 
Land surveys have been completed for the Wedges property and Berryman Outlot 3. A minor 
adjustment is necessary because a water valve was found to be located outside of the 
easement, and the Town will be responsible for the associated cost. 
 
Brown reported that estimates for a monument sign have ranged from approximately $40,000 
to $60,000, while $20,000 was originally budgeted for the project, including design costs 
already incurred. Additional information will be brought back to the Board after a third quote is 
received. 
 
Staff continues to work on preparation of the Town budget. 
 
Brown reminded the Board of the Main Street meeting scheduled for 5:00 p.m. and 
encouraged Board members to attend if available. Approximately ten members of the public 
were anticipated to participate. 
 
Brown provided a brief update regarding a potential annexation involving property that may 
include both residential and commercial development. 
 
Unapproved Minutes – 09/10/2026 
Regarding the Centennial Commerce Center, Brown reported that staff met with a new 
engineer and anticipates receiving a site plan addressing infrastructure. The project continues 
to await CDPHE approval of the lift station. 
 
Brown reported that Public Works Director Fernando Pina completed roadway assessments 
using Vitalytics. The resulting data provides detailed information regarding roadway conditions 
that will assist the Town with cost estimates, prioritization, and long-term maintenance 
planning. She also recognized Pina’s efforts in identifying safety concerns, including signage 
needs. 
 
The Town is testing a Zoom option that will allow members of the public to view Board 
meetings and work sessions online. The general public link will provide viewing access only 
and will not allow participation or public comment. Individuals who need to participate remotely 
will receive a unique link. Staff is testing the system before making it available for future 
meetings. 
 
Brown reported that the Town is finalizing its contract with the new IT service provider following 
the Town’s procurement process. The new provider is anticipated to begin services October 1, 
with services from the current provider concluding October 4. 
Mosquito spraying has concluded for the year. 
 
Brown discussed potentially upgrading the Town Hall audio/video security system to include 
audio recording in public areas. She also reported that policies have been developed regarding 
incident reporting and meetings with members of the public to improve documentation and 
employee safety. The Board expressed consensus for staff to obtain pricing for the proposed 
audio/video upgrades. 
 
Brown provided an update regarding the Main Street settlement and noted that the warranty 
period extends through mid-December. Staff will contact Mountain Constructors regarding 
completing necessary repairs before asphalt plants close for the winter. 
 
Brown reminded the Board of the September 15 informational session regarding the ballot 
initiative, scheduled for 6:00 p.m. at Town Hall. Mayor Roth plans to attend, and staff has 
prepared a frequently asked questions document for the meeting. 
 
Brown also reminded the Board of the CML Region 2 meeting in Frederick on September 24 at 
5:00 p.m. She plans to attend, and reservations are required.

MEMORANDUM 
 
Date:  October 13, 2026 
To:   Town Board 
Via:  Stacy Brown, Town Manager 
From:  Haley Curtis, Town Clerk 
Re: Ordinance No. 2026-0012 – Referral of Citizen-Initiated Ordinance to a 
Special Election 
Item #: B2 
Background 
As you are all aware, Bill Cannon and Tim Phippard filed a citizen initiative petition seeking to 
reduce the Town's property tax mill levy from 17.205 mills to 12.000 mills for a ten-year period 
beginning January 1, 2028, and ending December 31, 2037. 
The citizen initiative process is complicated on its own, and this particular initiative has been 
made more challenging by the fact that it involves a TABOR issue. While TABOR is a subject 
matter eligible for the citizen initiative process, the statutory requirements for citizen initiatives 
and the constitutional requirements of TABOR do not necessarily align neatly. 
I would like to provide the Board with a brief history of this petition and an explanation of the 
process moving forward. 
Petition Timeline 
• August 5, 2026: Mr. Cannon and Mr. Phippard made their initial contact with the Town 
regarding the proposed initiative. Town Attorney Rick Zier and I met with the proponents 
the following Monday. The initial document submitted was not a petition that could be 
approved for circulation. While neither Rick nor I could provide legal advice to the 
proponents, I did my best to explain the process and point them in the right direction. 
• August 17, 2026: I received a petition that met enough of the statutory requirements to 
begin the formal review process, although substantial revisions were still necessary. 
• August 24, 2026: I received an amended petition, which still required additional 
revisions. 
• August 27, 2026: I received a further amended petition for review. 
• August 28, 2026: I notified the proponents that their petition had been approved as to 
form and provided the neutral summary required to be furnished by the Town Clerk. 
• August 31, 2026: The proponents returned the completed petition for final approval prior 
to circulation. I issued that approval approximately two and a half hours after receiving it. 
• September 2, 2026: The proponents filed their completed petition, containing 64 valid 
signatures. A minimum of 51 valid signatures was required. 
• September 9, 2026: Following verification of the signatures, I issued the Notice of 
Sufficiency. 
 
 
 
 
 
 2 
By statute, the Town Clerk has 30 days to review and verify petition signatures. The 30-day 
review period and the 40-day protest period run concurrently. Although I was permitted the full 
statutory review period, I completed the signature verification and issued the Notice of 
Sufficiency within seven days of receiving the petition. 
Protest Period and Final Determination 
The deadline to file a protest concerning the petition is October 12, 2026. 
Ordinance No. 2026-0012 has been prepared under the assumption that no timely protest will 
be filed. 
Final determination of petition sufficiency occurs following expiration of the statutory protest 
period if no protest is filed, or, if a protest is filed, upon a finding of sufficiency following the 
protest process, as provided by C.R.S. §§ 31-11-103 and 31-11-110. 
Provided no protest is filed by the deadline, I anticipate issuing the final determination of 
sufficiency at approximately 10:00 a.m. on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, prior to the Board's 
regularly scheduled meeting. 
Board Action and TABOR Considerations 
Once the final determination of sufficiency has been issued, the Board of Trustees has 20 days 
to take action on the proposed measure. 
This is where the citizen initiative process and TABOR requirements become particularly 
complicated. 
Under the citizen initiative process, the proposed ordinance must be written in a manner that 
would allow the Board of Trustees to adopt the measure without alteration, should it choose to 
do so. Ordinarily, the Board would have the option to adopt the proposed ordinance or refer the 
measure to the voters. 
However, because this particular initiative involves a TABOR revenue measure, the Board 
cannot simply adopt the proposed ordinance. TABOR requires voter approval, meaning the 
measure must be referred to the Town's eligible electors. 
The proposed ordinance before you, Ordinance No. 2026-0012, accomplishes that referral and 
establishes the special election at which voters will consider the measure. 
It is important to distinguish that adoption of Ordinance No. 2026-0012 does not 
constitute approval of the proposed mill levy reduction. Rather, it fulfills the Town's 
obligation to place the citizen-initiated measure before the voters, who will ultimately determine 
whether it is approved. 
Special Election Date 
 
 
 
 
 
 3 
The applicable election requirements provide that the special election must occur no fewer than 
60 days and no more than 150 days following the final determination of petition sufficiency. 
Assuming the final determination is issued on October 13, 2026, this establishes an election 
window of approximately December 31, 2026, through March 12, 2027. 
My recommendation is to hold the special election on Tuesday, February 16, 2027. 
I am recommending this date for several reasons: 
1. Administrative Responsibilities: December and January are already particularly busy 
months, with several other required duties and year-end responsibilities that will need my 
attention. Holding the election in February provides additional time to properly prepare 
and administer the election. 
2. Election Preparation: A special election requires significant administrative work, 
including coordinating election materials, meeting statutory deadlines, preparing notices, 
arranging ballot printing and mailing, and ensuring compliance with both municipal 
election requirements and TABOR. 
3. Timing: February falls comfortably within the statutory election window, allowing 
sufficient preparation time without unnecessarily delaying the voters' consideration of the 
measure. 
4. Facility Availability: While voters are accustomed to elections being held on Tuesdays, 
the third Tuesday of the month is also the only Tuesday that Town Hall is not already 
committed to regularly scheduled Board or Planning Commission meetings. This makes 
February 16th a practical date for election administration. 
Ordinance No. 2026-0012 
The ordinance before the Board would: 
• Refer the citizen-initiated measure to the Town's eligible electors. 
• Establish February 16, 2027, as the date of the special election. 
• Establish the ballot language for the proposed measure, which would fix the Town's 
property tax mill levy at 12.000 mills from January 1, 2028, through December 31, 2037. 
• Require the full text of the proposed initiated ordinance to be included on the ballot. 
• Designate the Town Clerk as the election official responsible for conducting the election 
and providing all required notices. 
• Require publication of the proposed initiated ordinance in its entirety. 
• Provide for the required TABOR comment process and the distribution of comments to 
eligible electors. 
• Establish the process for implementing the measure should it receive voter approval. 
Recommendation from Staff or Commission 
While this has been a complicated process, I believe we have worked diligently to ensure that 
the proponents have been afforded the opportunity to exercise their initiative rights while also 
ensuring the Town complies with the applicable statutory and constitutional requirements. 
 
 
 
 
 
 4 
At this point, assuming no timely protest is filed and the final determination of sufficiency is 
issued as anticipated, the next required step is to refer the proposed measure to the voters. 
I recommend that the Board of Trustees approve Ordinance No. 2026-0012, referring the 
citizen-initiated ordinance to the eligible electors of the Town of Kersey and establishing 
February 16, 2027, as the date of the special election. 
Financial Impact 
This special election will be conducted as a mail ballot election, with the Town responsible for 
the associated election costs.

Background 
The Town of Kersey has placed Ballot Issue 2B on the November 3, 2026, General Election ballot, 
requesting voter approval to extend the Town's existing 17.205-mill property tax levy for an additional ten 
years, from 2028 through 2037.  This measure would not increase the current tax rate or establish a new 
tax. 
The existing mill levy is scheduled to expire at the end of 2027 and currently generates approximately 
$566,000 annually, representing roughly 25% of the Town's operating funds.  This revenue supports 
essential municipal services, including street maintenance, snow removal, public works, parks and 
recreation, public safety, senior programs, and general facility maintenance.  Without voter approval of the 
extension, the Town would face a significant reduction in operating revenue, requiring substantial budget 
adjustments and potential reductions to existing services and community programs.    
The proposed extension is intended to maintain existing services and preserve the Town's ability to 
adequately maintain public infrastructure and community assets without increasing the current property tax 
mill levy.  Colorado Revised Statutes § 1-45-117 permits a governing body to adopt a resolution stating its 
position on a ballot issue, subject to statutory restrictions.  The proposed resolution expressly acknowledges 
these limitations and prohibits the expenditure of public funds to advocate for or against the ballot issue 
except as otherwise permitted by law. 
Recommendation from Staff 
Staff recommends that the Board of Trustees approve Resolution No. 2026-0026, formally expressing 
support for Ballot Issue 2B, recognizing the importance of maintaining existing municipal services, 
protecting Town infrastructure, and providing financial stability for the community over the next ten years.
