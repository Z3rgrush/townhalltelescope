Consideration of Ordinance 2026-0010 An Ordinance Adopting the 2024 International Fire Code and Providing for its Enforcement - First Reading

Mayor Roth read the Ordinance aloud. 

Rick Zier, Town Attorney, explained that the ordinance was being introduced on first reading and that, if approved, a public hearing and second reading would be scheduled for the regular Board meeting in September. Final adoption of the ordinance would occur following the second reading, if approved by the Board.

Platte Valley Fire Protection District Fire Chief, Greg Becker, provided a physical copy of the proposed fire code and advised that an electronic version could also be made available for public review. Chief Becker stated that he was available to answer questions from the public regarding the provisions and intent of the proposed code.

Chief Becker explained that adoption would bring the Fire District into alignment with the Town’s previously adopted 2024 International Codes, including the building code, and would provide consistency for new development and construction within the Town. He noted that the 

Fire District currently operates under the 2018 International Fire Code and generally updates its adopted code approximately every six years. He further explained that newer code provisions address emerging development and safety considerations, including energy storage systems and facilities utilizing lithium-ion batteries.

Motion made by Trustee Dusin and seconded by Trustee Meeker to approve Ordinance 2026-0010, an ordinance adopting the 2024 International Fire Code and providing for its enforcement on first reading. Motion carried with a 5-0 vote. 

** Per C.R.S requirements, this ordinance will not take effect until after the public hearing and second reading on September 8, 2026, providing that it is approved at that time. **

Consideration of Resolution 2026-0016 Six P’s Community Design Standards Fence
Exception

Danna Ortiz, Town Planner, presented a request for an exception to the Town’s Community Design and Development Standards for the recently annexed and industrially zoned Six P’s property located at 27910 County Road 56. The applicant has submitted a site plan for a steel fabrication business that would occupy approximately 6.5 acres of the 50-acre property.

Danna explained that the applicant is requesting approval of a seven-foot metal fence around the developed portion of the property, exceeding the standard six-foot maximum height. The fence would match the proposed metal building and provide additional screening of business operations, trailers, and outdoor storage, while also providing security and helping reduce noise and fugitive dust impacts.
Staff determined that the additional fence height would provide better screening and buffering than a six-foot fence and would remain proportional to the proposed building and compatible with the surrounding area. The request is not based on financial hardship or maintenance concerns.

Staff recommended approval subject to conditions, including limiting the exception to the approximately 6.5-acre development area; requiring the fence and gates to be constructed of metal materials matching the proposed building; limiting the fence height to seven feet; requiring business operations to remain within the fenced area; maintaining the remainder of the property in dryland grass unless otherwise approved; and requiring Town review and approval of future modifications to the fence design, height, or location.

Danna stated that both staff and the Planning Commission recommend approval of Resolution No. 2026-0016, subject to the recommended conditions.

The Board of Trustees had no questions or comments and agreed that the request was straightforward.

Motion was made by Trustee Joens and seconded by Trustee Meeker to approve Resolution 2026-0016 Six P’s Community Design Standards Fence Exception. Motion carried with a 5-0 vote.  

Consideration of Resolution 2026-0020 A Resolution Approving the Development Agreement for Six P’s, Limited

Danna Ortiz, Town Planner, presented the proposed development agreement for 6Ps related to the construction and operation of its steel fabrication business. Ortiz explained that the agreement is based on the Town’s standard development agreement and that no public improvements are associated with the project.

Ortiz noted that the Town’s standard agreement requires landscaping to be installed with an irrigation system. The property is subject to a dry-up agreement but has an existing well permitted by the State. Because the well permit may need to be changed for industrial use, the State could prohibit its use for outdoor irrigation. The development agreement therefore provides that, if the State prohibits outdoor irrigation from the well, the landscaping identified in the approved landscape plan will not be required.

Staff recommended approval of Resolution No. 2026-0020, approving the development agreement for 6Ps. The Board of Trustees had no questions.

Motion made by Trustee Lagrimanta and seconded by Trustee Joens to approve Resolution 2026-0020 A Resolution Approving the Development Agreement for Six P’s, Limited. Motion carried with a 5-0 vote.

Consideration of Resolution 2026-0019 A Resolution Approving and Intergovernmental Agreement Between the Town of Kersey and Weld County for the Conduct and Administration of the November 3, 2026 Coordinated Election

Haley Curtis, Town Clerk, presented Resolution No. 2026-0019, approving the Intergovernmental Agreement with the Weld County Clerk and Recorder for the November 3, 2026, coordinated election. Haley explained that Weld County will conduct the election as a coordinated mail ballot election and will be responsible for ballot preparation and mailing, voter services, ballot processing, tabulation, and certification of results. The Town is responsible for providing ballot content and candidate information and reimbursing the County for its share of election costs.

Haley stated that the Town’s cost is $2.50 per registered elector, including active and inactive registered electors. Because voter registration continues through Election Day, the final cost will not be known until after the election. Funds have been appropriated in the Town’s budget for the election.

Trustee Lagrimanta asked whether an agreement is required for each coordinated election and about the cost of the previous election. Haley confirmed that an agreement is required each time and noted that the Town’s number of registered electors has increased in recent years.
Staff recommended approval of the IGA and authorization for the Mayor to execute the agreement on behalf of the Town.

Motion made by Trustee Joens and seconded by Trustee Lagrimanta to approve Resolution 2026-0019 A Resolution Approving and Intergovernmental Agreement Between the Town of Kersey and Weld County for the Conduct and Administration of the November 3, 2026 Coordinated Election. Motion carried with a 5-0 vote. 

Consideration of Ordinance 2026-0011 an Ordinance Referring a Ballot Issue to the Eligible Electors of the Town of Kersey, Namely, a Referred Measure Which Would, Without Increasing the Tax Rate, Authorize the Extension for a Limited-Ten-Year Period (2028 through 2037) of the Town of Kersey’s Taxes as Previously Approved by the Town’s Voters and Which are Scheduled to Expire, in Order for the Existing Property Tax Mill Levy of 17.205 Mills to Continue Necessary Support for the Current Level of Town Operations and Services, with Such Extension to Constitute a Voter-Approved Revenue Change Within the Meaning of Article X, Section 20 of the Colorado Constitution and Exception to the Limitation Set Forth in Section 29-1-301 of the Colorado Revised Statutes

Haley Curtis, Town Clerk, presented Ordinance No. 2026-0011, referring a ballot question to Kersey voters at the November 3, 2026, coordinated election regarding the Town’s existing property tax mill levy. Haley explained that the Town currently has voter authorization for a mill levy of 17.205 mills, which expires at the end of 2027. The proposed ballot question would ask voters whether to extend the existing mill levy for an additional ten years, from 2028 through 2037.

Haley clarified that the ordinance itself does not extend or increase the mill levy but places the question before the voters. If approved, the Town would continue to levy 17.205 mills to support current operations and services. If the measure is not approved, the existing authorization would expire at the end of 2027, resulting in a reduction in property tax revenue beginning in 2028. Haley also noted that placing the question on the ballot would not increase the Town’s coordinated election costs because those costs are based on the number of registered electors rather than the number of ballot questions.

During public comment, Bill Cannon expressed concerns regarding the wording of the ballot question, particularly the phrase “without increasing the tax rate,” and stated that he believed the Town should consider reducing the mill levy to provide property tax relief.

Jeff Gross also expressed concerns regarding the ballot language, stating that he believed it could be confusing to voters and that the measure should be characterized as a tax increase because the mill levy could otherwise decrease. He discussed TABOR requirements and statutory mill levy limitations and encouraged the Board to revise the language to more directly describe the effect of the measure.

Joan Lind stated that she found the proposed language understandable and viewed the measure as maintaining the Town’s existing mill levy rather than increasing taxes. She expressed concern that reducing Town revenues could negatively affect existing services and operations.

Town Attorney, Rick Zier, advised the Board that the Colorado Supreme Court has held that a revenue retention measure is not considered a tax increase for purposes of ballot measure wording.
Motion made by Trustee Dusin and seconded by Trustee Joens to approve Ordinance 2026-0011 an Ordinance Referring a Ballot Issue to the Eligible Electors of the Town of Kersey, Namely, a Referred Measure Which Would, Without Increasing the Tax Rate, Authorize the Extension for a Limited-Ten-Year Period (2028 through 2037) of the Town of Kersey’s Taxes as Previously Approved by the Town’s Voters and Which are Scheduled to Expire, in Order for the Existing Property Tax Mill Levy of 17.205 Mills to Continue Necessary Support for the Current Level of Town Operations and Services, with Such Extension to Constitute a Voter-Approved Revenue Change Within the Meaning of Article X, Section 20 of the Colorado Constitution and Exception to the Limitation Set Forth in Section 29-1-301 of the Colorado Revised Statutes. Motion carried with a 5-0 vote.
