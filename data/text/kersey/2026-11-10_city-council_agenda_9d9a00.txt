New Business 
1. Preliminary Budget Presentation 
 
Town Manager, Stacy Brown, presented the proposed 2027 Budget, noting that the 
presentation reflected discussions and direction provided by the Board during the September 
22, 2026, work session. She explained that the budget was preliminary and would return to the 
Board in November for consideration and formal adoption. 
 
Community Experience Department: Ms. Brown discussed staffing changes within the 
Community Experience Department following the departure of the Community Development 
Director and the appointment of an Interim Parks and Recreation Director. She noted that 
funding for a part-time senior services position was included in the proposed budget; however, 
staffing needs would be reevaluated following the election and approximately 30 days under 
the interim director. Potential adjustments to existing staff responsibilities could eliminate the 
need for the additional position. In the meantime, existing staff would continue providing senior 
services, front desk coverage, and LIDAR support. 
 
Ms. Brown also noted that the requested Kersey Days donation had been included in the 
proposed budget under the Board's Legislative Public Relations fund to provide more 
consistent tracking and accounting of community donations and funding requests. 
 
Police Department: Ms. Brown reviewed current staffing levels and discussed anticipated 
congressional funding exceeding $250,000, contingent upon approval of the federal budget. 
The funding would support various Police Department needs, including patrol vehicles, 
protective vests, computers, and clothing allowances. She noted that these expenditures 
remain in the proposed 2027 Budget but would be adjusted upon receipt of the federal funding 
to avoid duplicating expenses. Ms. Brown also indicated that a staffing needs assessment 
would be conducted at the end of the first quarter of 2027 to evaluate the potential need for an 
additional police officer. 
 
Public Works Operations Department: Ms. Brown reviewed staffing levels and anticipated 
increases in materials, fuel, and utility costs. Public Works Operations Director Fernando Pina 
provided an update on two vacant positions, noting that one employee was scheduled to begin 
work and the second was pending completion of pre-employment requirements. 
 
Ms. Brown discussed the addition of Vialytic software to assist with developing a street 
pavement management plan and prioritizing future improvements. She also reviewed the 
Town's ongoing street sign replacement program, noting that the proposed budget increased 
from $4,500 to $20,000 to continue replacing signs that do not meet reflectivity standards. 
Additional proposed expenditures included $25,000 for covered storage of winter road 
maintenance materials and equipment. 
 
Ms. Brown noted that, consistent with previous Board direction, no additional gateway signage 
was included in the proposed 2027 Budget. No capital expenditures were proposed for Parks 
and Trails, and no significant budget changes were anticipated for the Kersey Community 
Center. Town Hall repair and maintenance funding was increased from $12,000 to $18,000. 
Fleet maintenance funding was proposed to increase from $5,000 to $12,000 due to aging 
vehicles and equipment. The proposed budget also included $150,000 for the replacement of 
the Town's aging snowplow and the purchase of a work truck, with two existing vehicles 
anticipated to be traded in. 
Unapproved – 10/22/2026 
 
Public Works – Water, Stormwater, and Wastewater: Ms. Brown reviewed anticipated water 
infrastructure projects for 2027, including water tower maintenance, curb stop and valve box 
repairs, and continued water meter replacements and repairs. She discussed the financial 
condition of the Water Fund, noting that the Town has been subsidizing operations and is 
projecting approximately $499,417 in revenue against $640,000 in expenditures, resulting in a 
shortfall of approximately $140,000. Ms. Brown noted that Central Weld County Water District 
announced a 5% rate increase and that the Town's existing water rate study no longer 
accurately reflects operating costs or anticipated growth. She discussed the potential need for 
significant rate adjustments and indicated that additional information, including various rate 
increase scenarios, would be presented to the Board in November for consideration. 
 
Ms. Brown noted that no stormwater rate increase was proposed for 2027, although the 
existing stormwater rate study was also outdated. 
 
Regarding wastewater operations, Ms. Brown reviewed anticipated maintenance needs, 
including electrical corrosion at the treatment facility, repairs to the Headworks building, and 
increasing chemical costs. The proposed budget included $120,000 for a replacement sewer 
jetting trailer, potentially equipped with a vacuum system, due to concerns regarding the 
condition and effectiveness of the existing equipment. She noted that the sale of the existing 
equipment could offset a portion of the purchase cost. The proposed budget also included a 
9% increase to the wastewater base rate for 2027. 
 
Administration: Ms. Brown reviewed current staffing levels, noting that the vacant front desk 
position had been filled, with the new employee scheduled to begin October 26, and that no 
additional staffing was requested. She discussed anticipated increases in insurance costs and 
the addition of $3,600 for transitioning Caselle Express Bill Pay to a cloud-based system. 
 
Ms. Brown discussed the fluctuation in severance tax revenues, noting that actual revenues 
were significantly lower than originally anticipated. Due to the unpredictability of this funding 
source, severance tax revenues were moved from the General Fund to capital improvement 
projects. She also discussed plans to develop a long-term Capital Improvement Plan to assist 
with identifying, prioritizing, and funding future capital expenditures. 
 
Additional budget considerations included increased postage costs associated with changes to 
utility billing, increased auditing expenses for a required single audit, and adjustments to 
sanitation cleanup and mosquito control expenditures. The proposed budget included 
approximately $45,000 for auditing expenses, partially offset by $30,000 in EPA grant funding, 
and approximately $3,000 for a potential special election in early 2027. 
 
Ms. Brown also reviewed anticipated expenditures for Planning and Zoning, including 
continued efforts toward Main Street designation, as well as increased judicial and engineering 
costs. Judicial budget increases reflected higher prosecutorial fees, additional court 
proceedings, and the anticipated retirement and replacement of the Municipal Judge. 
Engineering expenditures were proposed to increase from $15,000 to $35,000 due to 
increased project activity and consultant costs, with some expenses anticipated to be 
reimbursed through grant funding associated with the Clark Street project. 
 
Salaries, Wages, and Benefits: Ms. Brown reviewed proposed personnel expenditures, noting 
that the 2027 Budget included a 3% cost-of-living adjustment for all employees, totaling 
approximately $45,978, consistent with previous Board direction. She discussed the potential

that existing staff would continue providing coverage while staffing needs were evaluated. She 
also confirmed that the position being vacated by Julie Piper's retirement would not be

Ms. Brown discussed a 12% increase in employee benefit costs and noted that the proposed

2. Ordinance No 2026-0012 An Ordinance Referring a Ballot Issue to the Eligible Electors of the 
Town of Kersey and Calling for a Special Election on Such Issue, Namely, an Initiated 
Ordinance Which Would Fix the Town of Kersey’s Property Tax Rate at 12.000 Mills for a Ten-
Year Period Commencing January 1, 2028 and Terminating December 31, 2037 
Town Clerk Haley Curtis presented Ordinance 2026-0012, referring a citizen initiative petition 
to the voters of the Town of Kersey. She explained that the petition, submitted by Bill Cannon 
and Tim Phippard, proposed reducing the Town's property tax mill levy from 17.205 mills to 
12.000 mills for a ten-year period beginning January 1, 2028, and ending December 31, 2037. 
Ms. Curtis provided an overview of the citizen initiative process, including the initial contact 
with the proponents, multiple petition submissions and revisions, approval of the petition for 
circulation, and verification of signatures. She noted that the petition contained 64 valid 
signatures, exceeding the 51 required, and that no protests were received during the statutory 
protest period. The final determination of sufficiency was issued on October 13, 2026. 
Ms. Curtis explained that the initiative involved both statutory citizen initiative requirements and 
constitutional provisions under TABOR, creating additional procedural considerations. She 
clarified that approval of Ordinance 2026-0012 would not constitute approval of the proposed 
mill levy reduction but would refer the question to the voters for their consideration. She further 
explained that the election was required to occur within 60 to 150 days following the final 
determination of sufficiency. 
Ms. Curtis recommended February 16, 2027, for the special election, citing election 
administration requirements, scheduling considerations, and the availability of Town Hall. She 
explained that the ordinance established the election date, set the ballot language, authorized 
the Town Clerk to administer the election, and provided for the required publication and 
TABOR notice procedures. Staff recommended approval of the ordinance. 
Town Attorney Rick Zier acknowledged the complexity of the process, particularly the 
interaction between citizen initiative statutes and TABOR requirements and emphasized the 
importance of accommodating the citizen initiative process. 
Mr. Cannon expressed appreciation for the assistance provided by Ms. Curtis and Mr. Zier 
throughout the petition process. He explained that the proponents had initially hoped to have 
the question included in the November 2026 election to avoid the expense of a separate 
Unapproved – 10/22/2026 
election. He also expressed his belief that the initiative could have been avoided had the Board 
previously considered reducing the mill levy. 
Mayor Roth acknowledged the importance of the citizen initiative process and expressed 
appreciation for the efforts of the proponents, Town staff, and legal counsel in bringing the 
matter before the Board for consideration by the voters. 
Mr. Justin Chittenden commented on his involvement in assisting the petition proponents and 
discussed the time and effort required to complete the citizen initiative process. He 
encouraged the Board to recognize the complexity of the process and the work involved in 
bringing an initiative forward. 
Motion made by Trustee Joens, seconded by Trustee Meeker to approve Ordinance No 2026-

Calling for a Special Election on Such Issue, Namely, an Initiated Ordinance Which Would Fix 
the Town of Kersey’s Property Tax Rate at 12.000 Mills for a Ten-Year Period Commencing 
January 1, 2028 and Terminating December 31, 2037. Motion Carried with a 3-0 vote.

3. Town Manager 
Stacy Brown, Town Manager, provided several project and community updates.  
 
Town Manager Stacy Brown began her report by recognizing Brandon for his service and 
dedication to the Town of Kersey. She thanked him for his contributions as both an employee 
Unapproved – 10/22/2026 
and community member, particularly his efforts to improve the community, and wished him 
success in his future endeavors. 
 
Ms. Brown provided an update on the Grant Elevator Project, reporting that environmental 
testing results confirmed no remaining asbestos on the property and that other tested 
conditions were within applicable CDPHE and federal guidelines. The final report is expected 
shortly. Approximately $30,000 in grant funding remains available to assist with the required 
single audit. Ms. Brown thanked Grant Administrator, Susie, for her work on the financial 
administration of the project. 
 
The Town continues working with Brendle Group on the Water Blueprint Plan, which is 
required to be completed by the end of the year. An agricultural stakeholder meeting is 
scheduled for October 20 to discuss water conservation, agricultural water rights, and 
opportunities to diversify and protect the Town's future water supply. Ms. Brown emphasized 
the importance of maintaining communication with agricultural property owners regarding 
potential water sales and the impacts of agricultural water being transferred out of the area. 
The approximately $50,000 project is being funded primarily through grants from the 
Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) and Northern Water, with the Town contributing 
approximately $2,500. 
 
Ms. Brown reported that construction on the Clark Street Project is scheduled to begin October 
19, and affected residents have been notified. Traffic control and detour plans are being 
coordinated, and staff continues working with the contractor. Although the project schedule 
allows for completion as late as June, an earlier completion is anticipated. Staff is also 
evaluating the potential rehabilitation of the sewer line between 5th and 9th Streets due to 
recurring obstructions. A cost estimate will be presented to the Board for consideration. Ms. 
Brown also noted that federal budget action is anticipated in December, which may provide 
additional information regarding the Town's pending congressional funding request. 
 
Ms. Brown provided an overview of recent meetings and community engagement activities, 
including a quarterly meeting with Weld County Commissioner Maxey, the Chief's Luncheon, 
the CMCA Region 2 meeting with Town Clerk Haley Curtis, the US 34 Coalition Board 
meeting, and the Weld County State of the County meeting. She also acknowledged the 
extensive staff involvement in preparing the 2027 budget and reviewing existing financial and 
administrative processes. 
 
Regarding economic development, Ms. Brown reported that staff met with representatives of a 
prospective manufacturing business interested in locating in Kersey. Discussions are ongoing 
regarding water pressure and infrastructure considerations associated with the potential 
development. 
 
Ms. Brown also provided several operational updates. The Town transitioned employee 
cellular services from Verizon to FirstNet, resulting in anticipated savings of approximately 
$100 per month and improved coverage for Police, Public Works, and other Town employees. 
The Town also completed the onboarding of a new information technology service provider. 
Additionally, staff is working with Plante Moran to review and reconcile financial information 
within Caselle, including fund balances and fixed assets, to ensure financial records, system 
reporting, and budget documents are accurate and consistent. 
 
Ms. Brown concluded by noting the appointment of Kaylee as Interim Parks and Recreation 
Director.

Background 
The Town of Kersey purchases its water from Central Weld County Water District (CWCWD).

operating expenses and is largely a pass-through expense.  As CWCWD increases its wholesale 
water rates, the Town must determine how those additional costs will be recovered.  Unlike

purchasing and delivering water.  The Board must therefore determine what additional increase 
to implement a higher rate adjustment, and/or what amount to utilize General Fund revenues to 
offset any remaining shortfall.  Future General Fund offset may not be available due to potential 
financial considerations if we do not have a property tax mill levy.  
Staff is seeking Board discussion and direction regarding what additional increase and general 
fund subsidizing you would like before finalizing the 2027 budget.

Budgeting and Financial Monitoring Policies
Attached is the Adopted 2027 Budget for the Town of Kersey.  It is the result  of many hours of review, discussion, and analysis.
Under this plan the Town can continue to provide an excellent level of service while assuring a sound financial plan.  
1.  Budgeting
1.1 Budget Development Purpose Statement
The annual budget is a fiscal plan which presents the services, which will be provided to the community, and the funds needed to perform those 
services.  The Board of Trustees in accordance with the State Statutes adopts an annual budget and appropriation resolution.  The Town Staff
is responsible for formulating the fiscal plan and presenting it to the Board of Trustees for approval and adoption.
1.2 Budget Law
The policies for the Town budget comply with Local Government Budget Law of Colorado as outlined in Colorado Revised Statutes
29-1-101 through CRS 29-1-104.
The budget policies apply to all departments of the Town of Kersey. Annually, the Town Board has the authority and responsibility
to adopt and oversee implementation of a budget approving the use of public funds for the operation of all Town departments. The Town's
fiscal year runs from January 1 through December 31. All appropriations lapse at the end of each fiscal year. 
1.3 The Budget will be structurally Balanced
A budget is structurally balanced if it does not spend more on ongoing services than it receives in ongoing revenues. A 
structurally balanced budget is a necessary component of good financial management and financial discipline and will 
help sustain the consistent delivery of services to residents. An unbalanced budget (spending more than is received) 
undermines the Town's ability to deal with financial problems. This may lead to reduced services in the future and 
inhibits the Town's ability to take advantage of opportunities which periodically occur. 
Each fund is balanced separately. Unless specifically authorized by the Town Board, expenditures will be equal to or less
than the revenues for each fund. In cases where expenditures exceed revenues, sufficient reserves and/or a transfer
from another fund must be approved by the Board. If sufficient reserves and/or a transfer from another fund is not available,
essential services will receive first priority for funding. Subsequently, lower priority services will be identified for reduction, suspension, or
elimination. Long range financial projections are prepared for major funds and analyzed to assure that impacts of the proposed budget
are fiscally sound in future years. 
1.4 Basis of Budgeting and Accounting
The town uses the modified accrual and accrual basis of accounting.  The town adheres to Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)
which require the establishment and maintenance of fund balances. GAAP does not make any requirements as to the level of those
balances; just that there is a policy so that they can be clearly and consistently accounted for. 
1.5 Budget Process Timeline
Budgeting on an annual basis provides time to review all revenue sources, develop solutions to previously identified
problems, and to discuss and decide on policies and priorities. It is the Treasurer's responsibility to distribute a 
budget process timeline to department heads in a timeframe that allows appropriate, responsible development of 
department budgets.  A typical timeline will be as follows:
January Prior year's budget due to the State of Colorado by January 31st.
January-

1.6 Budget Control and Execution
The level of budgetary control is at the classification level within each department. No department may expend, or contract to expend,
any money in excess of the amount appropriated in the appropriation resolution. No fund may carry a negative fund balance according to  
state statute. Administratively, operating budgets are controlled at the department level with departments having the authority to transfer
appropriations within certain classifications within a department without further Board approval. Those certain classifications are fixed
in the annual budget guidelines as approved by the Board and may be expanded to line item control. Increases in staffing and capital  
expenditure appropriations must be approved by the Board. 
1.7 Staff Responsibilities
Departments are responsible for the following in the budget development process:
a. Meeting with the Town Manager or Treasurer to discuss strategic budgetary planning
b. Submitting proposals and quotes for future expenditures. Capital project planning and cost estimates
     including pictures of the capital items requested. 
c. Monitoring and projecting revenues and expenses within the department
d. Communicating information appropriately to departments and attending budget meetings.
e. Reviewing all budget materials for accuracy, content, and style.
f. Identifying any additional staffing needs and providing this information during strategic planning sessions. 
1.8 Budget Adoption/ Adjustments
Following internal development and review of the budget, the Town Board will hold hearings on the budget to provide public review
of the document and to discuss further budget development. Following review at the Board level, the Board shall provide authority to expend 
funds through a budget ordinance adopted in an open meeting. 
a. The budget ordinance shall authorize the budget.
b. The amount appropriated to a specific fund and/or department may change as a result of subsequent Board actions. Budget
adjustments due to unforeseen expenditures, to recognize unanticipated revenue, or to adjust operating transfers may be 
authorized with a supplementary budget resolution. 
1.9 Supplementary Budget Process
The budget may require adjustments due to unforeseen expenditures, to recognize unanticipated revenue, or to adjust operating transfers.
During the year, adjustments that will change the budget at the Fund or Department level may be made by bringing a supplemental budget
resolution to the Board for their approval. It is the goal of each department to ensure actual expenditures
do not exceed the budgeted amounts appropriated. 
2.  Reporting Process
Amounts presented in the budget document shall be compared with actual revenues and expenditures for each month and year-to-date ended during
the budget year.  The quarterly reports will be presented in such a form that will enable the Town to maintain control over it's financial resources and 
communicate adherence to the intent of the Board of Trustees for the budget year.  This process will provide information to monitor the performance
of municipal services against the goals of such services.
3.  Revenues/Other Financing Sources
     A. Operating Revenues:
The Board of Trustees intends to generate revenues, which will not require dependence on any one revenue source, maintaining a 
stable and diversified revenue system.  However, the Board of Trustees recognizes that property taxes and sales taxes are the
Town's largest revenue source.  Other operating revenue sources available include the following:  Franchise, specific ownership
and occupation taxes, liquor and business licenses, building permits, and other licenses and permits; intergovernmental revenue,
charges for services, fines, and earnings on investments.
    B. Capital Financing:
The Town has and will continue to use two methods of financing capital purchases and improvements, as follows:
1. Lease/Purchase:  This method is used primarily for the provision of new and replacement equipment/vehicles, with the 
purpose of:
a. Ensuring the timely replacement of equipment and vehicles;
b. Decreasing the impact of inflation on the purchase of new and replacement equipment;
c. Using funds that would have otherwise been spent on the outright purchase of the assets for investment in
interest-bearing instruments to reduce the interest cost of the lease-purchase;
d. Eliminate one of the burdens of ownership in that the lease may be terminated at the end of each anniversary
date, or earlier, of the lease generally without penalty of a material decline in the residual value of the 
property leased.
2. Debt Financing:  The Town will consider debt financing when appropriate conditions exist as follows:
a. Long-term capital improvements are desired
b. It is determined that future citizens will receive a benefit from the improvement.
The debt will be soundly financed by:
1. Conservatively projecting revenue sources to finance the debt.
2. Financing the improvement over a period not greater than the useful life of the improvement.
4.  Operating Reserves
     A. General Fund:  The Town will maintain a reserve balance at the end of every year. This balance
will provide four or more months of operating revenue for the next year plus provide a buffer against possible economic decline or contingencies.
     B. Enterprise Funds:  To maintain uninterrupted water and sewer service operations, the Town will maintain net working capital equal to
three months of average expenditures.
    C. Special Revenue Funds:  Reserve balances for other funds maintained by the Town will vary depending on each activity requirement.
5.  Investments
    A. The Town analyzes the cash flow within each fund on a consistent basis to ensure maximum cash availability.
    B. The Town considers pooling the cash from several funds to maximize the return on investments.
    C. Market conditions and investments securities are analyzed to determine the maximum yield to be obtained.
    D. The Town invests substantially all of its idle cash on a continuous basis.
    E. The Town complies with state statutes regarding investments.
6.  Fund Structure
The accounts of the town are organized on the basis of funds and account groups, each of which is considered a separate accounting entity.  
The operations of each fund are accounted for with a separate set of self-balancing accounts that comprise its assets, liabilities, fund equity, 
revenues and expenditures, or expenses, as appropriate.  Resources are allocated to and accounted for in individual funds based upon the 
purposes for which they are to be spent and the means by which spending activities are controlled.  The various funds are grouped into two 
board categories and the six funds that the Town maintains are as follows:
* GOVERNMENTAL FUND TYPES

GENERAL FUND accounts for all revenues and expenditures not required to be segregated by statute regulations or type of accounting.
SPECIAL REVENUE FUNDS account for revenues designated by special needs or by outside agencies, and are generally required to be
segregated from general funds.
ENTERPRISE FUNDS account for programs designed to be primarily funded from user charges.  These funds should be fully self-supporting
and user charges should be determined from net income generated.
ACCOUNT GROUPS account for long-term items that have "passed through" another fund as a revenue or expenditure.  These are purely
custodial groups that maintain records on items that would otherwise be lost from accountability.  Examples are debt and assets 
purchased through capital outlay expenditures.
OVERVIEW OF THE ACCOUNTING SYSTEM
The General Fund is the largest operating fund for the Town of Kersey.  The General Fund operates the General Government.
The General Government functional component provides administrative support services to all other functions and departments of the
municipal government.  These services are coordinated and controlled here for efficiency and cost effectiveness.  They include personnel
hiring, payroll and benefit administration, accounting, budgeting, data processing, building maintenance, liability insurance and legal counsel.
Also provided here are the many functions required by law, such as maintaining all official documents and records of the Town and Town Board,
voter registration, elections, assessment of property, tax billing and collection.
The General Fund is comprised of the following departments:  Administrative, Police, Health, Other Protection, Elections, Planning and Zoning,
Inspections, Economic Development, Legislative, Judicial, Parks, Recreation, Library, Seniors, Engineering, Sanitation, and  
Information Technology.  Added for 2025 is the subsection in the general fund Grant Administration, to keep record of revenues and 
expenses with grants.  In 2024, Stormwater Fund was added to bring in revenue for stormwater development.
This is a governmental fund and uses the modified accrual basis of accounting.  Under this method, revenues are recognized in the accounting
period when they become both measurable and available as net assets.

The Planning and Zoning Commission advises the Board of Trustees and other governmental agencies and the general public on matters 
relating to the administration of the Town's Comprehensive Plan and Zoning ordinances.  Support services for the Planning and Zoning 
Commission is provided by the Town Clerk.  The town also contracts with a planner for development in and around the town. 
INSPECTIONS (09)
This activity accounts for expenses related to insuring public safety in all buildings.  The Town has an agreement with an inspection
agency to approve plans and inspect properties for code compliance.  The Town issues necessary permits.
ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT (10)
This activity accounts for Economic Development for the Town including Business Attraction & retention for primary employment and retail.  
Does include incentive negotiations with final Town Board approval. 
LEGISLATIVE (11)
The Legislative Body, the Mayor and Board of Trustees, adopt policies, resolutions and ordinances that govern the direction and priorities of the
Town.  This is done in public forum.  The objective is to serve the community as a whole ensuring equal representation of each citizen.
JUDICIAL (12)
The Municipal Court has jurisdiction within the Town.  The Board of Trustees appoints the judge.  All violations of municipal codes can be 
brought before the court.  Kersey Municipal Court is a court of record.
Municipal elections are held during even numbered years, the first Tuesday in November, for the purpose of electing Board of Trustees and Mayor.
The Court Clerk handles municipal court responsibilities, establishing contacts with attorneys, defendants, witnesses, officers, jurors and
others as required.  Fines and fees are collected and records of the court are the Court Clerks responsibility to maintain.
The Court aims to provide citizens with a swift and impartial forum concerning possible violations of local ordinances.
PARKS (13)
Revenue is collected through park reservations through the summer.  Payroll and benefits are paid to Public Works technician as well as 
seasonal workers.  Tree Board expenses and other park expenses not covered by CTF are paid here.  
RECREATION (14)  (The Recreation and Community Center are re-branded at Community Experiences)
The fees for activities are evaluated on an ongoing basis.  A Community Experiences Specialist was hired in 2024 to enhance 
the recreation program as well as the social media accounts associated with the Town.  
MUSEUM (15)
The museum  is located in a property in downtown Kersey.  The town pays for rent, utilities, and minor maintenance of the building.  
COMMUNITY CENTER (16)
The older adult program provides and maintains programs, which are available and accessible to all older adults.  Its purpose is to contribute to 
the physical, mental, social and cultural well being of the senior population.  A part-time employee has been hired to provide 
programs and fitness to the older adults and community.
