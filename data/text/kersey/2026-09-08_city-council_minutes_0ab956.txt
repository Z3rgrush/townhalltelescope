New Business 
1. Consideration of Resolution 2026-0022 Awarding the Contract for the Kersey Clark Street 
Storm Sewer Project to Mountain Constructors, Inc.; and Authorizing the Mayor to Execute the 
Contract 
 
Town Engineer, Gene MacDonald, stated that the Town received 13 bids for the project. 
MacDonald and Scott Larson with Benchmark reviewed the bids and compared the two lowest 
bidders. Staff recommended awarding the contract to the second-lowest bidder, Mountain 
Constructors, Inc., after identifying concerns with the lowest bidder, including negative 
references and issues with certain bid line items. 
 
MacDonald stated that Mountain Constructors received excellent references and that both he 
and Larson have previously worked successfully with the company. He also noted that 
Mountain Constructors agreed to hold its bid pricing for 180 days while the Town awaits 
availability of FEMA funding. The bid came in well below the engineer’s estimate. MacDonald 
clarified that the contract is for construction and that additional project-related costs will be 
incurred separately. 
There was no public comment and/or comments from the Board of Trustees. 
Motion made by Trustee Joens, seconded by Trustee Dusin to approve Resolution 2026-0022 
Awarding the Contract for the Kersey Clark Street Storm Sewer Project to Mountain 
Constructors, Inc.; and Authorizing the Mayor to Execute the Contract. Motion carried with a 4-
0 vote.

3. Town Manager 
Stacy Brown, Town Manager, provided several project and community updates.  
 
The Quality Assurance Project Plan (QAPP) for the EPA and CDPHE remains under review. 
Staff will return to the Board with options for pigeon control. 
 
The Town held a stakeholder meeting regarding the Water Blueprint. Due to State 
requirements and the project timeline, additional discussions are anticipated at the November 
and December work sessions, with adoption anticipated in December. Central Weld County 
Water District, Northern Water, and Latham Reservoir were among the stakeholders 
discussed. 
 
Brown reported that staff met with the Colorado Department of Local Affairs regarding EIAF 
grant funding and discussed the possibility of receiving approximately 8% reimbursement. 
Letters regarding the Town’s FEMA funding were also sent to representatives in the offices of 
Senator Hickenlooper, Senator Bennet, and Congressman Evans. FEMA funding remains 
pending. 
 
Brown attended Highway 34 transportation meetings and provided an update regarding 
ongoing fire watch requirements at the Penske property. The Town sent a certified letter to Mr. 
Sparrow regarding responsibility for the property, and an inspection is scheduled for 
September 10. 
 
Land surveys have been completed for the Wedges property and Berryman Outlot 3. A minor 
adjustment is necessary because a water valve was found to be located outside of the 
easement, and the Town will be responsible for the associated cost. 
 
Brown reported that estimates for a monument sign have ranged from approximately $40,000 
to $60,000, while $20,000 was originally budgeted for the project, including design costs 
already incurred. Additional information will be brought back to the Board after a third quote is 
received. 
 
Staff continues to work on preparation of the Town budget. 
 
Brown reminded the Board of the Main Street meeting scheduled for 5:00 p.m. and 
encouraged Board members to attend if available. Approximately ten members of the public 
were anticipated to participate. 
 
Brown provided a brief update regarding a potential annexation involving property that may 
include both residential and commercial development. 
 
Approved – 10/13/2026 
Regarding the Centennial Commerce Center, Brown reported that staff met with a new 
engineer and anticipates receiving a site plan addressing infrastructure. The project continues 
to await CDPHE approval of the lift station. 
 
Brown reported that Public Works Director Fernando Pina completed roadway assessments 
using Vitalytics. The resulting data provides detailed information regarding roadway conditions 
that will assist the Town with cost estimates, prioritization, and long-term maintenance 
planning. She also recognized Pina’s efforts in identifying safety concerns, including signage 
needs. 
 
The Town is testing a Zoom option that will allow members of the public to view Board 
meetings and work sessions online. The general public link will provide viewing access only 
and will not allow participation or public comment. Individuals who need to participate remotely 
will receive a unique link. Staff is testing the system before making it available for future 
meetings. 
 
Brown reported that the Town is finalizing its contract with the new IT service provider following 
the Town’s procurement process. The new provider is anticipated to begin services October 1, 
with services from the current provider concluding October 4. 
Mosquito spraying has concluded for the year. 
 
Brown discussed potentially upgrading the Town Hall audio/video security system to include 
audio recording in public areas. She also reported that policies have been developed regarding 
incident reporting and meetings with members of the public to improve documentation and 
employee safety. The Board expressed consensus for staff to obtain pricing for the proposed 
audio/video upgrades. 
 
Brown provided an update regarding the Main Street settlement and noted that the warranty 
period extends through mid-December. Staff will contact Mountain Constructors regarding 
completing necessary repairs before asphalt plants close for the winter. 
 
Brown reminded the Board of the September 15 informational session regarding the ballot 
initiative, scheduled for 6:00 p.m. at Town Hall. Mayor Roth plans to attend, and staff has 
prepared a frequently asked questions document for the meeting. 
 
Brown also reminded the Board of the CML Region 2 meeting in Frederick on September 24 at 
5:00 p.m. She plans to attend, and reservations are required.
