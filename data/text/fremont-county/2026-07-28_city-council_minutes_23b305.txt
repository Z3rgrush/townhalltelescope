Commissioner McFall announced the arrival of the annual Fremont County Fair; due to rain, the junior 
rodeo was postponed until the following day. They are also doing goat, chicken, and pig competitions.  
 
Commissioner Grantham said August 1st marks the official 150th anniversary of Colorado's formation. 
He also wanted to bring attention to one of the greatest abridgments of our constitutional freedoms that 
Colorado is imposing on August 1st as well. Senate Bill 3 prohibits the manufacturing, distribution, 
transfer, sale, or purchase of a specific and long list of semi-automatic firearms. It bans rapid-fire 
conversion devices and requires people who want to purchase one to take a firearm safety training 
program completed through Colorado Parks and Wildlife. 
 
Commissioner Bell commented that she and Commissioner McFall attended the NACO conference, 
and that she had the opportunity to serve on the Agriculture and Rural Affairs Steering Committee. For 
the last six years, she has led an annual policy resolution seeking and pledging financial support from 
the federal government for the agricultural industry. She expressed concern about who would take up the 
responsibility after her impending retirement, so she decided to run it as a permanent addition to the 
NACO Federal Policy. As soon as she reached out, she received 20 co-sponsors, and it sailed through 
the steering committee and was granted full approval from the NACO board. Commissioner Bell sadly 
revealed that a cherished community member, Geri Collette, recently passed away. She was a lifelong 
educator, spending her entire professional career teaching students in school and then moving out of the 
classroom to teach adolescents the importance of beef. Geri attended a board meeting last month to ask 
the commissioners to pay tribute to Gloria and Walter Schepp.

Ref. Fremont County BOCC Meeting 7/28/2026 31:22 
 
1. 2025 Fremont County Financial Statements & Management Letter 
Representative: Kyle Logan, Logan and Associates, LLC 
 
Mr. Logan said that they started the 2025 audit in January 2026. One of the checks focused on 
disbursements, including payroll transactions and certain revenue items. This was primarily to test 
Fremont County's internal controls. He explained that they review commissioner minutes to determine 
whether actions taken or agreements made had supporting documentation and were followed through. 
Because Fremont County received over a million dollars in federal funds, they are required to have a 
compliance-type audit. To complete this, they reviewed four programs in total, two of which were 
operated by the Department of Human Services, specifically Medicaid and child support services. Mr. 
Logan explained that they also evaluated the Coronavirus SLRG program funds and the airport 
improvement project, as it was one of the largest in 2025. Mr. Logan concluded that his opinion was 
unmodified, which is essentially a perfect score.  
 
  
Commissioner Grantham  moved t o accept the 2025 Fremont County financial statements and 
management letter as presented by Kyle Logan of Logan and Associates LLC . Commissioner McFall 
seconded the motion. Upon Vote: Commissione r Grantham , aye; Commissione r McFall , aye; 
Commissioner Bell, aye. The motion carried by unanimous consent.
