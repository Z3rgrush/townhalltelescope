4. Professional Review Fees Request 
Planning and Zoning requests the ability to bill an applicant for review fees that exceed the 
application fee for a current project. 
Representative: Dan Victoria, Director, Fremont County Planning & Zoning 
 
 
Director Victoria said the department should request additional billing from the applicant for review 
costs. The additional costs include the fees up to and including any amount that exceeds the application 
fee for the current project. The excess fees result from the Planning & Zoning Department engaging an 
outside consultant. The applicant requested an outside consultant after voicing a perceived staff bias 
during the review of this project. Section 306 of the Fremont County Resolution allows the board, at its 
discretion, to assess additional fees. In addition to the non-refundable application fee imposed by the 
board through a separate resolution, the board may, at its discretion, assess additional fees and amounts 
deemed appropriate by the board to cover the actual costs of evaluating the application, including, but 
not limited to, retaining any necessary expert review or investigative assistance for any portion of the 
application or its accompanying documents.   
 
Commissioner McFall reiterated that because the permit fee typically covers the time it takes to process 
an application, the cost of this application has risen well above that amount, so the Planning and Zoning 
Department is requesting additional funds. Commissioner McFall asked how they plan to obtain the 
exact extra amount owed. 
 
Director Victoria agreed with Commissioner McFall's statement. 
 
Commissioner Grantham asked whether this would apply only to this application or to all pending 
applications. 
 
Planning and Zoning Director Victoria said they are only asking for this application.  
 
Commissioner McFall wanted Attorney Bellas’s opinion on whether they should make this motion on a 
case-by-case basis, given that the Resolution authorizes them to impose additional fees, though the 
amount could vary depending on the consultants needed.  
 
County Attorney Bellas said the zoning resolution does permit billing applicants additionally; this 
occurs when outside professionals provide services beyond those provided by county employees. He 
said it doesn’t happen often, but some applications require specialized expertise or more staff than the 
county has. He said it would apply in any case where the county has hired an outside professional.  
 
Commissioner McFall asked Director Victoria whether they need to add any stipulations to ensure 
these fees are paid before the application progresses further. 
 
Planning and Zoning Director Victoria answered that, so far, his plan, depending on the board's vote, 
is to provide the applicant with the invoices. The Fremont County Planning Commission and this board 
of commissioners have not yet heard of this application. Whether the applicant chooses to contest the 
charges or the timeliness of payment has not been determined yet, since this has never happened before.  
 
Attorney Bellas said that it would depend on whether the additional services required would extend 
through the board of commissioners’ process, meaning more funds would be due even after those 
meetings occur. If additional services were still required after that point, it would be hard to figure out 
what kind of stipulation to impose.  
 
 
Commissioner Grantham interjected that the cost of an application process, because of its complexity 
or whatever else, should not hinder the taxpayers, and he is ready to move forward with a motion. 
 
Commissioner McFall had one more question for Attorney Bellas; to his understanding, the zoning 
regulations state that the resolution must be changed by a separate resolution. 
 
County Attorney Bellas replied that, to his knowledge, the board would only need to authorize the 
Planning and Zoning Department to separately bill for additional professional time.   
 
Commissioner Grantham moved to approve the Planning and Zoning Department's request, allowing it 
to bill an applicant for review fees that exceed the application fee for the current project . Commissioner 
McFall seconded the motion. Upon Vote: Commissioner Grantham, aye; Commissioner McFall, aye. The 
motion carried by unanimous consent.

5. Adoption of the 2027 Fremont County Holiday Schedules  
 
Commissioner McFall explained that the County has two holiday schedules: one for 8 -hour employees 
and one for 10-hour employees. 
 
County Administrator Carochi rationalized that this year's proposed schedule allows all county 
employees to receive a total of 100 hours of paid holiday time, with allocations adjusted to match each 
employee's regular work schedule. Staff who work 10-hour workdays will receive nine paid holidays (10 
hours each) and one floating holiday, totaling 100 hours.  Employees on an 8-hour workday will receive 
10 paid holidays (8 hours each) and floating holiday leave totaling 20 hours, for a total of 100 hours. This 
approach ensures consistency and fairness across the organization while recognizing the differences in 
daily work schedules . If the commissioners approve this today, our HR Director, Beltz, will post and 
distribute the 2027 holiday calendar. 
 
Commissioner Grantham moved to approve the holiday schedules as presented for the four-day-a-week 
and 10-hour schedule as well as the five -day and eight -hour schedule. Commissioner McFall seconded 
the motion. Upon Vote: Commissione r Grantham, aye; Commissioner McFall, aye. The motion carried 
by unanimous consent.
