Commissioner McFall shared the discussion from the previous Colorado Counties Incorporated 
meeting, at which he is the Mountain District President. It entailed the Lower Basin states (California, 
Nevada, and Arizona) of the Colorado River attempting to negotiate more water rights, but the main 
problem was that there isn’t enough. The 1922 Colorado River Compact was created when the Upper 
Basin (Colorado, New Mexico, Wyoming, and Utah) had ample snowpack, and the Lower Basin had a 
low population. Since an agreement could not be reached, the federal government, through the Bureau of 
Reclamation, decided that the Lower Basin must reduce its water use by 1.25 million acre-feet over 
2027 and 2028. Commissioner McFall commented that Arizona will likely struggle the most because it 
has the youngest water shares, meaning it must cut back the most. The settlement included a 
reevaluation every two years to see the impact of the reduction.

1. 2026 National Association of Counties Achievement Award presentation. 
 
Commissioner McFall said that every year the National Association of Counties hosts a recognition 
ceremony for programs that enhance and raise awareness in their communities. Commissioner McFall 
decided to nominate Michelle Boscia, a program coordinator at Fremont County Public Health and 
Environment, for her work on the community-based impaired-driving prevention initiative. The award 
honors innovative, effective community government programs that strengthen services for residents. 
Each year the achievement awards recognize outstanding programs in 18 categories aligned with the 
vast comprehensive services counties provide. 
 
Ms. Boscia approached the podium to thank the commissioners for their support, which she believed 
was essential to her accomplishments. She explained that the grant began in 2023 for Impaired Driving, 
and she conducted an Impaired Driving Data Walk to gather community input on how best to improve it. 
One of the initiatives was to create a DUI Task Force; it is still getting off the ground, and they meet 
every second Wednesday at 1:00 p.m., offered in person or online. 
 
Public Health Director Rick Gutierrez said that during his year as Director, Michelle consistently 
worked hard and delivered strong results. On his first day in office, the only thing on his desk was a 
copy of Michelle’s Fremont County Impaired Driving Data Walk report. Since then, she has created the 
Traffic Crash dashboard, the well-attended DUI Task Force, and the Youth Action Board. He thanked 
Michelle for bringing so much honor to Fremont County and said the award is well deserved.

1. ZC 26-002 Carr 
Requesting a Zone Change from Business to Residential One (R1). The property is located at 
7637 US Highway 50, in Howard. The applicant would like to build a garage/shop for personal 
use. 
Representative: Tyler Allen  
 
Mr. Allen explained that he intends to build a 50-foot-by-70-foot shop for the owner. The shop is intended 
for personal use, and they plan to add a full bathroom to the unit. Since this will be for personal use only, 
the owner is requesting that the lot be rezoned from commercial to residential. 
 
Planning and Zoning Director Victoria introduced the application as ZC 26-002, and the purpose of the 
rezone is to change the zoning district from business to residential . The applicant would like to build a 
garage or shop for personal use. The North, East, and South lots adjacent to this property are zoned 
residential one, and they currently have residential use. The West lot on the opposite side of Highway 50 
is owned by a business and  is vacant. The property is in the Upper Arkansas Planning District, which 
discourages commercial development along highway corridors when the highway is not essential to the 
development's success. There is one access point to and from Highway 50, and the property is in a flood 
zone. The Planning and Zoning Department did not receive any public comments or concerns . The 
Planning Commission recommended approval at the July 7th meeting. The Fremont County Planning and 
Zoning Department has found the application to be in complete compliance with the requirements of the 
Fremont County Zoning Resolution; staff recommended approval of the zone change from business to 
residential. 
 
Commissioner McFall asked Director Victoria if the lot he pointed out across the street is the only one 
in the area zoned commercial. 
 
Planning and Zoning Director Victoria responded that there are lots zoned for business scattered 
throughout the area on the other side of the highway, but the majority in the immediate surrounding area 
is residential. What triggered this request is that, if it remains business-zoned and he were to build a shop, 
it would require the owner to prepare a commercial development plan. This would require several public 
hearings and would be a substantial process. The department advised that the easiest way to get him there 
is to instead request a zone change. 
 
Commissioner Bell opened the public hearing at 9:42 am. 
 
Commissioner Bell closed the public hearing at 9:42 am with no public comment. 
 
Commissioner McFall moved to approve Zone Change 26 -002 CAR from business to residential one. 
The property is located at 7637 US HWY 50 in Howard.  Commissioner Grantham seconded the motion. 
Upon Vote: Commissione r McFall , aye; Commissione r Grantham , aye; Commissione r Bell , aye. The 
motion carried by unanimous consent.

4. Resolution No. 19, Series of 2026 Imposing an Emergency Temporary Moratorium on 
the Construction of Data Centers 
 
County Attorney Bellas explained that the board has the authority to manage land use within Fremont 
County and, within that authority, the board is able to establish a temporary moratorium not to exceed six 
months on any particular type of development in the event that the board believes that the moratorium is 
necessary in order to properly regulate that type of development. Mr. Bellas wanted to clar ify that they 
have not received any interest from anyone in building a data center in our area, but this debate is in the 
public eye. The public is very aware of da ta centers and their challenges, so, out of an abundance of 
caution, this moratorium will allow our planning and zoning department to develop and propose 
regulations governing the location of data centers within Fremont County.  
 
Commissioner Grantham noted that no one has even inquired about a data center, but it is their job as 
commissioners to evaluate the real risk it would pose to Fremont County citizens and any potential 
benefits. They do not intend to ban data centers but to evaluate the pros and cons of land use.  
 
Commissioner Bell commented that, year after year, at the National Association of Counties meeting, 
they have included discussions on data centers. Most of the time, it is negative, but there is a positive in 
the tax revenue it brings to the community.  
 
Commissioner McFall moved to approve Resolution No. 19 series of 2026, imposing an emergency 
temporary moratorium on the construction of data centers, which will apply in all zone districts within 
Fremont County. Commissioner Grantham seconded the motion. Upon Vote: Commissioner McFall, aye; 
Commissioner Grantham, aye; Commissioner Bell, aye. The motion carried by unanimous consent.

6. Resolution No.21, Series of 2026 Adding Centennial State Liquid Investment Pool as a 
Designated and Approved Official Depository for Fremont County 
 
County Attorney Bellas explained that the county may use only depositories approved by this board. 
Every year, the board adopts the resolution officially designating these depositories, which also contains 
the budget and appropriating monies.  Last year, as the board was preparing to adopt the regular four 
depositories, Fremont County Treasurer Elliott approached them and said she was considering adding 
another depository, called the Centennial State Liquid Investment Pool. After thorough research, she has 
now determined and recommended to the board that the board authorize the use of the Centennial State 
Liquid Investment Pool (CSLIP) as an official depository for Fremont County funds. A couple of weeks 
ago, they met with a representative from CSLIP to get more information. The investment is funded 
solely with public funds, and, given its liquidity, the county can deposit and withdraw at will. Another 
key difference is that the majority of the investments are not in commercial paper; most of these pools 
have most of their investments in commercial paper, whereas CSLIP has less than 50%. 
 
Commissioner Grantham further stated that taxpayer dollars are invested and depositories are 
approved by the commission or the legislature into safe, beneficial, and liquid investments, and that this 
investment pool meets these requirements. 
 
Commissioner McFall moved to approve Resolution No. 21, series of 2026, adding the Centennial State 
Liquid Investment Pool as a designated  and approved official depository for Fremont County.  
Commissioner Grantham seconded the motion. Upon Vote: Commissione r McFall, aye; Commissione r 
Grantham, aye; Commissioner Bell, aye. The motion carried by unanimous consent.
