Subject: September 8, 2026 Work Session Summary: Economic Development Investment 
Program 
Bottom Line 
The purpose of this memo is to document the summary of discussions during the September 8, 
2026 Work Session on Economic Development Investment Program. All Councilmembers were 
present in person. 
Discussion Summary 
Staff provided an overview of the Economic Development Investment Program (EDIP) including 
the relationship to the Business Assistance Policy, guiding principles, potential processes 
around distributions and approvals, funding mechanisms, risk and compliance metrics, and next 
steps in the project lifecycle. 
City Council provided general support for the plan and discussed the program’s foundations, 
how funding could be deployed, and accountability measures. Themes included: 
• Council expressed broad support for the program concept.
• Council recommended utilizing the EDIP to support community vitality initiatives over tax
revenue. 
EDD should work to frame the program in favor of quality of life, neighborhood
character, a
nd micro-business proof-of-concept rather than jobs or sales tax
generation.

• Role of the Economic Advisory Board. There was concern about added bureaucracy, 
delay, or unwanted veto power. Generally considering an after-the-fact review role rather 
than preliminary application review. 
• Equity and access. Concern was raised about businesses that struggle to navigate City 
processes, and how the program would reach those less able to interface with the City. 
• Accountability without added barriers. Council wanted compliance measures and didn't 
want to burden the program or businesses with heavy documentation or requirements. 
• Funding structure and sustainability. Questions about the initial dollar figure, one time 
versus ongoing appropriations, budget tradeoffs, and how the program scales with 
inflation. There was discomfort with ongoing annual commitments given the budget 
environment, with a preference for one-time funds instead. 
• Program identification of systemic barriers. An idea that the program could help surface 
common barriers such as sidewalk requirements that could then be fixed more broadly 
through code or process changes, rather than one-off funding. 
• Staffing capacity was discussed as to whether an added FTE would be needed, and a 
desire for Council input on how staff resources get split between this and other Business 
Assistance Policy work. 
• There was suggestion that the program's name should be changed. 
 
Next Steps 
• November 2026, Council adopts the 2027/28 budget. 
• Q4 2026, staff incorporates council feedback and direction into the EDIP program and 
targets date for potential adoption and appropriation of one-time money.

Subject: September 8, 2026 Work Session Summary: Safe Parking Pilot Program 
Bottom Line 
The purpose of this memo is to document the summary of discussions during the September 8, 
2026 Work Session on the Safe Parking Pilot Program. All Councilmembers were present.  
Discussion Summary 
Staff provided Council with an overview of Safe Parking Programs, City history exploring Safe 
Parking, and the City’s role in a pilot program. Staff then outlined the current pilot program 
proposal and status of neighborhood outreach around the two proposed sites. Staff sought City 
Council input on the following questions: 
1. Are Councilmembers interested in City staff bringing code updates to allow a Safe 
Parking Program to operate in Fort Collins? 
2. If yes, what considerations for code updates and enforcement are top of mind for 
Councilmembers? 
Highlights and key takeaways from the work session discussion include the following: 
• Councilmembers expressed general support to explore code changes that would allow a 
Safe Parking Program pilot in Fort Collins, with mixed input on whether to pursue 
permanent code changes or a pilot. 
• There was support for the City to hold neighborhood meetings prior to Council voting on 
an ordinance to ensure community and neighbor voices are heard.

• Councilmembers expressed gratitude to the faith community for partnering with 
nonprofits on this project, with encouragement to host shared dinners to build community 
through the program. 
• Councilmembers raised questions about site safety, site monitoring, and how to manage 
the cars that utilize the site; staff communicated that partners will be responsible for 
operational oversight. 
• Councilmembers acknowledged the two proposed pilot sites are different and discussed 
whether different expectations or monitoring could be applied to each site; while there 
was recognition the two sites differ, there was not consensus among Councilmembers 
for applying different expectations or requirements on the sites 
Next Steps 
Given Councilmembers’ direction, staff will bring forward an ordinance to enable approved Safe 
Parking pilot sites to operate in Fort Collins. Staff anticipate bringing forward an ordinance for 
Council consideration in the next two months.  
Staff are coordinating to host neighborhood meetings/conversations, tentatively scheduled for 
the week of October 19. Staff are also developing more specific pilot site approval guidelines, 
recommended pilot “off-ramps,” and other information to share with Council alongside the 
forthcoming ordinance and/or code changes. 
Follow-up Items 
A question was asked about the funding structure for this program; staff have followed up with 
the partner agencies and have the following information to share: 
• First United Methodist Church has funding available and is prepared to cover all related 
on-site costs. 
• Trinity Lutheran, or the “301 Partners” (as the site additionally hosts Mary of Magdala 
Ecumenical Catholic Community and St. Paul's Episcopal Church) have shared site 
costs during their eight-year partnership and would share SafeLot Program costs. The 
301 Partners have about $9,000 to put toward start-up costs and have budgeted an 
additional $2,000 annually. They have also applied for two grants and are awaiting 
decisions on those awards.
