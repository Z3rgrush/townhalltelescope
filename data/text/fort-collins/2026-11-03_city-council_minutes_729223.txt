 6. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 142, 2026, Amending Section 5.2.1 of the 
Land Use Code of the City of Fort Collins Concerning Affordable Housing to 
Align With State of Colorado Requirements. 
 
This Ordinance, unanimously adopted on First Reading on October 20, 2026, aligns 
the Land Use Code with Senate Bill (SB) 26-040, Affordable Home Ownership 
Program, and House Bill (HB) 23-1304, Affordable Housing Programs. This update 
specifically addresses affordable housing requirements to comply with HB23-1304 
adopted in 2023 and SB 26-040 adopted in 2026.  These bills working together 
require jurisdictions to qualify housing projects that restrict at least 50% of the 
dwellings for sale to 120% of the Average Median Income (“AMI”) as affordable 
housing as being eligible for expedited development review.  
 
Staff updated the Ordinance for Second Reading to clarify that affordable 
housing projects delivering for-sale units where 50% of units are affordable to 
households earning 120% of Area Median Income are eligible for expedited 
review and not the other incentives in the Land Use Code and Municipal Code.

 15. Items Relating to Stormwater Quality Requirements and Enforcement 
Processes. 
 
A. First Reading of Ordinance No. 153, 2026, Amending City Code Section 26-500 to 
Adopt by Reference an Updated Fort Collins Stormwater Criteria Manual.  
B. First Reading of Ordinance No. 154, 2026, Making Various Changes to City Code 
Article VII, Chapter 26 to Address Enforcement of Violations.  
C. First Reading of Ordinance No. 155, 2026, Making Various Changes to City Code 
Article VII, Chapter 26 to be Consistent with the Municipal Separate Storm Sewer 
System (MS4) Permit. 
 
This item has been amended to include a corrected version of the Stormwater Criteria 
Manual (November 2026) Redlines Subset. 
 
The purpose of this item is to propose updates to City Code and various sections of 
the Stormwater Criteria Manual (“the Manual”) by introducing changes that encourage 
predictable and sustainable growth. Key changes include: 
 
Criteria Manual: Replace erosion control escrow with other enforcement mechanisms, 
provide flexibility in permanent revegetation requirements, and formalize the Low 
Impact Development ("LID") payment-in-lieu option as an alternative to constructing 
LID facilities on-site. 
 
Code – Enforcement: Consolidate and clarify stormwater quality enforcement 
provisions, align them more closely with wastewater, and expand enforcement tools. 
The three main changes are adding abatement and administrative fines and shifting 
from criminal to civil enforcement, which complement the removal of erosion control 
escrow from the Manual. 
 
Code – MS4: One clarification was added to the allowable discharges to exclude 
stormwater runoff with incidental pollutants from the definition of contaminated water.

 19. First Reading of Ordinance No. 161, 2026, Adopting the 2027 Classified 
Employee Pay Plan. 
 
The purpose of this item is to recommend the 2027 City Classified Employee Pay 
Plan for consideration of Council approval, by adoption of this Ordinance. Classified 
jobs are grouped according to job functions, a business practice commonly used by 
both the public and private sectors. Pay ranges are developed by career group 
(management, professional, administrative, operations and trades) and level for each 
job function. The result of this work is a City Classified Employee Pay Plan which sets 
the minimum, midpoint and maximum pay ranges for the level within each career 
group and function. Actual employee pay increases are awarded through a separate 
administrative process in accordance with the budgeted amount approved by Council.

 22. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 148, 2026, Transferring Unexpended and 
Unencumbered Appropriation of the Halligan Capital Project to the Water Fund 
Operating (Lapsing) Budget for Participation in the NISP Twenty-Second Interim 
Agreement and Payment of Additional Participation Analysis Expenses Incurred 
by the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District. 
 
This Ordinance, adopted by split vote (6-1) on First Reading on October 20, 2026, 
transfers an appropriation. The City has been working since the late 1980s to enlarge 
Halligan Reservoir, previously owned by the North Poudre Irrigation Company 
(“NPIC”), to meet the City’s needs for additional water supply and storage. The City 
began seeking regulatory approval with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (“Corps”) in 
2006 to enlarge Halligan Reservoir as part of the Halligan Water Supply Project 
(“Halligan Project”). Throughout, City staff continued to evaluate water storage 
alternatives on a variety of factors (cost, supply impacts, water quality impacts, 
environmental impacts) as regulatory landscape, costs, and opportunities evolved. 
 
In light of developments over the last 18 months, including the availability of allotment 
units in the Northern Integrated Supply Project (“NISP”), updated project and 
operating costs, and significant permitting uncertainties for the Halligan Reservoir 
project, the City’s participation in NISP appears more certain than the enlargement of 
Halligan Reservoir through the Halligan Project. Participation in NISP is projected to 
result in lower overall rate increases, and, depending on the level of participation, may 
provide additional storage to help with longer-term City goals including climate 
adaptation, housing, and economic development. 
 
Staff has been working with the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District 
(“Northern”) to understand the impacts of joining NISP and negotiate potential 
participation.  Key to this evaluation, and the associated cost benefits to the City, is 
the ability to bring City water rights to the project. Through this effort, agreements 
have been drafted that allow the City to buy into NISP with the ability to store water 
attributable to some of the City’s own water in Glade Reservoir. 
 
To pursue these agreements in earnest, staff believes it is also necessary to notify the 
Corps of the City’s intention to join NISP and subsequently withdraw the application to 
the Corps for a permit under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act for the Halligan 
Project.
