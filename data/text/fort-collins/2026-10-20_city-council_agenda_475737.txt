CONSENT CALENDAR 
The Consent Calendar is intended to allow Council to spend its time and energy on the important 
items on a lengthy agenda. Staff recommends approval of the Consent Calendar. Agenda items pulled 
from the Consent Calendar by either Council or the City Manager will be considered separately under 
their own Section, titled “Consideration of Items Removed from Consent Calendar for I ndividual 
Discussion.” Items remaining on the Consent Calendar will be approved by Council with one vote. The 
Consent Calendar consists of: 
•  Ordinances on First Reading that are routine; 
•  Ordinances on Second Reading that are routine; 
•  Those of no perceived controversy; 
•  Routine administrative actions.

4. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 134, 2026, Appropriating Prior Year Reserves in Support 
of the Utility Network for the Wastewater Utilities. 
This Ordinance, unanimously adopted on First Reading on October 6, 2026, appropriates $60,000 
of supplemental funding within the Wastewater Utilities Fund for One Water’s Wastewater Utility 
Network technology project.  
The Utility Network (UN) is a project to be implemented in support of each Utility – Light & Power, 
Water, Wastewater and Stormwater. Based on the unique needs of each utility’s migration to the 
UN, proposed under different phases, th e project will span several years for complete 
implementation. Each Utility’s Fund has incorporated the UN in their respective Capital 
Improvement Plan. Additions to the project budgets within each Utilities Fund varies based on 
needs in standing up the UN.  
Based on the project timeline in support of the UN being operable for the Wastewater Utilities, 
there is a current need to obtain professional services for Maximo asset management integration. 
Absent available funds within Wastewater Utilities UN project budget, this supplemental funding 
request supports entering into a service agreement before year -end to maintain the project 
timeline. 
5. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 135, 2026, Annexing the Property Known as the Strauss 
Lakes Annexation to the City of Fort Collins, Colorado. 
This Ordinance, unanimously adopted on First Reading on October 6, 2026, annexes 147.499 
acres of land into the City of Fort Collins. The Strauss Lakes Annexation is a 100% voluntary 
annexation located on the northeast corner of t he Ziegler Road and East Horsetooth Road 
intersection, within the City's Growth Management Area. The annexation meets all State of 
Colorado statutory requirements for voluntary annexation, including a contiguous perimeter of 
40.5%, well above the one-sixth (16.67%) minimum required by State statute. The annexation is 
also consistent with the Intergovernmental Agreement for the Fort Collins Growth Management 
Area between the City of Fort Collins and Larimer County.  
There is a related agenda item for zoning on the property.

12. First Reading of Ordinance No. 142, 2026, Amending Secti on 5.2.1 of the Land Use Code 
of the City of Fort Collins Concerning Affordable Housing to Align With State of Colorado 
Requirements. 
The purpose of this item is to align the Land Use Code with Senate Bill (SB) 26 -040, Affordable 
Home Ownership Program, an d House Bill (HB) 23 -1304, Affordable Housing Programs. This 
update specifically addresses affordable housing requirements to comply with HB23 -1304 
adopted in 2023 and SB 26 -040 adopted in 2026.  These bills working together require 
jurisdictions to qualify housing projects that restrict at least 50% of the dwellings for sale to 120% 
of the Average Median Income (“AMI”) as affordable housing as being eligible for expedited 
development review.

19. Items Related to Water Storage for the City Water Utility. 
A. Resolution 2026 -119 Authorizing the City Manager to Withdraw the Application to the U.S. 
Army Corp of Engineers for a Permit Under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act for the Halligan 
Water Supply Project. 
B. Resolution 2026-120 Authorizing the City Manager to Execute Agreements with the Northern 
Integrated Supply Project Water Activity Enterprise for the City to Become a Participant in the 
Northern Integrated Supply Project and to Store Water Attributable to Some of the City’s Own 
Water Rights in Glade Reservoir. 
C. First Rea ding of Ordinance No. 148, 2026, Transferring Unexpended and Unencumbered 
Appropriation of the Halligan Capital Project to the Water Fund Operating (Lapsing) Budget for 
Participation in the NISP Twenty -Second Interim Agreement and Payment of Additional 
Participation Analysis Expenses Incurred by the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District. 
The purpose of this item is to authorize the City Manager to withdraw the application to the U.S. 
Army Corp of Engineers, execute agreements with the Northern Integrated Supply Project Water 
Activity Enterprise, and to transfer an appropriation. The City has been working since the late 
1980s to enlarge Halligan Reservoir, previously owned by the North Poudre Irrigation Company 
(“NPIC”), to meet the City’s needs for additional water supply and storage. The City began seeking 
regulatory approval with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (“Corps”) in 2006 to enlarge Halligan 
Reservoir as part of the Halligan Water Supply Project (“Halligan Project”). Throughout, City staff 
continued to evaluate water storage alternatives on a variety of factors (cost, supply impacts, 
water quality impacts, environmental impacts) as regulatory landscape, costs, and opportunities 
evolved. 
In light of developments over the last 18 months, including the availability of allotment units in the 
Northern Integrated Supply Project (“NISP”), updated project and operating costs, and significant 
 
 
 
City of Fort Collins Page 9 of 10  
permitting uncertainties for the Halligan Reservoir project, the City’s participation in NISP appears 
more certa in than the enlargement of Halligan Reservoir through the Halligan Project. 
Participation in NISP is projected to result in lower overall rate increases, and, depending on the 
level of participation, may provide additional storage to help with longer-term City goals including 
climate adaptation, housing, and economic development. 
Staff has been working with the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District (“Northern”) to 
understand the impacts of joining NISP and negotiate potential participation.  Key to t his 
evaluation, and the associated cost benefits to the City, is the ability to bring City water rights to 
the project. Through this effort, agreements have been drafted that allow the City to buy into NISP 
with the ability to store water attributable to some of the City’s own water in Glade Reservoir. 
To pursue these agreements in earnest, staff believes it is also necessary to notify the Corps of 
the City’s intention to join NISP and subsequently withdraw the application to the Corps for a 
permit under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act for the Halligan Project.
