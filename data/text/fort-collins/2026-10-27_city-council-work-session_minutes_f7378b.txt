Subject: October 27, 2026 Work Session Summary: Housing Action Plan 
The purpose of this memo is to document the summary of discussions during the October 27, 
2026 Work Session on the Housing Action Plan. Councilmember Hoeven was absent from the 
discussion. All other Councilmembers attended in-person. 
Discussion Summary 
City staff and Mollie Fitzpatrick, Managing Director at Root Policy Research, presented an 
overview of the Housing Action Plan process and requirements, along with preliminary strategy 
recommendations. Staff clarified that, once approved, the Housing Action Plan would replace 
the 2021 Housing Strategic Plan as the City’s guiding housing strategy document.  
Preliminary strategies were organized into five categories:  
1. Housing Supply (Production and Product Diversity) – Strategies to make development 
more efficient and predictable for developers; ensure minimum housing production 
targets are met; and encourage the development of the types of housing missing in the 
market. 
2. Housing Access and Affordability – Strategies to support the development of structured 
affordable housing products, specifically those projects filling the largest affordability 
gaps (under 50% AMI for rentals and under 100% AMI for homeownership); and to 
ensure efficient and predictable access to the affordable housing available. 
3. Housing Preservation – Strategies to maintain the existing stock of structured affordable 
housing and, when feasible, naturally occurring affordable housing.

4. Housing Stabilization and Homelessness Prevention – Strategies to support residents in 
maintaining and stabilizing in their housing. 
5. Funding Sources and Financing Options – Strategies to sustain (and expand if/as 
appropriate) funding; modify funding processes to create more efficient and streamlined 
processes for housing developers and providers; and to evaluate opportunities to 
increase revenue or engage in innovative financing options to support housing projects 
and programs. 
Generally, Councilmembers were supportive of the strategies presented and provided direction 
regarding using more action-oriented language, being clear about the anticipated impact and 
metric options for each strategy and creating an implementation plan that prioritizes and 
schedules out strategy implementation over the next six years.   
Councilmembers emphasized the need to continue to focus on housing supply, highlighted 
opportunities to make additional modifications to the Land Use Code to promote affordability 
and product diversity and expressed a desire to better incorporate some of the activities in the 
Affordable and Sustainable Growth Initiative (Ad Hoc Committee) to the HAP strategies. 
Councilmembers acknowledged the challenges for developers in producing units affordable to 
households at 50% AMI and below and raised questions about how the City can support the 
development of these units while acknowledging these units are dependent on limited funding 
and financing resources outside of the City’s control. Councilmembers also discussed the 
housing production targets, with some Councilmembers noting the 7,000 catch-up/keep-up 
number should be a minimum floor Fort Collins is trying to hit. 
Next Steps 
The City’s consultants, Root Policy Research, are scheduled to deliver a draft of the Housing 
Action Plan, including updated strategies based on Councilmember input, by the end of 
November. Staff are coordinating with the City Clerk’s Office to schedule presentations with 
Boards and Commissions interested in providing input to the Housing Action Plan and are 
targeting to complete Boards and Commissions presentations by early February. Concurrently, 
the City will post the draft Housing Action Plan for the minimum 30-day public comment period. 
After making any final edits based on Boards and Commissions and community input, staff will 
share the final, complete draft with Councilmembers in a memo. Based on Council direction at 
that time, staff can make additional edits or schedule to bring the Housing Action Plan to a 
Council meeting for approval.

Subject: October 27, 2026 Work Session Summary: Homelessness Response System, 
Strategy, and Funding 
Bottom Line 
The purpose of this memo is to document the summary of discussions during the October 27, 2026 
Work Session on Homelessness Response System, Strategy and Funding. Councilmember Hoeven 
was absent. All other Councilmembers were present and in-person.  
Discussion Summary 
Staff provided Councilmembers with an overview of the status of the regional homelessness response 
system, Regional Homelessness Strategy implementation, and evolving funding priorities. Staff 
sought City Council input on the following questions: 
1. What questions do Councilmembers have about the regional homelessness response system 
and the City’s role within it? 
2. In thinking about funding priorities, are there opportunities Councilmembers see to align 
funding differently to meet City goals? 
Highlights and key takeaways from the work session discussion include the following: 
 Councilmembers expressed interest in regional interaction and collaboration, including raising 
questions regarding the regional funding structure, how Larimer County is participating in the 
homelessness response system, and how regional service changes affect the demand on 
local providers. 
 Councilmembers asked for and staff provided clarification on housing problem solving, if this 
funding structure is based on models seen elsewhere, and what types of services it provides.

 There was a question about the Safe Parking Program, and staff clarified this item had moved 
to the December 1 Council Regular meeting for discussion. 
 Regarding funding, Councilmembers sought clarification related to sole sourced grants, 
funding for rapid rehousing, and status of federal funding. 
 Councilmembers noted the tensions inherent in establishing funding priorities and expressed 
general support for the three current homelessness priorities (shelter, housing assistance & 
supportive services, and prevention). 
 Staff clarified the cold weather shelter plan ahead of the opening of the new shelter facility (the 
Homeless Resolution Center, operated by the Fort Collins Rescue Mission, tentatively opening 
mid-January 2027) and heard general Councilmember support for continuing to allocate 
funding specifically to shelter programs.  
Next Steps 
 Given the rapidly changing service environment in Fort Collins, staff plan to submit a memo to 
Council during Q1 2027 after the necessary renovations to Samaritan House/Catholic 
Charities, the move into Family Housing Network’s new shelter facility at 811 E. Myrtle, and 
the new construction of FCRM’s Homeless Resolution Center are complete and shelter 
capacity has stabilized. 
 HCV staff are in process of developing options for modifying the human services and 
homelessness priority platforms, including options for establishing target funding amounts for 
each priority or recommendations to shift funding to certain community priorities. The Human 
Services and Housing Funding Board will consider these options and endorse the updated 
priority platforms at their December meeting. The Competitive Process for human services and 
homelessness funding will open in early 2027, the Human Services & Housing Funding Board 
will deliberate, and funding recommendations will be brought to City Council in early summer 
2027.

Subject: October 27, 2026 Work Session Summary: Tobacco Plus Licensing and 
Enforcement Program 
Bottom Line 
The purpose of this memo is to document the summary of discussions during the October 27, 
2026 Work Session on the Tobacco Plus Licensing and Enforcement Program.  Councilmember 
Hoeven was absent. All other Councilmembers were present and in-person. 
Discussion Summary 
Councilmembers generally supported the proposed requirements of the Tobacco Plus Licensing 
and Enforcement Program, which would regulate tobacco, nicotine, and age-restricted 
psychoactive products. Several wanted to learn more about banning flavored tobacco products, 
including the potential effect on sales tax revenue. Councilmembers also asked about Larimer 
County’s development of its own tobacco licensing program and expressed interest in 
coordinating efforts with the County. 
Staff recommended aligning local kratom regulations with the existing provisions of the state’s 
Daniel Bregger Act after hearing community feedback from the October 8 public meeting and 
consulting with the Larimer County Coroner. Individual Councilmembers indicated they are open 
to a complete ban on kratom products.
