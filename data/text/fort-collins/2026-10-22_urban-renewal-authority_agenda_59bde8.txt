SUBJECT FOR DISCUSSION 
UPDATE: North College Good Neighbor Coalition Charter and Framework 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The purpose of this item is to seek Board direction on the proposed Good Neighbor Charter and Coalition 
framework for North College redevelopment, beginning with 1636 North College and related superblock 
opportunities. The Charter describes how residents, businesses and project partners could share defined 
decision-making responsibilities while the FCURA Board retains oversight and reserved authority. 
The Board is asked to confirm the general direction, id entify revisions and indicate whether it would like 
to form a subcommittee to help refine the Charter and develop Coalition nomination recommendations. 
Full Board approval of the Charter is planned for December 2026. This discussion does not adopt the 
Charter, appoint Coalition members or authorize a spending envelope but seeks to validate overall 
direction. 
GENERAL DIRECTION SOUGHT AND SPECIFIC QUESTIONS TO BE ANSWERED 
1. Does the Board support the general direction of a Good Neighbor Coalition with meaningful community 
decision-making roles, defined partner commitments and continued Board oversight? 
2. What changes or safeguards should guide the next draft, particularly for representation, delegated 
authority and participation support? 
3. Would the Board like to form a subcommittee to help refine the Charter and develop Coalition 
nomination recommendations for full Board consideration? 
BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
The Charter formalizes requests from both the FCURA Board as well as synthesizes an emerging response 
to what the team is hearing through stakeholder groups about participation, partnership and community 
benefit. This charter proposes the creation and stewardship of a partnership body that can help guide 
redevelopment in North College, beginning with the 1636 North College property and related superblock 
opportunities. It translates community-led development from an engagement aspiration into an operating

system: representative membership, defined decision rights, reciprocal commitments, supported  
community leadership, transparent escalation and Board accountability. 
The model suggested here is grounded in delegated authority with guardrails. The   FCURA Board retains 
all powers that law, fiduciary duty or adopted policy require it to exercise. The Coalition receives meaningful 
authority over matters the Board expressly delegates with a formal concurrence and escalation role on 
major recommendations. 
 
PROPOSED FRAMEWORK 
Community leadership and representation. The discussion draft proposes 15 voting  seats: six 
residents, two small businesses, three community -serving or anchor institutions, two delivery or funding 
partners, and two at -large members. The eight resident and small -business seats provide a community -
rooted majority. Composition and selection remain open for Board direction. 
Evolution over time. The initial focus would be design and project formation. Later reviews could adjust 
skills, membership, functions, legal structure or geographic scope for operations and stewardship. Material 
changes would return to the Board, with community input and continued Good Neighbor commitments. 
Decision rights and accountability. The Coalition would decide defined matters only after express Board 
delegation, which are proposed to include shaping major project recommendations, weighing alternatives 
through the lens of both community benefit as well as feasibility considerations, and monitor community 
benefits. The draft proposes concurrence by two -thirds of voting members present, with a quorum, after 
efforts to reach consensus. Unresolved concerns and reasons for Board departures would require 
documentation, including efforts to mitigate concerns. FCURA would retain final authority over reserved 
financial, property and contractual actions. 
Participation and partner commitments. Two pathways connect partner contributions to scoped 
influence and establish a pathway that supports community members in moving from consultation to 
shared decision -making. Lived expertise counts as a contribution. Compensation, language access, 
childcare, transportation and independent technical assistance would support meaningful participation. 
Budget delegation. A future Board-approved budget envelope could fund engagement, learning, interim 
activation and small community projects. The Board would establish the amount, term, eligible uses, caps 
and active delegation tiers. Staff would administer compliance, contracts and payments. Costs and funding 
sources remain to be developed for the approval package. 
OPTIONAL BOARD SUBCOMMITTEE 
For Board consideration, a subcommittee could work with staff, NRP partners and community 
representatives to refine the Charter, examine authority and resource needs, and develop an accessible 
nomination process and recommended Coalition slate. The Boa rd would define its membership, charge, 
reporting schedule and duration with FCURA counsel’s review. 
The draft Charter currently proposes a mixed community nominations panel. If a Board subcommittee is 
preferred, the next draft should define how the two work together or consolidate their roles while retaining 
resident and small -business participation. The subcommittee would recommend; the full Board would 
approve the Charter and make appointments. Nominee recommendations could follow Charter adoption if 
additional outreach is needed.

Executive Summary for the Fort Collins Urban Renewal Authority Board 
October 22, 2026 discussion 
1 - What the Charter is intended to do 
The Board has expressed a goal that North College redevelopment activities benefit the people 
who live along the corridor. The Good Neighbor Charter would formalize that goal by establishing 
a Coalition through which residents, local businesses and partners help shape d ecisions from 
planning through implementation. It would define how community leadership works alongside the 
FCURA beginning with 1636 North College and related superblock opportunities. 
October’s FCURA Board discussion seeks direction on the framework and formation of a possible 
Board subcommittee; full Charter review and approval is planned for December. 
2 - Elements that support community led decisions 
The draft charter proposes creation of a 15-member Coalition, with eight seats for residents and 
local small businesses. Members would help frame priorities and evaluate alternatives before 
choices narrow. The Coalition would have defined authority to recommend major project choices 
and decide specific matters when expressly delegated by the Board to the Coalition.  For matters 
where the FCURA Board is the decision maker, the Coalition’s recommendation will be recorded 
for the Board including minority views that dissent from the majority recommendation.  The 
Coalition would act expressly as a bridge to the community so influence extends beyond people 
with the time or resources to attend meetings. 
3 - Balancing community expertise and development realities 
Residents bring knowledge of neighborhood needs, barriers and local opportunities. Staff, 
consultants and partners bring development, financing and operating expertise. The process  
proposed in the Charter  would put both forms of knowledge  on equal footing,  identifying 
community outcomes, developing costed options, and working through tradeoffs together.  
Capacity building to support the Coalition could include development and finance training, plain-
language project budgets, visits to comparable projects and independent technical assistance. 
Compensation, interpretation, childcare and transportation would support participation.

4 – Suggested activities for Coalition  
Learning before a decision: To facilitate shared vision, technical understanding, and community 
benefit, we would approach Coalition capacity building as a collective endeavor.  For example, 
before reviewing a hub proposal, members could complete a short workshop on construction 
costs, operating budgets and funding restrictions, then visit a comparable facility  to better 
understand both development details as well as community benefit lessons learne d. Staff and 
residents could compare options together, examining what each costs and whom it serves. 
A defined pool of funds:   One recommendation included in the charter is the creation of a 
defined budget envelope that the Coalition is charged with overse eing as a way to advance 
community ownership in the redevelopment.  As an example, the Board could authorize a $50,000 
envelope for a year of interim community activities, with a $10,000 cap per project. It would set 
eligible uses, selection criteria, conf lict rules and reporting requirements. The Coalition could 
select eligible projects within that authority; staff would check compliance, execute agreements 
and make payments. Requests beyond the limits would return to the Board. 
The amounts are hypothetica l. Participation support and training would also need identified 
funding. The Charter itself would not appropriate money or activate a delegation  but would give 
the FCURA Board a governance structure in which to formally engage in Community -Led and 
Community-Informed development practices.   
5 - How the participants would work together 
Community: Identifies needs, tests options and evaluates results through continuing outreach  
managed by the NRP and FCURA staff.   
Coalition: Works with FCURA and the NRP to weigh priorities and make recommendations for 
approval.  When authorized, the Coalition exercises delegated authority, and reports back  to 
FCURA Board on decisions and community benefit  recommendations as appropriate.  Further, 
acts as liaison to community on an ongoing basis.   
FCURA staff: Coordinates the work, assesses feasibility, prepares Board actions and administers 
authorized contracts and funds. 
NRP consultant team: Supports engagement, synthesizes findings and develops or coordinates 
technical opt ions within its assigned scope. It would support Coalition deliberations and staff 
recommendations; approval authority would remain with the authorized decision makers. 
FCURA Board: Sets direction, appoints members of Coalition, defines any delegation and retains 
reserved funding, property and contractual decisions. Material changes return to the Board.

The Fort Collins Urban Renewal Authority (“FCURA”) Finance Committee asked for an update on the 
finances and funding availability for the North College Urban Renewal Plan (“Plan”) at their August 13, 
2026, meeting. An initial draft of this material was pre sented to the committee on October 8, 2026. This 
item provides a revised update on the current financial situation within the Plan Area and availability of

GENERAL DIRECTION SOUGHT

2. Does the board want to allocate the remaining $4.33 million in anticipated funding differently than

BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
The North College Urban Renewal Plan (Plan) was adopted in 2004 and governs all activities that 
remediate or prevent blight within the Plan boundaries. To effectively invest remaining TIF funds generated 
within the Plan boundaries, FCURA adopted a strategic funding plan in 2020 that identified three primary 
focus areas: Community Hub, Connected & Vibrant Neighborhoods, and Infrastructure. The strategic 
funding plan called for an allocation of available funds split fifty (50%), twenty -five (25%), and twenty-five 
(25%) percent across the three categories respectively. At the time of adoption, there was approximately 
$13.8 million in projected available TIF funding across the remainder of the collection period (see Table 
1).

In 2025, the estimated TIF funding available between 2026 and 2030 grew significantly. In addition, FCURA 
had identified several strategic property acquisitions that would support the objectives of the Plan. As a 
result, FCURA authorized the issuance of bonds and closed on that funding in June of 2025. The resulting 
available funds, including cash on hand, projected net cash 1, and bond proceeds had increased to $ 28.1

 State Funding. During the 2026 legislative term the Colorado General Assembly passed House 
Bill 26-1065 creating Transit and Housing Investment Zones. Allows a local government and transit 
agency to jointly undertake a transit investment project. To finance the project, the local government 
may apply to the Colorado economic development commission to designate a transit investment 
area and an approved financing entity. The bill authorizes an urban renewal authority as an 
approved financing entity that can receive state sales ta x increment revenue to support the 
investment. This revenue consists of the state sales tax collected in the designated area above a 
base amount, plus an additional 20% to account for out-of-area deliveries. Staff are still evaluating 
the applicability of this funding source as a method for supporting aspects of the 1636 
redevelopment. 
 Owned Assets. The URA owns or will own several properties that might generate additional funds 
for Plan objectives. Both the property at 1513 N. College and 1220 N. College may not make sense 
for long-term FCURA ownership. Depending on their future use, FCURA may be able to recoup a 
portion of its cost to acquire and remediate both sites. Many things remain to be considered before 
either property is sold and staff will continu e to develop both disposition plans and estimates of 
potential revenue from the sale of one or both properties. 
 Property Value. The 6.0 acres of land owned or soon to be acquired by FCURA, also creates 
significant value, if not revenue, towards any future redevelopment at the site of the former grocery 
store. The fact that this land is owned without debt and can be offered at little to no cost for the right 
type of development can significantly reduce the cost of redevelopment of the site. Additionally, the 
property may be able to generate revenue in the future to support the Plan depending on the nature 
of the ground lease(s) entered as part of the redevelopment process. 
Currently, the exact value of these other funding sources is not known. However, staff will continue to 
evaluate and estimate their ability to generate funds to support the Plan.
