EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The purpose of this item is to authorize the Mayor to execute agreements with the Northern Integrated 
Supply Project Water Activity Enterprise. The City has been working since the late 1980s to enlarge 
Halligan Reservoir, previously owned by the North Poudre Irrigation Company (“NPIC”), to meet the City’s 
needs for additional water supply and storage. The City began seeking regulatory approval with the U.S. 
Army Corps of Engineers (“Corps”) in 2006 to enlarge Halligan Reservoir as part of the Halligan Water 
Supply Project (“Halligan Project”). Throughout, City staff continued to evaluate water storage alternatives 
on a variety of factors (cost, supply impacts, water quality impacts, environmental impacts) as regulatory 
landscape, costs, and opportunities evolved. 
In light of developments over the last 18 months, including the availability of allotment units in the Northern 
Integrated Supply Project (“NISP”), updated project and operating costs, and significant permitting 
uncertainties for the Halligan Reservoir project, the City’s participation in NISP appears more certain than 
the enlargement of Halligan Reservoir through the Halligan Project. Participation in NISP is projected to 
result in lower overall rate increases, and, depending on the level of participation, may provide additional 
storage to help with longer -term City goals includin g climate adaptation, housing, and economic 
development. 
Staff has been working with the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District (“Northern”) to understand 
the impacts of joining NISP and negotiate potential participation.  Key to this evaluation, and the associated 
benefits to the City, is the ability to bring some City water rights to the project. Through this effort, 
agreements have been drafted that allow the City to buy into NISP with the ability to store water attributable 
to some of the City’s own water in Glade Reservoir. 
To pursue these agreements in earnest, staff believe it is also necessary to notify the Corps of the City’s 
intention to join NISP and subsequently withdraw the application to the Corps for a permit under Section 
404 of the Clean Water Act for the Halligan Project.

BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
Need For Additional Storage 
As has been discussed with Council for a number of years, the Water Utility needs at least 8,200 acre-feet 
(AF) of additional water storage to store its senior water rights to help the City mitigate the effects of severe 
drought and meet anticipated future demands through buildout of the Water Utility service area (currently 
projected for 2065). 
Halligan Project 
To meet water storage needs, the City has been actively pursuing Halligan Reservoir expansion over many 
decades through the Halligan Project. The City secured ownership of the dam back in 2004 by executing 
a purchase option acquired in 1993 from NPIC for the purpose of expanding the reservoir. 
The essence of the Halligan Project is that the City would build a larger dam just downstream of the current 
dam to raise the water level and enlarge the existing reservoir on the North Fork of the Poudre River by an 
additional 8,200 AF. The City would own the dam and the enlarged capacity and use it to store water 
diverted under the City’s senior water rights. NPIC would continue to own and use the historical/existing 
capacity in the enlarged reservoir to irrigate agricultural lands north of Fort Collins. The City would be wholly 
responsible for the long -term operation, maintenance and capital projects for the enlarged reservoir for 
both the City and NPIC.  
The City officially began the federal permit ting process in 2006 along with other partners (at the time the 
reservoir was envisioned to be even larger). The key required approval is a permit under Section 404 of 
the Clean Water Act. The Corps issued the final environmental impact statement in Octobe r 2023. Over 
the course of this period, with the handful of changes to the Halligan Project, staff reviewed and analyzed 
other water storage options as part of the federal process and to verify that the Halligan Project was the 
right project for utility ratepayers and the City. Until recently, staff consistently viewed the Halligan Project 
as the best water storage option from various perspectives (e.g., economic, environmental, legal, etc.). 
Halligan Permitting Outlook 
The City completed all permitting requirements in 2026 and anticipated receiving the 404 permit from the 
Corps in August 2026. However, starting last year, the Corps hinted that the enlargement of Halligan 
Reservoir may not be identified as the least environmentally damaging practicable alter native (“LEDPA”), 
especially given participation in NISP appeared to be feasible as some NISP participants signaled they 
were leaving that project. Noting a few uncertainties with NISP, City staff continued with the permitting 
process for the Halligan Proj ect while participating in discussions with Northern regarding the viability of 
the City joining NISP and storing water attributable to some of the City’s senior water rights in Glade 
Reservoir. In June 2026, the Corps requested the City provide evidence b y July that NISP was not the 
LEDPA, which the City provided. In early August the Corps extended its review of the Halligan Project 
permit application until February 1, 2027. 
At a subsequent meeting with the Corps on August 11, 2026, the Corps indicated the  enlargement of 
Halligan Reservoir was not viewed as the LEDPA as compared to other water storage alternatives and the 
City had three options with the Halligan Project permit application: 
Option 1 Request a final permit decision, likely prompting an official denial from the Corps  
Option 2 Withdraw the application, pausing enlargement of the reservoir.

Option 3 Proceed with Corps’ delay; complete supplemental EIS analyzing City participation in NISP,

The Co rps’ new position makes the Halligan Project’s legal/regulatory path much more difficult and 
uncertain due to the discretion courts generally give to federal permitting agencies. Staff recommend 
Option 2 (withdrawing the application) as it may make it easier to restart the permitting process in case the 
City is unable to secure a final allotment contract to join NISP. Hence, staff is including a resolution to 
withdraw the application. 
Additionally, the City of Greeley (“Greeley”) has appealed the Halligan P roject’s Clean Water Act 
Section 401 water quality certification to the Water Quality Control Commission (“Commission”) on July 30, 
2026. Greeley believes the construction and operation of an enlarged Halligan Reservoir would impact the 
water quality of Greeley’s Seaman Reservoir and subsequently the raw water that enters Greeley’s water 
treatment plant. All required downstream impacts were modeled and addressed in the Halligan Project’s 
mitigation plan which demonstrated that there were no adverse water qu ality impacts from Halligan on 
Seaman Reservoir or the downstream reaches of the North Fork. In fact, there was a quantifiable net 
benefit to water quality and water temperature as well as improved flows. While City staff are confident that 
the Halligan Pr oject’s Section  401 water quality certification would prevail in front of the Commission, 
Greeley’s request generated additional uncertainty for the Corps related to its decision on the Halligan 
Project 404 permit application. The appeal proceedings are on going with a hearing in front of the 
Commission scheduled for August 2027. 
NISP 
Over the past two years, understanding the level of fiscal and resource commitment necessary to build an 
enlarged Halligan Reservoir, City staff explored other water storage options, including NISP, to verify the 
City was investing in the best storage project for the future of the Water Utility, its ratepayers, and the City.  
NISP is an off-channel water storage project comprised of two large reservoirs: Glade Reservoir near the 
mouth of the Poudre Canyon and Galeton Reservoir northeast of Greeley. In 2022, the Corps issued a 404 
permit for NISP. The NISP Enterprise, a water activity enterprise owned by Northern, will own, operate, 
and maintain NISP on behalf of many municipal participants. Glade Reservoir is favorably located in the 
Poudre Watershed and abl e to store water diverted under the City’s water rights with minimal upstream 
impacts (as is the case with the Halligan Project) and downstream impacts. 
By participating in NISP with specific terms addressing the City’s water rights storage, the City can u tilize 
NISP’s water rights or elect to store water using its own water rights in Glade Reservoir, offering flexibility 
for the City to address various conditions in the future.  
The City has worked to negotiate better outcomes for the Poudre River throughout the duration of the NISP 
permitting process and presently participates in the NISP Adaptive Management regional stakeholder 
committee. As a NISP participant, the City will continue to advocate for the health of the river and financially 
support a propor tional share of the Poudre River Improvement Fund and required NISP environmental 
mitigation projects. Staff will continue to work with project and community partners on future restoration 
and protection priorities for the river, aligning with other planne d City investments, where feasible and 
reasonable. 
Based on several factors, staff has concluded that participation in NISP at 5.8% of the Glade component 
(9,860 AF of storage) is now the preferred option. While no option can provide 100% certainty, some form 
of NISP participation in the Glade Reservoir portion of the project currently appears more certain from a 
legal/regulatory perspective than the Halligan Project. The City’s analysis also shows participation would 
result in lower rate forecasts and may provide additional yield to help with other City goals including drought 
mitigation and various other climate adaptations, regional water security, housing, and economic 
development. Due to the permitting challenges facing Halligan, the need for regulator y and infrastructure 
coordination, and NISP appearing to proceed toward construction, now would be the time to join NISP.

A. The City owns and operates a wate r utility that provides water service to 
customers in its service area and delivers water pursuant to various other contractual 
arrangements.   
   
B. The City has determined that it needs additional water supplies and water 
storage for various reasons, includ ing for drought protection, climate resilience, and to 
meet projected demands. 
 
C. The City previously determined that the enlargement of Halligan Reservoir 
could meet the City’s needs for additional water supplies and water storage.  Beginning 
in the late 1980s, the City began to work on a project for the enlargement of the existing 
Halligan Reservoir (“Halligan Enlargement Project”) , which includes the enlargement of 
the reservoir as well as related sub-projects and tasks.  See, e.g., City Council Resolution 
87-161; Water Utility Enterprise Resolution No. 1.   
 
D. The enlargement of Halligan Reser voir would require a permit under 
Section 404 of the Clean Water Act (“404 Permit”) from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers 
(“Corps”).  The City thus filed an application to the Corps for a 404 Permit  in 2006.  The 
Corps labeled its project stemming from the  application as the “Halligan Water Supply 
Project,” which includes an evaluation of the City’s purpose and need, alternatives to meet 
that purpose and need, and the environmental impacts of the various alternatives.  The 
Halligan Water Supply Project remains in the federal permitting process.   
 
E. It has become increasingly questionable whether the Corps will issue a 404 
Permit for the enlargement of Halligan Reservoir as currently contemplated in the Halligan 
Water Supply Project permitting proceedings pending before the Corps.  Moreover, rising 
construction and permitting costs have made the enlargement of Halligan Reservoir  
increasingly difficult from a financial perspective.   
 
F. The City has thus explored alternative means to meet the City’s needs for 
additional water supplies and water storage.  This has included exploring potential 
participation in the project of the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District 
(“Northern Water”) known as the Northern Integrated Supply Project (“NISP”) , which 
includes Glade Reservoir.
