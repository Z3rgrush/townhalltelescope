4. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 134, 2026, Appropriating Prior Year Reserves in Support 
of the Utility Network for the Wastewater Utilities. 
This Ordinance, unanimously adopted on First Reading on October 6, 2026, appropriates $60,000 
of supplemental funding within the Wastewater Utilities Fund for One Water’s Wastewater Utility 
Network technology project.  
The Utility Network (UN) is a project to be implemented in support of each Utility – Light & Power, 
Water, Wastewater and Stormwater. Based on the unique needs of each utility’s migration to the 
UN, propo sed under different phases, the project will span several years for complete 
implementation. Each Utility’s Fund has incorporated the UN in their respective Capital 
Improvement Plan. Additions to the project budgets within each Utilities Fund varies based on 
needs in standing up the UN.  
Based on the project timeline in support of the UN being operable for the Wastewater Utilities, 
there is a current need to obtain professional services for Maximo asset management integration. 
Absent available funds within Wastewater Utilities UN project budget, this supplemental funding 
request supports entering into a service agreement before year -end to maintain the project 
timeline. 
5. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 135, 2026, Annexing the Property Known as the Strauss 
Lakes Annexation to the City of Fort Collins, Colorado. 
This Ordinance, unanimously adopted on First Reading on October 6, 2026, annexes 147.499 
acres of land into the City of Fort Collins. The Strauss Lakes Annexation is a 100% voluntary 
annexation located on the northeast corner of the Ziegler Road and East Horsetooth Road

Colorado statutory requirements for voluntary annexation, including a contiguous perimeter of

also consistent with the Intergovernmental Agreement for the Fort Collins Growth Ma nagement 
Area between the City of Fort Collins and Larimer County.  
There is a related agenda item for zoning on the property.

Requirements. 
The purpose of this item is to align the Land Use Code with Senate Bill (SB) 26 -040, Affordable 
Home Ownership Program, and House Bill (HB) 23 -1304, Affordable Housing Programs. This 
update specifically addresses affordable housing requirements to comply with HB23 -1304 
adopted in 2023 and SB 26 -040 adopted in 2026.  These bills working together require 
jurisdictions to qualify housing projects that restrict at least 50% of the dwellings for sale to 120% 
of the Average Median Income (“AMI”) as affordable housing as being eligible for expedited 
development review.

19. Items Related to Water Storage for the City Water Utility. 
A. Resolution 2026 -119 Authorizing the City Manager to Withdraw the Application to the U.S. 
Army Corp of Engineers for a Permit Under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act for the Halligan 
Water Supply Project. 
B. Resolution 2026-120 Authorizing the City Manager to Execute Agreements with the Northern 
Integrated Supply Project Water Activity Enterprise for the City to Become a Participant in the 
Northern Integrated Supply Project and to Store Water Att ributable to Some of the City’s Own 
Water Rights in Glade Reservoir. 
C. First Reading of Ordinance No. 148, 2026, Transferring Unexpended and Unencumbered 
Appropriation of the Halligan Capital Project to the Water Fund Operating (Lapsing) Budget for 
Participation in the NISP Twenty -Second Interim Agreement and Payment of Additional 
Participation Analysis Expenses Incurred by the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District. 
The purpose of this item is to authorize the City Manager to withdraw the applicatio n to the U.S. 
Army Corp of Engineers, execute agreements with the Northern Integrated Supply Project Water 
Activity Enterprise, and to transfer an appropriation. The City has been working since the late 
1980s to enlarge Halligan Reservoir, previously owned by the North Poudre Irrigation Company 
(“NPIC”), to meet the City’s needs for additional water supply and storage. The City began seeking 
regulatory approval with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (“Corps”) in 2006 to enlarge Halligan 
Reservoir as part of the Halligan Water Supply Project (“Halligan Project”). Throughout, City staff 
continued to evaluate water storage alternatives on a variety of factors (cost, supply impacts, 
water quality impacts, environmental impacts) as regulatory landscape, costs, and  opportunities 
evolved. 
In light of developments over the last 18 months, including the availability of allotment units in the 
Northern Integrated Supply Project (“NISP”), updated project and operating costs, and significant 
Page 8
 
 
 
City of Fort Collins Page 9 of 10  
permitting uncertainties for the Halligan Reservoir project, the City’s participation in NISP appears 
more certain than the enlargement of Halligan Reservoir through the Halligan Project. 
Participation in NISP is projected to result in lower overall rate increases, and, depending on the 
level of participation, may provide additional storage to help with longer-term City goals including 
climate adaptation, housing, and economic development. 
Staff has been working with the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District (“Northern”) to 
understand the impacts of joining NISP and negotiate potential participation.  Key to this 
evaluation, and the associated cost benefits to the City, is the ability to bring City water rights to 
the project. Through this effort, agreements have been drafted that allow the City to buy into NISP 
with the ability to store water attributable to some of the City’s own water in Glade Reservoir. 
To pursue these agreements in earnest, staff believes it is also necessary to notify the Corps of 
the City’s intention to join NISP and subsequently withdraw the application to the Corps for a 
permit under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act for the Halligan Project.

3. Declaring the Month of October 2026 as Domestic Violence Awareness Month.
Page 15
 
PROCLAMATION 
 
WHEREAS, Colorado Criminal Code defines domestic violence means an act or threatened 
act of violence upon a person with whom has been involved in an intimate relationship and any 
crime against this person, their property, or any municipal ordinance violation against a person and 
property when used as a method of coercion, control, punishment, intimidation, or revenge; and 
 
WHEREAS, nationally 1 in 2 women and 2 in 5 men have been victims of severe physical 
violence by an intimate partner in their lifetimes; 15.5 million children are exposed to intimate 
partner violence each year, and 90% are eyewitnesses to this violence; domestic violence is a leading 
causes of homelessness for women and children; domestic violence rose by 8% in 2025; and 
 
WHEREAS, in Fort Collins, police handle 3 to 5 domestic violence calls every day; up to 
1,800 per year. In Colorado, domestic violence related fatalities have risen 24% since 2024; shelters 
are experiencing increased need in space; violence has become more extreme; eighty percent of 
victims are female and eighty-three percent of perpetrators are men, however these statistics do fail 
to represent our LGBTQ2+ and Black, Indigenous, an d other people of color who often face 
additional barriers to safety and experience domestic violence at disproportionately higher rates; and 
 
WHEREAS, in and around Fort Collins, Crossroads Safehouse & Services (since 1980) and 
the Zonta Club of Fort Collins (since 1997), and local human and social services agencies, together 
with volunteers, provide assistance and services to victims and their children including lifesaving 
crisis intervention, emergency shelter, safety planning, advocacy and support; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City of Fort Collins recognizes multiple efforts by our community partners 
to bring awareness to and serve our community to support victims of domestic violence. These 
annual efforts are Domestic Violence Awareness Month in October, and Zonta Says NO to Violence 
- 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence from November 25 to December 10. 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE , I, Emily Francis , Mayor of the City of Fort Collins, do hereby 
proclaim the month of October 2026 as

7. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 131, 2026, Amending Portions of Section 1 -15 of the 
Code of the City of Fort Collins Concerning General Penalties to Allow Restitution for 
Victims of Misdemeanors, Petty Offenses, and Civil Infractions, to Clarify what can be 
Requested as Restitution, and to Correct Cross References to Section 1-15. 
This Ordinance, unanimously adopted on First Reading on September 15, 2026, amends City 
Code Section 1-15 to align its restitution provision. 
City Code currently expressly allows for restitution for victims of misdemeanor offenses. This has 
been the language in the Code since 2005. Because the recent sentencing Code updates have 
reclassified many prior misdemeanor offenses as civil infractions or petty offenses, the Code must 
be amended to provide clarity of the intention to allow for restitution on offenses now classified as 
infractions and petty offenses, which include commonly charged offenses such as theft under 
$300, criminal mischief under $300, animal at large, and public nuisance. 
The proposed amendments ensure that restitution can be made to victims of all non -traffic 
offenses and clarifies the categories of expenses that can be compensated through restitution. 
There is also an error in the lettering of Section 1 -15.  This proposed amendment would correct 
that error to re -letter the Restitution section to subsection (f) and the Penalties section to 
subsection (g). 
The Ordinance also directs the codifier of the Code of the City to amend sections of the City Code 
to remove references to s pecific subsections of Section 1 -15 and replace such references with 
general references to Section 1-15. 
Adopted on Second Reading.

16. Second Reading of Ordinance No. 118, 2026, Amending the Land Use Code of the City of 
Fort Collins to Change the Type of Review for Certain Residential Uses. 
This Ordinance, adopted on First Reading on September 15, 2026, by a split vote of 5 -2 (Nays: 
Hoeven, Potyondy) brings an amendment to the Land Use Code. At the Ad Hoc Committee on 
Affordable and Sustainable Growth meetings on July 8, 2026, and August 5, 2026, the Committee 
directed Staff to bring an amendment to the Land Use Code that moves the level of review for 
single-unit dwelling, single -unit attached dwelling, two -unit dwelling, multi -unit dwelling, and 
mixed-use dwelling units to Basic Development R eview. The Committee requested staff make 
these amendments to the Land Use Code in alignment with the Council priority related to

affordable, sustainable growth. City Council also discussed potential changes to permitted uses 
at the July 14, 2026, work ses sion. Staff recommends that Council adopt an alternative to 
what the Ad Hoc Committee requested and this recommended alternative is included in 
the Ordinance. 
The recommended alternative changes the Land Use Code level of review for single-unit dwelling, 
single-unit attached dwelling, two-unit dwelling, multi-unit dwelling, and mixed-use dwelling units 
to Basic Development Review (“BDR”) in most zone districts that currently permit these uses. 
However, different from what the Ad Hoc Committee directed, staff recommends not changing the 
level of review for single -unit dwelling, single -unit attached dwelling, and two -unit dwelling in 
commercial and employment districts since City Plan envisions denser development in these 
corridors. The Planning & Zoning Commission voted 5-0 to recommend that Council adopt staff’s 
recommended Land Use Code changes with comments. 
If Council wishes to adopt the staff recommended Land Use Code changes, the Ordinance 
as presented should be adopted. This option does not change the l evel of review to BDR 
for single -unit dwelling, single -unit attached dwelling, two -unit dwelling, multi -unit 
dwelling, and mixed-use dwelling units in the commercial and employment zone districts.

change the level of review for single-unit dwelling, single-unit attached dwelling, two-unit

FIRST READING BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
The Lemay Avenue and Drake Road intersection (Project Location Map) has an excess crash cost of 
$460,000 compared to a typical four-legged intersection where the roadways meet at right angles. This is 
determined by evaluating statistical safety performance  using Colorado Department of Transportation 
(“CDOT”) methodology. The intersection is in the top five with safety concerns in Fort Collins and registers 
as a Level of Service of Safety (“LOSS”) IV using CDOT analysis techniques. This means the intersection 
has a high potential for crash reduction. It experi ences almost three more injury crashes per year than 
would be expected given volumes and geometry. Of special concern are approach -turn crashes , with 
statistically far more crashes than anticipated. 
The intent of this Project is to eliminate most of the conflicts that result in traffic crashes at the intersection. 
The proposed improvements (Project Typical Section and Site Plan) will add a southbound right-turn lane 
and convert the traffic signal from a "doghouse" style protected/permissive signal to flashing yellow arrows 
that can be operated protected-only on all four approaches. A protected signal is when, on the green arrow, 
drivers are given the right -of-way to  complete their turns, free of other traffic conflicts. A
