1) Variance Request for Setbacks for Shed Replacement – 159 E. Orchard (Lorena 
Salvador) 
 
Staff Presentation 
Don Dybala told the Board that this was discussed at the Planning Commission 
Meeting. The discussion included fire-resistant materials for the new shed and the 
positioning of the old shed. 
 
Scott Stoneburner, Building Inspector for the Town of Hotchkiss, said that he looked at 
the property and spoke with Lorena Salvador. She understands the rules and 
regulations pertaining to the new sheds. 
 
Don Dybala said that since this was on the alley, that location should be taken into 
consideration as well. 
 
Jim Jorgensen said that he does not think the Town should impose restrictions on this 
Variance that are not currently stated in the Ordinances. Jim noted that a Variance was 
recently approved in Willow Heights for setbacks with no further restrictions. He feels 
the Variance being requested is all that should be discussed, and adding extra 
stipulations sets an inappropriate precedent. 
 
Building Inspector Scott Stoneburner stated that the reason for the 7.5-foot setbacks in 
the code is to keep structures from sitting directly on a neighbor’s property line. 
 
Applicant Presentation 
Lorena Salvador said that they would like to replace the single old shed with two new 
metal ones. Replacing it with two units will give them more room than replacing it with 
one single unit as large as the current one. 
 
Public Comment 
• Opened at 6:50 p.m. 
• Tom Wills, 159 E. Orchard and current Planning Commission Chairperson, said 
that replacing the old shed cleans up a potentially hazardous situation. 
• Closed at 6:55 p.m. 
 
Board Deliberation 
The Board deliberated for several minutes regarding the setback request and the shed 
material. The Planning Commission recommended approval of this Variance with the 
stipulation that the structure be on skids and the sheds be metal, fire-resistant 
structures. 
 
Motion: Roger Christian moved to approve the Variance request for Erik Mendoza and 
Lorena Salvador with the stipulations recommended by the Planning Commission, 
seconded by Joel Reed. A verbal vote was taken with all Trustees voting in favor. 
Motion passed. 
 
2) Variance Request for Fence Height – 791 Barrow Mesa Road (Stacey Hodapp) 
 
Staff Presentation 
Building Inspector Scott Stoneburner told the Board that at the Planning Commission 
meeting held on May 27, 2026, the neighbors who attended were not opposed to the 
fence height. Mr. Hodapp will keep the existing chain-link posts to reinforce the fence, 
making it sturdier. Scott also noted that Stacey Hodapp has no problem following the 
codes and spoke with him at length about them. 
Sven Edstrom mentioned the 4-foot height limit required by the fencing Ordinance for all 
front-property fences within the 25-foot setback. Scott Stoneburner confirmed that Mr. 
Hodapp is aware of this requirement. 
Don Dybala noted that the Planning Commission had a tied vote (2 for, 2 against) on 
the motion for this Variance, meaning the motion did not pass at the commission level. 
 
Applicant Presentation 
Stacey Hodapp requested clarification on the 4-foot height restriction within the first 25 
feet from the front property line. He explained to the Board that this Variance Request 
for an 8-foot fence will help mitigate privacy issues created by his neighbors, who park 
by his backyard. The fence will block headlights from shining into his windows at night. 
He noted that only 138 feet of the perimeter will feature the 8-foot fence, while the 
remaining 141.73 feet will be left as is. 
 
Public Comment 
• Opened at 7:13 p.m. 
 
• Tom Wills, 159 W. Main Street, reiterated that the Planning Commission made 
no recommendation since the vote tied. He noted the main concern of the 
neighbors was the aesthetic attractiveness of the fence. 
 
• Closed at 7:15 p.m. 
 
Board Deliberation 
Don Dybala suggested that a 6-foot fence might solve the problem instead of an 8-foot 
fence. Jim Jorgensen noted there is no road where the fence is proposed, meaning Mr. 
Hodapp will only be blocking his own view line. Joel Reed commented that this was a 
unique situation. 
 
Following extended discussion, Attorney Bo Nerlin advised that the back-and-forth 
discussion needed to conclude so the Board could vote on the request. Mayor Pro Tem 
Sven Edstrom noted the Board had two options: 1) Grant the Variance Request, or 2) 
Ask the applicant to gather more information and return to the Planning Commission. 
 
Motion: Joel Reed moved to approve the Variance Request as presented, seconded by 
Daisie Kirk. A verbal vote was taken. Trustees Jim Jorgensen, Daisie Kirk, Joel Reed, 
and Roger Christian voted “Yes.” Trustee Don Dybala voted “No.” Motion passed. 
 
3) Variance Request for Fence Height – 319 Knob Hill Circle Drive (Joanna Clarke) 
Staff Presentation 
Building Inspector Scott Stoneburner told the Board that this is a unique, hill-situated 
property. He spoke with Joanna Clarke and noted the proposed structure will be a black 
mesh fence that will not impede traffic. 
Jim Jorgensen asked if it was a privacy fence; the reply was no. Tom Wills stated that 
the Planning Commission recommended approval due to the unique shape of the 
property. 
 
Applicant Presentation 
Joanna Clarke said their property is awkwardly shaped. The driveway sits beside the 
property line and all cars park outside of it. They want a 6-foot fence height around the 
property to deter deer from eating their garden once the drought ends. 
 
Public Comment 
• Opened and closed at 7:31 p.m. No comments were given. 
 
Board Deliberation 
Motion: Don Dybala moved to approve the Variance Request as presented, seconded 
by Joel Reed. A verbal vote was taken with all Trustees voting in favor. Motion passed. 
 
8. Action Items 
a. Agenda Application Request – Bill Babbel 
This item was deleted from the agenda. Bill Babbel located an alternative venue to 
distribute his free flags. 
 
b. Agenda Application Request – North Fork Pool, Park and Recreation District 
(Daniel Ihnot) 
Sven Edstrom recused himself from voting on this item, noting that he serves as the 
Vice President of the Recreation District. 
 
Daniel Ihnot told the Board that the District installed a sidewalk last spring. He 
requested cost-sharing under Ordinance 2013-01, which states the Town will pay for 
half of sidewalk construction costs. 
 
Don Dybala noted that sidewalks are included in the Master Plan updated in April of this 
year. Public Works Director Marvin Jackson stated that while the project falls under the 
Ordinance, approval is typically secured before construction begins. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem Sven Edstrom and Don Dybala commented positively on the community 
trail usage since the County completed its portion last year. Roger Christian and Jim 
Jorgensen agreed that budgeted funds should be utilized, despite the Ordinance stating 
requests should arrive 60 days before a project starts. 
 
Motion: Don Dybala moved to approve the request for cost-sharing with the North Fork 
Pool, Park and Recreation District in the amount of $10,375.00, seconded by Roger 
Christian. A verbal vote was taken with all Trustees voting in favor, excluding Mayor Pro 
Tem Sven Edstrom, who abstained due to his conflict of interest. Motion passed. 
 
c. Fireworks Permit Application – TNT Fireworks / Cowboys at the Cross 
Ministries 
Sven Edstrom noted that this stand has operated as a fundraiser in past years even 
during fire bans. Mayor Pat Medina and Chief Scott Green recommended via staff notes 
that the group seek an alternative fundraiser. Corporal Cody Prentice stated that local 
background checks were clear, but expressed safety concerns regarding fire risks this 
season. 
 
Don Dybala felt that permitting sales sends a message that setting them off is 
acceptable. Jim Jorgensen and Daisie Kirk supported the application, noting that legal 
fireworks have not historically caused major local fires and that banning local sales 
simply pushes buyers to adjacent areas. Chief Green noted that while a sales ban is 
wise if a fire ban is active, changing weather patterns could alleviate risks. 
 
Motion: Joel Reed moved to approve the Fireworks Application for TNT 
Fireworks/Cowboys at the Cross Ministries, seconded by Jim Jorgensen. A verbal vote 
was taken. Roger Christian and Don Dybala voted “No.” Jim Jorgensen, Daisie Kirk, 
and Joel Reed voted “Yes.” Motion passed. 
 
d. Possible Decision on Axon from May 28, 2026, Meeting 
Attorney Bo Nerlin reviewed the 10-year contract and noted language regarding upfront 
rebate reimbursements if the town terminates early. Chief Scott Green strongly 
supported the package for the Police Department. In response to Don Dybala, Chief 
Green noted it would not lower liability insurance rates directly but mitigates overall town 
liability. 
 
Daisie Kirk raised questions regarding Artificial Intelligence (AI) data usage. Chief 
Green clarified that built-in regulatory policies govern the AI elements. A work session 
and police ride-alongs were suggested to introduce the equipment to the public and 
trustees. 
 
Motion: Roger Christian moved to approve the 10-year Axon contract, seconded by 
Joel Reed. A verbal vote was taken with all Trustees voting in favor. Motion passed.

g. Attorney Contract Proposal and Possible Adoption 
Bo Nerlin introduced incoming Town Attorney Sarah Ross, who joined via Zoom. Joel 
Reed asked about future fee adjustments; Sarah Ross stated advance notice and 
formal Board discussion would occur before any rate updates. Chief Green asked about 
trial coverage in Municipal Court. Attorney Ross confirmed that if she cannot travel from 
Texas, Colorado-based firm attorneys or paralegals would cover, with standard travel 
and overnight adjustments billed as outlined. 
 
Motion: Jim Jorgensen moved to approve the contract appointing Wyatt, Hamilton and 
Findley as the new legal counsel for the Town of Hotchkiss, seconded by Don Dybala. A 
verbal vote was taken with all Trustees voting in favor. Motion passed. Sarah Ross will 
be formally appointed at the June 25, 2026, meeting.
