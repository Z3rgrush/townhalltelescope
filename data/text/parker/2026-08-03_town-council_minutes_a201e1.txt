each month, unless announced otherwise. The general public comment

provide comments on items that are not on that meeting’ s agenda. Public
comments can be made in person at the public meeting or in writing via
the online form below.

from noon on the Friday prior to the next meeting until 5 p.m. Monday ( the

Dear Members of the Parker Town Council, 
I am writing as a Parker resident and former Civics Academy
graduate to respectfully request that the Town revisit its current
regulations regarding the keeping of chickens. 
As it stands, Parker allows up to fifty ( 50) chickens on a
property, provided that they are kept at least 200 feet from any
off-site residence or business . While this standard is
reasonable for larger, agricultural- style properties, it
unintentionally excludes the vast majority of residential
homeowners who may wish to keep a small, non- commercial
backyard flock. 
Many surrounding communities— including Highlands Ranch, 
Castle Rock, and others across Colorado— have adopted more
flexible approaches that allow a limited number of hens
typically 4–8) on standard residential lots, with appropriate
safeguards such as coop setbacks, sanitation requirements, 
and prohibitions on roosters. These policies recognize the
important distinction between large- scale poultry operations
and small backyard flocks kept for personal use. 
Backyard chickens, when responsibly managed, offer several
community benefits: 
Sustainability and food resilience: Families can produce a small
portion of their own food locally. 
Educational value: Children gain firsthand experience with
animal care, responsibility, and where food comes from. 
Low impact: A small number of hens produce minimal noise
generally comparable to everyday neighborhood sounds) and, 
with proper care, do not create odor or nuisance issues. 
Alignment with broader trends: Many municipalities are
modernizing ordinances to support small- scale, non-
commercial urban agriculture. 
3
At the same time, I fully understand and respect the Town’ s
need to address concerns such as noise, odor, and
neighborhood compatibility. Fortunately, these concerns are
already well- managed in other municipalities through clear, 
balanced guidelines. 
I would respectfully propose that Parker consider adopting a
tiered or residential allowance, such as: 
Permitting a small number of hens ( e.g., 4–6) on standard
residential lots
Prohibiting roosters to minimize noise concerns
Establishing reasonable coop setbacks ( e.g., 5–15 feet from
property lines rather than 200 feet) 
Requiring clean, well- maintained enclosures to prevent odor or
pests
Maintaining enforcement mechanisms for nuisance complaints
This type of framework would preserve neighborhood quality
while allowing residents reasonable access to a practice that is
increasingly common, responsible, and beneficial. 
Parker is a community that values both quality of life and
thoughtful growth. Updating the current ordinance to distinguish
between large flocks and small backyard hens would better
reflect how residents live today, while still upholding the Town’ s
standards. 
Thank you for your time and consideration. I would welcome
the opportunity to support further discussion on this topic. 
Respectfully, 
Sara Moore
Parker Resident
