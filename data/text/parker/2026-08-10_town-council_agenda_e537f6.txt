SUBJECT: Small Business Development Center Update 
     
  
ITEM OVERVIEW:  
The Aurora–South Metro SBDC has provided its 2025 Annual Report summarizing support for 
Parker’s small business community. Ashvina Petel, Executive Director, will provide a full 
presentation during the Study Session. The report reflects strong year-over-year engagement, 
advising, training, and funding outcomes for Parker businesses. SBDC services reinforce 
Parker’s strategic priorities around economic vitality, business growth, and entrepreneur 
support. 
  
BACKGROUND AND ANALYSIS:  
The Colorado SBDC continues to serve as a statewide, accredited resource for small 
businesses, supporting up to 20,000 entrepreneurs annually.  
 
In 2025, Parker benefited from: 
• $4.29M in funding secured for local businesses.  
• 56 clients advised and 61.5 advising hours delivered.  
• 22 new business starts supported. 
 
Core programs supporting Parker included: 
• Business readiness workshops (“Fired-Up” courses). 
• Confidential, no-cost advising for startups and established businesses. 
• Access to statewide specialized programs such as TechSource, child-care business 
support, succession/exit planning, and LivePlan curriculum.  
 
Key benefit: SBDC programming continues to strengthen Parker’s entrepreneurial ecosystem 
and long-term business resilience. 
  
FINANCIAL AND POLICY IMPACTS:  
No immediate fiscal action required — $5,000 in the 2026 approved budget. The continued 
partnership with SBDC aligns with Parker’s adopted economic development priorities and 
supports business retention and expansion.

Supporting Small Business
Success
In 2025, the Small Business Development Center serving Aurora–South Metro
continued its mission of empowering businesses to thrive across Arapahoe and
Douglas counties, including the Parker community. The SBDC delivered a broad
range of accessible services to meet entrepreneurs where they are—offering in-
person, virtual, hybrid, and on-demand support to ensure that small business
owners could find guidance in the format that worked best for them. 
Training remained a core component of the SBDC’s work. Through a mix of
workshops and webinars, business owners gained practical knowledge across
topics essential to launching, operating, and growing a business.
Complementing these educational offerings, the SBDC provided confidential,
one-on-one consulting at no cost to clients. This individualized support helped
entrepreneurs navigate complex decisions, refine business strategies, and
address operational challenges with guidance from experienced advisors. 
In addition to direct advising and training, the SBDC played a critical role in
connecting Parker business owners to valuable local resources. Clients were
linked with partner organizations and networking opportunities that
strengthened their community ties and expanded their capacity for growth.
Through this combination of services, expertise, and resource connections, the
SBDC continued to provide Parker’s small businesses with a robust ecosystem of
support designed to foster resilience, innovation, and long-term success.

The Colorado SBDC empowers businesses to successfully navigate their full business
lifecycle—from launch through optimization to exit—by delivering impactful advising,
actionable training, and essential connections to community resources. 

The Aurora-South Metro SBDC continues to deliver two essential foundational
programs in Parker that prepare new and prospective business owners to stay
compliant and ready for effective advising. These workshops address the most
common early-stage needs—legal structure, taxes, permitting, readiness, and risk
reduction—and are required before one-on-one advising. This ensures every
entrepreneur enters the advising process with the core knowledge and
documentation needed to make informed decisions.
Our “Fired-up: I Want to Start a Business” workshop serves entrepreneurs with
new ideas or a businesses under two years old. This free class covers business
readiness, business registration, required licensing and permits, market research,
and how to build a strong business plan. It gives new business owners the clarity
and compliance basics necessary to launch successfully and transition smoothly
into individualized consulting.
Our new “Fired-up: I Want to Buy a Business” workshop supports individuals
exploring the purchase of an existing business—an increasingly important path in a
growing market like Parker. Participants learn where to find businesses for sale,
how the acquisition process works, what due diligence involves, and available
financing options. This early guidance helps clients avoid costly mistakes and
prepare for deeper advising on deal evaluation and long-term planning.
We work closely with Douglas Counties Libraries to offer both programs online, in
person, and in hybrid formats, ensuring accessibility for Parker’s busy and growing
population. The SBDC has delivered these workshops locally for several years and
will continue to do so, reinforcing our commitment to supporting a compliant,
resilient, and well-prepared small-business community in Parker.

Colorado’s innovation economy is growing, and the Colorado SBDC’s statewide
TechSource program helps drive that momentum. TechSource delivers specialized
advising, commercialization support, and access to nondilutive capital—direct
benefits for Parker’s expanding community of science and technology businesses.
A core strength of TechSource is its focus on nondilutive funding. Parker firms can
pursue SBIR/STTR grants and other federal research dollars without giving up
equity, helping startups and manufacturers scale more quickly. The program also
improves proposal quality, sharpens market strategies, and connects companies
with statewide innovation networks—shortening the path from concept to market.
Continued Town of Parker investment ensures local businesses benefit from both
SBDC’s core services and one of Colorado’s most impactful tech-focused programs.
TechSource supports high-quality jobs, new business development, and strengthens
Parker’s position in Colorado’s innovation economy.

The Aurora–South Metro SBDC has developed a new resource to support Parker’s
growing small-business community: the Financial Fitness Checkup. As conditions
evolve for local businesses, staying in touch, identifying their needs, and
strengthening their understanding of their financial position is essential. This
initiative aims to do just that.
Our Scorecard info sessions are designed to help business owners understand the
true health of their company before challenges arise. Much like a medical
evaluation reviews your overall wellness, this checkup provides a clear, holistic
snapshot of your financial stability and long-term readiness.
During these sessions, entrepreneurs will learn how our Small Business Scorecard
examines key indicators of business health, including:
 • Cash-flow performance and reliability
 • Credit strength and financing needs
 • Growth potential and expansion readiness
 • Stability of your foundational business systems
Launching this program in Parker reflects our commitment to equipping local
businesses with practical, data-driven tools that support long-term success in a
rapidly growing market. The Financial Fitness Checkup will be available online, in
person, and in hybrid formats to ensure accessibility for busy entrepreneurs. We
look forward to offering this new resource and helping Parker businesses build
stronger, more resilient financial foundations.

Funding Fridays brings together lenders from banks, CDFIs, and credit unions to
give small business owners clear, direct insight into the lending process.
Entrepreneurs can ask questions, explore financing options, and learn how to
strengthen loan applications. The program builds stronger relationships between
local businesses and financial institutions, improving community access to capital.
 • Direct access to lending experts
 • Clear guidance on loan requirements and financing options

As we update Funding Fridays, we’re aligning financing with each business stage—
early-stage entrepreneurs often need microloans, growing companies use lines of
credit and SBA products, and established firms require specialized or multi-lender
financing. The refreshed program will offer industry-specific sessions and
stage-based guidance so owners can quickly find the right lending partners. With
lenders actively competing for strong borrowers, Funding Fridays remains a neutral
space to compare options and make confident decisions.
The SBDC supports creative approaches to capital, including layered funding and
alternative financing. By modernizing Funding Fridays, we aim to help every small
business—across all industries and stages—access the capital, expertise, and
innovative solutions needed to grow.

SUBJECT: Land Development Ordinance Regarding Sign Enforcement and Temporary Signs 
     
  
ITEM OVERVIEW:  
Staff have identified minor amendments necessary to improve clarity of the Land Development 
Ordinance in the following sections: 
• 13.01.080 Enforcement and penalties,  
• 13.09.040 Temporary signs on developed parcels, 
• 13.09.050 Permanent signs on developed parcels, and  
• 13.08.110 Exterior lighting regarding light pole heights. 
  
BACKGROUND AND ANALYSIS:  
Staff have identified the following sections that would benefit from clarification and minor 
revision: 
• Section 13.01.080(b)(3) – Enforcement and Penalties: The current code does not 
clearly define what constitutes a violation or explain how violations and associated 
fines escalate over time. While the code establishes first through fourth offenses 
within a 12-month period, it does not clearly identify when the escalation period 
resets. The proposed amendment will define what constitutes a violation, clarify 
the process for escalating violations and fines, and define the "rolling 12-month 
period" used to track repeat violations. 
• 13.09.040(d)(3) Temporary signs on developed parcels: The current code regarding 
temporary freestanding signs is unclear. The proposed language will clarify the 
language of Temporary Freestanding. The current code allows for what is called the 
32/64 sign, which does not require a permit or registration. This has created 
confusion between permitting, enforcement, and the business community. The 
proposed changes would require registration. 
• 13.09.050 Permanent signs of developed parcels: Proposal is to clean up language 
regarding window signs to make it more clear and understandable. 
• 13.08.110 Exterior lighting regarding light pole height: The current code requires 
light poles to be set back from property lines by a distance at least equal to the 
height of the light pole. This standard has proven difficult to apply in commercial 

Page 2 
developments with shared parking lots or private drives where property lines often 
bisect functional parking areas. The proposed amendment exempts light poles 
adjacent to shared private drives and shared parking areas from this setback 
requirement while maintaining the standard along external property boundaries. 
 
  
FINANCIAL AND POLICY IMPACTS:  
There are no financial impacts. 
 
The code amendments are primarily cleanup and are limited to the specific changes proposed. 
 
COUNCIL DISCUSSION:  
Staff requests that Town Council provide input and direction for the proposed amendments.
