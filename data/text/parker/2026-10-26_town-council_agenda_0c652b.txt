SUBJECT: Parker Senior Center Update 
     
  
ITEM OVERVIEW:  
This study session will provide Town Council with an update on the Parker Senior Center's 
current operations, programs, services, and ongoing efforts to meet the needs of older adults in 
the community. Representatives from the Senior Center Board and management will highlight 
recent accomplishments, facility improvements, financial information, and operational 
information. 
 
Town staff will also provide an overview of the recently completed Parker Senior Center 
Feasibility Study and the initial steps being taken to implement its recommendations, with an 
emphasis on strengthening partnerships, improving service coordination, and supporting the 
long-term sustainability of senior services in Parker. 
 
This item is presented for Council's information and discussion. No formal action is requested. 
  
BACKGROUND AND ANALYSIS:  
Background/Prior Actions 
The Town has maintained a longstanding partnership with the Senior Center, including an 
annual financial contribution of $80,000 to support its operations. In addition to this annual 
contribution, the Town provided approximately $45,000 in supplemental funding in 2025 to 
address priority facility maintenance and repair needs, including drainage improvements, 
flooring concerns, and parking lot repairs.  
 
To help evaluate the evolving senior service needs in the community, the Town commissioned a 
comprehensive Senior Center Feasibility Study, conducted by BerryDunn and completed in 
2026. The study evaluated the existing facility, programs, operations, financial sustainability, 
governance, community demographics, and opportunities for improved coordination of senior 
services. The study findings were presented to the Town Council and Senior Center Board in 
the summer of 2026 and provided a foundation for identifying both near-term improvements and 
longer-term opportunities to strengthen services for older adults. 
 
Analysis/Discussion 
The feasibility study identified opportunities to enhance the delivery and coordination of senior 
services through improved organizational partnerships, operational efficiencies, and ongoing 
planning. 

Page 2 
The study emphasized the importance of maximizing existing resources, strengthening 
coordination among service providers, and establishing a collaborative approach to addressing 
community needs. 
Following completion of the study, Town staff and Senior Center representatives began working 
together to advance several initial implementation priorities, including: 
• Implementation Coordination: Establishing a collaborative framework between the 
Town and Senior Center to identify priorities, clarify responsibilities, and guide 
implementation of study recommendations. 
• Operational Improvements: Evaluating near-term opportunities to improve facility 
utilization, programming space, scheduling, and parking. 
• Community Partnerships: Exploring the establishment of a Senior Services 
Roundtable to strengthen communication and collaboration among organizations serving 
older adults. 
• Performance Measures: Identifying meaningful indicators to monitor participation, 
facility utilization, financial performance, and service delivery over time. 
• Long-Term Planning: Maintaining consideration of future facility and service needs 
while prioritizing practical, achievable improvements in the near term. 
These efforts represent an initial phase of implementation, with continued collaboration needed 
to refine priorities, establish timelines, and evaluate progress. 
 
  
FINANCIAL AND POLICY IMPACTS:  
No additional funding is being requested as part of this meeting. Any future funding proposals or 
policy considerations resulting from implementation efforts would be evaluated through the 
Town's established budget and approval processes. 
 
COUNCIL DISCUSSION:  
This study session is intended to provide Council with an opportunity to receive updates, ask 
questions, and offer general feedback regarding the Parker Senior Center and ongoing 
feasibility study implementation efforts. No formal Council direction is requested at this time.

SUBJECT: Town Hall Meeting Room Public Use Policy 
     
  
ITEM OVERVIEW:  
The Town Council is asked to review a proposed policy for the use of two Town Hall meeting 
rooms by eligible community organizations. Eligible nonprofit groups must be based in Douglas 
County or serve county residents through educational, civic, or cultural activities. 
 
Town business would have priority over use of the rooms, Pikes Peak and Antelope Creek. 
Groups could reserve rooms during business hours without a rental fee, subject to approval and 
staff availability. Meetings outside regular business hours would require special approval and 
payment for staffing and security to cover staffing costs incurred by the Town. The policy also 
sets rules for applications, safety, cleanup, and responsibility for damage. 
  
BACKGROUND AND ANALYSIS:  
Earlier this year, an organization that frequently partners with the Town requested to host a 
workshop in a Town Hall conference room at no cost. At the time, the Town had no policy or 
established practice for handling such requests. The policy now before Council would allow 
partner agencies to use select Town Hall conference rooms while giving priority to Town 
business. 
 
The primary purpose of Town Hall is to support official Town functions. The draft policy would 
allow public meetings when meeting room space is not needed by Town staff. The policy makes 
two conference rooms available for use by partnering agencies: the Antelope Creek room, 
which holds 10 people, and the Pikes Peak room, which holds 36. 
 
Eligible organizations wishing to reserve a room would apply at least 10 business days before a 
meeting and could request dates up to four months in advance. One application could cover 
recurring meetings for up to three months. A Town employee would need to be present during 
setup, the meeting, and cleanup. 
 
The policy would prohibit commercial events, private parties, fundraising, and campaign 
activities. Groups could not charge admission or collect donations. The Town Manager would 
oversee the policy and could waive requirements when in the Town’s best interest. Denied 
requests would have no appeal process. Reservations will also be received and managed by 
the Town Manager's Office. 
 
 

Page 2 
 
  
FINANCIAL AND POLICY IMPACTS:  
No significant financial or policy impacts are anticipated for the Town. Eligible organizations 
reserving rooms outside business hours would pay fees that fully cover the cost of Facilities and 
Police staffing. As a result, these reservations would have no net cost to the Town. Allowing free 
use of Town Hall meeting rooms for eligible organizations may take some rental business away 
from the PACE Center. 
 
COUNCIL DISCUSSION:  
Analysis/Discussion 
Adopting the policy will provide meeting space for eligible local groups and give staff clear rules 
for handling requests. Ensuring that Town departments have first priority in reserving rooms and 
requiring staff presence will help protect daily operations and building safety. After-hours 
charges will help cover staffing and security costs. 
 
The program will also require staff time to review requests and manage reservations. After-
hours costs, the application deadline, and limited room sizes could make access harder for 
some groups. 
 
Allowing eligible organizations to reserve space at Town Hall may reduce rental revenue at the 
PACE Center, particularly for events held during regular business hours. Town Hall reservations 
are free during weekday business hours, while a comparable rental at the PACE Center costs 
$40 per hour for nonprofit organizations. However, for events held outside regular business 
hours (weekdays after 5 PM. or on weekends), the PACE Center is the more cost-effective 
option due to additional staffing costs at Town Hall. For example, a four-hour Saturday rental 
would cost a nonprofit organization $160 at the PACE Center, compared to $564 at Town Hall. 
 
If approved by the Town Council, this policy will be integrated into the Town's Employee 
Handbook and made available for the public on the Town's website. 
 
Alternatives/Options 
Council could limit public use to business hours to reduce after-hours staffing needs, though this 
would limit options for evening and weekend meetings. The Town could broaden eligibility to 
serve more groups, which could increase demand for rooms but create additional impacts on 
staff and Town Hall itself.

Town Hall Meeting Room Public Use Policy  
Issued by the Town Manager’s Office 
1. Purpose and Scope 
This Policy governs the use of select public meeting rooms within Parker Town Hall by non-
profit and not-for-profit organizations not directly associated with the Town of Parker (the 
“Town”). It establishes terms, conditions, allowable uses, and user qualifications for the 
rooms listed in Attachment A. Town government functions always take precedence over 
public use, and the Town may suspend public access to meeting rooms if needed to 
protect operations or public safety. 
2. General 
• Town facilities exist primarily to serve Town departments, Town Council, and Town 
commissions. Town use supersedes public use. 
• Designated Town Hall meeting rooms, when not in use by the Town itself, are available 
for use at no charge by eligible non-profit and not-for-profit groups on a first-come, 
first-served basis. Use of the rooms by an eligible Applicant constitutes a limited public 
forum. Eligibility criteria are provided in Section 4 below. 
• The Town reserves the right to close meeting rooms to public use and end the limited 
public forum at any time. 
3. Interpretation and Implementation 
The Town Manager, or designee, interprets, administers, and enforces this Policy and may 
deny or terminate room use that does not comply. The Town Manager may waive 
requirements when doing so is in the Town’s best interest. 
4. Eligibility for Use 
Non-profit and not-for-profit organizations and groups based in Douglas County, or that 
serve Douglas County residents through educational, civic, or cultural purposes, may 
reserve eligible rooms for meetings. Groups may not charge admission fees to attendees.  
2 
A group may be determined ineligible if the Town Manager determines the use is outside 
authorized purposes. 
5. Prohibited Uses 
• Commercial, fee-based, promotional, or for-profit activities, including soliciting or 
selling products or services. 
• Programs involving the sale, advertising, or promotion of products or services. 
• Private social functions (e.g., showers, birthday parties, retirements, dances). 
• Meetings or activities that solicit or accept funds or donations from attendees. 
• Political fundraisers, partisan advocacy, rallies, or campaign activities/events. 
• Meetings whose noise levels interfere with Town business or adjacent properties. 
• Groups exceeding posted occupancy limits. 
• Illegal activity or uses contrary to law. 
• Activities inconsistent with the general business purpose of Town Hall. 
6. Reservation Policies and Procedures 
6.1 Application 
All organizations requesting room use must complete the Town Hall Meeting Room Public 
Use Form (Attachment B). The Applicant is responsible for the meeting and for the actions, 
behavior, and damages caused by attendees. 
Applicants must submit a completed application to the Staff Contact listed in Attachment 
A at least ten (10) business days prior to the requested meeting date. Applications 
submitted less than 10 days before the reservation require approval from the Town 
Manager. Applications must be complete. Organizations seeking recurring use may submit 
one application for up to three (3) consecutive months. Prior approval does not guarantee 
future approval. Applicants may request use up to four (4) months in advance. A $500.00 
refundable damage deposit is required for all reservations. 
When submitting the application, Applicants must attach the signed Policy Compliance 
and Indemnification and Hold Harmless Agreement (Attachment C). 
Application forms are available at Parker Town Hall, 20120 E. Mainstreet, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 
p.m., Monday through Friday, or on the Town’s website at parkerco.gov. 
6.2 Verification 
An Applicant will be required to verify the following information: 
3 
• Organization’s primary business address within Douglas County. 
• Description of the community benefit of the meeting. 
• Non-profit or not-for-profit status, if applicable. 
6.3 Approval Criteria 
Approval of applications will be considered in accordance with the following criteria: 
• Applicant is a non-profit or not-for-profit organization or group. 
• Applicant is based in Douglas County or serves Douglas County residents in a 
significant way, or the proposed use is deemed a community benefit. 
• Request is submitted with sufficient lead time for Town staff to assess impacts on 
Town Hall and scheduled meetings. 
• Applicant agrees to indemnify and hold harmless the Town and can carry sufficient 
liability insurance, if required. 
• Town staffing and security needs can be met. 
• Requested use will not negatively impact Town staff, resources, or facility capacity, 
including parking, traffic, and overlap with other events. 
• All reservations require the Applicant pay a $500.00 refundable damage deposit. 
 
There are no appeals on denials of applications. 
6.4 Supervision and Staffing 
The “Person Responsible” named on the application must be present at all times during 
room use. Non-compliance may result in denial of future requests. 
A Town employee presence at Town Hall is required whenever a meeting room is in use by 
the Applicant. Meeting room use is subject to the availability of Town staff. The Applicant is 
responsible for all room set-up and clean-up, and at least one Town staff member must be 
present on-site during the meeting and throughout the Applicant's set-up and clean-up 
period. Town staff are not available to provide service or support during the meeting. If the 
Town is unable to provide the required staff presence, the meeting room will not be 
available for use, and the reservation may be denied, canceled, or rescheduled. 
Reservations outside of Business Hours. For this Policy, “Business Hours” means 8:00 
a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday. 
Any use outside of Business Hours requires special approval of the Town Manager. 
Reservations outside of Business Hours require the Applicant to pay for facilities and 
security staffing. A fee will be assessed to the Applicant to cover staffing costs incurred by 
the Town. The Town will arrange for Facilities staff to be present upon completion of an 
4 
application and payment of required fees. It is the Applicant’s responsibility to arrange 
for the presence of an off-duty Parker Police Officer to provide security staffing. Use of 
private security is not permitted. 
Payment of the $500.00 refundable damage deposit and any estimated staffing fees are 
due no less than five (5) business days prior to the day of the event. The damage deposit 
and any staffing fees for reservations made less than 10 days in advance and have received 
approval from the Town Manager are due immediately upon the Town’s approval of the 
reservation application. 
Facilities staffing fees are collected in advance of the event; security staffing fees will be 
invoiced by the Parker Police Department after the event is completed. 
See Attachment D for rates and scheduling details. 
6.5 Cancellations 
Applicants wanting to cancel their reservation are encouraged to do so with as much 
advanced notice as possible. Notice of a cancellation must be directed to the Staff 
Contact listed on Attachment A. Repeated cancellations or cancellations given without 
sufficient advanced notice may result in the denial of future application requests. 
Cancellation of reservations must be made in writing (via email) 72 hours prior to the event 
to receive a refund of staffing fees. Applicants who do not cancel a reservation for their 
event scheduled outside of business hours at least 72 hours in advance, or who are no-
shows for their event, will be invoiced for the minimum staffing fees listed in Attachment D. 
7. General Rules and Regulations 
• Attendance is limited to the posted occupancy of each room as determined by the Fire 
Marshal and listed in Attachment A. If there is a conflict, the Fire Marshal’s 
determination controls. 
• Seating or additional furniture is not allowed in corridors. 
• Attendees may only use public entrances; doors may not be propped open; emergency 
exits must remain clear. 
• Town staff control HVAC, lighting, and other systems. Only the Applicant may request 
adjustments. 
• No adhesive items may be affixed to walls, doors, windows, or woodwork. 
• Meeting room doors must be closed during Business Hours. Conduct must be 
respectful and well governed. 
• Noise in lobbies and hallways must be kept to a minimum. 
5 
• Use must not interfere with Town business or unduly disrupt others in Town Hall or 
adjacent properties. 
• Only water and coffee are permitted in meeting rooms. The Town does not provide 
coffee service or supplies. 
• No catering, warming devices, or open flames are allowed.  
• All trash must be placed in proper receptacles. 
• No alcoholic beverages or illegal substances may be brought into Town Hall. 
• No pets are permitted; service animals are allowed. 
• Smoking, including e-cigarettes and vaping, is not allowed. 
• Glitter, confetti, streamers, pyrotechnics, and fog machines are prohibited. 
• Rooms must be left clean. Tables are not permitted to be moved; chairs must be 
returned to their original arrangement. 
• Town Hall may not be used for storage or as a mailing address; the facility may not be 
considered a permanent meeting place. 
• All minors (under 18) must be accompanied by an adult. 
• Applicants are encouraged to visit the reserved room at least three business days in 
advance to confirm setup; staff may or may not be able to accommodate setup 
changes. 
8. Equipment 
Use of the audio/visual equipment installed in each meeting room is permitted.  
Alteration or removal of Town equipment, including technology devices and computers, is 
prohibited. The Applicant are responsible for damages to Town-owned property. 
Audio-visual equipment and technical support are not provided by Town staff. 
9. Disclaimer 
Use of Town facilities does not constitute Town endorsement of any viewpoints, beliefs, 
ideas, or policies expressed by organizations or individuals using the space and may not be 
advertised or implied as such. 
10. Use of Town Name or Logo 
Use of the Town of Parker name or logo is prohibited for any purpose in connection with 
meeting room use, except to identify the physical location of the meeting.

11. Room Accessibility 
Available meeting rooms are ADA-compliant. Those requesting accommodations (e.g. 
accessible seating layouts, assistive listening devices, etc.) should note their request 
when completing the Town Hall Meeting Room Public Use Form (Attachment B).

12. Non-Discrimination 
The Town will not discriminate against any person based on age, sex, race, religion, color, 
national origin, disability, or other status protected by law, and will not permit 
organizations making reservations to engage in such discrimination. 
13. Releases of Liability 
The Town is not responsible for personal injury or damage to any property the Applicant 
brings into Town Hall in connection with room use. The Applicant waives all claims, losses,

By submitting the application, the Applicant agrees to comply with this Policy and to
