TITLE: A BILL FOR AN ORDINANCE TO LEVY GENERAL PROPERTY TAXES 
FOR THE YEAR 2026 TO HELP DEFRAY THE COSTS OF 
GOVERNMENT FOR THE TOWN OF PARKER, COLORADO, FOR THE 
2027 BUDGET YEAR 
WHEREAS, the Town Council of the Town of Parker has adopted the annual budget in 
accordance with the Local Government Budget Law, on November 16, 2026; 
WHEREAS, the amount of money generated by the Town’s 2026 property tax mill levy 
that will be collected in 2027 is approximately $3,799,000; 
WHEREAS, the 2026 valuation for assessment for the Town of Parker, as estimated by 
the County Assessor, is $1,459,956,443; 
WHEREAS, pursuant to Paragraph 8(b) of Section 20 of Article X of the Colorado 
Constitution, each taxing district in Colorado may enact cumulative uniform exemptions and 
credits to reduce or end business personal property taxes;  
WHEREAS, the personal property tax is a tax which is levied annually and is in addition 
to all applicable sales and use taxes collected when any personal property is first utilized in a 
business; and 
WHEREAS, the Town Council desires to establish a tax policy which will remain in effect 
unless otherwise change d by ordinance, it being the explicit purpose and understanding of the 
Town Council that a return to the prior exemption limits under Section 39- 3-119.5, C.R.S. is not 
a tax policy change or a new tax as contemplated by TABOR. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE TOWN 
OF PARKER, COLORADO, THAT: 
Section 1. Tax Levy.  For the purpose of meeting all general operating expenses of the 
Town of Parker during the 2027 budget year, there is hereby levied a tax of 2.602 mills upon each 
dollar of the total valuation for assessment of all taxable property within the Town for the year  
2026. 
Section 2. Certification Authorization.  The Finance Director is hereby authorized 
and directed to certify to the County Commissioners of Douglas County, Colorado, the mill levies 
for the Town of Parker as hereinabove determined and set. 
Section 3. Tax Exemption.  The Town Council hereby establishes a tax policy to align 
with Douglas County’s application of the uniform exemption from taxation upon business personal 
property for the year 2026, pursuant to Paragraph 8(b) of Section 20 of Article X of the Colorado 
Constitution.  This exemption amount shall apply to all parcels located in the Town of Parker and 
shall apply to those taxes levied by the Town of Parker.  Town Council further directs that the 
Town Finance Department take whatever actions are necessary to implement this policy.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
In 2027, the Town will move to a self-insurance model for the majority of its automobile, 
liability, and property insurance costs. If approved, this ordinance will establish a new Risk 
Management Fund to capture revenues and expenditures associated with those budget items. 
  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
Approve 
  
BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION 
Section 9.9 of the Town Charter states that "...in addition to funds provided for in this Charter, 
the Council may by ordinance establish special funds as it deems necessary and appropriate. The 
ordinance establishing such funds shall clearly state the purpose for the fund." The purpose of 
establishing the Risk Management Internal Service Fund is to pay: 
• General liability claims and automobile liability claims and related expenses brought 
against the Town, its employees or officials;  
• Claims against the Town or its officials or employees arising under Federal law, which 
the Town is legally obligated to pay;  
• Costs of defense, including expert witness fees, outside counsel legal fees, investigation 
and other related defense costs; 
• Operating costs related to managing the risk management program or claims brought 
pursuant to Section A; 
• Insurance premiums 
 
 
 
 

Page 2 
Other important information related to the establishment of this fund includes: 
• The Fund shall maintain reserves to provide for contingency in case contributed funds in 
any given year are not adequate to cover actual expenses realized in that year. 
• Interest earned from the investment of funds will stay in this fund and become part of the 
reserves.  
• Setting aside of reserves for self-insurance purposes shall not be construed as creating an 
insurance company.  
• Accounting of these funds shall be in a manner consistent with the applicable 
governmental accounting standards. All unexpended and unencumbered funds shall 
remain in the Fund. 
• Reserve levels will be monitored and budget appropriated each year to maintain adequate 
levels to support anticipated expenditures. 
• If at any time there are not adequate reserves to cover actual expenses, a transfer may be 
made into the Fund either through the budget process or a supplemental appropriation 
approved by Council. 
Revenues and expenditures will begin being recorded in the Risk Management Fund on January 
1, 2027, with approval of this ordinance. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPACT 
As part of the 2027 Proposed Budget, $1,512,000 in expenditures are budgeted in the Risk 
Management Fund to account for costs associated with Town property, liability, and automobile 
insurance. Revenues from allocations from the General Fund, Cultural Fund, Recreation Fund, 
and Stormwater Fund total $1,512,000, and interest earnings are budgeted at $20,000. 
Additionally, a transfer from the General Fund in the amount of $2,000,000 will provide a 
beginning fund balance for this new fund.

Title: ORDINANCE NO. 8.22.2 - First Reading  
A Bill for An Ordinance to Amend Sections 4.08.070, 4.08.100, and 
4.08.130 of the Parker Municipal Code Stormwater Management 
Program 
  Department: Engineering and Public Works, Alex Mestdagh 
  Second 
Reading: 
November 16, 2026 
 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
If approved, this ordinance will revise the chapter of the Town's Municipal Code related to the 
Stormwater Management Program. The revisions propose to adjust the Stormwater Utility Fee 
and its method of annual adjustment and to revise the costs the Town recovers related to 
pursuing delinquent fees. 
  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
Approve 
  
BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION 
Background/Prior Actions 
In 1999, the Town of Parker’s Stormwater Utility was created upon the recommendation of a 
citizen advisory board.  The purpose of the Utility was to provide a funding mechanism that 
would allow the Town to provide maintenance assistance for stormwater infrastructure, construct 
stormwater improvements, and provide regional stormwater planning.  The Stormwater Utility is 
considered an enterprise fund, meaning it is funded solely through a utility fee and receives no 
funding from the Town’s General Fund. 
 
The Town’s Stormwater Utility Fee is governed by the Town’s Municipal Code.  The Code 
states that “The Town Council shall review the amount of the [Stormwater Utility] fees described 
in this Chapter at least every five (5) years.” See Chapter 4.08.120.  To facilitate this review of 
the fees, the Town has traditionally commissioned a Needs Assessment for the Utility at these 
milestones to compare anticipated revenues to future expenses.  Most recently, Muller 
Engineering completed an update to this Assessment early in 2026. 
 
Analysis/Discussion 
The 2026 update to the Needs Assessment showed that, while the Utility’s base operating 
expenses could be met over the next ten years with anticipated revenues, rising construction 

Page 2 
costs have impacted the Utility’s ability to construct necessary capital improvement projects for 
stormwater infrastructure.  These projects include the reconstruction of damaged infrastructure, 
modernizing outdated or poorly functioning infrastructure, major preventative maintenance 
projects, and stream stabilization projects, among others.  These projects are vital elements of the 
Utility’s responsibilities, as they reduce the risk of stormwater infrastructure failure and the 
associated impacts to adjacent properties.  While the Town’s stormwater infrastructure is 
generally in good condition, large areas of the Town’s storm sewer system are nearly 30 years 
old and will likely need increased repair and maintenance in the coming years.  Staff has recently 
delayed capital improvement projects to ensure that an adequate balance was maintained within 
the Stormwater Utility Fund.  Without an adjustment to the Utility’s revenues, it is likely that 
additional projects and maintenance activities will be delayed or deferred, increasing the risk of 
failure within the stormwater system.   
 
A major reason why the Utility’s revenues have not kept pace with expenses is related to a recent 
spike in construction costs.  According to the Colorado Department of Transportation’s 
Construction Cost Index (CCI), from 2020 to 2025, construction costs in Colorado increased 
54.2%.  During those same years, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) for the Denver metro area, 
which is a measure of the costs of goods and services, increased 23.1%.  The Town’s Municipal 
Code currently requires the Stormwater Utility Fee to be annually adjusted by the percentage 
change in CPI, but the adjustment does not account for CCI.  This has resulted in the Utility’s 
revenues failing to keep pace with construction costs. 
 
Recommendation 
With the goal of allowing the Utility to be proactive in addressing current and future 
infrastructure needs, Staff has prepared two proposed adjustments to the Stormwater Utility Fee 
for the Town Council’s consideration: 
 
1. A one-time 9.8% increase in the fee to account for the large spike in construction costs 
observed over the last five years. 
The Utility spends approximately half of its annual revenue on construction-related items.  If this 
portion of the Stormwater Utility Fee had been adjusted by CCI during those years, the fee 
would currently be 9.8% higher. 
 
2. Revise the annual adjustment of the fee to use the average of CCI and CPI.  
Including construction costs in the annual adjustment would better reflect the Utility’s 
expenditures on construction-related items and would protect the Utility against any future spikes 
in construction costs. 
  
The Town’s current Stormwater Utility Fee is $9.53 per month for a single-family home. 
Commercial and industrial properties are billed according to the amount of impervious area on 
their property using this same rate.  The two proposed adjustments to the fee would result in a 
2027 monthly fee of $10.64 per month.  This would be an increase of $1.11 per month, or just 
over $13 annually, and would generate around $350,000 of additional revenue for the Utility 
compared to 2026.  This revenue would be used to advance capital improvement projects, 
including major maintenance work.  This new rate would still leave Parker near the median of 
stormwater fees of peer jurisdictions around the metro area. 
Page 3 
 
Staff has prepared a revision to Chapter 4.08 of the Town’s Municipal Code, which governs the 
Stormwater Utility.  The revision proposes the following: 
 
• Adjust the Stormwater Utility Fee to $10.64 monthly, effective January 1, 2027. 
• Revise the annual adjustment of the Stormwater Utility Fee to use an average of the percentage 
changes of CPI and CCI.  
• Allow the Town to recover its actual costs related to the filing of liens for delinquent fees 
(previously limited to $25). With the recent change to recording fees, it now costs $45.25 to 
record the lien and $45.25 to release it, for a total of $90.50. The previous cost was a total of 
$30.50. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPACT 
This revision to Municipal Code will increase the Stormwater Utility Fee to $10.64 per month 
for a single family home, effective January 1, 2027.  This represents a $1.11 per month increase 
from the 2026 fee.  This adjustment would increase the Stormwater Utility's revenue by 
approximately $350,000 from 2026 numbers.  The code revision would also impact future Utility 
revenues by incorporating construction costs into the annual adjustment of the fee, which will 
help the Utility's revenues keep pace with construction costs.

This ordinance proposes to deed Tract D, Newlin Crossing Filing No. 3, to University of

STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
Approve 
  
BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION 
Background 
The Newlin Crossing subdivision is located at the northeast corner of Mainstreet and Chambers 
Road. The Town owns Tract D, Newlin Crossing Filing No. 3, which is a small tract of land 
immediately to the east of Chambers Road. The Town owns Tract D because Tract D, along with 
several additional parcels along Chambers Road, historically accommodated a stormwater pond 
that served Chambers Road. 
 
The ultimate development plan for Newlin Crossing includes the construction of a sub-regional 
stormwater detention facility that will provide water quality and detention for the west half of the 
Newlin Crossing development, along with the areas of Chambers Road served by the historical 
stormwater pond. When this new drainage pond is constructed, the stormwater from Chambers 
Road will be routed to the new facility and the existing pond will no longer be needed. The 
Annexation Agreement for Newlin Crossing sets forth that, once the developer of the property 
has designed the new pond, granted the Town a drainage easement over the new pond area, and 
financially secured the construction of the new pond with the Town, the parcels that currently 
house the existing pond (including Tract D) must be deeded to the developer or the developer’s 
successor-in-interest.  
 
 

Page 2 
 
Analysis/Discussion 
98 W. Parker, LLLP is the developer of the tracts adjacent to Tract D within the Newlin Crossing 
subdivision. More specifically, 98 W. Parker owns Tract G, which is adjacent to Tract D to the 
east.  98 W. Parker intends to sell Tract G to University of Colorado Health, with a planned 
closing date of November 3, 2026. University of Colorado Health intends to develop a medical 
center on Tracts D and G, subject to the Town’s applicable development processes. In relation to 
that sale and in accordance with the requirements of the Newlin Crossing Annexation 
Agreement, 98 W. Parker is requesting that the Town convey Tract D to University of Colorado 
Health, as the successor-in-interest to 98 W. Parker.  
 
The conditions set forth in the Annexation Agreement for the conveyance of Tract D have been 
satisfied. This ordinance proposes to convey Tract D to University of Colorado Health, subject to 
the closing of the sale between 98 W. Parker, LLLP and University of Colorado Health and the 
conveyance of Tract G to University of Colorado Health. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPACT 
The Town already owns Tract D; the conveyance of Tract D to University of Colorado Health 
does not require any additional Town funds; and the conveyance of Tract D is consistent with the 
requirements of the Newlin Crossing Annexation Agreement.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
In April 2026, Town Council approved an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) between the 
Town of Parker, Douglas County, and Parker History to develop a plan for renovating the 
Heritage Center located in The Schoolhouse. The resulting plan was presented to Town Council 
at the September 14 Study Session, where Council provided direction to proceed with the next 
phase of the project that includes the design, fabrication, and installation of the new exhibits. 
 
Before this phase can begin, Town Council must approve an amended IGA that will provide for 
continued consultation from Douglas County Historic Preservation, through consultant Todd 
McMahon, as the project moves into exhibit development. The amended IGA outlines the goals, 
responsibilities, expectations, timeline, and cost associated with this consulting work. 
 
The design, fabrication, and installation of the exhibits will be procured separately through a 
competitive RFP process and will require a separate contract and funding approval. 
  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
Approve 
  
BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION 
For this project, the Town has collaborated with Parker History and Douglas County Historic 
Preservation to reimagine the Heritage Center, develop inclusive interpretive themes, identify 
previously untold stories, and create a framework for new displays and exhibits. 
 
The initial IGA supported the first phase of the project, including consultation from Todd 
McMahon, a Certified Interpretive Planner contracted through Douglas County Historic 
Preservation. This work resulted in a written Interpretive Plan that established interpretive goals, 
target audiences, a theme hierarchy, the desired visitor experience, recommendations for 
interpretive media, and the overall content framework for the space. 
 

Page 2 
The amended IGA will provide for Todd McMahon’s continued consultation as the project 
advances into its second phase, helping ensure that the exhibit development remains consistent 
with the Interpretive Plan. The funding associated with this IGA is specifically for this 
consulting work and does not include the cost of designing, fabricating, or installing the exhibits. 
 
The Town will conduct a separate RFP process to select an exhibit design and fabrication firm. 
The resulting contract, including the costs associated with exhibit design, fabrication, and 
installation, will be brought forward separately.

Heritage Center renovation project. Funding is available in account 205-4545 (Schoolhouse 
Historic Building Improvement).

consultation through Douglas County Historic Preservation, is not to exceed $14,000. 
 
Costs associated with the future design, fabrication, and installation of the exhibits, including any 
work related to the existing diorama, are not included in this amount. Once those costs are 
determined through the RFP process, any additional funding needed will be brought to Town 
Council through a supplemental budget request.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The Colorado Municipal Annexation Act of 1965, C.R.S. § 31-12-105 et. seq., requires that 
municipalities prepare and adopt a Three-Mile Area Plan prior to annexing property into their 
territorial boundaries. The Town of Parker prepares an updated Three-Mile Area Plan (the Plan) 
annually to comply with State law and support ongoing annexation activity. The Plan identifies 
unincorporated land that is located within three (3) miles of town boundaries, depicts the Town's 
planning area for future annexation and incorporates reference information for the provision of 
municipal services. 
  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
Approve 
  
BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION 
Background/Prior Actions 
The Town of Parker actively pursues annexation opportunities within the Parker 2050 
Comprehensive Plan’s designated planning area boundary. The 2026 Three-Mile Area Plan has 
been prepared to comply with the Municipal Annexation Act of 1965 to support annexation 
activity. The primary purpose of the Plan under State statute is to identify areas for potential 
annexation within three (3) miles of existing municipal boundaries, provide for an orderly 
annexation process and require municipalities to consider how they will provide services with 
annexation. 
 
The Annexation Act, at C.R.S. § 31-12-105(1)(e)(I), provides that: "Prior to the completion of 
any annexation within the three-mile area, the municipality shall have in place a plan for that 
area that generally describes the proposed location, character, and extent of streets, subways, 
bridges, waterways, waterfronts, parkways, playgrounds, squares, parks, aviation fields, other 
public ways, grounds, open spaces, public utilities, and terminals for water, light, sanitation, 
transportation, and power to be provided by the municipality and the proposed land uses for the 
area." The Three-Mile Area Plan constitutes the plan required by State statute with cross-
references to other plans adopted by the Town including the Parker 2050 Comprehensive Plan; 
Parks, Recreation and Open Space Master Plan; and Transportation Master Plan.
