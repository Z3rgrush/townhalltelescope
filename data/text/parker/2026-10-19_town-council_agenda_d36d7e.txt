SUBJECT: LDO Requirements for Wildlife Fencing Continued Discussion 
     
  
ITEM OVERVIEW:  
At the September 28, 2026, Town Council Study Session, staff presented a proposal to allow 
wildlife exclusion fencing for plant nurseries to protect inventory from deer. Council expressed 
general support for allowing wildlife exclusion fencing but requested additional information 
regarding fencing regulations in Colorado's Grand Valley and visual examples of potential 
fencing designs. 
 
Staff is returning to Council with the requested information and seeking direction on whether 
certain requirements of the Town's existing fencing design standards should apply to wildlife 
exclusion fencing or whether alternative standards should be considered. 
  
BACKGROUND AND ANALYSIS:  
Background 
The Town's Land Development Ordinance (LDO) does not provide specific standards for 
wildlife exclusion fencing. In order to allow for wildlife exclusion fencing, the LDO would need to 
be amended to define the type of fencing and allow for this type of fencing up to eight feet in 
height within front setbacks. Wildlife exclusion fencing would be limited to plant nurseries. 
 
Analysis/Discussion 
Following the September 28 study session, staff researched fencing regulations in Mesa 
County, Palisade, Grand Junction, Fruita, and Clifton. While these jurisdictions do not have 
specific regulations for wildlife exclusion fencing, this type of fencing is used throughout the 
Grand Valley. Staff also prepared visual renderings demonstrating various fencing designs, 
including fencing with galvanized steel posts, woven-wire fencing with wood posts, and fencing 
incorporating masonry columns. 
 
Staff is seeking direction on whether the following existing townwide fencing design standards 
should continue to apply specifically to wildlife exclusion fencing:

• Changes in fencing materials every 50 feet along arterial and collector roads (typically 
masonry columns). 
• Use of fencing materials that complement existing buildings and/or surrounding fencing, 
in this case probably wood posts or potentially painted posts to match metal wrought 
iron looking fencing. 
 
  
FINANCIAL AND POLICY IMPACTS:  
None. 
 
COUNCIL DISCUSSION:  
Staff is seeking Council direction on whether certain fencing design standards should apply to
