GUIDELINES FOR PUBLIC INPUT 
Public Input is a vital and important portion of every meeting and will be permitted 
throughout the meeting, but according to the following guidelines: 
a. Public input is allowed during the Agenda identified Public Input and Public
Hearing portion of the meeting.
i. If you would like to address the meeting during the appropriate times,
please raise your hand and when called upon you will be asked to come
to the podium.  Announce your name so that your statements can be
adequately captured in the meeting minutes.
ii. Please keep your comments to 3-5 minutes as others may want to
participate throughout the meeting and to insure that the subject does
not drift.
b. Throughout the meeting agenda calls for public input will be made, generally
pertaining to specific action items.  Please follow the same format as above.
c. At the conclusion of the meeting, if the meeting chair believes additional public
comment is necessary, the floor will be open.
We hope that this guideline will improve the effectiveness and order of the Town’s 
Public Meetings.  It is the intent of your publicly elected officials to stay open to your 
feelings on a variety of issues. 
Thank you, Rangely Mayor

COLORADO, DECLARING A STATE OF MUNICIPAL WATER URGENCY; ADOPTING 
A FINDING OF MUNICIPAL WATER SUPPLY NEED TO ENSURE A RESILIENT AND 
RELIABLE MUNICIPAL WATER SYSTEM IN RESPONSE TO DROUGHT, WILDFIRE, 
AND THE LOSS OF EMERGENCY STORAGE; AND AUTHORIZING NEGOTIATION, 
BUT NOT EXECUTION, OF A CONDITIONAL STORAGE AGREEMENT FOR THE 
WHITE RIVER REGIONAL WATER SUPPLY PROJECT – WOLF CREEK RESERVOIR 
RECITALS 
WHEREAS, the Town of Rangely, Colorado (the “Town”), acting by and through its 
Board of Trustees (the “Board”), is responsible for protecting the health, safety, and welfare of its 
residents, including the provision of a reliable and potable municipal water supply; 
WHEREAS, the White River is the Town’s sole source of municipal water supply; the 
Town relies entirely on direct flow surface water rights diverted from the White River at the lower 
end of the basin in Colorado; and the Town cannot divert when those rights are ou t of priority or 
when river flows at its intake are insufficient; 
WHEREAS, on August 12, 2026, flows in the White River fell to approximately 16 cubic 
feet per second at the U.S. Geological Survey gauge White River Below Boise Creek, Near 
Rangely, CO (USGS 09306290); the Town was unable to divert water from the river; the Town 
requested releases from Kenney Reservoir to maintain its supply; and the Town implemented Stage 
3 water restrictions under Emergency Ordinance No. 707 (Series 2026); 
WHEREAS, the Town recognizes that a 0% safety margin between available supply and 
demand is technically and operationally unfeasible for a municipal water supplier; 
WHEREAS, the Town owns no raw water storage reservoir; Kenney Reservoir, formed 
by Taylor Draw Dam, is owned and operated by the Rio Blanco Water Conservancy District (the 
“District”); and when the Town cannot divert from the White River, it depends on releases f rom 
the District to access stored water; 
WHEREAS, based on bathymetric data reported by the District, the storage capacity of 
Kenney Reservoir declined from approximately 13,800 acre -feet in 1984 to approximately 6,578 
acre-feet in 2024 as a result of sedimentation, an average loss of approximately 181 acre-feet per 
year, and the District has reported that at that rate the entire storage volume of the reservoir, 
including the approximately 1,735 acre-feet of municipal storage identified for the Town when the 
reservoir was developed, will be eliminated by 2060; 
WHEREAS, the 2025 Lee and Elk Fires burned more than 148,000 acres within the White 
River watershed upstream of the Town’s diversion, resulting in hydrophobic soils, increased 
erosion, debris flows, and ash-laden runoff;

WHEREAS, the Board finds that these post -fire conditions are expected to accelerate 
sedimentation in Kenney Reservoir beyond its historical rate and to degrade raw water quality at 
the Town’s intake for an extended period, during which river water of acceptable q uality may be 
unavailable to the Town even when flows are sufficient; 
WHEREAS, the Board further finds that the continuing loss of capacity at Kenney 
Reservoir diminishes available drought carry -over storage, limits operational flexibility, and 
increases the Town’s vulnerability to supply interruptions and water quality degradation, and that 
the loss of the reservoir to sedimentation will leave the Town without any emergency storage; 
WHEREAS, following administrative calls on the White River in 2022 and 2023, the 
Colorado State Engineer designated the White River basin above the Taylor Draw Power Conduit 
as over-appropriated, effective May 1, 2025, confirming that at times the river does not supply 
enough water to satisfy existing water rights; 
WHEREAS, the Town’s ability to divert depends both on the priority of its water rights 
and on physical flow at its intake, and during severe drought the Town’s surface diversions may 
be curtailed or rendered physically unavailable, as occurred in August 2026, leav ing the 
community dependent on stored water; 
WHEREAS, the Town maintains an active water conservation program and the four-stage 
drought response framework of Emergency Ordinance No. 707 (Series 2026), and the Board finds 
that conservation and demand management, while essential, cannot replace stored water when the 
Town’s surface supply is unavailable; 
WHEREAS, Colorado law permits a governmental water supplier to plan for and secure 
the water reasonably necessary to meet the reasonably anticipated needs of its community over a 
reasonable planning period, based on substantiated projections of future demand. C.R.S. § 37-92-
103(3)(a); Pagosa Area Water & Sanitation Dist. v. Trout Unlimited, 170 P.3d 307 (Colo. 2007); 
WHEREAS, the Town’s municipal water demands over a fifty-year planning period, from 
2025 through 2075, have been projected in Chapter 3 of the draft White River Regional Water 
Supply Project Purpose and Need Report prepared for the District  (June 2026) (the “Demand 
Projections”), using the Town’s water production, billing, and diversion records, population 
projections, and documented system loss rates, as summarized in Exhibit A; 
WHEREAS, from 2020 through 2024 the Town diverted between approximately 1,031 
and 1,361 acre-feet per year from the White River, averaging approximately 1,132 acre -feet per 
year, to serve approximately 2,619 people as well as the potable and raw water irrigation needs of 
Colorado Northwestern Community College, the hospital, schools, parks, the cemetery, and the 
golf course; 
WHEREAS, the Demand Projections estimate the Town’s total water demand at its points 
of diversion at approximately 1,178 acre-feet in 2025 and approximately 1,659 acre-feet in 2075, 
an increase of approximately 481 acre -feet per year, reflecting residential growt h of 0.7 percent 
per year, planned on-campus housing expansion at Colorado Northwestern Community College, 
21

planned expansion of the Town’s raw water irrigation system, documented system losses, and a 10 
percent safety factor applied to potable demand; 
WHEREAS, the Board adopts approximately 1,659 acre-feet per year as the Town’s firm 
annual demand for planning purposes (the “Firm Annual Demand”), and one year of storage is the 
volume of stored water required to meet the Firm Annual Demand for twelve months without any 
diversion from the White River; 
WHEREAS, the Board finds that five years of carry -over storage is the appropriate 
planning standard for the Town because: (1) the Town was unable to divert from the White River 
in August 2026; (2) storage filled under a junior decree in an over -appropriated basin  may be 
unable to refill for several consecutive years, so stored water must carry the Town through multiple 
years without replenishment; (3) post -fire degradation of raw water quality is expected to persist 
for multiple years; and (4) the loss of Kenney R eservoir will leave the Town with no other 
emergency storage; 
WHEREAS, five years of storage at the Firm Annual Demand totals approximately 8,295 
acre-feet (1,659 acre-feet per year multiplied by five years) (the “Storage Need”), and because the 
Town owns no raw water storage, the entire Storage Need is a need for new storage; 
WHEREAS, the Board further finds that additional stored water is necessary to: (1) 
provide multi -year drought protection and carry -over storage; (2) maintain water availability 
during periods of degraded source water quality; (3) support emergency response needs, including 
wildfire suppression and public safety; and (4) ensure continuity of essential municipal services; 
WHEREAS, the Board recognizes that off -channel storage facilities provide improved 
operational flexibility and water quality protection by allowing selective diversion and storage of 
higher-quality water supplies and by holding stored water out of the path of post-fire sediment and 
ash-laden runoff; 
WHEREAS, the District is pursuing the White River Regional Water Supply Project – 
Wolf Creek Reservoir (the “Project”), a proposed pumped, off-channel reservoir; 
WHEREAS, the Project would rely on a junior decree subject to curtailment in an over -
appropriated basin and could divert only during high -flow periods when senior decrees are 
satisfied, so that any municipal storage allocation in the Project must include a multi -year carry-
over pool to sustain the Town when the reservoir cannot be physically or legally replenished; 
WHEREAS, the Project is currently in the planning, permitting, and design phase, and a 
conditional (“if -and-when”) storage arrangement would allow the Town to reserve long -term 
capacity without incurring financial obligations before the Project is permitted, fina nced, 
constructed, and operational; 
WHEREAS, Senate Bill 24 -174 requires municipalities with a master plan to include a 
water supply element in that plan no later than December 31, 2026, pursuant to C.R.S. § 31 -23-
206; and

WHEREAS, the Board intends this Resolution to serve as a foundational component of 
the Town’s Water Supply Element and long-term water planning framework. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE 
TOWN OF RANGELY, COLORADO, AS FOLLOWS: 
Section 1. Adoption of Municipal Water Supply Need. 
(a) The Board hereby finds and declares that the Town requires a firm annual municipal 
water supply of not less than 1,659 acre-feet, being the Firm Annual Demand, sustained through a 
prolonged drought during which the Town’s surface diversions may be curt ailed under state 
administration or rendered physically unavailable and storage cannot be replenished, and that 
meeting this requirement necessitates multi-year carry-over storage of approximately 8,295 acre-
feet, being the Storage Need and equal to five years of the Firm Annual Demand. 
(b) This finding is based upon the Demand Projections, the Town’s historical diversion 
records, reasonably anticipated growth over a fifty -year planning period, drought and emergency 
reserve requirements, fire-suppression redundancy, and the loss of Kenney  Reservoir as a source 
of emergency storage. The derivation of the Firm Annual Demand and the Storage Need is set forth 
in Exhibit A, attached hereto and incorporated herein by reference. 
(c) The Board finds this need to be non-speculative and based upon the Town’s reasonably 
anticipated future requirements as a municipal water supplier planning for the normal and 
foreseeable growth of the community. 
(d) The Board identifies the Project as a highly viable and currently preferred strategy to 
meet the Town’s municipal water urgency and Storage Need. However, because the Project 
remains in the permitting and design phase, the Town must maintain planning flexibility to protect 
its residents and ensure supply resiliency. Therefore, the Town expressly reserves the right to 
satisfy all or any portion of the identified need through the Project, other storage facilities, 
restoration of storage capacity at Kenney Res ervoir in cooperation with the District, water reuse, 
demand management and conservation, acquisition of additional water rights, regional 
interconnection, or any combination of the foregoing. 
(e) The Board reserves the right to revise the Firm Annual Demand and the Storage Need 
by resolution following independent review of the Demand Projections by the Town Engineer, and 
any such revision shall correspondingly adjust the maximum quantity authorized under Section 4. 
Section 2. Declaration of Municipal Water Urgency. 
The Board determines that drought, recent wildfire impacts, declining reservoir capacity at 
Kenney Reservoir, and basin -wide over-appropriation constitute conditions of municipal water 
urgency requiring immediate planning and action to secure additional reliable water storage. 
Section 3. Water Quality and Supply Resiliency Strategy. 
The Board finds that off -channel storage is well suited to the conditions recited in this 
Resolution because it allows selective diversion of higher-quality water and protects stored water

from post-fire sediment and ash-laden runoff. The Board identifies participation in the Project as 
a potential component of a diversified water supply portfolio that may improve water quality 
reliability and operational flexibility. 
Section 4. Authorization to Negotiate Storage. 
(a) Authorization. The Town Manager is authorized to enter into discussions and to 
negotiate, but not to execute, any agreement, letter of intent, memorandum of understanding, term 
sheet, cost-sharing agreement, participation agreement, or other document relating to the Pro ject, 
whether binding or non -binding, including a conditional (“if -and-when”) water storage and 
reservation agreement with the District and other Project participants for up to 8,295 acre -feet of 
storage capacity in the Project. The quantity stated is a ma ximum. The Town commits to no 
minimum quantity and to no minimum level of financial participation. Any such document shall 
be presented to the Board for review and approval prior to execution. 
(b) Contingency. Any such agreement shall provide that the Town’s financial obligations 
and the District’s delivery obligations are contingent upon successful permitting, financing, 
construction, and operational filling of the reservoir, and shall be subject to final revi ew and 
approval by the Board. 
(c) Non-appropriation. Any such agreement shall include non -appropriation provisions 
providing that all obligations of the Town are subject to annual appropriation by the Board in its 
sole discretion, and that a failure to appropriate shall not constitute a default or event of breach, 
nor give rise to any penalty, acceleration, damages, or forfeiture. 
(d) Evaluation and reduction milestones.  Any such agreement shall grant the Town the 
right, at each of the following milestones, to review final and projected cost allocations and to 
reduce or terminate its requested storage allocation without penalty, damages, or forfeiture of any 
amounts other than the Town’s share of planning and permitting costs incurred after the effective 
date of the agreement, which share shall be stated in the agreement as a not -to-exceed dollar 
amount and shall not be increased without the prior written approval of the Board: 
(i) issuance of the Record of Decision or final permit decision by the U.S. Army 
Corps of Engineers; 
(ii) completion of thirty percent (30%) design; 
(iii) completion of ninety percent (90%) design; 
(iv) delivery to the Town of the final cost allocation among Project participants; 
and 
(v) not less than ninety (90) days prior to the execution of any binding construction 
financing. 
(e) Pricing. Any such agreement shall state a firm not-to-exceed unit cost per acre-foot of 
allocated storage, or a defined pricing methodology subject to a not -to-exceed ceiling, and shall 
not impose take-or-pay, reservation, capacity, or similar charges upon the Tow n prior to the date 
water is first available for delivery to the Town from the reservoir.

(f) No reallocation or step -up. Any such agreement shall provide that the withdrawal, 
default, or reduction in participation of any other Project participant shall not increase the Town’s 
allocated capacity, cost share, or any other obligation, and that no reallocation of another 
participant’s share to the Town shall be effective without the prior written approval of the Board. 
(g) Cost allocation methodology. The statement of storage need set forth in Section 1 shall 
not, standing alone, constitute the Town’s agreement to  any methodology for allocating capital, 
permitting, mitigation, operation, maintenance, replacement, or sediment -management costs 
among Project participants. Any such methodology shall be subject to separate review and 
approval by the Board. 
(h) Yield assurance. Any such agreement shall specify the firm annual yield that the Town’s 
allocated capacity is expected to deliver under the hydrologic and administrative conditions 
modeled for the Project, and shall include adjustment mechanisms or remedies in the event t he 
allocated capacity does not deliver that yield for reasons other than the Town’s own operations. 
Section 5. Protection of Existing Water Rights. 
The Board directs that any agreements or actions undertaken pursuant to this Resolution 
shall be structured to protect and maximize the beneficial use of the Town’s existing water rights, 
including senior and junior decrees, consistent with applicable law and decree limitations. 
Section 6. Integration into Master Planning. 
The Board directs that the Town’s municipal water supply needs be incorporated into the 
Water Supply Element of the Town’s Master Plan in compliance with Senate Bill 24-174, following 
independent review of the Demand Projections by the Town Engineer. 
Section 7. No Obligation; No Debt; No Third-Party Reliance. 
(a) No debt. Nothing in this Resolution constitutes or creates a multiple -fiscal-year direct 
or indirect debt or other financial obligation of the Town within the meaning of Article X, Section 
20 of the Colorado Constitution, a pledge of the credit, revenues, or taxing power of the Town, or 
a general obligation of the Town. 
(b) No commitment. This Resolution is a statement of the Town’s planning need and an 
authorization to negotiate only. It is not an offer, an acceptance of an offer, a letter of intent, a 
memorandum of understanding, or a commitment to purchase, lease, subscribe to, or pay f or 
storage capacity or any other interest in the Project. No obligation of the Town to pay any amount 
arises unless and until the Board, in its sole discretion, approves a written agreement and 
appropriates funds for that purpose. 
(c) No third-party rights or reliance. This Resolution confers no rights, benefits, or causes 
of action upon any person or entity, including the District and other Project participants, and no 
person may rely upon this Resolution as evidence of the Town’s agreement to participate 
financially in the Project or to accept any particular terms of participation.

(d) Not a co-applicant. The Town is not an applicant or co-applicant for any federal, state, 
or local permit or approval for the Project. The Town assumes no responsibility or liability for the 
Project’s permitting, environmental review, mitigation, or compliance obligations, an d no cost 
thereof, except as the Town may expressly agree in writing following approval by the Board. 
(e) Reservation of rights.  Nothing in this Resolution waives, limits, or subordinates any 
water right, decree, claim, or defense of the Town, or any right of the Town to participate in, protest,

Section 8. Interim and Alternative Measures. 
The Board recognizes that the Project, if permitted and constructed, will not deliver water 
to the Town for a period of years, and that the conditions of municipal water urgency declared 
herein require action in the interim. The Town Manager is directed to identify, evaluate, and report 
to the Board upon near -term measures to protect municipal supply and water quality, including 
sediment management and capacity restoration alternatives at Kenney Reservoir in cooperation 
with the District , raw water treatment improvements responsive to post -fire turbidity and ash 
loading, demand management and conservation measures, alternative and supplemental supply 
sources, and regional interconnection opportunities. The findings of this evaluation shal l be 
incorporated into the Town’s Water Supply Element. 
Section 9. Effective Date. 
This Resolution shall take effect immediately upon adoption.
