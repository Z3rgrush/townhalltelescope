GUIDELINES FOR PUBLIC INPUT 
Public Input is a vital and important portion of every meeting and will be permitted 
throughout the meeting, but according to the following guidelines: 
a. Public input is allowed during the Agenda identified Public Input and Public
Hearing portion of the meeting.
i. If you would like to address the meeting during the appropriate times,
please raise your hand and when called upon you will be asked to come
to the podium.  Announce your name so that your statements can be
adequately captured in the meeting minutes.
ii. Please keep your comments to 3-5 minutes as others may want to
participate throughout the meeting and to insure that the subject does
not drift.
b. Throughout the meeting agenda calls for public input will be made, generally
pertaining to specific action items.  Please follow the same format as above.
c. At the conclusion of the meeting, if the meeting chair believes additional public
comment is necessary, the floor will be open.
We hope that this guideline will improve the effectiveness and order of the Town’s 
Public Meetings.  It is the intent of your publicly elected officials to stay open to your 
feelings on a variety of issues. 
Thank you, Rangely Mayor

Water Treatment Plant:   
• Flow in the river has been over 300 CFS. Restrictions are extremely unlikely for the remainder of the year.

• Theresa Weidmann is still working on an analysis for whether it would be better to replace the Middle Zone 
Tank or repair it. SGM reviewed everything and determined that given how the tank would serve as the backup 
to low zone and/or high zone, the tank is better to stay as a 1 million Gallon tank. 
• We had no meetings with CDPHE regarding post-fire conditions in October. The meetings will continue to be on 
an as-needed basis. 
• Clarifiers will be drained and cleaned again sometime in late November or Early December. 
• Both water softeners, for the fluoride and MicroClor, have been re-bedded with new media. We were quoted 
nearly $4k to have someone else to it but were able to do it for under $500. 
• The majority of the MicroClor has been rebuilt, but we will need to wait until mid-November to install. 
• We have been working with DOLA and the County to secure more funding for the Middle Zone Tank Project. 
Wastewater Plant:  
• The September DMRs were completed and submitted with no violations.  
• October compliance samples were collected and analyzed with no permit exceedances. 
• While Jetting on Crest, the root cutter became lodged in the line and required the collection line to be dug up 
and cut to get it free. The collection line was patched the same day with no service interruptions. 
• We are continuing routine maintenance jetting throughout the collection system to meet the annual goal.

RE: Request for Local Historic Designation of 826 E. Main Street  
Dear Members of the Rangely Historic Preservation Board: 
On behalf of the Town of Rangely, I respectfully submit this letter requesting consideration of the 
Town-owned building located at 826 E. Main Street for designation as a local historic property. 
The property represents an important part of Rangely’s history and provides a tangible connection to 
the people, events, development, and character that helped shape our community. Historical 
photographs dating to approximately 1912 document the building during its early years. One 
photograph shows the building prominently displaying a sign reading, “The Colthorp Stores Co. 
General Merchandise.” A touring Model T, believed to be a 1911–1913 model, is parked in front of the 
building, while a horse and buggy is partially visible in the photograph. These details help establish 
the building’s presence and use within the community more than 110 years ago and provide a visual 
record of Rangely during an important period in its early development. 
As the current owner of the property, the Town of Rangely recognizes the importance of preserving 
the building and the history it represents. As Rangely continues to grow and evolve, preserving 
significant historic resources allows us to maintain a connection to our past while ensuring that these 
stories remain part of our community for future generations. Historic designation of this property would 
formally recognize its importance and support the Town’s ongoing efforts to identify, document, and 
preserve resources that contribute to Rangely’s unique history. 
The establishment of Rangely’s Certified Local Government program and Historic Preservation Board 
provides our community with an important opportunity to take a more active role in recognizing and 
protecting our local heritage. Designating the building would represent a meaningful step in that effort 
and would demonstrate the Town’s commitment to preserving significant pieces of Rangely’s history. 
As the property owner, the Town of Rangely respectfully requests that the Rangely Historic 
Preservation Board review the “Colthorp” Building in accordance with the Town’s historic preservation 
ordinance and applicable designation criteria and consider recommending its designation as a local 
historic property. 
Thank you for your commitment to preserving Rangely’s history and for your consideration of this 
request. 
Sincerely, 
 
Jonah Bollinger
