GUIDELINES FOR PUBLIC INPUT 
Public Input is a vital and important portion of every meeting and will be permitted 
throughout the meeting, but according to the following guidelines: 
a. Public input is allowed during the Agenda identified Public Input and Public
Hearing portion of the meeting.
i. If you would like to address the meeting during the appropriate times,
please raise your hand and when called upon you will be asked to come
to the podium.  Announce your name so that your statements can be
adequately captured in the meeting minutes.
ii. Please keep your comments to 3-5 minutes as others may want to
participate throughout the meeting and to insure that the subject does
not drift.
b. Throughout the meeting agenda calls for public input will be made, generally
pertaining to specific action items.  Please follow the same format as above.
c. At the conclusion of the meeting, if the meeting chair believes additional public
comment is necessary, the floor will be open.
We hope that this guideline will improve the effectiveness and order of the Town’s 
Public Meetings.  It is the intent of your publicly elected officials to stay open to your 
feelings on a variety of issues. 
Thank you, Rangely Mayor

Project status/Current Issues:  
Water Treatment Plant:   
• Water flows dipped as low as 63 CFS in July, but towards the end of the month we were over 100 CFS, which was 
higher than what it was at that time last year. As of this morning, we dropped all the way down to 42 CFS, 
forcing us to enter stage 2 restrictions. We will try to avoid stage 3 if we can, as there is supposed to be a lot of 
precipitation upriver in the second week of August. 
• Average daily water output from the plant in July was 714k gallons 
• Middle Zone tank #2 was disinfected, cleaned, and put into service so the primary tank could be taken down 
inspected and so work could begin on recoating. We will be holding off on any further progression of the tank 
project until council approves it. 
• We had no meetings with CDPHE regarding post-fire conditions in July. The meetings will continue to be on an 
as-needed basis. 
• Microclor has stopped rebuilding cells, so we will need to budget for at least two new cells next year instead of 
having them rebuilt like we anticipated. Regardless, it has been running well. 
• Granite replaced the communication equipment for bulk water, but we haven’t been able to get 
communications through from town hall. We are opting to purchase a network card for the kiosk and get 
internet to the building. 
• Our distributor can no longer get parts for our existing chemical pumps, and because the pumps are over 10 
years old at this point, we are just going to replace them with new peristaltic pumps. 
Wastewater Plant:  
• The June DMR was completed and submitted.  
• July Compliance samples were collected and sent to SGS Wheatridge. These samples were received and run 
within hold time with no exceedances to permit limitations.

Nuvonics no longer produces or sells the control board needed to replace this. While we can use the other parts

RE:  Authorization — Stage 2 Energy Campus Development & Municipal Participation Study 
 
Recommendation 
Approve the Stage 2 study engagement with Pure-Green Colorado, Inc. in an amount not to exceed 
$59,850, contingent on execution of a Town-prepared addendum addressing the terms in Section 4, and 
authorize retention of an Independent Registered Municipal Advisor to review the resulting work 
product. 
1.  Background 
In May 2026 the Town engaged Pure-Green Colorado to evaluate the geothermal resource beneath the 
Town-owned parcel on State Highway 139. That work was delivered in July. It identified a geologically 
promising site along with several open questions that only site-specific data can resolve. 
Stage 2 answers the business questions the first study did not: what could be built at the site in the near 
term, what it would earn, how the Town could hold an economic interest, and how any buildout would 
be financed without pledging Town taxes or Town credit. It also includes an independent assessment of 
Rangely's existing fiber connectivity. 
2.  What the Town Receives 
Five deliverables at a fixed price, within 60 -90 days, including at least one on-site visit and periodic 
check-ins with staff. Travel is included in the price. 
The Town owns all reports, models, and analysis upon final payment, and may share them with Rio 
Blanco County, state and federal agencies, grant programs, and its own attorney, bond counsel, and 
advisors. 
3.  Risk Disclosure 
Council should weigh the following before voting. 
• This is not independent analysis. Pure-Green Colorado is a project developer with a financial 
interest in the project the study evaluates. The firm discloses this plainly in its proposal. The work is 
useful, but it is a developer's case for its own project and should be read as such. That is the reason 
for the independent advisor recommended below. 
• All financial figures will be planning-level estimates. Projected revenue, bond sizing, and insurance 
costs are assumptions, not commitments. They require verification by the Town's own advisors 
before they can support any decision. 
• The geothermal resource is not confirmed. The first study established that the site is a credible 
candidate. It did not establish the depth at which target temperature is reached, nor the pressure 
31
Town of Rangely, Colorado  |  Office of the Town Manager  |  Page 2 
and permeability of the deep formations. Those remain the largest open variables and are 
acknowledged as such in the Town's own Stage 1 report. 
• Approval commits the Town to nothing further. No bonds, no land, no obligation to approve 
anything at a later stage. If Council declines to proceed after reviewing the work, the engagement 
ends and the Town owes nothing beyond the study fee. 
• Future financing, if any, would be non-recourse to the Town's general fund. No mill levy would be 
pledged, and no claim would attach to Town taxes. Council should nonetheless understand that a 
Town-issued bond that failed would still carry Rangely's name, and could affect the Town's ability to 
borrow for water, sewer, and street projects in the future. Staff is placing that risk on the record 
now rather than at a later stage. 
4.  Required Addendum 
Staff will not execute the engagement without an addendum covering, at minimum: 
• Non-appropriation 
• A defined Colorado Open Records Act notice process for third-party confidential material 
• Final payment conditioned on Town acceptance 
• Confirmation that the engagement grants no exclusivity or right of first refusal at any future stage 
5.  Fiscal Impact 
Study fee not to exceed $59,850, payable 30% at execution, 40% at milestone, and 30% upon 
acceptance. Proposed funding source: General Fund 
Separately, staff recommends retaining independent professional advisors — a registered municipal 
advisor and, as needed, bond counsel — to review the Stage 2 work product. Cost is not yet known and 
will depend on scope and the advisors selected. Staff will engage within existing purchasing authority 
and available appropriation, and will return to Council for approval if the cost exceeds that authority. 
This is standard practice for any municipality evaluating a bond-financed project, and was recommended 
by the Town's own Stage 1 evaluation. 
6.  Recommended Motion 
Move to authorize the Town Manager to execute the Stage 2 Energy Campus Development and 
Municipal Participation Study with Pure-Green Colorado, Inc., in an amount not to exceed $59,850, 
contingent on execution of a Town-prepared addendum consistent with the terms described in the 
accompanying memorandum; and and to authorize the Town Manager to retain independent 
professional advisors, which may include a registered municipal advisor and bond counsel, to 
review the resulting work product, within the Town Manager's existing purchasing authority and 
subject to available appropriation, returning to Council for approval should the cost exceed that 
authority.
