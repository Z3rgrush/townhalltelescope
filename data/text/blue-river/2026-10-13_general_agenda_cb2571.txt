local governments, complaints by residents of the Town, observation by administrative staff, and

WHEREAS, a 2021 study 2 of the local impacts  of short-term rentals in the State of Washington 
for two towns within resort areas concluded that the benefits of STRs was undeniably outweighed 
by the adverse impacts to a community.  Although benefits were largely economic in terms of 
support and expenditures of tourism dollars  to the town’s businesses , these benefits are largely 
absent in Blue River, which has no in -Town retail business es, restaurants, or shopping 
opportunities that can be supported by short-term rentals.3  The study reports that “[t]he negative 
impacts are substantial and often outweigh the positives, especially in small communities. ”  In 
short, the study concluded: 
 
o STRs, especially absentee- owner whole- house rentals, reduce the number of full -time 
residents, leading to a decline in social capital ( including neighbor relationships, 
volunteerism, and community engagement). 
o Communities with high STR density experience more transient populations, less 
familiarity among neighbors, and a weakened social safety net. 
o Where short -term rentals exceed 4% of the housing in a community, negative impacts 
become “significant.” 
o Rapid growth in STRs has led to overtourism, overwhelming local infrastructure (roads, 
parking, recreational resources) and degrading both resident quality of life and tourist 
experience. 
o While STRs bring in tourist spending, the loss of resident spending, increased housing 
costs, and loss of volunteer contributions result in a net negative economic impact. 
o The Study recommended that communities consider capping STRs at a low percentage of 
housing stock, prioritizing full -time residency, and enforcing regulations on absentee -
owner rentals.

Sec. 6-1-100. Purpose and Notice to Owners. 
 
Short-term rental of a residential dwelling unit is a business or commercial activity or operates in 
a manner that is substantially identical or substantially similar to a for-profit commercial business.  
Short-term rental of a residential dwelling unit is antithetical to the residential purposes of the 
Town’s Residential Zone Districts  due to the potential for adverse impacts upon the quiet 
enjoyment of neighboring residential properties.  Short- term rental can be an acceptable activity  
in the Town’s Residential Zone Districts, but only subject to licensing and the O w n e r ’s compliance 
with this Article.   
 
The purpose of this Article is to recognize a limited privilege for Owner s of lawfully existing 
residential dwelling units within the Town’s Residential Zone Districts to offer to the public all or 
a portion of the O w n e r ’s residential dwelling unit for short-term rental.   
 
As a privilege, the issuance of a license to engage in the activity  of the short -term rental of 
residential property shall not establish a right to such activity or a right to continue the activity 
beyond the term of any license.   
 
Owners should not rely on the potential that a license will be issued, or that a license will be 
renewed.  Purchasers of residential property should not expect that a license will be issued because 
the former Owner  held a license.   A prior Owner’s  license has no bearing on a new Owner’s  
application for or approval of a new license.   
 
The Town may alter, modify, suspend, limit, or revoke the availability of a short -term rental 
licensing program at any time to protect the health, safety, or welfare of the Town.  Licensed 
Owners accepting reservations for any period following the scheduled expiration of a license 
assume the risk that a license will not be renewed and the Owner  will be required to cancel 
reservations to comply with the law.

MEMORANDUM 
TO: Mayor and Board of Trustees, Town of Blue River 
FROM: Town Manager Chad Hull 
DATE: October 13, 2026 
SUBJECT: Code Compliance Administrator Reclassiﬁcation, Work Plan, and 
Compensation 
 
I. CONTEXT 
The Town is considering reclassifying the former Blue River Ambassador position as Code 
Compliance Administrator and adjusting compensation for a more specialized scope of 
work. The former hybrid Administration/Police position combined STR and property 
compliance with lodging-tax assistance, road and signage reporting, Goose Pasture Tarn 
enforcement, and limited grounds maintenance. The proposed position would focus on 
professional code administration, rental inspections, case management, administrative 
enforcement, and administrative hearing and court support. 
The Board is asked to review the revised position responsibilities, compensation analysis, 
and recommended direction in light of the Town’s operational needs, regional labor market, 
and implementation of an expanded short-term rental and code-enforcement framework. 
 
II. PROPOSED POSITIONAL WORK PLAN 
The former Ambassador already received complaints, conducted subdivision reviews, 
issued warnings or tickets, maintained records, reviewed STR advertisements, 
communicated with residents, and attended Municipal Court. The proposed 
reclassiﬁcation would retain that enforcement foundation while removing or reducing 
unrelated road liaison and maintenance duties. 

The Code Compliance Administrator would assume greater technical responsibility for 
code interpretation, rental inspections, evidence gathering, administrative penalties, 
ensuring due process, permit and land-use support, vendor-data reconciliation, and 
complete case ownership. Final duties should reﬂect the Board’s priorities and the 
adopted upcoming short-term rental ordinance. 
 
III. EXPANDED AND NEWLY ASSIGNED RESPONSIBILITIES 
1. Code and STR administration:  
 
Investigate violations; apply the Municipal Code, Land Use Code, STR regulations, 
permit conditions, and penalty schedules; prepare warnings, notices, citations, and 
penalties; monitor licensing, rental inspections, responsible-agent and insurance 
records, online listings, occupancy, parking, and other corrective actions. 
 
2. Inspections and case management:  
 
Conduct ﬁeld inspections, document evidence, research ownership and permit 
history, maintain complete case ﬁles and deadlines, prepare hearing or court 
materials, support administrative appeals, and coordinate Police referrals, Neumo 
data, lodging-tax records, and permit or land-use matters with appropriate staff and 
consultant representatives. 
 
3. Public service and reporting:  
 
Serve as the primary contact for complaints and compliance questions, encourage 
voluntary correction, handle contested matters neutrally, report activity and trends 
to the Town Manager and Board, and help standardize enforcement procedures. 
 
III. COMPARISON TO FORMER JOB DESCRIPTION 
The former Blue River Ambassador was a hybrid Administration/Police position that 
combined compliance work with road reporting, Tarn permit checks, tax assistance, and 
grounds maintenance. The role received complaints, reviewed subdivisions and STR 
advertisements, issued warnings or tickets, maintained follow-up records, and attended 
Municipal Court. 
The Code Compliance Administrator would be a dedicated compliance position with 
responsibility for formal notices and administrative citations, expanded STR licensing and 
inspection oversight, evidence and hearing preparation, data reconciliation, permit 
support, and complete case accountability. The primary change is to emphasize a more 
focused, technical, and accountable administration of the position’s code authority. 
 
IV. COMPENSATION RECOMMENDATION 
Staff recommends establishing the Code Compliance Administrator salary range at 
$72,000 to $87,000, with an initial hiring range of $72,000 to $79,000. Placement within the 
hiring range should be based on directly relevant municipal-code, inspection, licensing, 
investigative, and hearing experience, while the upper portion of the full range should be 
reserved for demonstrated proﬁciency and sustained performance.  
 
IV. COMPENSATION RATIONALE 
Compensation should reﬂect the position’s assigned responsibilities, specialization, and 
accountability. Although the former Ambassador performed meaningful enforcement work, 
the proposed role adds concentrated responsibility for formal code administration, rental 
inspections, penalties, evidence, due process, data reconciliation, permit support, and 
end-to-end case management. Comparable mountain communities also compensate 
dedicated compliance positions based on the level of regulatory judgment, case work, 
conﬂict management skills, and the region’s high labor and living costs. 
A properly classiﬁed position should improve recruitment and retention efforts while 
providing consistent follow-up, stronger inter-departmental and Neumo coordination, 
reliable STR monitoring, and clearer implementation of Board-adopted penalties. 
 
V. RECOMMENDED BOARD DIRECTION 
Staff recommends that the Board: 
1. Approve the reclassiﬁcation of the Code Ambassador position to Code Compliance 
Administrator based on the revised job description and expanded duties; 
2. Authorize placement of the position at the identiﬁed compensation level subject to 
ﬁnal budget and personnel documentation; 
3. Conﬁrm that the adjustment is intended to recognize the increased scope and 
accountability of the position, increased mountain-region living costs, and the 
Town’s goal of moving toward parity with positions within comparable mountain 
communities; and 
4. Direct the Town Manager to review the classiﬁcation and market position 
periodically as the Town’s STR enforcement program, administrative-penalty 
process, inspection workload, and permit-support responsibilities are 
implemented.

The Code Compliance Administrator reports directly to the Town Manager and serves as 
the Town's primary license and violation compliance, inspection, and enforcement official. 
The position administers and enforces the Municipal Code, Land Use Code, Short-Term 
Rental Regulations, and other applicable Town ordinances and regulations. 
The position administers the nuisance abatement and short-term rental licensing and 
compliance programs, documents violations, coordinates corrective actions, maintains 
enforcement records, and supports administrative hearings and Municipal Court 
proceedings. 
The Code Compliance Administrator works closely with residents, property owners, 
contractors, management companies, the Police Department, Deputy Clerk, Neumo, legal 
counsel, and other governmental agencies. The position requires sound judgment, 
professional communication, strong documentation practices, and consistent application 
of Town regulations.

Code Compliance and Enforcement 
• Administer and enforce provisions of the Municipal Code, Land Use Code, Short-
Term Rental Regulations, permit conditions, and other applicable Town regulations.

and regulatory noncompliance. 
• Conduct ﬁeld investigations to determine compliance with Town regulations. 
• Coordinate follow-up on Police Department referrals, including reported code 
violations, incident reports, corrective-action tracking, and subsequent action. 
• Issue warnings, notices of violation, correction notices, administrative citations,

conduct follow-up inspections as needed.

• Coordinate enforcement efforts with Town legal counsel, Municipal Court

initiatives as directed by the Town Manager. 
Short-Term Rental Compliance Administration

• Assess and administer warnings, penalties, suspensions, and other enforcement

• Coordinate public-facing short-term rental compliance communications, including

Land Use Compliance and Permit Administration 
• Monitor approved developments, permits, and land use approvals for compliance 
with Town regulations and conditions of approval imposed by the Planning & Zoning 
Commission, Board of Trustees, and Town staff. 
• Investigate and document violations of permit conditions and land use 
requirements. 
• Assist with administrative reviews of Type B and Type C permit applications and 
other planning-related reviews as assigned. 
 
Case Management and Record Administration 
• Develop, maintain, and administer comprehensive enforcement case ﬁles and 
compliance records. 
• Track inspections, complaints, warnings, citations, penalties, corrective actions, 
compliance deadlines, permit violations, and enforcement outcomes. 
• Maintain evidence and documentation necessary to support enforcement 
proceedings and legal actions. 
• Utilize compliance software, databases, and reporting systems to administer 
enforcement activities and regulatory programs. 
• Prepare periodic reports regarding compliance trends, enforcement actions, and 
violation statistics for the Town Manager and Board of Trustees. 
• Monitor enforcement metrics and recommend process improvements and policy 
modiﬁcations. 
 
Public Education and Customer Service 
• Serve as the Town's primary point of contact for questions, complaints, and 
concerns regarding code compliance, regulatory issues, and licensing matters. 
• Work collaboratively with community members to achieve voluntary compliance 
whenever practical while maintaining consistent enforcement standards. 
• Develop educational materials, notices, guidance documents, and outreach 
resources related to compliance programs. 
Hearings and Legal Support 
• Prepare reports, affidavits, exhibits, evidence packages, and enforcement 
documentation for administrative hearings and Municipal Court proceedings. 
• Coordinate enforcement matters with the Town Attorney, Municipal Prosecutor, 
consultants, and applicable agencies. 
• Testify before Municipal Court, administrative hearing officers, the Board of 
Trustees, the Planning Commission, and other regulatory bodies as required. 
 
Program Development and Special Projects 
• Assist the Town Manager with development, implementation, and administration of 
compliance programs, enforcement procedures, and regulatory initiatives. 
• Participate in review of proposed ordinance amendments and provide 
recommendations regarding enforceability and compliance impacts. 
• Develop policies, procedures, forms, and best practices to improve regulatory 
compliance and enforcement efficiency. 
• Complete monthly Town Park inspections and provide periodic weed and trash 
clean up of Town property as necessary. 
 
Other Duties 
• Performs other related duties as assigned by the Town Manager. 
 
Delegated Authority 
Acting under authority delegated by the Town Manager and applicable Town regulations, 
the Code Compliance Administrator may: 
• Conduct inspections, investigations, re-inspections, and compliance reviews as 
authorized by law. 
 
• Issue warnings, notices of violation, correction orders, administrative citations, and 
penalty assessments. 
 
• Establish compliance deadlines and require corrective actions. 
 
• Recommend permit suspensions, license suspensions, permit revocations, permit 
denials, and other enforcement actions authorized by Town regulations. 
 
• Coordinate directly with Town departments, law enforcement personnel, 
contractors, consultants, and legal counsel regarding compliance matters. 
 
• Represent the Town in Municipal Court proceedings, administrative hearings, and 
enforcement actions. 
 
Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities 
• Ability to read, interpret, explain, and apply municipal codes, ordinances, permit 
conditions, license requirements, written procedures, and regulatory requirements. 
 
• Ability to conduct ﬁeld inspections, document observations, take photographs, 
prepare written reports, maintain case ﬁles, and track follow-up requirements. 
 
• Ability to prepare reports, maintain legal records, organize evidence, and present 
information in administrative hearings, Municipal Court proceedings, or other 
formal settings as needed. 
 
• Strong customer service, conﬂict resolution, de-escalation, and public 
communication skills, including the ability to communicate professionally. 
 
• Ability to handle complaints, conﬂict, and difficult public interactions with sound 
judgment, professionalism, and discretion. 
 
• Basic proﬁciency with computers, email, document preparation, spreadsheets, 
records systems, permitting or compliance software, and related office technology.
