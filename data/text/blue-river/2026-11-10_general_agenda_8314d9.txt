SUBJECT: Draft Ordinance No. 2026-13  
 Short-Term Rentals – Potential Moratorium Exception #2 
 
 
Background: 
 
If amended and adopted, draft Ordinance No. 2026- 13 will provide a second exception for a 
class of landowners that can submit an application for the renewal of a short-term rental (“STR”) 
license notwithstanding the prohibition of applications imposed by the STR Moratorium that was 
adopted on May 19, 2026. 
 
The draft Ordinance No. 2026-13 includes several highlighted options for the Board of Trustees 
(BOT) to consider in fashioning a new exception.  Generally, the particular elements  for the 
options that the BOT can consider for inclusion for an exception include whether the owner: 
 
1. Submitted a substantially complete renewal application (meaning two or less 
submittal requirements were lacking). 
 
2. Submitted any document(s) for the renewal of an application. 
 
3. Paid the renewal application fee. 
 
4. Paid state and/or local taxes. 
 
The underlying short-term rental policy and the proposed content of the ordinance have been 
discussed and debated over the last several months of public meetings, with citizen public input 
at every meeting.   
 
If prior to the November 10 Special Meeting you have any questions, please do not hesitate to 
contact me by either email or telephone.

Section 3. Applications Subject to Determination of Eligibility.  The authorization

license.  Owners applying for a license must qualify for license issuance in accordance with Article

owners who are permitted to submit an application by this Ordinance.  However, no license shall

licenses for a short-term rental business activity.

various sections and provisions are severable.

SUBJECT: Resolution No. 2026-17 
 Matrix of Penalties for Violations of the Short -Term Rental Ordinance (both 2025 and 
2026 STR Ordinances) 
 
 
Background: 
 
If adopted, Resolution No. 2026-17 will adopt a new set of penalties for violations of the 2025 and 2026 
Short-Term Rental ordinances.  The matrix does not create violations; the matrix only imposes a penalty 
if the violation is found in the ordinance.  As a resolution, the matrix can be easily and quickly amended 
in the future to address changes in violation frequency and level of importance. 
 
The Board of Trustees (BOT) should note that the penalties might be considered as substantial in amount 
and result.  However, staff previously discussed with the BOT that substantial penalties are warranted 
due to the significant nightly rental income received by owners in the operation of a short -term rental.  
Imposing penalties that are far below the nightly rental income may not impress upon owners that 
violations impact the community and are matters that need to be seriously addressed by owners and 
prevented in the future.  Lower penalties may be found readily acceptable to owners who may consider 
the lower-level penalties as just a part of the cost of generating business profit.  
 
If prior to the November 10 special regular meeting you have any questions, please do not  hesitate to 
contact me by either email or telephone.
