Work Session 
 
1. Presentation from Summit School District on a proposed Mill Levy Override 
Summit School District Superintendent Tony Byrd and Dillon Valley Elementary School 
teacher Jenna Sherman presented information regarding two proposed education-
funding measures. 
The presenters discussed Proposition NN, a statewide ballot measure described as a 
long-term education-funding mechanism that would allow the State to retain additional 
revenue for specified educational purposes. Proposed uses included maintaining 
reasonable class sizes, recruiting and retaining qualified educators, and supporting 
career and technical education. The presenters stated that the measure would include 
annual financial oversight and could result in a significant increase in Summit School 
District funding over time. 
The presenters also discussed Summit School District Ballot Measure 4A, a local mill 
levy override that would generate up to $5.9 million annually. The proposed funding 
would be directed toward teacher and staff retention, career-connected learning, 
academic outcomes, and student resources. 
Approximately 76 percent of the proposed mill levy revenue would be used to support 
employee compensation. Approximately $400,000 would support career-connected 
learning, including construction, welding, health sciences, internships, apprenticeships, 
dual-credit opportunities, and industry certifications. Approximately $1 million would 
support academic outcomes and student resources, including reading, mathematics, 
language-learning, special education, and gifted and talented services. 
Discussion focused on employee turnover, declining applicant pools, the cost of living in 
Summit County, and the effect of limited staffing and educator experience on student 
outcomes. The presenters stated that Summit School District had hired approximately 
150 teachers during the Superintendent’s four-year tenure and continued to experience 
difficulty attracting and retaining qualified employees. 
The presenters estimated that the mill levy override would cost approximately $0.90 per 
month for each $100,000 of residential property value. They explained that revenue 
generated through the mill levy override would remain local and be used directly by 
Summit School District. 
Proposition NN will appear on the statewide ballot, while Ballot Measure 4A will appear 
on the local ballot. The presenters requested that the Board consider adopting a 
resolution supporting the education-funding effort. No action was taken. 
 
2. Presentation from G&G Services regarding current roadwork progress and capital 
improvements 
 
Kacey Grosskreuz presented an update regarding current road maintenance, drainage 
work, completed capital improvements, remaining 2026 priorities, and future drainage-
planning efforts. 
 
Initial grading of the Town road network was completed during June. Magnesium 
chloride was applied to approximately 90 percent of Town roads, and DuraBlend was 
tested on selected roads as an alternative treatment. Staff will evaluate DuraBlend’s 
performance following the winter season before determining whether its use should be 
expanded. 
 
Approximately 2,600 tons of road base were placed on deficient roadway areas during 
the season. Staff also inspected washout areas and other drainage concerns that may 
warrant inclusion in future capital improvement planning. 
 
Significant drainage improvements were completed in the Royal Drive and Regal Circle 
area. Work included reestablishing roadside ditches, installing driveway culverts, 
reducing ponding, and directing runoff toward an existing outfall along Blue River Road. 
Staff stated that the project should reduce flooding and improve the durability of the 
roadway. The project may also serve as a model for similar drainage improvements 
elsewhere in Town. 
 
Additional work included ditch cleaning along Blue River Road and Spruce Creek Road, 
installation and maintenance of cross culverts at several locations, and identification of 
previously unknown or buried culverts. Delineator posts with reflective markers are being 
installed near Town cross culverts to assist with identification during storms, winter 
conditions, and maintenance activities. 
 
Remaining 2026 work includes flushing culverts, cleaning newly discovered drainage 
infrastructure, completing planned dry wells, closing out current projects, and preparing 
for future work. Staff are also developing a mapped inventory of Town culverts to 
support maintenance planning and prioritization of future capital projects. 
 
The Board expressed appreciation for the work completed during the season. 
Discussion occurred regarding private property improvements that divert water from its 
natural drainage course and create impacts on Town roads or downstream properties. 
Particular concern was raised regarding a reported diversion near Coronet Drive. Staff 
stated that the Town has encountered berms, filled ditches, debris, and other private 
improvements that affect drainage and may require review and coordination among 
Town administration, public works, and legal counsel.

1. Resolution No. 2026-12 
a. Approval of Resolution No. 2026-12 – a Resolution Approving a Policy for the 
Setting of Agendas for Meetings of the Board of Trustees 
 
The Town Manager presented the resolution and explained that it would establish a 
formal policy for preparing agendas for meetings of the Board of Trustees. 
 
The resolution authorizes the Town Manager to prepare and set meeting agendas 
based on Town priorities, pending matters, and existing agenda commitments. 
Outside a public meeting, a trustee may request an agenda item by providing the 
topic and relevant background information to the Town Manager in writing and 
advising the Town Manager that at least three additional trustees support placing the 
matter on an agenda. The Town Manager may then schedule the item on an 
available future agenda based on priority and competing matters. 
 
During a public meeting, a trustee may request that an item be placed on a future 
agenda. If no objection is raised, the Town Manager may schedule the item. If an 
objection is raised, a formal motion and Board vote are required. A majority of the 
quorum present may also add an item to the current agenda when legally 
permissible. The Town Attorney may consult with the Town Manager regarding the 
addition of agenda items involving pending legal matters. 
 
Trustee Stimson moved to adopt Resolution No. 2026-12. Trustee Heckman 
seconded the motion. All ayes. Motion passed.

Public comment focused primarily on the Town's ongoing review of short-term rental regulations 
and enforcement. Several commenters encouraged the Board to prioritize enforcement of 
existing regulations against unlicensed operators and repeat violators before adopting additional 
restrictions. Numerous speakers advocated for stronger penalties, more proactive compliance 
efforts, and improved accountability for renters and property owners responsible for violations. 
Several STR owners expressed concern regarding proposed caps, annual rental-night 
limitations, and potential impacts on property values, lodging-tax revenue, second-home 
ownership costs, and future investment in the community. Commenters encouraged the Board 
to evaluate financial implications before adopting additional restrictions and expressed concern 
that limitations could impact otherwise compliant operators. 
 
Other residents described ongoing neighborhood impacts associated with short-term rentals, 
including parking, trash, noise, guest behavior, and changes to residential character. Multiple 
speakers expressed support for a cap or additional operational limitations, stating that STR 
concentrations have increased significantly in some neighborhoods and have negatively 
affected quality of life for residents. 
 
Commenters presented differing views regarding property values, community character, and the 
appropriate balance between residential use and short-term rental activity. Suggestions 
included creation of a community advisory committee, stronger enforcement mechanisms, 
limiting annual rental nights, minimum stay requirements, and enhanced monitoring of 
unlicensed operations.

Work Session 
 
 
Remaining General Policy Issues  
 
a. Law Enforcement and Community Impacts 
 
Police Chief Close provided comments regarding the public duty doctrine and the Town's 
responsibility to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the community. The Chief 
discussed increasing demands on police resources, the volume of STR-related code 
complaints, and the challenges associated with recurring renter behavior. Discussion 
included operational impacts on law enforcement, Town costs in the use of dispatch 
resources, enforcement costs, and community quality-of-life concerns. 
 
b. Annual Rental Night Limit 
 
Town Manager Hull presented information relating to annual rental-night limits, including 
occupancy data provided through the Town's compliance software vendor. Staff 
indicated that average occupancy rates suggested approximately 150 rental nights 
annually, while a minority of properties were operating well above that level. 
Trustees discussed whether annual rental-night limitations would help distinguish 
occasional rental activity from higher-intensity commercial operations. 
 
A motion establishing a maximum of 150 rental nights annually failed. Trustees Willey 
and Stimson voted in favor. 
 
Following additional discussion, Trustee Slaughter moved to establish a maximum of 
175 rental nights annually. Trustee Stuckey seconded the motion. All ayes – the motion 
passed unanimously. 
 
The Board further directed that the limitation take effect beginning with the 2027 license 
cycle. 
 
c. Minimum Stay Requirement 
 
The Board revisited prior discussion concerning minimum stay requirements. 
Following discussion, the Board reaffirmed support for a three-night minimum stay 
requirement for ordinance drafting purposes. 
 
d. Minimum Annual Rental Activity 
 
The Board discussed retaining the current minimum annual rental activity requirement 
intended to prevent inactive licenses from being retained indefinitely. 
 
Trustees expressed general support for maintaining the existing ten-night annual 
minimum rental requirement while additional program data is collected and evaluated. 
 
Proposed Enforcement Program  
 
a. Administrative Enforcement Program 
 
Town Attorney Widner presented a proposed administrative enforcement framework 
utilizing administrative assessments and appeals rather than relying exclusively on 
municipal court proceedings. 
 
The proposed process would include formal notices of violation, administrative penalties, 
appeal rights, and hearings before either a municipal judge or independent hearing 
officer. Appeals would be conducted through Rule 106 procedures, creating a formal 
record for judicial review if necessary. 
 
Trustees expressed support for utilizing a neutral hearing officer rather than a Board 
subcommittee for most appeals and for the Town Manager to hold the administrative 
discretion of using a hearing officer or the municipal court on a case-by-case basis. 
 
b. Violation and Penalty Matrix 
 
Town Attorney Widner reviewed a proposed violation and penalty matrix categorizing 
offenses by severity and assigning specific monetary penalties and licensing 
consequences. 
 
Discussion included violations related to: 
• Unlicensed operation; 
• Advertising without a license; 
• Operating during suspension or revocation; 
• Failure to pay taxes; 
• Inaccurate reporting; 
• Exceeding annual rental-night limitations; and 
• Repeated lower-level violations. 
 
The Board generally supported the concept of the proposed standardized penalties and 
fee structure and directed additional refinement of the matrix prior to formal 
consideration. 
 
Code Compliance Staffing/Position/Duties 
 
a. Code Compliance Administrator Position 
 
Town Manager Hull presented a draft Code Compliance Administrator job description 
intended to oversee licensing administration, compliance monitoring, code enforcement 
coordination, case management, public education, and STR enforcement activities. 
 
The proposed position would report directly to the Town Manager and serve as the 
Town's primary administrative compliance official for code and STR matters. The Board 
discussed coordination between the proposed position, Police Department, Town 
Manager, and compliance software provider. 
 
Trustees expressed support for moving forward with position as described and 
requested that salary information be presented at a future meeting. 
 
b. Complaint Tracking and Enforcement Coordination 
 
The Board discussed procedures for documenting and tracking STR-related complaints. 
Chief Close reported that the Police Department maintains a tracking system 
documenting calls for service, complaints, warnings, citations, and investigative findings 
which is shared with the administrative team. The Board discussed utilizing that 
information to support future administrative enforcement actions.  
 
Potential Moratorium Exceptions 
 
Town Attorney Widner and Town Manager Hull presented preliminary concepts for 
limited exceptions to the STR moratorium. 
 
The first proposal would apply to properties purchased shortly before adoption of the 
moratorium. Trustees discussed whether applicants should be required to demonstrate 
investments or improvements related to intended STR operation. Following discussion, 
the Board generally supported removing the proposed improvement requirement. 
 
The Board expressed preliminary support for eligibility based upon purchase between 
March 1 and May 19, 2026, with a November 1, 2026 application deadline to submit a 
completed application to Town staff. 
 
The Board also reviewed an exemption concept for new applicants who had 
substantially completed STR license applications before the moratorium. Discussion 
focused on defining substantial completion through submission of an application, 
payment of fees, and completion of all but a limited number of required documents. 
 
The Board expressed preliminary support for renewal in 2027 for new applicants in the 
2026 calendar year who submitted applications between January 1 – May 19 and 
demonstrated substantial compliance, which involved paying the correct application fee 
and missing no less than two items of documentation in the application. These renewals 
were likewise determined to be subject to a November 1, 2026 application deadline 
following the adoption of a resolution affirming this exemption at an upcoming meeting. 
 
Staff were directed to draft a resolution formalizing these exemption criteria for 
consideration at the next Board meeting.

Reading of Ordinance No. 2026-11 
 
1. Reading and Consideration of Ordinance No. 2026-11 An Ordinance of the Board 
of the Trustees of the Town of Blue River, Colorado, Amending the Blue River 
Municipal Code by: 
Conditionally Repealing Currently Effective Article 1 of Chapter 6 Titled Short-
Term Rental Licensing and Operation on April 30, 2027, Except to the Extent 
Currently Effective Article 1 of Chapter 6 Must Remain Effective for the Purpose 
of Enforcing Against Any Violations and Imposing Penalties for any Violations 
which Occurred in 2026 and Occurred During the Period of January 1, 2027, to 
April 30, 2027 
AND 
Conditionally Adopting a New Article 1 of Chapter 6 to be Titled Short-Term 
Rentals to be Effective on December 15, 2026, for the Purpose of Processing 
Short-Term Rental Applications and Issuing Licenses to be Effective on May 1, 
2027 and, Following License Issuance, for the Ongoing Regulation of Short-Term 
Rentals after May 1, 2027. 
 
Town Attorney Widner conducted a detailed review of the proposed ordinance, 
explaining the purpose and operation of each section and discussing how the 
ordinance implements prior policy direction provided by the Board during 
previous STR work sessions. 
 
Discussion began with the general provisions and definitions section, including 
clarification that the operation of a short-term rental is a licensed business activity 
and not a vested property right. The Board reviewed updates to definitions, 
including the creation of "license term" and "license year" to accommodate a two-
year licensing structure and revisions expanding the definition of residential zone 
districts eligible for STR licensing. 
 
The Board reviewed licensing eligibility requirements, including limitations 
restricting licenses to property owners, continuation of the one-license-per-owner 
requirement, restrictions on short-term renting of deed-restricted housing, and 
provisions affecting ownership through corporations, trusts, and other legal 
entities. Discussion also included vacation-club style ownership arrangements 
and whether future regulations may be needed to address such models. 
 
Town Attorney Widner reviewed provisions concerning ownership transfers, 
license transfer prohibitions, and newly proposed exceptions that would allow 
certain ownership changes without voiding an otherwise valid license. Examples 
discussed included divorces, ownership consolidations, trust modifications, and 
ownership changes involving corporations and family trusts. 
 
Discussion continued regarding owner responsibilities and compliance 
obligations. The Board reviewed provisions placing responsibility on property 
owners for the actions of guests, renters, property managers, and designated 
representatives. Trustees discussed requirements for updating application 
information in a timely manner. 
 
The Board reviewed administrative authority granted to the Town Manager, 
including authority to develop forms, administrative procedures, fee schedules, 
enforcement procedures, and related implementation documents necessary to 
administer the licensing program. 
 
Discussion of license terms and renewals focused on the transition to a two-year 
licensing cycle. Existing licenses would automatically extend through April 30, 
2027, and future licenses would operate on a two-year cycle tied to odd-
numbered years. Additional discussion addressed application timelines, renewal 
procedures, compliance reviews, and future administration of license availability 
under the cap system. 
 
The Board reviewed zoning structure, cap calculations, and procedures for 
determining available licenses. Discussion focused on how license percentages 
would be calculated within each STR zone and how the Town will administer 
future waiting lists and the issuance of available licenses. 
 
Significant discussion occurred regarding whether the initial STR cap should 
remain at 20 percent or be reduced to 15 percent during implementation of the 
new program. Discussion centered on evaluating the financial impacts, 
enforcement costs, administrative burden, law enforcement demands, community 
impacts, and overall effectiveness of the revised regulations before considering 
future expansion of the program. There was general agreement that the 2027–
2029 licensing cycle should provide an opportunity to evaluate the revised 
framework before additional policy changes are considered. 
 
The Board reviewed application procedures for both new licenses and renewals. 
Future applications will include owner certifications acknowledging that STR 
licenses are revocable privileges, that future bookings are made at the owner's 
risk, and that owners are responsible for understanding and complying with 
applicable regulations. Discussion also addressed removal of the current 
insurance-submittal requirement and replacement with an owner certification 
regarding insurance coverage. 
 
The Board reviewed proposed appeals procedures for license denials. The 
proposed process would allow administrative review by a randomly selected 
Board subcommittee to evaluate appeals of licensing decisions. 
 
Discussion then shifted to operational requirements for STR properties. The 
Board reviewed provisions requiring compliance with all applicable laws, 
establishing owner responsibility for guests and representatives, maintaining 
current application information, and that an owner must authorize the inspection 
of an STR property when necessary to verify compliance. 
 
Substantial discussion occurred regarding occupancy limits. The draft ordinance 
proposed limiting occupancy to two occupants per bedroom. Trustees discussed 
whether accommodation should be provided for families with children and 
considered several alternatives, including permitting a limited number of 
additional children under a specified age. Discussion included considerations 
related to septic system impacts, neighborhood impacts, enforceability, and 
consistency with common lodging practices. Staff were directed to prepare 
revised language for future review. 
 
The Board reaffirmed prior policy direction limiting STR licenses to 175 rental 
nights per license year while maintaining the existing requirement that a property 
be rented at least 10 nights per year to retain eligibility. The ordinance also 
continued the previously discussed minimum three-night booking requirement. 
 
The Board reviewed revised advertising standards intended to improve 
compliance and reduce misleading representations. Proposed standards require 
disclosures regarding occupancy limits, parking limitations, and license numbers 
while prohibiting advertisements suggesting unauthorized occupancy, excessive 
parking, or access to restricted amenities such as the Goose Pasture Tarn. 
 
Discussion continued regarding gathering limitations, lighting regulations, 
regulation notice postings, safety requirements, and wildfire mitigation measures. 
The Board reviewed proposed limitations on gathering sizes and reaffirmed 
requirements for local responsible agents capable of responding to complaints 
within established response times. 
 
Trustees also discussed wildfire concerns associated with open-flame devices. 
General support was expressed for prohibiting charcoal grills and similar open-
flame cooking devices for STR guests while continuing to allow approved 
propane-fueled appliances and permanent installations meeting applicable 
requirements. 
 
Town Attorney Widner reviewed the ordinance's enforcement provisions. 
Violations, notices, penalty assessments, appeals, suspensions, and revocations 
would be administered through an administrative process overseen by the Town 
Manager or designee. Future administrative procedures, penalty matrices, fee 
schedules, and implementation documents will be brought to the Board 
separately for review and approval. 
 
Town Attorney Widner advised that the revised ordinance would be updated to 
reflect Board comments and returned for public hearing and possible adoption at 
the October 20, 2026 Regular Meeting. 
 
 
Proposed Code Administration Ambassador Posting  
Town Manager Hull presented a memorandum regarding the proposed 
reclassification of the Blue River Ambassador position to a Code Compliance 
Administrator position. The proposed position would serve as the Town's primary 
administrative compliance coordinator and would focus on short-term rental 
administration, code compliance investigations, inspections, case management, 
evidence collection, enforcement support, hearing preparation, and coordination 
among Town staff, law enforcement, compliance software providers, and STR 
operators. 
 
Discussion focused on the Town's growing administrative workload associated 
with STR compliance and code enforcement, as well as the need for improved 
coordination and follow-through on complaints, violations, and enforcement 
actions. Staff explained that the position would provide dedicated administrative 
capacity to track complaints, manage enforcement cases, coordinate with the 
Police Department and compliance software providers, and administer the 
Town's evolving STR program. 
 
Town Manager Hull recommended a salary range of $72,000 to $87,000, with an 
anticipated hiring range of $72,000 to $79,000 depending on qualifications and 
experience. The proposed compensation reflects the expanded regulatory, 
technical, and administrative responsibilities associated with the position 
compared to the previous Ambassador classification. 
 
The Board discussed the importance of ensuring information that is received 
from law enforcement is effectively tracked and addressed through the Town's 
enforcement process. Trustees generally expressed support for the position 
framework and its role in strengthening the Town's administrative enforcement 
capabilities.  
 
No formal action was taken.

 None.

TO: Mayor and Board of Trustees

SUBJECT: Proposed 2027 Budget—Major Items and Policy Decisions 
 
Note: This is the consolidated overview of the budget for expediency. Please refer to the 
Detail Memorandum or the budget sheets for speciﬁc information and details. 
Budget Overview and General Fund 
The proposed 2027 General Fund includes $3,502,039 in revenue, $2,814,301 in 
expenditures, and approximately $687,739 in surplus. Revenue grows 7.8% over 
estimated 2026 results; expenditures grow 15.5%, reducing the surplus by approximately 
$124,937. Major priorities are staffing, STR compliance, and road and drainage investment. 
Revenue: Major sources are property tax, $984,200; sales tax, $1,056,350; lodging tax, 
$450,000; and STR registration, $500,000. STR registration increases from $265,000 
estimated in 2026. The proposed two-year licensing cycle concentrates renewals in 2027; 
comparable renewal receipts should not be assumed for 2028. Building Department 
revenue falls to $124,560 from $188,059. No Tarn boat-fee revenue is proposed. 
Staffing and Beneﬁts: Wages total $733,319, versus $558,940 in the 2026 salary chart. 
The proposal includes a generally stated 4% cost-of-living adjustment, a $82,160 Code 
Compliance Administrator salary, and a fourth police officer at a projected $74,929 salary. 
Beneﬁts rise from $120,000 to $195,000.  
Operations: Public Works totals $950,660, including $401,500 for street maintenance, 
$310,000 for snow removal, and $50,000 for wildﬁre programs. General Administration 
totals $364,780. Technology totals $185,000, which includes the $90,000 Neumo STR 
contract. Community Fund and Initiatives receives $50,000. Town Hall totals $52,000, 
including $18,000 for repairs. 

General Fund reserves: The projected ending balance is $5,826,066: $84,429 for TABOR, 
$3,500,000 committed to future capital outlay, $1,751,020 assigned for contingency, and 
$490,617 unassigned. Approximately 91.6% is restricted, committed, or assigned. The 
capital commitment increases by $500,000 over the 2026 adopted budget. 
Other Funds and Subfunds 
Capital Fund: Beginning balance is $1,568,538. Revenue is $195,209: $100,000 from the 
General Fund and $95,209 in interest. Expenditures are $426,000: $275,000 for road 
infrastructure, $150,000 for land acquisition, and $1,000 for easements. Ending balance is 
$1,337,747. Road construction increases $25,000 over the adopted 2026 budget, 
emphasizing drainage and roadway resilience projects. 
Broadband Subfund. Beginning balance is $193,338. Revenue of $58,942 comprises a 
$50,000 General Fund transfer and $8,942 in interest. With no expenditures, ending 
balance reaches $252,280. Staff recommends continued accumulation to cushion future 
project expenses. Any future project appropriation will require Board action and deﬁned 
scope, costs, funding, and responsibilities. 
American Rescue Plan Fund. Remaining ARPA resources were reported fully spent on 
broadband work in 2026, including the NEO Connect feasibility study. The 2027 proposal 
shows $0 beginning balance, revenue, expenditures, and ending balance; no ongoing 
funding remains. 
Conservation Trust Fund. The schedule displays a $185,127 beginning balance, $15,889 
in revenue, $17,500 in expenditures, and a $183,515 ending balance. Expenditures 
comprise $5,000 for trail easements, $500 for trails legal services, $5,000 for trail surveys, 
$5,000 for signage, and $2,000 for Town Park. 
Board Direction  
Board direction is requested on reserving part of the $500,000 STR registration revenue for 
2028; conﬁrming fee and license assumptions; affirming $150,000 for land acquisition; 
accumulating broadband reserves without project spending; omitting Tarn boat fees; and 
conﬁrming reserve classiﬁcations, including a possible STR assignment. Recurring staffing, 
beneﬁts, and technology costs should be considered against the uneven renewal cycle.

SUBJECT: Proposed 2027 Budget: Financial Overview, Major Adjustments, and Board 
Policy Considerations 
Purpose 
The proposed 2027 Budget continues the Town’s conservative approach to ﬁnancial 
management while incorporating several signiﬁcant policy and operational decisions. 
These include expanded code-compliance capacity, an additional police officer position, 
continued road and drainage investment, accumulation of broadband reserves, 
implementation of a two-year short-term rental licensing cycle, and the full expenditure of 
all American Rescue Plan funds in 2026. 
The proposed General Fund remains balanced. Revenues are projected to increase by 
7.8% over estimated 2026 results, while expenditures are projected to increase by 15.5%. 
The budget therefore anticipates a positive General Fund operating result of approximately 
$687,739, although that surplus is approximately $124,937 lower than the estimated 2026 
year-end surplus. 
Executive Summary 
The proposed 2027 General Fund includes:

The principal ﬁnancial themes are:

2. Expenditure growth is concentrated in personnel salary and beneﬁts, technology,

3. The proposed two-year STR licensing cycle concentrates registration revenue in

4. The Capital Fund includes $426,000 in proposed expenditures, including $275,000 
for road infrastructure and $150,000 for land acquisition. 
5. The Broadband Subfund continues to accumulate resources but includes no project

unassigned.  
 
General Fund Revenue 
Overall revenue trend 
General Fund revenue is proposed at $3,502,039, an increase of $253,272, or 7.8%, over 
estimated 2026 revenue of $3,248,767. The largest revenue categories remain consistent 
with property tax, sales tax, STR registration fees, and lodging tax. 
Taxes and fees 
Combined Taxes and Fees revenue is proposed at $2,623,340, compared with estimated 
2026 revenue of $2,569,038, an increase of $54,302.   
Major components include: 
• General Property Tax: $984,200.01, compared with $930,583 estimated for 2026. 
• Sales Tax: $1,056,350, compared with $1,050,000 estimated for 2026. 
• Lodging Tax: $450,000, unchanged from the 2026 estimate. 
Property tax remains a major stable revenue source, and sales and lodging tax projections 
remain near estimated 2026 levels.  
Short-term rental registration revenue 
The largest proposed revenue adjustment is Lodging Tax Registration revenue, which 
increases from an estimated $265,000 in 2026 to $500,000 in 2027. This represents an 
increase of $235,000, or 88.7%, over the estimated 2026 result and an increase of 
$367,000 over the adopted 2026 budget of $133,000.  
The proposed STR ordinance establishes a two-year licensing term. Renewed licenses 
issued May 1, 2027 would expire April 30, 2029, and renewals would generally be issued in 
odd-numbered years. This structure concentrates renewal applications and related fee 
collections in FY2027.  
The two-year structure also creates an important FY2028 issue. The Town should not 
assume that any fee revenue will recur in FY2028 because regular renewal activity would 
not occur again until the next odd-numbered licensing cycle. Some minor revenue could 
still be generated through administrative charges, appeals, penalties, or other program 
fees. 
Other Revenues 
Other proposed General Fund revenue includes: 
• Building Department revenue: $124,560, compared with $188,059 estimated for 
2026 due to a higher than anticipated fee intake for residential builds and interior 
remodels.  
• Investment interest: $90,000, compared with $80,000 estimated for 2026. 
• Natural gas franchise revenue: $85,000, compared with $90,000 estimated for 2026. 
The reduction in Building Department revenue should be monitored in relation to projected 
building activity. 
Goose Pasture Tarn revenue 
The budget includes no Tarn boat-fee revenue for 2027, compared with $6,680 estimated 
for 2026. This is based on prior Board guidance to remove the revenue source from the 
budget in upcoming years. The revenue from boat fees serve as a partial revenue source for 
restocking of the Tarn, signage and infrastructure maintenance at the Tarn, and onsite 
police patrol and enforcement efforts. 
 
General Fund Expenditures 
Overall expenditure trend 
General Fund expenditures are proposed at $2,814,301, an increase of $378,209, or 15.5%, 
over estimated 2026 expenditures of $2,436,091. Because expenditures are increasing 
faster than revenues, the projected annual surplus decreases from $812,675 in 2026 to 
$687,739 in 2027.  
The major expenditure increases are associated with personnel, the short-term rental 
compliance and enforcement contract with Neumo, Town Hall repairs, street maintenance, 
and the hiring of the proposed Code Compliance Administrator and fourth Police Officer. 
Personnel and Organizational Capacity 
Total wages 
The salary chart identiﬁes total proposed 2027 wages of $733,319, compared with 
$558,940 for 2026 in the salary chart. The 2027 total includes administration staff, elected 
and appointed officials, and the police department.   
The budget includes a generally stated 4% cost-of-living adjustment, consistent with the 
adjustments offered by neighboring municipalities and the general economic outlook.   
Administration & Personnel 
Administration-Personnel expenditures increase from an estimated $592,054 in 2026 to

new staff, their equivalent beneﬁts, and a general increase in the cost of health insurance.  
Police Department and Public Safety 
Public Safety expenditures increase from an estimated $583,315 in 2026 to $598,242 in

vehicle being totaled and subsequently replaced in 2026.  
Administration and Technology 
General Administration expenditures increase from an estimated $249,001 in 2026 to 
$364,780 in 2027. This is an increase of $115,779, or 46.5%. 
Technology 
Technology increases from an estimated $102,000 to $185,000, an increase of $83,000, or

Employee-housing HOA costs are budgeted at $5,300, and employee-housing utilities are 
budgeted at $8,500.

Street maintenance increases by $36,500, or 10%, over the estimated 2026 amount. Snow

Street Maintenance 
During 2026, G&G Services completed road grading, application of magnesium chloride to

ditch cleaning, cross-culvert maintenance, identiﬁcation of previously unknown or buried 
culverts, delineator installation, and development of a mapped culvert inventory.  
The 2027 budget continues signiﬁcant operational support for roadway infrastructure while 
accounting for inﬂationary pressures.  
 
Capital Fund 
The proposed Capital Fund is projected to begin 2027 with a fund balance of $1,568,538. 
During 2027, the fund is anticipated to receive $100,000 from the General Fund and 
$95,209 in interest earnings, resulting in total projected revenue of $195,209.  
Proposed Capital Fund expenditures total $426,000 and include: 
• Road Infrastructure Construction: $275,000 
• Land Acquisition: $150,000 
• Easements: $1,000 
After accounting for projected revenues and expenditures, the Capital Fund is expected to

Road infrastructure 
The $275,000 Road Infrastructure Construction appropriation represents a $145,000 
increase over the estimated 2026 expenditure of $130,000. The primary 2027 capital

Lakecrest Drive, Burntwood Lane, Crown Drive, Spruce Creek Road, Regal Circle, Coronet

sequencing will depend on 2026 completion status, engineering needs, available funding, 
and construction conditions. 
Land acquisition 
The budget includes $150,000 for land acquisition to allow for open space land acquisition 
beneﬁcial to the Town’s well-being and adherence to its mission statement.  
 
Broadband Subfund 
The Broadband Subfund is projected to begin 2027 with a fund balance of $193,338. During 
2027, the fund is expected to receive a $50,000 transfer from the General Fund and $8,942

The proposed 2027 budget does not appropriate any Broadband Subfund expenditures. As 
a result, the full amount of projected revenue is retained within the fund, increasing the 
projected fund balance from $193,338 to $252,280 by year-end 2027.  
No broadband expenditures are appropriated within the Broadband Subfund for 2027. 
Future broadband appropriations could still be authorized through budget amendments or 
future Board action if project scope, funding sources, and priorities are established. Any 
project appropriation should be based on a deﬁned scope, total estimated cost, external 
funding sources, Town ownership or partnership responsibilities, and anticipated operating 
obligations. 
 
American Rescue Plan Fund 
The American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) was enacted by Congress in 2021 in response to the 
economic and public-health impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. Through the State and 
Local Fiscal Recovery Fund program, municipalities received one-time federal funding that 
could be used for eligible governmental purposes, including infrastructure and broadband 
investments. 
For the Town of Blue River, ARPA funding was utilized to conduct a broadband feasibility 
study with NEO Connect.  
The American Rescue Plan Fund is projected to begin 2027 with a balance of $0. The 
proposed 2027 budget includes no beginning balance, revenue, expenditures, or ending

during 2026.

Major Decisions Requested of the Board 
Prior to ﬁnal budget adoption, staff recommends that the Board provide direction on the 
following matters: 
1. STR revenue timing 
Determine whether a portion of the $500,000 in projected 2027 STR registration revenue 
should be reserved or assigned to support the program during FY2028, when regular 
renewal revenue is not expected to recur at the same level. This may be completed by 
reserving a portion of fee revenue towards FY2028 operations, similar to the assigned fund 
balance reserves.  
2. STR fee assumptions 
Affirm the usage of a fee schedule necessary to produce the $500,000 revenue estimate, 
including the estimated number of licenses and the treatment of application, renewal, 
inspection, appeal, and administrative charges. The fee schedule will be created in 
accordance with Board ordinance adoption and will be provided at a later date. 
9. Land acquisition 
Discuss and if amenable, affirm the $150,000 appropriation for land acquisition in 
alignment with stated goals in 2026. 
10. Broadband 
Determine whether the Town should continue accumulating broadband reserves without a 
2027 project appropriation or authorize a deﬁned next phase following review of the NEO 
work. It is the recommendation of staff to continue accumulation in the Broadband 
Subfund rather than restricting the funds in General Fund ledger.  
11. Tarn revenue 
Affirm whether it is the intent of the Board to remove Tarn boat-fee revenue for 2027. 
12. Reserve classiﬁcations 
Conﬁrm the $3.5 million capital commitment, $1.75 million contingency assignment, and 
whether a separate STR program assignment should be established. 
 
Conclusion 
The proposed 2027 Budget maintains a positive General Fund operating result and 
increases the projected year-end fund balance. At the same time, expenditures are growing 
nearly twice as quickly as revenues, and several of the largest revenue and expenditure 
changes are tied to policy choices that will create recurring obligations or uneven multiyear 
revenue patterns. 
The most signiﬁcant issues are the concentration of two years of STR licensing revenue in 
FY2027, the addition of code-compliance and police staffing capacity and corresponding 
increases in employee beneﬁts, the existing contract with Neumo categorized as 
Technology, continuation of major road and drainage investments, and a proposed 
$150,000 land-acquisition appropriation. 
The budget is structurally balanced for 2027, but the Board’s decisions regarding 
personnel, technology, STR revenue stabilization, and capital projects will affect the Town’s 
recurring obligations and ﬁnancial ﬂexibility beyond the upcoming ﬁscal year.

TOWN OF BLUE RIVER, COLORADO 
 
ORDINANCE NO. 2026-10 
 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF 
BLUE RIVER, COLORADO, AUTHORIZING A CLASS OF PROPERTY 
OWNERS TO APPLY FOR A SHORT-TERM RENTAL LICENSE AND  
AMENDING ORDINANCE NO. 2026-03 
 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Blue River, Colorado (“Town”) is a statutory municipality 
incorporated and organized pursuant to Section 31-2-101, et seq., C.R.S.; and 
 
WHEREAS, on May 19, 2026, the Board of Trustees ( “Board”) adopted Ordinance No. 
2026-03 which was adopted as an emergency ordinance to establish a temporary moratorium on 
the acceptance and issuance of licenses for short term rentals; and 
 
WHEREAS, among others, the purpose of the temporary moratorium was to: 
 
• Enable the Board  to reconsider or review the polic ies underlying prior Boards’ 
decisions to allow owners to engage in the business activity of short-term rentals of 
the owner’s residential property in the Town; and  
 
• Assess and address impacts resulting from an ongoing increase over the past several 
years in both the number of licensed short -term rentals and the intensity  of the 
activity of short-term rentals in the Town; and 
 
• Temporarily cease the issuance of short -term rental licenses until the Board : (i) 
considers Town policy and the benefits and detriments of allowing s hort-term 
rentals: (ii) consider limiting the number of licenses  issued by the Town; (iii) 
consider addressing the increasing intensity and impacts of short-term rentals; and 
(iv) consider amending Article 1 of Chapter 6 of the Blue River Municipal Code ; 
and 
 
WHEREAS, property owners who purchased residentially zoned property in the months 
prior to May 19, 2026, and who planned and intended to apply for a short -term rental license 
asserted that the temporary moratorium unreasonably disrupted their ability to apply; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Board recognized the impact of the temporary moratorium on a defined  
and limited class of property owners; and 
 
WHEREAS, a majority of the Board desires to permit a defined and limited class of 
property owners to apply for short-term rental license and, if they qualify for the issuance of a 
license to receive a license, as an exception to the restrictions imposed by Ordinance No. 2026-03.

BE IT ORDAINED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF BLUE RIVER, 
COLORADO: 
 
Section 1. Class of Owners Authorized to Apply.  The following class of owners of 
real property in the Town of Blue River may submit an application  for short-term rental license  
notwithstanding the temporary suspension and moratorium which prohibits the submittal of 
applications imposed by Ordinance No. 2026-03: 
 
An owner who closed on the purchase of a single family dwelling unit located in a 
Residential Zone District1 between March 1, 2026, and May 19, 2026. 
 
Section 2. Deadline for Application Submission – 5:00 PM, December 4, 2026.  
Owners authorized by this Ordinance to apply for a short-term rental license must submit  to the 
Town a fully complete application for a new license  no later than 5:00 PM , Friday, December 4, 
2026.  Applications submitted or remaining incomplete after this deadline for submission shall not 
be accepted, considered, or processed. 
 
Section 3. Process for Application Submission.  Owners who are within the class of 
owners authorized by this Ordinance and who desire to submit a new short-term rental application 
are strongly encouraged to contact the Town Manager to understand the application process and 
requirements.  See www.townofblueriver.gov for contact information. 
 
The Town does not know the identity of owners who may be authorized to submit an 
application pursuant to this Ordinance.  It is not the Town’s responsibility to determine the identity 
of such owners and to notify the owners of the opportunity to apply and the process for application.  
The Town may, as a courtesy and as time and resources permit, provide such notice if such owners 
become known to the Town.   
 
Section 4. Applications Subject to Determination of Eligibility.  The authorization 
to submit an application in accordance with this Ordinance shall not constitute approval of a 
license.  Owners applying for a license must qualify for license issuance in accordance with Article 
1 of Chapter 6 of the Municipal Code.  Owners subject to enforcement action for violation(s) that 
would result in either (i) revocation or suspension of a license ; or (ii) disqualify the Owner from 
issuance of a license (e.g., having advertised, rented, or operated a short -term rental without a 
license during 2026) shall not be issued a license or may be issued a conditional license  pending 
the outcome of the enforcement action. 
 
 
1   “Residential Zone District” means a district in which a single -family dwelling unit is a principal permitted use.  
Such districts include the Residential Zone District (R -1), the Planned Residential Zone District (PRD), and the 
Transitional Zone District (TD).

Section 5. Manager Authorized to Accept Applications .  As of the date of the 
Board’s adoption of this Ordinance, the Town Manager is authorized to accept applications from 
owners who are permitted by this ordinance to submit an application.  However, the Town’s formal 
review and processing of a complete application and the issuance of a license (if appropriate) shall 
not be conducted or made until this Ordinance is effective.  If adopted, this Ordinance will become 
effective thirty (30) days after the Board’s approval of the ordinance and the publication of the title 
of the ordinance in the local newspaper.  
 
Section 6. Term of Issued License.  A license issued to an owner authorized to apply 
in accordance with this Ordinance shall expire on April 30, 2027.  Provided that the issued license

applying for renewal in accordance with Ordinance No. 2026-11.  If a renewal is granted, the term 
of such renewed license shall extend from May 1, 2027, to April 30, 2029, provided that the license 
is not revoked or the short-term rental program is terminated by the Town.

Ordinance No. 2026-03 by establishing a limited exception to the moratorium imposed by 
Ordinance No. 2026-03 which ordinance prohibited the acceptance of applications and the 
issuance of licenses for a short-term rental business activity.

Ordinance be judicially determined invalid or unenforceable, such judgment shall not affect, 
impair, or invalidate the remaining provisions of this Ordinance, the intention being that the 
various sections and provisions are severable. 
 
Section 9. Minor Revision or Correction Authorized .  The Town Manager, in 
consultation with the Town Attorney, is authorized to make minor revisions or corrections to the 
provisions of this Ordinance provided that such revisions or corrections are grammatical, 
typographical, or non- substantive and do not alter or change the meaning and intent of this 
Ordinance. 
 
Section 10. Effective Date.  The provisions of this Ordinance shall become effective 
thirty (30) days after publication following the final passage.

General Background 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Blue River, Colorado (“Town” or “Blue River”) is a statutory 
municipality incorporated and organized pursuant to the provisions of Section 31-2- 101, et seq., 
C.R.S.; and 
 
WHEREAS, Colorado Revised Statues §§ 31–15–401 and 31-15-501 grants municipalities police 
powers and the power to regulate the use of land and businesses within the community; and 
 
WHEREAS, Colorado Revised Statues § 31–15–103 grants municipalities the power to adopt 
ordinances “necessary and proper to provide for the safety, preserve the health, promote the health 
and prosperity, and improve the morals, order, comfort, and convenience” of the municipality and 
its inhabitants; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Blue River was incorporated as a municipality on August 26, 1964; and 
 
WHEREAS, at the time of in corporation, Blue River was largely comprised of platted lots 
available for single family residential dwelling units; and 
 
WHEREAS, following incorporation, the Town of Blue River is predominately zoned for single 
family residential uses and within the Residential (R -1) Zone District, together with other zone 
districts that were limited to residential use; and

currently prohibited in all zone districts, with the exception of limited types of smaller - scale

neighborhood. 
 
Short Term Rental History for Blue River 
 
WHEREAS, the Town has long declared the short -term rental of property to be a commercial or 
business activity that is generally antithetical to and inconsistent with the character and quiet 
enjoyment of residential neighborhoods and a residential mountain community; and 
 
WHEREAS, the short-term rental of property for a period of thirty (30) days of less has never been 
a use or a right associated with a residential dwelling unit, the activity is not a permitted use in any 
zone district of the Town, and the activity is not a permitted home occupation in any zone district; 
and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town first allowed short-term rentals of residential property in 1998; and 
 
WHEREAS, at its inception, the purpose of the Town’s short -term rental program was to allow 
full-time Town-residents and owners to rent their single family dwelling unit for short periods of 
time during a year (such as during an owner’s vacation or a seasonal absence), in order to assist 
owners in recovering the increasing costs and expenses of home owner ship (such as utilities, 
repairs, and upkeep); and 
 
WHEREAS, the Town recognizes that some full-time and part-time resident owners will use their 
dwelling unit for personal residential occupancy during all or a part of each year and, when not 
occupying their dwelling unit, some owners may make their residential dwelling unit available for 
short-term rental on a limited basis for limited periods of time each year – in effect, short -term 
rental is an ancillary or secondary purpose of the dwelling unit for such owners; and 
 
WHEREAS, over time, owners found that the conducting of a short -term rental of a residential 
dwelling unit for all or a substantial part of a year produced significant revenue for the owner; and 
 
WHEREAS, the success of the Breckenridge Ski Area and other Summit County ski destinations, 
and the availability and attractiveness of year -around recreational opportunities available 
throughout Summit County created an increased demand for short -term vacation rentals in Blue 
River with such demand significantly increasing since 2015; and 
 
WHEREAS, during the last several years of the Town’s short -term rental program, the Town has 
learned that there is an increasing interest from individuals and corporations  in acquiring 
residential dwelling units in Blue River for the singular purpose of operating a short -term rental 
and not for owner-occupancy; and

WHEREAS, the Town’s research of Blue River properties advertising the availability of short-term

WHEREAS, during several recent Town Board public work sessions  and meetings , several
