SUBJECT:  Town Manager Report 
 
 
• A groundbreaking for the Town of Mead’s new police facility was held on October 19th. The event was 
well attended and kicked off the construction of the new 20,000 sq foot facility to be located at the 
Municipal Facilities site on WCR 7 east of Liberty Ranch subdivision. Construction is anticipated 
through fourth quarter of 2027.  
• Fall Clean-Up Days registration ended on October 17th with 258 vouchers issued. The vouchers were 
available to households in Mead and may be used at Front Range Landfill from October 12 th–24th 
during regular operating hours. A list of accepted and prohibited items is available on the Town’s 
website: https://www.meadco.gov/parksandrec/page/2026-fall-clean-days 
• Mead’s regular election will be held on Tuesday November 3, 2026, in coordination with Weld County. 
Ballots will be received in mailboxes this week. If a registered voter does not receive a ballot, please 
contact Weld County Elections. Mead has the Mayor and four Trustee positions up for election. The 
following candidates will appear on the ballot: Mayor: Stephan K. Hucal and Colleen G. Whitlow.  
Trustees: Ryan Hathorn, Lee Peterson, Chris Cartwright, Chris T. Parr, Jeff Morgan, David Broshous, 
Christine Smith and Kim Heard.  For Trustee, the three highest vote getters will receive four-year 
terms, with the fourth highest vote getter receiving the two-year term. 
• The Mead Area Chamber of Commerce will host its annual Trick-or-Treat Street on October 31st along 
Main Street in Mead from 1:00 – 3:00 p.m. Local businesses and community booths will participate, 
each offering Halloween candy for trick-or-treaters.  
• The Public Hearing on the 2027 Proposed Budget will be held on October 26 th at 6:00 p.m. The draft 
budget was presented to the Board of Trustees on September 28 th. The budget represents a plan to 
effectively manage the financial resources of the Town while providing municipal services. The 
proposed budget is posted to the Town’s public records portal 
(https://records.townofmead.org/WebLink/).  
• Mead’s High Plains Library is located at 13815 Chaparral St, just on the east side of the Liberty Ranch 
subdivision of WCR 7. The library is open Monday through Thursday from 9:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m., 
Friday and Saturday from 10:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. and Sunday from 1:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m.  
• The Town has officially wrapped up the Built for Mead business promotional video project in 
partnership with the St. Vrain Valley School District Innovation Center. Students led the production 
and interviews for 15 local businesses, and the videos are now being released through a weekly social 
media campaign featuring one business each week. Each participating business has also received its 
video to use for its own marketing and social media efforts. Videos can be seen here: 
https://flic.kr/s/aHBqjCVWQF  
• The High Plains Blvd (HPB) road construction project is complete and the roadway open. A ribbon 
cutting was held on October 14th to showcase the construction and open the two miles of road between 
WCR 32 and WCR 36 which includes two travel lanes, bike lanes, two roundabouts located at CR 32 
and CR 34, and a ten-foot-wide detached sidewalk. The project was a successful collaboration 
between Mead, Weld County and local developers. The new roundabout intersections at CR 32 and 
CR 34 and High Plains Blvd are both now open in all directions. 
• There is quite a bit of activity going on at the Municipal facilities site located just east of Liberty Ranch 
and south of SH 66 on CR 7. This Town owned site is home to Mead’s first library, a new Mountain 
View Fire Station, and a Police headquarters. The library is completed and open to the public. 
Construction of the fire station is nearing completion and will open later this year. The police facility 
site is being prepared for construction. 
• Staff has attended several position-specific training courses and conferences. The finance team 
attended in-depth Caselle user training, the Town Clerk attended the Colorado Municipal Clerks 
Page 4 of 414
Annual Conference while the Municipal Court Clerk attended the Municipal Court Judges’ and Clerks’ 
Conference. Town Manager attended International City/County Management Association Conference. 
• A pedestrian and bicycle underpass that will go under SH 66 and connect the pedestrian trail that 
runs along CR 7 is under design and right of way acquisition. Construction is anticipated to begin on 
this project by late this year. 
• Mayor Colleen Whitlow convenes weekly office hours at Mead Town Hall. The office hours are on 
Mondays from 10:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. Please contact the front office for an appointment. Walk ins 
will be taken as time allows.  
• Coffee with the Mayor is scheduled for Saturday, November 7 th from 8:00 – 10:00 a.m. at the 
Community Center Peak Room. Mayor Colleen Whitlow hosts this monthly event to provide updates 
on active development in Mead and Town projects, along with answering questions and general 
discussion. Coffee will be provided by local roaster, Redemption Road Coffee.  
• Mead Municipal Court for November, is scheduled for November 17 th at 6:00 p.m. at Town Hall. 
Currently there are 77 cases on the docket for review hearings and arraignments. The docket is 
anticipated to be larger than average due to the early October court date.  Municipal Court is generally 
scheduled for the third Tuesday of each month at Town Hall. Starting in January 2027 court will be 
held monthly on Thursday afternoon, rather than evening court. If you have comments about the 
schedule change, please forward them to Town Clerk, Milissa Peters-Garcia.

• The Certificate of Occupancy for High Plains Library District’s new library was issued on October 16th. 
• Mountain View Fire Rescue’s new fire station continues exterior construction. Exterior sheathing is

• The State of Colorado Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) awarded $105,000 in grant funds (with a 
Town match of $35,000 or 25%) to support the Town’s completion of SB24-174’s required Strategic 
Growth and Water Supply Elements of the Comprehensive Plan and a Housing Action Plan. 
• The Town issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) soliciting consultant teams to assist the Town in 
preparing a Housing Action Plan as well as the Strategic Growth and Water Supply elements of the 
Comprehensive Plan. The RFP was issued on September 25 th, an Addendum with responses to all 
submitted RFP questions was issued on October 16th, and proposals are due back on October 30th. 
• The Land Use Code Amendment Project continues. Town staff completed review of the six draft 
modules and the Town Attorney’s Office is reviewing. Steps to follow include preparing a final draft for 
the Planning Commission’s and Board of Trustees’ consideration. The update ensures that the LUC 
complies with state laws, best practices and efficiencies in the development processes.

Ceremony on December 4 th, followed by the annual community celebration on December 5 th at the

visits with Santa.

through 8th grade. Pre-School/Kindergarten, 1st/2nd Grade, and 3rd/4th Grade basketball begin 
November 7 th, while Junior Nuggets programs for older youth run January through February 2027. 
These programs focus on skill development, teamwork, sportsmanship, and providing recreational 
opportunities for youth of all ages and abilities.

PROCLAMATION 
Native American Heritage Month 
November 2026 
 
 
WHEREAS, the history and culture of our great nation have been significantly 
influenced Native American and indigenous peoples; and 
 
WHEREAS, the contributions of Native Americans have enhanced the freedom, 
prosperity, and greatness of America today; and 
 
WHEREAS, their customs and traditions are respected and celebrated as part of a rich 
legacy throughout the United States; and 
 
WHEREAS, in 1990 President George H. W. Bush approved a joint resolution 
designating November 1990 “National American Indian Heritage Month.” Similar 
proclamations, under variants on the name (including “Native American Heritage 
Month” and “National American Indian and Alaska Native Heritage Month”) have been 
issued each year since 1994; and 
 
WHEREAS, in honor of Native American Heritage Month, statewide celebrations as 
well as numerous cultural, artistic, educational, and historical activities have been 
planned; and 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Colleen Whitlow, Mayor, of the Town of Mead, do hereby 
proclaim the month of November 2026 as Native American Heritage Month.  
 
Given under my hand and Seal of the Town of Mead, Colorado 
On this 26th day of October 2026
