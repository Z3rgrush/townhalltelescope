NEW BUSINESS   
A. Blight, Life Safety & Accessibility Application — Arkansas Valley CASA (Action) 
Ben Mason: There is a Blight & Safety application for Arkansas Valley CASA to repair 
their roof. They are a nonprofit. It does give us some flexibility with the grant amount. I'll 
open that up for discussion or questions. I do know that Cynthia Sollenbarger is here 
representing CASA. 
 
Cynthia Sollenbarger: We would be grateful for anything awarded; the roof is in pretty 
bad condition, as you can see on the photos. Not sure if this can be done in stages, as I 
report back to the ruling board in Pueblo who actually make all decisions for us. I am 
here to answer any questions. 
 
Toni Madrid: Are there currently any issues on the inside of the building due to the roof? 
 
Cynthia Sollenbarger: There has definitely been leakage into the building (toward the 
back area, which CASA has a lot of plans for that back area for the future). I think the 
roof is around twenty (20) years old. It is cracked and is showing signs of damage.  
 
Ben Mason: My concern is that the longer something like this just "sits", then the more 
damage that occurs over time. It would be best to address this sooner rather than later. 
 
Cheryl Lindner arrived at 4:11 p.m. 
 
Zach Miner: We've seen that time and time again in projects. Something that may be 
minor this year becomes major the next year, especially when dealing with costs. 
Regarding nonprofits, our application allows a case-by-case basis. What would freeing 
up money help you guys do? If we were able to grant more for this, how would that 
extra amount help you guys? 
 
Cynthia Sollenbarger: Our first project that we would like to do is install a kitchen, which 
will be used by us and our staff. We have volunteers that we are responsible for 
providing twelve (12) hours of continuing education. As a nonprofit, we try to be very 
frugal when it comes to spending. A lot of the time we will prepare food for the meetings 
and trainings that we have in-house. I also have a board where we prepare food 
monthly for them. On top of that, we have opened up our new building to various 
agencies and we can do that at my discretion. Just recently we hosted over fifty (50) 
people from the Colorado Coalition of Substance Abuse Disorder that are going to use 
our building for those trainings. It is my goal to share our building, as we have a very 
large area for that. Eventually, the goal is for our building to have a visitation center for 
families who have court-ordered visitation. I would like to have that area where families 
can fix up a meal together and sit down to eat together. This would give these families a 
bit of dignity. That is the long-term plan. 
 
Zach Miner: Do you have a preference regarding the quotes provided? Speaking 
personally, one of the things I appreciate is vying for local contractors and vendors. 
 
Cynthia Sollenbarger: As a member of the Chamber Of Commerce, I am definitely 
looking at local options. It will not be my decision, but I will have the opportunity to 
discuss and weigh in on the quotes. 
 
Toni Madrid: I would remind the board that we usually do not have meetings in 
November and December, so just as a funding heads up, this could be one of the last 
projects we fund for this year. 
 
Angel Saucedo: With what was said regarding the last few meetings, is the timeline for 
you to get this done before winter hits? As this is a roof repair or replacement that you 
are applying for. Have the leak(s) been significant at all since applying for this? 
 
Cynthia Sollenbarger: That would be ideal; I can definitely see problems arising, 
especially since this year the snow is predicted to be much more than last year. A light 
rain does not cause problems. However, if it ends up puddling in that area due to rain or 
snow, there will be issues with leaks.
