CITIZEN PARTICIPATION (5-minute time limit per person)  
Diana Remick, Ward 3: Ms. Remick shared that her husband, Billy, passed away in December 
2024 following a heart attack. She commended her 16-year-old son, Ryan, whose immediate 
CPR efforts preserved his father's life for an additional week, allowing the family to plan for his 
wishes to be an organ donor. On May 10, 2025, while driving near the high school on 22nd 
Street, Ryan’s vehicle was struck by large rocks thrown by local individuals. After driving to 
safety, Ryan returned to the scene to confront the perpetrators. He was subsequently struck in 
the face with a metal baseball bat by Virgil Eden, a resident of 13 West 3rd Street. The assault 
shattered the car window and caused severe facial injuries, requiring three hospital CAT scans. 
Ms. Remick expressed frustration that her son received a diversion charge for returning to the 
scene, while the felony assault case against Mr. Eden was abruptly dismissed by District 
Attorney Bullock without notifying the family. The dismissal was reportedly blamed on the lead 
investigator leaving the police department and an unrelated medical plea agreement involving 
Mr. Eden. Ms. Remick highlighted an operational disconnect, noting that 22nd Street is within 
city limits while 13 West 3rd Street falls under county jurisdiction, causing critical cases to be 
dropped. She noted ongoing dangers at the property, including a recent football practice 
evacuation due to gunfire. Though relocating in a year, she urged the city and county to unite 
immediately to protect local youth. 
CITIZEN PARTICIPATION  
Chuck Mondragon & Shanice Lopez, Ward 1: Mr. Mondragon stated that city and law 
enforcement officials failed to make his daughter's case a priority or conduct a proper 
investigation. He noted that his daily requests for updates went unanswered, forcing him to turn 
to social media to demand accountability. He asserted that the local department lacked the 
necessary resources and knowledge for the case but delayed seeking outside state assistance. 
Mr. Mondragon alleged internal unprofessionalism and a double standard within the police 
department. He noted that while officers freely discussed the open case in public, his family 
was denied basic updates under the guise of an active investigation. Furthermore, he reported 
that when a hired private investigator questioned the Chief of Police, the Chief claimed to have 
no knowledge of the case, which Mr. Mondragon criticized as a severe failure of leadership. As 
an active community member, business owner, and youth sports sponsor who recently 
collaborated with the Mayor and police department on an e-bike event, Mr. Mondragon noted a 
stark shift in treatment. He stated that officials who were previously friendly now avoid eye 
contact and refuse to offer a personal apology, relying instead on public social media 
statements. Backed by an extensive network of community support, Mr. Mondragon concluded 
by stating that no one is above the law. He vowed to continue fighting to ensure his daughter’s 
case is not ignored and called on officials to stop manipulating protocols to protect personal 
acquaintances. 
 
Lisa Doney: Brian Lovato was allegedly fast-tracked to sergeant by Chief Gary Todd Quick after 
only a few months of road experience, coinciding with Lovato announcing his candidacy for 
sheriff. Lovato allegedly made highly insensitive and inappropriate remarks at the Arkansas 
Valley Fair and local businesses regarding a deceased child ("Your daughter is dead, get over 
it, move on"), which was reportedly witnessed by multiple officers. Lovato allegedly worked out 
of uniform and campaigned for sheriff while on duty, which citizens are seeking to validate via 
court records. A formal complaint regarding Chief Quick’s cognitive fitness was dismissed as 
"unfounded" by HR Director Dawn Block, who is Chief Quick's former wife. Block allegedly 
bypassed medical protocols, failed to secure an official medical assessment, and heavily 
redacted public records requests. Mayor Joe Ayala publicly endorsed Lovato for sheriff. The 
mayor's brother-in-law is connected to the residence where the child died. While the mayor 
claims no involvement, officers allege he routinely exerts internal pressure on active 
investigations. The case was only handed over to the Colorado Bureau of Investigation (CBI) 
after public pressure.
