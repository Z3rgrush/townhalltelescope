CITIZEN PARTICIPATION (5-minute time limit per person)  
1. Lisa Doney: Addressed the council regarding a compounding crisis of leadership, 
transparency and accountability with the La Junta Police Department and city 
administration. Public trust cannot exist when systematic conflicts of interest and 
mishandling of evidence are allowed to bypass standard legal oversight. She plans to 
turn over her evidence to an outside law enforcement agency for review. 
2. Murissa Jackson: Believes the HR investigation of Chief Todd Quick was not neutral 
and cannot be trusted. HR is Dawn Block, Quick's former spouse. The documents 
provided to her were heavily redacted without providing a CRS citation as to why. She is 
asking the council to reject HR's investigation and order a new independent 
investigation by an outside agency, provide the CRS citation for the redactions made 
and create policy preventing HR from investigating anyone they have personal ties to. 
3. Merina Lopez: Is a mother advocating for the safety of her four-year-old son who has 
been diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder Level 2. Her son experiences an 
elopement, also known as wandering. This is not simply a child running away. It is a 
medically recognized behavior associated with autism that can place children in 
immediate life-threatening danger. She requests approval to install an Autism Childhood 
Area Sign in their neighborhood. 
INFORMATION  
A. Kitchen of Kindness 
Kasey Miehlke, President: Kitchen of Kindness started in 2022 with around five people 
at Santa Fe Plaza. They are now serving up to 150 plus meals and on July 30th, 
actually served 213 meals. They are in need of volunteers and are struggling financially. 
They are community-based and do apply and receive grants; however, they also rely on 
the community for funding as well. They have closed their location at 109 W. 2nd Street 
but will still be serving meals at the River of Life Church.  
B. YEEHAW 
Larry Chapman, YEEHAW Task Force: A total of 63 people have benefited from the 
services provided in this area. They continue to support the residents through the 
YEEHAW service pantry, which helps with bathroom and kitchen items not purchasable 
with food stamps, as well as individual support services. Volunteers are the core of 
YEEHAW and both community business partners and the community at large. There will 
be six community volunteer development sessions that are free to the public. Three 
have been held on ethics and motivational interviewing, three more on self-care, 
trauma-informed care and substance use and mental health awareness will also be 
presented. YEEHAW has also made several small and important contributions to the 
community that includes phones, clothing, food, cars, P.O. box rentals and even small 
car parts that have aided in supporting the unhoused neighbors on their journey home 
or just keeping them alive. 
C. AVRMC (Ron Hogan) 
Ron Hogan, AVRMC: The financial audit for fiscal year March 31 came back as a clean 
audit. Their financial position has gotten stronger. They still have no debt and their cash 
position has gone from roughly 20 to 68 days now. Three of the four covenants in the 
consolidation agreement were met to be in compliance. They continue looking to recruit 
physicians and announced they've got a new surgeon coming to town, Dr. Joy Dunn, 
from New Mexico. She anticipates arriving around the end of September. They have 
another nurse practioner that will go to Rocky Ford, Jennifer Pekarik. She will start 
roughly the same time as the end of September. The recruitment has been positive. 
They are also looking to do a couple more tours over the next two to three months, 
potentially bringing in some elected officials from the Denver area and the western side 
of the state so they can understand what takes place on the eastern side of the state. 
Especially with rural health care when they're making decisions that impact us on this 
side of the state.
