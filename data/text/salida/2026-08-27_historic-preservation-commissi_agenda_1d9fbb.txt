STAFF REPORT 
REVIEW DATE: August 27, 2026 
AGENDA ITEM TITLE: 125 E. 2nd Street – Major Certificate of Approval Application 
REQUEST: Shavano Holding Co. LLC requests a Major Certificate of Approval (CA) for the 
exterior renovation of the old Mountain Mail building at 125 E. 2nd Street. The intent is to 
transform the existing building into one accommodating a mix of uses, including a restaurant 
with a rooftop patio, two residences on the upper floor, and ground floor retail. The application 
includes all proposed exterior work, which may happen over the course of several phases, as 
follows: 
 
• Repairing the existing stucco as needed and painting it teal;  
• Replacing damaged wood boards around the ground-floor storefront with cedar boards 
finished with a teak-colored stain; 
• Replacing the ground-floor windows with new storefront windows and doors and 
installing a new awning, as shown in the elevations and precedent images; 
• Adding two small egress windows on the upper floor for fire-code compliance; 
• Completing exterior alterations associated with two upper-story residential units and a 
rooftop patio serving the proposed ground-floor restaurant use, including the rooftop 
stair/access enclosure and associated railing or parapet work shown in the elevations; 
and 
• Demolishing a deteriorated, detached rear accessory structure. 
 
APPLICANT: 
The applicant is Shavano Holding Co. LLC, 125 E. 2nd Street, Salida, Colorado.  
 
LOCATION: 
The Mountain Mail project includes the historically divided properties addressed as 121, 125, 
and 129 E. 2nd Street, including parcels R368132431369 and R368132431363. For purposes 
of this report, the combined project is referred to as 125 E. 2nd Street or the Mountain Mail 
project. The property is within the Mixed-Use Downtown (MD) District and Salida Downtown 
Historic District. 
 
Page 3 of 31
 
2 
BACKGROUND: 
The subject building is listed as non-contributing to the historic district. The project will retain 
the upper-story street-facing facade and its historic windows while rehabilitating the 
altered ground-floor storefront. Two egress windows will be added on a secondary elevation to 
serve the proposed upper-story residential units. 
The detached rear shack/garage is also non-contributing and is not connected to the main 
building. Photographs document substantial deterioration of its exterior walls, roof, and 
openings. The application proposes its complete removal. 
 
PROCESS: 
Section 16-7-60 requires a CA before work subject to historic review may proceed; a CA does 
not replace any required site plan, change-in-use, building, demolition, sign, or other permit. 
Staff has identified this project as requiring a Major CA because it includes renovation, exterior 
alterations, changes to wall or roof area, and demolition.  
 
A Major CA is subject to administrative staff review and decision by the Historic Preservation 
Commission (HPC). Table 8-A identifies a pre-application conference as required; the 
applicant completed a pre-application conference with staff. After completeness, the HPC must 
consider the application within 31 days, or within a longer period reasonably necessary to 
place the matter on an agenda. Public notice is required. 
 
OBSERVATIONS: 
1. The subject building and detached rear structure are non-contributing. The rear structure is 
visibly deteriorated, is not connected to the main building, and does not constitute a 
character-defining feature of the district. 
2. The existing upper-story street-facing windows, arched openings, and bay window will 
remain unchanged. Replacement windows and doors are limited to the altered ground-floor 
storefront, with two new egress windows on a non-street-facing elevation. 
3. The existing ground-floor stucco will be repaired as needed and painted teal. Damaged 
wood boards surrounding the storefront will be replaced with cedar boards finished with a 
teak-colored stain. The existing awning will be replaced with a new awning of a similar 
style, as shown in the elevations and precedent photos.  
4. The Design Guidelines generally call for roof additions to be set back from the primary 
facade and for the visual impact of rooftop features to be minimized. The patio extends to 
the front building line, so its railing and occupied area will be visible from E. 2nd Street. 
However, the enclosed stair/access structure is set back from the street.  
5. The CA review is limited to historic-preservation approval. The two residential units, rooftop 
food-and-beverage area, site design, utilities, parking, life-safety compliance, and 
construction remain subject to all separate land-use and building approvals. 
 
REVIEW STANDARDS: 
The non-contributing-resource standards apply, together with the separate demolition criteria: 
A.  Enhance District. The project restores an active storefront, repairs the existing exterior, 
and retains the upper-story window composition, enhancing the district.

3 
B.  Overall Character. The storefront glazing, stucco, stained cedar, awning, retained upper 
facade, and subordinate rooftop work are compatible with downtown character. 
C.  Specific Compatibility. The upper-story street facade windows are preserved, egress 
windows are away from the primary facade, and rooftop work remains below the district 
height limit. 
D.  Work Necessary. The upper-story egress openings address fire and life-safety 
requirements. Repair of deteriorated materials and storefront rehabilitation support 
continued use. 
E.  Demolition. The rear shack/garage is a small, detached, non-contributing accessory 
structure. Photographs demonstrate deterioration, and its removal will not adversely affect 
the historic character of the main building or district. 
F.  Design Guidelines. The proposal preserves the upper facade, restores a traditional 
storefront, and locates egress openings and rooftop work away from primary street-facing 
features. 
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION: 
Based upon the application materials, observations, and review standards outlined above, staff 
recommends approval of the Major Certificate of Approval with the following conditions: 
1. The upper-story street-facing windows, arched openings, and bay window shall be retained. 
Window and door replacement is limited to the ground-floor storefront, with two new upper-
story egress windows on a non-street-facing elevation. 
2. Demolition approval is limited to the detached rear non-contributing structure shown in the 
application photographs and survey. 
3. The rooftop patio, though not set back from the front property line, does not visually disrupt 
the historic scale of the structure or surrounding area. The building’s noncontributing status 
also supports greater flexibility in determining how the rooftop guidance should be applied.

Historic Preservation Committee Proposal: 
Our proposal is intended to restore a more active and pedestrian-oriented storefront while preserving the 
building's defining historic character. The project retains the historic second-story façade, including the 
distinctive bay window and arched window openings, which remain unchanged. 
At the street level, restore the storefront character by replacing non-historic window configurations with a 
traditional storefront glazing system that is compatible with th
e building's historic commercial architecture. 
Large display windows have historically been a defining characteristic of commercial buildings, and the 
proposed storefront glazing is intended to better reflect that traditional commercial pattern while 
accommodating the needs of contemporary businesses. 
The existing awning will also be replaced with a simpler
, more durable design that provides weather 
protection for pedestrians while maintaining the building's str
ong horizontal storefront line. The new 
awning has been designed to complement rather than compete with the historic upper façade. 
We are requesting approval to demolish the existing rear stucco shack, which is not attached to the main 
building. The building has deteriorated significantly over time and is no longer structurally sound, 
presenting an ongoing safety hazard due to its unstable condition. 
Lastly, the proposed second-story patio has been carefully designed to minimize its visual impact on the 
historic building and the surrounding streetscape. The patio is
 recessed behind the front parapet so that it 
remains subordinate to the building's historic façade. The rooftop access enclosure is similarly set back 
from the front of the building, reducing its visibility and preserving the prominence of the original 
second-story architectural features. This enclosure will be cons
tructed using the same materials as the 
rest of the building. By locating these new elements behind the primary façade, the project provides 
functional outdoor space while maintaining the building's historic appearance and respecting the 
character of Salida's downtown historic district.  
The proposed improvements represent a significant investment in the long-term viability of this property. 
By creating a more inviting and functional storefront, the proj
ect encourages active commercial use, 
supports downtown economic vitality, and contributes to the continued preservation of this historic 
building. We believe the work balances preservation with thoughtful modernization, allowing the building 
to remain a productive part of Salida's historic downtown for ye
ars to come. 
We respectfully request the Committee's approval of these proposed exterior improvements and 
appreciate your consideration.

Becca Paiz
