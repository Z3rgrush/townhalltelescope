PLANNING COMMISSION STAFF REPORT 
MEETING DATE:  
AGENDA ITEM TITLE: Discussion of Potential Amendments to Chapter 16, Land Use and Development  
AGENDA SECTION: Work Session 
 
OVERVIEW 
The purpose of the work session is to obtain Planning Commission direction on several Land Use Code 
provisions that were raised as concerns by Commission or staff members, may not be functioning as 
intended, or leave gaps in the Code. 
 
BACKGROUND 
The City Council recently adopted a comprehensive set of amendments to the Land Use Code. During that 
process, staff identified several issues that required additional research or policy discussion and could not be 
resolved within the adoption schedule. Planning Commissioners also brought up some additional questions/ 
areas for improvement. This work session allows staff and the Planning Commission to discuss those issues 
before staff prepares any additional code text amendments. 
 
DETAILS  
Staff is seeking direction on the following topics: 
COTTAGE AND CO-HOUSING 
Before the most recent Land Use Code update, Cottage Housing and Co-Housing were combined under one 
use. Staff separated them because Cottage Housing primarily describes a site and subdivision pattern, while 
Co-Housing describes how residents share space and facilities. Most of the definitions and standards were 
maintained for each use type, except for Cottage Housing now requiring each housing unit to be on its own 
lot. The updated Code now treats them as separate uses, but each requires additional policy direction to 
ensure they are functioning as intended.  
 
Cottage Housing  
Cottage Housing is currently defined in Section 16-10-20 as follows:

A residential development that combines four or more small, individually owned units, each on their own lot, 
that share a common open space. The residents in a co-housing/cottage development may agree to share in 
the provision of communal services such as cooking meals, maintenance of grounds, and childcare. 
 
Under the current standards, Cottage Housing must adhere to the following standards:  
- Must contain at least four units.  
- Each unit must be on its own lot. 
- Each unit may be no larger than 1,500 square feet.   
- At least 10 percent of the project area must be shared open space.  
- Must enter into a development agreement with the City requiring the condominium or other property 
owner’s association to maintain all streets, utilities, and infrastructure that are not dedicated to the 
City. 
- Short-term rentals are prohibited.  
- The underlying district's lot size and setback standards apply to the project boundary as a whole, 
while each cottage lot must also meet the district's dimensional and development standards, except 
that minimum lot frontage is applied as minimum lot width. Projects creating five or fewer lots follow 
the minor subdivision process; projects creating more than five lots follow the major subdivision 
process. 
 
Staff raises the following questions:  
- Is the current definition accurate? Does it need the sentence about cooking meals or childcare 
(generally not associated with Cottage Housing – this is a carry-over from the combined definition with 
Co-Housing).  
- Is Cottage Housing intended to be an affordable housing type? Or simply another form of housing? 
- Do we feel each cottage should be capped at 1,500 square feet?  
- Should the City reduce internal lot area, width, setback, landscaping, or parking standards? If Cottage 
Housing is intended to be a more affordable housing product, reduced standards could be used as an 
incentive.  
- Cottage Housing is currently permitted through minor site plan review in R2, R3, and MN. Are those 
districts appropriate? Do we want to add this housing type in more areas?  
- The Code lists Cottage Housing as a use, but the use-specific standards require subdivision review 
based on the number of lots created. Should Cottage Housing remain a separate use, and if so, how 
should site plan and subdivision review be coordinated to avoid duplicative approvals? 
- Staff recommends requiring every dimension of the shared open space to be at least 20 feet and 
confirming that required parking must be provided on-site. Does Planning Commission agree and, if 
so, should those items be reviewed through the subdivision application or site plan review?  
 
 
Co-Housing  
The definition of Co-Housing was revised when the uses were separated and currently reads as follows:

A residential development that combines four or more small, individually owned units with a shared 
community kitchen and dining space intended for regular communal use. The residents in a co-housing may 
agree to share in the provision of communal services such as cooking meals, maintenance of grounds, and 
childcare. 
 
The distinction is important. Cottage Housing is concerned with the layout of individual lots, while Co-Housing 
describes a particular, community-focused living arrangement, with residents sharing in communal resources 
and maintenance.   
 
Staff raises the following questions:  
- Co-Housing currently has the same district permissions as Cottage Housing because the allowed 
districts were not revisited when the uses were separated. It requires minor site plan review in R2, R3, 
and MN and major site plan review in MH. Should those permissions and review levels remain the 
same, or should they reflect the physical form and intensity of the use? 
- It is not explicit in code whether each unit must be a complete dwelling or if it may be a bedroom or 
suite that relies on shared facilities. Staff would like to make this clearer in the definition, after 
narrowing down what Co-Housing is intended to be.  
- Should we keep the requirement for Co-Housing units to be individually owned? If allowing for 
bedrooms with shared facilities, this requirement might need to be removed. Individually owned could 
also be changed to some version of “Occupied by one family.”  
- The current Code separately defines Group Living, Group Homes, and Dormitory, but it does not 
appear to define Emergency Shelter or Transitional Housing. A typical shelter would not meet the 
current Co-Housing definition because its units are unlikely to be individually owned. Should the Code 
create separate use categories and standards for these arrangements rather than stretching Co-
Housing to cover them? 
 
 
PARKING REQUIREMENTS FOR MULTIFAMILY HOUSING 
Section 16-4-70 requires 1.5 off-street parking spaces per unit for multifamily developments with three or 
more units. For developments with four or more units, an additional guest parking space is required. The 
Code rounds a fractional total of 0.5 or more to the next whole space. This means that a triplex requires five 
spaces and a fourplex requires seven. Staff is seeking direction on whether this reflects the City's intent, 
particularly for small multifamily projects.  
- Do we think this is the appropriate amount of required parking?  
- Should the 1.5 space requirement or guest parking space only be required for larger developments 
and, if so, at what number of units would this kick in?  
 
 
SIGNAGE 
Currently, Table 6-A states that in Residential districts, the maximum size for any one sign is nine square feet, 
and the total combined area for all signs is also nine square feet. Not included in the table, but found in the 
Temporary Signs section, is a provision stating that a residential lot may display up to three temporary signs 
at one time, totaling no more than nine square feet, for fewer than 90 days per calendar year. It is not clear if 
this is in addition to the normally allowed signage, or if temporary signs are included in the nine square foot 
maximum.

- The Code does not provide a separate election-season allowance; election signs on private property 
are regulated as temporary signs. Should the Code continue to regulate all temporary signs the same

- Is nine square feet an appropriate maximum for one residential sign, or is that too large? 
- Should temporary sign area be excluded from the permanent nine-square-foot total, allowing up to 18 
square feet of combined signage, or should permanent and temporary signs share one cumulative 
limit?
