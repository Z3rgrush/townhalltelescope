PROPOSED AMENDMENTS TO THE CITY CHARTER
City Councilor J. David Reed read a statement in opposition of the four proposed Charter
amendments associated with Ordinances 2727,2728,2729, and 2730, citing the division that
comes with partisan politics.
City Attorney Chris Dowsey presented an overview of the proposed amendments and the
timeline to get to this point.
Ordinance 2727: Ordinance 2727 sets the ballot language for an amendment that would move
general municipal elections from April to November of even-numbered years. lf successful, the
terms of all sitting City Council members would be extended by 7 months.
Ordinance 2728: Ordinance 2728 sets the ballot language to establish 8-year lifetime term limits
for City Councilors and removes the limit of three consecutive terms for the at-large seat. The
lifetime term limits would be addressed in candidate qualifications. No person who has served 8
years would be eligible for appointment or election. Mr. Dowsey stated that sitting Councilors
would be exempt from the lifetime limit during their current terms.
Ordinance 2729: Ordinance 2729 sets the ballot language for an amendment that would grant
City Council approval rights for the appointment and discharge of the police chief.
Ordinance 2730: Ordinance 2730 sets the ballot la nguage for an amendment to grant City Council
approval rights for the appointment and discharge of the city clerk. Mr. Dowsey stated that the
Ordinance wording was modified at the August 18 City Council meeting, and the modified version
was approved on first reading.

VEHICLE PURCHASE APPROVAL
City Council considered the purchase of a Ford F-150 from Sill-TerHar Motors of Broomfield,
Colorado, for the total purchase price of $63,500.00.
Public Works Director Jim Scheid reported that this vehicle purchase is for the Police Department
and was included in the 2025 budget. Mr. Scheid said that a transit van was sold and the proceeds
from the sale were applied to the purchase. Savings in 2026 patrol vehicle purchases will cover
the remaining cost. Mr. Scheid said that the F-150 has lower lifecycle costs and higher resale value
when compared to an SUV. This vehicle has a trim level of 2 out of 8 possible and is typical for
most City vehicles. Compared to the base model, this trim package holds resale value better and
includes a towing package.
Public comment was acce pted.
Leah Vandersluis questioned if other dealers were researched and considered. Mr. Scheid
reported that Sil-TerHar was awarded the state bid that allows the City to take advantage of
government pricing for vehicles, and the city has had good experiences with this vendor.
A motion was made by J. David Reed, seconded by Ed Ulibarri, to approve the purchase of a Ford
F-150 from Sill-TerHar Motors out of Broomfield, Colorado, for a total price of $63,500.00 as
presented. All voted yes. Motion passed.
