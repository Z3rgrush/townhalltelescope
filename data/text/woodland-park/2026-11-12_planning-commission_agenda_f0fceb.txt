A. Eagle Fire Lodge Rezoning and Conditional Use Permit (A26-0200): A request by Rick 
Dilsavor of Cedarwood Development, Inc (Applicant) on behalf of Woodland Capital, Inc 
(Property Owner) for approval of a zone district change from Planned Unit Development 
(PUD) to Multi Dwelling Urban Residential (MDU), as well as approval of a Conditional 
Use Permit to allow multi-household attached dwelling units within the MDU zone district. 
The subject property is legally described as Lot 3R, Block 3, C.D. Weaver #1A Subdivision 
of Woodland Park, Teller County (a.k.a. 777 East US Highway 24, Woodland Park, CO). 
Senior Planner Shull presented the staff report. The subject property is approximately 1.6 
acres, located directly west of US HWY 24 and south of E. Sheridan Ave. It was part of the 
original C.D. Weaver Subdivision platted in 1948. The property was formally annexed into the 
City in 1997 and approved for a Planned Unit Development and Final Development Plan of a 
conceptual 36-unit motel (Eagle Fire Lodge). Actual property development included 5 original 
cabin units, a main lobby, and subsequent construction of a 10-unit lodge and two 5-unit 
lodges. A 2010 replat amended the lot line boundaries, resulting in its current configuration. 
The property presently contains these 25 lodging units, 37 total parking spaces, exterior 
fencing/landscaping, a designated loading and dumpster area, and an outdoor patio/open 
space area. There is also a 500-year flood zone encumbering the property which overlaps 
three structures.  
The property is currently zoned Planned Unit Development (PUD). Adjacent property zoning 
includes Central Business District (north), Community Commercial (east), and Multi Dwelling 
Urban Residential (west). Additionally, the property is designated as future Commercial, with 
 
Page 2 of 6 
 
adjacent properties designated as future Multi-Family Residential (north, west, and south) and 
Downtown Mixed Use (east).  
The applicant’s request is to convert the 25 lodging units into 25 market-rate, long-term rental 
dwelling units. Planner Shull clarified that the maximum density restriction within the PUD 
zone district is two dwelling units per acre (DU/AC). Given the property’s acreage and number 
of future units created, the resulting density would equal roughly 16 DU/AC, far exceeding the 
2 DU/AC threshold. Alternatively, the MDU zone district allows up to 20 DU/AC. The request 
additionally calls out several minor improvements to the property, including a 300 sq. ft. 
building addition, a new maintenance vehicle/pedestrian pathway, a designated snow storage 
area, and outdoor common area enhancements.  
Referral comment requests were sent out to all required reviewing agencies/entities. 
Comments were received by CORE Electric Cooperative, Utilities/Public Works, and Colorado 
Department of Transportation (CDOT). CORE noted that they have existing underground and 
overhead electric facilities located on the subject property which will be retained / maintained 
within the established utility easements, and that the applicant will be required to modify the 
electric service to accommodate separate metering for each apartment unit in accordance 
with CORE's Builder/Developer Service Requirements. Utilities/Public Works noted that 
property is currently serviced by a 1.5” water line and a 1.5” commercial water tap. Based on 
the applicant’s water adequacy and availability findings, the City could support the proposed 
rezone at the current density. Further, per the City’s Water Tap Management Plan, a rezone 
to the MDU zone district triggers the requirement to obtain an additional 25 multifamily water 
taps which, pursuant to Municipal Code Section 13.27.050 D, must be approved (for sale and 
allocation) by City Council. The estimated cost for 25 multifamily water taps, less a credit back 
for the Commercial water tap, is approximately $144,474. Utilities/Public Works verified that 
there are currently 32 taps available in the “multifamily tap bank”, enough to support the 
applicant’s request. CDOT noted that a State Highway Access Permit will be required for the 
change-in-use.  
The City Planning Department followed City public notice requirements. Adjacent property 
owners within 200 feet were mailed a letter notifying them of this application as well as all 
meeting dates, the site was posted with public notice posters, and a notice of the public 
hearing was published in the Pikes Peak Courier in compliance with the Municipal Code. No 
public comments were received to date. 
Senior Planner Shull presented Staff findings for review against both the Zone District Change 
criteria and Conditional Use Permit criteria (per UDC Sections 6.06.160.D and 6.06.090.E). A 
summary of findings include:  
• Housing Need: The proposed rezoning from Planned Unit Development (PUD) to Multiple 
Dwelling Urban Residential (MDU) would establish 25 long-term rental units, addressing 
the City’s shortage of workforce and low-to-middle-income housing and supporting greater 
housing stability. 
• Compatibility and Traffic: The proposed multifamily use is compatible with surrounding 
apartments, offices, and commercial uses. Although the change is expected to increase 
vehicle trips by approximately 103 trips, staff considers the impact minor and does not 
recommend traffic mitigation measures. CDOT will likely require a State Highway Access 
Permit be updated for the change in use.  
• Comprehensive Plan Consistency: The request supports the City’s Comprehensive 
Plan housing goals by diversifying housing options and aligns with the surrounding 
 
Page 3 of 6 
 
multifamily residential development pattern. Staff determined the rezoning would not 
constitute spot zoning. 
• Infrastructure and Utilities: The existing property is served by water, sewer, gas, and 
electricity. Staff recommends requiring the applicant to coordinate with CORE Electric 
Cooperative to provide separate electrical metering for each apartment unit. 
• Proposed Improvements: All structures are being reused in servicing the future 
residential occupants with only one structural expansion being proposed. Minor functional 
and aesthetic improvements include snow storage area, landscaping, and reroofing. Staff 
recommends a condition requiring a Zoning Development Permit for the proposed 300-
square-foot building expansion. 
• Water Allocation and Approval Conditions: The City supports the proposed rezoning 
based on anticipated water-use reductions compared with motel operations; however, no 
additional density increases are supported. Approval should be conditioned on City 
Council authorization of 25 multifamily water taps and compliance with applicable water 
connectivity and tap fee requirements. 
Senior Planner Shull concluded by recommending Planning Commission recommend 
approval, clarifying that two separate motions are required, one for the Rezone and one for 
the Conditional Use Permit.  
VC Kennedy then called the Applicant to the podium to make a presentation. Mr. Dilsavor 
explained that he represents the applicant in this request, who also owns the adjacent Trail 
Ridge Apartments. In his words, this request represents a unique infill opportunity for the City 
to provide much-needed long-term housing options. The project is density-neutral, water-
conscious, and has minimal impact on traffic. He stated he is comfortable with the conditions 
of approval as presented by Staff, adding a request for support from Planning Commission for 
the 25 multifamily water taps to be approved by City Council. He further requested that 
Planning Commission consider accepting the pre-existing, non-conforming conditions of the 
property as they currently are with the approval. He concluded by reaffirming his interest in 
gaining a favorable vote from Planning Commission on both the Rezone and Conditional Use 
Permit. 
The Commissioners asked the Applicant if he could provide any statistics on current 
occupancy rate of the Eagle Fire Lodge to aid in understanding overall lodging demand for 
the City / need for converting to long-term housing. Mr. Dilsavor responded by suggesting that 
lodging demand was not a consideration given the request to change to long-term residential. 
He noted that the future units are anticipated to be fully occupied based on projected demand.

Municipal Code for 1) assigning water taps per the City’s Water Tap Management Plan under 
a separate multifamily tap “bucket”, and 2) for gaining City Council approval for the sale and 
allocation of water taps. The resulting water tap fee (for 25 multifamily units) is based on a

The Commissioners discussed how existing nonconforming structures and uses should be 
addressed following rezoning, including whether individual variances would be required or 
whether the structures and uses should be identified in a future resolution. Staff indicated that 
the Unified Development Code does not specifically address whether existing nonconformities 
must be brought into compliance following a zone change. Based on prior governmental 
experience, Staff and the City Attorney suggested that existing nonconforming structures and 
uses could remain in their current status unless altered or destroyed, with routine maintenance 
permitted. Staff did not consider individual variances appropriate and expressed a willingness 
to identify the affected structures and uses in the approval resolution. 
The Commissioners asked whether the Community Commercial or Central Business District

evaluated because the MDU district better aligns with the surrounding multifamily zoning

B. Text Amendment to add “Public Safety Facilities” to the Unified Development Code 
(UDC): Consideration to amend Section 4.03, Section 6.06.120.B.1., and Section 7.03 of 
the Unified Development Code to add a new use type of “Public Safety Facilities” in the 
Table of Allowed Uses, to label said use as applicable under the Special Use Permit 
process, and to define “Public Safety Facilities” under Words and Terms (L). 
Director Schminke began the staff report presentation by noting this topic was discussed at a 
prior Planning Commission work session, and those recommendations had been incorporated 
into this proposal. The City’s former Title 18 – Zoning, did not explicitly call out emergency 
service / public safety type uses or facilities. Requests for these types of uses and facilities 
were generally reviewed under the then-broad use category of public / semi-public facilities. 
The City currently has several active public safety facilities, including the Police Station on 
Tamarac Pkwy (zoned P/SPL), the NETCFPD Fire Station on Evergreen Heights Dr (zoned 
P/SPL), and two Ambulance facilities, one on Red Feather Ln (zoned CC) and one near the 
Safeway (zoned PUD).  
The UDC adoption in March of 2026 did not account for emergency service / public safety 
uses or facilities. The UDC contains a definition for “Ambulance Service Business” which is 
narrow in focus and does not account for other emergency uses like police and fire.  
Director Schminke displayed the recommended UDC text amendments, highlighting the new 
definition for “Public Safety Facilities” and elimination of “Ambulance Service Business”; the 
change to the Table of Allowed Uses adding Public Safety Facilities and associated Use Table 
Key values by zone district; deletion of Ambulance Service Business from the Use Table; and 
the modification to the Special Use Permit applicability language.  
• The new definition for “Public Safety Facilities” is to read: “Public Safety Facilities” are 
facilities, operated as either a government service or a private business, for the provision 
of local rapid response emergency services such as police, firefighting, and medical 
services, including areas for the storage and maintenance of emergency vehicles and 
equipment and housing and feeding of emergency personnel. 
• The Table of Allowed Uses (4.03-1) adds Public Safety Facilities to the list of use types, 
with the following use table key values assigned: Special use for CC, SC, CBD, and AG 
 
Page 6 of 6 
 
zone districts; permitted use for HSCLI and P/SPL zone districts; and general 
development plan for PUD zone district; the use being prohibited in all other zone districts.  
• The Special Use Permit applicability (6.06.120.B) is to be modified to read: “A special use 
permit is required whenever an applicant desires to develop, substantially improve, or 
change the use of a tract of land or structure. See Section 4.03-1, Table of Allowed Uses, 
to determine applicable use types.”  The subsequent list of uses requiring a special use 
permit is being deleted. 
Senior Planner Shull interjected, stating that Staff had followed through on talking to the 
various emergency services / public safety districts in the area (police, fire, and ambulance). 
In summary, the district’s staff supports the proposed UDC text amendment, posing no 
arguments that the text amendment might impact their future service expansion, siting, or 
capacity needs.   
Director Schminke concluded by reiterating Staff’s recommendation for Planning Commission 
to recommend the UDC text amendments as presented for approval by City Council.

for the entirety of both lots.

at Paradise, Filing 2. The Haven at Paradise ﬁnal plat was mistakenly platted over an 
existing unplatted city owned property, resulting in the zoning conﬂict in both lots.

RESOLUTION NO. 943, SERIES 2026 
A RESOLUTION APPROVING THE HAVEN AT PARADISE FILING NO. 2 
SUBDIVISION PRELIMINARY AND FINAL PLATS  
 
WHEREAS, the subject property is located northwest of the Red Haven Place cul-de-sac, and is 
legally described as Lots 4 and 5 of The Haven at Paradise subdivision, together with an unplatted 
2.5± acre tract in the SW ¼ of Section 19, T 12 S, R 68 W, in the City of Woodland Park, Teller 
County, CO, with the total land area being 3.43± acres; and 
WHEREAS, Lots 4 and 5 of The Haven at Paradise subdivision are zoned Suburban Residential 
(SR) and are owned by Ventus, LLC; and 
WHEREAS, the unplatted 2.5± acre tract is zoned Public/Semi-public Land (P/SPL) and is owned 
by the City of Woodland Park; and 
WHEREAS, this application for a preliminary and final plat for The Haven at Paradise Subdivision 
Filing No. 2 is made by Mark McNab, MBI Contractors, Inc.; and 
WHEREAS, it is the desire of the Applicant and Landowners to plat these three parcels into two 
Lots and one Tract; and 
WHEREAS, public notice of all public hearings was posted on the property, published in the Pikes 
Peak Courier, and mailed to all adjacent property owners pursuant to all applicable Municipal 
Code requirements; and 
WHEREAS, the Woodland Park Planning Commission considered the request on June 11, 2026, 
and based on evidence contained in the staff report and presented at public hearing, 
recommended conditional approval of the request; and 
WHEREAS, City Council considered the request, the Planning Commission recommendation, 
evidence contained in the staff report and presented at public hearing on July 16, 2026. 
NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED by the City Council of the City of Woodland Park: 
Section 1.  The Haven at Paradise Subdivision Filing No. 2 preliminary and final plats, platting 
3.43± acres into two lots and one tract, is hereby approved subject to the following conditions:  
 
1. Prior to conveyance of any proposed lot, the Final Plat must be recorded in the Teller 
County Clerk & Recorders office.  
2. Applicant must resubmit the Preliminary and Final Plat correcting any redline comments 
from Woodland Park Planning Department. 
a. Lot 3 must be relabeled as Tract A.  
3. Prior to recording the Final Plat, the Property Owner shall enter into an agreement 
satisfactory to the City of Woodland Park (the “City”) to dedicate to the City either or both 
of the following as may be satisfactory to the City in its sole discretion:  
(i) an amount of additional open space equal to or more than the City’s Park land 
area reduced in the proposed Haven at Paradise Filing No. 2 Final Plat; or  
(ii) additional access easements for public recreational use.  
 
 
Additional open space and additional easements refer to public open space and 
easements in excess of those identified in the current master plan. The agreement shall 
run with unplatted parcels of land contiguous with the area of this Final Plat in which the 
property owner has an interest. 
4. The owners of the new lots 1 and 2 of the Haven at Paradise Filing No. 2 subdivision must 
consent to a zone change so that the entirety of both lots will be zoned Suburban 
Residential.  
 
Section 2.  The recitals contained in this Resolution constitute the findings of City Council and 
are incorporated herein by reference.
