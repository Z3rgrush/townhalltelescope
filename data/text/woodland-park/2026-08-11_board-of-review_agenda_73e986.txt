VI. VARIANCE OR APPEAL HEARINGS: 
A. #A26-0137 – 209 E. Midland Avenue; Andrew Nguyen requests an appeal of a “Dangerous 
Building” declaration by the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department (PPRBD) pursuant to the 
Pikes Peak Regional Building Code (RBC) Section RBC112.1.7. NOTICE AND ORDER and 
NOTICE TO VACATE issued by PPRBD dated May 28, 2026. 
 
Chair McVicker opened th is quasi-judicial public hearing at 2:33 p.m. Prior to taking testimony, 
Chair McVicker asked the property owner, Andrew V. Nguyen, to confirm his preferred mailing 
address since conflicting addresses had been provided with the appeal documents. Mr. Nguyen 
requested that all future correspondence be sent to “9380 Burgess Road, Colorado Springs, 
Colorado 80908”. 
 
Chair McVicker asked PPRBD staff whether the filing of the appeal affected the compliance 
deadlines established in the NOTICE AND ORDER and NOTICE TO VACATE  (i.e. “the 
Notice”). Building Official Roger Lovell answered that while the appeal stayed the timeline, it did 
not extend the compliance deadlines  (permits for repair, rehabilitation, or demolition must be 
secured with 60 calendar days of the notice , and repairs must be completed within 90 days 
thereafter). 
 
Chair McVicker invited the appellant to present their case. Mr. Nguyen was represented by local 
architect David Langley, who introduced himself, local architect Linda Allred  of Linda Allred 
Associates LLC, and attending virtually was the proposed general contractor, Carl Andersen of 
Andersen Enterprises, Inc. Mr. Langley distributed to the Board copies of the proposed demolition 
plan he prepared and the structural repair plan as prepared by professional engineer, Chris 
Morrison, CME. 
 
 
Page 2 of 4 
 
Mr. Langley explained that his intent is to address the five violations as identified in the Notice. 
He stated that the building has remained unoccupied since Mr. Nguyen purchased it in 2019. He 
said that demolition work in the building was performed during previous ownership. He explained 
that Mr. Nguyen hired a contractor for repairs and remodeling, and after substantial payment to 
them, the contractor declared bankruptcy. No further construction has been completed as a result. 
The repair plan is intended to remedy the hazardous conditions in the building but is not intended 
to address remodeling or possible future occupancy.  He cannot recommend the expenditures 
required for complete rehabilitation design and building occupancy determination while the Notice 
and Stop Work Order are in effect. He outlined the violations as identified in the Notice and noted 
how some of the design requirements will ultimately depend on the occupancy type per the RBC 
and the International Building Code (IBC), which has yet to be determined by the owner. 
 
Mr. Langley  stated that the 60-day deadline for obtaining building permits is not feasible 
considering the current plan review timelines he has experienced with other projects he is involved 
with, and that the timeframe to complete improvements as mandated by the building code is not 
feasible considering current typical construction timing challenges , such as obtaining building 
materials and labor availability. He said similar projects can take up to one year to complete.  He 
asked if the time limitations can be waived. 
 
Mr. Langley added that the building has been recommended for approval as a Local Historical 
Landmark Designation by the Historical Preservation Committee (HPC). He said that both the Fire 
Department and the Teller County Health Department concluded that the deficiencies they 
identified are repairable. He solicited questions from the Board. 
 
Chair McVicker asked him how he can design a remodel if they have not determined the occupancy 
and use of the building. Mr. Langley replied that before they can justify the expense and continue 
with the in-depth design, they need assurances that the building won’t be demolished. 
 
Mr. Nguyen explained that he is considering several potential business uses for the future of the 
building. He acknowledged the significant amount of time that has elapsed since acquiring the 
property and stated that progress toward rehabilitating the stru cture has been delayed by several 
factors. These included financial losses sustained from the previous contractor, multiple deaths 
within his family, other personal and family hardships, and the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic 
and its associated challenges. 
 
Ms. Vogel asked if he needs grant funding to complete the restoration. He replied it would be 
beneficial to expedite the process, and he is currently exploring funding opportunities. 
 
Mr. Langley stated that the building's future use must be determined before the final, detailed 
design can be completed. He noted that potential uses, including an upper -level apartment, a 
museum, or a visitor center, in conjunction with food service, remain under consideration, and that 
the intended occupancy will directly influence the building's design requirements. 
 
Mr. Cahill asked to hear from PPRBD personnel. Building Official Roger Lovell stated that the 
appellant has acknowledged the dangerous conditions of the building, and it is his position that this 
is a “Dangerous Building” under the building code . He explained  that the code provisions are 
intended to rectify the public hazards of dangerous buildings through repair, with demolition being 
the ultimate outcome if those repairs cannot be made in compliance with Chapter 3 of the RBC and

the associated International Code Council (ICC) family of codes. He explained that the building is 
currently an “A-2” (assembly) occupancy, and that before the Notice was issued, there was nothing 
to prevent someone from occupying or using the building. He added that the potential uses and 
occupancies previously mentioned would require a full sprinkler system throughout the building. 
 
Ms. Vogle asked that for them to comply with the Notice, would they have to resolve the 
deficiencies and come into full compliance with the determined occupancy within the mandated 
timeframe. Mr. Lovell confirmed that would be the case due to the life safety risk and the need to 
address the hazard in a timely manner and as quickly as possible. He said that the RBC does allow 
for a one-time extension of up to 120 days, but that the Department has not received such a request. 
 
Mr. Cahill asked if the property could be brought into compliance within 240 days, would it be 
allowed to remain standing without demolition, even if the owner had not yet finalized or disclosed 
the building's ultimate use, provided all applicable code requirements were satisfied . Mr. Lovell 
clarified that if the building were brought into full compliance with Chapter 3 of the RBC within 
that timeframe, then the “Dangerous Building” declaration would be terminated. Mr. Cahill next 
asked if the owner makes a sizeable investment for improvements, but did not meet the criteria as 
specified, would they have any other recourse at that time . Mr. Lovell explained if the building 
cannot be brought into full compliance within the allotted timeframe, then an Order to Demolish 
would be issued to the City Engineer.  At that time, it is the City’s responsibility to demolish the 
building and PPRBD is no longer involved in the matter. 
 
PPRBD Non-Compliance Supervisor Luke Sandersen summarized the inspection of the property 
and described the observed 5 of 17 safety issues and structural deficiencies as the code specifies; 
he also noted the unsafe conditions cited in a letter by the Fire Chief, Tyler Lambert. 
 
PPRBD Regional Building Counsel Virjinia Koultchitzka stated that it is the Department’s position 
that the appellant has not appealed and is not appealing the “Dangerous Building” declaration. 
None of the documents filed for the hearing deny the conditions of the building. She clarified that 
building code time limitations are not within the Board’s authority and lie solely with the Building 
Official. She stated that PPRBD has not received a request for an extension of time by the appellant, 
and that this administrative remedy is available to them until July 27, 2026. If an extension of time 
is granted by PPRBD, and the appellant is not satisfied with that decision, they may appeal th e 
decision to the Board. 
 
During public comment, Ms. Allred stated that this is more than about one building, but rather 
about the city’s commitment to uphold and promote historic preservation. She stated that on July 
7, 2026, the Historical Preservation Committee, which she is a member of, recommended approval 
to City Council for this building to become a Historic Landmark, which will be considered by the 
Council on August 6, 2026. 
 
Mr. Andersen of Andersen Enterprises, Inc. stated he believes the issues with the building can be 
resolved, but the timeline presents significant challenges with the current state of building supplies 
and construction labor availability. 
 
Steve Plutt stated his recollection and opinion of the historical significance of the building and the 
contribution to the community by its owner, Clara Vos. This concluded the public comment portion 
of the meeting.

During rebuttal, Mr. Langley s tated that the structural repair plans that have been submitted to 
PPRBD are intended to remedy the building hazards as they have identified. It has been his 
experience that the plan review process is not quick and oftentimes requires multiple submittals 
before final approval. He did not foresee approval of the plans by PPRBD within the remaining 14 
days and asked for clarification of the process of extending the deadline.  
 
During Board discussion, Mr. Cahill expressed that he  would like to see the appellant take 
advantage of the time remaining to file their application for an extension with the Building Official 
by July 27, 2026. 
 
Ms. Vogel reiterated that the Board’s sole mission is to confirm or deny that the conditions for a 
“Dangerous Building” exists. 
 
MOTION: Chair McVicker moved that the Board of Review affirm the “Dangerous Building” 
declaration by Pikes Peak Regional Building Department (PPRBD) pursuant to the Pikes Peak 
Regional Building Code (RBC) Section RBC112.1.7. NOTICE AND ORDER and NOTICE TO 
VACATE issued by PPRBD dated May 28, 2026 , with the recommendation that the appellant 
request an extension of time with the Building Official by July 27, 2026. Seconded by Mr. Smith. 
Motion passed unanimously. 
 
Chair McVicker stated that the appellant has the right to appeal the Board’s decision to the City 
Council within 30 days.
