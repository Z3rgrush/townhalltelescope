3. Public Hearings  
a. Local Historic Landmark Designation for 209 E. Midland (Vos Building) (26-
0099): Consider recommending approval to designate 209 E. Midland (the Vos 
Building) a Local Historic Landmark, a request by Steve Plutt (Applicant) and 
Andrew V. Nguyen (Property Owner). The subject property is located at 209 E. 
Midland with a legal description of Lots 7, 8, and 9, Block 12, Steffa’s Subdivision 
of Addition No. 1 to the Town of Manitou Park, now known as Woodland Park, 
except that part lying within the bounds of Highway 24, County of Teller, State of 
Colorado and is zoned Central Business District (CBD).  (QJ) 
Chair Glauth noted Vice Chair Allred (absent) and Mr. Langley were advised by the City 
Attorney to recuse themselves from this case. Mr. Langley stayed seated at the dais. It was 
also noted that the City Attorney is available during this meeting via Zoom. 
Director Schminke presented the staff report. The subject property at 209 E. Midland Avenue 
is being considered for a Local Historic Landmark designation. It is nestled in between 
US24, Fairview Street, and E. Midland Avenue. The triangle-shaped property was adjacent to 
railroad ROW and became smaller when the ROW became a highway ROW. Steffa’s 
Subdivision was platted in 1889, and the Town of Manitou Park was platted in 1887. This 
property is zoned CBD.  
Director Schminke showed a photo comparison of the Vos Building circa 1950 and 2024, 
pointing out that it was built in 1939. The addition was added in 1993. She reviewed Clara 
Vos’ contributions including: a dental clinic, ideas on how to fundraise to fix the school 
gymnasium, Memorial Day Cemetery cleanup, sewing/quilting circles to support local Boy 
Scouts and Campfire Girls, held civic affair meetings to control wild dogs running at large 
and advocate for a fire alarm system, ideas for raising money to start the first Woodland Park 

 
Page 2 of 8 
 
4H club, visualized a “Woodland Park Town Hall” in the old Odd Fellows building, meetings 
that negotiated and facilitated the donation of 2.85 acres that expanded the Woodland Park 
Cemetery, and baking and giving countless cookies and cakes to the Red Cross during 
WWII.  
In 2015, a Reconnaissance Survey (inventory) of the Lake Addition Historic Neighborhood 
was completed. The inventory used the Cultural Resources Inventory Form OAHP #1417 
instead of the Architectural Inventory Form OAHP #1403, which would have provided more 
detailed information regarding the building (like setting, design, materials, workmanship, 
etc.). The Architectural Inventory criteria are what the National Park Service and State of 
Colorado use to evaluate structures for their historic designations. The building consists of: 
two stories with no style; a concrete foundation; stucco and concrete block; vinyl single-light 
fixed picture, vinyl single-light side-by-side sliding, a couple of wood-framed single-light 
fixed picture windows; asphalt composition flat roof with false front parapet and one small 
section that is side-gabled; concrete block chimney on east gabled elevation; an uncovered 
concrete pad porch. The document’s Table 5-1 notes this property is landmark “Eligible,” but 
no Field Eligibility Recommendation was made on the basis that it “needs data.”  
The original 1939 footprint of the building was 954 sf, and the 1993 addition consisted of 
another 940 sf. A slide was shown of images comparing the current south façade with an AI 
generated image of a proposed restored façade. 
The application included four letters of support for this proposal from Pikes Peak Historical 
Society (Florissant, CO), Teller Historic and Environmental Coalition (Teller County), Ute 
Pass Historical Society & Pies Peak Museum (Woodland Park, CO), and Victor Heritage 
Society (Victor, CO).  
This application was referred out for comments to the DDA, Main Street, NETCO Fire, 
Teller County Health, PPRBD, City Utilities, City Clerk and the City Manager. All 
publications and postings for the public hearing were met. Only one comment was received; 
Teller County Health asked who would restore the building and what the future use would 
be. 
The Local Landmark Designation Criteria per Resolution No. 602, Series 2003 are:  
1. The structure should be at least 50 years old,  
2. The property is historically significant through one or more of its: architectural design, 
association with a historical event, period or person; physical impact and location within 
the City of Woodland Park; and potential to provide important information concerning 
the history of Woodland Park.  
3. The structure should have sufficient original or accurately restored materials that 
maintain its original historic character.  
The applicant addressed Criteria #1 and #2 but omitted #3.  
Staff finds: 
1. The Vos Building is more than 50 years old, having been built in 1939. 
 
Page 3 of 8 
 
2. Even though the applicant addressed all four sub-criteria listed, the information 
provided primarily addresses the significance of the property as being associated with 
a person, Clara Vos, and her contributions to the community.  
3. The property as described in this staff report and the Colorado Cultural Resources 
Inventory Form OAHP #1417 (Resource Number 5TL.4149) does not provide 
documentation of “sufficient original or accurately restored materials that maintain its 
original historic character” of the structure. There are concerns that more data is 
needed and that the replacement of the divided lite windows in the original building, 
as well as the substantial addition to the east side of the original structure, have 
severely compromised the original historic character.   
The Staff recommends that should the HPC find there is a sufficient basis for designating this 
property a Local Historic Landmark, the HPC: 
Move to recommend City Council approve and ordinance designating 209 E. Midland 
Avenue (the Vos Building), having a legal description of Lots 7, 8, and 9, Block 12, 
Steffa’s Subdivision of Addition No. 1 to the Town of Manitou Park, now known as 
Woodland Park, except that part lying within the bounds of Highway 24, County of 
Teller, Colorado, as a Local Historic Landmark. 
Mr. Langley asked if he could make any comments or ask questions, and Director Schminke 
stated that he should have recused himself at the beginning of the meeting, but he stayed 
seated at the dais. Chair Glauth allowed Mr. Langley to comment at the pleasure of the HPC. 
Mr. Langley clarified that the building does have historical character. He also stated that 
since 1993, the State Historical Commission guidance on additions to historical structures 
was that additions look and be different from the original building to not to be confused with 
the original historical aspects. Chair Glauth clarified that this means that it’s very easy to tell 
the difference between the original historic structure and the new addition. Chair Glauth 
noted that the 2015 inventory does not note the exact location of the vinyl windows.  
The reviewing agency review and report period are concluded; therefore no additional 
agency review comments are expected before the City Council hearing. The HPC hearing 
recommendations will be added to the existing Staff Report, which will then go to City 
Council.  
Chair Glauth clarified Criteria #3, which Director Schminke believes the applicant did not 
address in the LHL application. The architectural form was not completed at the original time 
of inventory/survey. These criteria may require a professional survey. The applicant can 
address Criteria #3 in their presentation. Director Schminke is concerned that the changes to 
the windows and the significant addition are significant because of the building’s simple 
design. Chair Glauth added that the State Historical Commission guidance holds more weight 
than staff concerns; additions to historic buildings should be different to delineate the 
addition from the original structure.  
Applicant Steve Plutt of Lake George, CO, introduced himself. Before his presentation, he 
addressed the concern over the vinyl windows, stating that past practice would suggest vinyl 
windows do not make a difference in the LHL nomination or designation, and should not be 
considered in this case. He noted that many already designated LHL’s in Woodland Park 
contain vinyl windows or other modifications, including: the Ouray Inn/Brazenhead 
 
Page 4 of 8 
 
(windows, additions), the El Dorado (windows and doors), the Maytag House (windows and 
garage door), the Steffa House, and Carroll Cabin. He also noted that the Cowhand does not 
have period doors on the back entrance.  
He said that Mr. Langley is correct, the State Historical Society recommends that an addition 
to a historic building should be distinguishable from the historic building so the original, 
historically significant structure is obvious. The Vos Building thus qualifies as an LHL as it 
is what is accepted and expected from the State Historical Society. The State Historic Society 
website clearly states that vinyl windows do not automatically disqualify a building for State 
Historical Landmark designation in Colorado. 
Mr. Plutt then addressed the three points the City required for Local Historic Landmarks: 
1. The structure should be at least 50 years old: the Vos Building is 87 years old. It was 
built 1939 by John and Clara Vos using dimensional lumber provided by the Spillman 
Sawmill, which was located right across the street (now the Dinosaur Resource Center).  
2. The property is historically significant through one or more of its:  
a) Architectural design: The Vos Building has a distinct design and architectural style; it 
is an irregularly shaped building reminiscent of a right trapezium. The unique shape 
this building creates a remarkable and distinctive architectural style that is instantly 
noticeable on Main Street. This trapezium design very unusual; it is the only one in 
all of Main Street and all of Woodland Park.  
b) Association with a historical event, person, or period: This heritage building 
belonged to the esteemed Mrs. Clara Vos, who is a major figure in the historical 
chronicles of Woodland Park. Mrs. Vos: During WWII made by hand and sewed 
mattresses for the Red Cross; brought dental professionals and optometrists to 
Woodland Park to serve Woodland Park’s children in need; procured Woodland 
Park’s first city/town hall by convincing the City (Town) to buy the old Odd Fellows 
Building on Main Street to hold town hall meetings; she held movie nights (attended 
by hundreds of people on Friday nights) in that building to benefit the community; 
and more. Her most important contribution was raising the money to buy 12 
residential lots that now form Memorial Park. If it wasn’t for Mrs. Vos’ foresight, 
those lots would be houses today instead of Memorial Park. Mrs. Vos was the main 
propeller of getting Memorial Park established. All these ideas were conceived in the 
Vos Building.   
The building also served as a venue for numerous meetings where diverse ideas were 
cultivated, ultimately improving the lives of the entire local community.  
c) Physical impact and location within the City of Woodland Park: During its historic 
period of contribution and significance, the Vos Building was the gateway to 
Woodland Park. Until the expansion of Woodland Park’s city limits, this building was 
at the eastern edge of Woodland Park. In the 1940s and 1950s, it took at least an hour 
to get from downtown Colorado Springs to Woodland. When the Vos Building came 
into sight, you knew you were finally in Woodland Park, and would think, “Oh boy, 
we finally made it.”  
d) The potential to provide important information concerning the history of Woodland 
Park: Mr. Plutt stressed that this building does have the “potential” to serve as a

visitor, education, or interpretive center directly on Main Street, presenting the history 
of Woodland Park.  
3. The structure should have significant original or accurately restored materials that 
maintain its original historic character: Mr. Plutt inadvertently left this off the 
application. He explained that while this building has undergone an extension/addition, 
the main structure remains entirely in its original condition except for windows, which 
are acceptable to many other historic landmarks in Woodland Park. He noted that other 
Woodland Park departments, active committees, resolutions, and ordinances support the 
nomination of the Vos Building, including: 
 
• Woodland Park 2021 Comprehensive Plan, Preserving Our Heritage section (p. 108) 
tasks the HPC to “protect and preserve the historical and cultural heritage of the City 
through historical landmark designations.” 
• Certified Local Government (CLG): Woodland Park was designated a CLG by the 
State Historic Preservation Office. Part of the CLG mission statement is “to seek, to 
encourage, and expand local involvement in historic preservation.”  
• Main Street: Woodland Park is a Main Street Community. Main Street’s mission is to 
“coordinate resources and technical assistance for communities seeking to revitalize 
their historic downtown commercial districts. Colorado Main Street is committed to 
creating high quality places and building stronger communities through preservation-
based economic development.” As a Main Street Community, Woodland Park needs 
to follow through and practice preservation-based economic development.  
• Woodland Park 2021Comprehensive Plan, page 32: “Community identity is 
important to Woodland Park...visual quality is important to maintaining the small 
town character” and “encourages ways to identify the town’s architecture and 
celebrate the heritage of town.”  
• 2019 Woodland Park Community Placemaking Plan tells of enhancing the town 
through heritage that celebrates our mountain western character.  heritage.  
• Woodland Park Municipal Code Chapter 2.44.010 Local Historical Preservation, 
Protection and Landmark Designation: “Woodland Park is to promote the cultural 
well-being of community by way of the protection and preservation of the City’s 
historical and cultural heritage through historic landmark designation.” He reiterated 
that the City is tasked with preserving the City’s historical and cultural heritage 
through historic landmark designations.  
• Woodland Park’s Main Street Facebook page explicitly states that they will   engage 
the community in historic preservation, downtown revitalization and economic 
development by promoting a strong business climate.  
• Ordinance 607 Series 1994: The City will encourage and establish educational 
opportunities to increase the appreciation and understanding of the unique heritage of 
the City.  
• Woodland Park Downtown Development Authority (DDA): One of the DDA’s goals 
is to work together with the HPC. Part of the DDA mission is revitalization, 
enhancing the downtown district, prevent property deterioration and blight, and to 
improve the appearance, condition, and function of the district.

All those mission statements and organizations that Woodland Park belongs to work together 
with the Woodland Park HPC and support downtown revitalization while preserving the 
City’s historic character. Andrew Nguyen was doing exactly what the City wanted him to do 
(all the above philosophies, departments, memberships, plans, ordinances, etc.) when the 
City issued a notice to vacate. To reject this application would be at odds with the above 
mission statements, ordinances, plans, and documents.  Woodland Park should designate this 
building as a historic landmark based on the above documents. To reject this application 
would be at odds with the many above values of the City of Woodland Park. He doubts that 
Woodland Park simply belongs to these organizations to increase their pool of grant 
resources, not caring at all about historic preservation. He is sure Woodland Park would not 
stoop to that level, and that the City does have historic preservation in mind.  
Mr. Plutt noted in the last years, the owner, Andrew Nguyen, did not intentionally disregard 
the City and County concerns as during this time there were financial setbacks and several 
deaths, including of the matriarch. He explained that Mr. Nguyen is a first generation 
American whose family emigrated from Vietnam. In the Vietnamese culture, a death in the 
family puts everything on hold, so Andrew could not respond as the Vos Building became 
secondary to taking care of his family.  
Andrew Nguyen, the property owner, introduced himself. He thanked the HPC for hearing 
the case, and apologized for the past confusion. He bought the building in Woodland Park to 
expand his family’s restaurant business (Saigon Grill in Old Colorado City). He was raised 
with his two siblings by their single mother, working in the restaurant every day. It was hard 
to find trustworthy employees. The family wanted to move to Woodland Park for the history, 
culture, and a place to call home. He still believes Woodland Park is a good place to build a 
business and live the American dream. In 2019, he secured the financing to buy the Vos 
Building, but his grandmother passed away. She was the bedrock, the compass, and the 
passion behind his mother and family, inspiring and guiding them in the restaurant business.  
At the same time, Mr. Nguyen had an obligation to fulfill the promise he made to Abram 
Edwards, the seller of the Vos Building. He promised Mr. Edwards he would make 
something beautiful out of the building.  
Meanwhile, Mr. Nguyen’s mother believed in the reciprocal support of family, friends, and 
the community. Unfortunately, they chose a long-time, 15-year customer as the contractor, 
believing they were giving him an opportunity to grow his business. Instead, the contractor 
stole over $270,000. Then the Covid-19 pandemic started. Mr. Nguyen expressed that these 
were unprecedented times of loss: family deaths, money stolen, and the world shutting down. 
The pandemic was one of the reasons why the contractor was able to hide how much he was 
stealing. The Nguyen family tried to recapture some of the stolen funds in court, but since it 
was the contractor’s first business, he already filed for bankruptcy, and Mr. Nguyen would 
not receive any reimbursement. He appealed to the HPC that his family, like theirs, is strong, 
and if hardship comes your way, you pick yourself up and move forward. Unfortunately, 
after his grandmother died, his mother and then his stepfather also died. During these years, it 
was impossible to do anything quickly, navigating the turmoil was difficult.  
Mr. Nguyen knows the concerns the City has and position the City is in, and fully 
understands what is needed to improve and preserve the building. He is determined to get the

building back to its former glory. He asked the HPC to designate the Vos Building as a Local 
Historic Landmark. 
Chair Glauth thanked Mr. Nguyen for his explanation of the situation. On behalf of the HPC, 
she expressed gratitude for his commitment to the community and to the history of this 
particular property. She acknowledged the challenges as a private property owner. 
Unfortunately, this building’s location makes it extremely visible, which has contributed to 
bringing on some of the sanctions by the City and DDA, and noted that this building is not 
the only building in Woodland Park that needs attention. She thanked Mr. Nguyen for his 
Local Historic Landmark application. 
Chair Glauth opened the public comment period.  
Steve Plutt added that if this nomination is accepted, it will open the doors for the State 
Historic Fund grants. Mr. Nguyen needs these grants (after being bilked out of $270k) to help 
pay for the necessary repairs of the building.  
Mike Bryant, Board Member of the Ute Pass Historical Society, stated that the UPHS fully 
supports the restoration and the Local Landmark Designation of the Vos Building. 
Chair Glauth closed the public comment period. 
Mr. Black and Mr. Simonson expressed they were in favor of the nomination. Chair Glauth 
went on the record as that the HPC has been supportive of this LHL designation for quite a 
while, it has been on the HPC agenda for months, the HPC has wanted to see this extremely 
unique building preserved. The HPC has watched the evolution of Mr. Nguyen and his 
family through this process to today. The HPC believes the LHL designation offers 
advantages and opportunity. She added, “When things are gone, they’re gone.” There is no 
recovering a historic building once it’s gone. Mr. Plutt showed the City’s own commitment 
to historic preservation through various long-range plans and documents. Woodland Park has 
few buildings left, and this is an opportunity to preserve some of Woodland Park’s history.   
MOTION: Mr. Black moved and Mr. Simonson seconded to recommend that City Council 
APPROVE the application of Local Historic Landmark Designation for 209 E. Midland 
(Vos Building) (26-0099): designate 209 E. Midland (the Vos Building) a Local Historic 
Landmark.  
Mr. Black commented that the vinyl windows and addition are not an issue. He’d like to see 
it as a welcome center to Woodland Park. This building has the character and design of no 
other building in the City or State. He can still see Mrs. Vos coming outside and welcoming 
guests in her shop. When asked if she had something, she’d say, “Give me a couple minutes 
and I will find it,” which she did. She was a very pleasant human being, and significantly 
contributed to Woodland Park.
