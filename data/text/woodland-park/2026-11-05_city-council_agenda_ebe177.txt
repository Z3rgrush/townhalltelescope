Key to agenda abbreviations:  
 
(A) Administrative- matters involving day-to-day decisions such as approving contracts, hiring 
staff and the procurement of goods and services. Administrative actions generally do not 
require formal actions by the elected body. 
 
(L)Legislative-  typically in the policy arena; legislative matters affect large areas and large 
groups of people, such as enacting dog regulations or amending the City code. Legislative 
action generally involves motions, resolutions and ordinances. 
 
(QJ)Quasi-Judicial- apply general rules to a specific interest, such as zoning change affecting 
a single piece of property, or a special use permit. Quasi-Judicial actions generally involve 
adjudication, sometimes in writing, but not a resolution or ordinance.  Decision for Quasi-
Judicial proceedings are made exclusively based upon the testimony presented on the record.

movement to celebrate and support small, independent businesses for their vital contributions 
to local communities and the national economy; and
WHEREAS, the City of Woodland Park, Colorado, would like to celebrate its local small

of life; and
WHEREAS, small businesses create local jobs, provide essential goods and services, support families, 
and contribute to the vitality of Woodland Park and Teller County; and
WHEREAS, locally owned businesses help to strengthen the local economy by keeping more 
dollars circulating within our community; and

jobs, and preserves the character and vibrancy of our mountain community; and

businesses; and

TYPE OF ACTION REQUESTED:  Approval of contract with Respec in the amount of $685,000.00 
for Glen Aspen Dam - Preliminary Design report and 30% design package. 
 
BACKGROUND:  In Novemeber 2024, Respec and Vivid joined forces to perform the design and 
geotechnical work for the access roads and proposed reservoir dam site investigation.  Respec also 
started the permitting process for the access roads with El Paso and Teller Counties. In 2025 
Respec started design of the access point and the road construction drawings.  In May 2026 HCG 
Construction started construction on the access points.  
 
To date: Vivid has completed the geotech work for the access roads and the preliminary geotech 
investigation for the reservoir dam site.  Respec has completed the access road design for both 
locations and has worked through the permit process with El Paso and Teller Counties.  HCG 
Construction has completed both the primary and secondary access road construction.  
 
This contract with Respec is moving forward with an alternatives analysis/preliminary design, 30% 
design, Teller County permitting and El Paso County permitting.   
 
 
RECOMMENDATION: Approve the contract with Respec for $685,000.00  to complete the Dam 
alternative analysis and 30% design

PROJECT BACKGROUND 
 
The City of Woodland Park, CO would like to construct an off-line reservoir capable of storing Homestake 
Pipeline water diverted from the existing Loy Gulch reservoir for municipal uses.  The proposed site of the 
off-line reservoir is currently located at the Glen Aspen Ranch, which is owned and operated by the Scouts of 
America.  The City had previously engaged with JDS-Hydro Consultants, Inc. to conduct preliminary 
feasibility studies to evaluate the potential of constructing an off-line reservoir in this location.  These 
studies (conducted in 2009 and 2018) were beneficial in determining how much land it would need to 
procure from the Scouts to secure sufficient surface area to facilitate a maximum 345 AF reservoir.  Please 
see Figure 1  for a representation of the proposed dam and reservoir area developed from the 2009 and 
2018 site investigations. 
 
Figure 1. Proposed Glen Aspen Dam and Reservoir   
 
 
 
Page 28 of 69
Scope of Work   / City of Woodland Park - Glen Aspen Dam / Preliminary Design Rep ort  and 30% Design  //   C-2 
In 2025 the City of Woodland Park commissioned RESPEC Company, LLC to conduct a preliminary 
foundation and geotechnical investigation to inform the City of the existing condition of the proposed dam 
footprint.  In addition, the Phase 1 Geotechnical Analysis also included the permitting and design of two 
access roads through the Scouts of America Glen Aspen Ranch site to the proposed dam site. The Phase 1  
Geotechnical Analysis and Access Road designs have been completed, facilitating the next phase of the 
Glen Aspen Dam project.  This next segment of design is to include an alternatives analysis of potential dam 
embankment designs and then 30% design of the selected alternative.  
 
Following the completion of the Phase 1 Geotechnical Analysis, the following main components of the 
proposed dam facility is anticipated to include the following: 
 
1)  Dam foundation – similar to Loy Gulch Dam.  Structural stable, but lacking in water tightness.  Preliminary 
packer tests results yielded seepage rates upwards of 40 gpm at some levels of the foundation.  
Ultimately, the proposed site of the dam embankment features almost 200’ deep of decomposed 
granite.  The proposed dam foundation may feature either a bentonite slurry wall, grout treatment, or 
other method to improve the imperviousness of the Pikes Peak decomposed granite foundation.  The 
foundation treatment methods will be evaluated as part of this Phase 2 Alternatives Analysis. 
2)  Dam Embankment - 90’ tall (maximum section) x 650’ long (along dam crest) earthen dam.  The proposed 
dam will facilitate a normal water surface of Elevation 9165 and feature an embankment volume of 
approximately 326,100 CY.  Preliminarily, the reservoir is planned to include a multi-zoned clay core with 
chimney drain, though may ultimately require embankment lining and grouted curtain wall to mitigate 
seepage through the embankment and under the foundation.  Seepage mitigation alternatives will be 
evaluated as part of the Phase 2 alternatives analysis. 
3)  Offline reservoir - to be filled using Homestake Pipeline water from adjacent Loy Gulch Dam.  Reservoi r 
to store a maximum of 345 AF, depending upon embankment sloping alternatives as evaluated in the 
Phase 2 Alternatives Analysis. 
4)  Emergency spillway - sized to accommodate the appro priate Inflow Design Flood (IDF) according to 
Colorado Dam Safety Branch Rules and Regulations.  Emergency spillway will most likely feature a 
double box culvert alignment due to the relatively small nature of the upstream drainage basin to 
facilitate access to the dam crest from the access road to the north.  Location of emergency spillway will 
most likely be separate from the left groin, contrary to original assumptions.  Final location of spillway will 
be determined in the Phase 2 Alternatives Analysis. 
5)  Primary and secondary permanent access roads - These roads are currently under construction using a 
previously designed set of construction drawings prepared by RESPEC. 
6)  Open channel emergency spillway chute - More than likely will be rip rap lined and open channel to 
reduce clogging potential and remain free flowing according to DSB requirements.  Spillway chute will 
also be located away from the left downstream abutment.  The discharge end of spillway will also include

level emergency outlet pipe will also feature a cast-in-place United States Bureau of Reclamation (USBR) 
outlet dissipation structure to reduce flow energies through the camp.  The low level outlet pipe will also

WHEREAS, the City of Woodland Park, Colorado (the “City”) has been duly organized 
and is validly existing as a Home Rule City under Article XX, Section 6 of the Colorado 
Constitution and the City Charter;  
 
 WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 31-15-401, the City, by and through its City Council 
(the “Council”), possesses the authority to adopt laws and ordinances within its police power in 
furtherance of the public health, safety, and welfare; and 
 
WHEREAS, C.R.S. § 29-20-101, et seq., provides the City with the broad authority to 
plan for and regulate the use of land to best protect and promote the health, safety, and general 
welfare of present and future inhabitants of the City, to guide future growth, development, and 
distribution of land uses within the City, and to issue a temporary moratorium when necessary on 
the acceptance of certain land use applications while engaging in a comprehensive analysis to 
determine appropriate and necessary amendments to its Land Use Code as it relates to a particular 
use within the City; and 
 
WHEREAS, generally, a local government shall not enact or enforce an anti-growth law 
affecting property; and

explicitly limits either the growth of the population in the governmental entity’s jurisdiction or the 
number of development permits or the building permit applications for residential development or 
the residential component of any mixed use development submitted to, reviewed by, approved by, 
or issued by a governmental entity for any calendar or fiscal year” under C.R.S. § 29-20-
104.2(2)(1)(I), (3); and 
 
WHEREAS, however, pursuant to C.R.S. § 29-20-104.2(4)(a)(II), a government entity 
may enact and enforce a temporary, nonrenewable anti-growth law for “the purpose of developing 
or amending land use plans or land uses laws covering residential development”; and  
 
WHEREAS, the City has a commercial zone with some residential development 
component, Central Business District (CBD) zone district, within the City’s boundaries as 
described and depicted in Exhibit A, attached hereto; and
