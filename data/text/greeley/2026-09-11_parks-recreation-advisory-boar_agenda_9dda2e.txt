6. Diana Frick 2027 Budget and triennial Review- 
•  Budget Proposal Presentation and Discussion: 
o Background: Diana presented the budget reduction proposal that was shared with the City 
Council on July 7, 2026. The City of Greeley faces an $18 million deficit in its operating funds. In 
February, the City Council prioritized city programs and assigned the Culture, Parks, and 
Recreation Department (CPRD) a 17% general fund reduction target, amounting to $3.7 million. 
Enterprise funds like Golf and the Cemetery are not part of this exercise. 
o Methodology: The department used a weighted average approach to determine reductions, 
considering community impact, revenue generation, and program usage. This was to protect 
services for vulnerable residents (e.g., Rodarte, Active Adult Center) and account for revenue 
impacts. A flat 17% cut was avoided as it would disproportionately affect revenue-generating 
areas, requiring deeper cuts elsewhere. 
o Proposed Reductions ($2.9 Million Identified): 
 Personnel ($877,000): Elimination of 10 full-time positions (8 filled, 2 unfilled) and 27 
seasonal/part-time positions. Some reductions were achieved through a voluntary 
separation program. 
 
 Operational Impacts: Nearly every program received some level of reduction, except for 
capital planning and facility maintenance. 
 Parks: Reduced service levels for mowing, chemical applications, and irrigation 
repairs. Ballfields will remain fully staffed due to paying users and safety 
expectations, while park practice fields will see reduced service due to a lack of 
revenue.

 Natural Areas & Trails: Elimination of two planner positions, impacting future 
planning capabilities but preserving maintenance teams. 
 Recreation, Youth, & Aquatics: Reduced program offerings and hours. 
 Museums & Culture: Reduced Museum hours at the downtown museum and 
Centennial Village, with a proposal to stagger operating schedules. There will be 
fewer exhibits and city-run events. 
 Community Events (UCCC & Island Grove): Reduced staffing and a greater 
emphasis on revenue-generating events. 
o Revenue Strategies ($600,000): The proposal includes $600,000 in new revenues, informed by a 
recent cost recovery study and other initiatives. 
 Revenue and Program-Specific Discussions: 
 Liquor Service Expansion: A significant portion of the $600,000 in new revenue is 
attributed to expanding city-operated liquor service, proven successful at the 
UCCC, to Island Grove (excluding the Greeley Stampede). 
 Event Venue Trends: Island Grove attendance is estimated at 800,000–900,000 
annually (non-Stampede events account for 500,000–600,000). The Union 
Colony Civic Center has also seen attendance growth. Revenue projections are 
conservative and based on multi-year trends. 
 Athletic Fields & Ice Haus: Staff committed to maintaining service standards for 
outdoor fields despite fee changes, as user feedback indicates Greeley's fields 
are high-quality. The Ice Haus cost recovery model is aligned with market rates. 
 Soccer Program: Transitioning the city-run recreational soccer program to a 
private user group could generate park revenue to maintain fields. Guardrails for 
this partnership include keeping the program recreational, aligning the price 
point with the city's plan (~$60), and ensuring Greeley residents retain access to 
the Youth Assistance Fund. 
 Revenue Right-Sizing: Budgets for FY2027 have been corrected to reflect realistic 
projections, moving away from previously inflated revenue targets (e.g., Active 
Adult Center membership). 
 Fee Sensitivity: The current budget proposal's revenue increases are based on 
trends and new sources, not the full implementation of the cost recovery study. 
Future cost recovery work will be sensitive to community affordability and 
market comparisons. 
o Budget Gap ($800,000): The proposed $2.9 million in reductions leaves an $800,000 gap. Staff 
noted that cutting further would result in "skeleton" programs with severely diminished 
service. 
o Options Presented to Council to Close the Gap: 
 Accelerated Cost Recovery: A multi-year approach to align user fees with market rates 
and community benefit, focusing on larger user agreements. It will not achieve the full 
target in the first year. 
 Museum System Transition: Transitioning, not closing, the museum system to a partner 
(e.g., 501(c)(3), Friends group) to assist with operations, programming, and fundraising. 
The current annual expense is $1.4 million. No partner discussions have occurred 
pending council approval. Council also raised legal questions regarding the city charter 
language to “provide for museums,” and the city legal department is preparing an 
opinion. 
 Natural Areas System Reduction: Eliminating the planning and development division 
would net $250,000 but not close the gap and would create long-term opportunity loss

Press Release 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

Sam Spencer, Coach Shawna Ludy, Aubrey Frezza, and Sam Taylor pose in Boston 
GREELEY, Colo. — This summer, Greeley Ice Haus figure skaters earned top honors at 
state and national competitions. These local athletes showed skill, dedication, focus, and 
determination on the ice. 
National Stage 
Local athletes Aubrey Frezza, Samantha Spencer, and Samantha Taylor traveled to 
Norwood, Mass., July 9-12. They spent a week at the 2026 Excel National Festival, a world-
class competition by the Skating Club of Boston. The girls representing Mountain View 
Skating Club attended with 900 figure skaters from across the country. 
 
Coached by City of Greeley staff, Shawna Ludy and Carla Villavicencio, the three figure 
skaters train at the Greeley Ice Haus. 
 
“Stepping onto the ice alongside top national talent was a major milestone for these 
athletes,” said Maria Taylor, parent of Samantha. “Their hard work paid off with 
outstanding results in their respective divisions.” 
Individual results: 
• Samantha Taylor – 3rd place (Bronze Medal) in the Aspire 3 Division 
• Aubrey Frezza – 4th place in the Pre-Preliminary Excel Division 
• Samantha Spencer – 5th place in the Aspire 1 Division 
State Competition 
The Ice Diamonds took second place at the 2026 National Showcase, a U.S. Figure Skating 
event. The competition was at the South Suburban Sports Complex in Highlands Ranch on 
August 8. Reagan Mitchell, Tala Gardiner, Lucy Lemon, Samantha Tayler, Arbury Frezza, 
and Frida Barrera delivered strong performances. 
The Greeley Ice Haus offers both Learn to Skate Basic Skills and figure skating classes for 
all ages and abilities. The Mountain View Skating Club also practices at the Ice Haus. They 
provide students with opportunities to develop their skills and compete.

RESTORE COLORADO 
In late August, NAT hosted the National Fish and Wildlife Federation 
(NFWF) and funders of the RESTORE Colorado partnership at Arroyos 
del Sol Natural Area for a tour of the site. RESTORE is a multi-partner 
conservation funding program to support ecological restoration 
projects in Colorado, and NAT has received a RESTORE grant in year 
two of implementation on the property. The project consists of 
drone herbicide application in the arroyos on the property to 
address cheatgrass and other invasive species, along with prescribed 
burning and re-seeding the upland portions of the site. The tour was 
attended by about 20 participants and included representatives 
from Colorado Parks and Wildlife, the Walton Family Foundation, 
Oxy Petroleum, and other funding partners.

GENERATION WILD 
August was a busy month for the Greeley Generation Wild coalition, with lots of activities for youth in east Greeley 
to get involved and learn some new outdoor skills. A two-week summer camp was held at East Memorial Park, 
where over 50 young people rotated through different stations, learning new skills and having new experiences 
along the way. They participated in everything from soccer to archery, and fishing to basketball. 
In addition, both high school students and middle school students participated in several outdoor field trips this 
summer with Greeley Dream Team. As part of the college campus overnight trip to University of Colorado, Colorado 
Springs (UCCS), 67 high school students explored Garden of the Gods, which many had never been to before. 
Additionally, 45 middle school students strengthened their communication skills and tackled team challenges while 
exploring CU Boulder's outdoor low ropes course. 
Finally, Voices of the Land hosted a Mexica Dance lesson at Delta Park for youth and their families. This dance lesson 
celebrated both black and indigenous members of the community through movement. All of these activities were 
made possible by the Generation Wild grant NAT has received from Great Outdoors Colorado. 
RESTORATION 
After a hot and dry start to the 2026 growing season, the August 
monsoon weather patterns brought some much needed rain to 
Greeley’s natural areas, which gave late season vegetation a chance 
to pop and provide some additional noxious weed and mechanical 
(mowing) work for the NAT crew. Over 60 hours of chemical 
application and 30 hours of mechanical management were 
completed in the NAT system in August. Efforts focused on List A 
weed species, fire mowing, and general late season weed 
management. Staff is also partnering with a UNC ecology class with 
data collection at several natural areas. Data collected will inform 
management approaches on those sites.
