4. Citizen Input 
¢ Northridge High School bus stop: Public comment was moved earlier in the agenda while 
the board lacked a quorum. Lisa Bernal raised concerns about the removal of the bus stop 
serving Northridge High School, particularly for students participating in extracurricular 
activities. She described the distance to the remaining stop, the prospect of walking after 
dark, and transportation barriers for families. She asked what analysis and school district 
consultation preceded the change and requested board review of the impacts and possible 
alternatives. 
e 14th Avenue bollards: Ms. Bernal also raised concerns about the bollard installations at 
14th Avenue and 4th Street and at 14th Avenue and 8th Street. Her concerns included 
turning movements, vehicle conflicts, effectiveness in reducing speeds, damage, 
appearance, trash accumulation, and snow removal. She requested an evaluation and 
consideration of a safer long-term solution. 
¢ Staff follow-up: Steven Younkin will follow up with Ms. Bernal about the bollard 
installations and planned improvements. Michelle Johnson or Hanna Feldmann will 
follow up regarding the Northridge issue and coordination with District 6. Staff also
proposed bringing the service analysis and mitigation efforts to the board for further 
discussion. 
Old Business: 
CTAB community engagement QR code and form: The board discussed a City-managed form and QR code to collect transportation feedback and requests to provide public comment. Potential locations included buses and community events, with routing through the City website or 311. Michelle Johnson will coordinate with Community Engagement to determine the process and information to collect. Wendi Young volunteered to participate in that work. Michelle will also explore having Community Engagement attend a future meeting. 
National Week Without Driving: Michelle Johnson reported that Gallop partnered with Jim Riesberg and Community Engagement to offer free rides during the event and encourage residents to try the revised service. Promotion included social media, a press release, and the transit website. Staff also reported that the Gallop website had launched. 
SS4A Safety Action Plan: Steven Younkin summarized nearly 800 community engagement touch points through public events, downtown businesses, schools, community organizations, and online maps, with bilingual support. Feedback showed broad support for safer walking and bicycling, better downtown access, and traffic calming. Concerns included parking impacts, bollard appearance and maintenance, chicanes, roundabouts, and access for larger vehicles and emergency equipment. Design remained paused pending federal approval for the next phase, anticipated in mid-October. Construction of quick-build improvements using paint, bollards, and other temporary materials remained planned for 2027, potentially near the end of the first quarter or beginning of the second quarter. Final design will consider signage, alternative materials, maintenance access, and existing snow-removal equipment. A Disabilities Commission 
presentation will conclude the initial outreach phase; further engagement is planned during and after construction. Board members requested earlier notice of outreach opportunities, preferably with calendar invitations and approximately a month of advance notice when possible. They also discussed weeds and maintenance along 59th Avenue and the need to account for maintenance when designing traffic-calming installations. 
CIP Updates: The board reviewed progress on MERGE, Keep Greeley Moving, and related capital improvement projects. 
o MERGE and capital improvement projects: Staff reported progress on the engineer’s opinion of probable cost for the federal portion of MERGE and continued environmental 
coordination with CDOT. The discussion referenced a target of approximately June 2027 for the current milestone. Preliminary design work also exists for the 47th Avenue 
component. The board discussed the US 34 and Weld County Road 17 project, noting that right-of-way needs and any potential acquisition action depend on further design and 
project direction. 
o Keep Greeley Moving: Steven Younkin reported approximately $16.74 million in 
completed 2025 work against an approximately $17 million contract value. Work included approximately 35,000 tons of asphalt, 51 lane miles of asphalt improvements,
more than 9.5 miles of sidewalk rehabilitation, and restoration of 350 ADA ramps. Board 
members suggested adding landmarks or school locations to public maps to make 
completed work easier to understand. Upcoming work was discussed with a planned 
budget of approximately $10.74 million. Projects included Centerplace Drive 
reconstruction, improvements to nearby streets west of 47th Avenue, chip seal on 59th 
Avenue between 1 0th and 20th Streets, and rehabilitation of 23rd Avenue. Centerplace 
pavement improvements will account for anticipated bus traffic at the future mobility 
hub. Staff intend to retain KGM funding primarily for maintenance and rehabilitation and 
to advance 2027 contracting earlier in the year. The board emphasized advance 
communication with businesses, schools, and residents about construction access, traffic 
control, and changing conditions. Staff discussed maintaining access where feasible and 
mitigating neighborhood cut-through traffic during MERGE construction. The anticipated 
November opening of the 29th Street connection and planned traffic calming on that 
corridor were also discussed. 
e Safe Routes to School: Hanna Feldmann summarized engineering assessments at 10 elementary 
schools: Bella Romero, Billy Martinez, Maplewood, Jackson, Meeker, Scott, James Madison, 
Shawsheen, Christa McAuliffe, and Monfort. Work included staff interviews, pickup and drop -off 
observations, crash-data review, infrastructure assessments, and coordination with District 6 staff. 
Recommendations also addressed education, engagement, and enforcement. Potential 
improvements included missing sidewalks, speed feedback signs, crossing enhancements, bicycle 
facilities, improved visibility, and vegetation removal. Some recommendations have been 
incorporated into SS4A. The assessment provides supporting information for future state and 
national grant applications. Board members requested the recommendations, reports, and maps, 
along with more detail on implementation priorities and funding. Members also asked about 
future assessments for secondary schools and possible connections to the Northridge concerns; 
staff noted limited funding and uncertainty about future budgets. 
e CTAB work plan implementation: The board continued implementation planning for its 
adopted Community Engagement and Transportation Feedback Strategy and Mobility Services 
Review and Ongoing Planning goals. Wendi Young will work with Michelle Johnson and 
Community Engagement on the QR code and form. Members discussed having a board 
representative meet regularly with transit planning staff and report back, while maintaining 
CTAB’s advisory role. Staff will coordinate a meeting with the new transit planner; Staff 
described ongoing analysis of ridership, on-time performance, and service needs, including 
January service recommendations, a proposed connection to UCHealth, improved Aims -to-UNC 
connectivity, and possible Poudre Express adjustments to direct resources toward local service. 
Staff noted that current RouteMatch and automatic passenger counter data require validation 
against Genfare data and that planned technology improvements should support better analysis. 
The board revisited Northridge service, discussing ridership, operating costs, delays, equity 
concerns for students living farther from school, and staff concerns about federal school bus and 
charter requirements. Staff offered to bring supporting data and a fuller explanation of the service 
change to a future meeting.
