Hispanic Heritage Month 
WHEREAS, the City of Greeley is a community enriched by the variety of the ethnicities of the 
residents who call Greeley home; and 
WHEREAS, residents who identify as Hispanic comprise nearly 40% of its residents; and 
WHEREAS, the City of Greeley City Council benefits from the service of several of its Hispanic 
residents as employees, volunteers, representatives of City boards and commissions, and as members 
of City Council; and 
WHEREAS, the roots of Hispanic Heritage Month go back to 1968 by recognizing the contributions 
made and the important presence, heritage and culture of Hispanic and Latino Americans to the 
United States; and 
 
WHEREAS, the term Hispanic or Latino refers to Puerto Rican, South or Central American, or 
other Spanish culture or origin regardless of race; and 
 
WHEREAS, Dia De Los Muertos is a significant holiday to this community that follows Hispanic 
Heritage month. 
 
NOW THEREFORE, I, Dale Hall, Mayor, of the City of Greeley, Colorado, do hereby proclaim 
September 15 – October 15, 2026 as Hispanic Heritage Month  and encourage all community 
members to join in celebratory events this month and throughout the year in recognition of the 
contributions of this important culture within our community. 
IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand and caused to be affixed the official seal 
of the City of Greeley, this 6th day of October 2026. 
 
 
 
 
Dale Hall, Mayor

Week Without Driving 
 
WHEREAS, access to mobility is fundamental to health, safety, economic opportunity, and 
community connection, enabling residents of Greeley to reach education, employment, medical 
care, food, recreation, faith communities, and social networks; and  
WHEREAS, nearly one-third of people in the United States do not have a driver’s license due 
to age, disability, income, health conditions, or other barriers, and many others face temporary 
or permanent circumstances that prevent them from driving; and 
WHEREAS, not all neighborhoods in Greeley have safe sidewalks, accessible curb ramps, 
protected bike infrastructure, reliable public transit, or land use patterns that allow daily needs 
to be met within a reasonable distance; and 
 
WHEREAS, expanding safe, accessible options for walking, rolling, biking, and public transit 
supports climate action, cleaner air, and environmental stewardship; and  
 
WHEREAS, communities designed with multiple transportation options support local 
businesses, reduce traffic injuries and fatalities, improve public health, and create vibrant, 
connected neighborhoods; and  
 
WHEREAS, experiencing a week without driving offers residents and leaders an opportunity to 
better understand the barriers faced by nondrivers and to identify practical solutions that 
promote safety, affordability, accessibility, climate resilience, and equitable land use. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Dale Hall, by virtue of authority vested in me as Mayor of the City of 
Greeley, Colorado, do hereby proclaim October 1 - 8, 2026 as Week Without Driving  in 
Greeley.  
 
IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand and caused to be affixed the official 
seal of the City of Greeley, this 6th Day of October 2026.

Fire Prevention Week 
 
WHEREAS, in 1925, President Calvin Coolidge proclaimed Fire Prevention Week a national observance, 
making it the longest-running public health observance in our country; and 
WHEREAS, fire is a serious public safety concern in all communities, including Greeley, causing injury, 
death, and property loss each year; and 
WHEREAS, having working smoke alarms in every sleeping room, outside each separate sleeping area, and on every 
level of the home can significantly reduce the risk of dying in a home fire; and 
WHEREAS, the National Fire Protection Association has designated October 4–10, 2026, as Fire Prevention 
Week, with the theme “Charge into Fire Safety™: Safe Charging Is a Superpower,” encouraging everyone to take 
simple steps to charge and use their devices safely; and 
WHEREAS, residents can reduce the risk of charging-related fires by using the cord and charger that came 
with their device or one approved by the manufacturer, charging devices on hard, flat surfaces, and unplugging 
devices once they are fully charged; and 
WHEREAS, larger items such as e-bikes, scooters, and power tools should be charged outdoors when 
possible and never in a location that blocks an exit; and 
WHEREAS, devices that become unusually hot, swell, smell strange, leak, smoke, or make popping or hissing 
sounds should immediately be disconnected from use, and devices should always be kept within sight while 
charging during air travel; and 
WHEREAS, the Greeley Fire Department is committed to reducing home fires and fire-related injuries in 
our community through prevention, education, and public awareness. 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Dale Hall, by virtue of authority vested in me as Mayor of the City of Greeley, 
Colorado, do hereby proclaim October 4 –10, 2026, as Fire Prevention Week in the City of Greeley, and 
urge all citizens to join in observing this week by ensuring their homes have working smoke alarms and by 
practicing safe charging habits at home, at work, and while traveling. 
IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand and caused to be affixed the official seal of the 
City of Greeley, this 6th day of October 2026. 
 
 
 
Dale Hall, Mayor

7. Comprehensive Financial Policies  
Director of Finance Jones introduced the item at 5:57 p.m. 
 
Mr. Jones presented the proposed Comprehensive Financial Policies (CFP), 
describing the document as the product of several months of work across the finance 
department with input from executive leadership, the budget office, and other city 
stakeholders. 
 
Mr. Jones explained that the CFP consolidated existing financial rules and standards 
currently scattered across the city charter, municipal code, Colorado Revised Statutes, 
Page 22 of 185
City Council Work Sessions Proceeding August 25, 2026-Draft 
 
individual council resolutions, and administrative directives. Three drivers for the 
timing were:  
• certain policies are genuinely dated,  
• growth in Greeley requires updated financial standards, and  
• the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) identifies formal 
financial policy frameworks as a best practice that supports sound bond ratings 
and reduces the city's cost of debt issuance. 
 
The CFP was organized into eleven policy areas:  
• accounting and reporting standards,  
• annual audit,  
• budgeting and financial planning,  
• debt and financing,  
• cash and treasury management,  
• investments,  
• fund balance and reserves,  
• capital assets,  
• revenue administration,  
• grants and donations, and  
• procurement 
 
Mr. Jones highlighted that the document does not override existing legal authority. 
Where the charter, municipal code, or state law govern a subject, the CFP implements 
and clarifies that authority. Where those sources are silent, the CFP sets the city's own 
standards.  
 
He further noted that the vast majority of the CFP's content restates or consolidates 
existing policy, with three new elements:  
• a process for real property acquisitions including written analysis, independent 
appraisal, and title insurance;  
• a governance structure for donations; and  
• indirect cost recovery on grants 
 
Key policy provisions included: 
• Reserves: General Fund and Enterprise 
• Debt: Bond maturities, Balloon repayment, Debt Service, Refinancing and 
Interfund Loans  
• Grants and Donations: Grants exceeding $500,000 or requiring an unbudgeted 
match come to Council for approval 
• Capital Assets: Capitalization threshold, Depreciation, Leases, and Capital 
Improvement Plan   
• Policy Maintenance: The Finance Director will conduct a comprehensive 
annual review timed to the budget process.  
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked whether any conflicting policies were encountered 
during the consolidation process. Mr. Jones confirmed there were no conflicts and 
added that the City Manager will issue the policy with operational responsibility.

conditions, a financial overview through mid-year 2026, and grant summary. 
 
Mr. Miller highlighted several economic conditions:  
• Retail spending  
• inflation  
• unemployment  
• short-term interest rates  
• Economic growth  
 
Key financial highlights in the first six months of 2026 reveal:  
• new construction revenues  
• cash and investments exceed budget  
• Oil and gas revenues are projected to beat the budget 
• sales tax growth lags behind  
• Food tax revenues dropped

the general fund.  
• A cross-fund variance of about $11.7 million above the budget which included 
a $1.8 million positive variance. Most of the variance was tied to restricted 
development impact fees.

9. Long Range Financial Update  
Director of Budget and Policy Nathan Mosley introduced the item at 6:42 p.m. 
 
Mr. Mosley presented the long-range financial projection update, focused specifically 
on the general fund. He outlined the series of fiscal challenges since 2023, including a 
structural deficit caused by economic volatility, unfunded new programs, and rising

4. Resolution approving a Pre-Annexation Agreement between the City of Greeley 
and GlobalAI (Colorado) LLC  
Senior Strategic Advisor John Hall introduced the item at 6:01 p.m. 
 
Mr. Hall prefaced the discussion by establishing an important boundary around the 
scope of the evening's action: the pre-annexation agreement does not annex the 
property, does not establish zoning, and does not approve a development plan or site 
plan. Rather, it established a contractual framework giving the City and GlobalAI 
(Colorado) LLC approximately 18 months to work through potential annexation and 
the related issues that will need to be resolved if annexation ultimately moves 
forward, with a target completion deadline of March 31, 2028. 
 
Mr. Hall provided background on the subject property, located in unincorporated 
Weld County east of Windsor and adjacent to the Great Western Industrial Park 
annexation — historically associated with the Carestream campus. He noted the 
property's unusual, decades-long extraterritorial water service relationship with the 
City of Greeley, with the current arrangement set to expire in March 2027. Rather 
than creating another long-term extraterritorial water agreement, the proposed pre-
annexation agreement connects continued water service to a defined process leading 
toward potential annexation. 
 
Mr. Hall outlined the principal terms of the agreement: an annexation timeline with a 
deadline of March 31, 2028; conditioned continued water service contingent upon 
annexation, with GlobalAI assuming responsibility for infrastructure necessary to 
transition to direct city service; a framework for future infrastructure and utility 
Page 39 of 185

discussions; and preservation of the City's full regulatory authority, including the 
ability to apply generally applicable development regulations and fees. Mr. Hall noted 
that this agreement was intended to lead to negotiation of a more comprehensive 
annexation agreement, and that subsequent proceedings — including actual 
annexation, zoning, and site plan and development review — would each address 
their own distinct questions and requirements. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked about water use and cooling systems. He stated 
that the current understanding was that GlobalAI would be permitted to use no more 
water than the site historically used and asked about open-cell evaporative cooling

Chief of Police Adam Turk introduced the item at 6:42 p.m.  
 
Chief Turk presented alongside Commander Tanya Gutierrez, with Sergeant Greg 
Ryan and Police Legal Adviser Sean Reinhart also in attendance. Chief Turk 
stated the purpose of the presentation was to provide an overview of the 
department's use of automated license plate readers (ALPR) and its drone 
programs, in response to a Council initiative requesting more information. 
 
Chief Turk provided a timeline of the Real Time Information Center (RTIC) 
development, beginning in 2023 with the formation of a project team to research 
best practices. He noted that a May 2023 Council work session addressed noise 
enforcement, red light cameras, and speed cameras.  
 
In March 2024, Council appropriated $2.3M from the 0.16 public safety tax fund 
for RTIC startup and operational costs. A Department of Justice grant received in 
September 2024 covered two years of the software platform. A contract with 
Flock Safety for license plate readers and the Raven gunshot detection system 
were subsequently executed using savings from the RTIC remodel. 
 
ALPR Technology and Use 
Commander Gutierrez described the department's use of ALPRs. She said ALPR 
data were used exclusively for law enforcement purposes and that all searches 
require a valid case number, offense type, and reason, with every action logged 
and audited. A total of 94 employees had access to the Flock system, 82 of whom 
were active users including sworn and non-sworn staff.

Prohibited uses include immigration enforcement, general traffic enforcement, 
personal use, and searches based solely on a protected class. Data was retained for 
30 days and never sold to third parties. 
 
Ms. Gutierrez noted that over 4.5 years, ALPR technology has assisted in serious 
felony cases including homicides, kidnappings, arson, and robberies, resulting in 
19 arrests and 31 arrests of stolen vehicles, and there was a 58% decrease in

Flock Raven Gunshot Detection 
Commander Gutierrez reported that the department operates two Flock Raven 
gunshot detection areas, each approximately a quarter mile wide. She said the data 
gathered since the system went live in November 2025 does not support renewal 
of the program, and that recent sensor damage has reduced coverage to a single 
area. She confirmed the department has never had the ability to capture voices or 
audio through the Raven devices. 
 
Current Issues with Flock Safety 
Chief Turk addressed national attention on Flock Safety, noting major themes of 
data sharing, privacy, and local control. He stated that Flock has publicly said 
customers control their data sharing relationships, with exceptions for legal 
process. He confirmed that no federal agents have access to the system under any 
circumstances. He noted that other vendors exist in the market who store data 
solely on local servers and cannot access it even under court order, in contrast to 
Flock's model. 
 
Drone Program 
Ms. Gutierrez described the department's Skydio drone program, including four 
docked Drones as First Responders (DFR) located at Fire Stations 1, 2, 4, and 5,

90 seconds for calls within a 1.5-mile radius.  
 
Since the program went live on March 27 of the current year, over 1,600 drone 
flights have been documented, averaging approximately 10 per day. DFRs were 
first on scene for 1,055 instances, representing 74.6& of approved DFR calls for 
service, and have assisted in 201 arrests. Chief Turk has been providing biweekly 
DFR updates to Council.

Title: 
Resolution of the City of Greeley Council declaring support for the November 3, 2026 Greeley Ballot 
Question 2F, concerning 0.60% Sales and Use Tax Rate increase to fund Capital Projects 
 
Summary: 
On November 3, 2026, Greeley voters will consider Ballot Measure 2F, a proposed 0.6% sales and use 
tax increase to provide dedicated funding for identified community capital improvement projects.  If 
approved, the tax would equate to 60 cents for every $100 spent on taxable purchases. The tax would not 
apply to purchases of groceries that are subject to the City's food tax. The City estimates the tax would 
generate $20 million in the first year. 
 
The City of Greeley is experiencing sustained residential growth, bringing new residents, housing 
demand, and corresponding needs for public infrastructure and municipal services. While residential 
development is an important part of a healthy community, it does not by itself generate the level of 
ongoing revenue needed to maintain and expand the services, facilities, and amenities residents expect. 
The City Council is committed to supporting a balanced and growing tax base, including commercial, 
retail, industrial, and employment-generating development. Without an adequate tax base, the City faces 
increasing service demands without proportionate revenue to meet them.   
 
The current capital improvement projects, including the Catalyst project — a master planned 
entertainment district, and the Civic Campus project — a revitalization of the downtown area, are 
designed to spur economic development and diversify the City’s tax base. These capital improvement 
projects support the maintenance and improvement of public assets such as buildings, trails, outdoor 
spaces, and community facilities. Without a new funding source for these projects, existing City funds 
may not be sufficient to support these other important City priorities. When capital funding is limited, 
the City may need to delay planned projects, maintenance, or repairs. This may increase future repair 
costs and may create service or safety concerns over time.  
 
The City Council supports Ballot Question 2F and urges Greeley residents to vote Yes on November 
3rd. 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
The Resolution demonstrates Council's support for a proposed tax measure. The proposed tax is 
estimated to generate $20 million dollars in the first year.
