4. Citizen Comment: Megan Weiler read the citizen comment received via email into 
the record for the board.  
 
Chair Embry and Members of the Museum Advisory Board, 
 
My name is Geoffrey Havens. I served as the City’s Historic Sites Supervisor until 
May 18, 2026. 
 
This is realistically the last regular meeting at which this Board can meaningfully 
advise City Council before the September budget retreat. So I want to be direct. 
You have asked the right questions. The evidence now shows why those questions 
mattered. And the Department still has not answered several of the most important 
ones. 
 
Records I obtained through a Colorado Open Records Act request show that in 
April, after museum leadership had reached its assigned reduction target, 
department leadership directed additional staffing reductions, including 
elimination of part-time positions and another full-time position. 
 
The Museum Manager objected in writing at the time. His concern was that staffing 
outcomes were being determined first and the operating model redesigned around 
them afterward, rather than first determining what staffing was necessary to 
responsibly operate the museum system. 
 
That concern was not invented after the outcome was known. It was documented 
while the budget was being built. 
 
The CORA records also show museum leadership warning Budget that projected 
revenues did not reflect what a dramatically reduced workforce could actually earn. 
By June, while the proposal was still being reconciled, the Museum Manager wrote 
that he had not participated in developing the proposal then moving forward, that 
the museum team’s alternative had been rejected, and that he had been kept away 
from those discussions. 
 
I separately put the City Manager on notice of these concerns in writing on May 18 
and directed him to the existing museum-management record. He later personally 
confirmed to me that he had received that communication. Ten days later, the 
Museum Manager independently raised many of the same concerns directly with 
him. 
 
Then this Board asked its own questions. 
In July, you unanimously requested an operating plan and information about 
CPRD’s organizational structure and administrative growth. You had previously 
delayed action so that you could wait for additional information. You did what an 
advisory board is supposed to do: you asked, you waited, and you gave the process 
an opportunity to answer you. 
 
You now have an operations analysis prepared at your request. It concludes that 
under the proposed CPRD structure, services previously identified as Guaranteed 
Core become conditional or undeliverable, major functions depend on single 
employees, and the museum system operates with effectively no staffing 
redundancy. 
 
History Colorado has independently warned Council that the proposed staffing is 
below professional standards for a museum of Greeley’s holdings. 
And yet the central questions remain unanswered: 
Why were deeper staffing cuts directed after the museum team had reached its 
target? 
 
Why was the professional alternative not completed, reconciled, and presented to 
Council alongside the Department proposal? 
 
Why was the Museum Manager increasingly excluded from development of a 
structure he would have been responsible for operating? 
 
And why does the Department appear prepared to continue substantially the same 
course now that its own operational analysis documents that core museum 
services cannot reliably be delivered under it? 
 
Your bylaws do not make you spectators. They make you representatives of 
Greeley’s citizens, responsible for advising Council and specifically for aiding in 
establishing priorities in the budget process. 
 
You have tried to perform that responsibility. 
 
At this point, I do not believe the problem is that the Department has failed to hear 
your questions. 
 
It has heard them. 
 
The problem is that the answers have not materially changed the course being 
taken. 
 
If, after reviewing the record before you, you conclude that the proposed model is 
not operationally credible, that material professional concerns were not adequately 
presented to Council, or that your own advice has not been meaningfully 
incorporated, then say so plainly and act accordingly. 
 
Council should not enter its September budget retreat under the impression that 
this is simply a difficult but workable reduction if its own appointed Museum 
Advisory Board concludes that the proposed model is not operationally credible 
and that its concerns have not been meaningfully addressed. 
 
If that is your conclusion, Council needs to hear it clearly now. 
 
This is the moment when your advice can still matter. 
 
Please use it. 
 
Respectfully, 
Geoffrey Havens 
 Former Historic Sites Supervisor, Ward 3 Resident

• Ex-Officio Board Seat:  
The board addressed the status of the ninth board member designated for a 
representative from the Friends of the Greeley Museums. 
Discussion: 
o Bylaws state the ninth member shall be the president of the Friends of 
the Greeley Museums or their appointed representative. This contradicts 
a previous communication suggesting the position was not in the 
municipal code. 
o The current President of the Friends, Patty Harrell, was identified as the 
rightful ninth member. 
o A discrepancy arose regarding whether this member would be a voting or 
non-voting (ex officio) member. The bylaws do not specify a non-voting 
status. Historical precedent from 2021–2022 was cited, during which the 
Friends representative was a voting member. 
Action: Finn, will consult the City Attorney’s office for a legal opinion on the 
voting status of the Friends representative but will log the position as a voting 
member for now, pending clarification.
