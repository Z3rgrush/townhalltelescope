KEEP GREELEY MOVING 
Roch Labossiere provided an update to the committee on the Keep Greeley Moving program, including results from 
the 2025 pavement and concrete work and the proposed 2026 work plan. The 2025 contracts were completed at an 
estimated final cost of approximately $16.74 million, which was approximately 5.8% below the combined contract 
value. About $10 million supported pavement work and approximately $6.6 million supported concrete work. Roch 
emphasized that the savings reflected efficient project delivery and did not represent a reduction in the planned 
scope of work. 
The 2025 pavement program used approximately 35,000 tons of asphalt and treated roughly 51 miles of roadway. 
The concrete program addressed approximately 9.59 miles and included numerous accessible concrete-ramp 
installations. Roch explained that each road receives a treatment appropriate to its individual condition and role in 
the transportation system. Selection decisions consider roadway condition, type and classification of the roadway, 
traffic volumes and level-of-service needs, visible distress such as cracking, the projected consequences of deferred 
maintenance, lifecycle costs, available funding, and opportunities to coordinate work with other City projects. The 
intent is to preserve streets before deficiencies become more extensive and substantially more expensive to repair.

For 2026, approximately $10.7 million is planned for roadway work covering an estimated 25.9 miles. The plan 
includes approximately 14.4 miles of surface treatments, with the balance of funding supporting overlays and 
reconstruction. It was clarified that annual lane miles are not the sole indicator of program value because each 
treatment type has a different cost, purpose, and expected service life. Four bids had been received for the 
upcoming contract, and award of the contract was still pending. 
The overall Keep Greeley Moving budget was approximately $23 million. Approximately $10.7 million was planned 
for annual roadway work, while approximately $12.8 million was available for other high-priority infrastructure needs. 
These investments include Centerplace improvements, downtown revitalization work, road-surface treatments, 
sidewalk projects, and missing sidewalk connections. One identified sidewalk project is planned along 4th Street 
and 23rd Avenue between A Street and 6th Street. The program is intended to balance preventive preservation, 
reconstruction, pedestrian improvements, and Council-established infrastructure priorities. 
The committee also received background on the program’s dedicated revenue source. Keep Greeley Moving is 
funded through a 0.65% sales tax on eligible purchases, excluding food purchases, for roadway and infrastructure 
replacement and repair. The tax is scheduled to sunset at the end of 2029, and the City anticipates seeking voter 
renewal in November 2028. The importance of tracking expenditures, completed projects, and public outcomes so 
the City can communicate clearly how the revenue has been used was stressed. 
Members asked about the reduction in annual pavement and concrete spending from 2025 to 2026. Roch explained 
that the program maintains fund balance to manage large annual contracts and provide flexibility for targeted 
infrastructure investments. Centerplace was identified as a particularly important investment because it is expected 
to carry increased traffic during construction phases associated with the Merge project and related interchange 
work. The fact that the tax supports citywide roadway repair and replacement while allowing the City to direct 
resources toward critical infrastructure needs was reiterated. 
FOOD TAX UPDATE PRESENTATION 
Kalen Myers gave the committee its annual update on the Food Tax Fund. The committee serves as the citizen 
advisory body for the Food Tax and is responsible for reviewing expenditures, raising questions or concerns, and 
providing recommendations when appropriate.  
The food tax is a 3% tax on food purchased for home consumption, such as grocery items. It is separate from the 
City’s general 3% sales tax. In 1990, Greeley voters repealed the local exemption that prevented food from being 
taxed, allowing the City to impose the tax. The tax was renewed every five years until voters approved it 
permanently in 2024. Food tax revenue is dedicated to capital improvement purposes.  
Food tax revenue is approximately $12 million annually, although collections vary according to taxable food sales 
and economic conditions. The fund is an important source of capital maintenance and replacement funding for the 
City. Unlike Keep Greeley Moving, which is focused on streets, or development impact fees, which generally support 
infrastructure needed for new growth, food tax revenue can be used to address a broad range of existing City 
infrastructure needs including the acquisition, construction, improvement, maintenance, and repair of streets, parks, 
recreation facilities, and public buildings. 
Over the previous decade, food tax revenue generally increased because of population growth and inflation. 
Average annual revenue during the reviewed period was approximately $9.4 million, with recent collections reaching 
approximately $11 million to $12 million annually. However, 2024 and 2025 revenues were close to one another, and 
2026 collections were projected to be approximately 1% below 2025. This trend was consistent with broader sales-
tax performance and would continue to be monitored, but it was not considered an immediate concern. 
Food tax expenditures vary from year to year because capital projects often span multiple years and expenditures 
occur at different points in project design, procurement, and construction. The fund maintains a separate fund 
balance to help support major projects and manage uneven annual spending. 
Over the prior five years, approximately $46 million in food tax revenue was spent on capital projects. Approximately 
$14 million, or 31%, supported safe and accessible City buildings, including emergency facility repairs, HVAC 
replacements, roof replacements, flooring, and other building-system needs. Approximately one-quarter of historical 
spending supported roads, although this share is expected to decline because the City has not transferred food tax 
revenue to roads since 2022, after Keep Greeley Moving became the primary dedicated roadway funding source. 
Approximately $8 million supported parks, including irrigation systems, playground maintenance, shelters, and 
restroom improvements. Additional funding supported recreation amenities, including the Senior Center, Recreation 
Center, Rodarte Center, and Ice Haus, as well as trail maintenance. Kalen noted that these investments may not 
always be highly visible, but they address important safety, access, and facility-function needs that become 
apparent when maintenance is deferred. 
The 2027 budget and five-year Capital Improvement Plan include approximately $4.5 million annually for capital 
maintenance needs, including facilities, parks, roofs, pools, and similar infrastructure. However, annual maintenance 
funding was reduced by 25% over the next three years to accommodate certificate of participation repayment 
obligations associated with major capital projects. Kalen described deferred maintenance as an ongoing concern 
and indicated that grants, additional revenue, or project savings would be directed toward restoring maintenance 
funding whenever possible. 
Major planned food tax investments include a $7 million City contribution to the Greeley-Weld County Airport’s 
Wingway Road development. The project is being completed in partnership with Weld County and includes Federal 
Aviation Administration grant funding. It was identified as a Council priority. The 2027 budget also includes high-
priority HVAC replacements at the Recreation Center and Active Adult Center. 
Food tax revenues are planned to support several debt-related commitments. Approximately $9 million over the five-
year period is planned for West Greeley certificate of participation repayments beginning in 2027. The repayment 
obligation is expected to shift in later years to share costs with the Quality-of-Life Fund. Civic Campus certificate of 
participation repayments are expected to increase beginning in 2029 and continue through 2031 at approximately 
$4.3 million annually. Because Civic Campus financing includes deferred interest, future payments will become 
substantially higher, and the City is planning for the obligation through the food tax fund balance. 
The City is also preparing for future Merge Transportation Project TIFIA loan repayments expected to begin around 
2030. The financial planning model used an interest-rate assumption of approximately 4.75%. Although the City may 
be eligible for a 50% rate discount, Kalen stated that the discount was no longer considered guaranteed under 
federal guidance. The City is therefore using a conservative Treasury-rate-based assumption while continuing to 
pursue the potential discount through federal advocacy. 
The food tax program includes an income-qualified rebate program. Eligible households may receive a rebate of 
$100 per person to offset food tax paid. Kalen reported that use of the program has increased each year and that 
funding is reserved for eligible participants. 
The committee discussed the pending ballot measure for a 0.6% sales-tax increase. If approved, the new revenue 
could support several capital categories currently funded through food tax. It could remove the West Greeley 
certificate of participation repayment from the food tax fund and potentially shift all or part of the Civic Campus 
repayment obligation as well. This would reduce pressure on food tax revenues and could allow the City to restore 
additional capital maintenance and replacement funding. The City’s current financial planning, however, does not 
assume that the ballot measure will pass. 
Members expressed concern about the timing and process through which the ballot measure advanced. Members 
stated that the committee was not afforded a meaningful opportunity to provide input before the proposal moved 
forward, despite an earlier expectation that a measure would not be pursued. Kalen acknowledged that the process 
was unusual and that several stakeholders were surprised by the timing. Members also expressed uncertainty about 
voter reception and noted that polling suggested the measure could face challenges. The committee did not take 
formal action, but it clearly documented concern about its opportunity to participate in the process. 
CBAC BUDGET RECOMMENDATION STATEMENT 
The committee reviewed the draft Citizen Budget Advisory Committee budget-recommendation statement included 
in the meeting packet. The purpose of the review was to confirm that the statement accurately represented the 
committee’s earlier discussion and informal email approval. Nathan Mosley would prepare a formal memorandum 
and send it to members for a final email vote. The final vote would then be included in the official meeting record. 
The committee discussed presenting the statement to Council during an upcoming work session. Members 
preferred the October 13 work session rather than a later meeting because it would occur before the second budget 
reading. Members indicated that the draft statement was acceptable as written, subject to any final edits identified 
before the formal email vote. 
The committee asked about recent budget adjustments reported publicly, including an additional $150,000 for 
Culture, Parks and Recreation Department services. Kalen explained that the City Manager’s Office made several 
adjustments between the summer budget work sessions and the Council retreat. These included an additional $3 
million for capital improvement projects funded through the Public Improvement Fund and one-time funding of 
$150,000 for part-time and seasonal staffing supporting natural areas, trails, and museums. The staffing support 
responded to public feedback regarding service reductions in those areas. 
Kalen also explained that the budget included a recommended 2% non-base-building employee payment to assist 
with health insurance costs. This payment was not a permanent base-pay increase. Members noted that the 
changes reflected responsiveness to public feedback and Council interest. The committee agreed to move the 
budget-recommendation statement forward for formal approval. 
AI/DATA CENTER 
The committee discussed whether it should receive financial information concerning the proposed data center 
project. Kalen stated that the project remained in preliminary stages, with negotiations and related discussions still 
underway. A formal financial presentation was not available at that time, but the group agreed that a future 
presentation to the committee would be appropriate once project details become more defined. 
Kalen indicated that the project could create substantial revenue opportunities for the City, particularly through use-
tax revenue associated with the technology refresh cycle. Because data-center equipment may need to be replaced 
or modernized every five to seven years, the City could receive use-tax revenue repeatedly as new equipment is 
installed. However, the specific fiscal effects remain uncertain until negotiations and project details are finalized. 
The committee recognized that the project is controversial and that public education will be important. Members 
noted that the community will likely evaluate the project in part based on potential public benefits, including revenue 
generation and the City’s intended use of new revenue. Kalen agreed that the committee could serve as an 
important audience for a financial and policy discussion once sufficient information is available. 
DIRECTOR’S UPDATES 
Kalen reported that the City Council budget retreat was productive. The City Manager and budget staff presented 
information to Council, including the committee’s budget-recommendation statement. The committee’s feedback 
was described as helpful in setting the tone for Council’s discussion and demonstrating that the committee had been 
actively engaged in the budget process. 
Council did not request substantive changes to the financial assumptions or budget figures during the retreat. Given 
the difficult budget cycle, anticipated reductions, and challenging policy discussions, the budget staff viewed the lack 
of major requested revisions as a positive outcome. Final adoption remained subject to the first and second 
readings of the budget, but the retreat provided Council an opportunity to identify concerns or request changes 
before those readings. 
Kalen announced that two new budget analysts had joined the budget team. The additional capacity was described 
as beneficial to the City’s financial planning, budget analysis, and public-engagement work. 
The committee also discussed the budget roadshow presentations being conducted throughout the community. 
Nathan planned approximately 10 presentations to provide information about the 2027 budget, answer questions, 
correct misconceptions, and provide context beyond what may be available through news coverage or public 
comment alone. It was reported that the presentations had generated constructive questions and productive 
dialogue rather than highly confrontational feedback. 
Members noted that direct public engagement can strengthen confidence and trust by helping residents understand 
the work behind budget decisions and providing visible City contacts. The committee viewed the roadshow effort as 
a valuable result of the budget cycle. 
AGENDA PLANNING 3-MONTH LOOK AHEAD 
The committee reviewed potential agenda items and meeting priorities for the next several months. The next 
meeting was expected to include a Treasurer’s update, continued work on committee governance documents, a 
discussion of the 2027 budget after Council action, and consideration of additional projects or issues requiring 
committee attention. Members also discussed whether a December meeting would be needed, recognizing that 
quorum has historically been difficult to achieve during that month. 
CBAC Bylaws and Ordinance Drafts Review 
The committee discussed the continued development of formal bylaws and a related ordinance amendment. The 
bylaws are intended to clarify quorum requirements, membership considerations, governance procedures, and other 
operational matters. Laura anticipated that the ordinance and bylaws package could potentially be presented to 
Council in December after legal review and final documentation are complete.  Laura is awaiting final clarification 
from the City Attorney’s Office regarding one remaining issue before completing the documents. The proposed 
ordinance is necessary because the City Code must be amended to allow the committee to establish membership-
composition preferences. The intended membership preferences are not mandatory requirements, but would 
provide guidance for Council when considering candidate appointments, including a preference for broad 
geographic representation across Greeley to ensure that the committee represents the community as a whole rather 
than being concentrated in only one or two wards.  
Project Updates 
The committee discussed agenda priorities for the October and November meetings. Members requested updates 
on matters that could affect the City’s financial outlook, capital planning, or the committee’s work. The committee 
agreed that future agendas should remain flexible enough to address significant developments while avoiding 
unnecessary meetings. 
The committee considered whether to hold a December meeting. The preliminary consensus was not to schedule a 
December meeting unless a clear need emerges, at which time a meeting may be added. 
2027 Goals/Objectives Preliminary Discussion 
The committee discussed having a preliminary conversation about its 2027 goals and objectives at the next meeting 
in October. The purpose is to identify the committee’s priorities, desired work plan, and key areas of focus for the 
coming year. Members would be asked to consider the role they want the committee to play in budget oversight, 
public communication, capital planning, financial education, and other issues that may arise during the upcoming 
budget cycle to help create a clearer and more proactive committee work plan rather than relying solely on agenda 
items that arise during the year. The committee recognized that identifying goals early will help budget staff prepare 
appropriate presentations, schedule relevant discussions, and ensure the committee’s advisory role is aligned with 
City budget and policy timelines. 
2027 Budget Recap 
The committee discussed conducting a high-level review of the 2027 budget after Council’s budget readings to 
understand where the budget ultimately lands, identify any significant changes made through the Council process, 
and determine whether there are outstanding matters that require committee attention. 
Members discussed the timing of the recap and whether it would be more useful after first reading or after second 
reading. The committee concluded that a discussion after second reading would provide the most complete picture. 
The recap is intended to focus on major budget outcomes, material amendments, unresolved issues, and responses 
to significant questions raised during the budget cycle rather than repeat prior presentations. 
CBAC Communication Subcommittee Update 
The Communications Subcommittee provided an update on its work with Nathan and C&E staff, particularly 
concerning the budget roadshow and public information resources. Members reported that the roadshow 
presentations have created productive opportunities to engage residents, answer questions, and provide accurate 
information directly from City sources. 
The subcommittee encouraged members to review the Speak Up Greeley webpage, particularly the section 
addressing the 2027 budget, and to direct residents to the webpage when questions arise so the public may access 
reliable information that provides more depth and context than news headlines or informal discussion. 
It was noted that referring residents to official resources can reduce the burden on individual staff members while 
increasing public understanding of the City’s budget process. The group agreed that the roadshow and website 
efforts strengthen communication, improve access to information, and help establish the City as a more accessible 
source of information for residents. 
The subcommittee discussed its future meeting schedule and considered whether meetings should occur bimonthly 
or quarterly. Members generally supported a quarterly schedule, while retaining the flexibility to add meetings if 
communication needs or emerging issues require more immediate attention. It was pointed out that the budget 
roadshow and website have been substantial fall projects that continue to produce useful results. 
GlobalAI 
The committee requested that GlobalAI be considered as a potential agenda item for a future meeting, likely once 
sufficient information is available to support a meaningful presentation. Members recognized that the project is 
developing quickly but that negotiations, financial analysis, and project details may need additional time before a 
substantive public discussion can occur. 
The committee also discussed the relationship between the project and the City’s broader financial outlook. 
Members noted that the pending ballot measure could affect future budget amendments and funding decisions. If 
the ballot measure passes, the City may need to discuss resulting changes in revenue assumptions and capital 
commitments. If the measure does not pass, the City will continue under its existing revenue assumptions and 
budget constraints. Members agreed that the committee should remain prepared for either a brief update or a more 
comprehensive GlobalAI presentation as project information becomes available.

Background: The Citizen Budget Advisory Committee meets monthly to review, discuss and provide 
feedback and advice to City staff regarding the budget process.  CBAC also reviews the City Manager's 
recommended budget annually and provides their recommendations to the city council.  
 
Committee Discussion: After multiple meetings discussing Target-Based-Budgeting and gaining insight 
into the challenges surrounding the City’s general fund structural deficit, as well as conducting a thorough 
review of the proposed budget, the board recommended that the 2027 Budget be adopted as presented by 
staff. The board believes the 2027 budget represents a responsible and substantial step toward addressing 
the City’s fiscal challenges and building a more sustainable financial future and fully support the 2027 
Recommended Budget as it has been presented to city council. 
 
Recommendation:  
The Citizen Budget Advisory Committee (CBAC) has strong confidence in the work of City staff and the 
remarkable effort that went into developing the FY2027 budget. We believe the process was thoughtful, 
thorough, and necessary, and that Council should honor that work while recognizing its responsibility to 
evaluate impacts and consider adjustments as warranted.  
 
CBAC encourages Council to: 
• Trust the process and the work of staff that developed the proposed budget. 
• Recognize that the burden is being shared across the organization and that reductions are based 
on relative priority to protect core services and minimize impact to quality of life to the greatest 
extent possible. 
• Reinforce that meaningful change is necessary; the city cannot continue with the status quo and 
that comes with supporting the hard decisions made in this budget process. 
• Exercise its fiduciary responsibility by maintaining a focus on long-term financial sustainability. 
• Evaluate impacts and adjust going forward when experience or new information demonstrates that 
changes are warranted. 
• Maintain fiscal discipline by not adding expenditures without identifying corresponding reductions or 
sustainable funding elsewhere. 
• Be mindful of the trust placed in staff and avoid decisions that unintentionally undermine the difficult 
work and progress made through the budget process. 
 
This recommendation was passed on October 13th, 2026.

recommendations to the city council. 
Committee Discussion: After multiple meetings discussing Target-Based-Budgeting and gaining insight into the challenges
surrounding the City’s general fund structural deficit, as well as conducting a thorough review of the proposed budget, the board
recommended that the 2027 Budget be adopted as presented by staff. The board believes the 2027 budget represents a
responsible and substantial step toward addressing the City’s fiscal challenges and building a more sustainable financial future
and fully support the 2027 Recommended Budget as it has been presented to city council.
Recommendation: 
The Citizen Budget Advisory Committee (CBAC) has strong confidence in the work of City staff and the remarkable effort that went
into developing the FY2027 budget. We believe the process was thoughtful, thorough, and necessary, and that Council should
honor that work while recognizing its responsibility to evaluate impacts and consider adjustments as warranted. 
CBAC encourages Council to:
·     Trust the process and the work of staff that developed the proposed budget.
·     Recognize that the burden is being shared across the organization and that reductions are based on relative priority
to protect core services and minimize impact to quality of life to the greatest extent possible.
·     Reinforce that meaningful change is necessary; the city cannot continue with the status quo and that comes with
supporting the hard decisions made in this budget process.
·     Exercise its fiduciary responsibility by maintaining a focus on long-term financial sustainability.
·     Evaluate impacts and adjust going forward when experience or new information demonstrates that changes are
warranted.
·     Maintain fiscal discipline by not adding expenditures without identifying corresponding reductions or sustainable
funding elsewhere.
·     Be mindful of the trust placed in staff and avoid decisions that unintentionally undermine the difficult work and
progress made through the budget process.

2027 Budget Process – Lessons 
Learned
Nathan Mosley, Budget & Policy Director
Citizen Budget Advisory Committee 
October 28, 2026

22
Agenda
• Introduction
• The Challenge & the Approach
• What Worked Well
• What We Learned & What We Would Do Differently
• Board Discussion & Feedback
33
The Challenge 
How did we get here?
• Service expectations and commitments expanded
• Inflation, benefits and compensation increased operation costs
• Temporary and grant revenues ended while some service needs continued
• Recurring revenues could no longer sustain recurring expenditures
44
The Approach
How did we address the challenge?
• Target-Based Budgeting
• Examination of programs, staffing and service delivery
• Voluntary separation agreements supported department planning and proposals
• Leadership reviewed proposals, challenged assumptions and evaluated tradeoffs
55
What Worked Well
• Adjusting budget approach, realizing that our "normal" approach was not sufficient to meet this 
challenge
• Starting with the end goal in mind (I.e. $18M deficit)
• Providing clear goals for departments
• Having support/buy-in from City Council and City Manager's Office provided the freedom of budget 
staff to communicate the expectations for departments and hold them accountable
• Department Presentation Format – Creating consistent presentations across all departments helped 
council and the community digest that information
• The teamwork that was exhibited through the process across the organization – everyone 
contributing
66
What We Learned 
• Match ongoing commitments with realistic ongoing funding
• Evaluate future operating costs before committing one-time resources
• Connect staffing and funding to defined service expectations (ongoing)
• Monitor impacts, report results and adjust when evidence warrants
• Adjust approach for enterprise funds and departments that are direct reports to City Council
• Making decisions are hard, so create a process that requires fewer decisions
77
What Would We Do Differently
• More visualization of financial/budget information 
• More communication earlier in the process and use every opportunity to steer people to 
speakupgreeley.com
• Budget Open Houses outside the normal work session/ council meeting schedule
• Take time to dig deeper into other parts of the organization that could potentially contribute to overall 
efficiency and cost savings

October 2026 CBAC Directors Update 
2027 Budget Development Updates 
Since the September meeting we hosted the City Manager’s recommended budget during 
a work session on October 13th.  Barry Eastman, Board Chair, was in attendance and 
shared the statement that was approved by the board via email vote.  No public comment 
occurs during work sessions, but council had several questions and comments including: 
• Questions to clarify total revenue v/s total expenditures for 2027 
• Request to include more line-item detail in the budget 
• Question about CBAC involvement in the process  
• Concerns about service impact from the reductions and requests for ongoing 
reporting back to council on impacts 
 
Overall the work session went well with lots of support and appreciation for the work that 
staff undertook during this budget season.   
 
The following week, on Tuesday, October 20th, Nathan presented the 2027 Budget 
ordinance for 1st reading.  The 4th appropriation of 2026 was also included on the consent 
agenda. 
 
With the recent submittal of an intent to recall member of council, flock cameras and the 
2F tax initiative, there was not much in the way of public comments on the budget other 
than continued concern about the impacts of reductions to the museum. 
 
Based on the questions from City Council it is not expected that there will be any 
additional changes to the recommended budget and the second reading and public 
hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, November 17th.  Please plan on attending that meeting 
if you are interested and able to do so. 
 
Organizational Updates 
• 2F continues to dominate public comments during City Council meeting and 
individuals social media feeds.  From a staff perspective we are unable to engage in 
our official capacity other than providing factual information, i.e. how much 
revenue would likely be available if the measure passes.  At this point staff is waiting 
to see the outcome of the election before any formal next steps.  Read the full ballot 
language. 
• On Friday, October 16, a “notice of intent to recall” was submitted that included 
Mayor Hall, Mayor Pro-tem Melissa McDonald, Council Member Johnny Olson and 
Council Member Ryan Roth.  The submittal of the notice is the first step in a very 
structured process that includes a signature gathering period, time for signature 
verification and if needed a special election.  As more information becomes 
available it will be shared in future updates.

Erie, Colorado.  Kelli’s portfolio of departments included CPRD, Housing & 
Homeless, Community Development, Communication and Engagement.  Her last 
day with the City is November 11th.  City leadership is currently determining both 
interim and long-term plans to respond to her departure.  We wish her well!
