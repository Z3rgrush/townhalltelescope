6. Public Hearing to Consider a Request for Certificate of Designation for the Savig House at 
1611 12th Avenue, Case No. GHR2026-0001. 
 
Chair Podell introduced the item and asked for any conflicts of interest. Seeing none, Chair 
Podell invited staff to speak. 
 
Betsy Kellums (Planner III – Historic Preservation) began her presentation as published. 
When she concluded, she invited the applicant forward to speak.  
 
Margaret Thompson, owner and applicant, introduced herself and asked the Commission if they 
had any questions for her.  
 
Commissioner Clough asked that based on the photo, it appeared that the roof over the porch 
may still be original, while the front supports appear to have been added later. Mrs. Thompson 
shared that the roof appeared to have always been part of the addition, with the front porch later 
enclosed. When those walls were removed, square outlines from the original support columns 
were visible, so square columns were chosen for historical accuracy. The stained wood was left 
exposed to provide contrast with the darker exterior paint.  
 
Commissioner Clough then asked if the floorboards were original. Mrs. Thompson confirmed 
that they were original. 
 
Commissioner McHattie asked about the structure in the back and asked whether it was a garage 
or if there was space for someone to stay there. Mrs. Thompson explained that the Savigs had 
used the rear structure as an art and performance studio during the time they lived there. The 
main floor originally had two bathrooms, which had since been combined into one. The structure 
also had two levels; a rickety interior ladder had been replaced with a small spiral staircase, and 
the exterior deck included standard stairs for access. Overall, the space had potential for a variety 
of uses. 
 
With no further questions for Mrs. Thompson, Chair Podell opened the public portion of the 
meeting at 4:25pm. With no one in attendance or online wishing to speak, Chair Podell closed 
the public portion of the meeting at 4:25pm and turned it over to the Commission for discussion 
or action. 
 
Commissioner Azari shared that he had worked on his music degree at UNC and had known 
Norm. He described him as someone who had a deep respect and appreciation for history, was 
very well read, and would have been honored that his house had been nominated for designation. 
He also believed he would have thanked the Thompsons for taking that step. 
 
Motion by: Commissioner Clough 
He moved that based on the application received and the preceding analysis, that the 
Commission finds and concludes that the Savig House meets significance criteria of Section 24-
1003(d)(1) and integrity criteria of Section 24-1003(d)(4) of the Greeley Municipal Code, as 
summarized below, and therefore approve the designation and issue a Certificate of Designation 
to be recorded by the Weld County Clerk and Recorder.   
(a) Historical Significance: 
1. Has character, interest and integrity and reflects the heritage and cultural development of the 
City, State or Nation. 
3. Is associated with an important individual or group who contributed in a significant way to the 
political, social and/or cultural life of the community. 
(b) Architectural Significance: 
1. Characterizes an architectural style or type associated with a particular era and/or ethnic 
group.  
4. Has a strong or unique relationship to other areas potentially eligible for preservation because 
of architectural significance. 
(c) Geographical Significance: 
1. Has proximity and a strong connection or link to an area, site, structure or object significant in 
the history or development of the City, State or Nation. 
 
Second: Commissioner Azari 
Vote: Motion approved 7-0

4. Report from Historic Greeley Inc 
Julia Richards expressed support for the awning rehabilitation project on the Greeley History 
Museum building, characterizing routine maintenance of historic structures as vital. She also 
noted anticipation for the Weld Walls Mural Festival, expressing hope that the addition of murals 
would complement rather than jeopardize historic materials along the 16th Street streetscape. 
Mrs. Richards raised concern about the proposed significant reduction in museum staffing, noting 
that stewardship of the city's many historic properties requires a specialized skill set, including 
condition monitoring, finding and working with preservation contractors, and ongoing 
documentation. She questioned who would absorb those responsibilities under the proposed 
staffing changes and suggested the Historic Preservation Commission itself might share that 
concern. 
 
 
 

5.  Public Hearing to Consider a Request for a Certificate of Approval for Major Alterations 
at 714 8th Street, Case No. HPDR2026-0009 
Betsy Kellums (Planner III – Historic Preservation) presented a historic preservation design 
review request for the rehabilitation of the canopy/awning on the north side of the historic 
Greeley Tribune building, currently the Greeley History Museum, located at 714 8th Street. 
Commissioner Clough inquired about the condition of the exterior doors and lamps. Kellums 
deferred to Brett Ford (Facilities Manager for the Culture, Parks, and Recreation Department) 
who confirmed that the doors and window frames on the north side of the building had recently 
been refurbished and repainted and are in good condition. He noted that the lamps are also in 
good condition but explained that a gutter system integrated into the awning, one side of which is 
currently missing, is important for directing water away from the lamps and preventing further 
water damage. 
Chair Podell asked about the project timeline. Jeff Shawver of J&J Fabrication, who prepared the 
scope of work, explained that a precise timeline is difficult to establish until the awning is 
removed and disassembled, as the extent of rust and damage cannot be fully assessed until that 
point. He indicated that work could begin within a few weeks of approval and expressed the 
intent to complete the restoration before winter. 
With no further questions for the applicant, Chair Podell opened the public portion of the 
meeting at 4:14pm. No members of the public offered comment, in person or online. Chair 
Podell closed the public portion of the meeting and turned it over to the Commission for 
discussion or vote. 
Motion by: Commissioner Clough 
He moved that  based on the application received and the preceding analysis, the Commission 
finds that the proposed awning rehabilitation at 714 8th Street meets (1) Criteria and Standards a, 
c, e, f, and h of Section 24-1003(j)(1) of the Greeley Municipal Code and therefore approves the 
request for a Certificate of Approval.

6. Weld Walls Mural Festival Presentation, Armando Silva  
Armando Silva and Brianna Harris, co-founders of the Weld Walls Mural Festival, presented 
plans for the third annual festival, scheduled for September 15–19, 2026. The 2026 festival 
theme is “Homecoming” and will focus on the 16th Street block between 9th and 10th Avenues, 
with eight new public art pieces planned on surfaces that are already painted or previously used 
for public art. Building owner permissions have been secured for all locations. 
Planned projects include murals in the alley between DP Dough and High Brau, replacement of 
an existing city-commissioned mural on Pinocchio’s east-facing wall, artwork in Jenny’s Malt 
Shop courtyard, and a panel installation in Margie’s courtyard. Silva and Harris also noted they 
plan to highlight the historic Color Portraits Sign on 16th Street through interpretive signage and 
community engagement, without altering the sign. 
The presenters emphasized coordination with the City, Downtown Development Authority 
(DDA), Greeley Art Commission, historic preservation interests, and local businesses. 
Commissioners asked clarifying questions about permissions, historic preservation, and the 
Color Portraits Sign, and expressed support for the organizers’ thoughtful approach. The festival 
will also partner with the City of Greeley and the University of Northern Colorado (UNC) for a 
September 18 ribbon-cutting celebrating completed 16th Street corridor improvements. 
 
 
7. Common Treasures: A History of Greeley Museums, Dr. Chris Bowles, Museums 
Manager  
Dr. Chris Bowles, Museums Manager, presented virtually the history and evolution of Greeley’s 
municipal museum system. He traced its origins to Nathan Meeker’s 1870 call for a community 
museum and noted evidence that Greeley may have established one of Colorado’s earliest history 
and natural history museums. 
Bowles outlined key milestones, including the Meeker Memorial Library Association’s 
fundraising efforts that helped the City purchase the Meeker Home in 1929; early curators and 
later renovations to the site; the City’s 1958 creation of a Department of Culture; the 
development of Centennial Village during the bicentennial era; the acquisition of White Plumb 
Farm; and the 2003 bond initiative that led to the Greeley History Museum’s opening in the 
historic Greeley Tribune/Hansen Building in 2005. He noted that the museum collection has 
grown to nearly 100,000 items, among the largest municipal collections in Colorado. 
Bowles also discussed current financial uncertainty, including a proposed 27% funding reduction 
that would significantly affect staffing. He reported that the Friends of the Greeley Museums has 
reactivated to help explore options and asked the commission, “Where should we go now?” 
Commissioners and staff expressed appreciation for the presentation, concern about the proposed 
cuts, and support for continued community advocacy. Staff noted that all four museum sites are 
locally designated historic properties, with three also listed on the National Register of Historic 
Places.

The first letter was from Geoffrey Havens, former Historic Site Supervisor for the Greeley 
Museums.  
 
Hi Betsy, 
 
Thank you again for inviting me to attend Monday’s Historic Preservation Commission 
meeting. I wanted to put my concerns in writing beforehand so you have them as the 
City’s Historic Preservation Planner and can determine how best to share them with the 
Commission. 
 
I am writing from my experience as the former Historic Sites Supervisor for the Greeley 
Museums and now as a concerned citizen who places great value on preserving our 
community’s history, heritage, and physical historic resources. 
 
The proposed museum reductions are not only a question of operating hours or public 
programming. They also raise a fundamental historic-preservation question: what 
specialized capacity will remain to care for the City’s historic buildings and sites? 
 
The museum system is responsible for 68 historic structures, along with archaeological 
resources, collections, archives, and several designated historic properties. Stewardship 
of those resources involves considerably more than routine building maintenance. It 
includes condition monitoring, preservation planning, documentation, project 
development, treatment review, contractor coordination, regulatory compliance, and 
continued oversight of how historic resources are used. 
 
During my time with the City, Chris Bowles and I both performed portions of that work 
in coordination with you and other staff. Even then, we were already triaging projects and 
deferring work because available time and funding were limited. 
 
I recognize that Recreation Facilities has capable staff and a broad portfolio. Its staff also 
have limited time, however, and are not positioned to absorb all of the museum system’s 
preservation planning, documentation, monitoring, and project-review responsibilities. 
You have preservation expertise and substantial familiarity with the sites, but you also 
have your own workload and cannot simply absorb the duties of eliminated museum 
positions. 
 
The last museum staffing model I saw before leaving City employment would have 
reduced the museum workforce from sixteen employees to five. If the proposed cuts 
leave the museum system with only five staff members, I do not see how the City retains 
enough specialized capacity to meet these responsibilities. Period. 
 
At its July 16 meeting, the Museum Advisory Board unanimously adopted a resolution 
expressing serious concern about the proposed museum reductions and the process used 
to develop them. The Board also formally requested staffing and operating information 
showing how museum services and responsibilities would function under the reduced 
budget. As of this writing, that information has not yet been provided. 
 
I recognize that I am writing as an individual citizen and cannot formally ask the Historic 
Preservation Commission to request a particular analysis. I do hope, however, that once 
the Commission is informed of the potential effects on the City’s historic resources, it 
will consider whether additional impact analysis is needed from Culture, Parks and 
Recreation through the appropriate City channels. 
 
Such an analysis could help clarify: 
 
• which preservation and stewardship functions are currently performed by 
museum staff;  
• who would perform those functions under the proposed structure;  
• which duties would shift to Recreation Facilities, Historic Preservation staff, 
contractors, volunteers, or an outside operator;  
• what work would be reduced, deferred, or left without an identified owner; and  
• what standards, authority, funding, and City oversight would apply under any 
external-management or transfer proposal.  
 
If responsibility for the sites is transferred, the City still needs to demonstrate how those 
resources will be cared for, who will hold treatment authority, what preservation 
standards will govern the work, who will monitor compliance, and which legal and 
financial obligations will remain with the City. 
 
Thank you for helping ensure that the preservation consequences are understood before 
staffing and governance decisions become final. 
 
Best, 
Geoffrey Havens 
 
The second letter is from Marcus Embry dated July 20th.  
 
July 20, 2026 
My fellow Greeley Citizens and Commissioners, my name is Marcus Embry, and I am 
the chair of the Greeley Museum Advisory Board.  I stand before you to seek your 
audience and assistance.   
 
Greeley is in a substantial financial deficit, and all departments of the city are tasked with 
cutting costs.  The Greeley Museums are part of the Culture, Parks, and Recreation 
Department.  The CPRD’s budget reduction goal is 3.7 million.  On July 7, CPRD 
presented a budget proposal in which they reduced their budget by 2.9 million, which left 
a budget gap of 800,000 dollars.  The budget proposal states explicitly, in multiple places, 
that the budget proposals “intentionally concentrate reductions within one service area, 
allowing higher service levels to be maintained elsewhere across the department.”  That 
one service area to be reduced is the Greeley History Museums.   
 
CPRD presented four budget options, two of which neither singly nor together amount to 
800,000 dollars.  Budget Option 2 raises 800,000 dollars by closing the museums and 
canceling downtown events such as the Farmer’s Market, Arts Picnic, and other city-
funded downtown cultural events.  It is, as they say, an option with poison pills.  Option 
1, which the budget leaves as the only viable alternative, has the CPRD pursue 
“Accelerated Cost Recovery and User Fee Adjustments,” which include turning the 
funding of the Greeley History Museums over to a 401(c)3 which does not exist. 
 
My point to you is that the CPRD is determined to defund the Greeley History Museums.  
We need your assistance to convince the City Council that this is not acceptable.  With 
regard to the work you do, at present the CPRD’s Staffing and Operating Analysis and 
Stewardship framework have not been made public nor shared with the Museum 
Advisory Board, but those plans appear to include reducing the museum staff from 18 to 
5.  You can imagine the impact that will have on artifact access and historical research.  A 
museum in caretaker status cannot offer the services our citizens expect and deserve.  In 
fact, such staffing could result in the downtown museum being closed during the summer 
months when Centennial Village is open.   
 
I do not believe that the City of Greeley or its citizens are willing to abandon the history 
of this remarkable city.  I ask you to please advocate in every manner you can for the 
Museums as a legacy and trust of the City of Greeley.   
 
Thank you for your attention. 
 
Marcus Embry, PhD 
Chair, Greeley Museum Advisory Board 
 
Commissioner Clough proposed that the commission draft a formal letter to be signed by all 
commission members and submitted to city council, expressing the commission's concern 
regarding the historic preservation implications of the proposed museum staffing reductions, 
drawing on the substance of both letters. He volunteered to prepare the draft and circulate it to 
commission members for review and signature prior to a work session. 
Chair Podell expressed support for the proposal, noting that once funding and dedicated personnel 
are eliminated, recovery to the current level of operation would be extremely difficult. 
The commission agreed to hold a special work session on Monday, July 27, 2026 at 3:00 PM in 
Room 1105 at City Center North to review and finalize the draft letter. Commissioner Clough 
committed to having a draft circulated by Wednesday, July 23rd. Staff noted that the session 
would be held as a hybrid meeting, and that Geoff Havens and Chris Bowles were invited to 
attend if their schedules permitted.
