22. Pulled Consent Agenda Items  
Item 14 – Resolution Approving a Development Services Agreement between 
the City of Greeley and Richmark Vertikal, LLC related to the Civic Campus 
Project  
Deputy City Manager Kelli Johnson and Senior Strategic Advisor John Hall 
presented a summary of the Civic Campus project and the terms of the 
Development Services Agreement.  
 
City Council Proceedings August 4, 2026 - Approved 
 
 
Ms. Johnson provided background on the project's origins in 2022–2023, rooted in 
the City's Downtown Development Master Plan adopted in 2023. The project 
encompassed several blocks of downtown redevelopment involving the City, 
Weld County, and Greeley School District 6, alongside private development 
components including a hotel and approximately 160 market-rate apartments. 
 
Mr. Hall outlined the key terms of the Development Services Agreement, which 
replaced the previously approved predevelopment agreement. He noted that 
incentive amounts reflected only revenues generated by the project itself, and that 
council retained authority over any budget increases and construction contracts. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked for clarification that the $8.7 million in 
incentives was already designated in the City's redevelopment fund dating back to 
2023 and was not a new fiscal impact. Mr. Hall and Ms. Johnson confirmed. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked the representatives whether the project would 
be feasible without the incentives. Adam Frazier of Richmark Vertical confirmed 
the hotel project could not meet a bankable internal rate of return without gap 
funding, noting that steel costs alone had risen approximately 20% since year-end.  
 
Councilmember Olson stated that when factoring in all incentives, the total 
incentive value amounted to approximately $24 million, or roughly 28% of the 
overall budget, and that the land sale at $200,000 for a 24,000-square-foot lot 
represented a significant concession. While he said he viewed the development 
partners as good partners, he stated he would vote no because he believed the City 
should resolve the West Greeley Catalyst project budget situation before 
committing to additional financial obligations. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez made the motion to adopt the resolution. 
Councilmember Rudy seconded the motion. The motion passed 5–1 at 7:36 
p.m. with Councilmember Olson voting “nay” and Mayor Hall absent.

23. Public Hearing and Resolution for Approval of 2026 HUD Annual Action 
Plan for the Proposed Community Development Block Grant and HOME 
Investment Partnership Entitlement Programs  
Housing Director Deb Callies and Housing Investment Manager Cece Cervantes 
introduced the item at 7:45 p.m.  
 
Ms. Callies clarified that a news report had mistakenly stated the City was 
allocating $4.4 million in HUD funds; in fact, the total available for allocation was 
$2.4 million, against $4.4 million in requests received. She also noted that HUD 
funds are restricted to eligible activities and cannot be used to address the City's 
general fund deficit. 
 
Ms. Cervantes described the application and review process, which included a 
public notice of funding period from May 20 through June 19, independent staff 
reviews using a standardized scoring matrix, and evaluation for eligibility, 
financial capacity, and program sustainability. The City received 18 applications 
totaling $4 million in requests. Staff recommended funding 11 community 
projects and programs using the full $2.48 million allocation. Five applications 
were found ineligible. Applicants who were not funded this cycle will receive 
extra points when reapplying next year. 
 
Ms. Callies provided additional context on the Hope Springs Habitat for Humanity 
funding recommendation, which was lower than the amount requested. She stated 
the recommendation aligned with the program's historic spending pace and that 
the City remained on track to support 75 homes over the life of the consolidated 
plan. 
 
City Council Proceedings August 4, 2026 - Approved 
 
 
Ms. Cervantes described a proposed change to the City's HUD program year, 
shifting from a calendar year (January–December) to a fiscal year ending June 30. 
She stated the change would improve alignment with HUD timeliness regulations, 
increase predictability for community partners, and better align with other city 
departments.  
 
Councilmember Olson asked how the change would affect the City's budget 
appropriation process. Budget and Policy Director Nathan Mosley stated that 
grant funds would be appropriated as needed and carried across fiscal years, 
consistent with current practice. Ms. Callies and Ms. Cervantes confirmed that the 
change was not expected to create any material difference in how the City 
manages these funds. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald opened the public hearing at 7:59 p.m.

Mr. Mosley noted that prior to COP issuance, staff would present financing 
options to Council, with first reading currently scheduled for September 1st and 
second reading for September 15th.
