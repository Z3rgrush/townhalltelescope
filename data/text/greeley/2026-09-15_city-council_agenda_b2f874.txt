works best for them. 
 
Watch Meetings: 
Meetings are open to the public and can be attended in person by anyone.

participation options as noted above.

National Public Lands Day 
WHEREAS, National Public Lands Day is historically the nation’s largest single-day 
environmental stewardship volunteer effort and celebration; and 
WHEREAS, Natural areas are recognized for their significant positive impacts to Greeley 
residents’ quality of life; and 
WHEREAS, The City of Greeley encourages all residents to join in the care and celebration of our 
public lands; and 
WHEREAS, National Public Lands Day provides an opportunity for the people of the United 
States to connect with and steward their local natural areas; and 
WHEREAS, 2026 marks the 32nd annual National Public Lands Day celebration. 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Dale Hall, by virtue of the authority vested in me as Mayor of the City 
of Greeley, do hereby proclaim September 26, 2026, as National Public Lands Day in the City of 
Greeley, and encourage all residents to engage in the celebration and stewardship of Greeley 
natural areas.  
IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand and caused to be affixed the official seal 
of the City of Greeley, Colorado, this 15th day, September 2026.

Allen Jost Opinion and Presentation to Greeley City Council September 15, 2026. (lam not an 
attorney) 
I’m talking about CO Rev Stat § 29-20-111 referred to as the “Home Act”. The law states that 
local governments, which include Home Rule Cities, shall not limit the number of unrelated 
adults that can live in a single house. However, governments can continue to enforce single 
family occupancy limits if there are demonstrated negative impacts on health and safety. 
Apparently, without some of you knowing it, the city HAS implemented this law by default by 
administratively NOT enforcing the single family zoning provision. By not enforcing this 
provision, the city has changed the zoning of my property, and 20,000 other properties, from 
Low Density Single Family to Medium or High Density zoning without following any of its 
comprehensive planning and zoning ordinance laws. 
| propose the city take the following actions (which you are allowed to do according to the 
legislation): 
1. Issue a press release which states that the City of Greeley intends to comply with 
Home Act, BUT NOT until after it has conducted a comprehensive study of all 
Single Family Zoned properties in order to determine if there are any negative 
health, safety and welfare impacts that would result from implementation of the 
Home Act. This study and the delay is in full compliance with the legislation. 
Therefore, the city will enact a moratorium to delay enforcement of the Home 
Act AND will continue to enforce the Single Family zoning provisions. 
2. Conduct Public Relations campaign to notify citizens and businesses that the city 
will continue to enforce Single Family zoning until the public health safety and 
welfare impacts of the legislation are studied and determined to be adequate, as 
allowed by the law. This PR is critical to prevent speculators from_buying 
properties and remodeling them to accommodate 10-15 unrelated adults 
because the speculators may believe the Home Act is in effect in Greeley. 
One hundred years ago the supreme court affirmed that cities have the right to separate 
single-family residential zoned districts from high density residential housing. They said 
that cities not only have the right to do so, but it is only common sense.

Title: 
Resolution authorizing the City to enter into a contract for consulting services with Interval, LLC for 
Housing Needs Assessment 
 
Summary: 
SB24-174 requires jurisdictions of 100,000, or more, to have housing needs assessments, action plans, 
and particular revisions to the city's comprehensive plan by December 31, 2026. DOLA has awarded the 
City of Greeley funds (HPLN-H26048) to hire a consultant to complete this work. City Council 
approved this award on August 4th, 2026. We are amending the scope, payment amount, and timeline of 
the existing contract with Interval LLC (F26-02-004) to complete this work bringing that total 
professional services contract amount to $265,000 which requires City Council approval under contract 
F26-02-004.  
 
The work contemplated under the HPLN-H26048 grant is closely intertwined with the work already 
underway by Interval Consulting (F26-02-004). Procuring a different consultant would create 
unnecessary duplication, increase costs, and introduce significant schedule risk. Interval is currently 
conducting a comprehensive evaluation of Greeley's land use regulations, building and fire codes, water 
and sewer standards, development review procedures, and stakeholder engagement processes, all of 
which directly inform the Housing Needs Assessment, Housing Action Plan, Strategic Growth Element, 
and Water Supply Element required under the HPLN grant.  
 
The two efforts are intended to occur concurrently, with findings from the existing Housing Solutions 
Study serving as foundational inputs to the grant-funded work, ensuring consistency and avoiding 
conflicting recommendations. Continuity of personnel and methodology will allow the City to capitalize 
on the extensive data collection, departmental coordination, and stakeholder relationships already 
established by Interval LLC, rather than requiring another consultant to duplicate work that is currently 
in progress. A competitive procurement at this stage would likely delay completion of the grant 
deliverables and jeopardize the City's ability to meet state-required housing planning (SB-24-174) 
deadline of December 31, 2026. Accordingly, a sole source procurement is justified because Interval 
possesses unique project-specific knowledge and is already performing substantially similar and 
complementary work on the same schedule.

4. Purpose 
The Parties entered into the Agreement to hire two (2) qualified applicants for term-limited, 
full time staff positions in the Community Development and Housing Solutions Departments. 
Grantee requested and received Supplemental Grant Funds in the amount of $50,000 for 
achieving the Proposition 123 milestone for early adoption of Fast Track. Grantee requests the 
Scope of Project be changed to accurately reflect an updated staffing structure, Project 
activities, and deliverables required to aid in the City’s successful Fast Track implementation 
process for affordable housing, including the addition of preliminary planning for infill parcels; 
the Grant Agreement Expiration Date be extended to allow time for the additional Work; the 
addition of $50,000 dollars of Supplemental Grant Funds be added to the Project Budget; and 
the adjustment of the Project’s Budget to include consultant services and 
architectural/engineering services. This Amendment modifies the Agreement as requested.
