5. Water Treatment Plant Transmission Master Plan Update  
 
Keri Fishlock and Stephanie Elliott from Stantec discussed that the Water Treatment Plants and 
Transmission Master Plan project started in 2025 with a goal to provide a clear framework for 
phased water treatment and transmission system improvements that support reliable service 
today and position the system for long-term growth.  
The Master Plan builds on prior planning efforts and integrates five parallel technical evaluations 
focused on: 
• Bellvue WTP 
• Boyd WTP 
• Hydraulic Modeling of the Transmission System 
• Transmission Infrastructure  
• Electrical Reliability  
 
Together, these evaluations establish a prioritized capital improvement path to meet projected 
treatment capacity milestones of 50 MGD by 2033, 75 MGD by 2057, and approximately 102 
MGD at buildout in 2075. 
The chosen consultant for this work, Stantec, has delivered the first draft of the completed 
Master Plan report, which will be presented to the Board. The Master Plan provides a phased, 
defensible roadmap that maintains reliable day-to-day service while positioning the system for 
future demand, evolving regulatory requirements, and operational resiliency. 
Key findings highlight near -term capacity, reliability, and resiliency needs across major system 
assets. At Bellvue WTP, clearwell disinfection and gravity thickener limitations constrain 
capacity, while future rerating and expansion can increase treatment  capacity to 60 MGD within 
the planning horizon. At Boyd WTP, future ozone and residuals improvements will support 
changing source water conditions, improve treatment performance, and reduce solids production.

6. Wastewater Treatment and Reclamation Facility Solids Master Plan Update 
 
Zeke Havard and Rob Ringle from Black and Veatch discussed that in 2025, the Solids Long-
Term Master Plan (SLTMP) project for the Wastewater Treatment and Reclamation Facility 
(WTRF) kicked off with the following key objectives: 
- Assess the condition and capacity of solids treatment equipment 
- Review and update projections for population growth, flow, and solids loading 
- Assess existing and emerging regulatory risks to current solids operations 
- Evaluate opportunities for beneficial use of biogas 
- Evaluate biosolids management processes  
- Develop a long-term (50-year) biosolids strategy for the WTRF 
- Develop a capital improvement plan for 5- and 20-year timeframes to support solids 
treatment needs 
 
The chosen consultant for this work, Black & Veatch, has delivered the first draft of the 
completed Master Plan report. 
 
In parallel to the SLTMP project, W&S department staff have continued working on evaluating 
long-term site expansion opportunities for the WTRF. Flow projections for the next ~100 years 
indicate that the plant will eventually need to expand beyond the boundaries of the current site. 
Through a high-level capacity assessment and development of conceptual site layouts, W&S 
staff have identified key properties adjacent to the WTRF which would be viable for supporting 
long-term growth.

Title: 
Approve Amrize Augmentation Lease 
 
Summary: 
Greeley has leased augmentation water to Amrize (formerly Holcim) since 2019, with up to 10 acre-feet 
per year of wholly consumable water released from Milton Seaman Reservoir. The annual release 
schedule is agreed upon at the beginning of each year. The initial lease rate was $1,200 per acre-foot, 
with annual adjustments based on the Denver-Boulder-Greeley Consumer Price Index (CPI). The 2026 
rate is $1,473 per acre-foot. 
 
Amrize has requested an additional 22 acre-feet per year, increasing the total allocation to 32 acre-feet, 
to support operations at its new Ingleside quarry. Amrize has requested that the existing lease terms and 
CPI-based annual price adjustments remain unchanged. 
 
  
 
Recommended Action: 
Water and Sewer staff recommend amending the existing lease to increase the annual water allocation to 
32 acre-feet while retaining all other terms. Staff determined that the proposed rate is consistent with 
Greeley's water rental program, provides a reliable source of revenue for the department, and builds on a 
lease arrangement that has worked well since 2019. 
 
Recommended Motion: 
“I move that the Board approve a proposed amendment to the City’s existing water augmentation lease 
with Amrize North America Inc. (formerly Holcim US, Inc.) by which the Greeley Water and Sewer 
Department purposes to increase the total amount of water leased under the agreement from 10 acre-feet 
annually to 32 acre-feet annually, said modification to be accomplished substantially in the form of the 
draft amendment included in the board packet. In connection therewith, I move that the Board delegate 
authority to the Director of Water and Sewer, or his designee, to make non-substantive alterations to the 
proposed amendment as may be necessary in the course of negotiation and execution.”

which is hereby acknowledged, the parties mutually agree as follows:

in this Amendment that are defined in the Agreement shall be construed and interpreted in 
accordance with the Agreement. 
 
2. Amended Delivery Schedule. Paragraph 3 of the Agreement is hereby deleted in 
its entirety and replaced as follows: 
 
“The Augmentation Water to be made available by Greeley pursuant to this agreement 
shall be released in accordance with the following annual schedule (“Delivery Schedule”)

Title: 
Approve State Land Board Lease Renewal for the Milton Seaman Caretaker House Property 
 
Summary: 
Since Milton Seaman Reservoir came into service in 1947, the City of Greeley has constructed and 
maintained a caretaker residence on 2.44 acres owned by the Colorado State Land Board (SLB). A City 
employee resides in the home year-round to support safe, continuous operation and maintenance of the 
reservoir. The City’s current 10-year lease, executed in 2016, expires on November 1, 2026. The City 
has negotiated a new 10-year lease with the SLB for continued use of the existing City-owned 
structures, with rent set at $5,000 per year for the first five years and increasing to a minimum of $5,250 
in 2031. The lease outlines permitted uses and includes requirements for insurance and a $5,000 
performance bond, while preserving SLB rights related to access, monitoring, and potential future 
property actions. 
 
Recommended Action: 
Staff recommends that the Board approve the Renewal of the Lease with the State Land Board of 
Commissioners for the Milton Seaman Caretaker House Property.  
 
Recommended Motion: 
“I move that the Water & Sewer Board approve the State Land Board Ground Lease for the Milton 
Seaman Caretaker House Property in the form presented, and delegate authority to the Director of Water 
& Sewer or their designee to approve minor revisions to the lease before execution, provided the 
material substance remains unchanged.”

2. DESCRIPTION OF THE PREMISES 
The Board leases to the Lessee and Lessee leases from the Board, exclusively for the purposes indicated below, 
the trust lands, in the County of Larimer, Colorado, as described in Exhibit A (the "Premises"). 
3. CONDITION OF LEASED PREMISES 
Lessee represents that Lessee has had an opportunity to inspect the Premises prior to entering into this lease, 
and Lessee accepts the Premises in their present condition and acknowledges that the Premises are in all 
respects suitable for the Permitted Uses. The Board disclaims any and all obligation to provide access to the 
Premises or to fence, make any repairs to, or construct any improvements upon the Premises; and the Board 
does not warrant that the Premises are suitable for the Permitted Uses. 
4. USE OF THE LEASED PREMISES 
The use of the Premises shall be limited to continued use of the existing Lessee-owned structures located on 
the Premises together with the right of ingress and egress twenty-four (24) hours per day, seven (7) days per 
week as necessary (the “Permitted Uses”). No other use of the Premises by Lessee shall be permitted. For 
purposes of clarification, and without limiting the breadth of the foregoing limitation, the Lessee hereby 
agrees that no businesses of any kind related to the marijuana industry shall be operated on the Premises. 
Lessee shall be solely responsible for all of its subcontracting and agency arrangements and performance by 
such parties in compliance with the provisions of this Lease.  
5. LEASE TERM 
The initial term of this Lease (the “Initial Term”) is for a term of 10 years beginning 11/01/2026, being until 
11/1/2036, unless terminated sooner as provided for in this lease. 
6. EXTENSION TERM 
At the Board’s sole discretion, the Board and the Lessee may agree to an extension of this Lease on terms and 
conditions set forth by the Board and agreed to by the Parties. Lessee shall provide to the Board a written 
notice of intent to enter into such extension no later than one (l) year prior to the expiration of the Initial 
Term.

The rental amount for the first 5 years of the Initial Term is $5,000.00 per year (“Base Rent”). At the midpoint 
of the lease term (11/01/2031, the “Adjustment Date”), the rental amount  shall be adjusted to reflect changes
