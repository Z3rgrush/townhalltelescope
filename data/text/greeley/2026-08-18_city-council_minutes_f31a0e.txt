14. Public hearing and second reading of fifteen ordinances annexing to the City of 
Greeley, Colorado, certain unincorporated territory located in Weld County, 
Colorado, known as "Great Western Industrial Park (GWIP) Serial 
Annexations No. 1 through No. 15"  
Interim Director of Community Development Don Threewitt introduced the item at 
7:55 p.m.  
 
Mr. Threewitt presented the staff report covering all 15 serial annexations and the 
associated zoning case in a single presentation. Council previously passed substantial 
compliance resolutions in March and eligibility resolutions in May. Mr. Threewitt 
stated that the proposal met all statutory annexation criteria, that notice was provided 
to property owners within 1,000 feet, and the Planning Commission recommended 
approval on a 4-1 vote on May 26, 2026. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked about the permitted use of energy production under 
the proposed Industrial High Intensity (IH) zoning. Mr. Threewitt said the 
development code does not have a specific definition for private utilities, and that this 
was something that would be addressed in ongoing code updates.  
 
City Council Proceedings August 18, 2026- Approved 
 
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked that the code clearly define what types of energy 
production would and would not be allowed so that impacts could be properly 
evaluated. 
 
Mark Savala of the BRO Real Estate Group provided background on the firm's 
national focus on rail-served industrial development, noting the park was dual-served 
by both Burlington Northern Santa Fe and Union Pacific railroads. He described the 
historical significance of the site, noting that Greeley provided water service to the 
area since 1968 under a long-standing out-of-territory service agreement. He stated 
that the primary driver for pursuing annexation into Greeley was service amenities. 
 
Mayor Hall opened the public hearing at 8:04 p.m. 
1. Julie Cline, Mayor of Windsor, requested the annexation be paused for 
intergovernmental talks with Greeley, asking for dialogue and collaboration. 
2. Ron Steinbach, Windsor Town Board member, noted the area was in Windsor's 
long-standing growth plan for many years, and was actually 99% surrounded by 
Windsor, had concerns about services, and requested postponement of the 
annexation. 
3. Greg Flebbe, former Greeley planner, opposed the annexation, claiming 
construction violations by the applicant in addition to zoning incompatibilities. 
4. Steve Teets opposed the annexation, noting the city's inability to maintain current 
properties. 
5. Bill Gillard supported the annexation noting heavy industrial development was 
needed for higher-paying jobs. 
6. Mary Metzger yielded her allotted speaking time to Greg Flebbe, who continued 
to express concerns regarding zoning and alleged applicant violations. 
7. Rhonda Solis asked Greeley to consider better community involvement in 
decisions and collaboration with neighbors. 
8. Jeff Kennedy supported the annexation to restore industrial jobs in the area and 
questioned why Windsor had not done more while the property was in their 
growth area. 
With no additional speakers, the public hearing closed at 8:27 p.m. 
 
Mr. Savala clarified two points raised during public comments. He stated that BRO 
Real Estate was not associated with Global AI, does not have any open violations 
with Weld County, and was not involved in the data center project being processed 
through the county.  
 
He also stated that the property was not 99 percent surrounded by Windsor, noting 
that approximately 350 acres adjacent to the subject parcel remain in unincorporated 
Weld County and have not chosen an annexation path, making the actual Windsor 
adjacency closer to 50 percent. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald asked Mr. Savala directly whether the city had in any way 
coerced, bribed, or pressured the applicant to annex into Greeley. Mr. Savala said that 
no such pressure was applied and that the decision to pursue annexation into Greeley 
was entirely the applicant's own, driven by access to water resources and services. He 
confirmed that the applicant did not wish to be annexed into Windsor. 
Councilmember Huddleston was excited about the prospect of this property bringing 
industrial and blue-collar jobs back to Greeley.

map of the City of Greeley, Colorado, to establish I-H (Industrial High Intensity) 
and C-D (Conservation District) zoning for property located generally south of 
Eastman Park Drive, east of State Highway 257, west of Great Western Drive, 
and north of Weld County Road 62 1/4  
Mr. Threewitt introduced the item at 8:43 p.m.  
 
The presentation was included in the Item 15 presentation. Mr. Threewitt clarified that 
the permitted uses by right under Windsor's Heavy Industrial (HI) zoning and 
Greeley's proposed Industrial High Intensity (IH) zoning are substantially similar, and

16. Public hearing and second reading of an ordinance annexing to the city of 
Greeley, Colorado, certain unincorporated territory located in Weld County, 
Colorado, known as "Arroyos del Sol"  
Mr. Threewitt introduced the item at 8:48 p.m.  
 
He presented the staff report for the Arroyos del Sol annexation, covering both the 
annexation and the associated zoning in a single presentation. He stated that the 
property, comprising approximately 999.72 acres, was currently in unincorporated 
Weld County and zoned agricultural. The property was owned by the City of Greeley 
and used as a natural area. The Planning Commission recommended approval 6-0 on 
July 14, 2026. 
 
Councilmember Olson asked whether the city had previously attempted to annex the 
property. Mr. Threewitt confirmed that an annexation process started but paused 
pending completion of the property ownership transfer and the placement of a 
conservation easement on the land. This was the first time the annexation ordinance 
came before Council. 
 
Mayor Hall opened the public hearing at 8:50 p.m. 
1. Mayor Cline of Windsor said that Windsor contributed $250,000 toward the 
original purchase of the Arroyos del Sol property because both communities 
shared a vision of preserving open space as a regional community separator 
between Greeley and Windsor. She noted that Windsor did not oppose the 
annexation with the original shared vision. 
2. Steve Teets asked for more information on the annexation and benefits. 
With no additional speakers, the public hearing closed at 8:54 p.m. 
 
Mayor Hall responded to Mr. Teets's question by noting that bringing the property 
into the city's Conservation District zoning allows the investment into the natural area. 
Mr. Threewitt added that with the conservation easement already on the property, 
combined with the proposed Conservation District zoning, helps to substantially limit 
future land uses and ensure the natural area remains in the current state. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald moved to adopt the ordinance with reference to title 
only. Councilmember DeBoutez seconded the motion. The motion passed 7–0 at 
8:57 p.m.

18. Resolution of the Greeley City Council authorizing the City to enter into an 
Intergovernmental Agreement with Weld County to participate in the November 
3, 2026, Coordinated Election, and authorizing the City Clerk to sign said 
Intergovernmental Agreement  
City Clerk Leatherwood introduced the item at 9:01 p.m.  
 
Ms. Leatherwood explained that the city regularly coordinates its November elections 
with the Weld County Clerk and Recorder through an intergovernmental agreement, 
which provided shared costs, access to trained election staff, established processes, 
and typically higher voter turnout. She clarified an important procedural distinction: 
approving the IGA by the county's August 25th deadline does not commit the city to 
the approximately $180,000 election cost.  
 
The city would incur that cost upon submitting final ballot content to the county by 
September 4th. Council's decision on whether to place an item on the ballot was 
scheduled for the September 1st work session and meeting. The cost was based on a 
flat rate of $2.50 per voter, applied to 72,104 registered Greeley voters as of August 
7th. 
 
Councilmember Olson confirmed that if Council ultimately decided not to place 
anything on the ballot, the IGA approval had no financial consequence. Ms. 
Leatherwood confirmed. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston stated that he was willing to continue the conversation 
but clarified that his support for moving forward was conditional on two things: that 
the COPs for the civic campus would not be put at risk by the ballot measure, and that  
they would not be delayed.  
 
Mayor Hall clarified that the ballot language itself would be discussed at the August 
25th work session and the September 1st meeting, and that this item was solely about 
preserving the option to place something on the ballot. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez noted she would continue the conversation but stated that 
if a downtown civic campus component was included in the ballot question language, 
she would not support it. 
City Council Proceedings August 18, 2026- Approved 
 
 
 
Councilmember Olson moved to adopt the resolution. Councilmember Rudy 
seconded the motion. The motion passed 7–0 at 9:07 p.m.
