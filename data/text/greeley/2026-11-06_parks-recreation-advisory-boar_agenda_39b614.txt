Parks (Report by Eric) 
• Water Management: 
o Greeley-Loveland Ditch shut off earlier than expected; parks transitioned to potable water. 
o Cutbacks implemented at passive-use parks (Epple, Leavy, Franklin, city landscapes, fire 
stations) to maintain watering at priority parks (Monfort, Twin Rivers). 
o Expect earlier dormancy in turf; health not compromised. Hoping for a wet winter. 
• Redtail Park: 
o Grand opening held ~1.5 weeks prior; high attendance; park consistently busy (basketball and 
BMX track usage noted). 
• Upcoming Ribbon Cutting: 
o Twin Rivers Playground: October 28, 2026, 4:00–6:00 p.m. (may end early due to cold). New 
structure is among the tallest on the market; design emphasizes inclusivity (wheelchair 
transfers and universal access), similar to Aven’s Village at Island Grove. 
• Playground Construction Pipeline: 
o Calabaza Park (near old Hewlet Packard site off 4th): Purchase orders signed; equipment arrival 
expected by end of October; installation in 3–4 weeks; target opening by November 2026. 
o Coyote Park: Equipment ordered; aim to install before snowfall. 
o Peak View: Next in sequence after Coyote. 
• Winter Constraints: 
o Synthetic turf can be installed in winter; pour-in-place rubber requires >50°F for 48+ hours— 
season window closing. 
• Surface Material Strategy: 
o Moving away from pour-in-place rubber due to sun/weather degradation and maintenance 
issues. 
o Favoring synthetic turf (durable; performing well at Youth Sports Complex) and foam rubber 
tiles (used at Lincoln). 
o Engineered wood fiber remains cost-effective upfront but requires refreshing every 2–3 years; 
long-term costs considered. 
• Water and Ponds: 
o City temporarily shut down filling of ponds (Highland Hills, Bonner, Twin Rivers, etc.) to stabilize 
potable system. Plan to top off Glenmere Pond within two weeks before winter; currently at 
historically low levels.

Natural Areas and Trails (Justin) 
• Poudre Learning Center Open House: 
o Topics: Narrows trail repair project; closures; construction timelines; neighbor Q&A. 
o Construction start target: December 1, 2026; work continues through winter into spring. 
o Design: 13-foot wide trail (up from ~6 feet); within floodway—no rise requirement; ~50-year 
lifespan target. 
• Wildfire Mitigation: 
o Interface at Signature Bluffs Natural Area and Poudre River Ranch neighborhood identified as 
Grassland Urban Interface (GUI). 
o Past prescribed burns successful (fuel reduction and resilience). Further mitigation planned 
(ladder fuel management). 
o Exploring goats for vegetation management to reduce fuel loads in lieu of heavier clearing. 
Budget (Presented by Diana) 
• Process Timeline: 
o City Manager’s recommended budget presentation to City Council: October 21, 2026 (work 
session). 
o Following Council direction, formal adoption via resolution at subsequent administrative 
Council meetings: late October/early November; first Tuesday in November targeted. 
• September All-Day Work Session Outcomes: 
o Seasonal labor partially restored: 
 Museums: +$60,000 
 Parks, Natural Areas, and Trails: +$90,000 (allocation between trail maintenance and 
parks to be determined by Eric and Diana). 
o Council did not select additional options presented. 
o Department reductions: Target 3.7%; achieved 2.7% overall reduction. Culture, Parks & 
Recreation reduced by ~12% vs. initial 17% target. 
• Anticipated Community Impacts: 
o Changes will vary by service (Active Adult Center hours, recreation facilities operations, 
parks/open spaces/trails service levels, museums hours). 
o Expect increased public feedback once changes take effect in 2027.

WHY THIS MATTERS 
Taking care of what our community has built 
A coordinated approach to understanding, maintaining and planning 
for CPRD’s long-term public assets. 
Know what we own 
Create a reliable picture of our 
facilities and infrastructure. 
Plan ahead 
Anticipate repairs and replacement 
before assets fail. 
Use dollars wisely 
Support consistent priorities and 
responsible capital planning.
