5. Resolution Authorizing to File a Water Court Application to Adjudicate Ground Water 
Rights underlying the State Land Board Parcels 
 
Jim Noble presented to the Board that staff recommended approval of the Resolution 
authorizing the City of Greeley, acting through its Water and Sewer Board, to file a Water 
Court application jointly with the Colorado State Board of Land Commissioners for 
nontributary groundwater rights associated with State Land Board property in Weld County. 
The application seeks a determination of nontributary groundwater rights in the Upper 
Laramie Aquifer underlying approximately 10,495 acres of State Land Board property in 
Weld County for approximately 1,102,826 acre-feet of groundwater, equivalent to 11,028 
acre-feet over a 100-year aquifer life. 
  
Under a State Land Board groundwater production lease, Greeley has the State Land Board’s 
consent to adjudicate, permit, withdraw, develop, and use groundwater from the Upper 
Laramie Aquifer beneath the leased lands. The proposed Water Court application would seek 
to obtain a decree from the District Court, Water Division No. 1, confirming that the 
groundwater is non-tributary and quantifying the amount legally available for withdrawal and 
beneficial use.  
  
This action supports Greeley’s long-term water supply planning by preserving an additional 
potential water resource for future municipal and other lawful uses.

6. Water Supply and Drought Update 
 
Leah Hubbard went over that current forecasts indicate Weld County is likely to experience 
above-average temperatures and precipitation through the rest of this summer and into fall. 
Below average snowpack significantly reduced runoff and ditch yields throughout the basin, 
and reduced supplies. Specifically, junior water storage rights have not come into priority, 
limiting opportunities for reservoir filling. The Greeley Loveland Irrigation Company (GLIC) 
was out of priority on their reservoir rights and irrigators relied primarily on water in storage 
from past years. 
 
On July 13, 2026, the Boyd Water Treatment Plant intake at Boyd Lake could no longer 
provide water to the raw ponds due to low levels and water quality concerns. Water Resources 
staff and GLIC had already started pre-positioning C-BT water in Lake Loveland due to this 
predicted occurrence. In order to keep the Lake Loveland inlet in the water, Water Resources 
brought in 2,800 acre-feet of water (700 Windy Gap and 2,100 C-BT) into Lake Loveland to 
keep the treatment plant tower intake submerged and keep the ditch running longer.  
 
Staff analysis predicts the outlet from Lake Loveland to the treatment plant will be out of the 
water and inoperable starting August 20th due to the demand for water within GLIC, the level 
of the inlet tower within Lake Loveland, and drought conditions. The Boyd Lake Treatment 
Plant will start receiving water directly from a pump in the Greeley Loveland Canal, which 
was installed after 2002 drought and brought fully operational in 2026.  
 
Greeley declared an adequate water year in April 2026, due to projected storage volumes in 
April of 2027. Greeley is still above the required 21,300AF for mandatory drought restrictions 
this year. However, due to the infrastructure disruptions of the upper end of the GLIC system, 
and the potential need to treat additional potable water for high use turf facilities such as 
athletic fields, staff is recommending voluntary drought management of non-functional turf on 
city-owned parks and golf courses. This would allow low-use turf or higher water use 
landscapes to go dormant for the rest of the season.

Title: 
Water Treatment Plant and Transmission Master Plan Update 
 
Summary: 
The Water Treatment Plants and Transmission Master Plan project started in 2025 with a goal to provide 
a clear framework for phased water treatment and transmission system improvements that support 
reliable service today and position the system for long-term growth.  
 
The Master Plan builds on prior planning efforts and integrates five parallel technical evaluations 
focused on: 
• Bellvue WTP 
• Boyd WTP 
• Hydraulic Modeling of the Transmission System 
• Transmission Infrastructure  
• Electrical Reliability  
Together, these evaluations establish a prioritized capital improvement path to meet projected treatment 
capacity milestones of 50 MGD by 2033, 75 MGD by 2057, and approximately 102 MGD at buildout in 
2075. 
The chosen consultant for this work, Stantec, has delivered the first draft of the completed Master Plan 
report, which will be presented to the Board. The Master Plan provides a phased, defensible roadmap 
that maintains reliable day-to-day service while positioning the system for future demand, evolving 
regulatory requirements, and operational resiliency. 
Key findings highlight near-term capacity, reliability, and resiliency needs across major system assets. 
At Bellvue WTP, clearwell disinfection and gravity thickener limitations constrain capacity, while future 
rerating and expansion can increase treatment capacity to 60 MGD within the planning horizon. At Boyd 
WTP, future ozone and residuals improvements will support changing source water conditions, improve 
treatment performance, and reduce solids production. Transmission system screening identified targeted 
rehabilitation needs, the importance of a comprehensive pipe condition assessment, and the need for 
dedicated Zone 2 storage to improve system resiliency.

Title: 
Wastewater Treatment and Reclamation Facility Solids Master Plan Update 
 
Summary: 
In 2025, the Solids Long-Term Master Plan (SLTMP) project for the Wastewater Treatment and 
Reclamation Facility (WTRF) kicked off with the following key objectives: 
 
• Assess the condition and capacity of solids treatment equipment 
• Review and update projections for population growth, flow, and solids loading 
• Assess existing and emerging regulatory risks to current solids operations 
• Evaluate opportunities for beneficial use of biogas 
• Evaluate biosolids management processes  
• Develop a long-term (50-year) biosolids strategy for the WTRF 
 
Develop a capital improvement plan for 5- and 20-year time frames to support solids treatment needs. 
The chosen consultant for this work, Black & Veatch, has delivered the first draft of the completed 
Master Plan report, which will be presented to the board. In parallel to the SLTMP project, W&S 
department staff have continued working on evaluating long-term site expansion opportunities for the 
WTRF. Flow projections for the next ~100 years indicate that the plant will eventually need to expand 
beyond the boundaries of the current site. Through a high-level capacity assessment and development of 
conceptual site layouts, W&S staff have identified key properties adjacent to the WTRF which would be 
viable for supporting long-term growth.
