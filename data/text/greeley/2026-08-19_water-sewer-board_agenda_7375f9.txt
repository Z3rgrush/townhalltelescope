master plans and meeting the high level of service expected when providing water and sanitary

Greeley’s citizens pay. Projects such as the Water & Sewer F Street Operations Facility are

Title: 
Resolution Authorizing filing an application to adjudicate the ground water rights underlying the State 
Land Board Lands 
 
Summary: 
Staff recommends approval of the Resolution authorizing the City of Greeley, acting through its Water 
and Sewer Board, to file a Water Court application jointly with the Colorado State Board of Land 
Commissioners for nontributary groundwater rights associated with State Land Board property in Weld 
County. The application seeks a determination of nontributary groundwater rights in the Upper Laramie 
Aquifer underlying approximately 10,495 acres of State Land Board property in Weld County for 
approximately 1,102,826 acre-feet of groundwater, equivalent to 11,028 acre-feet over a 100-year 
aquifer life. 
 
Under a State Land Board groundwater production lease, Greeley has the State Land Board’s consent to 
adjudicate, permit, withdraw, develop, and use groundwater from the Upper Laramie Aquifer beneath 
the leased lands. The proposed Water Court application would seek to obtain a decree from the District 
Court, Water Division No. 1, confirming that the groundwater is non-tributary and quantifying the 
amount legally available for withdrawal and beneficial use.  
 
This action supports Greeley’s long-term water supply planning by preserving an additional potential 
water resource for future municipal and other lawful uses.  
 
Recommended Action: 
Staff recommends Greeley Water and Sewer Board approve filing of the State Land Board Non-
Tributary water rights application

RESOLUTION _3_, 2026 
 
A RESOLUTION CONCERNING THE ADJUDICATION, PERMITTING, 
WITHDRAWAL, AND USE OF NONTRIBUTARY GROUNDWATER RIGHTS IN THE 
UPPER LARAMIE AQUIFER UNDERLYING CERTAIN LANDS OWNED BY THE 
COLORADO STATE BOARD OF LAND COMMISSIONERS IN WELD COUNTY, 
COLORADO 
 
WHEREAS, the City of Greeley (“City” or “Greeley”) is a Colorado home-rule municipality 
empowered pursuant to Sections 1 and 6 of Article XX of the Colorado Constitution to, inter 
alia, construct, purchase, acquire, lease, add to, maintain, conduct, and operate water works and 
everything required therefor, within or without its territorial limits; and 
WHEREAS, Section 17-4 of the City of Greeley City Charter empowers and requires the City of 
Greeley Water and Sewer Board (“Board”) to acquire, develop, convey, lease, and protect water 
and sewer assets, supplies, and facilities; and 
WHEREAS, the Colorado State Board of Land Commissioners (“State Land Board”) owns 
certain lands in Weld County, Colorado, consisting of approximately 10,495.42 acres and 
including portions of Sections 19, 20, and 30, Township 12 North, Range 66 West of the 6th 
P.M.; Sections 22, 24, 26, 28, 34, and 36, Township 12 North, Range 67 West of the 6th P.M.; 
and Sections 2, 4, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 22, 24, and 36, Township 11 North, Range 67 West of 
the 6th P.M. (“Overlying Land”); and 
WHEREAS, pursuant to State Land Board Groundwater Production Lease No. OT-111631, 
dated July 9, 2026 (“State Land Board Lease”), Greeley is leasing and has the State Land 
Board’s consent to adjudicate, permit, withdraw, and use groundwater from the aquifer occurring 
in the Upper Laramie formation (“Upper Laramie Aquifer”) underlying the Overlying Land; and 
WHEREAS, the City and the State Land Board intend to file, as co-applicants, an application in 
Colorado Water Court Division No. 1, for determination of rights to nontributary groundwater 
located outside of a designated groundwater basin, underlying the Overlying Land, a draft of 
which was presented to the Board at its meeting held on August 19, 2026; and 
WHEREAS, the contemplated application seeks a determination of the nontributary character 
and quantity of groundwater in the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the Overlying Land that is 
available to Greeley for withdrawal and beneficial use under the State Land Board Lease; and 
WHEREAS, Greeley’s engineering consultants have conducted aquifer testing and analyses and 
reviewed available data and analyses concerning the Upper Laramie Aquifer, including data and 
analyses conducted for previous applications for determinations of nontributary water rights in 
the Upper Laramie Aquifer, and based on Greeley’s engineering consultants’ analysis of that 
information Greeley believes that the groundwater in the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the 
Page 18 of 77
Overlying Land meets the definition of nontributary groundwater as defined in C.R.S. § 37-90-
103(10.5); and 
WHEREAS, based on currently available data, Greeley’s engineering consultants estimate that 
approximately 1,102,826 acre-feet of groundwater, or an average annual amount of 
approximately 11,028.26 acre-feet based on an aquifer life of 100 years, is available from the 
Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the Overlying Land, subject to revision based on better or 
additional data and any decree entered by the Water Court; and 
WHEREAS, the contemplated application requests the right to construct wells and use the 
subject groundwater for all beneficial uses, including but not limited to agricultural, domestic, 
irrigation, municipal, commercial, stock watering, industrial, and augmentation uses, and further 
requests the right to store, use, and reuse the water to extinction on or off the Overlying Land by 
co-applicants or others to whom the co-applicants may lease, sell, assign, or otherwise provide 
the water, consistent with the terms of their leases; and 
WHEREAS, the Board finds that adjudication, permitting, withdrawal, development, and use of 
the Upper Laramie Aquifer groundwater underlying the Overlying Land pursuant to the State 
Land Board Lease will assist the City in acquiring, developing, protecting, and managing water 
supplies and water assets for the benefit of the City’s integrated water supply system; and 
WHEREAS, the Board desires to memorialize the City’s intent concerning the contemplated 
Water Court application and related actions necessary to adjudicate, permit, withdraw, develop, 
use, store, reuse, lease, sell, assign, or otherwise provide the nontributary groundwater rights in 
the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the Overlying Land, consistent with the State Land Board 
Lease and any Water Court decree. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE WATER AND SEWER BOARD OF 
GREELEY, COLORADO: 
Section 1. The City of Greeley hereby approves and authorizes the filing and prosecution, jointly 
with the Colorado State Board of Land Commissioners as co-applicant, of an application in the 
Colorado Water Court Division No. 1, for a determination of rights to nontributary groundwater 
in the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the Overlying Land. 
Section 2. The City of Greeley hereby confirms its intent to adjudicate, permit, withdraw, 
develop, and use the nontributary groundwater in the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the 
Overlying Land pursuant to the State Land Board Lease, C.R.S. §§ 37-92-302 and 37-90-137, 
and any decree entered by the Water Court. 
Section 3. The City of Greeley hereby authorizes the requested determination that the 
groundwater in the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the Overlying Land is nontributary 
groundwater as defined in C.R.S. § 37-90-103(10.5), and the quantification and adjudication of 
the groundwater rights in the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the Overlying Land that the co-
applicants are entitled to develop and use, exclusive of any artificial recharge. 
Section 4. The City of Greeley hereby authorizes pursuit of a decree recognizing the right to 
withdraw the legally available groundwater from the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying the 
Overlying Land at an annual rate determined by the Water Court based on an aquifer life of 100

2. General Description of Application: 
 
2.1 Overview: Co-Applicants are filing this Application pursuant to C.R.S. § 37-92-
203 for determination of rights to nontributary groundwater located outside of a designated basin 
underlying approximately 10,495.42 acres owned by the State Land Board. C.R.S. § 37-90-137(4) 
defines the nature and extent of the water rights claimed in this Application. Pursuant to the State 
Land Board Groundwater Production Lease No. OT-111631, dated July 9, 2026, (the “State Land 
Board Lease”) Greeley is leasing and has the State Land Board’s consent to adjudicate and 
withdraw the groundwater from the aquifer occurring in the Upper Laramie formation (the “Upper 
Laramie Aquifer”) underlying the State Land Board’s property subject to the Lease (the 
“Overlying Land”). A description of the Overlying Land  is attached as Exhibit A. A map of the 
Page 22 of 77
3 
 
Overlying Land is attached as Exhibit B. This Application seeks a determination of the 
nontributary character and quantity of the groundwater in the Upper Laramie Aquifer underlying 
the Overlying Land that is available to Co-Applicants for withdrawal and beneficial use. Greeley’s 
engineering consultants have conducted aquifer testing and analyses and have reviewed available 
data and analyses of the Upper Laramie aquifer, including the data and analyses conducted for 
previous applications for determinations of nontributary water rights in the Upper Laramie 
Aquifer; and the Colorado Supreme Court’s decision in In re the Application for Water Rights of 
Lazy D Grazing Association, 2024 CO 63. Based on information developed by Greeley’s 
engineering consultants and the technical reports, factual findings, and precedent from the other 
nontributary decrees in the area, Greeley’s engineering consultants have determined that the water 
in the Upper Laramie aquifer underlying the Overlying Land meets the definition of nontributary 
groundwater, as defined in C.R.S. § 37-90-103(10.5). 
 
2.2 Landowner: The State Land Board holds legal title to the Overlying Land 
subject to the State Land Board Lease. The federal government granted the Overlying Land to 
Colorado under the Colorado Enabling Act of 1875. The State Land Board separately obtained 
title to one parcel within the Overlying Land by warranty deed. The State Land Board holds lands 
granted to the state by the federal government, and otherwise acquired, in public trust , as provided 
in Colo. Const. Art. IX, § 9(6) and C.R.S. § 36-1-101.5(6)(b).  As such, the State Land Board is 
the legal owner of these lands.  
 
The State Land Board Lease authorizes Greeley, with State Land Board as a co-applicant, 
to file this Application to adjudicate, permit, withdraw, and use the Upper Laramie Aquifer

Title: 
Water Supply Update 
 
Summary: 
Current forecasts indicate Weld County is likely to experience above-average temperatures and 
precipitation through the rest of this summer and into fall. Below average snowpack significantly 
reduced runoff and ditch yields throughout the basin, and reduced supplies. Specifically, junior water 
storage rights have not come into priority, limiting opportunities for reservoir filling. The Greeley 
Loveland Irrigation Company (GLIC) was out of priority on their reservoir rights and irrigators relied 
primarily on water in storage from past years. 
 
On July 13, 2026, the Boyd Water Treatment Plant intake could no longer provide water to the raw 
ponds due to low levels and water quality concerns. Water Resources staff and GLIC had already started 
pre-positioning water in Lake Loveland due to this predicted occurrence. In order to keep the Lake 
Loveland inlet in the water, Water Resources strategically brought in Colorado-Big Thompson (C-BT) 
supplies. Greeley has moved 2,800 acre-feet of water (700 Windy Gap and 2,100 C-BT) into Lake 
Loveland to keep the treatment plant intakes submerged and keep the ditch running longer.  
 
The outlet from Lake Loveland to the treatment plant will be out of the water and inoperable this week 
due to the demand for water within GLIC, the level of the inlet tour within Lake Loveland, and drought 
conditions. The Boyd Lake Treatment Plant will start receiving water directly from a pump in the 
Greeley Loveland Canal, which was installed in 2002 and brought fully operational in 2026.  
 
Greeley declared an adequate water year in April 2026, due to projected storage volumes in April of 
2027. Greeley is still above the required 21,300AF for mandatory drought restrictions this year. 
However, due to the infrastructure disruptions of the upper end of the GLIC system, and the potential 
need to treat additional potable water for high use turf facilities such as athletic fields, staff is 
recommending voluntary drought management of unused turf on city-owned parks and golf courses. 
This would allow low-use turf or higher water use landscapes to go dormant for the rest of the season.
