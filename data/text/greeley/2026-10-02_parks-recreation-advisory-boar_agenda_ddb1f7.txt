RUSSIAN OLIVE REMOVAL 
Russian olives are a non-native tree species, originally from Asia and 
eastern Europe that were brought to North America in the late 1800s. It 
spreads easily and tolerates saline and other soil conditions that native 
species can’t establish in. Russian olives are a List B species on the State 
weed list, and the species is ubiquitous in most of the state. 
NAT has spent the past several years removing thousands of Russian 
olive trees from the City’s natural areas. In September, NAT partnered 
with Wildland Restoration Volunteers to conduct more removals at 
Signature Bluffs Natural Area. Over 140 trees were removed with 145 
hours of staff and volunteer time, and stumps are treated with an 
herbicide to prevent re-growth. Another Russian olive removal event will 
happen at 1st Ave Pond on Oct 20-21. A sign up link is in the CPRD 
events calendar. Crews removing Russian olive trees at 
Signature Bluffs Natural Area.

TRAIL COUNTER TECHNOLOGY 
NAT has implemented trail counters for years to count the 300,000 annual users on the City’s trail system, and over 
the years the technology has grown by leaps and bounds. The early generations of counters were simple in what 
data they could log – how many users passed by. A staff member would then have to manually download the data 
on-site. The next generation of counters had cellular technology, avoiding a monthly trip to the counter and adding 
more real-time data analysis capability. In addition, these counters could discern direction of travel and could 
separate bikers from walkers. 
Enter the next generation of trail counters. NAT just installed an Eco Counter CITIX-AI Evo at the intersection of the 
Poudre Trail at the red barn at Signature Bluffs Natural Area due to the configuration of the site with a variety of 
user types traveling in all direction. This new counter is well-suited for mixed traffic areas. With artificial intelligence 
and a high-precision sensor, it counts users by mode (8 in total) along with their direction. It can measure traffic 
flows on multi-use paths, intersections, bridges, and more. The counter uses radar technology to identify types of 
users, does not take or store any images or videos, and does not have any facial or other recognition ability. 
A richer dataset allows staff to better plan for future amenities and site management. If this pilot project is 
successful, these counters will likely replace old versions as they age out in the future.
