Union Colony Civic Center Board 
September 16, 2026

3. Presentation by City of Greeley Budget department. 
• Presentation given by Nathan Mosley, Director of Budget and Policy, City of Greeley 
o Part of the 2026 “budget roadshows” to increase transparency about budget 
challenges. Resources are available on the “Speak Up Greeley” page. The 
recommended budget was released on September 4, 2026, with adoption 
proceedings in October–November. 
o City Governance: Greeley is a home rule city (charter adopted 1958) with a 
Council-Manager form of government. 
o Budget Overview (FY2026 Total ~ $566M): 
a. Operating: ~$336M; Capital: ~$171M. 
b. Reserves Policy: 16.7% (two months operating). Projections indicated 
reserves would be breached by the end of 2026 without corrective action. 
o Revenues and Trends: Sales tax is the largest revenue source and is 
experiencing a downturn. Development-related revenues are affected by 
interest rates. Dedicated taxes/fees (e.g., food tax, quality of life) are restricted 
and cannot backfill the general fund. Enterprise funds (water, sewer, 
stormwater, golf, cemetery) operate on user fees. 
o Structural Deficit: An $18.1M (~12%) structural deficit emerged in the general 
fund around 2023 due to optimistic revenue projections, expense growth, and 
the expiration of one-time funds. 
o Budgeting Approach: Shifted from incremental to a revenue-first, zero-based 
approach. In February, the City Council grouped departments into three priority 
cohorts to guide reduction targets. 
  
 
   
 
 
  
  
   
   
 
   
 
 
 
   
 
 
  
   
   
 
   
   
 
  
   
  
  
  
   
   
  
  
  
 
c. Tiered Reductions: Police/Fire (4% target, ~$1M each); Mid-cohort including 
CPRD (17% target); Lower-cohort (25% target). 
d. Rationale: To avoid disproportionate cuts (no department exceeding ~30%) 
while ensuring all contributed. Department heads were given numeric 
targets. Council work sessions were held in May, June, and July, followed by 
a budget retreat on September 11, 2026, with no further changes requested. 
o Staffing Measures: Personnel costs are ~70% of the general fund. Over 60 staff 
accepted Voluntary Separation Agreements (VSAs), leading to a 67.75 FTE 
reduction in the general fund. Revenue enhancements covered ~15% of the 
deficit. 
o Taxes and Ballot Measures: Polling for a Police/Fire tax was unfavorable. A 
current ballot measure for an economic development project is restricted and 
won't address the general fund. The city is avoiding ballot fatigue ahead of the 
2028 “Keep Really Moving” reauthorization. 
o Fiscal Outlook: The city aims to rebuild reserves over five years while 
maintaining a conservative revenue posture. Residential growth often costs 
more than it yields; commercial activity is crucial. 
• CPRD/UCCC Budget Impact Discussion 
• Presenter: Diana Frick, Director, Culture Parks and Recreation 
o Initial Target: CPRD was assigned the largest general fund dollar reduction target 
in the city: $3.7 million. 
o Prioritization: A "dot exercise" was conducted using community surveys, 
attendance data, and Council priorities to rank 14 programs. Capital planning 
and facility management were protected due to deferred maintenance and 
development obligations. 
o Final Outcome: The CPRD leadership team identified $2.9 million in reductions, 
$800,000 short of the target. After Council declined more controversial program 
cuts, they returned $150,000, resulting in a final reduction of approximately 
$2.75 million (12.5%). 
o UCCC-Specific Impact: 
o Original Target: $255,000 
o Final Reduction: Approximately $190,000 
o Total UCCC Budget: ~$2.2 million (expenses and revenues) 
o Revenue Initiatives: Increased revenues proposed through in-house food 
and beverage operations (to be extended to Island Grove), tiered user fee 
increases, and a review of long-standing agreements.

• Bylaw Review Discussion 
o Discussion: The Chair initiated a discussion on the board's status as 
"advisory" versus "oversight" to establish a shared understanding. 
o Bylaw Status: Copies of 2018 bylaws were distributed. Staff (Megan) noted 
they were never officially signed by the City Clerk. However, Mark Pugatch's 
account was read, stating they were executed on May 4, 2018, approved by 
the board on May 9, 2018, last amended on June 24, 2024, and attached to 
the triennial review approved by City Council. It was clarified Council 
approved the review packet, not the bylaws themselves. 
o Path Forward: A member (Doran) suggested prioritizing a review of the City 
Code (Title Ten, Chapter Two), as it is the foundation for the bylaws. Staff 
(Diana) agreed and stated new draft bylaws based on a city template are 
being developed. 
o The board discussed its historical and current role, noting it has evolved 
from booking shows to a more advisory "safety valve" function, potentially 
reviewing controversial shows in place of City Council. Members agreed the 
board's purpose needs clarification via the City Code and discussion with 
City Council. 
o Process: 
o Staff and the City Attorney will review and propose updates to the City 
Code. 
o Proposed code changes will be presented to the board for input. 
   
 
   
 
  
   
 
  
 
 
   
  
   
   
 
 
    
 
 
  
   
   
 
 
    
   
  
     
 
   
   
 
  
  
  
  
o New bylaws will be drafted based on the revised code and approved by 
the City Attorney's office. 
o Outcome: The Chair tabled the bylaw discussion until the next meeting. 
• Ticket Donation Policy 
o The Chair asked the board to review the current ticket donation policy for 
future discussion. Staff (Stephen and Megan) requested changes to 
streamline the process and create clear criteria for applicants to protect the 
city from potential legal or political issues. 
• Vote for Board Vice Chair 
o The Board Chair, Dan Kanoff, announced his resignation effective after the 
current meeting. He explained that he felt he was not a good fit for the 
board's current advisory role, noting that he is an "oversight" thinker rather 
than an "advisory" one. He expressed that he felt he was driving staff crazy 
with his questions and that he could not continue to serve effectively in the 
current capacity. Regarding his vision for the board, Knopf stated that he 
viewed the group as an oversight committee rather than an advisory one, 
believing the board should have the authority to pick shows and make 
operational decisions. 
o Gii Astorga nominated himself for the role of Vice Chair with the note that the 
next meeting date would need to be moved out one week.  Board consensus 
was reached.
