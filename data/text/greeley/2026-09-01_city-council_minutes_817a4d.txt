15. Consideration of a Resolution Submitting to the Registered Electors of the City 
of Greeley, at the Coordinated Election to be held November 3, 2026, the 
question of Increasing the City Sales and Use Tax for the period from January 1, 
2027, through December 31, 2067, to fund Capital Projects  
City Manager McBroom and City Attorney Stacey Aurzada introduced the item at 
8:49 p.m. 
 
Mr. McBroom noted that since the previous week's work session, he held individual 
conversations with several councilmembers, noticing alignment around the ballot 
question. He stated that staff were recommending 0.6% sales and use tax to fund three 
capital projects:  
• West Greeley project,  
• Downtown Civic Campus, and  
• MERGE transportation project 
 
The tax would be structured for 40 years and would terminate early if the underlying 
debt obligations were paid off before that date. He acknowledged that council may 
wish to remove the MERGE project from the measure. 
 
Ms. Aurzada confirmed the tax would not apply to food purchases and would not 
authorize any new debt beyond the existing Certificates of Participation (COPs) for 
the West Greeley, Civic Campus projects, and the TIFIA loan for the MERGE 
project. She said she had prepared an alternate version of the resolution if Council 
chose to remove MERGE from the list.  
 
 
City Council Proceedings September 1, 2026 - Approved 
 
 
Councilmember Olson stated he had consistently opposed including the MERGE 
project, saying voters approved a food tax extension two years ago specifically for 
that purpose and there is no need to return to them for more. He supported the ballot 
measure to address deferred maintenance and protect programs.  
 
Mayor Hall stated that proposal was clearer than what had been before council the 
prior week. He referenced the Citizen’s Oversight Committee vote 8–1 in favor of 
placing the measure on the ballot. He supported the measure going to voters but was 
also concerned about the compressed timeline for voter education.  
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald stated she voted for the West Greeley COPs 
acknowledged that better communication was needed and she could do more. She 
supported placing the measure on the ballot. And she wanted there to be an oversight 
committee to monitor the spending. 
 
Councilmember Rudy advised moving forward to find a solution to the situation and 
help with the projects. He supported placing the measure before voters. 
 
Councilmember Roth stated he was altering his vote from the prior week. The revised 
ballot language, which included deferred maintenance items, and that moved his 
position. The measure addressed the city's financial challenges without requiring 
deeper cuts to quality-of-life services. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston stated he was opposed to the 0.6% rate and the 40-year 
length of term. He stated that a vote in favor would not represent his endorsement of 
the underlying projects. He requested guardrails for spending the proceeds if it passed. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez stated she remained opposed to bundling other projects 
with the Cascadia/West Greeley debt She stated she would vote “no”. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald moved to amend the resolution to remove references 
to the MERGE project. Councilmember Rudy seconded the motion. The motion 
passed 7–0 at 9:27 p.m. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald moved to adopt the resolution as amended. 
Councilmember Rudy seconded the motion. The motion passed 5-2 at 9:27 p.m. 
with Councilmembers Huddleston and DeBoutez voting “nay”. 
 
16. Introduction and first reading of an ordinance of the City Council of the City of 
Greeley concerning financing the acquisition and construction of a Downtown 
Civic Campus with associated amenities and supporting public improvements, 
and in connection therewith authorizing the execution and delivery by the city of 
a site lease agreement, a lease purchase agreement, and related documents and 
other documents related thereto; and providing other matters related thereto 
(2026 Certificates of Participation)  
Division Treasurer Robert Miller introduced the item at 9:29 p.m. 
 
Mr. Miller presented a summary of the proposed ordinance to issue the 2026 
Downtown Civic Campus Certificates of Participation.  
 
City Council Proceedings September 1, 2026 - Approved 
 
 
 
Key details included: 
• Maximum COP issuance of $95 million, maturing in 20 years; 
• Estimated average annual payment of $8.4 million beginning in 2032; 
• Issuance cost of approximately $900,000; 
• Total maximum lease payments capped at $163 million, to be paid from food 
tax revenues; 
• The city anticipates maintaining its Moody's double-A-3 COP rating; 
• Capitalized interest would be funded in years 2027 and 2028, with interest-
only payments from the food tax beginning in 2029 through 2031; 
• A competitive bond sale was anticipated in October, with final funds expected 
in early November. 
 
Councilmember Olson stated his opposition to adding another $95 million in debt, 
noting the combined potential payback across the West Greeley and Civic Campus 
projects of over $420 million over 20 years.  
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald stated her concern for issuing more debt at this time and 
suggested polling when large projects are being considered. She will not be 
supporting the ordinance on second reading. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked about the parking structure's total cost and the 
responsibility of the City to share in that cost. Deputy City Manager Kelli Johnson 
said that the city's commitment is approximately $20 million for its parking spaces 
plus $10 million pledged for Weld County, totaling approximately $30 million not 
currently budgeted in the COP. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez moved to introduce the ordinance and schedule the 
public hearing and second reading for September 15, 2026. Councilmember 
Rudy seconded the motion. The motion passed 6–1 at 9:38 p.m. with 
Councilmember Olson voting “nay”. 
 
17. Public Hearing and resolutions for the Metropolitan District Service Plan and 
Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) approval for West Greeley Commons 
Metropolitan District  
Interim Community Development Director Don Threewitt introduced the item at 9:38 
p.m. 
 
Mr. Threewitt presented the request to create a single commercial metropolitan 
district for a 36-acre property located at the northeast corner of U.S. 34/10th Street 
and 83rd Avenue, known as the Boomerang West minor subdivision. The district 
would finance approximately $12.14 million in public improvements, including 
roadways, water, sanitary sewer, storm utility, and landscaping infrastructure.  
 
The applicant is requesting a 70-mill levy cap. Mr. Threewitt stated staff finds the 
proposal to be consistent with all applicable criteria and recommends approval. 
 
 
City Council Proceedings September 1, 2026 - Approved 
 
 
The applicant's representative, Robert Rogers, presented on behalf of the developer 
and land use counsel. Mr. Rogers noted that staff, the city's municipal adviser Hilltop, 
and the law firm Butler Snow had all reviewed the service plan. He stated the district 
is solely commercial and that the city bears no financial responsibility for district 
debt, improvement costs, or development expenses. The estimated total debt limit is 
$15.5 million.  
 
He outlined safeguards including required disclosure notices, an annual report to the 
city, a public district website, and compliance with open meetings laws and the 
Colorado Open Records Act. He noted that any increases to the debt limit, mill levy 
cap, or imposition of fees would require additional city approval. 
 
Mayor Hall opened the public hearing at 9:48 p.m. 
1. Steve Teets asked how metro districts affect the tax burden on future commercial 
property owners and how long such districts typically last. Mayor Hall asked Mr. 
Rogers to speak with Mr. Teets following the meeting to address his questions. 
With no additional comments the public hearing closed at 9:51 p.m. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez moved to find that the request for approval of the 
Colorado Revised Statutes Title 32 Special District Service Plan for West 
Greeley Commons Metropolitan District is in conformance with City of Greeley 
Municipal Code Title 24, Chapter 12, and approve the request and adopt the 
resolution. Councilmember Olson seconded the motion. The motion passed 7–0 
at 9:51 p.m. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez moved to adopt the resolution approving the 
Intergovernmental Agreement between the City of Greeley and the West Greeley 
Commons Metropolitan District regarding the service plan for the districts. 
Councilmember Olson seconded the motion. The motion passed 7–0 at 9:52 p.m.
