4. 2027 Budget Retreat  
Opening Remarks 
City Manager Brian McBroom thanked the budget team, Director of Budget and 
Policy Nathan Mosley, Deputy Director Kalen Myers for their professionalism, 
patience, and persistence throughout the process.  
 
Mr. Mosley provided a budget overview, presenting key assumptions underlying 
the 2027 proposed budget. He covered audit highlights, revenue estimations, FTEs 
and trajectory showing the need for budget reductions.   
 
Mr. Mosley highlighted that the Citizen Budget Advisory Board had submitted a 
statement of support, which was included in the Council packet. He also noted 
that budget information, including department presentations and work session 
materials, was publicly available through the SpeakUp Greeley platform. 
 
Key changes since department presentations: 
• no further reductions to the Culture, Parks and Recreation Department 
(CPRD) beyond those already proposed;  
• additional funding recommended for museums and natural areas within 
CPRD;  
• a one-time contribution of $1 million recommended for the homeless 
shelter;  
• bonuses for employees in 2027 in lieu of base salary increases;  
• selective compensation adjustments for certain employee groups; and  
• continuation of the contract for private security services in the downtown. 
 
The next steps were:  
• incorporating Council feedback into the budget document;  
Page 17 of 211
• a City Manager's Proposed Budget Work Session on October 13;  
• first reading and public hearing on October 20; and  
• tentative final adoption date of November 17  
 
Council Discussion Topics — Roundtable 
Council members identified topics for discussion throughout the day.  
 
Councilmembers asked for explanation of factors that led to the structural budget 
deficit, primary areas of focus for additional funds, one time funding issues,  
dedicated tax funds, proposed employee bonus structure, service levels after 
budget reduction, deferred maintenance, staff reduction effects, one time and 
ongoing budget requests, contacted consultant and service costs, pay band 
changes, and communication/engagement services.   
 
Keep Greeley Moving (KGM) and Pavement Management  
Public Works Director Seth Sorensen reviewed the City’s pavement management 
program, including the use of Pavement Quality Index (PQI) scores to prioritize 
treatments across approximately 800 miles of streets. Discussion focused on the 
gap between deferred maintenance needs and available (KGM) funding, 
prioritization of older neighborhoods, public communication, and coordination 
with utility projects.  
 
Other Purchased Services and Consultant Spending  
Council discussed the need for clearer descriptions of large budget categories, 
particularly other purchased services and internal IT charges.  
 
CPRD — Museums, Natural Areas, and Special Events  
Culture, Parks and Recreation Director Diana Frick reviewed the 2027 service 
level changes, noting that previously considered reductions to natural areas and 
trails, the Farmers Market, and Arts Picnic will not be implemented.  
 
The Winter Market will transition to Zoë’s Cafe, the Farmers Market will move to 
Island Grove Regional Park, and revenue-generating and legacy partner events 
will continue with some reduced service levels.  
 
Homeless Solutions  
Deputy City Manager Kelli Johnson outlined the City’s transition from direct 
homeless services to a nonprofit partnership model beginning January 1, 2027, 
with $1.2 million budgeted to support contracted services including housing 
navigation, case management, and direct assistance.  
 
Communication and Engagement  
Council reviewed the Communication and Engagement (C&E) department’s 
budget and consolidated service model, including oversight of City social media 
and capacity impacts resulting from the elimination of a digital marketing 
position. Council discussed evaluating how effectively the restructured model 
meets department communication needs and maintains consistency across City 
social media channels.  
 
 
Page 18 of 211
One-Time Funding  
Budget and Policy Director Mosley reviewed the 2027 one-time expenditures, 
including funding for parks and museums, homeless services, employee 
compensation, health fund support, planning initiatives, and other non-recurring 
needs. Council requested clarification between one-time expenses and temporary 
multi-year commitments. 
 
Staffing Levels and Core Services  
Council discussed the need to define desired service levels before determining the 
staffing necessary to deliver core City services, rather than focusing solely on 
position reductions or external staffing benchmarks.  
 
Budget Transparency: Revenue and Expenditure Balance  
Council discussed the difference between 2027 all-funds revenues and 
expenditures, which staff attributed to prior-year fund balances reserved for 
capital projects rather than an operating budget imbalance. Council requested 
clearer separation of operating and capital funding and greater visibility of the 
five-year CIP and dedicated tax revenues, including food tax, quality of life tax, 
and KGM.  
 
Employee Bonuses vs. Salary Increases  
Council reviewed the proposed 2027 non-base-building compensation payment of 
2% for most employees and 1% for department directors and above, intended to 
offset increased health insurance costs without permanently increasing the budget 
baseline. Council also discussed market-based compensation adjustments, 
eligibility and implementation of the payments, position control procedures, and 
the City Manager’s authority to make classification and salary changes.  
 
Deferred Maintenance  
Council discussed the need for a comprehensive, prioritized inventory of deferred 
maintenance to better understand the total financial impact and support future 
funding decisions.  
 
Unused and Orphan Funds  
Council discussed special revenue funds that may no longer have an active 
purpose and requested greater clarity regarding their balances and authorized uses.  
 
Enterprise Fund Rate Increases  
Water and Sewer Director Sean Chambers reviewed the proposed 2027 utility rate 
increases.  
 
Council discussed development impact fees and the importance of growth 
contributing appropriately to infrastructure costs, with staff noting an impact fee 
study is nearing completion.  
 
Public Safety Tax — Structural Concern 
Council discussed the structural concern in public safety tax revenue, which has a 
future sunset date, for ongoing police operations and agreed that future budgets 
should reduce reliance on this funding.

Summary of Actions and Direction 
Director Mosley summarized Council’s direction as requests for additional budget 
detail, improved communication and transparency, and several topics for future 
work sessions. He confirmed that Council directed no fundamental changes to the 
recommended budget, with feedback focused primarily on greater clarity and 
supporting information.

18. Public hearing and second reading of an ordinance amending Title 12, Chapter 6 
of the Greeley Municipal Code relating to use of Dynamic Braking Devices  
Chief of Police Adam Turk introduced the item at 7:31 p.m. 
 
Chief Turk explained that dynamic braking devices were safety features installed on 
large commercial vehicles to assist in rapid stopping. The City's ordinance prohibits 
their use within city limits except when necessary to avert imminent danger. The 
existing classification as a misdemeanor offense required mandatory court appearance 
and carried the possibility of jail time.  
 
Chief Turk stated that this structure was disproportionate for what is primarily a 
quality-of-life violation generating noise complaints, and that it prevented violators 
from paying a fine without losing work time. The proposed amendment reclassified 
the violation as a misdemeanor infraction under Title 1, Chapter 9, preserving 
accountability while removing the mandatory court appearance. 
 
Chief Turk noted that approximately 30 violations per year were issued, primarily in 
response to complaints along Highway 34, West 10th Street, and the 83rd Avenue 
corridor.

Title: 
Resolution to apply for grant funds to construct the Sheep Draw Trail underpass at 83rd Ave 
 
Summary: 
The City’s Natural Areas & Trails (NAT) division is pursuing grant funds from the Non-Motorized 
Trails Grant portion of the Colorado State Recreational Trails Grant Program, operated by the Colorado 
Department of Natural Resources, to construct the Sheep Draw Trail underpass at 83rd Avenue. 
 
Currently, the Sheep Draw Trail extends over four miles from approximately 90th Ave to 59th Ave, 
where it connects to the Poudre River Trail. The underpass at 83rd Ave is a missing connection in this 
otherwise continuous off-street trail corridor. Providing a grade separated crossing of 83rd Ave will 
improve safety, connectivity, and accessibility.  
 
This project is a high priority for the NAT division and for the community; this missing trail connection 
is consistently mentioned as a need when public outreach is conducted. 
Construction documents are prepared, and this project is shovel ready. In 2024, construction bids were 
solicited, but they were much higher than the City funds available at the time. If grant funds are 
awarded, it will allow NAT to leverage existing City funds and move forward with this construction 
project. The total construction cost is estimated at $1,200,000, with $950,000 from City funds and 
$250,000 from the Non-Motorized Trails Grant program, if awarded. 
 
If grant funds are awarded, construction would be scheduled for 2028, following necessary contracting 
and permitting. The Colorado Department of Natural Resources requires a resolution from Council at 
the time of application, expressing support for the grant-funded project. Staff requests that Council 
approve a resolution to apply for these grant funds. 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
This resolution alone does not impact the budget. This resolution only expresses support for the grant 
application. 
 
If the grant is awarded, it would result in a one-time $250,000 positive impact to the budget. A grant 
award would require appropriation. The grant award notification is anticipated in March 2027. 
 
There are no long-term financial impacts as a result of this resolution.
