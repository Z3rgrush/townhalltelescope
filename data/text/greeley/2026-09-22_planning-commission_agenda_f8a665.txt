5. A public meeting to consider a request for a preliminary subdivision to consolidate the

known as the Judicial Center Subdivision (SUB2026-0011) 
Chair Yeater introduced the item as presented and asked if any of the Commissioners wished to see a 
presentation. Seeing none, Chair Yeater asked if anyone in attendance or online wished to see a 
presentation. Seeing none, Chair Yeater asked if the Applicant was present and wished to speak.

related to the project. Chair Yeater asked the Commissioners if they had any questions. Seeing none, 
Chair Yeater opened the public portion of the meeting at 1:18pm. Seeing no one in person or online 
wishing to speak, Chair Yeater closed the public portion of the meeting at 1:18pm and turned it over 
to the Commission for discussion or action. 
 
Commissioner Andersen asked when to expect to see the site plans, the next steps for this area. Doug 
May (Principal Planner) stated that the site plans are currently under review. The subdivision process 
must proceed first, beginning with approval of the preliminary plat, followed by recording of the 
final plat. Staff have been reviewing the subdivision and site plan materials concurrently, allowing 
the site plan review to continue while the remaining items are completed. Once the final plat has 
been recorded, staff can finalize the site plan review and proceed with approval. 
 
Motion by Commissioner Andersen: 
She moved to recommend that based on the application received and the preceding analysis, the 
Planning Commission finds that the proposed preliminary subdivision plat is in compliance with the 
Development Code Section 24-2031 and therefore approves the preliminary subdivision plat as 
presented. 
Second: Commissioner Franzen 
Vote: Motion passed 7-0

D. BACKGROUND 
The subject property was annexed to the City of Greeley through the Cedarwoods 
Annexation, Ordinance 47, 1997 (rec. number 2565618 & 2550640). Zoning case Z 12:97 
established single-family residential (R-1 now R-L), multifamily residential (R-3 now R-H), 
and local business zoning (C-2 now C-H) within the annexation area. Over the years, the 
annexation area developed with single-family homes on the north side, residential condo 
units in the center, and commercial services and business condos to the south along 20th 
Street. The subject property has remained undeveloped property since it was annexed. 
 
The subject property is established as Lot 2 of 1st Replat of Cedarwoods Retail Center (rec. 
number 4410539). The 1st Replat of Cedarwoods Retail Center created the current property – 
3.04 acres, vacated and dedicated easements, zoned in the R-H district. The site is currently 
owned by CH-Greeley, LLC.

Project Summary 
The Cedarwoods Rowhousing project proposes the development of 32 residential rowhomes on a currently 
vacant infill parcel located at the southeast corner of 71st Avenue and 19th Street in Greeley, Colorado. The 
property is located within the City’s Long Range Expected Growth Area (LREGA) and is zoned R-H (Residential 
High-Density), which directly supports the proposed use and density. 
 
This project represents a thoughtful infill development that delivers attainable, ownership -oriented housing in a 
location already supported by existing infrastructure, services, and surrounding development. The proposed 
rowhome product type responds directly to the City’s identified need for “missing middle” housing by providing 
a balanced alternative between single-family homes and larger-scale apartment developments. 
 
The site plan maintains a compact, efficient layout while emphasizing neighborhood character through 
enhanced pedestrian connectivity, front porches, and unit orientations that foster interaction and a sense of 
community. The development has been further refined through the review process to strengthen landscaping, 
buffering, and overall site design in alignment with City feedback and applicable code requirements.  
 
By introducing attainable homeownership opportunities within an established and growing area of Greeley, 
Cedarwoods supports the City’s goals for housing diversity, infill development, and the creation of complete 
neighborhoods. The project is designed to be both context-sensitive and forward-looking—contributing to the 
long-term stability, livability, and character of the surrounding area.

clearly demonstrates a pressing demand for diverse and affordable housing options. A residential rowhouse infill 
development directly addresses the challenges outlined in the report and aligns with the city's growing needs in 
several keyways: 
 
− Meeting the Urgent Housing Demand 
Greeley requires 6,700 new housing units in the next five years and nearly 19,400 by 2042. Rowhouses 
offer an efficient way to add much-needed residential units within the city’s existing infrastructure, 
maximizing land use without requiring significant urban sprawl targeting a missing middle type housing
