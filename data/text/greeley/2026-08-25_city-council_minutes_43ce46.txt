6. City of Greeley Audit Presentation to the City Council: Plante Moran  
Director of Finance Kirk Jones introduced the item at 5:48 p.m. 
 
City Council Work Sessions Proceeding August 25, 2026- Approved 
 
Mr. Jones recognized the Greeley finance team and the auditing partner, Plante 
Moran, for completing both the 2025 financial audit and the 2025 single audit on 
schedule.  
 
Timothy St. Andrew, Partner at Plante Moran presented the audit results and key 
findings: 
 
• The city received the highest level of audit assurance. 
• The general fund balance decreased by $16 million from 2024, ending 2025 at 
approximately $52.1 million, of which $38.9 million is unrestricted.  
• Utility operations remained financially stable, with water, sewer, and 
stormwater funds carrying a combined unrestricted net position of $109 
million at year-end.  
• The city invested over $203 million in infrastructure during 2025 and issued 
$115 million in debt for the West Greeley development project. 
 
Mr. St. Andrew noted that the 2025 consolidation of the sales and use tax fund into 
the general fund affected year-over-year comparisons, with taxes increasing and 
transfers decreasing by roughly equivalent amounts. Total general fund expenditure 
decreased from $170 million in 2024 to $163 million in 2025, with public safety 
remaining the largest expenditure category. The general fund ended the year with a 
fund balance representing 36% of the annual budget. 
 
Mr. St. Andrew reported no transactions lacking guidance, no improper period entries, 
no difficulties encountered, and no disagreements with management. 
 
With respect to the federal Single Audit, Mr. St. Andrew reported that the City 
expended $13.6 million in federal funds in 2025. This triggered the single audit 
requirement of three funds: the Federal Transit Cluster, the Drinking Water State 
Revolving Fund, and the Outdoor Recreation Acquisition Development and Planning 
Program. Unmodified opinions were received for all three programs, confirming 
compliance with applicable federal requirements. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez stated her appreciation for the auditors and city staff, 
noting that the audit had been a source of significant stress in prior years. 
 
There were no further questions from Council. 
 
Mayor Hall thanked staff and the consultant for the presentation. 
 
7. Comprehensive Financial Policies  
Director of Finance Jones introduced the item at 5:57 p.m. 
 
Mr. Jones presented the proposed Comprehensive Financial Policies (CFP), 
describing the document as the product of several months of work across the finance 
department with input from executive leadership, the budget office, and other city 
stakeholders. 
 
Mr. Jones explained that the CFP consolidated existing financial rules and standards 
currently scattered across the city charter, municipal code, Colorado Revised Statutes, 
City Council Work Sessions Proceeding August 25, 2026- Approved 
 
individual council resolutions, and administrative directives. Three drivers for the 
timing were:  
• certain policies are genuinely dated,  
• growth in Greeley requires updated financial standards, and  
• the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) identifies formal 
financial policy frameworks as a best practice that supports sound bond ratings 
and reduces the city's cost of debt issuance. 
 
The CFP was organized into eleven policy areas:  
• accounting and reporting standards,  
• annual audit,  
• budgeting and financial planning,  
• debt and financing,  
• cash and treasury management,  
• investments,  
• fund balance and reserves,  
• capital assets,  
• revenue administration,  
• grants and donations, and  
• procurement 
 
Mr. Jones highlighted that the document does not override existing legal authority. 
Where the charter, municipal code, or state law govern a subject, the CFP implements 
and clarifies that authority. Where those sources are silent, the CFP sets the city's own 
standards.  
 
He further noted that the vast majority of the CFP's content restates or consolidates 
existing policy, with three new elements:  
• a process for real property acquisitions including written analysis, independent 
appraisal, and title insurance;  
• a governance structure for donations; and  
• indirect cost recovery on grants 
 
Key policy provisions included: 
• Reserves: General Fund and Enterprise 
• Debt: Bond maturities, Balloon repayment, Debt Service, Refinancing and 
Interfund Loans  
• Grants and Donations: Grants exceeding $500,000 or requiring an unbudgeted 
match come to Council for approval 
• Capital Assets: Capitalization threshold, Depreciation, Leases, and Capital 
Improvement Plan   
• Policy Maintenance: The Finance Director will conduct a comprehensive 
annual review timed to the budget process.  
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked whether any conflicting policies were encountered 
during the consolidation process. Mr. Jones confirmed there were no conflicts and 
added that the City Manager will issue the policy with operational responsibility. 
 
City Council Work Sessions Proceeding August 25, 2026- Approved 
 
Councilmember Olson noted that the CFP does not address the question of Council

9. Long Range Financial Update  
Director of Budget and Policy Nathan Mosley introduced the item at 6:42 p.m. 
 
Mr. Mosley presented the long-range financial projection update, focused specifically 
on the general fund. He outlined the series of fiscal challenges since 2023, including a 
structural deficit caused by economic volatility, unfunded new programs, and rising 
inflation.  
 
The 2026 budget will have a 16.7% reserve, or two months' expenses. To mitigate the 
deficit, a target-based budgeting strategy was developed for 2027, resulting in a $16.8 
million reduction but still facing a $1.3 million gap. Factors for improvement include 
$1.1 million from 2025 carryover and service chargebacks tied to reduced budgets. 
The budget retreat on September 11, 2026, will review the City Manager's proposed 
2027 budget, with adoption scheduled for November 17, 2026. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez asked about the cost of providing a 3% salary increase in 
2027. Mr. Mosley stated that the recommended budget currently included a 2% bonus 
for staff and a 1% bonus for executive-level employees, totaling approximately $1.3 
million. A three percent base salary increase, by comparison, would cost nearly twice 
that amount and a raise to base is not considered feasible in 2027. The bonus approach 
could help offset the average increase to health benefit costs without creating an 
ongoing structural expense.  
 
Councilmember DeBoutez asked if staff could work on compiling the impact to 
operations if a straight 17% reduction across each CPRD division was considered. Mr. 
Mosley agreed to work on it. 
 
Councilmember Roth asked for clarification on the annual debt capacity review and 
its inclusion in budget and audit materials. Staff confirmed the information was 
presented annually in both the budget and the financial statements. 
City Council Work Sessions Proceeding August 25, 2026- Approved 
 
 
Councilmember Huddleston asked if staff had conducted an analysis of the root cause 
of the deficit. Mr. Mosley stated no single cause drove the deficit, identifying a 
combination of revenue projections that did not materialize, programs started with 
grant funding without a long-term sustainability plan, and salary growth. He stated 
that the budget process was now structured to require more vetting of grants and 
programs before they are accepted.  
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald noted that the divergence in revenues and expenditures 
was traceable to 2023 and stated that positions added at executive discretion by prior 
city management contributed to the structural imbalance. She asked for clarity on the 
2026 salary increases and the city's compensation plan process. Mr. Mosley 
confirmed that raises were provided this year, and bonuses were recommended for 
next year. 
 
Mayor Hall added that the compensation plan and pay bands are adopted by Council 
at a high level and that more discussion may be warranted. 
 
Councilmember Olson stated that the compensation plan adopted in late 2022 
represented a significant multi-year cost to bring positions to market rates.  
 
Mayor Hall thanked staff for the information. 
 
10. Ballot Measure Follow Up  
City Manager Brian McBroom introduced the item at 7:29 p.m. 
 
Mr. McBroom presented a follow-up to the August 11, 2026, Work Session 
discussion. He noted that most of the information had been provided to Council in 
advance and that the purpose of the item was to seek direction. 
 
Mr. McBroom stated that if Council wished to place a question on the November 
2026 ballot, a resolution would need to be adopted at the next regular Council 
meeting. He elaborated on one change to the project funding slide. Staff now 
recommended that Civic Campus COP payments be funded entirely out of food tax 
rather than a combination of quality of life, general fund, and food tax, due to 
increased demands on the other funding sources. 
 
Mr. McBroom noted a legal opinion had been provided separately to Council 
providing context on impact of a ballot measure delay on the Civic Campus 
development agreement. As for credit rating impact, if the tax were to pass, bond 
experts believe the city's credit rating would improve from triple-B to at least double-
A, representing savings of approximately $5 million per year, or $200 million over 
the life of a 40-year revenue bond.  
 
For bond terms, Mr. McBroom said that a shorter term was generally more favorable 
for voters, but bond experts advised that a longer term provided a stronger credit 
enhancement. He also noted that private acquisition of the revenue bonds upon 
issuance could potentially result in a slightly higher interest rate due to lack of market 
competition. 
 
City Council Work Sessions Proceeding August 25, 2026- Approved 
 
Councilmember Olson asked if a 40-year tax generating sufficient revenue could 
allow both the Catalyst and Civic Campus bonds to be paid off ahead of schedule with 
savings. Deputy City Manager/CFO Elena Portis confirmed that $200 million in 
savings included both principal and interest, and that the tax revenues modeled would 
allow the Catalyst project debt to be retired approximately in 2050. 
 
Councilmember Olson asked for a chart illustrating the payoff impact of the 0.1% 
Civic Campus portion separately. He suggested that residents understand what CIP 
projects would not be funded if a revenue solution was not found.  
 
Mr. McBroom acknowledged that if no tax increase passed, the three large obligations 
(Merge, Civic Campus, and Catalyst COP payments) would be drawn from the same 
funding pools that currently support capital projects, effectively deferring those 
projects. He said a specific list of deferred projects was somewhat speculative given 
shifting priorities but acknowledged the list of projects on-screen represents near-term 
unfunded needs. 
 
Councilmember Huddleston also asked whether bond market conditions could affect 
the city's ability to sell bonds even with an improved credit rating. Staff indicated they 
had not heard specific concerns directed at this project but acknowledged it is a valid 
consideration and noted that the 40-year term is in part intended to improve the city's 
attractiveness as a credit risk to the bond market. 
 
Councilmember Roth stated he shared the concern about the magnitude of debt being 
taken on and the generational implications of a 40-year obligation. He acknowledged 
that without a funding solution, the deferred project list on-screen would be a reality 
affecting residents across all city services and facilities. 
 
Councilmember Olson asked whether the tax could be sunset if the bonds are paid off 
ahead of schedule. Staff confirmed that if the revenue bonds were fully repaid, there 
would be no need to continue collecting the tax and it could be ended. 
 
Councilmember DeBoutez stated she was committed to finding a solution for West 
Greeley but does not believe there was sufficient time to bring a significant question 
to voters responsibly. She was willing to work on solutions in the coming year. 
 
Councilmember Rudy stated he was not in favor of placing a ballot measure before 
voters at this time. He recognized the need to address the West Greeley debt 
obligation but would not support a structure in which both pass or fail together. 
 
Mayor Hall stated that the window was too compressed to do justice to a decision of 
this magnitude. Issues extended well beyond the Catalyst project and maybe exploring 
a CIP-type approach with more preparation time could be placed on a 2027 ballot. 
 
Mayor Pro Tem McDonald disagreed with waiting until 2027, stating that financial 
obligations already in place make this year's ballot the appropriate time to act. She 
questioned why a CIP-focused option had not been presented earlier if it was under 
informal discussion and supported a combined measure for both West Greeley and 
Civic Campus. 
City Council Work Sessions Proceeding August 25, 2026- Approved 
 
Mayor Hall asked for consensus on whether to direct staff to add a resolution to the 
September 1, 2026, agenda for a ballot question. 
 
Following discussion, it was determined that there was no consensus to proceed with 
a resolution for the November 2026 ballot.
