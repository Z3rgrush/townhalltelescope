City Council Meetings are held on the 2nd and 4th Tuesdays of each month in the City Council Chambers.

City Council members may participate in this meeting via electronic means pursuant to their adopted policies 
and protocol.

are intended for discussion and an overview of key topics to inform future decision-making. While no 
formal action will be taken, we appreciate public interest; however, participation (virtually or in- 
person) is not part of the work session format. Public comments are made during City Council 
meetings. 
 
 
Watch Meetings: 
Meetings are open to the public and can be attended in person by anyone. 
Meetings are livestreamed on the City’s Meeting Portal https://greeleyco.portal.civicclerk.com/. 
For more information about this meeting, to request reasonable accommodations for accessibility purposes in 
an alternative format, or for meeting agendas, minutes, and archived videos, please contact the City Clerk’s

Key Staff Contact: Seth Sorensen, Public Works Director, Steven Younkin, Chief Engineer 
Title: 
Keep Greeley Moving: Pavement & Concrete Asset Funding Outlook, 2026-2045 
Background: 
Staff will present a long-range outlook for the Keep Greeley Moving (KGM) program, which invests in 
pavement and concrete assets. From 2016-2025, the program invested $116 million: $90 million in 
pavement and $26 million in concrete, raising the pavement condition index (PCI) from 61 to 69. 
The City maintains an estimated $1.3 billion portfolio of public works assets. The estimated life cycle 
funding need is $26.7 million annually in 2026 dollars. 
Greeley added approximately 15 centerline miles of streets from 2018 through 2025, or approximately 
2.7 miles annually. 
Staff will review pavement-condition scenarios, funding outlook, asset-growth impacts, and the 
2026-2027 program packages. A $14 million annual pavement scenario growing with KGM revenue is 
projected to maintain PCI near the City target of 70; lower investment scenarios reduce the long-term 
PCI. 
Life cycle funding needs are planning estimates based on 2026 costs, specified service lives and 
forecasted asset growth.  The pavement life cycle estimate assumes the network is distributed evenly 
across its life cycle.  Because much of Greeley's street network is newer, near-term needs may be lower 
than the modeled long-term average.

Infrastructure and Mobility
