4. Citizen Comment: There was no citizen comment 
 
5. Reports:  
• CPRD Report:  
▫ City Council Retreat: A City Council retreat was held on September 11, 2026. 
A recording is now available on the city council meeting portal. 
▫ Budget: The budget commission endorsed the budget process and 
recommended the budget move forward. 
o The first reading of the budget is scheduled for October 13, 2026. 
o The second reading is scheduled for November 3, 2026. 
▫ Museum Funding: The budget includes an additional $150,000 for CPRD to 
support part-time and seasonal staff for museums, natural areas and trails. 
Of this, $60,000 is allocated to the museum. 
▫ Museum Leadership: The museum leadership team, consisting of the 
curators, will meet on September 18, 2026, to plan for the upcoming year 
with the allocated resources. 
o Kristjana has accepted the role of interim museum manager. 
o The team will discuss the plan for part-time staff after the end of the 
year. 
▫ Metrics & Fees: 
o A board member recommended diversifying the metrics used to 
assess the museum beyond just attendance, especially given budget 
constraints. 
o Questions were raised about the updated fee schedule, specifically 
regarding items marked as "removed." It was clarified that this was 
mostly a technical cleanup (e.g., removing a fee for free admission) or 
consolidating separate fees into single categories rather than 
eliminating services. 
▫ Future Budget Concerns: A board member expressed concern over 
comments from the City Manager suggesting future budget difficulties, as 
the city remains in a structural deficit. The board questioned whether they 
would have an opportunity for input in the next budget cycle, referencing a 
past violation of the bylaws where their advisory role was bypassed. The staff 
liaison acknowledged the past violation and committed to bringing priorities 
to the board for feedback in the next cycle. 
• Museum Manager Report:  
▫ The board commended the interim manager, Kristjana, and her team for their 
efforts. 
▫ Kristjana reported that the team is moving forward with planning and 
operations. She will split her time between locations. 
▫ She is working to have Gus accept an interim Curator of Education position 
to increase hours. 
▫ The recent "Fiesta" event was a success. It went smoothly with an 
attendance of over 600 people (601 not including volunteers), food, and 
performances. The interim manager was thanked for successfully organizing 
the event. 
 
• Exhibits Report:  
▫ The "Our History, Our Voice" exhibit on the upstairs mezzanine is scheduled 
to close on September 27, 2026. The mezzanine will remain closed 
temporarily while the next exhibit is prepared. 
▫ The next exhibit is planned for an end-of-October opening. 
▫ A work-study student is in the process of being hired. 
▫ A suggestion was made to use social media to create "sneak peek" videos of 
the installation process for the new exhibit to engage the public. 
▫ Regarding the Mexican American History Project exhibit, the banners will go 
to Poudre Heritage Alliance, and the design files have been offered to MAHP 
for their future use. 
 
• Collections Report: 
▫ Last month, staff assisted 29 researchers and fulfilled three orders for 
historic media reproductions. 
▫ A project to process and catalog accumulated reference books is complete. 
▫ Work continues on a project involving sorting image files. 
▫ A "walkabout" was conducted in the historic village to inventory agricultural 
equipment. An issue was noted where a large road grader could not be easily 
found in the collection records. 
 
• Historic Sites:  
▫ Contractors are scheduled to assess the pointing on the Carriage House. 
▫ Work on the adobes is planned for the next month. 
▫ The roof and railing project at the Monfort House is expected to be 
completed before winter, though an installation date is not yet set. 
 
• Education and Interpretation: 
▫ Field trip season has begun. 
▫ Three seasonal interpreters have been hired to assist through the end of 
December 
▫ Gus is attending a national conference and is expected to return with new 
information to share. 
 
6. Task Force Reports:  
• Experience Team: 
▫ The task force reviewed key performance indicators (KPIs) to better reflect 
the team's work for budgeting purposes. This will be a larger discussion at a 
future meeting. 
▫ Various retail options were discussed to increase revenue, focusing on what 
is reasonable and feasible. 
▫ A past initiative involving using the museum's printing presses to create 
holiday cards for sale in the gift shop was recalled as a potential model for 
future retail products. 
 
• Acquisitions Task Force:  
▫ No meeting was held since the last board meeting. 
▫ The next meeting is scheduled for the end of September 2026. 
 
7. Old Business:

▫ Action: A list of current board members who are also members of the Friends

• Resolution Passed in August 2026: 
▫ Concern was raised that the resolution passed by the board in August 2026 
had not been formally sent to the city council. 
▫ The process stalled after initial communications with the city clerk's office. 
▫ It was noted that boards do not typically pass resolutions, which created 
procedural uncertainty. 
▫ It was clarified that while the council received an unofficial copy of the 
resolution, the formal, signed document was not transmitted, which was 
deemed unacceptable by the board. 
▫  The resolution will be printed for physical signatures from Nancy and 
Marcus to ensure it is officially processed and sent forward. 
 
8. New Business:  
• Approval of the Triennial Report:  
▫ The Triennial Review Report was presented for approval. A minor typo had 
been corrected since the draft was circulated. 
▫ A question was raised regarding Project Task 3, which listed an anticipated 
outcome of the Friends organization transitioning into a 501(c)(3). It was 
confirmed that this goal has already been achieved. 
▫ Motion to approve the Triennial Review Report made by Annareli Morales and 
seconded by Emma Pena McCleave. Motion passed unanimously.  
▫ A separate work plan document, derived from the Triennial Review Report, 
was presented for approval. The plan outlines four key areas with ongoing 
timelines. 
▫ Motion to approve the Work Plan as written made by Annareli Morales and 
seconded by Donna Hendren. Moiton passed unanimously.  
 
• Elections:  
▫ It was announced that elections for the positions of Chair and Vice-Chair will 
be held at the next meeting in October 2026. 
▫ Board members were encouraged to consider nominations. The current 
chair noted their term ends in March 2028 but expressed a willingness to 
support a new chair. 
 
• Deerfield Day:  
▫ George announced that "Dearfield Day" will take place on Saturday, October 
3, 2026, starting at noon at the Dearfield site. The event will feature music, 
food, entertainment, and descendants of the founders. Two of the standing 
buildings, the future visitor center (formerly the grocery store) and the 
Jackson House, will be open to visitors to show recent reconstruction and 
furnishing efforts. 
▫ It was suggested that the museum's social media channels, including the 
Facebook page, should be used to promote the event in coordination with 
the preservation committee.
