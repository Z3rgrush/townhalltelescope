2. Interpretation Regarding Requalification of Tenants for Affordable Housing in Block 9, Willits 
Town Center (300 Robinson Street) 
James Lindt presented a request from the Aspen Skiing Company for clarification on the renewal policy 
for the eight deed-restricted daycare professional priority units located in the HUB building at 300 
Robinson Street in Willits Town Center. The units were established under Ordinance No. 10, Series of 
2019, and required a daycare professional occupancy priority. This specific situation involved a tenant 
who originally qualified as a daycare professional but had since transitioned to employment with the 
Roaring Fork School District, while remaining employed within the Aspen-to-Glenwood Springs 
employment area. James Lindt noted that neither the deed restriction nor the original ordinance 
contained language addressing tenant requalification under these circumstances. 
James Lindt stated that staff's recommendation was to allow the tenant to remain and requalify under 
the Town's standard policy, which permits tenants to stay in deed-restricted units after a job change 
as long as they remain employed within the Aspen-to-Glenwood Springs employment area. 
Jay raised a concern about the potential for individuals to game the system by taking a daycare job 
specifically to qualify for priority housing and then switching occupations once housing was secured. 
Katie agreed and suggested that if there were daycare workers on a waiting list, the priority should 
 
 
2 
 
remain with them. Katie noted that daycare workers have limited earning potential and that 
supporting them with housing was a key community value. Jay and Katie both expressed that while 
they did not want to displace the current tenant, any future policy should include a mechanism to 
give priority back to a daycare professional if one came forward seeking a unit. 
Bentley noted that the original occupancy language appeared to be an oversight and suggested that 
the current tenant be grandfathered in, while updated policy language be drafted going forward. 
Bentley referenced the Town of Snowmass Village's approach of requiring tenants to maintain 
qualifying employment status and suggested similar language could be incorporated, including a 
notice period for vacating if a new daycare professional sought the unit. 
Amanda expressed reluctance to require a teacher to move but agreed that the daycare priority must 
be preserved for future applicants. David agreed that clear policy language was needed to prevent 
precedent setting that could eventually result in none of the eight units being occupied by daycare 
professionals. 
James Lindt summarized the board's consensus as allowing the current tenant to remain and requalify 
given the absence of specific language in the original approvals, while directing staff to amend the 
policy language to ensure that future tenants who leave daycare employment would be subject to 
clearer requalification requirements tied to the daycare priority. 
Motion to allow the current tenant to remain in the unit and to amend the policy language so that 
this situation is addressed more clearly going forward was made by Amanda and seconded by Bentley. 
The motion carried unanimously.  
3. Update on Leasing of Town-Owned Units in BCC Project 
James Lindt provided an update on the Town's six deed-restricted rental units in the BCC building, 
also known as the Midland Residences. He reported that three of the six units had been successfully 
leased to Town employees at Category 1 rental rates, which staff considered a strong initial result. 
The remaining three units were to be advertised to the Roaring Fork School District, nonprofits, and 
special districts in the Basalt area as the next tier in the approval ordinance, followed by general 
Basalt-area employees if units remained available. 
James Lindt noted that staff isstill working through punch list items with the developer on some of 
the units and that most of those items were being resolved. He also reported that the new Stotts Mill 
duplex had recently received its Certificate of Occupancy after a correction was made to the range 
hood clearance above the stove. James Lindt confirmed that the cost of the correction was absorbed 
by the contractor and that the unit would be advertised soon. 
James Lindt reported on a recent lottery for a Valley Pines deed-restricted for-sale unit that drew 28 
applicants, demonstrating continued strong demand. The lottery was held among the ten applicants 
in the highest occupancy priority group, and the unit was ultimately awarded to a Basalt school 
teacher. 
James Lindt also briefed the board on a trend he was monitoring involving Aspen, Pitkin County, 
Snowmass Villageconsidering a temporary reduction to the minimum annual work hours required to 
qualify for deed-restricted housing. This was being considered in response to the prior year's poor 
 
 
3 
 
snow season and anticipated impacts from the Aspen airport closure. James Lindt stated that staff 
had not yet observed requalification issues related to work hours in Basalt and recommended a wait-
and-see approach rather than proactive amendments at this time. 
Amanda stated her view that ensuring employees receive adequate hours was the responsibility of 
the employer, not the housing program, regardless of snow conditions. Jay confirmed that the

Katie noted the issue was likely more relevant to Snowmass Village than Basalt. James Lindt noted 
that the Town already has a process through the Special Housing Evaluation Committee to consider 
individual waivers for tenants who fall short of the hours requirement with good justification, and 
that documented volunteer hours are also accepted toward the total. 
No action was taken on this item. 
4. Member Updates & Other 
Member Amanda inquired about the status of the Pitch View development on Parcel 9 of Sopris 
Meadows. Planning Director Lindt reported that the project has received its final entitlements and

works facility project instead. 
Member Amanda noted she would be out of town the following week and traveling internationally 
for two weeks in October and would be unavailable for any scheduled meetings during those

RE: Amendments to Town Policy on Town-Owned Units Regarding 
Deed Restricted Unit Tenants Owning Residential Property 
 
 
DETAILS:   The Town’s Town-owned unit policy has included a requirement that 
tenants of Town-owned rental units cannot own improved residential real estate 
in Eagle, Pitkin, Garfield or Gunnison Counties or within the Colorado River 
Drainage from No Name to Rifle.  In advertising the Town-owned Midland 
Residences Units and Stott’s Mill Duplex Units, the Police Chief, Town Manager, 
and Planning Department heard from multiple Police Department and Public 
Works’ employees desiring to rent deed-restricted units in Basalt to be closer to 
work and be more available in emergency/on-call situations, but they own 
residences west of Glenwood Springs.  These emergency responders and 
essential Town employees indicated that they need to hang onto ownership of 
their current residences in the Colorado River Valley between Glenwood and 
Rifle to make a Basalt rental scenario feasible. 
 
As such, since it has been difficult to recruit and retain first responder and 
essential employees as defined in the Community Housing Guidelines, it was 
determined appropriate by the Special Housing Evaluation Committee (SHEC) to 
introduce an amendment to allow for first responder and essential employees 
that live in Garfield County west of Glenwood Springs to be able to rent a Town-
owned deed-restricted unit and retain their residences in the Colorado River 
Valley west of Glenwood Springs. 
 
 2 
 
 
PROPOSED AMENDMENT:  Below is the proposed amendment language for 
BACH’s consideration in underline and strikethrough format: 
 
A qualified person must not own developed residential real estate or a mobile 
home that has an address within the Roaring Fork Drainage situated in Eagle, 
Pitkin, Garfield or Gunnison Counties or within the Colorado River Drainage from 
and including the unincorporated No Name area to and including Rifle, and 
including but not limited to the incorporated areas of Aspen, Basalt, Carbondale, 
El Jebel, Glenwood Springs, Marble, Meredith, New Castle, no Name, Redstone, 
Rifle, Snowmass, Snowmass Village and Woody Creek.  An exception to this 
requirement shall be that a Town first responder or essential employee as 
defined in the Town’s Community Housing Guidelines may occupy a town-owned 
rental unit and own developed residential real estate or a mobile home west of 
Glenwood Springs, not including Glenwood Springs. 
 
In addition to the proposed policy adjustment above, Staff has received a request 
from another Town employee that owns a residential unit in Marble (Gunnison 
County) that would like to rent a Town-owned unit to be closer to work.  This 
employee works on the Town’s Special Events and often has to stay in Basalt 
until late in the evening.   SHEC thought it appropriate to bring this individual 
request to BACH for consideration as an individual waiver request instead of a 
policy change.   
 
PROCESS:   Staff recommends that BACH hear a discussion from Staff and 
then discuss the proposed policy amendment and individual waiver as outlined in 
this memo.  BACH shall make a recommendation to Town Council on these 
items. 
 
RECOMMENDATION:  Staff recommends BACH forward a recommendation of 
approval on the proposed policy amendment to Town Council.
