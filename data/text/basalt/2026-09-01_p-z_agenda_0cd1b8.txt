Staff Presentation 
Planner Carlton Henry provided a reintroduction of the development application at 218 East Valley 
Road. He noted this was a continued public hearing from the June 16, 2026 meeting. The revised 
proposal, updated since the prior hearing, is for a 93-key Residence Inn by Marriott hotel with three 
deed-restricted, for-rent affordable housing units for commercial mitigation, reduced from 94 keys 
and two units presented in June. 
The approximately 2.1-acre site is located at the northwest corner of the Town of Basalt, zoned PUD 
with an underlying C-3 commercial designation, and identified as Lot 2A in the Old Orchard Plaza 
PUD. The three proposed affordable units range from 457 to 643 square feet and are proposed to be 
deed-restricted at Category 2. Staff determined that the applicant has met or exceeded the 
commercial mitigation standards with the addition of the third unit and did not refer the application 
back to BACH following the resubmittal, noting that BACH had unanimously approved the 
application on June 11, 2026. 
Key discussion items identified by staff included: consistency with the Master Plan; the level and unit 
mix of affordable housing mitigation; compliance with minimum livability requirements; private 
open space; occupancy priorities (including the applicant's request to prioritize hotel employees); 
storage and bike storage; and a mixed-use parking reduction request. Staff calculated a minimum 
parking requirement of 97.5 spaces for the site. The applicant proposed 93 on-site spaces, with an 
additional 12 spaces required to be made available through a pre-existing parking sharing agreement 
with the adjacent parcel, yielding an effective 16.9% reduction in required parking. Staff also noted 
that since the June meeting, the applicant had proposed a PUD amendment to address building 
height (proposed maximum of 34 feet, 11 inches to top of parapet, with a maximum building height 
of 34 feet, 5 inches), to cap the floor area ratio at 0.8 (versus the C-3 allowable 2.0), and to remove 
prior floor area limitations. Staff highlighted that the architecture had been substantially revised 
following public comment regarding height at the June meeting. New 3D renderings were provided 
at the meeting, though not included in the advance agenda packet. Staff additionally noted receipt 
of updated referral comments, a formal will-serve letter from Mid Valley Metro District, and the 
most recent traffic counts from McDowell Engineering prepared for Eagle County in connection with 
the El Jebel Road reconstruction. A condition requiring monitoring of the Sopris Village well was 
added to the draft conditions. Staff presented three recommendation options to the Commission: 
recommend approval with conditions, recommend approval with amended conditions, or 
recommend denial, with the additional option to direct the applicant back to work with staff. 
Applicant Presentation 
The applicant's representative presented a summary of revisions and additional data assembled in 
response to the June 16 hearing. Key points included: 
Hotel Demand: The applicant referenced STR/CoStar market data showing that non-luxury, select-
service hotels in the valley achieve over 10% higher occupancy than luxury properties, with an 
Page 4 of 49 
average daily rate difference of more than $200, supporting market demand for the proposed 
product. Google search data for "Basalt Hotel" was cited as up 40% over two years. 
Building Height: The revised building stands at 34 feet, 5 inches from grade to top of parapet—a 
significant reduction from the prior 46-foot architectural elements. The applicant noted that the 
adjacent two-story building within the same PUD measures 34 feet from finished floor to the top of 
roof. Updated renderings were shown illustrating massing from Sopris Village Drive, and the 
applicant confirmed the existing trees within the adjacent easement would be retained. 
Lighting: The applicant described the selection of exterior fixtures with backlight controls to 
minimize light trespass off-site, consistent with Town of Basalt lighting standards. 
Employee Generation and Housing: The applicant provided supplemental data from their Durango 
Residence Inn (66 rooms, 13 full-time and 3 part-time employees) to support their employee 
generation estimates. The applicant also noted the addition of a third deed-restricted unit, 
representing a 50% increase over the prior application, and highlighted that the 2% lodging tax 
contribution to the Town's subsidized housing fund is estimated to generate approximately $1.4 
million over 10 years. 
Traffic: The applicant's traffic consultant from Kimley-Horn participated remotely and clarified that 
the analysis used a full-service hotel trip generation model (more conservative than a limited-service 
model) and that the study analyzed peak AM and PM commute hours consistent with regional traffic 
patterns. The consultant noted the existing road network was designed to accommodate a 662-seat 
movie theater, and that the hotel—even modeled conservatively—generates fewer peak-hour trips 
than the prior use at full capacity. 
Parking: The applicant provided comparisons to comparable extended-stay hotels in mountain 
communities, including the Homewood Suites in Steamboat Springs, to demonstrate that the 
proposed 93-space parking supply is appropriate for the use. 
Community Benefit and Tax Revenue: The applicant presented a summary of estimated tax revenue, 
including property taxes, sales taxes, and lodging taxes, totaling an estimated $11.8 million to the 
Town and associated jurisdictions over 10 years, with approximately $1.4 million directed to Basalt's 
workforce housing fund, $578,000 to RFTA, and $181,000 to the Early Childhood District. 
Good Neighbor Plan: The applicant described measures to address impacts on adjacent Sopris 
Village residents, including: reduced and downsized Marriott signage pursuant to a brand waiver; 
shielded and downward-directed exterior lighting; bear-proof trash receptacles; front desk scripts 
directing guests to Crown Mountain Park rather than the private Sopris Village park; regular 
landscaping and snow removal; quiet hours from 10:00 PM to 7:00 AM; and a guest parking tag 
system. The applicant also noted that Eagle County has agreed to install a no-through-traffic sign at 
the start of Sopris Village Drive if neighbors request it. 
Commissioner Questions 
Commissioner Maron inquired about the finality of the circulation and site plan, and the applicant 
confirmed it was very close to final, shaped in part by the fire department's requirement for a 
secondary access gate off Sopris Village Drive and the need to avoid the Sopris Village water line. 
Commissioner Kaplan confirmed that the parking sharing agreement with the adjacent retail parcel 
has been in place since the original PUD was established. 
Commissioner Leavitt sought clarification on the parking reduction percentage, which staff clarified 
encompasses both the shortfall from the code requirement and the 12 spaces fulfilled through the 
existing sharing agreement, resulting in the 16.9% figure.

tie the height and FAR modifications to the specific hotel development approval. Commissioner 
Oberkoetter expressed concern that, once recorded, a future property owner could rely on those

Commissioner Feirstein asked whether mechanical screening provisions similar to those at Willits

Commission Discussion 
Commissioner Fierstein expressed several unresolved concerns: the absence of any traffic circulation 
improvements beyond the boundaries of Lot 2A; the placement of an EV charger within the Sopris 
Village water easement; the unresolved legal question of whether entitlements run with the land; 
and the three-story character of the building relative to the surrounding two-story commercial 
context. He also questioned the tangible community benefit beyond the $1.4 million lodging tax 
contribution to the housing fund. 
Commissioner Oberkoetter reiterated his concern that the PUD and annexation agreement 
amendments, once recorded, effectively open the property to C-3 zoning standards in a way that 
could outlast the current application and developer, and expressed a preference for variances tied to 
the application rather than to the land. 
Commissioner Leavitt reviewed the Master Plan goals and objectives included in the staff memo and 
assessed the project against them, concluding that the application satisfies approximately 3 out of 
11 identified objectives—primarily those related to walkability and proximity to services—while 
falling short on housing diversity, mixed-use development, workforce housing, and distinctive 
architecture. 
Commissioner Maron, speaking from his background in architecture and hospitality, offered 
extensive commentary. He acknowledged the additional deed-restricted unit favorably and noted it 
exceeds minimum code requirements. However, he characterized the building's design as 
aesthetically inadequate for a gateway location, particularly given the Master Plan's directive to 
incorporate distinctive architecture at highly visible sites. His primary concern was the site plan's car-
oriented orientation and the absence of meaningful pedestrian connectivity to the adjacent RFTA 
bus rapid transit stop and park-and-ride facility. He argued that with modest site planning 
adjustments—improved pedestrian routes, bike amenities, and transit-oriented marketing—the 
building could meaningfully reduce vehicle trips and better serve Master Plan goals. He encouraged 
the applicant to engage a landscape architect with local experience to address non-motorized 
connectivity rather than relying solely on a traffic engineering firm. 
Commissioner Hirschfield noted that while she does not oppose the concept of a hotel at this 
location given anticipated increases in down-valley visitors, she does not believe the application is 
ready for approval in its current form. 
Commissioner Kaplan echoed concerns about traffic, the appropriate scale of development at a 
three-story versus two-story height, and the precedent-setting nature of the entitlement variances. 
He noted that Aspen One's nearby workforce housing project will also affect the El Jebel/Highway 82 
intersection, compounding future traffic impacts. 
Commissioner Vozick expressed cautious support for the concept, noting the applicant had been 
responsive between hearings, but agreed the project had not yet met the community's expectations 
for integration with transit, pedestrian infrastructure, and Master Plan objectives.

4A Continued Public Hearing – Jadwin/Black Mountain Final Plan Application  
4B Recommended Action: Staff and Applicant Comments, Public Comments, P&Z Discussion, 
Potential Motion 
Staff Presentation 
Planning Director James Lindt provided a summary of the Jadwin/Black Mountain Final Plan 
Application submitted by Jadwin Park, LLC for property located at 431 Emma Road. The 9-acre 
parcel, located just down-valley of the post office on Emma Road, contains a single-family residence 
and a mobile home and has received both sketch plan and preliminary plan approvals, with the 
Commission having recommended approval at both prior phases. 
The final plan proposes 64 residential units (a mix of multi-family buildings, townhomes, and 
duplexes) and 12 short-term rental cabins. Of the 64 units, 32 are proposed as deed restricted—24 
as category units with price and rental caps and 8 as RO units—plus an additional 20 "First Tracks" 
units available to local residents for the first 90 days of listing, bringing the total deed-restricted 
percentage to 81%. Approximately 4 acres of open space are proposed on the north edge of the 
property, with lightly improved river access. 
Staff noted that final plan review is primarily focused on consistency with preliminary plan approvals 
and resolution of outstanding items. Since the prior hearing, the applicant had revised the 
landscaping plan to comply with the Town's newly adopted wildfire resiliency code. Staff confirmed 
the applicant remains bound by the sketch plan condition affording the mobile home's current 
occupants first priority for deed-restricted units. 
On the "dog leg" parcel—a separate portion of the property that had previously been proposed for a 
daycare (denied at sketch plan as outside the urban growth boundary)—the applicant now proposes 
a single-family home of up to approximately 3,000 square feet to replace the existing mobile home. 
Pitkin County supports annexation of this parcel for this purpose, and staff expressed support for the 
approach, noting it does not constitute urban-level development that is required to be in the Urban 
Growth Boundary. 
The applicant is requesting waivers from three categories of fees: the water tap fee (renewed from 
preliminary plan, where council did not support it), the daycare fee (offset by the $700,000 
community benefit contribution), and the parkland dedication fee (offset by the nearly 4-acre open 
space dedication). Staff supports the daycare fee and parkland dedication fee waivers but noted the 
water tap fee waiver remains a council-level decision. 
Page 9 of 49 
On phasing and vested rights, the applicant proposes to file the first sub-plat within 5 years, all sub-
plats within 7 years, and have all buildings under construction within 10 years of approval—a 
structure similar to Sopris Meadows Parcel 5 and Park Modern. Staff confirmed this approach is 
acceptable. 
Staff recommended the Commission hear from the applicants and public, then consider a motion to 
recommend approval to Town Council. 
Applicant Presentation 
Architect Ramsey Fulton and development partner Michael Forrest presented for Jadwin Park, LLC. 
Fulton provided a brief recap of the project's evolution from the 2020 Master Plan through sketch 
plan, preliminary plan, and now final plan review, noting the applicant's iterative response to 
community and Commission feedback, including the increase in affordable housing from 35% to 50% 
category units plus the First Tracks program. He summarized the public benefits as: 81% deed-
restricted housing; 4 acres of open space and trails; a $700,000 community benefit gift; Emma Road 
and Midland Avenue improvements; improved public river access; a permanent water well 
easement; and pedestrian connection to Old Town. 
Forrest addressed financial feasibility, stating the project is viable as currently structured without 
seeking additional fee waivers beyond those already requested. He noted construction drawings are 
being prepared in advance of final approval to minimize delay, and that the development team has 
been in continuous dialogue with Alpine Bank and other local lenders. He highlighted ongoing 
interest from local employers and essential service organizations in purchasing or renting the deed-
restricted units. 
Forrest also raised the applicant's request for flexibility in the category designation of individual 
units—not changing the total number of units in any category, but allowing the designation of a 
specific unit (e.g., a studio versus a one-bedroom) to shift between categories in response to market 
demand. This flexibility would require approval by BACH and the Town's Technical Review 
Committee but would not require full council action. Fulton added that this flexibility is designed to 
maximize occupancy by Occupancy Priority 1 households. 
Commissioner Questions 
Commissioner Kaplan asked about the anticipated timeline for permit submittal, and Forrest 
confirmed the team intends to proceed immediately upon approval, beginning with infrastructure 
work, given that construction drawings are already being advanced at risk. 
Commissioner Hirschfield inquired about the status of an adjacent neighbor who had previously 
raised concerns during sketch plan review. Forrest acknowledged that one neighbor had requested 
significant tree screening, which is now affected by the new wildfire mitigation code, and confirmed 
the team had not yet contacted that neighbor to discuss the revised landscaping approach—
committing to do so. 
Commissioner Maron asked about confidence in the proposed material selections given cost 
constraints. Fulton expressed commitment to maintaining the material palette for aesthetic 
consistency across both deed-restricted and free-market buildings, noting the team has priced out 
materials and is not pursuing lower-cost substitutions for the affordable units. 
Commissioner Fierstein asked how category flexibility between unit types would work in practice—
specifically whether it would alter finishes or unit quality. Forrest clarified that unit finishes would 
remain identical regardless of category designation; the flexibility is purely about which income tier 
is assigned to a given unit based on market response at the time of sale or lease. 
Page 10 of 49 
Commissioner Fierstein also asked whether Teddy Street and Hawks Street were designed to the 
same drive aisle width as the library parking lot and post office for potential future connection as 
shown in the Master Plan; the question was directed to the civil engineer. 
Commissioner Vozick asked why not all free market units are proposed as First Tracks. Forrest 
explained that lenders treat First Tracks units as deed-restricted for underwriting purposes, which 
creates financing constraints, and that the financial feasibility of the project is substantially 
supported by the free-market townhomes and hospitality cabins. 
Public Comment 
The public hearing was opened at 8:46 PM. No members of the public—in person or online—
requested to speak. 
The public hearing was closed at 8:47 PM. 
Commission Discussion 
Commissioner Fierstein inquired whether the project would be eligible for Proposition 123 funding. 
Staff explained that the Town has opted into Prop 123 and completed the required expedited review 
process, but that current income caps make the project ineligible. Staff noted a regional needs 
assessment is underway that may result in higher income cap eligibility in the future. 
Commissioner Oberkoetter expressed ongoing concern about overall financial feasibility, and 
suggested as an accommodation that the $700,000 community benefit contribution could be 
structured to be payable from proceeds of the first three for-sale homes, reducing upfront capital 
requirements while still ensuring the town receives the full amount. Staff and the Commission noted 
fee waiver and contribution timing issues are matters for Town Council consideration. 
Commissioner Leavitt noted that the approximately 4 acres of dedicated open space remain largely 
within the 100-year floodplain (the floodplain boundary having been adjusted through a FEMA 
Letter of Map Revision), and that while a trail to the river is permitted, the land would not have been 
developable in any event. He expressed some reservation about characterizing undevelopable land 
as a community benefit of equal weight to constructed amenities. 
Commissioner Maron expressed continued support for the project's intent, while restating his earlier 
concern that as the last greenfield transit-oriented development opportunity within the urban 
growth boundary, a higher density may have been appropriate. He noted that this concern was 
raised during the sketch plan phase. He encouraged the applicant and town to ensure adequate 
public parking (noting two designated public spaces as potentially insufficient) and clear public-
facing signage identifying the trail access. He also recommended the town consider designating the 
Emma Road section fronting the project as a bicycle boulevard given the increasing prevalence of e-
bikes on the shared path. 
Commissioner Hirschfield stated she would vote in favor but wished to formally register her 
objection to the category flexibility provision, expressing concern that it could set a precedent for 
developer influence over occupancy priority allocation in a manner inconsistent with the Town's 
community housing guidelines. 
Commissioner Kaplan expressed appreciation for the applicant's perseverance and commitment to 
affordable housing and indicated support for the project. 
Motion to recommend approval of the Jadwin/Black Mountain Final Plan Application to Town 
Council was made by Commissioner Maron and seconded by the Commissioner Vozick. Upon 
roll call, the motion carried 6-1, with Commissioner Leavitt voting no. Commissioner Hirschfield 
voted yes with her objection to the category flexibility provision noted for the record.

TO:  Basalt Planning & Zoning Commission   
THRU:  James Lindt, AICP, Planning Director 
 
FROM:  Sara Nadolny, AICP, Assistant Planning Director 
 
DATE:  September 1, 2026 
RE: Public Hearing - 135 E. Homestead Drive Special Review for Setback Adjustments in 
the R-3 TN Zone District. 
I. Purpose 
The Applicant, Eric Hendrix, is proposing the addition of a 286 square foot garage to be attached to 
the existing residence at 135 E. Homestead Drive.  The addition will encroach into the front and side 
yard setbacks for the property.  Section 16-27 of the Code allows for the consideration of 
reductions in the front and side yard setback requirements through the Special Review process. 
II. Background 
135 E. Homestead Drive is located within Basalt’s Hill District and has a zoning designation of R-3 TN 
(Traditional Neighborhood/Hill District).  The site is currently improved with a single-family 
residence without a garage.  The site contains a short driveway area to the west of the home. 
The residence was initially constructed in 
1900 with a significant remodel of the 
structure in 2000.  The remodel did not 
change the residence’s proximity to 
Homestead Drive, with the home’s front 
façade situated approximately six feet from 
the property line.  Section 16-27(d) 
indicates that the setbacks for structures 
that were developed prior to August 22, 
1995 shall be the building envelope as it 
exists.   
The subject site contains significant slopes to the rear of the lot but is flat in the area of 
improvement adjacent to Homestead Drive.

III. Proposal 
The Applicant is proposing to add a 286 square foot garage to the western façade of the existing 
residence.  The garage is considered new development and is therefore subject to the setbacks of 
the R-3 TN zone district, which are as follows: 
Front Yard 15’ for living space 
20’ for garages 
Side Yard 7.5’ 
 
The proposed garage is setback five feet six inches (5’6”) from the front façade of the existing 
residence, and 12 feet from the front property line.  The garage will extend five feet into the side 
yard setback, leaving approximately 
two-and-a-half feet between the 
westernmost garage wall and the 
property line.   
The Applicant has indicated that the 
garage will be used for the storage of 
trash containers as well as other 
items that would otherwise be kept 
outdoors in plain sight and pose a 
temptation to wildlife.  The garage 
will also provide vehicle parking that 
may otherwise be parked along 
Homestead Drive, thereby 
contributing to the proliferation of 
on-street vehicles in this 
neighborhood.

Staff identified the following discussion items for the P&Z’s consideration.

1. Consistency with the R-3 TN Zone District.  The Code states that the intent of the R-3 TN 
zone district is to recognize and allow for existing patterns of traditional neighborhood 
development, emphasizing the single-family residential character of the traditional 
neighborhoods.  Staff finds the proposed garage to be consistent with the neighborhood’s 
single-family residential character. 
 
2. Consistency with Special Review Criteria for the R-3 TN Zone District.  
a. Conformance with Master Plan policies and guidelines and any illustrations and 
graphic guidelines included therein for maintaining traditional neighborhoods.   
The parcel is located within the Master Plan’s Single-Family Residential Typology 
area which emphasizes dividing buildings into a series of discrete masses that 
appear smaller in scale and avoids large garage footprints.  The proposed garage is 
setback approximately 5’6” from the home’s front façade, meeting the zone 
district’s offset requirement.  The garage cannot be placed at the rear of the site due 
to the steep slopes and lack of access.  Staff finds these criteria to be satisfied. 
 
b. Preservation of existing structures including sheds and outbuildings.   
There are no changes proposed to the existing residential structure that currently 
occupies the site. 
   
c. Treatment of and relationship to public areas including sidewalks, streets and alleys.  
There is no sidewalk on the south side of Homestead Drive.  The alleyway to the east 
of the property will not be impacted by the proposed garage.  On-street parking is a 
concern within the Hill District.  There is a benefit to Homestead Drive by potentially 
removing additional vehicle parking from the street and placing it on-site.  It should 
also be noted that the proposed garage would be setback approximately five (5) feet 
from the front façade of the existing residence.  The existing residence already 
impacts the Town’s ability to add a sidewalk in front of the property if the Town 
were to desire a sidewalk in the future. 
 
d. Consistency of architectural treatments and building materials.   
The materials and colors used on the proposed garage will match the existing home.  
The garage is proposed with a slightly pitched roof which compliments the gabled 
roofline of the existing structure.   
 
e. Consistency of mass and scale of new structures with other existing structures in the 
R-3 TN Neighborhood.   
The proposed garage is subordinate to the existing structure and is set back from 
the structure’s front façade over five feet.  This is similar to what is found at the 
neighboring parcel (125 E. Homestead Dr.).  Overall, Homestead Drive contains an 
eclectic mix of architectural styles.  Staff does not find the proposed garage to be

out of character with other existing structures within the neighborhood. 
 
f. Conformance with all applicable building and fire code provisions.   
The Applicant has stated their intent to comply with all applicable building and fire 
code provisions. 
 
3. Side Yard Setback Reduction.  In addition to the criteria above, the following items must be 
considered for a reduced side yard setback in the R-3 TN zone district. 
a. Concerns raised by the Fire District have been adequately mitigated. 
The Fire District has no concerns with the application, as provided in comments by 
Fire Marshall Brooke Stott dated August 20, 2026. 
 
b. There remains adequate light and air around the development to be allowed through 
the approval. 
The garage addition will not block adequate light or air around the residence or 
neighboring residence. 
 
c. There are practical or legal instruments in place that make it possible to conclude 
that there will be adequate separation between the buildings (e.g. maintenance 
easements between the applicant and the adjacent property owner or fire wall 
construction on the building walls built within the seven-and-one-half-foot setback).   
Staff has included a draft condition of approval requiring the Applicant to either 
enter into a maintenance easement agreement with the owner of the neighboring 
property or to provide fire wall construction for the garage addition.    
 
4. Front Yard Setback Reduction.  In addition to the criteria above, the following items must be 
considered for a reduced front yard setback in the R-3 TN zone district. 
a. The provisions of this district create an unreasonable hardship for the property 
because of exceptional and unique circumstances including size shape, topography, 
location or surroundings. 
The existing home was constructed prior to the setback regulations for the R-3 TN 
zone district, which causes any new construction to the front or sides of the 
structure to be out of compliance with the zone district’s setbacks.  The garage 
cannot be constructed at the rear of the property due to the steep slopes in that 
area and is being proposed where there is an existing street cut for the driveway. 
Similarly, the garage cannot be accommodated on the east side of the home as the 
structure abuts the alleyway.     
 
b. Exceptional circumstances exist on the property which are not a general condition 
within the zone district or surrounding neighborhood.

5 
 
As stated above, the Applicant is proposing the garage in an area that is most 
practical for the site due to the existing home’s location on the site, the steep slopes 
to the rear, and the alleyway to the east. 
 
c. The requested change would be consistent with the spirit and intent of this zone 
district and the Basalt Zoning Code. 
The request is found to be consistent with the intent of the zone district and the 
Code.  
 
V. Review Process 
The Special Review request requires review by the Planning & Zoning Commission, which will make 
a recommendation to the Town Council.  The Council is the final decision-making authority in this 
matter. 
VI. Referrals 
The application materials were reviewed by the Town’s standard referral agencies.  The 
summarized comments below have been provided in relation to the application.  Referral 
comments are provided as Exhibit B to this memo. 
1. Basalt Sanitation District – The garage is proposed to be constructed outside of the 
recorded sewer easement that crosses the backyard of the lot.  The District offered no 
further comments on the application.    
 
2. Basalt Arborist/Horticulturist – There are several Aspen trees and a chokecherry tree along 
the property line, and it is unclear to which property they belong to. These trees will 
presumably be affected by the proposed development.  The Applicant will be required to 
submit a tree removal permit for any expected tree removals with a trunk size at or over 6” 
diameter breast at height (DBH).  If the impacted trees are on the neighboring property, a 
letter from the neighbor acknowledging and agreeing to the removal of the trees will be 
required.  
 
3. Public Works – There is an existing curb cut for the driveway where the garage is proposed.  
Any changes to the drainage or driveway, if applicable, shall be reviewed as part of the 
building permit process. 
 
4. Roaring Fork Fire Rescue Authority – No issues were noted. 
 
VII. Recommendation 
Staff recommends that the P&Z hear a presentation from Staff and the Applicants, consider public 
comments, and provide P&Z discussion.  Staff supports the approval of the Special Review request.

MEMORANDUM 
 
To: Chair Vozick and Basalt Planning and Zoning Commission 
 
From:  James Lindt, Planning Director 
 
Date:  September 1, 2026 
 
RE: Courtesy Review- Code Amendment to Chapter 18 to Correct 
Renewable Energy Mitigation Program (REMP) Fees for Hot 
Tubs/Spas 
 
 
I. Purpose  
 
The Town Council made a decision on an appeal of a code interpretation in May 
regarding the Town’s Renewable Energy Mitigation Program (REMP) fees for spas.  The 
appeal that was considered by the Town Council challenged the Town Staf f’s 
interpretation that REMP fees apply to spas that are not pre-packaged spas and that the 
only spas that are exempt from REMP fees are pre -packaged/self-contained spas that 
are 64 square feet or less  as is discussed in Town Code Section 18 -23, Sustainable 
Building Regulations .  The Appellant made the argument that pre -packaged/self-
contained spas are not defined in the Town Code and that the Town’s fee schedule in 
Appendix C of the Town Code does not discuss the criteria for a  spa to be exempt from 
REMP f ees that are set forth in Town Code Section 18 -23, Sustainable Building 
Regulations. 
 
The Council agreed with the Appellant and directed Staff to amend the Town Code to 
correct the issue that the Town Code does not define what constitutes a pre -
packaged/self-contained spa and that Appendix C does not reference the criteria for a 
spa to be exempt from REMP fees.  Therefore, Staff has proposed language attached as 
Exhibit A to correct this issue.  The proposed code amendments add language to define 
self-contained spas and reference to the REMP fee exemption criteria in Appendix C. 
 
II. Process 
 
Code Amendments to Chapter 18 require review by the Town Council at a public 
hearing.  Traditionally, Town Staff has reviewed code amendments to Chapter 
18, Building Regulations, with the P&Z as a courtesy review to see if P&Z has 
any feedback.
