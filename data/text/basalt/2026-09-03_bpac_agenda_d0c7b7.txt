2. Basalt Project Updates. 
Kaasch-Buerger stated that the intent of her presentation is to provide information regarding the 
proposed shared Public Works and Police facility that has been prioritized by the Town Council.  The 
shared facility would be located on the Town-owned lot at the end of East Valley Road, adjacent to 
Highway 82.  The Town purchased this lot in 2022 with the intent of locating this facility there.  
Town Staff have worked with a consultant on conceptual designs, and it’s been clear that it makes 
financial sense to combine these departments in this location.   
Kaasch-Buerger discussed the reasons why the current police department is inadequate, including 
that it is currently located in a building that contains other offices, there is no holding cell, only one 
restroom, and lacking adequate facilities overall.   
She also explained that Public Works is spread out within five different properties throughout Basalt, 
including two trailers that are past their useful life.  The goal is to combine most of the Public Works 
offices into the same space along with the equipment to support better efficiency.   
Kaasch-Burger explained that Town Staff is working to ensure that public art is integrated into the 
plans for the building.  Currently the focus is on site planning.  The project is currently estimated to 
2 
 
cost $25million. The plan is to go out for a .5% sales tax on the November ballot and a sunset to the 
tax is being considered.  The plan includes being proactive about the future building maintenance 
and adding this into the plan. 
Kaasch-Buerger said that her intent is to provide the boards with a heads up that this is happening 
and is a huge priority for the Council and Town staff.  The BPAC can spread the word within the 
community and direct folks to contact staff with questions.  Gale said she has seen the word out in 
the paper and the Town is doing a good job keeping the public informed.  She expressed that this is 
so needed with the amount of growth. 
Kaasch-Buerger explained that the lot is currently going through the annexation process as it is 
located outside of Town boundaries.  The goal is to begin developing the lot in 2028, and the Town is 
working with the consultant Wold Architects on designs so that it is ready to go.   Wold Architects 
specializes in the design of police facilities.   
3. Sculpture RFP 
Nadolny presented the draft Call to Artists for a permanent sculpture to be placed in the roundabout 
at Willits in West Basalt.  The goal is to advertise the Call to Artists on Café later this month and have 
it close on October 23rd.  The BPAC will make their final selection of their top choice at the November 
meeting.  Staff will then bring the matter to Town Council for approval and get the artist under 
contract before the year’s end.   
Nadolny explained that she had largely borrowed language from the previous Call to Artist that was 
created for the Basalt River Park.  Since Willits Town Center is a different environment, the language 
has been updated to describe the walkable, urban environment.   
The BPAC members discussed potential adjustments to the Call, including providing additional 
images of the roundabout and Willits Town Center area so that applicants who aren’t familiar with 
the area can get a feel for the neighborhood. 
4. Updates 
a. BPAC Grant Awardees.  Nadolny provided an overview of the grant projects or 
programs completed to date, which includes the majority of the awardees.  There are 
some that are still underway, such as Mitzi Rapkin’s First Draft series at TACAW.  
Nadolny is working with Kelly Peters to secure park space for her adult painting 
series, which will likely take place late summer/early fall.  And she has just recently 
connected with Dance Aspen who didn’t know that they had an award pending.  
Nadolny said that this is the first year where so many awardees had completed their 
project or program by this time.  She will be sending out a request for a grant report 
from those applicants whose project or program is complete. 
 
b. Sculptures Consignment in Willits Town Center.  Nadolny reported that the Mountain 
Flower Gate sculpture, which was ranked highly by the BPAC, is proving problematic.  
The location at Patty’s Park where the gate was going to be placed now has a large 
park name sign.  Additionally, there are a number of light poles in the immediate 
area, making it difficult to properly locate these large sculpture pieces.  Lastly, there 
3 
 
is the issue of the marble blocks.  The intent is to place the marble blocks, which 
serve as plinths, in a location where the sculptures will be found year after year.  With 
the gate two marble blocks will be needed, and they will need to be moved to a 
different location after the consignment period has ended (and one removed).  
Coupled with the difficulty in finding two blocks that are the same height to 
accommodate the two sides of the gate, Staff has decided the best option will be to 
pivot to another sculpture.   
 
Reven Swanson’s original sculpture Taffeta is no longer available, but Reven did send 
images of a similar sculpture for the program.  Nadolny showed the BPAC the new 
sculpture for their consideration.  The BPAC expressed interest in this piece to be 
placed at the One 10 Harris Street parklet rather than at Patty’s Park.   
 
Gale is interested in Staff creating an image of the gates in Patty’s Park to better 
understand the context.  Staff will see if this is feasible to do. 
 
No members had any updates or future agenda items to discuss.   
M/S Gale and Mullen to adjourn the meeting at 6:10 pm.  Motion approved 3 to 0.
