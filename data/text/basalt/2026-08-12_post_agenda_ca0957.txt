Christoff introduced the design development update with Lionheart Places and reminded

Hillside Park.  Wolf also reviewed the results of the neighborhood survey.  Wolf reviewed the 
90% design/CD level site plan.  Wolf talked through the swing on the upper level, the monkey

asphalt pathway and EWF play surfacing.  Wolf talked about the restoration of flexible lawn

furnishings for the park including the picnic table, bench and trash can.  Bjurstrom noted that 
the irrigation battery operated clock would be relocated outside of the p ark, from its current 
location in the center of the park.  Trantow noted need for doggy bag station.  Brown asked 
about the two trees to be removed.  Beiser noted that the trees are borderline hazardous due

the possibility for a tee in the southeast corner of the property, and an opportunity to add 

 
 
 
additional shade from the southern sun.  Henry and Beiser acknowledged the comment and

4:10  3. Cliff’s Hillside Park Update 
 
Christoff gave an update on the Cliff’s Hillside Park renovation project by first reviewing the 
color choice that was ultimately made. After receiving the renderings in the Spanish Moss, 
Granite, and Silver color palette selected by POST, both the design team and Town staff felt 
the overall appearance closely resembled the color palette commonly associated with a 
desert military installat ion. As a result, staff and the design team have decided to move 
forward with the Redwood, Granite, and Silver color palette instead, which better 
complements the park's setting while maintaining the desired aesthetic. The POST committee 
did not voice any objections to this selection. 
 
Christoff also noted that demolition has begun and that the structural integrity of the play 
structures was minimal during demolition.

PUBLIC WORKS
CURRENT FACILITY
The central office is in an aging portable building with 
limited space for staff. It also lacks meeting space, 
lockers, changing areas, and restrooms.
Equipment and materials are stored in five different 
locations across Basalt. Each site has limited space and 
aging facilities.
Many pieces of equipment must be stored outdoors, 
which exposes them to the elements and increases 
wear and tear over time. Together, these conditions 
reduce operational efficiency, create challenges for staff, 
and contribute to more frequent equipment 
replacement.

PUBLIC WORKS
A MODERN PUBLIC WORKS FACILITY
A new facility would serve the Basalt community and be 
designed to meet future needs. New and additional 
features would include:
• Centralized space for staff and day-to-day operations
• Organized site access for vehicles, equipment, 
materials, and staff
• Heated and protected storage for Town vehicles, 
equipment, and supplies
• Dedicated space for training, meetings, lockers, and 
showers
• Functional work areas that improve efficiency and 
support long-term care of Town assets
