2. Basalt Project Updates.  
Gloria Kaasch-Buerger introduced herself as the Town Manager.  She wanted to provide the Green Team 
with an overview of a proposed development project.  She explained that ambassadors for the Town and 
stewards of the Town’s resources, the Green Team and other citizen committees are very important.   She 
introduced the concept of the combined Public Works and Police Dept. Facility, noting that this is an 
important goal of the Basalt  Town Council.  The objective is to provide the Green Team with solid 
information to share with the community and help members to be knowledgeable about initiatives 
coming down the pipeline. 
Kaasch-Buerger explained that the combined Public S ervices Facility is planned to be located on the site 
along East Valley Road that was recently annexed into the Town.  It is the location of the former Umbrella 
Roofing business and is the site where the Compost Outpost is located.  The site is ideal for it’s location 
and size. 
Kaasch-Buerger explained that both departments have outgrown their current locations.  Public Works 
currently operates out of five separate locations around town.  She noted that there is a lot of synergy 
between the two departments, which makes sense to co-locate.   
2 
 
She explained that this is about a  $26 million project, with $18 million of the project to be financed.  A 
.5% sales tax request will go to the voters in November.  This will increase the sales tax in the Pitkin County 
portion of Basalt to 10.05% sales tax , and the Eagle County portion of Basalt to  8.95% sales tax.  The 
measure is currently written to sunset the tax in 20 years.  Kaasch-Buerger said that she might be able to 
figure out how to use POST funds for the initiative, noting that there is less funding from state and federal 
governments these days.  The Town has applied for a USDA loan.  She noted that the Police Department 
is hosting an open house today at 5 pm to discuss the current condition and possible future facilities. 
Kaasch-Buerger reiterated that the land has been annexed into the Town.  Utilities will need to be moved 
onto the site.  Staff is working through some preliminary designs with a consultant to be ready to hit the 
ground running should the ballot measure be successful.  She said construction will likely begin in 2028.   
She will be leaning on the committees, like Green Team, for expertise.  For instance, the Green Team will 
provide guidance on how to make this to most sustainable building possible.   
Sales tax is a more predictable source of funding than lodging tax. Additionally, there are restrictions at 
the state level on how lodging tax can be used.   
Kaasch-Buerger invited the Green Team to direct questions that are received by the public her way. 
3. Building Benchmarking Program.   
Gamble provided an overview of CORE’s projects and successes to date, noting that the goal is to reduce 
greenhouse gas emissions throughout the Roaring Fork Valley.  
Currently CORE provides larger rebates to community priority participants, including local workforce that 
earn below the area median income. Gamble observed that the project is  off to a great start with 
$42,445.83 in rebates distributed thus far.  He noted that CORE is doing twice as much work this year than 
last year at this time for the entire region.  The CORE team is larger and doing more work.  The focus is on 
electrifying systems within buildings; CORE is no longer focused on solar.   
Cold climate heat pumps are the biggest project type for both residential and commercial projects.  T he 
technology has delivered reliable heating in recent years.  Also, with increased temperatures and smoke 
events, cooling is not so much a luxury anymore – there is a health benefit along with the decarbonization 
benefit.   
Gamble provided a project spotlight on Z Group Architects, explaining that they are fully electrifying the 
building and also undertaking an air sealing project.  This is currently a very leaky building.  Gamble shared 
a graphic of the building’s current energy use and then the future projected energy use , which was 
significantly lower. 
Gamble explained that b enchmarking provides a means for building owners to track energy usage  and 
allows for comparisons of buildings of similar size around country.   Benchmarking also s upports the 
adoption of building performance standards in the future if the Town chooses to go in that direction. 
He explained that the 2025 program year looks at data from 2024, noting that 2026 will benefit from 
having a full year of data from 2025.  He is interested in adding eight more buildings this year.   
Out of the 10 buildings currently benchmarked in Basalt, Town Hall and Valley Pines are two that are  
actively working on energy projects and analysis.  T he Town is looking into  an electrification project for

Education and outreach will be required to get the information out to the public.  The Green Team will 
focus on those items in the months ahead, and a work session will be scheduled with the Town Council in 
the coming months. 
Nadolny explained that t he continuing education credit opport unities that are a requirement of the 
Town’s Su stainable Bui lding Regulations program need to be expanded.  Outside of the live credit 
opportunities that are updated through the US GBC, the on -demand credit op portunities have not 
changed since the pr ogram was implemented ap proximately 7 years ago.  Staff is interested in working 
with consultants who could either create a new video for the program, or who could reco mmend a 
professional in the sustainable building sector.

The only other trail specific rules are outlined in the electric bicycle section of code:
1. The speed limit for all electric assisted bicycles is 15 miles per hour in all areas of Town when operated on a designated bike path, sidewalk, or non-motorized trail. Any electric assisted bicycle operated on the roadway must abide by the posted speed limit and be operated in compliance with the Model Traffic Code, as adopted by the Town.
2. Helmets shall be worn for all operators, riders, and passengers of all classes of electric assisted bicycles under the age of 18.
3. An operator shall come to a complete stop at all intersections and stop at all crosswalks with pedestrians present, before proceeding.
4. An operator shall not use a mobile phone while operating an electric assisted bicycle, unless they are utilizing hands free methods.
5. An operator shall not allow more passengers on an electric assisted bicycle than the electric assisted bicycle is originally designed and built for. This shall not prevent the use of aftermarket products, such as child carriers.
6. All electric assisted bicycles shall be equipped with a permanently affixed headlight and taillight if used between dusk and dawn. Headlights and taillights shall be visible from at least 200 feet and shall be activated when electric assisted bicycles are operated between 30 minutes before sunset and 30 minutes after sunrise. Handheld lights and rider-worn lights are not acceptable substitutes.
7. Operators shall at all times yield the right-of-way to pedestrians, equestrians, bicycles, wildlife, and all uphill traffic. Pedestrians have the primary right-of-way.
AND
A. Electric assisted bicycles may be used on designated bike paths, sidewalks, and non-motorized trails or operated on roadways in compliance with the Model Traffic Code, as adopted by the Town.
B. Electric assisted bicycles are prohibited on all-natural surface (dirt, gravel, grass) trails, other than Second Gulch Trail.
C. Electric assisted bicycles are prohibited at the BMX bike park.
D. Electric assisted bicycles shall be operated on the right-hand side of all sidewalks, bike paths, and non-motorized paths, except where the sidewalk or path width is less than 36 inches wide.
E. An operator shall dismount from their electric assisted bicycle on the sidewalks in the following areas:
a. 1.Downtown Broadway: Broadway Street between Grand Avenue and 5th Street, half a block (to alleyway) in each direction from Broadway Street on cross streets and sidewalks only. The dismount zone does not include the street surface.
b. 2.Downtown Eagle Ranch: Capitol Street between 1000 Capitol Street and Sylvan Lake Road, and 200 feet each direction along Founders Avenue, from the intersection of Founders Avenue and Capitol Street on sidewalks only. The dismount zone does not include the street surface.
F. Parks, streets, or other public spaces that are hosting special events shall be closed to electrical assisted bicycle usage beginning 15 minutes prior to the designated special event start time, as designated on the special event permit, and shall remain closed until 15 minutes after the end time of the special event, as designated on the special event permit, unless such special event is specifically for the use and primary purpose of electric assisted bicycle education, training, or competitions.
G. The Chief of Police or designee may, with or without notice, temporarily close any public space, park, or other public property to the use of electric assisted bicycles when in the interest of public safety, or due to an emergency incident.
