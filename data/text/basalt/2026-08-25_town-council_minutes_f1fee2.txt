expected to come in under budget, as the COOT streets project, some public works fleet

to reserves.

transactions, as well as lower Water Fund revenue tied to reduced tap fees associated with

Town Attorney Conklin advised the Council that once the ballot issue is formally referred, the 
Town as an institution may not expend funds in advocacy for or against the measure, though 
individual Council members may campaign on their own time. 
Mayor David Knight opened the public hearing at 9:07 PM. One public comment was 
received online from Ryan Slack, who expressed skepticism that the tax would pass and 
suggested the $40 million-figure being discussed was too high. Town Manager Kaasch­
Buerger clarified for the record that the project is currently contemplated at $25 million - not 
$40 million -with $18 million to be financed. 
There were no further comments and the public hearing was closed at 9:08 PM. 
Council Member Stevens offered remarks supporting the project, noting that the need for a 
consolidated Police and Public Works facility was identified in the 2020 Master Plan and in 
predecessor planning documents as far back as the prior decade. He expressed conndence 
in the Town's track record of fiscal responsibility and urged no further deferral.
