Basalt Public Services Building Update: 
 

 
 
 
Kaasch-Buerger presented on the Basalt Public Services Project. She explained the process 
of the Town Manager meeting with each citizen committee, and mentioned the potential for 
the group to be ambassadors for the Town given their involvement. The Town acquired the 
parcels for the project with the proposed use in mind. Kaasch-Buerger described the 
relationship between the Public Works and Police departments. 
 
Kaasch-Buerger described the “why” for the project, siting outdated facilities, multiple 
locations (5 for PWX), limited space for police work, outgrowing of the current condo space. 
 
Kaasch-Buerger described the new building as a facility under one roof, a functional space 
for the intended uses, and example of the deficiencies in current space being that there is 
only one bathroom in the police department. 
 
Project costs – Current budget is $25M of which $7M could be paid from reserves. There is 
potential to monetize some assets (like PD’s current space) and grant funding like the USDA 
facilities grant. 
 
Trantow asked about growth and flexibility being built into the new space. 
 
Kaasch-Buerger described the project as having some flexibility and the community space 
could be built out as additional office.  
 
Kaasch-Buerger confirmed that the tax will have a 20 year sunset. The tax will affect the sales 
tax in both counties. The campaign rules were explained, and Kaasch-Buerger offered ideas 
on how POST members could be more involved. 
 
Kaasch-Buerger described the relationship to POST – Public Works takes care of everything 
POST works on. Want them to be as proud of their office working conditions as they are of 
the Town’s parks. Council has fast tracked the project which is currently in design. 
 
Trantow asked about looking at the trail connections near the site. 
 
Hilts suggested the project is long overdue and asked about the long -term viability of the 
project. Will other properties still be used? Has the Town considered selling some properties? 
 
Kaasch-Buerger doesn’t expect the need for change at the building for at least 30 years. The 
Town will need to keep the Fiou PWX site and the water plant. The site referred to as the 
Annex is in flux, and sales are being considered to help offset some costs.  
 
Trantow suggested the Town look at ways to accelerate the pay down. Feels this would help 
the message as he usually votes down similar questions. 
 
Kaasch-Buerger noted that if the Town has more income via the 0.5% it would all go towards 
the project, for a faster pay down. If the Town sees other tax ballot balloons it could be 
contemplated to go toward the project pay down. 
  
Trantow asked if there are any other sunsetting taxes. 
 
 
 
 
Kaasch-Buerger said no, but POST currently has a  80/20 split for capitol/maintenance and 
capitol has bene ballooning in recent years. 
 
Groves asked about the pool COPs.  
 
Kaasch-Buerger confirmed the Town paid these off in 2026. 
 
Trantow suggested the Town might win over the nay -sayers with the sunset detail, and was 
glad to know the Town had paid down bonds. Suggested that the Town has capacity for this 
project. 
 
Hilts asked what other groups have said. 
 
Kaasch-Buerger noted that P&Z liked it but wanted to confirm the cuing at the Hwy 82 
streetlight was reasonable. BPAC was interested in an art install at the site. Green Team was 
interested in LEED certification, efficiency, that is is a model building, and suggested looking 
at RMI as a model. She confirmed that staff heard POST’s interest in creating trail 
connections.

POST Financials Review: 
 
FY27 Revenues by Revenue Source 
 
Doug Pattison, Finance Director for the Town of Basalt, introduced himself and provided an 
overview of the Town's proposed FY2027 budget and the projected funding available to 
POST. Pattison distributed a handout titled "FY27 Revenues by Revenue Source," which 
outlined the Town's funding sources and revenue structure. He highlighted that the FY2027 
budgeted revenue is projected to be $3,186,896. He explained that the Town has seven 
primary revenue sources. As an example, he noted that for every $100 spent in Town, 

 
 
 
approximately $3 is allocated to the Town, $2 is allocated to committees, and $1 is available 
to POST. Pattison also reported that the Town begins the year with approximately $3 million 
in reserves and stated that the goal is to avoid drawing from those reserves. 
 
George Trantow asked how the reserve funds had accumulated. Chris Beiser explained that 
revenues during the COVID-19 pandemic exceeded budget projections. Pattison added that 
increased sales tax revenue following the Wayfair decision, along with modest pop ulation 
growth in the Town, may have also contributed to the reserve balance. However, he noted 
that reserve levels fluctuate from year to year. 
 
FY27 Expenditures by Department 
 
Pattison distributed a handout titled "FY27 Expenditures by Department" and reviewed 
departmental expenditures with the Board. During the discussion, he noted that additional 
funding is needed for maintenance operations, which have historically been underf unded.  
Chris Beiser explained that community expectations for parks and recreational facilities have 
increased, resulting in greater maintenance demands.  Board members also discussed the 
distinction between expensing and capitalizing funds. Pattison explained that certain funds 
are restricted because they are classified as capital funds and therefore cannot be used for 
operating expenses. Gloria Kaasch-Buerger stated that she would like to see this issue placed 
on the 2027 ballot to allow the Town greater access to capitalized funds. 
 
FY27-FY31 Capital Cost by Department 
 
Pattison distributed a handout titled "FY27 -FY31 Capital Cost by Department" and reviewed 
the Town's projected capital expenditures over the next five years. He noted that Phase II of 
the Willits Lane Safety and Mobility Project is expected to be one of the most significant capital 
projects during that period. Charlie Eckart asked how the project would be funded when the 
capital plan showed approximately $160,000 allocated to the project, while previous outreach 
materials indicated an estimated project cost of approximately $11 million. Catherine Christoff 
clarified that the project scope, budget, and funding plan are still being finalized.

2026 POST Project Review: 
 
Cliff’s Hillside Park   
 
Catherine Christoff provided an update on the progress of Cliff's Hillside Park. She noted that 
the project is currently on a brief pause while the play structures are being delivered. 
Contractors want to avoid resurfacing the park twice, so work will resu me once the playsets 
arrive. At that time, the contractors will continue construction and complete the project. Charlie 
Eckart asked how old the photos in the presentation were. Chris Beiser responded that the 
park still looks much the same and that the photos were taken approximately one month ago. 
 
Midland Park Boardwalk 
 
Chris Beiser reported that the Midland Park Boardwalk bridge project was recently completed. 
After visiting the site, he said the bridge looks excellent. He noted that the project used a new 
material recommended by the contractor, which offers greater durability than traditional wood. 
 
 
 
 
Duroux Park (Boat Ramp + RFOV Site Work)  
 
Catherine Christoff shared that the project is ongoing and has received overwhelmingly 
positive feedback from Town residents. She noted that the project is nearing completion and 
that contractors were pouring concrete on the day of the meeting. The park is expected to be 
accessible to the public within the coming weeks. Charlie Eckart asked about the purpose of 
the green tube located next to the boat ramp. Christoff explained that the tube contains water 
and is used to maintain a lower water level in that area than in the rest of the river, making it 
easier for contractors to complete their work. 
 
Willits Lane Safety and Mobility Project 
 
Catherine Christoff provided an update on the Willits Lane Safety and Mobility Project, noting 
that the project plans have been completed. Chris Beiser added that the planning and public 
outreach phase is now complete, and the Town is moving into the next stage of the project. 
Beiser explained that the project faces several challenges, including inconsistent property 
lines. Because the proposed improvements cross approximately 30 properties, consent from 
affected property owners will be required before the project can move forward. Christoff noted 
that a small park near the Hooks Lane intersection will be relocated to improve accessibility 
and enhance safety in the area. George Trantow expressed concern about a small parcel of 
land that is currently being used for parking. Beiser said he was familiar with the area and 
noted that it was not intended for parking because it is unpaved and uneven. However, since 
residents are using the space for parking, he agreed to look into the matter further. 
 
Willits Lane Boat Ramp 
 
Catherine Christoff provided an update that the Town is running into some easement issues 
with this project.  
 
Park Monument Signs + Wayfinding Signs 
 
Catherine Christoff provided an update on the park monument signs, noting that the signs 
have been completed and installed throughout Town. She explained that staff considered the 
Board's feedback regarding the sign materials to improve their visibility an d appearance. 
However, the original material was retained because an alternative glossy finish would require 
annual maintenance. Chris Beiser added that the proposed glossy finish and material would 
likely begin to rust within six months. He noted that the fish feature on the sign could serve as 
a test case to observe how the material weathers over time.  Paul Hilts stated that he 
understood the decision but suggested that future signs could be made from materials that 
improve visibility and make the signs stand out more. 
 
 
Confluence Park STRS Phase I 
 
Catherine Christoff noted that this project is taking a little longer than expected but is soon to 
complete phase I as the contractors pour final concrete. Once this part of the project is 
completed it will move onto phase II. Charlie Eckart asked for clarification on where they are 
pouring concrete and Christoff clarified the location. 
 
 
 
 
 
Other 2026 Projects 
 
Chris Beiser provided an update on several smaller projects completed in 2026. He noted that 
the Roaring Fork Outdoor Volunteers completed work on Light Hill and Basalt Mountain. The 
group also planted a pollinator garden at Basalt River Park and contribut ed to the Basalt 
Mountain wildfire mitigation project. 
 
Beiser also shared updates on improvements to the mini golf facility, including the installation 
of a fence, irrigation system, and new landscaping. Board members expressed their 
satisfaction with the completion of the project and noted that local children  will benefit from 
the improved facility. Beiser added that the only ongoing expense is replacing the golf balls 
approximately every six months. 
 
Lastly, Beiser updated the Board on plans to install new furniture at Triangle Park, add bike 
racks at Basalt River Park, and increase tree planting throughout the park. 
 
Charlie Eckart recommended that staff look into the bike racks currently being used by the 
Town of Aspen, which were designed by a member of the Aspen community. Beiser agreed 
to explore the recommendation. 
Members asked about an update to a trail that is controlled by Pitkin County.  
 
Capital Improvement Funding for Court Resurfacing Midvalley Health Institute 
 
Catherine Christoff noted that an additional agenda item had been added: a request to 
allocate $17,000 in POST funding for resurfacing the tennis courts at Mid Valley Institute. She 
explained that the funds would be used to improve the courts, make them mo re versatile for 
multiple activities, and enhance their overall usability. Christoff clarified that this was a one -
time capital improvement request and not a request for ongoing operations or maintenance 
funding. 
 
Board members expressed support for the request, noting that the facility is well used by 
Town residents and is accessible to the broader community. 
 
George Trantow stated that he could personally attest to the facility's value and frequent use. 
He noted that many of the people who use the courts are his neighbors and friends and that 
he worked in a building adjacent to the courts for many years, allowi ng him to observe the 
facility's consistent use over time.
