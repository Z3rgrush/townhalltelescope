RECITALS 
 
A. The Town 
of Basalt,  Colorado (the “ Town”), is a home rule municipality, duly 
organized and existing pursuant to Section 6 of Article XX of the Colorado 
Constitution and the laws of the State of Colorado. 
 
B. The Town Council has the power to adopt this Resolution pursuant to Colorado 
Fair Campaign Practices Act, C.R.S. § 1-45-117(1)(b)(III)(A).    
 
C. The Town has submitted Ballot Issue 3A – Increase Sales Tax for Public Services 
Facilities - to the Town’s registered voters to request voter approval of a one-half 
percent (0.5%) increase in the Town’s sales tax to fund Public Services facilities, 
infrastructure, and equipment. 
 
D. The need for a consolidated Public Service Building was identified in the Town of 
Basalt's 2020 Master Plan, reaffirmed in the Town's 2025 Strategic Workplan, and 
prioritized by the Town Council for advancement in 2026 as a critical investment 
in the Town's long-term operational capacity and public service delivery. 
 
E. Basalt's Public Works and Police Departments currently operate in facilities that 
no longer meet modern operational, safety, or staffing standards, limiting the 
Town's ability to efficiently deliver essential municipal services, recruit and retain 
qualified employees, and accommodate the community's future growth and 
evolving service need. 
 
F. The Basalt Police Department operates from a facility that was not designed for 
law enforcement purposes and lacks essential features, including secure parking 
for patrol vehicles, dedicated detainee processing areas, private interview and 
investigation rooms, adequate evidence storage, and sufficient space to support 
emergency operations, creating operational, safety, and privacy challenges for 
both staff and the public. 
 
G. The Basalt Public Works Department operates from facilities dispersed across five 
separate locations, including temporary structures that have exceeded their useful 
life, resulting in operational inefficiencies, increased travel time and fuel costs, 
reduced emergency response coordination, inadequate employee amenities and 
training space, and accelerated deterioration of equipment due to outdoor storage, 
all of which increase long -term operating costs and hinder the Town's ability to

Facilities  Facilities staff completed a variety of maintenance and improvement 
projects throughout Town, including installing blinds at the new Midland 
Residences units the Town owns, painting trash cans, repairing and adding 
bear-resistant hinges on trash cans due to bear damage, assembling a desk 
for the Planning Office, and replacing a Town Hall bathroom fan. Staff 
replaced a pressure-reducing valve at Basalt River Park bathrooms, flooring 
and painting in the Basalt Center Circle hallways, removal of pool 
sunshades, assisted with pool power washing, and a microwave repair at an 
affordable housing unit. Additional work included completing the Midland 
Residences punch list, assisting with cleanup and landfill runs for affordable 
housing units, and accommodating a CIRSA insurance inspection. Staff also 
accompanied a state boiler inspection at Planning and Town Hall. Facilities 
as well as all of Public Works helped with covering the pool for the winter 
season.  Staff also worked with a consultant to perform inspection on the

Streets Department Streets staff focused on preparing equipment and operations for the 
upcoming winter season, including plows and sanders, while coordinating 
winter plowing preparations across Public Works and advancing the Midland 
sidewalk snow removal RFP. Crews completed numerous maintenance 
tasks, including repairing and installing street signs and center bollards, 
repairing streetlight baseplates and fixtures, replacing bulbs, patching 
potholes, and assisting with tree removals and multiple burials at Fairview 
Cemetery. Staff also responded to a water break near MVMD and addressed 
drainage concerns along Hillcrest, Original Street, and the Frying Pan River, 
with extensive ditch and drainage clearing following recent rain and leaf 
accumulation. Seasonal work included leaf collection and weed trimming 
throughout Town rights-of-way. Residents should keep leaves on their 
property whenever possible rather than blowing them into the public right-of-
way, where they can obstruct drainage and create maintenance issues. 
Streets crew was a big help with the Basalt 125 Celebration with bringing over 
a stage and cabana.  Crews also assisted with vacuuming out storm drains 
throughout Town to remove accumulated debris and maintain proper 
drainage. 
 
 
 
Parks & Gardens  
Parks & Gardens staff continued seasonal maintenance and winter 
preparations, including irrigation system blowouts, winterizing planting 
areas, garden cutbacks, and coordinating the annual leaf and brush drop-off 
at the PWX west yard each Saturday through November 7. Crews completed 
multiple tree permits, removals, and pruning projects, repaired irrigation 
systems throughout. Staff also hired a contractor to address damaged  
fencing at Ponderosa Park and the Community Garden. Staff also assisted 
with the Cliffs Playground installation, removal of shade sails at BRP, and 
started a pollinator area improvements at BRP and planting efforts at the 
Safe Routes to School bridge project. Additional work included coordination 
with BLM and RFOV on the Light Hill bridge and continued wildfire mitigation 
efforts on the project that began last year in Hill District area.

valves, conducting meter reads and assisting with replacements, hydrant 
maintenance, and responding to a customer service line break. Crews 
assisted with storage tank inspections, repaired a hydrant near Midland 
Avenue, and removed various water fountains for the season. Staff also 
completed numerous utility locates and coordinated a repair of a 
compressor at the water treatment plant compressor. Additional work 
included coordinating with multiple developments on future plans, 
advancing enhancement planning for the Southside Tank, and addressing the 
RE-1 well pump with the School District.

SITE ANALYSIS
• Sit e Description: Parcel 2E is approximately 1.4 acres lot in Willits 
that receives ample sunlight. Two asphalt hammerheads exist along 
Lewis Lane to provide future access to the site. 
•
 Sit
e Context: The site is directly south of the TACAW Arts Campus 
and residential townhomes. South of the site is a vegetated buffer 
that separates the site from a single-family residential neighborhood. 
Willits Lane forms the western border and designated open space is 
to the east. 
•
 Gr
ading and Drainage: Parcel 2E sits approximately 15-feet below 
grade of the adjacent streets of Willits Lane and Lewis Lane. The site 
drains into a riparian corridor to the south of the site where it travels 
west through a culvert under Willits Lane. There is a low point near 
the center of the lot. 
•
 L
andscape and Vegetation: The site is vegetated with tall grasses 
and woody shrubs. The west side of the site is lined with trees that 
serve as a vegetated wall between the site and adjacent street. 
Species that appear to be willow line the banks of the vegetated 
buffer. Several smaller trees that seem to be a volunteer species are 
along the slope adjacent to Lewis Lane. One larger tree sits southeast 
of the center of the site. 
•
 Conne
ctivity: Concrete sidewalks along Willits Lane and Lewis Lane 
border the northern and western edges of the site. The corner of 
Willits Lane and Lewis Lane is a designated school bus stop. 
•
 P
arking: Along Lewis Lane there are two sections of street parking 
that consist of 15 spots to the north of the site and 20 spots to the 
east. 
•
 Utilitie
s and Infrastructure: There are three existing manholes on 
the northeast portion of the site that sit adjacent to two utility boxes 
located on concrete pads.
