68. SIDEWALK SNOW REMOVAL - MIDLAND
Public Works Director Justin Forman and Streets Foreman Jeff Blevins presented the staffs
recommended approach for winter snow removal along the newly completed Midland
Avenue streetscape. Director Forman explained that the primary operational challenge is not
snow storage capacity-the completed streetscape provides adequate median and island
areas for snow placement-but rather timing. Snow must be cleared early in the morning,
before parking spaces are occupied and before the Town's street plowing operation begins,
in order to coordinate efficiently.
Director Forman outlined three options: continued use of a contracted snow removal service, 
returning responsibility to adjacent business owners consistent with Town code, or having 
Public Works assume the work directly with existing staff. Staff recommended continuing the 
contracted approach, noting that the previous manual-only contract cost approximately 
$15,833 per month over a six-month season but that, with the warranty period now complete 
and mechanical equipment permitted, preliminary estimates from contractor conversations 
suggest a cost of $6,000 to $9,000 per month going forward. 
Council discussion was supportive of the contracted approach. Council Member Elyse Hottel 
inquired whether hiring a dedicated seasonal staff member might be more cost-effective and 
suggested exploring that option for the 2027 season, while agreeing that contracting was the 
practical choice for the upcoming winter given the compressed timeline. She also expressed 
a preference for per-event pricing over a flat monthly retainer, given climate variability. 
Council Member Rick Stevens and Director.Forman both emphasized the importance of 
having a reliable, dedicated resource given the risk of ice formation if morning clearing is 
delayed. The consensus of the Council was to proceed with procurement of a contracted 
service, structured with a per-event or combined pricing approach, and to evaluate a 
potential seasonal staff position for future budget years.

78. Public Hearing and Second Reading Ordinance No. 15, Series of 2026
Ordinance of the Town Council of the Town of Basalt, Colorado, Granting a PUD
Amendment to Amend the Allowable Total Square Footage at 739 Hillcrest Drive to 5.,474
Total Square Feet, Basalt, Colorado
Planning Director James Lindt summarized this application from Glenn and Laura Yeager to 
amend the PUD at 739 Hillcrest Drive to increase the allowable total floor area from the 
town-wide maximum of 5,000 square feet to 5,474 square feet. The proposed 605-square­
foot first-floor addition would accommodate a bedroom and accessible bathroom for the 
applicant's mother, who has recently become wheelchair bound. James Lindt noted that the 
Town Attorney's office had previously provided a memo analyzing the request as a 
reasonable accommodation under the Fair Housing Act, weighing three factors: whether the 
accommodation undermines legitimate zoning purposes, the benefit to the individual with a 
disability, and whether alternatives exist to accomplish the same benefit more efficiently. No 
substantive changes had been made to the ordinance since first reading, and the Planning 
and Zoning Commission had recommended approval.

Applicant Glenn Yeager was present and responded to Council questions. He explained that 
the bathroom was designed in consultation with Shirley Ryan and Abilitylab therapists to 
accommodate a Sara Steady lift, which requires a straight-line approach rather than the side 
transfer clearance prescribed by commercial ADA standards. He also explained that a stair 
lift was not a viable option given his mother's complete lack of mobility. The Town Attorney 
clarified that the ADA governs places of public accommodation, not private residences, and 
that the Fair Housing Act analysis is the appropriate framework here, focused on the specific 
needs of the individual with the disability. 
The public hearing was opened at 7:57 PM and closed with no public comment received.

they did not view the approval as setting a precedent and found the Fair Housing Act 
framework clearly applicable. Council Member Dupre-Butchart echoed support, noting the 
importance of aging in place for both health and dignity. Council Member Hottel, while 
sympathetic, expressed reservations about adding square footage to an already large home 
and questioned whether the need could be accommodated within existing space, though she 
acknowledged the applicant's constraints. Council Member Berman noted support for the 
project as well.
