7E. Resolution No. 41, Series of 2026: Resolution of the Town Council of Basalt, 
Colorado, Approving an Exemption from Non Residential Lighting Standards for the 
Midland Avenue Catenary Lights (Catherine Christoff)  
 
Town Engineer Catherine Christoff presented a resolution exempting the Midland Avenue 
catenary lights from non-residential lighting standards so that their shades could be 
removed. Following feedback that the installed lights were too dim, staff and the contractor 
tested alternatives on a five-bulb span. These included a different bulb type, smaller shades, 
and no shades, and staff recommended removing all shades, at a cost of just over $9,000 
for the labor, equipment rental and traffic control. The lights operate on a photocell from dusk 
to dawn, on the same controls as the streetlights. A similar exemption was previously 
granted for lighting at Basalt River Park, and the Technical Review Committee raised no 
objection. 
 
Town Engineer Catherine Christoff explained this Ordinance exempted only the removal of 
the shields from the catenary lights from the lighting standards, it did not exempt the 
business or streetlights. 
 
Councilor Shaffran said he supported dark skies, that the Town should hold itself to the 
standard it asks of residents, and that the lights did not need to burn from dusk to dawn.  
Council Shaffran and Councilor Anderson Ward asked about the cost of a timer, separate 
from the one that controls the streetlights, and Anderson Ward wondered if the shades could 
be returned.  The catenary lights that cross Market Street in Willits were approved through 
an exemption approved by the Willits Town Center PUD.   
 
Mayor Knight asked how the Town arrived at an outcome so far from expectations, a 
rhetorical question, calling it a lessons-learned question for future design projects. Ms. 
Christoff said the design responded to complaints about brightness of the street lights on 
Midland Spur and that the installed lights met Code.  Rick Stevens said he felt the catenary 
lines clutter the open view up Midland Avenue and hoped for alternatives. Mayor Knight and 
Councilor Anderson Ward did not want Council to select bulbs and hats, and the Mayor 
noted that the lights without shields looked good.  Council member Anderson Ward was in 
favor of removing all the hats and getting an estimate for a time.  Town Attorney Conklin said
