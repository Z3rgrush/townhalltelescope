3A Public Hearing — Code Amendments to Chapter 15, Chapter 16, and Chapter 17 
3B Recommended Action: Staff and Consultant Comments, Public Comments, P&Z Discussion, 
Motion to Continue to October 20, 2026  
 
Planning Director, James Lindt, introduced the public hearing and explained that the proposed 
amendments were part of the Town's broader zoning and development code rewrite. He stated that 
the code had become difficult to use after many years of piecemeal amendments and that Design 
Workshop had been selected through an RFP process to assist with the rewrite. He noted that staff 
and the land use code working group had been involved throughout the process, including several 
current commissioners. Lindt recommended that the Commission treat this meeting as an 
introductory review, hear the consultant presentation, ask questions, take public comment, and 
continue the hearing to the October 20, 2026, meeting. He also advised that additional amendments 
tied to updates in the public works manual would be added before the next meeting. 
Jessica Garrow of Design Workshop presented the draft code amendments, with Reilly Thimons 
participating remotely by Zoom. Garrow said the project was at the 95 percent draft stage and 
nearing final milestones and entering adoption hearings with Planning and Zoning and then Town 
Council. She summarized prior work sessions with the Commission, Council, and the working group, 
as well as community outreach that included spring pop-up events, an Earth Day table at Basalt River 
Park, a high school classroom discussion with Chair Eric Vozick's students, an open house, and a 
public survey open from the end of June and most of July. Garrow reviewed the reorganization of 
the code structure. She explained that annexation had been separated into its own chapter so 
applicants could find all annexation provisions in one place. She said zoning had been moved into 
Chapter 15 and refocused on zone district regulations, while development standards were placed in 
Chapter 16 as a separate chapter. Subdivision provisions were also reorganized. 
Chapter 14— Annexations 
On annexation, Garrow stated that the changes were largely organizational and intended to align the 
code with state law. She noted the addition of de-annexation language and updates to review 
criteria. She also highlighted a change from "general conformance" to "substantial conformance" 
with the master plan. Commissioner Ben Fierstein asked whether additional clarity was needed 
concerning annexation agreements and how they relate to county platting and future amendments.   
Lindt responded that the Town retained flexibility in annexation and PUD matters and could require 
Town zoning and standards rather than relying on Eagle County standards. He added that the draft 
standards had been strengthened to provide more clarity. Garrow added that any changes to an 
annexation agreement would require Town approval. 
Chapter 15 — Zoning  
Garrow then reviewed Chapter 15, which covers zoning districts. She said the draft consolidated 
definitions, added specialized use regulations, and introduced more conditional uses to create a 
middle level of review by Planning and Zoning, while retaining special review uses that proceed to 
Town Council. She also explained that the former Developing Resources district would become a 
legacy district rather than serving as a base zone for annexations. A significant update discussed in 
Chapter 15 concerned data centers. Garrow said that after prior discussions and review with the 
Town Attorney, the 95 percent draft prohibited data centers in all zoning districts. Commissioner Bill 
Maron asked whether accessory data center functions such as server rooms within office buildings 
would still be allowed. Lindt and Garrow clarified that incidental server space as part of a primary 
office or business use would remain allowed, but a standalone data center could not be the primary 
use. Garrow also reported that the Midland Avenue design guidelines had been incorporated into 
the document. She noted that bed and breakfast uses had been revised from conditional use to 
special review use in all residential districts based on working group discussion. She further 
Page 4 of 27 
explained that storage uses in the industrial district were identified as special review uses, and she 
shared that students participating in outreach had expressed concern about additional storage uses 
and data centers in town. 
Garrow noted a specific change to setback requirements in the R-3TN district. The amendment 
would allow setbacks that became nonconforming before August 22, 1995, to remain while 
prohibiting any expansion of a building footprint further into a nonconforming setback. The change 
is intended to clarify a longstanding interpretation of the code that had allowed some property 
owners to expand building footprints within existing setback encroachments. Lindt noted that 
property owners would still be allowed to repair, rebuild, or redevelop within an existing 
nonconforming footprint, but could not enlarge it. The amendment is intended to help preserve the 
established character of the Hill District and address redevelopment concerns in the R-3TN zone. 
Commissioner Fierstein asked whether a property owner could disturb and rebuild within a 
nonconforming setback area, and staff confirmed that they could. He also asked whether this 
provision was unique to the R-3TN district, and Lindt explained that it was, due to the district's 
unique lot patterns, varied setbacks, and neighborhood character. 
Chapter 16 — Development 
Garrow next reviewed Chapter 16, which addresses development standards and procedures. She 
described it as the largest chapter and said it consolidates review procedures from administrative 
reviews to discretionary approvals by Planning and Zoning and Town Council. She said the chapter 
clarifies environmental and hazard standards, updates PUD review procedures, includes staff-level 
review opportunities for minor changes, and incorporates the recently adopted Proposition 123 
affordable housing expedited review provisions. She also said the chapter creates a dedicated 
section for accessory dwelling units and a formal review process for them. 
Commissioner Maron made a comment regarding the expedited review provision for affordable 
housing in Section 16-26. He said the Town might consider whether other strong master plan 
priorities, such as daycare, transit-related projects, or other community-serving uses, should also 
receive streamlined review treatment. Garrow responded that the revised use tables and conditional 
use framework were already intended to create more flexibility, but she acknowledged that the 
concept could be discussed further. Lindt said daycare was a useful example and suggested Planning 
and Zoning could discuss whether to recommend such ideas to Council. Commissioner Fierstein 
mentioned transportation as another possible category.  Lindt explained that larger public facilities 
and transit projects often fall in the P-Public zone district or within PUD amendment processes, 
which already have their own procedures. Garrow noted that some transit stops may already be 
permitted, while larger transit-related redevelopment could raise a different policy question. The 
discussion then narrowed to daycare uses. Garrow explained that small daycare operations serving 
seven or fewer children were drafted as permitted uses in some residential districts and conditional 
uses in others, while large daycare operations were designated as special review uses because of 
potential traffic and neighborhood impacts. Lindt stated that the policy question for the Commission 
was whether larger daycare uses should remain special review uses or be reduced to conditional 
uses reviewed only by Planning and Zoning. Commissioner Maron said the broader issue should be 
discussed with Town Council because the master plan identifies those services as important 
community priorities. 
Chapter 17 — Subdivisions  
Garrow then summarized Chapter 17 on subdivision. She said the changes were primarily 
organizational, including consolidation of definitions and updating references from general 
conformance to substantial conformance with the master plan. Lindt added that some engineering 
standards currently in the subdivision code would be removed and placed into the public works 
manual, and those changes would return with the next round of review. 
Page 5 of 27 
This led to discussion about road widths and engineering standards. Commissioner Maron 
commented that some dimensional standards can be too wide and may reflect the size of fire trucks 
rather than community design goals such as traffic calming and pedestrian safety. Garrow agreed 
that coordination with the fire district would be part of that discussion. Lindt said most streets in 
town are already established, but for new subdivisions there is a process to consider reduced lane 
widths in coordination with the fire district. He cited Stotts Mill as an example where narrower lane 
widths were approved with accommodation for turning movements and parking geometry. 
Commissioner Mike Horvath asked how future changes to the public works manual would be 
adopted, and Lindt answered that amendments would still require Planning and Zoning and Town 
Council approval, similar to code amendments. Commissioner Fierstein asked whether the manual 
would also define surety, warranty, and acceptance items, and Mr. Lindt said it would address those 
topics more clearly. Commissioner Maron asked whether the manual referenced the most current 
AASHTO guidance, particularly for bicycle and pedestrian design. Staff said they would confirm that 
for the next meeting. 
Garrow closed the presentation by noting that updated tables and color graphics had been 
incorporated into the draft code document to improve readability and usability. She explained that 
several graphic formats were evaluated before selecting a color version to help distinguish 
information throughout the code. Commissioner Maron commented that the layout worked well 
and was easy to read. The Commission also briefly discussed accessibility considerations, noting that 
the graphics should not rely on color alone to convey information, and expressed support for the 
updated format. 
Garrow then outlined the next steps in the code update process. She explained that the Commission 
would hold an additional meeting following the opening of the public hearing to review information 
related to childcare uses and the Public Works Manual. After the Planning and Zoning Commission 
makes a recommendation on the proposed code amendments, the draft code will be forwarded to 
Town Council for consideration. Garrow also noted that Town Council will receive a work session 
update on the code revisions before formal adoption discussions begin. Commissioner Fierstein 
asked when topics such as rent fees, pools, spas, and other building code issues would be addressed, 
and Lindt responded that those items would be considered during a future phase of the code update 
process. Commissioner Maron also remarked that elder care may become an important community 
consideration in the future given Basalt's aging population.

RE: Public Hearing – 72 Sunset Drive Minor PUD Amendment and 
Special Review for an Events Space 
 
 
I. Purpose 
 
Greg Topper and Mike Ziemer of Aspen Elevated Catering  (“Applicant”) are 
requesting approval for a Minor PUD Amendment to the Mid Valley Design 
Center PUD. The Applicant seeks to add Event Space as a Special Review Use 
to the PUD and obtain Special Review approval for an Event Space at 72 Sunset 
Drive.  
 
II. Background 
 
The Mid -Valley Design Center PUD includes specific permitted and special 
review uses that vary from the underling IN (Industrial) Zone District.  A 
restaurant is a permitted use in the Mid -Valley Design Center PUD and the 
Applicant has applied for a building permit to convert their catering kitchen into  a 
restaurant at 72 Sunset Drive.  In association with the restaurant, the Applicant 
would like ability to operate the 1,000 sf dining spa ce as an Events Space when 
the restaurant is not operating.  In order to use the space as an Events Space for 
parties and other significant events, a PUD Amendment is required to add Events 
Space as a Special Review Use to the PUD.  Additionally, a Special Review is 
required to review the operational characteristics of the Events Space. 
 
III. Review Process 
 
The proposed PUD Amendment  and Special Review require a P&Z 
recommendation to the Town Council with public hearings at each level of 
review.  Staff has provided draft conditions for P&Z consideration.

 2 
IV. Discussion Items 
 
Compatibility of Use – The restaurant use is a permitted use in the Mid -Valley 
Design Center PUD  and the Applicant has a pending building permit to convert 
the space for restaurant use in addition to the current catering kitchen use that is 
currently operating in the space .  However, the Event s Space use that is 
proposed in association with the restaurant at 72 Sunset Drive has similar but 
potentially more significant impacts on surrounding area than does the restaurant 
use.  Staff analyzes the impacts of the Events Space herein. 
 
Off-Street Parking –Pursuant to Town Code Section 16 -93, P&Z may make a 
determination for the appropriate number of parking spaces where specific uses 
are not listed in the Code.  The Code does not include Event s Space as a 
specified use in Town Code Section 16 -91, Off-street parking spaces  required.  
The PUD specifies off -street parking requirements for retail and service 
commercial uses; however, as Events Space is not currently a permitted use, a 
parking requirement is not included in the PUD for Events Space.   
 
The parking requirement for service commercial in the PUD matches the parking 
requirement for restaurant uses in the Town Code at 1 space per 300 square 
feet.   Fourteen (14) usable parking spaces exist and are to be organized on the 
site.  Staff feels that an Events Space is not comparable to a service commercial 
or restaurant use as events create demand for parking all at once.  As such, Staff 
believes that it would be appropriate to limit the occupancy for events to a certain 
number of people that could be effectively accommodated by the number of 
parking spaces.  Staff has included a draft condition limiting the number of event 
attendees to twenty -two, anticipating that three (3) of the parking spaces will be 
used for employees and the other eleven (11) parking spaces would be available 
to event attendees.  For the eleven (11) parking spaces, the draft occupancy limit 
would anticipate two event attendees per vehicle. 
 
For some other uses in the Industrial Zone District, the Town Staff has built into 
approvals certain allowances for business operators in the past to keep a log with 
their occupancy and parking usage to demonstrate that clientele generally attend 
their business in a greater  ratio than two (2) people per vehicle.  In this case, 
Staff has included the maximum occupancy of twenty -two (22) attendees per 
event in the draft conditions with an allowance for the Applicant to keep a log of 
the numbe r of event attendees and parking spaces used for such events for a 
period of six (6) months, with the ability  for the TRC to approve a greater 
occupancy in the future if the Applicant’s logs demonstrate it to be feasible 
without impacting the adjacent properties. 
 
Noise-  The subject site is not in close proximity to residential uses.  As such, 
Staff does not anticipate the noise from the Events Space use will be an issue.  A 
draft condition is included limiting the Event s Space use to the hours of 11 A M to 
10 PM.

From:  James Lindt, Planning Director

RE: Continued Public Hearing- Code Amendments to Town Code 
Chapters 15, 16, 17 
 
 
Background: The Town of Basalt has not completed a full update to the Town’s 
development regulations for many years due to the Town Staff’s significant workload 
and has instead adopted piecemeal code amendments over time as needed.  Town 
Staff and the Town’s review boards determined that a co mprehensive update is 
necessary to the Town’s Zoning Code, Subdivision Regulations, and Development 
Review/Annexation Processes and Criteria.  
 
Town Staff published a request for proposals in the spring of 2025 and Design 
Workshop was selected from the firms that made proposals.  A contract for 
Design Workshop’s services was executed in June of 2025.   
 
Details:  Design Workshop  has drafted proposed code amendments to 
reorganize the Town’s Annexation, Zoning, and Subdivision regulations and 
added graphics to make the Town’s development regulations more user -friendly.  
Additionally, there have been some miscellaneous substantive changes that 
include the following topics: 
 
1. Special Review Uses vs. Conditional Uses 
2. Regulation of Data Centers 
3. Setback Treatment in R-3 TN 
4. Bicycle Parking 
 
Throughout the code amendment formulation process, there has been a code 
amendment working group  made up of citizens and code users from the Town’s 
various citizen boards, including three (3) members from the P&Z, that have

Chapter 14 – Annexations  
 
50% Draft: Revisions to the Annexations chapter are primarily structural and procedural, with limited changes to underlying policy. The 
chapter is reorganized to include Colorado annexation and disconnection statutes, including clearer references to statutory requirements 
and the addition of applicability language to clarify when annexation procedures apply. The annexation process will be further clarified 
through the introduction of defined timelines and process graphics. A new set of annexation review criteria is added to forma lize evaluation 
standards and provide clearer guidance for decision-making. 
90% Draft: New annexation graphic and updated formatting throughout chapter. 
95% Draft: Updated language for compliance with Master Plan changing “general conformance” to “substantial conformance”.  
 
Chapter 15 – Zoning 
50% Draft: Updates to the Zoning chapter focus on reorganization and clarity. Definitions from chapters 15,16, and 17 are consolidated 
and categorized to reduce duplication and ambiguity, with supporting graphics added where appropriate. Several specialized us e 
regulations, including medical marijuana and natural medicine uses, are relocated to other chapters to improve organization. The chapter 
structure is revised to more clearly distinguish residential, commercial, industrial, and other zoning districts. A key polic y change is the 
introduction of conditional uses across residential, commercial, and industrial districts, allowing greater land use flexibility while maintaining 
discretionary review. Land uses are also formally categorized in a new section, and the Developing Resources District is tran sitioned to a 
legacy district. 
90% Draft: Introduction of Data Center definitions and inclusion of Data Centers in Use Schedules. Updated C -2 and CC Chapters with 
references to adopted Midland Avenue District Design Guidelines, and updated formatting throughout chapter. 
95% Draft: Updated tables to reflect prohibition of Data Centers across all use categories, updated Bed and Breakfast from Conditional 
Use to Special Review Use in all Residential Zone Districts, updated storage uses in Industrial Zone to be Special Review Use , updated 
Master Plan references from “general conformance” to “substantial conformance”, updated R3TN setback requirements.  
 
Chapter 16 – Development 
50% Draft: Chapter 16 is established as a standalone Development chapter, consolidating development standards, discretionary review 
procedures, and administrative processes that were previously distributed across multiple chapters. Environmental and hazard -related 
regulations, including hillside, floodplain, and environmentally sensitive area standards, are consolidated into unified articl es for clarity and 
consistency. Consolidated and expedited review opportunities are introduced by allowing staff-level approval of insubstantial PUD 
amendments, affordable housing development, and administrative approval of certain parking reductions. New policy additions include a 
dedicated Accessory Dwelling Unit article, a formal Conditional Use and Special Review procedures article, a Standard Develop ment 
Procedures section to standardize review pathways, and provisions addressing Proposition 123 requirements.  
90% Draft: Updates to Prop 123 language and updated formatting throughout chapter. 
 
Chapter 17 – Subdivisions 
50% Draft: Revisions to the Subdivisions chapter are largely organizational and administrative. Definitions are relocated to the zoning 
chapter to establish a single source for defined terms, and Technical Review Committee provisions are moved out of the chapte r. The 
chapter structure is simplified by combining general and administrative provisions. Substantively, subdivision -related fees are relocated, 
and parkland and school district dedication requirements are removed from this chapter. 
90% Draft: Updated formatting throughout chapter. 
95% Draft: General formatting updates, updated Master Plan references from “general conformance” to “substantial conformance” .
