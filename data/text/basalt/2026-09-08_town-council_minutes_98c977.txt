6. PRESENTATIONS
GA. Glenwood South Bridge Project Presentation
Glenwood Springs City Manager Steve Boyd and City Engineer Ryan Gordon presented an 
update on Glenwood's South Bridge project. City Engineer Gordon provided background on 
the project, which has been in development since approximately 2003. A significant redesign 
effort beginning in 2023 eliminated a tunnel under the existing runway, replaced a complex 
concrete segmented bridge with a more standard steel girder bridge, and removed two large 
retaining walls along Highway 82. These changes saved approximately thirty million dollars 
and eighteen months of construction time. 
The project received a $50 million federal Rural Surface Transportation grant, which as of 
today, had been fully obligated, meaning all requirements for accessing the funds have been 
satisfied. Additional funding contributions include $4.0 million from RFTA's (Roaring Fork 
Transportation Authority) 2040 Destination Planning allocation and $4.5 million from COOT, 
with a remaining funding gap of approximately $4 to $5 million dollars being addressed 
through value engineering efforts. 
City Engineer Gordon described the primary purpose of the bridge as providing fire 
evacuation egress for approximately four thousand residents in South Glenwood and the 
Fourmile area who currently have limited emergency exit routes. Additional benefits include 
a ten-foot-wide shared-use path connecting to the Rio Grande Trail, a reconfiguration and 
full rebuild of nearly a mile of Highway 82, and elimination of the hazardous County Road 
154 intersection, which has seen at least two fatalities and upwards of sixty major accidents 
over the past decade. Traffic modeling suggests the bridge will remove approximately three 
thousand vehicle trips per day-roughly ten percent of current volume-from Highway 82 
through Glenwood Springs. 
The project is anticipated to go to bid at the end of 2026 or early 2027, with two construction 
seasons planned and completion projected around 2029. Construction is expected to 
maintain two travel lanes in each direction through the work zone, with reduced speed limits 
through the construction area. 
A member of the public, Casey Johnson of 21 Lazy Glen, asked about construction impacts 
on afternoon commuters. City Engineer Gordon acknowledged there would be some impact 
but expressed confidence that the wide right-of-way would allow traffic to be shifted without 
reducing lane counts, with speeds posted at 40 to 45 miles per hour through the zone. 
Following the presentation, Mayor David Knight indicated Council's support for drafting a 
letter of support addressed to the Glenwood Springs City Council, as several other Valley 
municipalities have done. Town Manager Kaasch-Buerger confirmed a draft was ready.

Planning Director James Lindt introduced the item, describing it as an introduction meeting 
and public hearing for a proposed PUD amendment, special review, and associated 
development review actions to construct a 93-key hotel with three deed-restricted residential 
units on the former Movieland site at 218 East Valley Road. The site is designated Service 
Commercial in the 2020 Master Plan Future Land Use Map and is located within the Old 
Orchard Plaza PUD, which currently restricts the subject parcel exclusively to movie theater 
use. A PUD amendment is therefore required to allow a hotel use. 
Staff noted that the Basalt Affordable Community Housing Commission (BACH) reviewed 
and accepted the affordable housing aspects of the proposal. The Planning and Zoning 
Commission conducted several public hearings and voted 5-2 to recommend denial of the 
application. Staff recommended treating the evening's session as an introduction meeting, 
allowing the applicants to present the full project, receiving public comment, and continuing 
the public hearing to October 13, 2026, with a more complete staff presentation to follow at 
that time. 
Applicant Presentation: Property owner Ben Moss provided background on the Movieland 
property, which his family developed beginning in the 1980s and which opened in 1991. He 
explained that the theater operated at a loss for its final four years following the pandemic, 
and that the existing PUD restriction to theater use necessitated the requested amendment. 
He expressed his commitment to remaining the majority owner and long-term steward of the 
property. 
Grant Dupart of Walsh Dupart, the applicant's development partner, then delivered a 
detailed project presentation. He and his partner John Walsh founded Walsh Du part in 2021, 
focusing on high-quality extended-stay hotel development in the Rocky Mountain region. The 
firm recently opened a hotel in Steamboat Springs and is completing one in Fraser, 
Colorado. The proposed hotel brand is Residence Inn by Marriott, which the applicants 
described as a limited-service extended-stay product with suite-style rooms, a 
complimentary breakfast, no bar or restaurant, and a range of demand sources including 
leisure travelers, government workers, traveling medical professionals, and wildland 
firefighting crews. 
Grant Dupart presented data indicating that Google search interest in "Basalt Hotel" had 
increased forty percent over the preceding two years, and that non-luxury hotel occupancy 
across the Roaring Fork Valley runs eleven percentage points higher than luxury hotel 
occupancy. He also noted approximately 142 active short-term rental listings in the Basalt­
to-El Jebel area, arguing that hotel supply helps reduce pressure on residential housing 
stock used for short-term rentals. 
The proposed project includes 93 hotel keys and three deed-restricted housing units on the 
ground floor. Through the planning process, the applicant reduced the building height from 
46 feet (inclusive of architectural features) to 34 feet 5 inches. The building materials include 
stone veneer, wood-tone siding, and board-and-batten, described as consistent with 
materials used in the firm's other projects. Signage has been reduced to a single sign with 
reduced dimensions and lumens, following a variance approved by Marriott. 
The applicant noted a potential opportunity to convert the second floor of the adjacent Old 
Orchard Plaza condominium building for up to nine housing units, moving the three deed­
restricted units from the hotel building into that space and converting those hotel-building 
units back to hotel keys. The applicant indicated this would be feasible only in conjunction 
with the hotel project given construction crew efficiencies, and described it as a discussion 
item rather than a finalized proposal.
