2. Building Benchmarking Program.   
Nadolny introduced Gina McCrack in from CORE to provide an overview of the Town’s Building 
Benchmarking Program to date.  She noted that the program has entered its second year, and that CORE 
would be able to provide data from the first year of the program.  The intent is to offer this as a voluntary 
program for the first two years, and then to move to a mandatory phased approach after that period.      
McCrackin indicated that Julia Wilson and Cedric Gamble from CORE  are the staff members that 
administer the building benchmarking program for the Town.  She will connect with Julia about attending 
a future Green Team meeting.  
McCrackin reported th at ten buildings are currently participating in Basalt ’s ben chmarking program.  
Benchmarking is valuable in supporting capital improvement planning and establishing relationships with 
building owners.  The program identifies key variables for under or overperforming buildings and can aid 
in the creation of a successful pipeline for future projects.   
2 
 
The Green Team was interested in knowing if any of the 2025 benchmarking effort s have translated to 
projects, and if CORE is engaging in conversation s with those building owners to generate projects .  At 
this time there ha ve been no projects that have come  out of the building benchmarking project .  
McCrackin will check in with the program administrators to determine if conversations are being had with 
program participants. 
McCrackin recommended requiring benchmarking as a phased approach to get to a building performance 
standard plan. She noted that this process will take several years. Aspen’s program has been in place for 
several years and they are just now get ting to the meat of the discussion around building performance 
standards.  This is the first year Aspen has not on-boarded any new buildings. 
McCrackin reminded the Green Team that project dollars from the IMPACT Accelerator Grant have been 
earmarked for providing additional funds for energy efficiency projects that have been identified through 
participating in the building benchmarking program.   
Through additional questions b y the Green Team McCrackin revealed that the City of Aspen has a very 
high compliance rate with the program, and that building owners are very engaged.  A significant portion 
of Aspen’s funding and work with CORE is their Building IQ program.   
McCrackin said that a future meeting can feature more information regarding the program, including how 
the data may be interpreted and what projects within a building should be prioritized.   The program 
drives the importance of building right from the start and new construction and energy code.  Retrofits 
are more expensive than building right from the start. 
Eckart is interested in having Staff provide the Green Team with semiannual data to see what buildings 
have been benchmarked to better understand the trends and process.   
Staff will share with the Green Team the building benchmarking report prepared by CORE prior to the next 
meeting.  McCrackin will in vite Cedric and Julia to join the next Gre en Team meeting to provide more 
information regarding Basalt’s building benchmarking program. 
3. Update and Information Items.    
• Green Business Recognition Program – the pilot program is intended to be launched in July. 
 
• Basalt Connect – Staff has worked with Via, the parent company of the Basalt Connect service, 
and managed to successfully expand the Basalt Connect territory so that covers a portion of 
Emma (unincorporated Pitki n C ounty) and a stop at the Eagle County building at Crown 
Mountain Park, as well as the Summit Vista neighborhood.  The expansion was achieved with 
no additional cost or need for additional service vans.  These routes must either start or end 
at a transit stop, or at City Market.  The expanded area services workforce housing units. 
 
• Bike-to-Work Day – this annual event is scheduled for June 24th.  There will be pop up stations 
at Triangle Park in Willits Town Center and at the Basalt River Park downtown that will serve 
coffee and morning snacks.

• Annual Community Recycling Event – the event is scheduled for Saturday, July 27th, from 9 am 
– 1 pm at the Basalt High School parking lot.  Nadolny asked for volunteers to help out at the 
event.  
 
• IMPACT Accelerator Grant – McCrackin indicated that the regional application was successful 
with an award of over $4 million.  The communities are waiting on the state’s press release 
before putting out a regional press release, which will include all award winners and the total 
amount.  Project funding will provide a big injection of funds to the region  and the increase 
in funds to give back to community in rebates will be very significant.  The policy work will be 
done together to develop the next code update that the region will adopt  towards a  
consistent regional net zero roadmap effort.  This will result in new developments requiring 
all-electric buildings (with some exceptions that will be determined as a cohort).  The regional 
group is still meeting weekly and working through contract negotiations with the state.  Aspen 
will be rolling out an intergovernmental agreement with all partner communities in the near 
future.  Policy work will begin which will remove the financial strain on a ny one community 
going forward with these co de amendments solo .  She noted that this will  still require 
significant time dedication, but the structure will lighten the burden on any one community.
