2. Budget Overview.   
Doug Pattison, the Town’s Finance Director, introduced himself, and explained that he was meeting with 
each of the Town’s citizen committees to provide an overview regarding each committee’s budget, and 
an understanding of how the committee’s work is funded. 
He explained that the Green Team’s work is funded through REMP (Renewable Energy Mitigation 
Program), Holy Cross Energy’s Community Enhancement Fund, and RETAs (Real Estate Transfer 
Assessments).  These are all restricted funds, meaning that the funds gain ed from these sources are 
limited in how they can be used.   
Pattison explained that a RETA is a fee that is provided to the Town upon sale of a property.  Some RETAs 
have been set up through the development review/approval process to be used toward affordable 
housing or public arts, and some have been restricted to sustainability initiatives within Basalt.  The RETAs 
that help to fund Green Team activities include the Basalt River Park, Park Modern, Lake Modern, and the 
Roaring Fork Club developments. RETA funds should be used for capital expenses rather than operational 
expenses.    
REMP fees are paid to the Town when the property owner installs exterior energy use additions, such as 
firepits, pools and spas.  REMP fees also apply to heated garages and snowmelt. 
2 
 
And lastly, the Holy Cross Energy Community Enhancement Fund is a voluntary financial resource program 
established through the Town’s franchise agreement with Holy Cross Energy, and it provides the Town 
with yearly funding that can be used to help fund sustainability projects.   
WE-Cycle and Basalt Connect are under the Green Team’s budget, with the latter being the largest Town 
expense.  These programs are both subsidized by RFTA, but the funding is decreasing each year, according 
to the agreement with RFTA.  Pattison noted that the Lake Modern RETA has been used to help fund the 
Basalt Connect service.  Town Staff is exploring funding options for the Basalt Connect service, as that 
program needs a sustainable funding source. 
Pattison explained that the bag fee (which the Town receives six cents from each of the ten cents to 
purchase a carryout bag from a store or restaurant) helps to offset expenses associated with the general 
fund and is not required to fund green initiatives specifically. 
Pattison fielded some additional questions from the Green Team and let them know that he is available 
to answer future questions about funding or financing.   
3. Safety Standards Across our Regional Trails/Pathways/Sidewalks 
Nadolny reminded the Green Team that she has been attending meetings on the topic of safety, with an 
emphasis on E -bike safety.  The meetings are facilitated by RFTA  and include attendees from all of the 
area municipalities, Pitkin County and even the Town of Eagle.  The goal of the group is to create a list of 
unified policies that are aimed at enhancing public safety.  The unified policies will make it easier for 
cyclists to maintain the same trail expectations throughout the Valley and will implement the same 
signage and graphics.   
Nadolny shared a spreadsheet that listed requirements from other Towns and noted that Basalt does not 
have requirements at this time.  She asked the Green Team to look at these and provide any thoughts on 
what might not be currently considered.  She said s he would share the Green Team’s thoughts with the 
regional group. 
The Green Team members provided the following items for consideration: 
• Speed limit – City of Denver has a 15 -mph speed limit on their trails.  Nadolny noted that Aspen 
has a 20-mph speed limit.   
• Define the use of the trail by its width.  For instance, narrow walkways should not accommodate 
bikes and pedestrians. 
• Concern was voiced about off-leash dog areas abutting trails that are used by cyclists. 
• Add dedicated bike lanes where possible. 
• Post signage about required announcing before passing. 
• Give away bike bells to reinforce signaling. 
• Keep the messaging about common courtesy – keeps things positive rather than punitive.

RE: Gas-Powered Handheld Lawncare Equipment  
 
Purpose.  The Basalt Green Team is being asked to brainstorm options that will support 
eliminating gas -powered handheld lawncare equipment throughout Basalt.  This has been 
flagged as a Town Council priority for 2027. 
Background.  In 2025 the Basalt Town Council adopted Resolution No. 03 which banned the use 
of combustions leaf blowers on Town -owned property.  The approval is limited to leaf blowers, 
and does not include other handheld lawncare devices such as trimmers, edgers, etc.   
Leaf blowers are excessively noisy and are known to contribute significantly to air pollution.  Due 
to advances in electric lawn care equipment technology, electric options are now available that 
do not produce direct exhaust emissions and are significantly quieter than their combustion-style 
counterparts.   
Community Models.  Basalt is not al one in exploring this change to small lawncare equipment.  
The City of Aspen banned the use of gas -powered leaf blowers in April of 2003 in response to a 
citizen petition that targeted excessive noise and two-stroke engine emissions.   
Recently Snowmass Village implemented a ban on gas-powered leaf blowers between June 15th 
and September 15 th.  There are some allowances for commercial operat ors du ring shoulder 
seasons.  Full compliance for residents, commercial landscapers, HOAs, etc. is required by 2029. 
There are several cities across the country that have implemented bans on the use of gasoline-
powered leaf blowers.  A quick search turns up the following:

RECITALS 
WHEREAS, the Town of Basalt is committed to addressing the urgent challenges 
of climate change by adopting strategies that reduce carbon emissions, promote 
sustainability, and enhance the resilience of our community; and 
WHEREAS, the Town of Basalt has declared a climate emergency, has committed 
to reducing community-wide greenhouse gas emissions 50% by 2030 and 80% by 2050, 
and recognizes that achieving these climate action goals requires beneficial changes in 
the daily operations and activities; and 
WHEREAS, the Basalt Green Team recognizes the significant negative impacts 
that combustion leaf blowers have on environmental pollution and public health, safety 
and welfare; and 
WHEREAS, a combustion leaf blower is defined as any leaf blower powered in 
part by gasoline or oil; and 
WHEREAS, the Basalt Green Team has proposed a policy to ban the use of 
combustion leaf blowers on Town-owned properties to begin the transition away from 
these devices to cleaner technologies, and to reduce the harmful air pollution and 
excessive noise associated with their use; and 
WHEREAS, the Town Council wishes to adopt a policy prohibiting the use of 
combustion leaf blowers on Town property and believes it is in the best interest of the 
Town to do so. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Basalt Town Council of Basalt, Colorado, 
as follows: 
1. The Basalt Town Council incorporates the above recitals as set forth herein.
2. The Basalt Town Council hereby adopts a policy to prohibit the use of gas-powered
leaf blowers on Town-owned property.
