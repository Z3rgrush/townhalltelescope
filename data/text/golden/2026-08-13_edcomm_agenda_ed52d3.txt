Dear City of Golden Small Business Grant Reviewers, 
 
Thank you so much for the opportunity to submit this request for new freezers at Golden 
Pantry and Thrift, which we need urgently for the storage of pork and fish at the pantry.  
 
Golden Pantry and Thrift’s mission is to provide food, financial assistance, and referrals to other 
community services to Golden area residents in crisis and/or experiencing hardship. The 
organization has been serving Golden residents in need for more than 60 years. This year, we 
intend to distribute 275,000 pounds of food to 1,830 community members coping with food 
insecurity. These efforts are threatened by the recent failure of two of our freezers used to 
store the food we distribute to clients, reducing our ability to accept donated and rescued food 
items for redistribution, and ultimately reducing our ability to alleviate food insecurity for the 
clients who rely on us. 
 
Golden Pantry and Thrift’s services reach the ZIP codes of 80401 and 80403, representing 
a particularly vulnerable cross-section of our community, including long-time Golden 
residents/retirees, unhoused community members, unemployed people, refugee families, and 
many who are in temporary need of extra help. Most of our clients live in the West Lakewood / 
South Golden Road corridor, a designated Colorado Enterprise Zone, where community 
members face particularly high unemployment rates and low incomes. At least 30% are 
handicapped, 25% are older adults age 65+, and 25% are under the age of 18—all populations 
particularly susceptible to food insecurity. Eight percent of our clients are veterans, and 19% of 
clients report being unhoused or living in a shelter, making access to food even more critical. 
Forty-five percent of those served belong to historically marginalized racial or ethnic groups. 
One hundred percent of clients are living below Area Median Income, 100% are living at or 
below the Federal Poverty Level, and 67% are receiving government assistance with basic 
needs, yet still need additional food support.  
 
Of clients who responded to our 2025 survey:  
- 95.2% are very satisfied with the food Golden Pantry provides

and make them feel welcome

get from the pantry (combined with the rest of their food supply)   
- In the past six months, 53% experienced an increased need for the pantry, and

Per your request, we have prepared a menu of three options for further enhancing the City of 
Golden’s monitoring and evaluation of the performance of the local economy.  
 
Option 1: Develop an “Annual Economic Scorecard” for City of Golden 
The first option is the development of an economic scorecard presenting data for a mix of key 
economic indicators for the City that reflect trends in the health and performance of the local 
economy. The scorecard can help City leaders evaluate performance, track baseline trends, 
and make informed strategic choices. It will synthesize complex regional data into a digestible 
dashboard for use by policymakers and local businesses.  
 
Option 2: Complete Update of 2023 Economic Contribution Study (using IMPLAN) 
The second option is a complete replication of the 2023 Economic Contribution Study 
providing a detailed analysis of the composition and structure of the Golden economy.  An 
analysis conducted in 2027 would be reflective of the economy as of 2024-25.  The Golden 
employer database by detailed (6-digit NAICS) industry would be updated.  Total jobs (private 
and public) and labor income in the City of Golden will be estimated by detailed (generally 4-
digit NAICS) industry sector, along with the City’s gross domestic product (GDP), and total 
value of goods and services produced (output) by industry.  Golden’s total economic 
contribution to the Jefferson County economy will also be updated by detailed industry sector.  
 
Option 3: Update of 2023 Economic Contribution Study using summary data (2-digit NAICS 
industries) and RIMS II Multipliers and BEA Data Tables Most of the components of the 2023 Economic Contribution Study would be updated to year 
2024 (for an analysis conducted in 2027) utilizing U.S. Census Bureau estimates of 
employment by 2-digit sector and the use of RIMS II multipliers from the U.S. Bureau of 
Economic Analysis (BEA).  This option would significantly reduce the estimated cost of the 
study effort, although the study results and estimated economic contributions will not be 
directly comparable to the 2023 study result given the changes in methodology and 
employment data source.  Additionally, a few of the outputs and estimates from the 2023 
study could not be updated in this analysis because they are unique to IMPLAN.

Option 1 – Development of an “Annual Economic 
Scorecard” for City of Golden 
 
Develop an annual economic indicators scorecard for the City of Golden that provides an 
ongoing data-driven snapshot of the community's economic and financial health.  The 
scorecard can help City leaders evaluate performance, track baseline trends, and make 
informed strategic choices.  
 
Key Purposes of the Economic Scorecard include: 
• Tracking Economic Performance: Helps measure the raw growth or contraction of 
the local economy  
• Guiding Policy and Investments: Help inform public and private resource allocation 
processes and help design targeted economic development initiatives,  
• Providing Early Warning Signals: Can flag early warning signs of sector-specific 
slowdowns or wider recessions before they hit critical stages. 
• Attracting Businesses and Talent: Can serve as a tool in assisting external investors 
and corporations looking at the area's workforce availability, market viability, and 
overall demographic stability.  
• Benchmarking Against Peers: Can help put into context local economic/financial 
progress by directly comparing the data against Jefferson County, state-level statistics, 
or economically similar peer cities

Option 2: Complete Update of 2023 Economic 
Contribution Study (using IMPLAN) 
 
Prepare a complete update of the 2023 Economic Contribution Study providing a 
detailed analysis of the composition and structure of the Golden economy as of 2024-
25.  The detailed Golden employer database by detailed (6-digit NAICS) industry would 
be updated.  Total jobs (private and public) and labor income in the City of Golden will 
be estimated by detailed (6-digit NAICS) industry sector, along with the City’s gross 
domestic product (GDP), and total value of goods and services produced (output) by 
industry.  Golden’s total economic contribution to the Jefferson County economy will 
also be updated by detailed industry sector.  
 
 
SCOPE OF WORK 
 
Task 1:   Prepare a profile of all industries located in the City of Golden, Colorado.  
Utilizing commercially available databases, a profile of business establishments in 
Golden will be prepared with as much NAICS industry specificity as possible.   The 
profile to include firm name, nature of business, industry, and estimated employment in 
the City of Golden.  In the 2023 Study, over 1,200 business establishments and sole 
proprietorships employing workers in Golden were identified, categorized by 6-digit 
NAICS industry, and employment estimated based on commercially-available 
databases and other sources.  Review and revise the database utilizing commercially-
available databases, building-by-building fieldwork, and manual searches of 
establishments to confirm their status, location in Golden, and industry sector. 
 
 
Task 2:  Prepare estimates of total employment, by detailed industry, within the City of 
Golden.  The Colorado Department of Labor and Employment’s “Quarterly Census of 
Employment and Wages” regrettably does not include any estimates of employment by 
industry.  So, an independent estimate of employment by detailed industry must be 
developed.  U.S. Census Bureau estimates of employment by major industry division 
developed through its Longitudinal Employer Household Dynamics (LEHD) program will 
again serve as the starting point for preparing estimates of employment by detailed 
industry (at the 3-4 digit NAICS industry level).  The detailed industry profiles prepared 
in Task 1 will then be used to inform and align the distribution of employment across the 
3-4 digit NAICS industries.  Other adjustments to the detailed industry employment 
estimates will be made using data from other commercially available databases (Data 
Axle, Dun & Bradstreet, Esri).  Employment and departmental expenditure data will be 
collected for local government organizations.  Federal government employment, and 
employment at other major employers, will also be collected from any other available 
sources.  Also, as necessary, corrections and adjustments to the data to reflect the 
impacts of remote-working may be incorporated.   
 
 
Task 3:  Estimate the economic contribution of each industry (including direct, indirect, 
and induced contributions to the Golden area economy).  The detailed industry 
 
employment estimates developed in Task 2 will serve as inputs to the IMPLAN (Input- 
output Modeling for PLANning) economic impact software.  Utilizing IMPLAN, estimates 
of the direct economic impacts resulting from the ongoing employment and business 
activity in each industry in the Golden area economy will be generated.  The IMPLAN 
software will then be employed to estimate the indirect and induced effects of the 
economic activity and direct employment by industry.  Outputs from the modeling will 
include total, direct, indirect, and induced: 
 
ü Output - total sales or revenues generated. 
ü Value-added - newly created goods and services resulting from revenues and 
expenditures, analogous to Gross Domestic Product (GDP). 
ü Labor Income - employee salaries and benefits and proprietor income. 
ü Employment - total full-time and part-time jobs generated. 
An IMPLAN model will be set up and run for each detailed industry.  Over 350 separate 
IMPLAN model runs will be required.  IMPLAN software is typically used in the 
preparation of economic impact analyses of a new firm or in a change in the level of 
output (or employment) of a given firm.  Economic contribution analyses are a bit 
different in that they are estimating the indirect and induced effects that the current level 
of output (or employment) of an existing industry as a whole has on other industries in 
the region.  In an economic contribution analysis, the goal is to generate a total output 
effect that is equal to the current level of production of that industry, while showing the 
indirect and indirect effects that this current level of output has on other industries in the 
region.  Therefore, special modeling techniques will be required to ensure that the 
results do not overstate/understate the economic contributions of the industry being 
studied.  
 
Totals for the Golden area economy will be prepared.  Comparisons and relative shares 
of the total area economy attributable to each detailed industry will also be presented.  
Also, relative contributions of Golden-based employment and industries to the total 
Jefferson County economy will also be prepared.  
 
 
Task 4: Estimate total business-to-business sales made in Golden, and by Golden 
businesses and government elsewhere in Jefferson County.  Estimate total sales made 
by worker spending in the Jefferson County economy my Golden jobholders and supply 
chain effects.  Estimate taxable worker spending made in the City of Golden by Golden 
jobholders, and the total contribution of worker spending to Golden’s sales tax base.    
 
 
Task 5: One preliminary summary report and one final summary report describing the 
methodology and key findings of Tasks 1-4.  All reports will be provided in electronic 
PDF format.  A PowerPoint presentation containing the key findings and conclusions of 
the economic impact analysis.  A presentation of preliminary findings will be delivered 
by Anderson Analytics project team members.

Option 3: Update of 2023 Economic Contribution 
Study using summary (2-digit NAICS industries) and 
RIMSII Multipliers 
 
 
Most of the components of the 2023 Economic Contribution Study would be updated to 
2024 utilizing U.S. Census Bureau estimates of employment by 2-digit NAICS sector 
and the use of Regional Input-Output Modeling System (RIMS II) multipliers from the 
U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), along with other BEA data tables available at 
the county level.  This option would significantly reduce the estimated cost of the study 
effort, although the study results and estimated economic contributions will not be 
directly comparable to the 2023 study result given the changes in methodology and 
employment data source proposed under this Option.  Additionally, a few of the outputs 
and estimates from the 2023 study could not be updated in this analysis because they 
are unique to IMPLAN, and also because of the BEA’s discontinuation of some key data 
tables in 2025. 
 
 
SCOPE OF WORK 
 
Task 1:  U.S. Census Bureau estimates of employment in the City of Golden by 2-digit 
NAICS industry sector developed through its Longitudinal Employer Household 
Dynamics (LEHD) program will be collected and will serve as the primary input to the 
economic contribution analysis.  NOTE: The 2023 Economic Contribution Study used 
detailed employment estimates (at the 5-digit NAICS industry sector) that were 
prepared through the compilation and analysis of a comprehensive database of Golden 
employers.     
 
Task 2:  Estimate the economic contribution of each industry (including direct, indirect, 
and induced contributions to the Golden area economy).  The industry employment data 
collected in Task 1 will serve as inputs to a spreadsheet model developed by Anderson 
Analytics utilizing RIMS II input-output regional multipliers that are prepared by the U.S. 
Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), along with other BEA tables available at the 
county level.  Utilizing the RIMS II based model, estimates of the direct economic 
impacts resulting from the ongoing employment and business activity in each industry in 
the Golden area economy will be generated, based on countywide averages by industry 
at the 2-digit NAICS level.  The RIMS II model will then be employed to estimate the 
indirect and induced effects of the economic activity and direct employment by industry.  
Results from the modeling will include total, direct, indirect, and induced impacts on the 
Jefferson County economy from the City of Golden: 
 
ü Output - total sales or revenues generated. 
ü Labor Income - employee salaries and benefits and proprietor income. 
ü Employment - total full-time and part-time jobs generated. 
 
The total (direct, plus indirect and induced) contribution of Golden’s economy to total 
county GDP would also be estimated. 
A RIMS II model will be set up and run for each detailed industry.  About 65 separate 
RIMS II model runs will be required. Totals for the Golden area economy will be 
prepared.  Comparisons and relative shares of the total area economy attributable to 
each industry will also be presented.  Also, relative contributions of Golden-based 
employment and industries to the total Jefferson County economy will also be prepared.  
 
Task 3: One preliminary summary report and one final summary report describing the 
methodology and key findings of Tasks 1-2.  All reports will be provided in electronic 
PDF format.  A PowerPoint presentation containing the key findings and conclusions of 
the economic impact analysis.  A presentation of preliminary findings will be delivered 
by Anderson Analytics project team members.  Please note that under this Option 
the updated final report will not include updates to all the tables and graphics 
included in the original 2023 Economic Contribution Study.  The data in some of 
those tables are unique outputs from IMPLAN software that cannot be replicated 
using RIMS II and BEA based models.
