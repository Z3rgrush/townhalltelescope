MEMORANDUM 
To:               Parks and Recreation Advisory Board (PRAB) 
 
From:           Director, Parks and Recreation 
 
Date: October 15, 2026   
 
Re: 2026 Master Plan Update 
 
Purpose: This memo is an update on 2026 projects that supported the goals of the 
Parks and Recreation Master Plan and Open Space Master Plan. After this update, the 
Parks and Recreation Department will provide updates on a quarterly basis.  
 
Background: Master plans set a long-term vision and goals for an organization 
(typically over a 7- 10 year timeline). In 2025, City Council adopted the Parks and 
Recreation Master Plan (PRMP) and the Open Space Master Plan Phase 2 (OSMP), 
establishing a long-term vision and strategic recommendations to guide the stewardship, 
maintenance, enhancement, and future development of Golden's parks, recreation, trails, 
and open space systems. Since adoption, Parks and Recreation staff have been 
advancing implementation of both plans through ongoing projects, operational 
improvements, and policy development. In addition, the Parks and Recreation Advisory 
Board has received regular monthly updates through the Director's Report on activities 
related to Forestry, Open Space, and Wildfire Mitigation. This memo provides a 
consolidated progress update on implementation efforts completed or underway to date. 
 
The PRMP and OSMP conclude with a list of goals and action items for implementation. 
The following update begins with an overview of the process and structure that the 
Department has developed for completing the plans, followed by a list of projects or 
improvements that address specific goals from the PRMP and OSMP. 
 
Overview  
A notable area of progress has been creating a process and structure to ensure the work 
of the PRMP will be accomplished in the next 5 -7 years. The department hosted two 
strategy workshops for all supervisors on September 17th and October 1st, to refine the 
department’s vision for the future, to introduce the supervisors to all aspects of the PRMP 
and define and prioritize action items for the next two years. At those workshops the team 
identified priority areas of focus. Staff are currently working to refine these areas of focus 
into priorities which can be implemented in the next two years. Potential areas of focus 
include staff retention, support and capacity; strengthening data capture and usage; 

developing a long-term financial strategy; taking care of existing assets and buildings; 
standardizing and strengthening program service delivery; and trail and forestry 
management.  
Once priority action items are identified and introduced to staff, each workgroup will outline 
relevant work for 2027 on group work plans. Work plan items will then be incorporated in 
individual performance goals, so that each person in the department will have relevant 
work tie directly back towards Master Plan goals.  
 
2026 Parks and Recreation Master Plan accomplishments 
 
• Developed a Capital Investment Project (CIP) prioritization matrix that prioritizes reinvestment 
needs across parks, facilities, and trails, and included project scoring criteria, funding strategies, 
and prioritization by year to ensure long-term financial sustainability. Parks staff from various levels 
in the department reviewed a list of proposed capital projects for the next ten years and evaluate 
them against the matrix that was reviewed by PRAB in May and June of 2026. Goal 2-A: Enhance 
Financial Sustainability and Goal 3-A: Improve Data and Performance Tracking. 
 
• Completed a major renovation to the Ulysses Sports Complex  that expanded recreation 
opportunities outside of the downtown core, including expanded multiuse fields, a new playground, 
several covered pavilions and picnic areas, improved lighting and a relocated dog park. Goal 1-B: 
Expand Programming Beyond the GCC. 
 
• Contracted with Davey Resource Group to complete the city’s second urban tree canopy 
assessment (UTC), ten years after the original to benchmark where Golden’s canopy stands and 
how it has changed since the first UTC was completed. This UTC will provide additional focus on 
current challenges the city is facing, including the arrival of emerald ash borer and increased 
wildfire threats. The UTC will aid in overall decision making of how and where to prioritize ongoing 
fuel reduction work. Goal 3-A: Improve Data and Performance Tracking and Goal 1-C: Proactive 
Maintenance and Care.  
 
• Collaborated on an Intergovernmental Agreement to formally establish the Clear Creek Trail 
Collaborative to provide a forum for agencies to facilitate a regional, coordinated approach to 
achieving common goals related to the Clear Creek Trail corridor. Goal 1-F Maintain and Improve 
Existing Trails.  
 
• Completed procurement and training for a new Recreation Software System, replacing the 
existing RecTrac software with SmartRec (Amilia) to improve customer service, enhance 
programming and create stronger internal controls for financial management. Goal 2-A: Enhance 
Financial Sustainability and Goal 3-A: Improve Data and Performance Tracking. 
 
• Invested in preventative maintenance and equipment replacement in the Golden Community 
Center, including the replacement of upstairs flooring, scuff and buff of hardwood flooring, 
replacing HVAC duct brackets in the pool area, and weightroom equipment replacements. Goal 
2-C: Plan for Facility Renovation and Replacement, and Goal 3-C: Modernize the Golden 
Community Center.  
 
• Conducted an irrigation audit to help prioritize system replacements and efficiency 
improvements. Goal 1-C: Proactive Maintenance and Care. 
 
• Completed community engagement and 60% design of Lions Park, a major renovation and 
replacement project on the city’s most heavily used playground. Goal 2-C: Plan for Facility 
Renovation and Replacement. 
 
• Replaced critical systems in city-owned facilities such as the hot water heater in fire station and 
the roof at Tony Grampsas Gym. Goal 1-C: Proactive Maintenance and Care 
 
2026 Open Space Master Plan accomplishments 
 
• Completed the Noxious Weed Management Plan and did Noxious Weed management in nearly 
all of the city’s Open Spaces. Action item 2(c) Enhance Restoration Efforts. 
•  
• Coordinated with the Golden Fire Department to implement wildfire risk reduction measures in 
open spaces including fuels mitigation projects in Kinney Run. Action item 4(c) Integrate Wildfire 
Mitigation.  
 
• Enacted various trials for social trail closure, management and restoration to test effectiveness, 
such as fencing and signage at New Loveland Mine Park and along Clear Creek. Action item 
2(c), enhance restoration efforts and Action item 9(h), Identify and assess social trails.  
 
• Partnered with planning, communications and museum to kick off an interpretive sign planning 
effort to create standards for existing and future interpretive, educational and wayfinding signs 
citywide, including in parks and open space. Action items 3(a) Install Property Name and 
Wayfinding Signage, 3 (b) Expand Signage and Interpretation, and 9(g) Strengthen Wayfinding 
and Visitor Education on Designated Trails.  
 
• Allocated funding through conservation trust funds to support grant matching, conduct field studies 
and support future open space projects . Action items 8(a) Secure Long-Term Open Space 
Funding, 8(c) Apply for State and Federal Grants, and 8(d) Address Capital Needs. 
 
• In process of formalizing partnership with Friends of Dinosaur Ridge through a license agreement, 
which will govern activities and the development of educational and fossil preservation assets in 
the Parfet Prehistoric Preserve. Action item 4(e) Formalize Partnership with Friends of Dinosaur 
Ridge.
