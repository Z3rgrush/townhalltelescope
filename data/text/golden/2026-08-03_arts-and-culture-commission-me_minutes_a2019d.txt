Update on Municipal Building and East Amenity Zone 
Steve Glueck, Assistant to the City Manager, provided an overview of the Heart of Golden project, with a focus on the East Zone and Municipal Building. He reviewed the project's history and current planning efforts and asked the Commission to begin considering opportunities for public art throughout the area.
Steve discussed potential changes and amenities in the East Zone, including improvements to Tucker Gulch, connections between parks, ecological restoration, pedestrian improvements, and potential recreational amenities. A feasibility study is underway, with additional planning and community engagement expected in 2027.
Steve also reviewed the area between the Municipal Building and Farmers High Line Canal. The landscape architect provided potential public art locations, and the Commission discussed whether to concentrate artwork in the more active areas or distribute artwork throughout the East Zone, including the more secluded eastern area.
The group discussed several potential public art opportunities, including an artist-designed fence, shade structures, a themed art walk, and a possible outdoor performance space. Steve emphasized the importance of developing focused artist calls that align with the specific opportunities and available funding.
Steve reviewed anticipated project timelines and funding. The area east of Archer Street is in final design, while planning for the area from Ford Street to Archer Street is beginning this year and construction is anticipated in 2028/2029. The group discussed the importance of planning for future public art projects now so funds are available later as phases of the project move forward.
Steve noted that he is running the planning side of the project this year, but that the Parks and Recreation Department is responsible for site decisions and recommendations. 
Nathan noted that the ACC approved to increase the Lion’s Park Artwork budget through their funds and encouraged the Commission to begin considering how to allocate future funding for the Heart of Golden project.
Steve provided an update on artwork planned for the Municipal Building. He reviewed the current public art budget and discussed potential opportunities for additional two-dimensional artwork inside the building.  
Steve also shared that the City is considering a mural on the parking structure for the new Municipal Building at 10th Street and Vasquez Boulevard and requested the ACC's involvement in the process. He noted that using the history of the surrounding neighborhood as a theme could be appropriate for an open call.
The Commission discussed the ACC's role in selecting artwork purchased with the City's 1% public art allocation. Members emphasized that the Commission must be involved in all public art decisions from the beginning of the process, including decisions regarding two-dimensional artwork and the use of an outside art broker.
Steve agreed to continue the discussion with the AIPP Subcommittee. He anticipates having a more substantial update on the Municipal Building artwork in October, when the surrounding construction is further along.
Update on Integrated Department Strategic Plan
Nathan Richie transitioned into a discussion about the integrated department strategic plan. The Commission expressed concerns that the revised plan had become overly corporate and had lost the spirit and language of the original document.
Commissioners and Staff recommended establishing an ad hoc subcommittee to review the plan and work to create a more accessible version while maintaining the intent of the original. A revised draft will be brought back to the full Commission for review one the subcommittee has met.
Grants Subcommittee UpdateSaoirse Charis-Graves provided an update on the grant program timeline and application revisions. Staff incorporated the subcommittee's recommendations, including improvements to financial accessibility language and refinements to the grant tiers. No major eligibility or funding changes were made. Applications will open August 28th, with an open house, feedback, and recommendations taking place in the following months.
AIPP Subcommittee UpdateRick Souders reported that the AIPP Subcommittee will meet monthly through October due to the number of upcoming projects. The Lions Park Subcommittee will review applications and provide recommendations on August 20.
Staff Update
Nathan provided updates on current public art projects, including Lions Park, the 150 artworks, and the recently acquired mountain lions sculptures by artist Bill Gee at the Golden History Museum.
Staff are coordinating a potential joint reception for the 150 artworks and Bill Gee’s mountain lions once Graham’s mural is complete. The group suggested that this event could be used to highlight the ACC’s work.

Work Plan and Benchmarking Discussion
Rick opened a discussion about the Commission's work plan and the need to begin thinking about the 2027 work plan and longer-term priorities.
The Commission discussed benchmarking with the Leeds School of Business to better understand the size and impact of Golden's creative sector. Members expressed interest in developing a more comprehensive understanding of the economic, social, community, and cultural impact of arts and culture.
The Commission also discussed public art maintenance funding and how other municipalities fund maintenance of their public art collections. Members agreed that additional benchmarking and research would be useful in providing information to City Council and informing future funding discussions.
Nathan explained that the current benchmarking work is primarily focused on employment data and that the Commission should identify additional quantitative and qualitative measures that would better capture the impact of arts and culture. Members agreed to submit ideas for metrics, benchmarking information, and ways to communicate the broader impact of arts and culture to staff.
The Commission discussed the distinction between the annual work plan and longer-term priorities. Members expressed interest in thinking beyond immediate projects and developing one-, three-, and five-year priorities, including a potential funding map.
Rick Souders proposed holding a study session to further develop the Commission's priorities and long-term direction. The Commission will continue gathering ideas and information before determining whether additional funding requests are appropriate.
