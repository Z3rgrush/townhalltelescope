2. Golden Economic Data: Key Indicators and Dashboard Options
Fo
llowing input and direction from the board in the August Meeting, Staff have begun 
investigations with Anderson Analytics, the consulting firm that produced the Economic 
Contribution Study about what their proposed option for an annual economic dashboard will 
be.  Staff will be meeting with the consultant the same day as the board meeting and can 
provide a verbal update on further progress to the board.  
St
aff is concurrently evaluating economic indicator and data visualization platforms to enhance 
how the City tracks, analyzes, and communicates Golden’s economic story to the public and to 
city management and city council. Currently, obtaining timely, localized economic intelligence 
presents significant structural challenges due to geographic scale and reporting boundaries. 
Golden is split across two primary ZIP codes and multiple census tracts without its own 
Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA) designation. As a result, standard municipal-level releases 
rely heavily on multi-year American Community Survey (ACS) snapshots, which quickly become 

2  
outdated, require recurring investment to rebuild, and frequently suffer from data suppression 
in smaller sample sizes. 
 
By contrast, regional neighbors benefit from dedicated MSA-level tracking. The Bureau of Labor 
Statistics (BLS) Mountain-Plains Regional Information Office publishes frequent, high-
granularity employment, wage, and consumer data for designated Colorado metro areas (such 
as Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins and Colorado Springs). Because Golden is subsumed within 
broader county- and MSA-level aggregates, local economic shifts are difficult to isolate through 
standard public reporting alone. To address these constraints, staff is exploring dedicated 
dashboard platforms that will enable quick, centralized access to publicly available 
data, facilitating regular updates to a dashboard of relevant economic indicators, delivering 
more frequent workforce data, and powering public-facing economic storytelling. 
 
Vendor Exploration & Platform Overview 
Staff conducted exploratory discussions with two leading platforms in the municipal data and 
visualization space: mySidewalk and Lightcast. While both solutions aggregate public and 
proprietary datasets into user-friendly web dashboards, they approach economic storytelling 
from slightly different angles:  
• mySidewalk: Emphasizes narrative-driven reporting, community indicator tracking, and 
public engagement. The platform specializes in translating broad socioeconomic 
datasets into accessible, visual stories that support strategic planning, grant 
applications, and community recruitment. 
• Lightcast: Specializes in deep labor market intelligence and workforce analytics. The 
platform provides detailed insight into local employment trends, industry clusters, and 
real-time job posting activity, paired with embeddable visual dashboards designed for 
municipal web pages.  
Next Steps & Trial Period 
Both vendors have agreed to provide staff with complimentary trial access over the coming 
weeks. During this pilot period, staff will evaluate each tool’s user experience, data availability 
at the local level, and dashboard functionality. Following the trial evaluations, staff will return 
to the board with a formal recommendation regarding platform selection and next steps. 
 
To better support staff in selecting a method and vendor to support annual economic data 
tracking and data presentation to stakeholders, a review of what types of economic data would 
be most useful for the Golden community, City Staff and Council, as well as prospective 
business prospects is requested.  Staff requests that the board consider the best way to gather 
input from the board and other stakeholders.

COUNCIL MEMORANDUM 
To: The Honorable Mayor and City Council  
From: Joe Allaire, Director of Finance  
Date: August 13th, 2026 
Subject: May 2026 Sales, Use, Lodging, and Marijuana Tax Report 
The attached reports present sales and use tax, lodging tax, and marijuana tax revenues 
through May 2026. 
Sales tax revenue through May is trailing budget projections by $89,688; however, overall 
collections remain relatively strong. As anticipated, use tax revenue continues to trail the 
prior year, although the City received an increase in use tax audit revenue that is discussed 
later in this memorandum. 
Lodging tax revenue declined in May compared with the same month in recent years. The 
finance department will continue to monitor this trend  as the City enters the summer, 
historically its strongest period for lodging tax collections. Marijuana tax revenue continues 
to demonstrate steady growth, exceeding both budget projections and prior -year 
collections. 
Sales Tax 
Sales tax revenue in May increased by $98,934 compared with May 2025. This positive 
monthly performance continues the relatively consistent collection pattern observed during 
the first five months of the year and helps explain why revenues remain close to the City’s 
budget trend line. On a year-to-date basis, sales tax revenue is $264,005 higher than during 
the same period in 2025, providing an encouraging indication of the City’s overall sales tax 
performance. 
As expected, out-of-city sales continue to generate strong year -over-year growth and 
remain an important contributor to the City’s overall collections. Performance varies among 
the City’s geographic reporting areas, however, with the Heritage Square area experiencing 
the largest year-over-year decline. The Finance Department will continue monitoring these 
geographic trends to determine whether they represent temporary fluctuations or a broader 
shift in local sales activity. 
Use Tax 
Through May, use tax revenue continues to follow the anticipated trend of trailing 2025 
collections. May 2026 represented the lowest May total within the period displayed in this 
report, and year-to-date collections are 22.97% below the same period in 2025. Although 

⚫ Page 2 
this decline was expected, the Finance Department will continue monitoring collections 
throughout the remainder of the year. 
One encouraging development has been the City’s recovery of $466,013 in use tax 
revenue through its 2026 audit and compliance efforts. Most of this revenue resulted from 
an audit of a local energy and utilities company, which determined that the City had not 
received its share of use tax owed on software used at the company’s location within 
Golden. This recovery demonstrates the importance of the City’s ongoing tax audit program 
in promoting compliance and ensuring that all revenue legally due to the City is properly 
collected. 
 
Lodging Tax 
Lodging tax revenue decreased by $45,267 in May compared with May 2025, leaving year-
to-date collections $62,993 below the City’s budget trend line. Although the current variance 
is not significant, it comes as the City enters the summer months, which historically 
represent the most important period of the year for lodging tax collections. If this trend 
continues, the City could experience a year-end revenue shortfall. Any projected shortfall 
could be incorporated into the 2027 –2028 biennial budget by reducing planned 
expenditures to align with updated revenue expectations. 
Although the City does not disclose information regarding individual taxpayers, the Sales 
Tax Division is actively pursuing outstanding lodging tax collections. These efforts may 
improve overall lodging tax revenue and help offset a portion of the current variance. 
Marijuana Tax 
Marijuana tax revenue continues to be a bright spot for the City, with actual collections 
consistently exceeding budget projections. May was the strongest-performing month within 
the period displayed in this report, and current collections have the City firmly on pace to 
achieve its highest annual marijuana tax revenue total on record. 
The City will continue monitoring this revenue stream to determine how long the current 
growth cycle continues and what level of collections can reasonably be considered 
sustainable. Establishing a reliable long-term revenue baseline will allow the City to prepare 
more accurate forecasts and make responsible decisions regarding the amount of ongoing 
spending that this revenue can support. 
Final Thoughts 
Overall, tax revenue performance through May presents a mixed but generally stable and 
positive financial picture. Sales tax collections remain close to budget and continue to 
exceed the prior year, while marijuana tax revenue is performing exceptionally well and is 
on pace for its strongest year on record. Although use tax collections remain below 2025 
levels, this decline was anticipated, and the City’s recovery of $466,013 through audit and 
compliance efforts will help offset a portion of the reduction. 
Lodging tax represents a significant area to monitor as the City enters the important 
summer collection period. The Finance Department will also continue evaluating 
geographic sales tax trends, use tax performance, and the sustainability of marijuana tax 
growth. These results will inform the City’s revenue projections for the 2027–2028 biennial 
⚫ Page 3 
budget and allow planned expenditures to be adjusted, if necessary, to reflect the most 
current financial outlook.
