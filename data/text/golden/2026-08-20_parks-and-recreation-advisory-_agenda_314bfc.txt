Public Comment 
Tracy Evanko and Matthew Myers addressed PRAB in follow-up to written communications submitted 
to City staff regarding the large elk statue located in the roundabout at 4th and Campbell in the 
Heritage Dells neighborhood. Her written correspondence will be entered as formal written public 
comment. Since the installation of the artwork, a local neighbor had assumed responsibility for 
installing and maintaining the landscaping flowers and plants around the roundabout. That neighbor 
DRAFT
has recently passed away, and some neighbors are pitching in to continue watering, but do not want 
to continue the maintenance long term. They are asking the City to assume maintenance of the 
landscaping, including regular hand watering since there is no irrigation to the roundabout. There 
existed a formal agreement between the neighborhood group and the City for the purchase and 
installation of the artwork, but there is no agreement governing the maintenance of the landscaping.

Dear Mr. Sweat, 
Seven years ago, a small group of Heritage Dells residents succeeded in matching funds 
provided by the City, a 50/50 partnership, for the "Protecting the Future" (mother elk and 
two calves) statue that stands in the roundabout at Heritage Road and Kimball Avenue. 
At that time, we also provided for vegetation around the base of the statue. We researched 
suitable drought-tolerant plants for our environment and have taken care of them each year 
since. Not only do we replace plants that have died (at our own expense), we also take 
time to weed the entire roundabout (plants and rock area) each spring and most 
importantly, to water the plants on a regular basis during the growing season. We have 
included photos so you can see what is there. 
The gentleman who spearheaded the project took on the responsibility of the watering of 
the flowers, along with occasional help when needed. He faithfully visited the roundabout 
several times a week for seven years during the early morning hours to ensure they were 
getting enough water to continue thriving in that location. 
Earlier last month that gentleman passed away, and we have decided to honor him by 
continuing to maintain the plants in the roundabout. The weeding of the area and the 
DRAFT
replenishing of the plants is not a hardship at all. However, In the several weeks we have 
tried to keep up with the watering, we have realized that it is a commitment we are unable 
to make long-term. 
With this letter we are asking if the Parks & Recreation Department would consider adding 
the roundabout at Heritage Road and Kimball Avenue to its summer watering rotation. We 
estimate that the area will need close to 36-60 gallons per week. We believe the City has a 
watering truck, but another option might be to extend the underground sprinkler under the 
road from one of the landscaped areas on the east side of the roundabout if that is 
possible. We will plan on attending the next Parks and Recreation Advisory Board meeting 
so we can answer any questions you may have. 
The residents of Heritage Dells are very fond of the entrance to our neighborhood that we 
have created together with the City of Golden, and we are hoping that the Parks & 
Recreation Department can help us continue to make it lovely for people who use Heritage 
Road. 
Thank you so much for your consideration. 
Tracy Evanko & Matthew Myers

From:  Rheana Rogers, Recreation Supervisor-Administration & Projects 
Through: Jeremy Sweat, Parks and Recreation Director  
Date: 8- 14-26 
Re: Lions Park Playground Replacement Project Design Update 
Purpose of Agenda Item:  
To provide an update on the design progress on the Lions Park Playground Replacement 
Project to the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board. 
 
 
Background & Update:   
The Lions Park Playground Replacement Project continues to advance through design 
following extensive community engagement and selection of the community's preferred 
playground concept. The project will replace the existing playground and includes new play 
equipment, improved accessibility, replacement irrigation, new picnic amenities, and an 
outdoor classroom and native garden designed to support nature play and environmental 
education. 
 
Staff continue to pursue funding opportunities to enhance the project. The City's Great 
Outdoors Colorado (GOCO) Community Impact Grant concept paper was selected to 
advance to the full application phase, and staff are currently preparing the full grant 
application. Staff are also exploring an additional grant opportunity through Colorado Gives 
Foundation to support project enhancements. 
 
The purpose of this presentation is to provide the Board with an update on the project's 
progress and share the current design as it advances toward final construction documents. 
Staff and the project design team will present the proposed site layout, playground equipment, 
and precedent imagery illustrating the intended character and design of the park, as well as 
highlight how community feedback has been incorporated into the project. 
 
The presentation will also include an overview of the project schedule, including the next steps 
in design, public art integration, and anticipated construction. 
 
Construction is expected to begin in late 2026 or early 2027, with project completion currently 
targeted for late spring or early summer 2027.
