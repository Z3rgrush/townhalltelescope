Public Comment  
Julie Smith, resident and member of Sustainable Golden, provided public comment regarding the 
Clayworks amendment memo. She supported Clayworks maintaining its original sustainability 
commitments and opposed additional natural gas use for new construction. She raised concerns about

Public Comment Closed: 6:06 p.m. 

Business and Action Items 
A. Public Electric Vehicle Charging Station policy review 
Staff presented the past 12 months of public EV charging station data, including station usage, revenues 
and costs, regional fee comparisons, and a potential overnight charging policy to improve access for 
residents without home charging. The Board discussed cost recovery, vandalism and cable theft 
prevention, usage trends by station, charger uptime, continued grant opportunities, and the importance 
of expanding charging access near multifamily housing. The Board agreed that no fee change is needed 
at this time and supported recommending an overnight charging policy that would remove the three-hour 
time limit and overstay fee between 7 p.m. and 7 a.m. The Board also discussed flagging future 
consideration of broader multifamily charging policy and using a future EV charging master plan update 
to review year-over-year usage, uptime data, and potential new locations. Staff will prepare the Board’s 
recommendation for City Council consideration and coordinate communications if the overnight 
charging change is approved. 
B. 2027 Budget Request Discussion 
Staff reviewed the proposed 2027 sustainability budget and noted that some new program requests are 
expected to be approved due to anticipated revenue constraints and a more conservative Citywide 
budget approach. The Board discussed several funding priorities within the constrained 2027 budget, 
including rebate funding, bear-resistant cart needs, battery education events, and construction and 
demolition waste work. The Board identified sustainability rebates and construction and demolition 
waste planning as priority additions to the proposed budget and agreed to recommend increasing rebate 
funding from $85,000 to $160,000 and adding $10,000 for construction and demolition work, without 
reducing funding for other proposed sustainability programs. 
C. Universal Recycling Ordinance (URO) 
Staff provided an update on development of a URO, including execution of the CEO grant agreement, the 
upcoming waste outreach RFP, and research into other communities’ ordinance and hauler licensing 
models. Staff discussed a potential phased approach that would begin with hauler licensing 
requirements to provide recycling service, timed with the availability of the state Producer Responsibility 
Program recycling reimbursement funds, before considering a more comprehensive ordinance structure 
with greater property owner responsibility and administrative needs. The Board supported staff 
continuing to explore a hauler licensing approach as a practical first step and discussed the importance 
of hauler reporting to support both recycling reimbursement and City program tracking. 
Current Projects 
A. Staff & Board Updates 
Staff and the Board discussed the draft response to the Clayworks amendment request, including 
additional comments and public input to consider before finalizing the memo. The Board discussed 
strengthening the memo’s recommendations related to emissions analysis, renewable energy offsets, 
and other potential mitigation measures if additional natural gas use is approved, including whether 
mitigation should include on-site renewable energy, additional energy efficiency measures, future gas-
use limitations, or fees that could support offset programs. Staff will revise the letter, selected Board 
members will review it, and the final memo will be sent to City Council with Planning Commission 
copied. 
Staff and the Board also discussed potential dates for the year-end meeting, including options before 
Thanksgiving or during the week of December 7. Staff provided an update on commercial energy 
benchmarking, including work to develop the covered buildings list, and plan outreach to affected 
buildings. The Board emphasized the importance of having a clear plan for how reported data will be 
used to support action, and commercial energy benchmarking will return as an agenda item at the next 
meeting.

Adjournment 
There being no further business, the meeting was adjourned at 8:31 p.m.

CSAB Clayworks Memorandum - Comments – 9/23/26 
Hi, I’m Julie Smith, A member of Sustainable Golden 
Thank you for putting together the memo for council on the Clayworks exception request for 
installation of natural gas ammenities.  I had a few comments to add. 
1. I completely agree with Clayworks keeping to their original commitments.  We only have 
about 20 years to rein in the environmental devastation on our planet, and now is not 
the time to grant exceptions for burning yet more natural gas on new construction. 
2. I am completely opposed to a pool and spas, when public facilities are available less than 
a mile away.  These are the very sorts of unnecessary luxuries that are destroying our 
planet.  If they end up getting approved, they should absolutely be heated with 
geothermal topped by heat pumps for net zero.  AC stated that six geothermal wells 
would delay the project.  That should be on them to decide whether they feel these 
facilities are worth delaying the project.  If they wanted to avoid delays, they should 
have proposed these facilities in the original bid.  AC stated that heat pump technology 
is more expensive than natural gas.  This may be true for the installation, but it’s 
absolutely not the case for a long-term proper economic analysis, which would include 
cost and carbon emissions of continued burning of natural gas as said gas becomes more 
expensive to produce and purchase, compared to renewable electricity, which is only 
purchased once, and is now more economical than fossil fuels. 
3. Communal grills can be powered with propane or, better yet, with ethanol, which is 
renewable. 
4. Natural gas is absolutely not required for pizza ovens.  Blue pan makes excellent pizza 
with electricity, as does my husband, who is a professional chef and has been making 
pizza in our electric oven for years, the best on the planet. 
5. AC’s argument for natural gas ﬁreplaces was vague and subjective.  Aesthetics was cited.  
I mean, how is aesthetics deﬁned anyway?  Personally, I’ve seen both natural gas and 
electric ﬁreplaces and they both look equally fake.   
a. The analysis of gas vs electric ﬁreplaces is incomplete.  First, for an indoor gas 
ﬁreplace, it would have to be vented, removing by deﬁnition a portion of heated 
air from inside the building, adding to heat demands, not necessary for an 
electric ﬁreplace. 
b. Second, they failed to mention that a natural gas ﬁreplace emits more than 10 
times more carbon (inside or outside) than an electric ﬁreplace of comparable 
size. 
6. For any analysis of construction and operating costs of natural gas amenities, to be 
complete, these should be compared with comparable renewable/electric amenities, 
and should not be limited to cost, but should also include long term (30 year) carbon 
emissions. 
7. I am opposed to using PV as justiﬁcation to oﬀset unnecessary natural gas.  Oﬀsets are 
one of the many greenwashing excuses that are killing our planet. 
8. We have to start thinking diﬀerently if we want to save our planet.  Business as usual 
isn’t going to do it.  Gas is going to get harder to produce and extremely expensive in just 
the next couple decades, and there will be a point where it will be unaﬀordable.  Why 
not stop burning it now, rather than later?  Why warm our planet even more with 
business as usual?  Let’s set an example and drive this project home in the right way, and 
set an example for future projects.  Let’s make Clayworks a project that we can be proud 
of! 
Thank you for listening to my comments, and thank you for all you do.
