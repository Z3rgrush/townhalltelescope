Public Comment  
Garrett Baum, a real estate developer with properties in Golden, expressed concerns about the 
proposed Commercial Energy Benchmarking Program, particularly reporting costs, administrative 
burdens, and potential future requirements. He encouraged the City to engage directly with business 
owners and consider lessons learned from similar programs in Denver and Boulder. 
Chris Ball, a downtown Golden property owner and commercial real estate professional, provided 
public comment regarding the proposed Commercial Energy Benchmarking Program. He encouraged 

the City to clarify the program’s goals, consider the differing needs of building owners and tenants, and 
explore a voluntary pilot program with case studies and incentives. He also recommended engaging 
businesses directly through existing community organizations and emphasizing potential cost savings. 
Public Comment initially closed at 6:17 p.m. and then opened again during the Commercial Energy 
Benchmarking agenda item. 
Business and Action Items 
A. Water Conservation Update 
Staff provided a year-end update on 2026 water conservation programs, drought response efforts, and 
planned updates to the City's water management policies. Nels Bjarke from Western Water Assessment also 
presented a new weather-normalization model developed for the City to evaluate water conservation 
progress while accounting for climate and weather variability. The Board discussed the City's water savings, 
including questions about increased water use in June, reservoir storage levels, and the effectiveness of 
drought restrictions. The Board noted the value of using the new model to inform future water conservation 
goals and inquired about the extent of drought-related turf damage in City parks and whether reduced 
irrigation levels could be sustained in future years. Staff will provide updated water conservation metrics 
following the conclusion of drought restrictions on October 31. 
B. Commercial Energy Benchmarking Program 
The Board welcomed public input during the discussion and reviewed written comments received, with 
concerns raised about reporting burdens, potential future building performance requirements, and the need 
for clear program goals and benefits. The Board discussed the importance of defining how reported data 
would be used, understanding the needs of businesses, and communicating potential financial benefits and 
incentives rather than focusing solely on reporting requirements. The Board expressed strong interest in 
exploring a voluntary pilot program with incentives, involving a diverse group of building types and businesses 
to demonstrate benefits and inform future policy development. The Board also encouraged targeted 
engagement through existing business organizations and City partnerships, with a focus on gathering feedback 
and building trust before advancing program requirements. 
C. Energy in Existing Buildings 
The planned staff presentation to review progress on energy efficiency and renewable energy in existing 
buildings, including potential building code amendments and incentives, was postponed due to unexpected 
staff absences. The Board briefly discussed previous work identifying potential gaps in building code 
requirements for major renovations and expressed interest in reviewing progress since adoption of the City's 
electric-preferred energy code. The Board requested that the topic be rescheduled, with preference for 
presentations from the City's Chief Building Official and a representative from SWEEP to provide updates on 
local implementation and approaches taken by other communities. 
Current Projects 
A. Staff & Board Updates 
The Board reviewed the draft memorandum to City Council requesting additional funding for sustainability 
rebates and construction and demolition (C&D) waste reduction in the 2027–2028 budget. The Board 
recommended strengthening the justification for increased rebate funding by highlighting the program’s high 
demand, rapid exhaustion of available funds, and significant interest in solar and battery rebates. The Board 
also recommended clarifying how the proposed $10,000 for C&D waste reduction would be used, including 
who would provide the planning and technical assistance. Staff will incorporate the feedback before 
forwarding the memo to Council. 
The Board also reviewed the staff update memo and discussed the appliance and mattress recycling program, 
waste diversion rates at special events, particularly the Fourth of July Festival and wanting more information 
on diversion, and the status of the Downtown Waste District and electric waste truck. The Board expressed 
enthusiasm for the proposed climate resilience capstone project and discussed the potential for a City rebate 
for plug-in solar systems, particularly to expand access to affordable solar options for renters.
