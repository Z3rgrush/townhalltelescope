Current Business
Call for Public Comment
No one was present to provide public comment. Staff acknowledged receiving several written comments by email, which were included in the meeting packet. Commissioners did not provide response to comments. 
Mission and Vision Statement Discussion 
Kristie Brice provided an overview of the process used to develop the proposed mission and vision statements. The Strategic Planning Subcommittee identified the need for updated statements, solicited input from commissioners, and incorporated that feedback into the versions presented in the meeting packet.
Commissioners discussed the proposed statements and offered feedback. Some expressed a desire for greater clarity around the word “culture,” while others noted that the term is defined in the Arts and Culture Master Plan. Staff acknowledged the importance of a clear definition, particularly as it relates to the Arts and Culture Grant Program. Sarah Vaine suggested that the ACC consider a study session with City Council to present the new Strategic Plan and accompanying mission and vision statements.
Cindy Jaye raised a question about specifically including the word “inclusivity” in the mission statement. Saoirse Charis-Graves noted that the ACC has emphasized accessibility through its grant program and that inclusivity is a broad term that encompasses accessibility. Tammy Ahmed added that the ACC has an opportunity to present art in which all members of the community can see themselves represented.
Laura Tanner and Pariss Hazelman reflected on their experience serving on the Strategic Planning Subcommittee. They explained that the subcommittee considered feedback from all commissioners and worked to consolidate several longer ideas into cohesive statements.
Kristie explained that the subcommittee approached the mission as defining the ACC’s “what” and “why,” while the vision describes where the Commission hopes to be in five to ten years.
A motion to approve the mission and vision statements as presented passed.
Lions Park Artwork Jury Recommendation
Kristie Brice provided background on the Lions Park public art selection process, including the number of submissions received and the composition of the jury. The jury narrowed the applicant pool to three finalists, each of whom received an honorarium to develop a more detailed proposal. Following its review, the jury recommended Alexander Bond’s Pollinators at Play. Commissioners discussed the recommendation and shared feedback on the selected proposal.
Staff explained that the project will move quickly into the contracting phase. Once the contract is carried out, staff will connect the artist with the project design team. Sarah Vaine added that staff have been meeting with City departments to help clarify when and how to engage the ACC in projects involving public art.
A motion to approve the Lions Park jury recommendation of Pollinators at Play by Alexander Bond, passed.
Location Charrette Open House
Nathan Richie provided details about the Public Art Open House scheduled for October 28, including a meet-and-greet with ACC commissioners, dedication of the two 250/150 artworks, and remarks by Governor Polis. The event will also include a public artwork location activity through which community members can provide input on potential locations for future public art.
Nathan explained that he also plans to speak directly with City departments that regularly interact with the ACC, including Parks and Planning, to gather additional location input. Staff noted that this process will help the Commission establish priorities for future public art locations and identify opportunities to pool available funding when appropriate.
Nathan emphasized that the resulting location document will remain flexible and evolve over time. Exceptions may be necessary as unique opportunities arise, including special projects such as the 250/150 artworks and privately donations.
The discussion then shifted to murals. Staff explained that upcoming revisions to the Art in Public Places (AiPP) Handbook will include updates to the mural policy and provide greater clarity about what qualifies as a public art mural. Most notably, AiPP funds will no longer support murals installed on privately owned property.
Kristie noted that although the program has funded murals on private property in the past, the change reflects the continued development and maturation of the AiPP program. Staff clarified that privately funded murals located on private property do not fall under the ACC’s purview.
Arts and Community Impact Brainstorm
Nathan Richie opened a discussion about the ACC’s goals for measuring the impact of arts and culture in Golden. He asked commissioners to consider the types of impact they would like to better understand and measure, including economic, health, well-being, educational, and social impacts.
Sarah Vaine shared that the Mayor had expressed interest in a State of the Arts event where the ACC could present data about arts and culture in Golden to City Council and the broader community. Sarah provided examples of how such data could be used but emphasized the need to first determine the story the ACC wants to tell. Commissioners expressed mixed views about creating a new event, with some noting the opportunity to engage the broader community and others raising concerns about the administrative effort required.
The Commission discussed several potential areas of focus, including economic, health, well-being, and community impact. Commissioners also considered methods for gathering information, including surveys and grant reporting, and discussed the value of incorporating both quantitative and qualitative data.
Heather Mullins noted that arts and culture activity has historically been easier to quantify in downtown Golden but emphasized the importance of considering impacts across the entire city. Saoirse Charis-Graves suggested researching approaches used by other municipalities.
Tammy Ahmed and Len Matheo discussed the role of advocacy and community building and suggested that these impacts should also be reflected in the data. Commissioners cited examples of private businesses hosting arts events whose community impact extends beyond attendance numbers.
Cindy Jaye suggested using the newly adopted mission and vision statements to guide decisions about which data the ACC should prioritize.
Tammy Ahmed recommended first taking inventory of information the City already has or is connected to, including the Golden Makers feasibility study and the Leeds School data. Heather Mullins invited ACC members to participate in the feasibility study and noted that Economic Development and Arts and Culture will likely collaborate on the project.
Sarah Vaine explained that identifying specific impact goals could also help guide future funding decisions, allowing the Commission to direct grant resources toward activities that advance those goals.
Tammy Ahmed asked about the possibility of developing a 501(c)(3) organization to work alongside the ACC, similar to models used by other municipal arts programs. Staff acknowledged that establishing a separate nonprofit would require significant resources. They also recognized the substantial amount of work the ACC has accomplished since its formation. Nathan Richie noted that opportunities such as this could be considered in the future, while current priorities remain focused on foundational work such as policy development and program prioritization.
Saoirse Charis-Graves expressed particular interest in exploring health and well-being as an area of impact.
Nathan Richie asked commissioners what they would like to see as the next step in the discussion. Kristie Brice asked commissioners to consider what they would want to communicate at a future State of the Arts event, noting that the answer could help determine which data should be prioritized. Sarah Vanie offered examples of potential content, including economic development information, findings from the feasibility study, and data highlighting high-impact arts and culture events, projects, and programs. Tammy Ahmed suggested holding a roundtable when the time is right.
