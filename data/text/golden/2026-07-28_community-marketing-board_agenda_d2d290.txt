UNCIL MEMORANDUM 
To: The Honorable Mayor and City Council 
From: Joe Allaire, Director of Finance  
Date: J uly 10th, 2026 
Subject: April 2026 Sales, Use, Lodging, and Marijuana Tax Report 
The attached reports present sales and use tax, marijuana tax, and lodging tax revenues 
for April 2026. 
Sales tax revenue for April was remarkably consistent with the same month in the prior 
year, and year-to-date collections remain in line with budget projections. Use tax revenue 
experienced a notable year-over-year decline, though this was an anticipated trend and 
remains consistent with the City's expectations. 
Lodging tax revenue continues to perform well, exceeding collections from each of the 
previous two Aprils. This is an encouraging indicator as the City enters what has historically 
been the strongest period of the year for lodging tax collections. Marijuana tax revenue also 
continues to demonstrate steady growth, reflecting a positive trend in this developing 
revenue source. 
Sales Tax 
Sales tax revenues continued to demonstrate remarkable consistency year over year. 
April collections were down just $18,461 compared to the same month in 2025, while 
year-to-date revenues are up $165,072. Over all, sales tax collections remain in line 
with budget projections, and the Finance Department has no significant concerns  as 
the City moves into the middle of the year. 
Taxable sales occurring outside the City's primary tax centers continue to lead overall 
growth, with Downtown Golden and the Corporate Center rounding out the top three 
revenue-generating areas. General Merchandise remains the largest contributor to sales 
tax revenue and continues to be the primary driver of growth within this revenue source. 
Use Tax 
As noted in last month's report, use tax continues to follow the anticipated downward trend 
after reaching record collections in 2025. April was a particularly weak month, with 
the lowest use tax collections of the four-month period presented in this report. As a result, 
year-to-date collections are down $425,103 compared to the same period last year 
and are trailing recent historical levels. 

 Page 2 
Although use tax revenues can fluctuate significantly from month to month, and this decline 
was anticipated during the budget process, the Finance Department is not overly 
concerned at this time. We will continue to closely monitor this trend as the City moves into 
the summer months, when construction activity and other economic factors may influence 
future collections. 
Lodging Tax 
Lodging tax revenues rebounded in April, resulting in the strongest April collections since 
2023. This is an encouraging outcome as the City enters the summer travel season, with 
May through September historically representing the strongest months for lodging tax 
revenue. 
Overall, year-to-date collections remain slightly below budget projections; however, the 
Finance Department does not consider this to be a cause for concern at this time. We will 
continue to monitor performance throughout the peak tourism season to ensure revenues 
remain on track with expectations. 
Marijuana Tax 
Marijuana tax revenues continue to perform strongly, with 2026 collections once again 
outpacing all prior years on record. This performance is consistent with the Finance 
Department's expectation that this revenue source remains in a growth phase. Year-to-date 
collections are up $33,201 compared to the same period in 2025. 
In 2025, marijuana tax revenues remained relatively consistent from May through 
December. The Finance Department will continue to monitor this revenue stream 
throughout the remainder of the year to determine whether a similar pattern emerges in 
2026 and to better understand the long-term trends of this developing revenue source. 
Final Thoughts 
Overall, the City's major tax revenues continue to perform in line with expectations and 
support a positive financial outlook as we move into the middle of 2026. Sales tax, the City's 
largest and most stable revenue source, remains consistent with both prior-year collections 
and budget projections, providing continued confidence in the City's primary revenue 
stream. While use tax has experienced a notable year-over-year decline, this trend was 
anticipated following record collections in 2025 and does not currently present a significant 
concern. 
Lodging tax showed encouraging improvement in April as the City enters its historically 
strongest tourism season, while marijuana tax continues to demonstrate steady year-over-
year growth and remains on pace for another record year. Although the Finance 
Department will continue to closely monitor each revenue source, particularly use tax and 
lodging tax through the summer months, the overall trends remain positive. At this point in 
the year, there are no significant revenue concerns, and the city remains well positioned to 
meet its budget expectations for 2026.
