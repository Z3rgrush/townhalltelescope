A. Golden Community Marketing Program (Visit Golden) Update and Discussion  -  Per 
the Board request, representatives of the Community Marketing Program (aka Visit Golden)

background may provide context.

The City of Golden Community Marketing Pr ogram was established in 2000, following 
requests from local small businesses.   It is funded by a suspension of a credit otherwise due 
to businesses that remit sales tax payments to the City, known as the “vendors fee credit”.  
The vendors fee credit was calculated at 2.5% of the amount of sale tax remitted to the City.  
In other words, for every $1,000,000 in retail sales by a business, the local sales tax due to 
the City would be $30,000, and the vendors fee credit would have been $750.  A substantial 
portion of the annual funding for the program is generated by the largest retailers, while the 
benefits generally accrue to the small business community.  The program is intended to 
benefit the entire community, however, traditionally downtown Golden has been a significant 
beneficiary. The original suspension of the vendors fee credit was for five years, however, 
City Council has extended the program a number of times. 
 
Originally, the program funding was limited to direct marketing activities and in the early years 
it was largely an advertising program, administered by a marketing consultant.  Program 
guidance was originally provided by EDComm, however guidance evolved to a stakeholder 
committee and in September 2025 a full-fled ged City board appointed by City Council. 
Starting in 2010, the ordinance establishing the program was amended to allow program 
funds to be directed to staffing costs as well as marketing efforts.  Over the years, as use of 
the internet and social media expanded greatly in the community marketing sphere, staff 
efforts increased.  Current staffing now includes a community marketing manager, a tourism 
coordinator, and a social media/multimedia coordinator who shares his time between Visit 
Golden and the City. 
 
Over the past year, Visit Golden has been intentionally shifting its approach from traditional 
destination marketing toward destination management, recognizing that success is about 
ensuring tourism benefits our community. Through strategic planning, responsible recreation 
initiatives, stronger local business partnerships, and a greater focus on resident quality of life, 
the team is working to encour age visitation that  is more intentional, balanced, and 
sustainable. Ultimately, their vision is to welcome visitors in ways that support Golden’s local 
economy while protecting the natural, cultural, and community assets that make Golden 
special.

B. 807 13th Street Grant Application for Emergency Repairs – Gardner Building Operations 
LLC, on behalf of the historic property at 807- 809 13th St., is requesting $15,884 (50% of 
project cost) for emergency sewer mainline replacement and infrastructure repairs. The total 
cost of the improvements is  $31,768. The building houses two long-standing downtown 
businesses, Abejas Modern Bistro (which has recently closed) and Faith Tattoo Gallery. The 
scope of work entailed emergency hand and concrete excavation beneath 807 13th St. to 
address a total blockage, saw-cutting and interior trenching inside 809 13th St. to connect lab 
sink tie-ins, replacing collapsed 80-year-old cast-iron piping with new 4-inch PVC out to the 
street right-of-way via pipe-bursting, installing dual cleanouts, pouring replacement concrete, 
and street asphalt patching. The work was completed by High 5 Plumbing, and the required 
two quotes were provided. 
 
Due to the critical health hazard and risk of prolonged business closures, the repairs had to 
be undertaken immediately. The full scope was completed on September 21, and final paid 
invoices have been submitted. While DDA grant guidelines typically require approval prior to 
commencing work, staff worked closely with the property owner during the emergency and 
believes the urgent circumstances warrant funding consideration. As noted, DDA Board 
Member Terry Weber is a tenant in the building (Faith Tattoo Gallery) and will recuse himself 
from the discussion and vote on this item. The DDA Grants Subcommittee can provide their 
input at the meeting. The grant application and all supporting documentation are attached. If 
approved, the grant will be issued on a reimbursement basis in accordance with standard 
City procedures. 
 
Board discussion and action is requested.

E. DDA 2027/ 2028 Budget Continued Discussion – As discussed annually, the DDA board 
is required to adopt a budget for the subsequent year in November, in order to be reflected in 
the City budget.  Since the City does a two year budget, the draft DDA budget is for 2027 and 
2028, however, the formal approval (and submittal to the State of Colorado) will only be for 
2027 appropriations.   Since the board saw an initial draft version in August and September, 
the October meeting board discussion may be only a brief update.   
 
For this update, there are only a few line items to point out: 
 
 The state legislature again adopted legislat ion essentially reducing the assessed 
valuation of real property, which will result in a sli ght decease in the DDA base 
property tax revenue (line 14) and a lower than previously anticipated property tax 
increment revenue (line 7).   2027 will be reassessment year, with to be determined 
effects on 2028. 
 
 Similarly, the TIF Agreement  Reimbursement Line items (58 thru 64) are slightly 
reduced from the initial draft,  and from 2026 actuals. 
 
 Traditionally the amount budgeted for DDA grants has been $130,000 annually and 
actual expenses have been less.   For 2026, the major change was the Public Safety 
Power Shutoff (PSPS) grant program.   That was anticipated to be a one time event.  
The balance of grant expenses for 2026 are projected to be about $150,000.  The 
draft budget document includes $200,000 for 2027 and 2028. 
 
 The major planned expenditure in 2027 and 2028 will again be the DDA contribution 
to Joint and City projects (line 49).  The final number for 2026 is still being finalized (in 
part based on the timing of the Ford Street electric line undergrounding contribution).  
In general, the board can reasonably assume up to a total of $2,250,000 over the next 
two year period and still maintain the amounts “committed” to the potential Welcome 
Center and Miners Alley Performing Arts Center capital projects, and base reserves.  
Since many projects take more than one year to plan and implement, the board may 
wish to put a higher amount in 2027 than 2028.  The determination of specific projects 
can start late this year, and continue into 2027.
