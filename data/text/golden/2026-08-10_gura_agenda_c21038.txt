2. Junction Plan Area – Next Steps 
In addition to the Conditions Survey, and if blight conditions are found in accordance with 
state statute, consultant Pioneer Development Company will be responsible for producing 
several critical companion documents that together form the complete statutory package 
required under Colorado Urban Renewal Law. Working concurrently with the Conditions 
Survey to expedite the overall timeline, Pioneer will develop an Urban Renewal Economic 
and Fiscal Impact Report, which will include a Tax Increment Financing (TIF) model 
forecasting future tax increment generated by the proposed development, a Fiscal Impact 
Analysis quantifying potential tax impacts on all affected taxing districts that collect a mill 
levy within the project area, and demographic and school district projections.  
Consistent with the approach used for GURA's recently completed 17455 West 16th 
Avenue Urban Renewal Plan (The Church of the Nazarene), the Impact Report will 
forecast development within the plan area over the life of the plan, up to 25 years, the 
maximum period permitted by Colorado's Urban Renewal Law for TIF collection, and will 
provide each taxing entity that levies property tax within the plan area with an incremental 
tax revenue estimate.  
Alongside the Impact Report, Pioneer will draft the Urban Renewal Plan document itself, 
which will include a description and map of the plan area boundary and will be prepared 
in close coordination with GURA's legal counsel to ensure full compliance  with all 
statutory and procedural requirements under C.R.S. 31- 25-107. The Plan will be 
supplemented by the Conditions Survey and Impact Report, and the consultant's principal 
will be available to present all three documents in person to GURA, to the City's Planning 
Commission, and ultimately to City Council for a vote on plan area formation.  
Pioneer will also produce a "But-For" Feasibility and Gap Funding Analysis, which will 
model the proposed development's costs, revenues, expenses, and return on  
investment, and will include a sensitivity analysis to determine the amount of public 
financing necessary to make the project financially viable, a critical tool for demonstrating 
to taxing partners and City Council that TIF support is both justified and necessary.  
These four deliverables; the Conditions Survey, Impact Report, Urban Renewal Plan 
document, and "But -For" Feasibility Analysis form the foundational documentation 
package that will position GURA to formally initiate the tax increment revenue sharing 
negotiations with impacted taxing entities, which for The Junction plan area are 
anticipated to include Jefferson County, the City of Golden, Jefferson County School 
District, and Mile High Flood District.  
Staff will continue to keep the Board informed as work on these next deliverables 
commences.

Executive Summary 
The Junction Conditions Survey: 
The Golden Urban Renewal Authority (GURA) is evaluating a specific area for potential Urban Renewal treatment. 
This area is tentatively named “The Junction Urban Renewal Plan” (the Plan). The Plan encompasses one 51.28 acre 
parcel of vacant land located along the west side of State Highway 93, directly across from the North Table Mountain 
Park West Trailhead. The parcel was recently annexed into the City of Golden and is bounded by Highway 93 to its 
east, an existing light industrial business park to its south, and vacant land, a single family residence, and an active 
clay mine to its west. GURA and the City elected to study this area due to its development potential, its unique 
physical constraints, and the significant public infrastructure investments required to serve the site. 
GURA and the City have long identified economic development and attainable housing as local priorities. The 
Junction presents a unique opportunity to advance both goals. Jefferson County Open Space sold the parcel to a 
private entity in December 2025 through a competitive RFQ process. Evergreen Development Company proposes to 
develop the site as a light industrial business park containing 13 buildings and approximately 499,000 square feet of 
commercial, office, and retail space. At build out, the development is projected to support a daytime population of 
approximately 1,500 people. The jobs created within this business park are expected to produce higher wages, 
helping local workers afford to live within Golden’s municipal limits. The City has also zoned a portion of the parcel 
for higher density multi family residential uses, although no apartments are proposed at this time. 
This report evaluates existing physical and environmental conditions that may be arresting sound development on 
the site. Field work and a literature review identified steep topography, a mining subsidence hazard covering 
approximately 25 acres, expansive soils with high swell potentials, a defective and unsafe street layout along State 
Highway 93, and a complete absence of public infrastructure as key barriers to development. The entire parcel 
remains vacant and generates no property tax revenue. These conditions collectively arrest sound development and 
support the inclusion of this parcel for Urban Renewal consideration. 
While the property faces physical and environmental challenges, its development potential aligns directly with 
Golden’s community goals. The Plan supports the City’s efforts to attract new businesses, create meaningful 
employment, and improve traffic safet y along State Highway 93. Planned public improvements include a new 
roundabout and pedestrian bridge that will serve both the subject parcel and the North Table Mountain Park West 
Trailhead. Establishing an Urban Renewal Plan for The Junction would help mi tigate existing deficiencies, fund 
required public infrastructure, and enable a project that advances community and public interests. 
Before an Urban Renewal Plan can be established, a specific geographic area within the community must be found 
to exhibit certain conditions. These conditions are known as “blighting factors” , and they must be documented 
before Urban Renewal Plan areas can be formed and urban renewal projects commence. This process is known as a 
Conditions Survey, a report that carefully analyzes a geographic area to determine the existence of conditions 
defined by Colorado’s Urban Renewal Law. In general, these are conditions that “substantially impair or arrest the 
sound growth of the municipality, retard the pr ovision of housing accommodations, or constitute an economic or 
social liability, and are a menace to the public health, safety, morals, or welfare” (C.R.S. 31-25-103.2). 
A Conditions Survey does not create a new Urban Renewal Plan or Urban Renewal Project. The Conditions Survey is 
the first step in that process, one that is required before the City of Golden can establish the The Junction Urban 
Renewal Plan. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the proposed Plan area and determine whether it meets the 
statutorily required threshold of blighted area factors. Four (4) of these factors must be identified before an Urban 
Renewal Plan can be formally established and adopted.

were conducted during a field visit on July 14, 2026 to identify the presence of statutorily defined blighting factors

planning, engineering, and geotechnical documents. Data from the Jefferson County Assessor, FEMA, EPA, and local 
market databases were also utilized to evaluate conditions not readily observable during field work. 
This report also reviewed City and County planning documents, engineering and geotechnical studies, GIS datasets, 
and County property records. This information was synthesized to determine which statutorily defined conditions 
are present within the study boundary. These conditions were geo -located and mapped to provide GURA and the 
City of Golden with a clear understanding of the property’s urban renewal eligibility. The Conditions Survey

within the proposed Urban Renewal survey boundary. This methodology included a detailed literature

Evaluation of Blighting Factors 
Defining Blighting Conditions 
Colorado Revised Statutes 31-25-103 states that for an Urban Renewal Plan area to be established, there 
must exist certain conditions known as “blighted area factors” (Blighting Factors). State statute defines 
eleven separate factors for blight. The law in dicates that if four or more of these factors are found in an 
area of the municipality, that area may be declared blighted and qualify for urban renewal treatment1. 
Blighted Area Factors Defined: 
 
a) Slum, deteriorated, or deteriorating structures 
b) Predominance of defective or inadequate street layout 
c) Faulty lot layout in relation to size, adequacy, accessibility, or usefulness 
d) Unsanitary or unsafe conditions 
e) Deterioration of site or other improvements 
f) Unusual topography or inadequate public improvements or utilities 
g) Defective or unusual conditions of title rendering the title nonmarketable; 
h) The existence of conditions that endanger life or property by fire or other causes 
i) Buildings that are unsafe or unhealthy for persons to live or work in because of building code 
violations, dilapidation, deterioration, defective design, physical construction, or faulty or 
inadequate facilities 
j) Environmental contamination of buildings or property 
k) The existence of health, safety, or welfare factors requiring high levels of municipal services or 
substantial physical underutilization or vacancy of sites, buildings, or other improvements   
 
These eleven factors define the “conditions” that are arresting the municipality’s sound development. Per 
statute, these factors give “reason” to declare an area “blighted”, provided that a specific number of 
factors are identified. The presence of at least four of these conditions satisfies the state statute defining 
an area blighted. 
This Conditions Survey will unpack each blighting factor to describe various real world “conditions” that 
indicate the presence of said factor. Only one factor need be identified for a municipality to declare the 
need to establish an urban renewal authorit y. An Urban Renewal Plan or Project requires at least four 
factors identified within its boundaries. It is the culmination of four or more blighting factors which 
indicate that renewal activities should be applied within the proposed project area. 
Blighting Factors were identified based on objective analysis and urban renewal best practices. The 
following sections outline the blighting factors found within the survey area. Blighting factors that were 
not identified are excluded from this report. 
 
1 In addition to this list, C.R.S. 31-25-103(2) lists a twelfth condition. This final condition only applies when there is unanimous

Factor (b) – Predominance of Defective or Inadequate 
Street Layout 
Description: 
This factor refers to surface transportation conditions throughout the survey area. Surface transportation 
conditions may include road surface quality, the location or existence of streets and sidewalks, multi -
modal improvements, traffic safety infrastruct ure, ingress/egress locations, and emergency access 
considerations. If surface transportation conditions exist that negatively impact sound development, 
redevelopment, or threaten safety, the survey includes this factor in its catalogue. Examples of this factor 
include: 
• Inadequate street widths 
• Lack of streets, dead ends 
• Overall faulty layouts that impede vehicular access and internal circulation 
• Inadequate Traffic Safety facilities 
• Streets that exhibit high degrees of traffic or accidents 
• Streets that are in need of repair or reconstruction 
• Poor emergency access or active transportation 
Findings 
Field observations along State Highway 93 show clear evidence of defective and inadequate street layout. The parcel 
is served by a single ingress and egress point, an unimproved dirt road that connects directly to Highway 93. This 
entrance has no intersect ion, no deceleration lane, and no acceleration lane. Vehicles entering or leaving the site 
must slow or merge within active highway travel lanes. This entrance is a traffic safety risk today, and it is not 
adequate to facilitate future development within the parcel. 
The parcel also contains no internal street network. All 51.28 acres consist of vacant land without a single improved 
street, sidewalk, or emergency access route. Development cannot occur without an entirely new internal road 
network, and emergency vehicle s currently have no means of reaching the interior of the site. These conditions 
impede vehicular access and internal circulation throughout the survey area. 
The street layout also creates safety problems for the North Table Mountain Park West Trailhead, located directly 
across Highway 93 from the parcel. Vehicles travelling southbound have difficulty turning into the recreation area. 
Vehicles leaving the recreation area cannot turn left to return to the City of Golden. During the July 14 th field visit, 
Pioneer Development Company representatives observed multiple drivers attempt this illegal left turn, a sign that 
the road network is unsafe and inadequate as currently constructed. 
Active transportation facilities are also missing. Highway 93 lacks adequate shoulders in this area, and cyclists and 
pedestrians arriving from the north have a difficult and often dangerous time crossing the highway into the North 
Table Mountain area. There are no sidewalks, multi use paths, or safe crossings serving the parcel or the trailhead. 
Overall, the single unimproved access point, the missing internal street network, the unsafe turning movements at 
the North Table Mountain trailhead, and the lack of active transportation facilities demonstrate a defective and 
inadequate street layout. These conditions meet the definition of Blight Factor (b).

Field work and a literature review identified multiple unsafe conditions throughout the survey area. The former Rocky

Factor (f) – Unusual Topography or Inadequate Public 
Improvements or Utilities 
Description: 
This factor refers to unusual topography or lack of public infrastructure that have the effect of arresting 
sound development in a study area. Areas that exhibit steep grades which cause development to be 
incompatible or unprofitable would fall under this factor. Properties that are lacking public infrastructure, 
or are served by deteriorating public infrastructure, would also fall under this factor. This factor shares 
aspects of factors two (2), four (4) and five (5). Conditions that justify the inclusion of this factor in an area 
may include: 
 
•  Steep slopes or unusual terrain 
• Overhead utilities in need of repair 
• Deteriorating parking lots, street surfaces, sidewalks  
• Poor storm water drainage facilities 
• Lack of central sewer or water 
• Lack of internal street network 
• Broken or inadequate street lighting 
 
Findings 
The parcel’s topography was the most obvious reason for inclusion of this factor. Steep slopes rise from Highway 93 
until they crest as a ridgeline immediately west of the parcel. These grades include steep drop offs from existing grade 
along the parcel’s frontage. Development of the site will require substantial grading, retaining walls, and stormwater 
infrastructure before any project is feasible. 
The parcel also lacks public infrastructure entirely. The site contains no internal streets, no water or sanitary sewer 
facilities, no stormwater detention, and no parks or landscaping improvements. Every category of public 
infrastructure must be constructed before development can occur. 
The cost of these improvements underscores this factor. Required public improvements include an internal street 
network, water and sewer facilities, stormwater detention facilities, grading and retaining walls, and parks, open 
space, and landscaping improvements. The total cost of these improvements exceeds $59 million in today’s dollars. 
Infrastructure costs of this magnitude arrest sound development, and without coordinated public and private 
investment the site is likely to remain vacant and unproductive. 
The combination of steep topography, the complete absence of public improvements, and the extraordinary cost to 
correct these deficiencies confirms the presence of unusual topography and inadequate public improvements or 
utilities within the survey area, satisfying Blight Factor (f).

• Buildings or property not in compliance with building codes 
• Areas that are in a floodplain or flood prone area

• Areas with buildings or land that violates environmental regulations (this can range from

Description: 
This factor references a broad category of health, safety and welfare factors. The common conditions for 
this factor to be present are instances where high levels of municipal service are required, substantial 
physical underutilization of property is exhib ited, or high levels of vacancy are common. Vacancy can 
include land, buildings, or tenancy. This factor shares similarities with many of the factors on this list. 
Conditions that would justify the inclusion of this factor in an area include: 
 
• Numerous vacant buildings or property throughout the area 
• Evidence of underutilized buildings 
• Underutilized or vacant sites  
• Broken or inadequate street lighting 
 
Findings 
Field work and County records underscore the property’s underutilization and vacancy. The entire 51.28 acre parcel 
is vacant. The site contains no buildings, no active uses, and no programmed function of any kind. It is one of the 
largest vacant parcels within the City of Golden’s municipal limits. 
The parcel is also substantially underutilized given its location and potential. The site sits adjacent to an established 
light industrial business park and enjoys extensive frontage along State Highway 93. Jefferson County Open space 
initiated an RFQ process to find eligible developers for the site, recognizing  its economic importance to the county 
and Golden relative to its recreational potential. The development of this parcel is therefore desired both by its new 
landowner, but also the greater community, otherwise JeffCo Open Space would not have sold the land. 
The site’s vacancy is directly tied to the blighting factors catalogued in this report. The mining subsidence hazard, 
expansive soils, extreme topography, defective street layout, and complete lack of public infrastructure have kept 
this parcel idle. Without addressing these conditions, the parcel is likely to remain vacant and unproductive. 
The combination of complete site vacancy, substantial physical underutilization, and the property’s inability to 
support development in its current condition satisfies the statutory definition of Factor (k.5).

Conclusion 
This Conditions Survey catalogs the presence of statutorily defined blighting factors within the proposed 
The Junction Urban Renewal Plan area. This report was designed to assist the Golden Urban Renewal 
Authority (GURA) in determining whether this area is: 
1. Eligible for urban renewal projects and activities, and 
2. That the proposed boundary is drawn as narrowly as possible. 
This Conditions Survey identified five (5) blighted area factors as defined by Colorado’s Urban Renewal 
Law within the survey boundary. The presence of these factors meets the requirements outlined in (C.R.S. 
31-25-104), in which at least four blighting factors must be present for that area to be declared “blighted” 
and therefore eligible to be designated as an Urban Renewal Plan area. 
The survey area satisfies the statutory recommendation of “being drawn as narrowly as possible.” The 
boundary encompasses a single 51.28 acre parcel under single ownership, where public infrastructure 
must be constructed before development can occur. The b lighting factors identified across the property 
collectively represent conditions that complicate sound development and investment. 
This report recommends that GURA use this survey as a foundation for continued coordination with the 
City and the development team. Jefferson County Open Space conveyed the property to a private entity 
through a competitive RFQ process, and Evergreen Development Company is advancing a light industrial 
business park with approximately 499,000 square feet of new commercial space. The proposed project 
would construct more than $59 million in public improvements, including a roundabout and pedestrian 
bridge serving both the parcel and the North Table Mountain Park West Trailhead. This project driven 
effort aligns with GURA’s partnership oriented approach to Urban Renewal and demonstrates the 
collaborative intent of the parties involved. 
This Conditions Survey finds that the proposed The Junction Urban Renewal Plan area can be declared a 
“blighted area” as defined by Colorado’s Urban Renewal Law and therefore qualifies for urban renewal 
treatment.
