Current business: 
Nathan opened the meeting with an update on the progress of the handbook update by City staff and Kendall. He shared that he plans to retain Kendall to develop a location charrette through meetings with the Arts & Culture Commission (ACC), City staff, and community members.
Steve Glueck then presented information on potential artwork for the Heart of Golden and Municipal Building. He reviewed the 1% budget for the Municipal Building and discussed potential artwork locations within the East Zone, including Tucker Gulch and the Farmers Highline Canal. He also provided an anticipated construction timeline for the East Zone, noting that ACC planning should begin in 2027.
Nathan suggested that if the Heart of Golden is considered as one large project, the Central Zone should also be included in the discussion, particularly given the desire to establish an arts and culture “zone.”
The group discussed several potential ideas, including a concentrated sculpture park in the Central Zone, concrete pads near the Farmers Highline Canal for rotating artwork, and a vertical sculpture within the detention pond.
The group also discussed what a potential public art loan program could entail, including transportation and installation costs, loan agreements and paperwork, and artwork footprint requirements. Members agreed to research how other cities structure and manage public art loan programs.
Steve agreed to harden three sites within the two areas identified by the landscape architect. These sites could accommodate rotating or permanent artwork, or could instead be used for tables and chairs if the ACC chooses not to place artwork there. The group also agreed to explore one potential permanent artwork for the retention pond in the future. The $15,000 allocated to the East Zone will remain reserved for future use.
Steve then reviewed the Municipal Building artwork budget and shared that the City is interested in commissioning a mural for the parking garage at 10th Street and Vasquez Street. He requested that the call for artists be drafted in September or October 2026, with installation beginning in April 2027.
He also shared that planning has begun for artwork in the public-facing areas of the Municipal Building. Steve has been in conversations with a consultant who purchases large bodies of artwork for hospitals and hotels. Rick Souders suggested that the ACC, Steve, and the consultant work together to identify local artists who could meet the City's needs. Nathan noted that reproductions may not qualify for public art funding.
The group agreed that any remaining 1% CIP funds from the Heart of Golden project should remain within the corridor for future use.
Staff agreed to earmark $38,000 for the mural project and reserve the remaining funds for either purchasing interior artwork or pooling the funds toward a larger project within the corridor.
Steve shared that a coffee shop has approached the DDA about funding a mural on its building and asked whether the ACC would be interested in participating. The group agreed that when City funds are used to support artwork on private property, the ACC should have some level of involvement in the process.
Nathan shared that he is still working with Kendall to edit the Handbook and has identified significant repetition within the document. The group agreed to remove the framework plan language and focus the handbook on policy rather than historical context. Staff will complete the handbook update internally, and Kendall’s scope will be revised to focus solely on the location charrette project.
