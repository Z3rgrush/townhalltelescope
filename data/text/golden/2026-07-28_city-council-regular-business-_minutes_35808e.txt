Mayor Weinberg acknowledged that a racist incident happened in the Golden Fire Department. 
The City just shared the information in a public statement. The Mayor read the public 
statement – “An Open Letter to the Community – Acknowledging a Racist Incident in the 
Golden Fire Department.”   
 
Councilor Kurzinski commented that she was deeply saddened and disturbed by the racist act 
by the Fire Department. She stated that racism has no place in Golden. She stated she will be an 
ally and continue to work on REDI steps with the City. She states that they are committed to 
being better and everyone’s voices matter.  
 
Councilor Vitry gave her sincere apologies to all who has been affected. She recognizes that the 
issue is larger than the incident itself. She wants the City to have real conversations concerning 
this topic that is 100% inclusive and she is available to talk.  
 
Councilor Evans stated that this was horrific and she is devastated that it happened in our 
community. She acknowledged the harm with the employees and community, especially the 
employees of color. She stated she is committed to rebuilding the trust with REDI. She quoted 
an employee, “meaningful accountability is not about punishment alone but acknowledging the 
seriousness of what occurred, demonstrating transparency, rebuilding trust and ensuring no 
employees ever has reason to question whether racism will be treated with the urgency and 
seriousness it deserves.” 
 
Councilor Knecht reiterated that she was horrified and deeply sorry about this incident. This is 
an opportunity to learn and do better and its critical. She wants us to use the outcome to these 
events to rebuild the trust that was lost.  
 
Mayor Weinberg commented that we have some folks here to talk during public comment. She 
stated that Council has received emails concerning this topic. She confirmed that councilors did 
receive emails but they are internal so not a part of public comment.  
 
Public Comment 
Mayor Weinberg opened the public comment period at 6:41 p.m. She acknowledged the public 
comment received via email.  
 
Beth Bidwell, 9th St, came to comment on Creek management. She stated that this topic has 
been going on since 2009, 17 years. She acknowledged the efforts the City has made over the 
years but stated that effective creek management has not been achieved. July 4th this year was 
particularly frustrating with parking issues and charcoal grills. She shared concerns over these

issues and the limited citations being written to the individuals at the creek. She stated that the 
City needs to assess and address these issues. 
 
JJ Trout, S. Golden, former Golden City Councilor and the Councilor member who brought forth 
the idea to start REDI. She stated that through REDI today they learned about the incident with 
the GFD. She shared that the incident was unacceptable, especially in a public safety building. 
She stated that this incident raises serious concerns about the workplace culture. She calls for 
more accountability, transparency and serious action moving forward.  
 
Dr. Holly Olivares, S. Golden, member of REDI task force spoke concerning the racist incident in 
the Golden Fire Department.  She shared that, according to the census, there are 220 black or 
African American residents in Golden. She stated that it was hard to hear that these are our 
values because she doesn’t feel that as a community member. She shared comments from 
other REDI members. She said that the City must act to protect employees and community 
members. She encouraged Councilors to come to REDI.  
 
Andrea Philleo, 12th St, stated that she is here to advocate for the Creek. She shared that the 
Creek is a disgusting garbage can. She stated that people need to care enough to do the right 
thing. Ideas are one thing, actions are another. She encouraged Council to stop allowing people 
to destroy the creek and asked Council to do more enforcement of the Creek.  
 
Julie Favre, 12th St, stated that Creek management needs to be increased. She stated that the 
Creek is completely unprotected. She feels like nothing will be done by City Council, she hasn’t 
seen anything changed since last year. She asked for drastic change, making rules and enforcing 
rules.  
 
There being no further comment, Mayor Weinberg closed the public comment period at 6:59 
p.m.  She acknowledged the emailed public comment provided in the public record.

She stated she is in complete agreement that we have not solved the problem. She thanked the 
individuals who provided comment concerning the racist incident with the Fire Department. 
She hoped to be part of the change moving forward.  
 
Mayor Pro Tem Vitry thanked Andrea, Beth and Julie for coming and sharing about the Creek.

Carly Lorentz, Deputy City Manager gave a Creek update and presentation.

Chief Michele Wagner provided information concerning creek enforcement and their actions. 
There is inconsistent in enforcement efforts and a breakdown in communication. She stated 
they work on encouraging compliance but it doesn’t seem to be working. She assured Council 
that they are working on it and that their efforts are going to be coming more in line with 
Councils expectations.  
 
Councilor Evans stated that she believes this Creek update is the worst and she is appalled at 
the behavior at the Creek. She believes that we are failing the Creek and our residents. She 
requested better signs with clear instructions. She stated that there is so many behavior issues 
on the creek banks. She stated that we need to expect better from our visitors and we have to 
do better. Its not sustainable. She also stated that she feels like visitors and residents should 
cool down at the creek but use the picnic area for food, drink and picnicking. She believes we 
should say no food or drink at the creek.  
 
Councilor Knecht stated that this is her first summer on the Creek committee. She viewed over 
a dozen offenses when she walked along the creek. She stated that is our obligation to protect 
the Creek bank which includes not climbing over fences and not putting chairs in the creek. She 
believes we remain on the right track, but there are gaps and we aren’t there yet.  
 
Councilor Cameron stated that we should close the creek banks more often and regularly to 
help ease the load. He suggested a test day or hours to manage closing it. He suggested setting 
up shade structures in Lions Park. He encouraged enforcement of the rules and issuing 
citations.  
 
Mayor Weinberg echoed Councilor Cameron’s comments on the gate closures to protect the 
creek banks and rehabilitation. She also encouraged additional signs on the bank side. She fully 
supports shade structures and additional picnic tables in Lions Park.  
 
Mayor Pro Tem Vitry wanted to pause on the no food and drink on the food banks. She 
believed there is a difference in the food and drink conversation between picnicking and 
individual food/drink. She would like more discussion. She asked Chief Wagner if there will be 
changes this weekend and Chief stated yes. She also asked about adding additional code 
enforcement officers and Carly stated they are looking at that with the budget process.  
 
Councilor Pfau asked how many Code Enforcement officers are walking the creek. Chief stated 
it varies but on Saturday and Sunday they have 3 seasonal from 10am-6pm and 2 full-time 
CSO’s. He stated he believes the next step is to enhance the Code Enforcement and to give 
them permissions to enforce the Code that exists. He encouraged staff to bring Code
