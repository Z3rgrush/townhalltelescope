Re: 2027 Recreation Service Fees Discussion 
 
Purpose of Agenda Item:  
Recreation services for rentals and general facility access are codified and set on an 
annual basis through market analysis and research, staff advisement and council 
approval. Council will review the full city fee schedule in early December, and staff are 
requesting feedback from the PRAB on proposed fee adjustments to inform the final 
recommendations presented to council.  
Recreation program fees are not codified fees and are not included in the December 
presentation to council. Program fees are reviewed on a seasonal basis through 
standard recreation service delivery frameworks which include ongoing market analysis, 
evaluation and adjustment.  
Background:  
The proposed fee schedule changes are organized by service areas, including athletic 
fields and courts, Clear Creek RV Park, Fossil Trace Golf Club, Golden Community 
Center, Park Pavilions, and Splash. Each section will include a fee table that shows, in 
track changes, the adjustments from previous fees and information of note regarding 
changes.  
In most cases, fee adjustments are minimal, and instead updates are intended to simplify the 
fee schedule, better align discounting models, and ensure a seamless transition into the new 
recreation management software (RMS).  
 
Additionally, with a transition to a new RMS, discounts, promotion, and operating practices 
are being reviewed.

Credit Card Processing Fees 
In 2026 staff intended to pass credit card service fees directly to users, as is the practice 
in most other city services. In the existing RMS, that was not possible and instead a 
modified version of cash discounting on select items was introduced at the GCC. This 
has created a challenging customer service experience and confusion. As example, an 
adult paying for a daily entry at the GCC at a rate of $10.25 might attempt to pay in exact 
change and end up getting 30 cents back. This becomes increasingly challenging as 
pennies are often involved and in short supply.  
 
In early 2027, as originally intended, fees for entry, services, and programs at the GCC, 
Clear Creek RV Park, Splash, and the athletic facilities will begin charging users paying 
with a credit card the transaction processing fees directly through the payment terminal. 
For those paying by credit card, this will result in a processing fee of up to 3%. Other 
accepted forms of payment will not incur this fee, and additional processing fees will not 
be charged directly to refunds or payment corrections. General cancelation policy service 
fees may still apply.  
Athletics Fee Schedule 
Prior to the improvements at Ulysses Sports Complex, athletic field rental rates were 
included on one table outlining just hourly, daily, and tournament rental rates by user 
type. With the addition of premier athletic fields, additional fee types have been 
introduced to represent the varied level of service provided at different facilities. Tables 
have also been separated for diamond ballfields and rectangular multiuse fields to create 
clarity for renters.   
Starting in 2024, Golden began scheduled fee increases over five years for what were 
considered Priority User Groups. To better reflect the groups receiving discounted 
pricing, staff have re-labeled the Priority User Groups to Community Youth Sports 
Organizations (CYSO). These groups’ rental usage has been historically heavily 
subsidized, with fee increases targeted to reduce overall subsidy provided over time. 
While subsidy has decreased, CYSOs remain highly subsidized, paying daily what a 
general user group may pay hourly for the same field space in 2027. 
New in 2027, staff are introducing outdoor sport court fees, including those for pickleball 
and tennis. Fees are listed by court type and location with pricing determined by market 
analysis and site-specific demand. Community members will be able to pre-book courts 
to guarantee play at a scheduled time through a permitting process. Free walk-up play 
will still be possible when courts are not already booked, and one court at each location 
will remain open for walk-up play only. A court reservation process helps community 
members preplan for the week ahead. Revenue generated will help to support the long-
term upkeep of the courts and surrounding park areas.

Golden Community Center 
Fee increases are not scheduled for review at the Golden Community Center (GCC) until 
2028. Included fee adjustments for 2027 are for the purpose of simplifying fees, further 
promoting membership options, and aligning discounting models across fee types.  
The GCC has historically offered both resident and non-resident rates on daily 
admissions. Verifying residency for a daily user is not practical or efficient, and providing 
subsidies or discounts on a daily entry does not align with prevailing best practice. Staff 
recommend no longer providing discounted entry for daily admissions and instead using 
the possible savings to encourage membership sales and allow for resident and age-
based discounts on memberships. 
10-visit cards are offered with a bulk discount model of buy nine facility entries, receive 
one free, with rounding. Proposed pricing for non-resident 10-visit cards will remain priced 
at this amount in 2027 with minor corrections to fees based on updated calculations. 
Resident 10-visit card fees are included with a 15% discount from the non-resident rate. 
This 15% discount will align with resident discount rates across most program and service 
areas. This fee change will slightly increase the existing fee for resident 10-visit cards, 
and the team is pursuing promotional memberships, including household membership 
rates to address potential access challenges.  
Currently, 10-visit cards do not expire. As the department transitions from an RMS that 
has been used for the last 30 years to a new system, this has resulted in unused 10-visit 
cards that are sometimes 30 years old and with accounts that do not include contact 
information. 10-visit cards purchased in the last three years will automatically be 
transferred into the new RMS and given a five-year expiration date from time of transfer. 
Staff will be communicating with any 10-visit card holders with purchases before 2024 to 
encourage them to use their existing visits, or with an option to roll visits into a different 
pass type before the transition. Beginning in January, newly sold 10-visit cards will include 
a two-year expiration.  
Monthly membership fees have not previously been included in the fee schedule, as in 
the existing RMS these passes are considered annual memberships paid monthly. 
Starting in 2027 with the new recreation software, SmartRec by Amilia, monthly recurring 
memberships will be offered to create additional flexibility for community members. The 
rates listed are the existing rates for an annual membership paid monthly. Annual 
memberships are priced at 11 monthly payments, so buying ahead gets community 
members the equivalent of one month free. There are no fee changes to monthly or 
annual memberships.  
New in 2027, the team is pursuing promotional membership offerings for households up 
to four and two-person households. These packages will replace some of the existing 
“buy one get one” discounted pricing and will be designed to support access for families, 
couples, and single parents. Pursuing household memberships was outlined as a goal in 
the 2025 Parks and Recreation Master Plan, and the team hopes to introduce these new 
promotional memberships in mid-January 2027.

Fiscal Impact:  
2027 fee adjustments are largely intended to simplify and align discounting practices 
across areas, when possible, without drastically increasing fees, as large scale fee 
increases were introduced in the last two years. As such, much of the included fee 
adjustments are meant to be revenue neutral or simply promote the sale of memberships 
instead of encouraging daily entries. Staff intend to prepare a comprehensive market 
analysis and fee assessment in 2027 to inform 2028 fees, when increases are 
scheduled.  
Increases to athletic fees that have been scheduled to occur in years 2024-2030 are 
intended to reduce the use of unintended subsidies by select user groups close to 10% 
each year. While expenses continue to increase, staff project the resulting subsidy 
savings will be close to $12,000 in 2027. Projected revenue from the proposed new court 
booking fees is $15,000 in 2027.  
 Page 14 
 
 
 
Passing along credit card service fees directly to the user will allow the department to 
avoid close to $100,000 in annual service fees that are otherwise not recovered through 
standard charges for services.  
Community Impact:  
Credit card processing fees being directly distributed to users is likely to most impact 
those reliant on debit to afford services. This may have a disproportionate impact on 
those who cannot afford to purchase services outright, or who don’t have the financial 
security to arrange for automatic bank transfer payments. Additionally, those who are 
accustomed to the convenience of paying with a credit card can expect a close to 3% 
increase on each transaction. These fees can easily be avoided by using other payment 
methods; however, that is not possible for everyone.  
Residents who visit the GCC infrequently are likely to be impacted by changes to daily 
admissions and 10-visit cards. While staff do not have an immediate solution to address 
this impact, the infrequency of use from these community members itself will minimize 
the burden, and those who are using the facility often enough may instead choose 
membership.  
Those taking advantage of the current “buy one get one” discounts can expect new or 
different promotional offerings in the future. To reduce the possible burden and 
encourage more entire household usage, staff will introduce household membership 
promotional packages.    
Existing tennis and pickleball court users will be impacted by the introduction of new 
court rental fees. With all courts being first-come-first-served, those with flexible 
schedules and close proximity to court locations most benefited. With some courts now 
allowing reservations, those who can afford to pay are more likely to benefit from 
increased access to courts. Staff are addressing this challenge by creating both rental 
and walk-up play at each location.  
Still, introducing fees at current market rates when no fees have previously existed is 
likely to result in negative community feedback. Potential options to help address this 
concern would be to instead introduce new tennis and pickleball court fees at a lower 
starting rate and increase them each year to match market rates after three to five years. 
Additionally, it will be important to ensure proactive communication with court users so 
they know why and when rental fees would be put into place and how to reserve courts. 
Given all tennis and pickleball courts are currently walk-up only, identifying these 
community members could be a challenge.
