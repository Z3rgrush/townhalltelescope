A. PC 26-28: Call-Up review of Form Zone Site Plans PC26- 03, PC26-04, and PC26- 05 at 
2122 Jackson Street. 
Onsager presented the item and summarized the staff report.  Onsager explained that this case will 
be presented to affirm/defending the Director’s approval vs approval for the application. 
 
Commissioner asked if the alley spur was the primary question from Council’s call up.  Onsager 
confirmed that Council did call the application up for Planning Commission (PC) to discuss the 
alley spur specifically and for PC to review the application in totality.  
 
Commissioner asked if the only form type where an alley spur is mentioned is in the  Compact 
Urban Row House form type. Onsager said that it is mentioned specifically in the Compact Urban 
Row House form type and in the enhanced compatibility for the combo of form types to allow for 
flexibility. Onsager added that the definition for an alley spur could be used as a broader definition 
of an alley. Commissioner asked  what form zone the Compact Urban Row House form type is  
allowed in. Onsager said Outer Edge and added that the property is zoned Transition.  He explained 
the difference between them  noting geography and intensity of the size of buildings  that are  
allowed in them. 
 
Commissioner and Onsager discussed the  form type, lot configuration, and what triggers a lot to 
be subdivided.   
 
Commissioner asked about the definition of an alley and if the development would include a tract.   
Onsager explained that a preliminary minor plat was reviewed and will be finalized after the Site 
Development Plan is approved.  He added that t here will be an access easement (alley spur) to 
allow all the properties to use it and a five-foot setback from the edge of the tract to the properties 
acting as a rear lot line and not a Right of Way.  
 
Commissioner asked if the adjacent property was developed, could the alley be shared for access.  
Onsager said that it is a private alley  not public, but it could be a private property negotiation for 
shared access.  
 
Commissioner asked about what triggered the call up. Onsager said that there was a question they 
received from Council regarding the definition  of an alley spur, which he said  met the broader 
definition of an alley, but they wanted Planning Commission to review the application.  He also 
said there was a question about the front porch on lot 1 regarding wrapped porches and that they 
also asked Planning Commission to review and determine what a porch is and what is a patio. 
 
Commissioner asked how many other  private alleys  are around the city and were they always 
considered an alley in the context of being used for form types.  Onsager said that this was the first 
alley developed under the form-based code which added in more alley access requirements  that 
weren’t necessarily there before. He added that there are many properties that have alley access 
but still do not use them for access, which is a pattern that the City is working on shifting. 
 
Fleming presented the second half of the item and staff report.

Commissioner asked where additional park ing, like visitor parking, would be found in the area.  
Fleming sited 22 nd street for additional parking for residence .  Commissioners and Fleming 
discussed parking along Jackson not being an option due to a project that eliminated parking along 
the road.  
 
Commissioner and Fleming discussed a design solution for the minor adjustment for the historic 
home setbacks.  Onsager added that staff thought it would be good to have the historic home closer 
to the front to highlight the historic structure and make it a prominent feature to the development.  
 
Onsager also added that cottages have a one parking space per unit requirement, which this site 
had additional parking spaces incorporated in the plan, and from a parking ration standpoint, the 
property was overparked. 
 
Commissioner asked if there was a site distance analysis for coming out of the alley spur.  Onsager 
said that Engineering reviewed it and since there is a five-foot separation between the building and 
alley spur .  He said that Engineering did not raise concern  during their review, but he would 
confirm with them regarding the matter. 
 
Chair Fraser OPENED the Public Comment at 6:22 p.m. 
 
Kim Turner, resident of the property directly west of the subject property, said that they were never 
given the opportunity to comment on the application.  She said the alley spur was narrow and that 
area does get a lot of traffic and trash has a hard time in the area.  Turner added that there was no 
green space for families or trees proposed in the application.  She also asked if the historic home 
cannot be moved, what happens when they can’t move it. 
  
Natalie Palmer, lives next store to the property, reiterated that the alley is very small  and she had 
concerns for the reduction of the plot lines.  She also stated that she was concerned about snow 
removal, trash, available parking, and lights from the new homes.  Palmer also asked if the four 
foot plot line could be looked at , as it seemed unfair to the community and she didn’t think the 
property was big enough to hold five units. 
 
Richard Gardner, Jeffco historical commission, recommends restoring master craftsman home that 
is existing on the property.  He also recommended restoring the original porch of historic home.
