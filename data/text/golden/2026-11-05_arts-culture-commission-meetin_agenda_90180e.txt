Approval of Agenda and Minutes 
The Commission approved the meeting agenda as presented.  
 
Current Business 
Call for Public Comment 
No one was present to provide public comment. Staff acknowledged receiving several written 
comments by email, which were included in the meeting packet. Commissioners did not provide 
response to comments.  
 
Mission and Vision Statement Discussion  
Kristie Brice provided an overview of the process used to develop the proposed mission and 
vision statements. The Strategic Planning Subcommittee identified the need for updated 
statements, solicited input from commissioners, and incorporated that feedback into the 
versions presented in the meeting packet. 
Commissioners discussed the proposed statements and offered feedback. Some expressed a 
desire for greater clarity around the word “culture,” while others noted that the term is defined in 
the Arts and Culture Master Plan. Staff acknowledged the importance of a clear definition, 
particularly as it relates to the Arts and Culture Grant Program. Sarah Vaine suggested that the 
ACC consider a study session with City Council to present the new Strategic Plan and 
accompanying mission and vision statements. 
Cindy Jaye raised a question about specifically including the word “inclusivity” in the mission 
statement. Saoirse Charis-Graves noted that the ACC has emphasized accessibility through its 
grant program and that inclusivity is a broad term that encompasses accessibility. Tammy 
Ahmed added that the ACC has an opportunity to present art in which all members of the 
community can see themselves represented. 
Laura Tanner and Pariss Hazelman reflected on their experience serving on the Strategic 
Planning Subcommittee. They explained that the subcommittee considered feedback from all 
commissioners and worked to consolidate several longer ideas into cohesive statements. 
Kristie explained that the subcommittee approached the mission as defining the ACC’s “what” 
and “why,” while the vision describes where the Commission hopes to be in five to ten years. 
A motion to approve the mission and vision statements as presented passed. 
Lions Park Artwork Jury Recommendation 
Kristie Brice provided background on the Lions Park public art selection process, including the 
number of submissions received and the composition of the jury. The jury narrowed the 
applicant pool to three finalists, each of whom received an honorarium to develop a more 
detailed proposal. Following its review, the jury recommended Alexander Bond’s Pollinators at 
Play. Commissioners discussed the recommendation and shared feedback on the selected 
proposal. 
Staff explained that the project will move quickly into the contracting phase. Once the contract is 
carried out, staff will connect the artist with the project design team. Sarah Vaine added that 
staff have been meeting with City departments to help clarify when and how to engage the ACC 
in projects involving public art. 
A motion to approve the Lions Park jury recommendation of Pollinators at Play by Alexander 
Bond, passed. 
Location Charrette Open House 
Nathan Richie provided details about the Public Art Open House scheduled for October 28, 
including a meet-and-greet with ACC commissioners, dedication of the two 250/150 artworks, 
and remarks by Governor Polis. The event will also include a public artwork location activity 
through which community members can provide input on potential locations for future public art. 
Nathan explained that he also plans to speak directly with City departments that regularly 
interact with the ACC, including Parks and Planning, to gather additional location input. Staff 
noted that this process will help the Commission establish priorities for future public art locations 
and identify opportunities to pool available funding when appropriate. 
Nathan emphasized that the resulting location document will remain flexible and evolve over 
time. Exceptions may be necessary as unique opportunities arise, including special projects 
such as the 250/150 artworks and privately donations. 
The discussion then shifted to murals. Staff explained that upcoming revisions to the Art in 
Public Places (AiPP) Handbook will include updates to the mural policy and provide greater 
clarity about what qualifies as a public art mural. Most notably, AiPP funds will no longer support 
murals installed on privately owned property. 
Kristie noted that although the program has funded murals on private property in the past, the 
change reflects the continued development and maturation of the AiPP program. Staff clarified 
that privately funded murals located on private property do not fall under the ACC’s purview. 
Arts and Community Impact Brainstorm 
Nathan Richie opened a discussion about the ACC’s goals for measuring the impact of arts and 
culture in Golden. He asked commissioners to consider the types of impact they would like to 
better understand and measure, including economic, health, well-being, educational, and social 
impacts. 
Sarah Vaine shared that the Mayor had expressed interest in a State of the Arts event where 
the ACC could present data about arts and culture in Golden to City Council and the broader 
community. Sarah provided examples of how such data could be used but emphasized the 
need to first determine the story the ACC wants to tell. Commissioners expressed mixed views 
about creating a new event, with some noting the opportunity to engage the broader community 
and others raising concerns about the administrative effort required. 
The Commission discussed several potential areas of focus, including economic, health, well-
being, and community impact. Commissioners also considered methods for gathering 
information, including surveys and grant reporting, and discussed the value of incorporating 
both quantitative and qualitative data. 
Heather Mullins noted that arts and culture activity has historically been easier to quantify in 
downtown Golden but emphasized the importance of considering impacts across the entire city. 
Saoirse Charis-Graves suggested researching approaches used by other municipalities. 
Tammy Ahmed and Len Matheo discussed the role of advocacy and community building and 
suggested that these impacts should also be reflected in the data. Commissioners cited 
examples of private businesses hosting arts events whose community impact extends beyond 
attendance numbers. 
Cindy Jaye suggested using the newly adopted mission and vision statements to guide 
decisions about which data the ACC should prioritize. 
Tammy Ahmed recommended first taking inventory of information the City already has or is 
connected to, including the Golden Makers feasibility study and the Leeds School data. Heather 
Mullins invited ACC members to participate in the feasibility study and noted that Economic 
Development and Arts and Culture will likely collaborate on the project. 
Sarah Vaine explained that identifying specific impact goals could also help guide future funding 
decisions, allowing the Commission to direct grant resources toward activities that advance 
those goals. 
Tammy Ahmed asked about the possibility of developing a 501(c)(3) organization to work 
alongside the ACC, similar to models used by other municipal arts programs. Staff 
acknowledged that establishing a separate nonprofit would require significant resources. They 
also recognized the substantial amount of work the ACC has accomplished since its formation. 
Nathan Richie noted that opportunities such as this could be considered in the future, while 
current priorities remain focused on foundational work such as policy development and program 
prioritization. 
Saoirse Charis-Graves expressed particular interest in exploring health and well-being as an 
area of impact. 
Nathan Richie asked commissioners what they would like to see as the next step in the 
discussion. Kristie Brice asked commissioners to consider what they would want to 
communicate at a future State of the Arts event, noting that the answer could help determine 
which data should be prioritized. Sarah Vanie offered examples of potential content, including 
economic development information, findings from the feasibility study, and data highlighting 
high-impact arts and culture events, projects, and programs. Tammy Ahmed suggested holding 
a roundtable when the time is right.  
Staff Report 
Sarah Vaine reported that staff received 15 applications for the Arts and Culture Grant Program. 
Commissioners had no additional questions. 
Adjournment 
The meeting adjourned at 7:30 p.m.

OVERVIEW 
In 2025, the arts scene in the City of Golden grew, with a variety of cultural institutions contributing to 
the economy. Some key overall statistics include: 
 
• 402 creative jobs in Golden 
• A location quotient of 0.8 for creative jobs in Golden (i.e., 20% lower concentration of creative 
jobs when compared to the U.S.) 
• $17.6 million in total earnings in creative industries in Golden 
• $14.1 million in Golden arts nonprofit revenue in 2024, across nine nonprofit organizations 
The statistics represent a breadth of creative organizations in Golden, which create employment and 
earnings opportunities for Golden residents.

Employment 
Golden featured employment across many creative categories in 2025. Creative employment in Golden 
totaled 402, which translated to a location quotient of 0.8—indicating a concentration of creative 
industries employment 0.8 times that of the nation, or roughly 20% below the national level. Location 
quotients are calculated by comparing the ratio of a specific category of employment in Golden to the 
same category of employment in the United States. Therefore, the United States has a location quotient 
of 1.0. If a specific category in Golden has a location quotient lower than 1.0, it indicates a lower 
concentration compared to the United States. 
 
Across the six categories assessed in this study indicating creative jobs, four of them had a lower 
concentration than 1. The highest was Writing, Publishing, and Journalism, at a location quotient of 2.1. 
This was followed by Radio, Music, and Media Networks (1.9). The lowest location quotient was 0.1 for 
Other. The largest category of creative employment was Writing, Publishing, and Journalism, with 195 
employees.

NONPROFIT IMPACT 
In 2024, eleven nonprofit organizations categorized as arts in Golden generated $24.5 million in 
revenue. The largest category generating revenue was Environment, which generated $10.4 million in 
revenue in 2024. This was followed by History Museums, at $7.5 million, Arts, Culture, and Humanities, 
at $2 million, and Multipurpose Arts and Cultural Organizations, at $1.7 million.

METHODOLOGY 
Nonprofits 
The nonprofit data comes from IRS filings of Form 990, which is the tax form nonprofits are required to 
file with the IRS. The dataset is publicly available on the IRS website. Data was sorted for nonprofit 
organizations located in Golden with an arts category tag. 
 
Employment and Earnings 
The Bureau of Labor Statistics’ QCEW survey was utilized for employment and earnings data. The BLS 
groups employment by various NAICS code, which break down employment by industry. The NAICS 
codes used for this study were based on a previous study completed by the Creative Vitality Suite, with 
some minor adjustments. The categories that each of the NAICS codes fall into were selected at the 
discretion of the Business Research Division and are featured in the table below.  
 
Limitations 
While this report presents an aggregation of public data about employment, proprietors, and nonprofits 
serving creative industries. This data has limitations. For instance, gig workers and contract workers may 
be left out of economic statistics, depending on how they are hired and paid. Some sole proprietors are 
counted in their county of residence, and not where work is performed. Volunteer data is also absent in 
public economic data. Other data, such as nonprofit filings, lag in their reporting. Thus, this analysis is 
best understood as a sample based on available data. It can be best used externally and internally as a 
tool that helps create a baseline employment value and to crosscheck with known anecdotal 
employment.
