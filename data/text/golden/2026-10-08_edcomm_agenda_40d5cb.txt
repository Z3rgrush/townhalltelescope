Economic Indicator Dashboard: Platform Evaluation & Draft Indicator Review
Following direction from the Commission at the August and September meetings, staff 
completed a hands-on comparative trial evaluating two leading economic indicator and 
visualization platforms: mySidewalk and Lightcast.  
As
 outlined in previous discussions, tracking and communicating Golden’s economic 
performance poses unique administrative challenges due to municipal boundary constraints. 
Golden is divided across two primary ZIP codes and multiple census tracts without an 
independent Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA) designation. Standard public releases often 
rely on lagged multi-year American Community Survey (ACS) snapshots, which suffer from data 
suppression at smaller scales. Meanwhile, regional neighbors tracked by the Bureau of Labor 
Statistics (BLS) Mountain-Plains Regional Information Office benefit from dedicated MSA-level 
reporting. The objective of this evaluation was to select a centralized tool capable of delivering 
consistent indicator tracking, clear visual storytelling, and long-term administrative 
maintainability.  
P
latform Comparative Findings 
• Public Datasets & Baseline Indicator Parity: Both mySidewalk and Lightcast
demonstrate parity regarding standard public socioeconomic data. Each platform
integrates federal and state data repositories, including the U.S. Census Bureau
(Decennial Census and ACS), BLS (Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, Local
Area Unemployment Statistics), and Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA). For standard
demographic, income, and community-level tracking, both platforms pull from
essentially identical baseline sources.
pkt pg. 2
• La
bor Market Intelligence & Employer Demand: Lightcast is one of the industry leaders
in specialized labor market analytics. The platform excels at scraping real-time job
boards, deduplicating employer postings, and tracking specialized skill clusters. This
capability serves as an early indicator of regional employer hiring demand and
workforce shifts.
• A
ccessibility, Usability, and Operational Longevity: While Lightcast provides robust
labor analytics, mySidewalk provides a more modular dashboarding environment
tailored for municipal communication. Due to the intuitive and modular interface of
mySidewalk, it has the potential to allow city staff to build, modify, and publish
interactive visualizations without specialized econometric training or ongoing IT
support. This has the potential to ensure institutional longevity, as the platform can be
seamlessly maintained and updated across future staff transitions without losing
continuity.
S
taff recommends the selection and adoption of mySidewalk as the City of Golden’s platform 
for building and maintaining the municipal economic dashboard. While Lightcast offers granular 
job-posting scrapes, mySidewalk provides identical coverage across standard public data, offers 
a more modular narrative dashboarding capabilities, ensures long-term operational longevity 
for staff, and matches the tools used by our regional peers.

within the potential dashboard. The draft framework focuses on core municipal pillars:

ID Indicator Description
1.1 Total number of residents living within municipal boundaries to establish baseline population scale and multi-year growth trends.
1.2 Breaks down the population by age cohorts and midpoint age to distinguish college-age students from working-age adults and retirees.
1.3 Tracks the racial and ethnic composition of the resident population to monitor demographic shifts and diversity over time.
1.4 Percentage of adults aged 25 and older holding a bachelor's degree or higher to benchmark local human capital concentration.
1.5 Midpoint household income and distribution across income tiers to assess local household earning power and economic stratification.
2.1 Quantifies the total physical employment base located within Golden, regardless of where the workforce resides.
2.2 Measures the share of working-age residents active in the workforce alongside the proportion actively seeking work.
2.3 Net daily commuting flows by comparing residents who work locally against external workers commuting in and residents commuting out.
2.4 Resident workers by their primary standard occupational classifications to identify the skill and professional profile of Golden's workforce.
2.5 Tracks job counts by commercial sector to identify primary employment clusters, specialization, and private-sector concentration.
2.6 Tallies the number of operating commercial entities to evaluate small business density versus enterprise employers.
2.7 Evaluates local entrepreneurial activity and technical innovation through registered patent filings.
3.1 Counts occupied residential units and average occupants per unit to determine household formation rates and housing stock utilization.
3.2 Estimated midpoint market value of owner-occupied housing to monitor home equity growth and purchase barriers.
3.3 Prevailing monthly contract rent levels to assess affordability pressures across the local tenant market.
3.4 Percentage of homeowners and renters who allocate more than 30% of gross household income toward monthly housing costs.
3.5 Proportional split between owner-occupied and renter-occupied residential units across the city.
4.1 Municipal tax revenues across retail and industry segments to assess consumer spending patterns and fiscal health.
4.2 Tax receipts to gauge hotel occupancy, seasonal tourism performance, and business travel volume.
4.3 Unoccupied leasable square footage across commercial property types to evaluate market demand and real estate absorption.
4.4 Residential and commercial permits to track physical development activity and capital investment.

COUNCIL MEMORANDUM 
To: The Honorable Mayor and City Council  
From: Joe Allaire, Director of Finance  
Date: September 11, 2026 
Subject: June 2026 Sales, Use, Lodging, and Marijuana Tax Report 
The attached reports present sales and use tax, lodging tax, and marijuana tax revenues 
through June 2026. 
Tax revenue results through the first half of 2026 include several encouraging 
developments. Strong June sales tax collections brought year-to-date revenue $79,504 
above the City’s seasonally adjusted budget. Use tax collections also improved compared 
with June 2025, narrowing the year-to-date decline discussed in the previous report. 
Marijuana tax revenue continues to exceed both budget projections and prior -year 
collections. Lodging tax remains below budget and 2025 levels, although June collections 
increased from May as the summer season began. The Finance Department will continue 
monitoring these trends as the City develops its 2027–2028 biennial budget. 
Sales Tax 
Sales tax revenue in June totaled $2,714,329, an increase of $89,157, or 3.40%, compared 
with June 2025. Year-to-date collections reached $12,192,116, exceeding the same period 
in 2025 by $353,162, or 2.98%. June’s performance moved collections from $89,688 below 
the budget trend line through May to $79,504 above budget through June, providing a 
positive result at the midpoint of the year. 
Out-of-city sales continue to support overall growth, increasing 7.91% compared with the 
first six months of 2025. Several local reporting areas also posted gains, including South 
Golden, Colfax, North Golden, and Downtown Golden.  
Use Tax 
Use tax revenue in June totaled $460,270, an increase of $84,656, or 22.54%, compared 
with June 2025. This stronger monthly result narrowed the year -to-date decline from 
22.97% through May to 17.15% through June. Collections for the first half of the year totaled 
$2,435,307, or $504,250 below the same period in 2025. Although year-to-date revenue 
continues to trail the prior year, June’s improvement is encouraging. 
The City’s audit and compliance program continues to contribute to revenue recovery. 
Through June, the report identifies $466,224 in use tax audit revenue and $16,167 in

⚫ Page 2 
refunds, resulting in a net amount of $450,057. These efforts support fair and consistent 
tax administration and help ensure that revenue due to the City is properly collected. 
Lodging Tax 
Lodging tax revenue totaled $328,108 in June, an increase from May’s $269,078 as the 
City entered the summer collection period. June collections were $13,593, or 4.0%, below 
June 2025, but slightly above June 2024. Through June, lodging tax revenue totaled  
$1,208,854, which is $69,374 below the seasonally adjusted budget and $71,012, or 5.5%, 
below the same period in 2025. 
The summer months remain an important part of the City’s annual lodging tax performance. 
The Finance Department will continue reviewing collections as additional summer results 
become available and will use those results to refine revenue expectations for the 
remainder of 2026 and the upcoming biennial budget. 
Marijuana Tax 
Marijuana tax revenue continues to be a bright spot, with June collections of $63,082 
representing the highest monthly total within the period displayed in the report. June 
revenue increased 12.4% compared with June 2025. Year -to-date collections reached 
$343,607, exceeding the seasonally adjusted budget by $101,804 and the same period in 
2025 by $47,539, or 16.1%. 
This continued growth provides a positive contribution to the City’s revenue picture. The 
Finance Department will monitor performance during the second half of the year to evaluate 
the sustainability of these gains and develop a reliable revenue baseline for future budgets. 
Final Thoughts 
Overall, tax revenue results through June provide several reasons for encouragement. 
Sales tax collections are now slightly above the seasonally adjusted budget and ahead of 
the prior year, June use tax collections improved, and marijuana tax continues to perform 
strongly. These results provide a constructive foundation for the City’s financial planning at 
the midpoint of 2026. 
The Finance Department will continue monitoring lodging tax, year -to-date use tax 
collections, and differences in sales tax performance across the City. As work progresses 
on the 2027–2028 biennial budget, these results will help refine revenue forecasts and align 
spending plans with available resources while supporting the services and priorities of the 
Golden community.
