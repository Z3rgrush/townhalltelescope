MEMORANDUM 
To: Arts & Culture Commission Members 
From: Nathan Richie, Arts, Culture & Museums Manager 
Date: October 5, 2026 
Subject: Measuring the Impact of Arts, Culture & Museums in Golden 
 
PURPOSE 
As the City of Golden continues to develop its new Arts, Culture & Museums Department, staff are 
exploring ways to better understand and communicate the value that arts, culture, history, science, 
nature, and museums bring to our community. 
One potential department initiative is the development of a comprehensive approach to measuring and 
communicating the impact of arts, culture and museums on Golden's economy, community well-being, 
quality of life, and civic identity. 
This memo introduces a proposed framework for discussion with the Arts & Culture Commission and 
outlines potential steps the City could take to establish a baseline, develop consistent measures, and 
eventually report on the impact of cultural activities in Golden. 
 
BACKGROUND 
Arts and culture contribute to communities in many ways. Cultural organizations and events can 
generate economic activity, attract visitors, support local businesses, create opportunities for 
employment and entrepreneurship, and contribute to a community's identity and sense of place. 
At the same time, participation in arts and cultural activities can provide opportunities for learning, 
creativity, social connection, community engagement, and personal well-being. 
Golden has a particularly rich cultural ecosystem that includes: 
• Golden History Museum & Park 
• Public art 
• Arts organizations and creative businesses 
• Historic resources and heritage programs 
• Cultural events and festivals 
• Arts education 
• Community organizations and nonproﬁts 
• Local artists and makers 
• Cultural and educational institutions 
• Natural and cultural landscapes 
• Community partners and volunteers 

The creation of the Arts, Culture & Museums Department provides an opportunity to better understand 
these interconnected resources and demonstrate how they contribute to the broader goals of the City 
and the community. 
 
PROPOSED INITIATIVE 
Staff propose developing a Golden Arts, Culture & Museums Impact Framework that establishes a 
consistent approach for measuring the department's impact over time. 
The framework would focus on ﬁve interconnected areas: 
1. Cultural Participation 
Understanding who participates in Golden's cultural opportunities, how often they participate, and 
whether opportunities are accessible to a broad range of community members. 
2. Economic Vitality 
Understanding how arts, culture and museums contribute to local economic activity, including visitor 
spending, employment, business activity, tourism, and public and private investment. 
3. Community Well-Being 
Understanding how cultural experiences contribute to individual well-being, learning, creativity, quality of 
life, and personal enrichment. 
4. Connection & Civic Life 
Understanding how arts and culture create opportunities for social connection, belonging, community 
engagement, volunteerism, and civic participation. 
5. Identity & Place 
Understanding how arts, culture, history, science, nature, and museums contribute to Golden's 
distinctive identity, sense of place, and community pride. 
 
PROPOSED STRATEGIES 
1. Establish a Cultural Impact Framework 
Develop a shared framework for measuring the contributions of Arts, Culture & Museums across the ﬁve 
areas identiﬁed above. 
The framework would establish common deﬁnitions, identify appropriate measures, and provide a 
structure for collecting and reporting information over time. 
The intent would be to create a system that is useful, sustainable, and credible, rather than attempting to 
measure every possible outcome. 
2. Establish a Golden Cultural Baseline 
The ﬁrst step would be to understand what is already happening in Golden and what information is 
currently available. 
Staff would inventory: 
• Cultural organizations and partners 
• Cultural programs and events 
• Museum participation 
• Public art 
• Arts education 
• Cultural employment 
• Cultural expenditures 
• Attendance and participation 
• Volunteer engagement 
• Visitor participation 
• City investment 
• Community and philanthropic investment 
This baseline would provide a starting point for measuring change over time. 
 
3. Develop Consistent Methods for Measuring Participation and Impact 
Staff would develop simple, standardized tools that can be incorporated into existing programs and 
events. 
Potential measures could include: 
• Attendance and participation 
• Visitor origin 
• Repeat participation 
• Participant demographics 
• Visitor spending 
• Learning and personal enrichment 
• Community connection 
• Social connection 
• Well-being 
• Sense of belonging 
• Connection to Golden 
• Satisfaction and future participation 
Whenever possible, the department would incorporate these questions into existing registration, 
ticketing, event, and program processes rather than creating additional administrative requirements. 
 
4. Measure Economic Impact 
The department could work with City departments and community partners to better understand the 
relationship between cultural activity and Golden's local economy. 
Potential partners could include: 
• Economic Development 
• Finance 
• Visit Golden 
• Downtown Golden 
• Golden Chamber of Commerce 
• Local businesses 
• Cultural organizations 
• Tourism partners 
Potential measures could include: 
• Cultural organization expenditures 
• Cultural employment 
• Visitor spending 
• Restaurant and retail spending 
• Lodging associated with cultural visitation 
• Visitor origin 
• Length of stay 
• Cultural tourism 
• Business activity associated with cultural events 
• Public and private investment in culture 
Where appropriate, Golden could utilize established national methodologies so that results can 
eventually be compared with other communities. 
 
5. Explore the Relationship Between Culture and Well-Being 
A second area of focus would be understanding how participation in arts and cultural experiences relates 
to community well-being. 
Rather than attempting to immediately establish causal health outcomes, the City could initially focus on 
participant-reported outcomes such as: 
• Positive mood and well-being 
• Learning 
• Creativity 
• Social connection 
• Sense of belonging 
• Community pride 
• Connection to place 
• Intergenerational connection 
• Personal enrichment 
• Quality of life 
Over time, the City could partner with qualiﬁed researchers or academic institutions to conduct more 
rigorous studies of speciﬁc programs or populations. 
 
6. Conduct Targeted Impact Studies 
Once a basic measurement system is established, staff could select two or three programs, events, 
facilities, or initiatives each year for deeper evaluation. 
Potential areas of study could include: 
• Museum programs and family connection 
• Public art and perceptions of place 
• Cultural events and local business activity 
• Arts participation and social connection 
• Museum experiences and connection to local history 
• Cultural programming and youth development 
• Cultural participation among older adults 
• Cultural experiences and visitor behavior 
These focused studies would help the City better understand not just how much participation is 
occurring, but how and why particular cultural experiences create value. 
 
7. Build Research and Community Partnerships 
The City could explore partnerships with organizations that have research, data, economic development, 
public health, or evaluation expertise. 
Potential partners could include: 
• Colorado School of Mines 
• Other higher education institutions 
• Local and regional cultural organizations 
• Public health organizations 
• Economic development partners 
• Tourism organizations 
• Community organizations 
These partnerships could provide opportunities for student research, internships, survey development, 
data analysis, GIS analysis, and longer-term research projects. 
 
8. Develop an Annual Arts, Culture & Museums Impact Report 
As the program matures, the department could produce an annual Arts, Culture & Museums Impact 
Report. 
The report could combine data, trends, stories, and community perspectives to communicate the 
department's impact to: 
• City Council 
• Arts & Culture Commission 
• City leadership 
• Community partners 
• Residents 
• Businesses 
• Visitors 
• Funders and donors 
The goal would be to create a clear and accessible picture of how cultural activity contributes to Golden. 
 
9. Use Data to Inform Future Decisions 
Ultimately, impact measurement should be more than a reporting exercise. 
The information collected could help inform: 
• Department strategic planning 
• Annual work plans 
• Budget development 
• Program evaluation 
• Capital planning 
• Grant applications 
• Partnership development 
• Community engagement 
• Future investment priorities 
The goal is to use evidence and community input to understand where Arts, Culture & Museums can 
create the greatest community value. 
 
PROPOSED THREE-YEAR APPROACH 
YEAR 1 — Establish the Foundation 
The ﬁrst year would focus on creating the system and establishing a baseline. 
Potential actions include: 
• Deﬁne the impact framework. 
• Inventory existing data. 
• Identify data gaps. 
• Establish a manageable set of core indicators. 
• Develop standardized participant and visitor surveys. 
• Begin collecting baseline participation and economic data. 
• Begin measuring well-being and community connection. 
• Identify potential research and community partners. 
• Produce a baseline report. 
YEAR 2 — Measure and Analyze 
The second year would focus on developing a deeper understanding of impact. 
Potential actions include: 
• Continue consistent data collection. 
• Conduct two to three targeted impact studies. 
• Expand economic impact measurement. 
• Analyze visitor and participation trends. 
• Examine relationships between cultural participation and well-being. 
• Identify participation gaps and underserved audiences. 
• Develop an internal impact dashboard. 
YEAR 3 — Demonstrate and Apply 
The third year would focus on making impact measurement part of ongoing organizational decision-
making. 
Potential actions include: 
• Publish an annual public impact report. 
• Establish multi-year trends. 
• Expand benchmarking with peer communities. 
• Incorporate ﬁndings into budgeting and strategic planning. 
• Develop a public-facing impact dashboard. 
• Share ﬁndings with City Council, the Arts & Culture Commission, and community partners. 
• Use results to identify future priorities and investments. 
 
GUIDING PRINCIPLES 
As this initiative develops, staff recommend several principles to guide the work: 
Measure What Matters 
Focus on indicators that connect directly to community priorities and the department's strategic goals. 
Keep It Sustainable 
Use existing data whenever possible and avoid creating a measurement system that is overly 
burdensome to administer. 
Measure Trends, Not Just Numbers 
The greatest value will come from understanding changes over time. 
Combine Data and Stories 
Quantitative information can demonstrate scale, while community voices and stories help explain 
meaning and impact. 
Avoid Overclaiming 
Especially in the area of health and well-being, the City should distinguish between participation, 
correlation, and causation. 
Use Data for Decisions 
The purpose of measurement should ultimately be to improve programs, inform investment, and better 
serve the community. 
 
ROLE OF THE ARTS & CULTURE COMMISSION 
The Arts & Culture Commission can play an important role in helping shape this initiative. 
Staff would welcome Commission discussion and feedback regarding: 
1. What outcomes should Golden be measuring? 
2. Which aspects of arts and culture's impact are most important to the community? 
3. What information would be most useful to the Commission and City Council when considering 
future cultural investments? 
4. Are there community partners or research opportunities the City should explore? 
5. What would meaningful success look like ﬁve years from now? 
The Commission's perspective can help ensure that the measurement framework reﬂects not only what 
can be easily counted, but also what is most meaningful to the Golden community. 
 
NEXT STEPS 
Staff propose beginning with a relatively small and manageable ﬁrst phase: 
1. Review and reﬁne the proposed ﬁve-part impact framework. 
2. Identify existing City and community data sources. 
3. Develop a core set of approximately 20–25 indicators. 
4. Identify approximately 10 priority "Tier 1" indicators for initial implementation. 
5. Develop standardized participant and visitor survey questions. 
6. Explore potential research partnerships. 
7. Establish a baseline during the ﬁrst year. 
8. Return to the Arts & Culture Commission with initial ﬁndings and recommendations. 
Through this process, the City can begin building a long-term evidence base demonstrating how arts, 
culture, history, science, nature, and museums contribute to Golden's economic vitality, community 
well-being, quality of life, and civic identity. 
The ultimate goal is not to reduce the value of culture to a single number. Rather, it is to develop credible 
evidence that helps Golden understand, communicate, and strengthen the many ways that arts, culture 
and museums contribute to a vibrant community.

An Interactive Art Installation Proposal for Lions Park in Golden, Colorado

Narrative: As a native and lifelong resident of Colorado’s front range, a graduate of CSU’s College of 
Natural Resources, an active father to young playful children, and a professional artist working 
full-time in the public realm, I am proud to introduce ‘Pollinators at Play’ for Golden’s Lions Park. 
‘Pollinators
 at Play’ aims to connect people, and especially children, to the curiosities of the 
natural world through the installation of an abstract, colorful, whimsical, and interactive flower 
bouquet. The bouquet will include three spherical pods that will allow children (and youthful adults) to 
climb on and enter each pod, placing people in the lens of the pollinator with an up-close and 
personal experience of one of nature’s most iconic partnerships- pollinators and flowers. Not only will 
Lions Park visitors benefit from the installation from an aesthetic perspective, but the installation will 
serve to educate a diverse array of people on the parts of a flower and the role of a pollinator in 
nature. Furthermore, the pod-shaped forms of the installation will create a special, quiet, and 
grounding pocket of Lions Park for children to explore outside of the spotlight of the park’s more 
intense attractions (swings, playground, slide, etc.). Furthermore, the smooth, glossy, and colorful 
powdercoat will allow for a rich tactile experience, similar to the touch of a smooth flower petal itself. 
Finally, and most notably, park users will be able to physically spin and rotate certain portions of the 
flower anatomy (the anther and stigma). The kinetic elements of the sculpture will draw families and 
children into the installation in the same way sweet aromas and bright colors attract pollinators into a 
flower. ‘Pollinators at Play’ will manipulate perspective in a way that shrinks the human experience 
and catapults play as a catalyst for environmental education. 
I am confident
 that I can design, fabricate, and install an installation that aligns with the overarching 
nature play theme of Lions Park. ‘Pollinators at Play’ is a thoughtful installation that will serve to 
inspire curiosity, to educate, to engage multiple senses and personalities, and to connect every type 
of visitor to Lions Park with themselves, other people, and with nature.

COLOR + CEDAR 
Warm cedar columns, black steel botanical details and 
layered powder-coated aluminum create a colorful, 
approachable expression. 
Cedar remains above grade with protected end grain, 
metal cap and exterior finish. Periodic maintenance 
would be expected. 
WEATHERING STEEL 
Weathering steel provides a quieter, more elemental 
expression with reduced finish maintenance.
