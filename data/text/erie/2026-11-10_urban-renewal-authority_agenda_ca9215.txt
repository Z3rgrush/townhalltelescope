authority, with each plan area operating independently. TOEURA’s primary sources of 
revenue are property and sales taxes. TOEURA’s primary expenditures are capital 
projects and economic development. TOEURA often receives several requests for 
rollover appropriations for capital projects in the spring which can substantially impact 
the budget. 
 
The URA Fund is broken down by divisions into the Authority's seven separate plan 
areas: Airport, Daybreak/Colliers Hill, Historic Old Town, Highway 287/Nine Mile, Four 
Corners, I-25/Gateway, and Erie Town Center. Currently, only four of the Authority's 
seven plan areas (Daybreak, Old Town, Highway 287, & Four Corners) generate 
revenue. 
 
URA Revenues 
The URA fund is projected to have $5.75M of revenue in FY2027, which represents a 
6% decrease from the prior year. Taxes are the primary source of revenue for the URA. 
This includes property taxes collected from all plan areas, and sales taxes collected 
from the Historic Old Town and Highway 287 plan areas. The URA also generates 
investment earnings on its holdings, and keeps a small portion of the taxes it collects as 
fees to offset administrative costs. 
 
URA Expenditures 
The URA fund has $5.22M of expenditures budgeted for FY2027, which represents a 
57% decrease from the prior year. This large reduction is largely due to the completion 
of several capital projects. The URA's core expenditures are on capital and economic 
development. The primary capital expense budgeted for 2027 is the Briggs Alleyway 
Stormwater Improvements. The URA's economic development incentive agreements 
are organized under the Other Expenses category and expenditure levels largely 
correlate to the tax revenue generated by each project. Professional Services includes 
legal and consulting costs for the URA plan areas, while Debt refers to the debt 
payments on the bonds issued to fund public infrastructure improvements in Historic Old 
Town. The maintenance expenses are for lawn care of URA-owned property in the 
Historic Old Town plan area.

Re: Annual Budget Appropriation 2027 – URA 
 
 
We are pleased to present the Board of Commissioners with the 2027 Budget for the 
Town of Erie Urban Renewal Authority (“TOEURA”).   
 
TOEURA adopts an annual budget for the authority, with each plan area operating 
independently. The TOEURA’s primary sources of revenue are property and sales taxes. 
TOEURA’s primary expenditures are capital projects and economic development. 
TOEURA often receives several requests for rollover appropriations for capital projects 
in the spring which can substantially impact the budget. 
 
The total budgeted revenues for 2027 are $5,749,571. The total budgeted expenditures 
for 2027 are $5,218,570. 
 
The 2027 Budget is discussed below by each Urban Renewal Plan area. 
 
1) Airport URA 
The Airport plan area currently generates no revenue due to a lack of new 
development. There is a minimal budget for administrative expenses to facilitate 
new development. 
 
2) Daybreak/Colliers Hill 
Budgeted revenues declined significantly from 2026 due to the $14M cap on tax 
increment for this plan area. Budgeted expenditures also declined significantly 
from 2026 due to the completion of several capital projects, including the Colliers 
Hill – Historic Old Town Erie Pedestrian Bridge and the Colliers Hill Detention 
Pond Improvements. 
 
3) Historic Old Town URA 
Budgeted revenues are projected to increase slightly from 2026 due to an 
increase in property tax increment. Budgeted expenditures also declined slightly 
from 2026 due to the completion of the Historic Downtown Improvements 
project and the one-time reimbursement for 656 Kattell Street. TOEURA’s 
contribution to the Briggs Alleyway Stormwater Improvement project is the only 
capital expense in the 2027 Budget. 
 
Page 6 of 20
4) Highway 287 URA 
Budgeted revenues are projected to increase slightly from 2026 due to an 
increase in property tax increment. Budgeted expenditures are projected to 
increase in accordance with the increased revenues, as those revenues are 
pledged to the Nine Mile Metropolitan District for the repayment of bonds. 
 
5) Four Corners URA 
Budgeted revenues are projected to increase slightly from 2026 due to an 
increase in property tax increment. Budgeted expenditures are projected to 
increase in accordance with the increased revenues, as those revenues are 
pledged to the Four Corners Business Improvement District for the repayment of 
bonds. 
 
6) I-25 URA 
The I-25 plan area currently generates no revenue, but the site is under 
development. There is a minimal budget for administrative expenses to facilitate 
development at the site. 
 
7) Erie Town Center 
The Erie Town Center plan area currently generates no revenue, but staff are 
actively working on finalizing a development plan for the site. There is a minimal 
budget for administrative expenses to facilitate development at the site.

URA Plan Areas Summary
The URA Fund is broken down by divisions into the Authority's seven separate plan areas: Airport, Daybreak/Colliers 
Hill, Historic Old Town, Highway 287/Nine Mile, Four Corners, I-25/Gateway, and Erie Town Center.
URA Revenues & Expenditures by Plan Area
Currently, only four of the Authority's seven plan areas generate revenue. The Airport plan area has yet to see new 
development that would generate tax increment revenues, though staff continue to actively pursue development 
opportunities. The I-25 plan area was created in 2024 and should start generating tax increment revenue in 2028. The 
Erie Town Center plan area was created in 2025 and staff are actively working on finalizing a development plan for the 
site. Revenues and expenditures are closely correlated for the Highway 287 and Four Corners plan areas as the tax 
increment generated in these plan areas is pledged to the developers to help pay for public improvements. In the 
Daybreak/Colliers Hill and Historic Old Town plan areas, the tax increment revenues are largely not pledged, giving 
staff greater discretion to expend funds on the most impactful public improvements.
