Policy Issues: 
The proposed DDA advances the goals of the Historic Old Town Erie Urban Renewal 
Plan by redeveloping a vacant gateway site with new restaurant and retail uses, 
supporting local business growth, and expanding the long-term tax base. The 
agreement also reflects TOEURA’s use of targeted, performance-based incentives to 
facilitate private investment and redevelopment within the urban renewal area. 
 
Staff Recommendation: 
Aprove the Agreement. 
 
Summary/Key Points: 
• The proposed agreement would enable redevelopment of the TOEURA-owned 
property at 150 S. Briggs Street with approximately 13,100 square feet of new 
commercial space. 
Page 7 of 60
• An independent feasibility analysis estimates the total development cost at 
approximately $7.3 million and identifies an approximately $1.8 million feasibility 
gap on a present-value basis. 
• TOEURA would convey the property to the Developer for $0 and provide 
reimbursement of up to $1.5 million in property tax increment and $500,000 in 
sales tax increment through the remaining life of the TIF. The sales tax 
reimbursement is limited to 15% of project-generated incremental sales tax 
revenues.   
• The agreement includes development milestones, reimbursement conditions, 
and a right for TOEURA to repurchase the property at no cost if the Developer 
fails to obtain approvals or meet required construction milestones.  
 
 
Background of Subject Matter: 
On Feb. 26, 2024, the Town of Erie Urban Renewal Authority (TOEURA) acquired the 
approximately 1.12-acre property at 150 S. Briggs Street for $585,000 to support 
redevelopment of a key gateway into Historic Old Town Erie. In February 2026, the 
Town issued a new Request for Qualifications for the site, and TOEURA selected 
Meyers & Meyers as the preferred developer in April 2026 following a competitive 
review process. 
 
The proposed development includes approximately 13,100 square feet of new 
commercial space for Malbec, a full-service Argentine restaurant, and an existing Erie 
business seeking to relocate and expand. The project is intended to activate the site, 
support local businesses, and generate new sales and property tax revenue. The 
current feasibility analysis identifies approximately 5,113 square feet for Malbec and 
8,000 square feet for the other Erie business. 
TOEURA retained Pioneer Development Company to independently evaluate project 
feasibility and the level of public participation needed for the project to proceed. The 
analysis incorporated three construction bids and the Town’s updated estimate of 
approximately $865,753 in permit, plan review, tap and utility, impact, and construction 
use tax costs. Pioneer estimates the verified total development cost at approximately 
$7.3 million and identifies an approximately $1.8 million feasibility gap on a present-
value basis. 
 
The proposed agreement provides for conveyance of the property to Gaia 
Development, LLC at no cost and reimbursement of up to $1.5 million in property tax 
increment and $500,000 in sales tax increment through the remaining life of the TIF. 
The property has an estimated present-day value of approximately $600,000. The sales 
tax reimbursement is limited to 15% of incremental sales tax revenues generated by the 
project. The Developer remains responsible for project financing, design, entitlements, 
construction, and applicable development fees. All tap, permit, impact, and other 
development fees are assessed and paid by the Developer at the then-current rate. The 
agreement includes performance milestones and protections for TOEURA if the project

Executive Summary 
Briggs Maxwell Project Gap Funding Analysis: 
This report evaluates the proposed Briggs & Maxwell Project in Erie, Colorado and the public participation 
that may be required to make the development financially feasible. The analysis focuses on the 
commercial program proposed by Meyers & Meyers for the URA-owned parcel at 150 S. Briggs Street. 
Meyers & Meyers is seeking a public -private partnership with TOEURA to deliver approximately 13,113 
square feet of new commercial space, comprising a 5,113 SF full- service restaurant for Malbec and an 
8,000 SF retail building for CyclErie. Both users have direct local ties, and the development team has 
provided executed letters of intent supporting full initial occupancy. 
When this analysis began, the development concept had not yet produced bid-level construction costs or 
executed lease terms, and PDC built its estimates from interviews with Town staff and the development 
team and from local and regional market research. Since then, the cost basis has been verified in two 
ways. At the request of Town staff and PDC, the development team obtained three construction bids, 
which PDC analyzed and normalized against its initial estimates. Town of Erie staff then provided the 
project's estimated development fees —  approximately $865,753 for all associated permit fees, plan 
review fees, tap and utility fees, impact fees, and construction use tax — roughly a $300,000 increase over 
the prior analysis. Incorporating the bids and the Town's fee estimates produces a verified total 
development cost of $7.3 million ($557 per rentable square foot), which is within approximately 1% of 
PDC's independently benchmarked construction cost and therefore within that benchmark's margin of 
error. This verified cost is used throughout the report to assess the project's feasibility and structure the 
recommended incentive package. 
PDC evaluated this development on behalf of TOEURA to determine if the project exhibits a feasibility gap 
and if so, the level of financial support necessary to achieve market returns. This report provides a detailed 
analysis of the project’s assumptions b enchmarked to the market. It also performs a gap funding range 
analysis on various levels of financial assistance and examines the effect on feasibility indicators. Finally, 
the report estimates TIF generated by the project as proposed and compares these estimates with the 
identified funding gap. 
After completing the analysis, the reviewers find that the Project will require funding assistance to 
become financially feasible. At the verified development cost, the project fails to yield returns over the 
market target benchmarks — cost-to-value ratio, return on cost, unlevered 10-year IRR, and net present 
value — without additional gap funding. However, the Project exceeds those benchmarks when 
$1,800,000 of gap funding is applied  to construction equity. Fortunately, the value of the URA -owned 
land, together with the tax increment revenues the project is expected to generate, exceeds the estimated 
funding gap. This report recommends that TOEURA structure a three -part incentive package — a no-
cost land conveyance, a pledge of 100% of the project's property tax increment, and a 15% share of the 
project's municipal sales tax increment — worth approximately $1.8 million in present value, effectively 
closing the funding gap and making the project viable. 
At a recommended gap funding input of $1,800,000, the project clears the primary feasibility tests. Return 
on cost is 7.57% against a 7.50% target, the unlevered 10-year IRR is 9.21% against an 8.50% benchmark, 
and NPV is positive at approximately $296,038 discounted at the 8.50% hurdle rate. These return on 
investment indicators, and the recommended gap funding scenario, are detailed in the following tables:

Tax Increment Financing Projections 
The project is expected to generate meaningful sales tax revenue for the Town of Erie. At stabilization, 
the two tenants are projected to produce roughly $8.7 million in annual taxable sales. 
Because the parcel sits within the Historic Old Town Erie Urban Renewal Plan Area, the tax forecast 
captures increment only through the plan's expiration. Over that remaining term, the project generates 
combined sales and property tax increment with a present value of approximately $3,000,000 at a 6.50% 
discount rate, compared with the verified feasibility gap of $1,800,000. This means that the project's 
projected tax increment revenues can be leveraged, together with the value of the URA-owned land, to 
fully close the estimated feasibility gap. 
This report recommends that TOEURA close the gap through a three-part incentive package. First, TOEURA 
should convey the URA -owned parcel to the developer at no cost — an incentive worth approximately 
$600,000 in present value after accounting for closing costs, title insurance, and contingencies. Second, 
100% of the project's incremental property tax — projected to total approximately $1,500,000 through 
the Plan's sunset, or approximately $900,000 in present value — should be remitted back to the 
developer. Third, 15% of the project's incremental municipal sales tax — approximately $300,000 in 
present value — should be remitted back to the developer. Together these components are worth 
approximately $1.8 million in present value , effectively closing the funding gap. Because the developer 
cannot practically capitalize future reimbursement streams the way a larger development would through 
bonds or separate loans, the land conveyance provides needed up -front capital while the tax i ncrement 
pledges close the balance of the gap.

Scope of the Review 
PDC reviewed the proposed Briggs & Maxwell Project from the perspective of an open-market feasibility 
analysis. The review focused on the following assumptions and feasibility tests: 
• Independent estimation of market rent, cap rate, yield, and discount-rate assumptions. 
• Review and normalization of three construction bids, Town of Erie fee estimates, development 
cost, one-time fees, use tax, and remaining cost-diligence items. 
• Financial gap analysis using return-on-cost, unlevered IRR, and NPV indicators. 
• Review of project-generated sales tax and property tax increment capacity. 
• Identification of development, reimbursement, and tax-model risks. 
 
Methodology 
The review draws on developer materials, TOEURA source documents, three construction bids obtained 
by the development team, the Town of Erie's development fee estimates, PDC's feasibility model, the tax 
forecast model, CoStar market research, investor return benchmarks, and interviews with Town staff and 
the development team. PDC analyzed the proposed project as an income-producing retail asset and tested 
whether the resulting cash flows satisfy market return thresholds. 
When this review began, the concept had not yet produced detailed construction cost estimates, executed 
lease terms, or itemized operating expenses, and PDC estimated those inputs from local and regional 
research and from direct discussions with Town staff and the development team. The cost basis has since 
been verified: the development team provided three pre -construction bids at the request of Town staff 
and PDC, and the Town of Erie provided its estimate of all development fees associated with the projec t. 
PDC analyzed the bids and fee estimates, placed them on a common scope basis, and compared them 
with its independent cost benchmarks; the verified development cost now anchors the underwriting in 
this report. Lease terms and operating expenses remain PD C estimates that were shared with both the 
Town and the developer to confirm their reasonableness before being underwritten in the model. The 
analysis uses the known development program only; no speculative residential, office, lodging, or future 
commercial phase is included. 
PDC also reviewed the Town’s solicitation materials and the staff recommendation memo to understand 
the public-purpose context. The parcel functions as a southern gateway to Historic Old Town Erie, and the 
project objectives include activation, sales tax g eneration, readiness to proceed, and compatibility with 
the Historic Old Town Urban Renewal Plan. 
This review does not determine final agreement terms or certify eligible reimbursement amounts. Those 
items should be resolved during negotiation after the Town, TOEURA, and developer confirm final costs, 
fee obligations, land-disposition terms, revenue-sharing mechanics, and the legal structure for any public 
participation.

Current Economic Environment 
Erie straddles two distinct market areas, with local commercial activity influenced by both Boulder County 
and Weld County. The town's population has more than tripled since 2010, and the Elevate Erie 
Comprehensive Plan projects continued long -term growth.  That growth supports demand for locally 
serving retail, restaurants, and destination commercial uses. 
At the same time, Erie remains a relatively thin commercial retail comp market. The project is located in 
a high-visibility gateway location, but recent comparable data for newly delivered restaurant and retail 
space in Erie is limited. PDC therefore revie wed multiple nearby comparison areas, including Boulder 
County, Boulder, Longmont, Erie/Lafayette/Superior, Broomfield, and Northglenn. 
The review indicated a broad range of market rent observations, with project-specific support for a $30.00 
PSF NNN underwriting assumption. That assumption is above some backward -looking market averages 
but reasonable for the project's location, visibility, tenant profile, and intended role as a catalytic gateway 
development. 
Current construction cost pressure remains the principal feasibility challenge. The proposed project has a 
relatively small building program, includes two separate commercial buildings, and requires site work, 
fees, and construction costs that are difficult to support through achievable retail rents alone. The verified 
development cost — built from three construction bids and the Town of Erie's confirmed fee estimates — 
confirms this dynamic: achievable retail rents cannot support the full private cost basi s. Public 
participation is therefore needed to reduce the private cost basis to a feasible level. 
The parcel is located in the Weld County portion of Erie. As a result, Boulder County sales and use taxes 
do not apply, and the Town's 3.5% municipal sales tax is the operative local revenue stream for incentive 
and TIF analysis. That distinction is import ant because the project is expected to generate substantial 
taxable sales, but the reimbursement structure should be tied to the Town-controlled revenue units that 
can legally and practically support the assistance. 
Investor return requirements also support the need for assistance. For a small-format retail and restaurant 
project in Erie, a market -oriented underwriting case should test return-on -cost, unlevered IRR, NPV, 
going-in cap rate, terminal cap rate, and yield-on-cost thresholds. The Briggs Maxwell model clears those 
thresholds only after applying the recommended gap funding input.

Development Budget 
Town of Erie staff and PDC requested that the development team obtain at least three construction bids 
for the project. PDC analyzed the three bids — submitted by Ushuaia Builders, Van Vleet Construction, 
and Lefort Built — and compared them with its initial cost estimates. Because the bids were not submitted 
on a common scope basis, PDC normalized each bid by adding the municipal fees, design, tenant finish, 
contingency, escalation, and bonding scopes that individual bidders excluded. Town of Erie staff ha ve 
since estimated the fees associated with the development, which now total approximately $865,753 for 
all associated permit fees, plan review fees, tap and utility fees, impact fees, and construction use tax — 
approximately a $300,000 increase over the p rior analysis. Incorporating the bids and the Town's fee 
estimates moves the verified development cost to $7.3 million. 
As submitted, the three bids totaled $6,163,866 (Ushuaia Builders), $4,748,897 (Van Vleet Construction), 
and $4,739,955 (Lefort Built). The totals are not directly comparable: Ushuaia is the only bidder carrying 
design and engineering, municipal permits an d use tax, tap and impact fees, and escalation to a 2027 
construction start — roughly $1.19 million of scope the other two expressly push to the owner — while 
Lefort's estimate reflects core and shell only. Stripped to hard construction cost, the three bids fall within 
approximately 5% of one another, which provides strong support for the underlying pricing. On PDC's 
normalized common-scope basis, the bids range from approximately $5.98 million to $7.29 million in total 
construction and delivery cost. 
PDC completed its cost estimate using the Ushuaia bid — the most complete of the three — and added 
the Town of Erie's confirmed fee estimates, financing costs based on current commercial loan interest 
rates and a 70% loan-to-cost ratio, a developer fee at the industry-standard 3%, and project contingency.

Feasibility Gap Analysis 
Retail Component 
The commercial program is the only modeled revenue-producing component. Both tenants are expected 
to generate sales-tax activity, but the real estate economics are constrained by the relationship between 
achievable rent and total development cost. 
The tenant mix reduces several risks normally present in a speculative retail project. Malbec is a new full-
service Argentine concept proposed by the operators of Piripi, and the developer materials indicate it 
would create more than 25 jobs. CyclErie is a n established Erie business that would relocate and expand 
into the larger retail building, freeing two smaller Old Town spaces for future tenants. These tenants, and 
the project’s signed LOI, help mitigate risks typically associated with ground-up development that may 
be searching for tenants to fill the new commercial space. 
At $30.00 PSF NNN, stabilized NOI is approximately $416,191. Applying a 6.25% going-in cap rate produces 
estimated stabilized value of approximately $6,659,055. That value remains below total development 
cost, which creates the feasibility gap. From a feas ibility perspective, the retail component is strong 
enough to justify continued negotiation but not strong enough to proceed on a purely private basis: the 
known users improve marketability, while the small scale of the buildings and the current constructi on-
cost environment limit the project's ability to absorb the full cost burden without assistance. 
Development Risk 
The principal risks are manageable if addressed directly in the final participation agreement. PDC 
recommends that TOEURA treat the current model as a negotiation framework and require updated 
evidence before committing to a final reimbursement amount. Some development risks associated with 
this project are listed below: 
• Construction cost risk has been substantially resolved: the development team provided three pre-
construction bids, which PDC normalized and compared against its market estimates, and the Town 
of Erie has confirmed the project's permit, plan review, tap and utility, impact fee, and construction 
use tax estimates. The verified development cost is within approximately 1% of PDC's independent 
benchmark; 
• Increased competition and prolonged inflation may decrease sales and increase financial risks 
with both the business and development; 
• Retail sales may be overstated. The project’s weighted average sales per square foot equals $664. 
This represents a high performing retail business within Erie; the figures are not astronomical but 
are optimistic. These figures were provided by the developers and their tenants, and so PDC 
incorporated them within their model. If the restaurant and its retail tenants fail to generate sales 
at this level, it will likely be unable to provide the gap funding necessary for market feasibility. 
• Capitalization Rates may be lower than can be realized, making the gap higher than expected; 
• Potential Rent Concessions as a deduction to asking rents may become more common in the 
market, although this is unlikely given the signed Letter of Intent; 
• Public participation by the Town of Erie Urban Renewal Authority and the Town of Erie may be 
limited; and 
• Current economic uncertainty.

Recommended Incentive Package 
The verified feasibility gap of $1,800,000 can be closed through a three -part incentive package that 
combines a land conveyance with pledges of the project's property and sales tax increment. Although the 
project sits within the Historic Old Town Erie Urban Renewal Plan Area and could leverage future property 
and sales tax increment revenue, TIF incentive agreements are typically structured as reimbursement 
agreements paid out over time. Meyers & Meyers, as a small-scale developer, does not have the practical 
ability to capitalize those future revenue streams the way a larger development would through bonds or 
separate borrowing. The developer therefore prefers an incentive structure that provides up-front capital 
toward the funding gap, complemented by ongoing revenue sharing. 
The Town of Erie owns the project parcel, which it recently purchased for $585,000. Assuming the land is 
appraised and valued at a similar price, PDC estimates that a conveyance of the parcel to the developer 
— accounting for closing costs, title insurance , and contingencies — represents approximately $600,000 
in value. PDC recommends that the Town convey the land to the developer at no cost, providing an 
incentive worth approximately $600,000 in present value and delivering immediate, up-front capital to 
the project. 
The remaining $1,200,000 of the gap can be closed with the project's tax increment. First, 100% of the 
project's incremental property tax should be remitted back to the developer: the project should yield 
approximately $1.5 million in property tax increment revenue between now and the Plan's 2038 sunset, 
worth approximately $900,000 today at a 6.50% discount rate. Second, 15% of the project's incremental 
municipal sales tax should be remitted back to the developer — approximately $500,000 on a gross basis 
over the same term, worth approximately $300,000 in present value. Combined with the proposed land 
conveyance, these pledges bring the incentive package to approximately $1.8 million in present value, 
effectively closing the project's funding gap and making the project viable. 
Town Return on Incentive 
From the Town's perspective, this incentive package represents a sound investment in Erie's downtown . 
Even after the land conveyance and  tax increment reimbursements, the Town's return on incentive is 
positive. The Town's direct financial contribution consists of the approximately $600,000 land 
conveyance, its own share of the pledged property tax increment, and the 15% sales tax increment share. 
The property tax increment is generated by all taxing districts in the Plan Area  (with the exception of the 
Metro District), not only the Town —  which is why the $900,000 pledged increment is larger than the 
Town's own share. The Town's 13.237 -mill share, discounted to present value, equals approximately 
$113,000, and the 15% municipal sales tax share equals approximately $311,000, bringing the Town's total 
financial contribution to the project to approximately $1,024,000 in today's dollars. 
The project is expected to generate significant sales tax revenue once open . Revenues not subject to the 
incentive agreement. PDC worked with the development team to estimate stabilized annual sales for both 
the restaurant and the cycle shop. The retained 85% of the Town's 3.50% municipal sales tax increment 
amounts to approximately $2.9 million on a gross basis between 2028 an d 2038 — approximately $1.76 
million in present value at the same 6.50% discount rate. Because this retained revenue remains outside 
the incentive agreement, it recompenses the Town for its land conveyance and tax increment shares,

Conclusion 
The Briggs & Maxwell Project represents a creative project and excellent candidate for a public private 
partnership between the Town, TOEURA and the Developer. The development would activate a high -
visibility URA -owned gateway parcel, construct a new and a ttract commercial amenity, support the 
expansion of two local businesses, and fulfill the original goals and objectives outlined in the Historic Old 
Town Erie Plan. This development project will create new jobs, sales tax revenues and advance TOEURA’s 
redevelopment objectives. 
The project is also more actionable than a typical speculative commercial proposal. Meyers & Meyers has 
local development and operating experience, the Town has completed a competitive selection process, 
both buildings have identified users with executed l etters of intent. Staff materials evaluated by this 
report also indicate the project has incorporated pre-application feedback from Town staff and planning 
departments. The development team is committed to realizing this vision but requires additional publ ic 
assistance to make their vision a reality. 
This report finds that the conceptual project is not financially feasible without assistance under the current 
underwriting case. The verified total development cost of $7,300,000 — built from three pre-construction 
bids and the Town of Erie's confirmed fe e estimates —  exceeds supportable stabilized value of 
approximately $6,660,000, and the model requires gap funding of approximately $1.8 million to satisfy 
market return thresholds. This report recommends that TOEURA use $1,800,000 as the gap funding input 
for its incentive agreement with Meyers & Meyers, and that the gap be closed through a three-part 
incentive package: a no -cost conveyance of the URA -owned parcel, worth approximately $600,000 in 
present value; a pledge of 100% of the project's property ta x increment through the Plan's 2038 sunset, 
worth approximately $900,000 in present value; and a 15% share of the project's municipal sales tax 
increment, worth approximately $300,000 in present value. This structure provides the up -front capital 
the developer requires while limiting the Town's own financial contribution to approximately $1,024,000 
in present value — its land equity, its mill-levy share of the pledged property tax increment, and its sales 
tax increment share —  against approximately $1.76 mi llion of retained sales tax increment, a return on 
incentive of approximately 1.7 times. 
The cost basis of this analysis is verified. The three pre-construction bids obtained by the development 
team and the Town of Erie's confirmed fee estimates produce a total development cost within 
approximately 1% of PDC's independently benchmarked constru ction cost — within that benchmark's 
margin of error. Lease terms and operating expenses remain PDC estimates that were reviewed with the 
development team and the Town to confirm their reasonableness. PDC recognizes that the Town, 
TOEURA, and the developer  all wish to advance this public -private partnership; the recommended 
structure ties the tax increment components of the package to actual project-generated revenues, so the 
Town's participation remains proportionate to the project's performance. 
The final agreement should therefore be structured as a capped, performance- based package —  
combining the land disposition with the property tax increment pledge and the 15% sales tax increment 
share — restricted to eligible costs and actual project-generated tax increment within the plan's remaining 
term. This is not a recommendation to commit all available project revenue; 85% of the Town's sales tax 
increment remains entirely outside the agreement. It is a recommendation to advance a right -sized 
assistance package that allows the project to proceed while protecting TOEURA through reimbursement 
limits and revenue-performance controls.
