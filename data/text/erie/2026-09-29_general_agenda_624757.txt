The position of Town Manager has been vacant since Feb. 18, 2026 with an Acting 
Town Manager currently in the role. The Town opened applications for Town Manager for the 
month of August and collected 60 applications from candidates in Colorado and nationwide. An initial

Town of Erie   
Town Manager  
Interview Instructions 
 
1. In advance of the interviews, please review the position brochure, schedule, interview 
questions, and candidate materials. The position brochure continues to be our roadmap and 
reminder of what we are looking for in a Town Manager.  
 
2. We are using panel-style interviews and scripted behavioral-based interview questions to keep 
bias at bay and level the playing field. Our goal is to allow the candidates to showcase to you 
how their past experiences will translate to this role. 
 
3. We encourage you to ask follow-up or clarifying questions as needed. We want you to leave 
each conversation with a sense of what the candidate can add to the Town Manager position.  
 
4. Please remember that your interview notes are subject to public records requirements. Keep 
notes focused on the candidate’s responses, qualifications, and job-related competencies. Do 
not document personal information, speculation, assumptions, or comments about 
characteristics unrelated to the position, including age, family or marital status, health, disability, 
religion, race, sex, or other protected characteristics. 
 
5. As you meet with candidates, please keep in mind the most common biases that occur during 
the interview process:  
• Stereotyping Bias: Forming an opinion of someone based on gender, religion, race, 
appearance, or any other type of characteristic.  
• First Impression Bias: Making judgments about an interviewee based on their first 
impression being positive or negative.  
• “Similar to Me” Effect: Thinking highly of someone who has a similar mindset or 
personality to the interviewer.   
• Negative Emphasis Bias: When the interviewer receives a small amount of negative 
information that over-influences their assessment. We tend to weigh negative information 
heavier than positive information.   
• Halo and Horn Effect: The Halo Effect is when the interviewer lets one positive fact about 
the candidate overshadow everything else they say or do. The Horn Effect is the opposite 
and allows a weak fact to influence everything else.  
• Cultural Noise: The interviewer’s ability to distinguish between a candidate’s socially 
acceptable answer and their true opinion. 
• Contrast Effect: When a stronger candidate interviews after a weaker candidate, they 
may appear more qualified than they are because of the contrast between the two.  
 
6. Interview practices should follow EEOC rules. Please refrain from asking questions that have 
the potential to place the organization at risk. Illegal questions corresponding to the following 
protected categories, such as national origin, citizenship, age, marital status, disabilities, arrest 
and conviction record, military discharge status, race, gender, or pregnancy status, are not 
allowed.

Biographies for the Community Meet and Greet 
Corby Alexander 
Kelli Johnson 
Kathleen Osher 
Corby Alexander currently serves as the City Manager for the City of La Porte, Texas, a 
position he's held since 2012. Prior to his current role, Corby served several Texas 
communities as City Manager for Bonham, City Administrator of Jefferson, and Assistant 
to the City Manager in College Station. He holds a Master of Public Service and 
Administration and a Bachelor of Arts in Political Science from Texas A&M University. 
Corby is also currently serving as Region 6 President for the Texas City Management 
Association. 
Kelli Johnson currently serves as the Deputy City Manager for the City of Greeley, 
Colorado. She joined the City of Greeley in 2019 as the Director of Communication and 
Engagement and was promoted to Chief of Staff. Prior to the City of Greeley, she was the 
Public Information Manager for the High Plains Library District. Kelli holds a Master of 
Science in Organizational Leadership from Regis University and a Bachelor of Arts in 
Journalism from the University of Northern Colorado. She is also a graduate of the 
Harvard Kennedy School Senior Executives in State and Local Government program. 
Kathleen Osher currently serves as the Deputy City Manager for the City of Littleton, 
Colorado. She joined the City of Littleton in 2018 and served as the Manager of Innovation 
and Performance Excellence, Director of Community Services, and Assistant City 
Manager. Prior to the City of Littleton, she served as the Executive Director of the Transit 
Alliance in Denver. She earned a Bachelor of Arts in Political Science and German from 
McDaniel College, completed a PPP International Fellowship, and graduated from the 
University of Denver's Mini-MBA High Performance Leadership Program.
