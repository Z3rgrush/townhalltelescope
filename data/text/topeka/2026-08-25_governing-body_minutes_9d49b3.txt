DISCUSSION regarding the City of Topeka 5-Year Financial Forecast, was presented.  
Dr. Robert M. Perez, City Manager, stated as part of his annual work plan they have 
completed a 5-year financial forecast identifying viable solutions to address the projected 
shortfalls for FY2028 and FY2029. Although FY2027 budget is balanced, recent sales tax 
collection is lower than anticipated for the first five months of 2026. Flattened sales tax 
collections and franchise fees coupled with slow growth in property taxes and increased 
personnel cost driven through higher contract wages have created a structurally imbalanced 
budget where costs exceed revenues. Viable options will be presented for cost reductions and 
tax increases to include options with a recommended timeline for a sales tax initiative that 
could be on the ballot in March 2027. In reference to the high number of identified personnel 
reductions and filled positions, he would request any detailed discussions regarding personnel 
be reserved for executive session on September 1, 2026.       
Josh McAnarney, Deputy Finance Director, provided a high-level overview of the 5-year 
financial forecast for the General Fund. He reminded the Governing Body that 75% of the 
General Fund is used for salaries, with the majority (70%) being dedicated to public safety. He 
provided an overview of the following:  
General Fund Categories: FY 2027 budgeted General Fund expenditures and revenues 
total $134.5 million (balanced budget); approximately 76% of expenditures are 
personnel related; and almost 83% of revenues come from property/motor vehicle tax, 
sales tax, and franchise fees.  
 
Surplus/Deficit History for Years 2020-2026 in the General Fund. He noted in 2026, 
other fund balances were utilized to offset expenses in the General Fund.

Forecasted Income Statement: Structural Income Statement Problem: Revenue 
generated by the General Fund is insufficient to cover budgeted expenditures, creating a 
recurring imbalance between ongoing revenues and expenses. Assuming no change in 
expenditures or tax base and capping the 2027 Budget, projected revenue growth would 
not break even with expenditures until 2033. 
 
City Manager Perez referenced forecasted revenues and expenses through 2031. They 
are looking at a $13.3 million deficit in 2028; however, without any major revenue adjustment 
or new income and based on the personnel positions that have been froze and eliminated the 
deficit will be nearly $22.8 million by 2031. He reported this year they have effectively frozen 
or eliminated 49 positions consisting of 32 General Fund vacant positions realizing a savings of 
$3.2 million that includes 19 police officers, 10 firefighters, 1 engineering tech, and 2 assistant 
chief positions (police/fire) as well as eliminate 17 positions outright from various departments.    
17 positions have been reduced permanently and 32 positions frozen for a total of 49 positions 
that have effectively been taken out of the budget for 2027. This forecast assumes these 
positions remain vacant, frozen or eliminated.  
Forecasted Income Statement Assumptions: Revenues: Sales tax growth projected at 
1.5%; Franchise fee growth projected at 1%. Assessed value growth projected at 3%. 
Expenditures: FY 2027 position reductions/freezes are assumed going forward, other 
fund considerations (Retirement Reserve, Workers Comp, and Special Highway) 
supplemented by the General Fund. Current union contract increases assumed; future 
union contract increases assumed at 3% growth. 
  
Sales Tax Collection History: Sales tax is historically projected with a 2.75% growth. 
Through five months, 2026 sales tax collections trail 2025. Even with 1.25% growth over 
the remaining months, collections would still be approximately $566,005 below budget. 
Lower 2026 sales tax collections reduce the base for future forecasts, creating a 
compounding impact on future projections. In 2025, sales tax accounted for approximately 
33% of General Fund revenues. 
 
Property Tax Distribution: In 2028, more property tax revenue must be prioritized for the 
Debt Service Fund to cover scheduled principal and interest payments and support future 
CIP projects, while assuming an overall flat mill levy. Without structural changes to CIP

Sales Tax Ballot Question: To address budget shortfalls, sales tax initiatives have been 
considered for: 
 
• A new, half-cent, citywide sales tax (would generate $20M annually) for general 
purposes to adequately fund the City’s operations and services such as fire department, 
police department, code enforcement, municipal court, and public works.  
• A new, one-tenth of a cent, citywide sales tax (would generate $4M annually) to fund 
the affordable housing trust fund and establish market-rate housing fund. 
• As part of a March 2027 ballot, in addition to the new measures, it would be 
recommended to renew the existing half-cent, citywide infrastructure sales tax, which 
expires in 2029. 
 
Sale Tax Ballot Question Timeline: For a March 2027 ballot, the Governing Body would 
have to consider the schedule below: 
 
• September 3, 2026: Policy and Finance Committee Discussion  
• September 15, 2026: Council Discussion 
• October 6, 2026: Council Consideration and Approval of Ballot Language 
• October 20, 2026: Council Consideration and Approval to Call for An Election 
• March 2, 2027: Special Election Held 
• July 1, 2027: If ballot questions are approved by voters, this would be the first day of 
new sales tax collections. 
 
Staff recommends a mail-in ballot which historically has higher participation and an 
average cost of $115,000 versus in-person elections which would have a significantly 
higher average cost of $189,000.
