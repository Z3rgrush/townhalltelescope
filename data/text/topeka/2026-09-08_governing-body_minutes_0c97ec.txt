 ORDINANCE NO. 20684 introduced by City Manager Dr. Robert M. Perez establishing 
utility rates for 2027 through 2030, amending Topeka Municipal Code Sections 13.05.20 through 
13.05.060 and repealing original sections, was presented.  
 Dr. Robert M. Perez, City Manager, stated discussions began June 2, 2026, on the need to 
make utility rate adjustments to support operations and increase investment in capital 
improvements for utilities. Over the past several months Staff has presented utility rate options. 
Staff recommends the removal of the readiness to serve charge (base charge) along with 
incremental increases in base and consumption charges across all customer classes with a phased in 
approach for commercial and industrial customers over the next four years.     
 Sylvia Davis, Utilities Director, thanked everyone who has contributed to the rate 
discussions over the past several months. She gave special thanks to her Utilities Team for their 
support as they have worked to find a feasible utility rate proposal that will help them manage 
infrastructure and improve services across the board. She advised City employees are as equally 
impacted by utility rates as other residents and community members. She stated as they have 
worked to balance competing priorities, it has been through collaborative dialog that they have 
come to a proposal that does not promise to make everyone happy, but it does demonstrate City 
Administration’s commitment to investing in Topeka’s future. She noted this was not the end of 
dialog and collaboration no matter the outcome of the proposal as they continue to plan and invest 
in the reliability, sustainability, and affordability for Topeka’s utility infrastructure.

residential and industrial paying the same rate preventing average people from subsidizing

 DISCUSSION regarding the Topeka Fire Department (TFD) 2026 Station Relocation Plan, 
was presented. 
 Dr. Robert M. Perez, City Manager, stated the proposed relocation plan was based on the 
goal of decreasing response times across the city.   
09-08-26 
382 
 
 The Topeka Fire Department (TFD) 2026 Station Relocation Plan was presented by Topeka 
Fire Chief Chad Longstaff, Deputy Fire Chief Kevin Gill, and Division Chief Ty Forshee. A short 
video was displayed, and the following information was highlighted: 
• The goal of the plan is to decrease response times across the city. TFD has been 
evaluating options for new fire stations and how to expand four-minute response 
coverage.  
• TFD current four-minute response coverage covers 75% of households in the city.  
• With the recommended plan, the four-minute response coverage would increase by 7% 
covering 82% of households in the city.  
• Station operational challenges that need to be addressed include lack of bay space due 
to larger apparatus, increased maintenance due to aging stations, and difficulty 
retrofitting stations for separate female facilities.  
• Public meetings were held on July 6, 9, 10 and 15, 2026, to gather public input and 
discuss the relocation of Fire Station Nos. 1, 6 and 11.  
   
➢ Phase I would include building Fire Station No. 13 at SW 6th and Governor's Place 
and relocating Engine Company 11 to staff Fire Station No. 13. (Fire Station No. 11 
would operate with Truck Company 11).  
➢ Phase II would include building Fire Station No. 11 at Lyman and N. Topeka Blvd 
and relocating Engine 1 and Truck Company 11 to the new Fire Station No. 11, 
which would close Fire Station No. 1 and No. 11.  
➢ Phase III would include building Fire Station No. 6 at Billard Airport and relocating 
Engine Company 6 to this location. Once completed, current Fire Stations 1, 6 and 
11 would be repurposed to house Topeka Police Units, Topeka Fire Investigators 
and fire apparatus overflow and storage.
