Governing Body Minutes – August 19, 2026 
CITY COUNCIL CHAMBERS, Topeka, Kansas, Tuesday, August 19, 2026. The 
Governing Body members of the City of Topeka met in a Special Work Session at 5:00 P.M. with 
the following Councilmembers present: Councilmembers Hiller, Valdivia-Alcala, Ortiz, Banks, 
Kell, Miller, Bradberry, McGee and Hoferer- 9. Mayor Duncan Presided - 1. 
No public comment was allowed at the work session as it was designated for discussion 
and information gathering by the Governing Body. The special meeting work session was open 
to the public to listen and observe. The meeting could be viewed online 
at https://www.topeka.org/communications/live-stream/ or 
at https://www.facebook.com/cityoftopeka/. 
AFTER THE MEETING WAS CALLED TO ORDER, the PLEDGE OF 
ALLEGIANCE was recited by meeting participants. 
COMPASS DATA CENTER representatives provided opening remarks and participated 
in a question-and-answer session regarding the proposed Compass Datacenter Development in 
Shawnee County, located south of the city of Topeka limits. Brett Collard, Senior Vice 
President of Land Development, Clift Pompee, Vice President of Power and Emission, Eric 
Williams, Senior Vice President Solutions Engineering. and Janise Murphy, Principal of 
Corporate Communications, was present for questions. Compass Datacenters are Hyperscale 
Class A, community first developments with a proven track record of delivering reliable, 
sustainable and customizable data centers derived from their unique and transparent approach 
to development. They focus on proactive site selection to health and safety in construction. In 
response to the data center industry growing faster than the workforce that supports it, 
Compass has created new pathways into high-paying, high-impact careers  “The Legacy v. Lie

In response, the following Topics were discussed:  
Environmental and Water Impact 
• Water Management: Policies, safety protocols, cooling system maintenance, and 
stormwater collection. 
• Ecosystem Effects: Impacts on local land, air quality, surrounding wildlife, and farm 
animals. 
• Compliance: Responses to recent Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) violations. 
 
Economic and Tax Impacts 
• Investments and Jobs: The $4 billion capital investment timeline and actual local 
construction jobs created. 
• Taxes and Exemptions: Property tax assessments, projected exemptions, abatements, 
and documentation from other communities. 
• Revenue: Franchise fees and adjacent property values. 
 
Infrastructure and Utilities 
• Grid Capacity: Ensuring sufficient utility grid infrastructure for power demands. 
• Facility Mechanics: Heat generation, internal facility temperatures, and forecasting 
market trends between 2026 and 2028. 
 
Community, Legal, and Governance 
• Legal Policies: Regulations addressing noise pollution, traffic, electricity costs, and 
environmental burdens. 
• Surveillance and Privacy: Liability regarding increased digital surveillance of 
customers. 
• Community Engagement: Commitment assurance, education materials, and 
communication transparency. 
 
Councilmember Hoferer left the meeting.
