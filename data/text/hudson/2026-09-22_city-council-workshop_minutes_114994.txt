Discussion Items3.
A. 26-0080 Truck Traffic along SR 91 and SR 303
Brief Description:   Staff would like to discuss with Council restricting truck 
weight limits along SR 91 and SR 303 and designating an alternate truck route 
to alleviate congestion at the intersections of SR 91 and SR 303.
Vehicle ClassificationsAttachments:
Mr. Kosco shared information he received from the Ohio Department of Transportation regarding potential 
limitations on truck traffic along SR 91 and SR 303 . He explained that any reduction in truck traffic could only 
be based on vehicle weight, not vehicle type, and outlined the constraints involved in implementing such 
measures. In response to a request to explore options for improving the turn radius at the SR 91 and SR 303 
intersection, Mr. Sheridan and Mayor Anzevino noted that this was reviewed several years ago, and it was 
determined that the existing green space should not be disturbed to widen the turn radii in that area .
This matter was discussed
B. 26-0081 W. Barlow Road Railroad Exemption
Brief Description:   Staff would like to discuss with Council the desire to 
formally request an exemption for the W. Barlow Road railroad tracks.
Barlow Road RR ExemptAttachments:
Mr. Kosco provided an explanation on the reason for this exemption request and the process to make it exempt . 
Council was in agreement with moving forward with the exemption, with legislation appearing on the next 
workshop agenda.
This matter was discussed

Dr. Bird noted that the proposed amendments would prohibit children who require car seats from riding in low 
speed vehicles. He also outlined his additional amendments, including restricting vehicles that travel below the 
posted speed limit on roadways. Mr. Brezovec stated that he agreed with either of Mr. Sutton’s proposed 
amendments, but his preference is to use stickers instead of on-board documentation, while Dr . Weinstein 
expressed support for the sticker approach. Mr. Sutton clarified that Dr. Bird’s amendment includes language 
requiring DOT approved tires, which do not exist for these types of vehicles and would therefore prevent all of 
them from operating on public roads. In response to a question, Chief Tabak stated that his staff would be able 
to enforce the Ordinance under Dr. Bird’s proposed amendments. The majority of Council members expressed 
support for Dr. Bird’s amendments but requested clarification regarding the requirement for DOT approved 
tires.
Ordinance No. 26-113 was forwarded for further consideration at the October 6, 2026, Council 
meeting.
