Public Comments5.
Ms. Linda O'Neil, 7063 St. Ives Blvd, announced plans for Piper's Key Garden, a remembrance garden for 
children.  She also stated that Hudson Inclusive Playground t-shirts would go on sale and help fund inclusive 
and accessible equipment and thanked  all City departments, Councils, and Mayor who were involved in the 
creation of the DREAM Playground.
Mr. Ryan Beam, 2633 Hudson Aurora Road, stated that the proposed residential component for Clinton 
Crossing should be increased in order to repay the City's expenses .  
Mr. Kent Cleveland, 71 Keswick Dr., requested Council to leave the low-speed vehicle legislation as-is .  
Correspondence and Council Comments6.
Mr. White provided an update on the Environmental Awareness Committee's goals, and announced an open 
forum on e-bikes, taking place on August 19 from 6:00-8:00 p .m. at Barlow Community Center.
Dr. Goetz announced an open forum for short-term and long-term rentals on August 31 at 7:00 p .m. at Barlow 
Community Center.  She stated that many residents in Ward 1 have expressed concerns about the topic .
Several Councilmembers praised the ribbon-cutting event of the DREAM Playground at Oak Grove Park, as 
well as a productive joint meeting between Council and Planning Commission regarding Clinton Crossing .
Dr. Bird was complimentary of the surface material on the DREAM Playground, which would be helpful in 
absorbing the impact of a falling child, but noted that the equipment in direct sunlight felt hot .  He also 
addressed Mr. Beam's comments, stating that neither his Facebook poll nor the charettes were statistically valid 
due to the number and demographic of people who responded, and that it is challenging to get input on what 
residents really want.
Mayor Anzevino announced that the Western Reserve Community Band will be playing on Sunday at 6:30 p .m. 
with a 250th Anniversary theme, where he will recite the Gettysburg Address; the Hudson Area Chamber of 
Commerce golf outing will take place at Ellsworth Meadows Golf Course; the Biergarten event was 
well-attended; and that Green Valley Brewing Company will re-open on Wednesday after its chiller broke .  
Report of Manager7.
Mr. Sheridan stated that fire renovations and full-time staffing should begin before the end of August, 
renovations at EMS will begin in September, and an open house for the Hines Hill Road Railroad Bridge project 
will be held at City Hall on August 12 from 5:00-7:00 p .m.

A. 26-0064 Brandywine Creek Watershed Study - Model and Final Draft Report
Brief Description:  Staff and the City’s consultant, GPD Group, will present 
the Brandywine Creek Watershed Study development, findings and 
recommendations.
Exhibit - Brandywine Crk Watershed Presentation 8-11-26Attachments:
Mr. Kosco introduced Mr. Jesse Rufener, the City's consultant from GPD Group, who presented the findings of 
the Brandywine Creek Watershed Study.  Mr. Rufener discussed the study area, why the study was needed, 
existing conditions, and a recommended ranking of capital projects .  Discussion followed regarding how the 
ranking list was determined, the impact of what residents can do, storm frequencies, and the budget process .
This matter was discussed.

C. 26-0065 Clinton Crossing Project Update 
Page 4City of Hudson, Ohio
August 11, 2026City Council Meeting Minutes - Final
Brief Description: City staff will be present to provide an update on the 
engagement summary from the July 22nd event and the single preferred 
project concept plan which will be presented to the community on August 
22nd for feedback.
Clinton Crossing memo to Council 8.11.26 final
Clinton Crossing - Input Summary - Site Plan Survey Round 2
CC Site Plan Pulse Check Boards Results Packet - July 22
Attachments:
Mr. Hannan provided an update on the Clinton Crossing project, which included the consultant working 
towards a preferred plan to present to the community on August 22 at the project site .  He indicated that there 
was strong support for small single family homes, and substantial support for townhomes .  For non-residential 
uses, Mr. Hannan stated that Plans 1 and 3 garnered the most support, with elements of a boutique hotel, 
market hall, and community flex space.
There was further discussion on the consultant's high level reviews that all three plans would recoup or 
significantly recover the City's $11 million investment, with the caveat that publicly-owned facilities would 
reduce that number.
Additionally, Mr. Hannan said that a traffic study is underway and paid parking is being studied, however, a 
parking deck is not preferred.  
This matter was discussed.
