26-1346
37 S. Main Street (PNC Bank)
Commercial Alteration (Awnings, Windows, and Vestibule)
Submitted by Michelle Cocos, GPD Group
a) Staff notes the proposed scope of work includes the removal of existing 
windows and installation of new first story glazing systems on west 
elevation, two proposed awnings on west elevation, new entry door and 
storefront glazing system on west and south side elevation bump out, 
relocation of existing exterior lighting on west side elevation, and 
proposed window replacement on second story south side elevation. Staff 
notes the proposed building signage is not part of this application.
b) Staff notes the building was constructed in 1893; however is not located 
within the Historic District or a designated individual landmark.
c) Section IV-4 (e)(2) of the Architectural Design Standards states that wings 
usually have a simple composition that is dependent on the main body. 
Question proposed bump out glazing system at west and south side 
elevation and how it relates to the main mass. Suggest the use of more 
traditional exterior materials to be more compatible with the main body.
d) Question proposed wall material at west-side elevation bump out. 
e) Section IV-4 (g)(1) of the Architectural Design Standards states that roofs 
on projections should match the roof material of the building (unless both 
roofs are flat) and to the extent possible, shall be the same kind of roof. 
Natural finish metals such as copper, terne coated steel, or lead may be 
substituted for any roofing material. Staff notes a metal roofing material is 
proposed.

B. AHBR 8929 80 N Main Street (Hudson’s Restaurant) (Informal Review)
Alteration (Doors and Patio)
Submitted by Kevin Altomare
a) Staff notes the proposed scope of work includes relocation of the front 
entryway door, removal of existing windows and brick columns along the 
east side elevation, expansion of the existing outdoor patio and the 
installation of two overhead doors.
b) Staff notes the building was constructed in 1946.
c) Staff recommends the Board consider the guidance provided in the 
National Parks Service Preservation Brief #11, Rehabilitating Historic 
Storefronts in conjunction with the Secretary of the Interiors Standards for 
Rehabilitation when evaluating the proposed modifications. Preservation 
Brief #11 is attached for the Boards review.
d) Staff notes the intent of the proposed project is to widen the existing 
outdoor patio and install overhead doors for an enhanced outdoor dining 
experience. Question overall compatibility with the surrounding Main 
Street storefronts; however, staff notes the proposed improvements would 
activate the existing building frontage and contribute to a more vibrant 
downtown environment.  
e) The National Park Service Preservation Brief #11 for historic storefronts 
identifies recessed entrances as a traditional storefront feature and 
recommends retaining historically accurate entrance locations. 
Recommend the applicant recess the proposed entryway door a minimum 
of three feet to better meet this requirement.
f) The National Park Service Preservation Brief #11 for historic storefronts 
states the importance of evaluating and retaining existing storefront 
features where possible and that wood may be a more appropriate 
replacement than masonry for storefront framing. Staff recommends the 
Board consider the extent of alterations proposed to the existing brick 
columns, including opportunities to retain or salvage the existing brick as 
part of the proposed improvements. The Board may also wish to consider 
reconstructing the storefront from the existing header downward using 
painted wood or another compatible material to achieve a more cohesive 
design, rather than attempting to replicate the existing brick and mortar.
