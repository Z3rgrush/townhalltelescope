AHBR 26-813 5740 Nottingham Gate Boulevard
Addition (Primary Bedroom & Basement)
5740 Nottingham Gate Blvd - AHBR PacketAttachments:
Ms. Coffman introduced the application by displaying the site plan and reviewing the staff 
comments.
Mr. Michael Langelier of Overhouse Studio explained that the setback of the addition was 
intended to align with the main body of the house as shown in the floor plan. He noted that if 
the addition were required to be set back 18 inches, the house would appear lopsided due to 
the front-facing gable, and a difficult corner condition would be created. Regarding the 
introduction of a new material, it is intended to break up the front façade and complement 
neighboring properties; he stated that the water-table height requirement would be met with a 
revised plan. He added that a window will be included on the east elevation as needed and 
explained that the wall without fenestration is a closet, offering possible alternatives to 
installing a window.
Mr. Andrew Schultz, contractor, described the reason the foundation veneer is lower.
The Board, applicant, and staff discussed: The possibility of allowing the addition to align 
with the main mass of the house rather than being set back 18 inches, roofing considerations 
for allowing the proposed alignment, the potential for adding a window above the first floor, 
and the option of using siding instead of brick.
The Board recommended that the elevations be revised.
A motion was made by Mr. Workley, seconded by Ms. Kenney, that this AHBR 
Application be continued. The motion carried by the following vote:
Aye: Mr. Caputo, Ms. Kenney, Mr. Workley, Ms. Sredinski, Mr. Brown and Ms. 
Putzier

AHBR 26-796 328 W Streetsboro Street
Addition (Front Porch, Family Room, Laundry, Mud Room & Pergola)
328 W Streetsboro Street - AHBR PacketAttachments:
Ms. Coffman introduced the application by displaying and describing the project and 
reviewing the staff comments.
Mr. Jonathan Paul Fleming, architect, described the shed roof on the house and the proposed 
roof above the porch, noting that it is consistent with other projects of this type. He explained 
that the single windows on the second floor are due to the slope of the roof, and that the 
window style carries throughout the structure. He also stated that the front porch column will 
be a double post wrapped with Azek to achieve a simple appearance, composed of 4x4 posts 
wrapped with wood.
The Board, applicant, and staff discussed the appropriateness of the shed roof, agreed that the 
double columns are acceptable, noted that the lower-floor window is smaller than the 
second-floor windows due to the interior layout, and that the wall containing the window is 
only 12 feet, 3 inches wide, suggesting the window may not be necessary.
A motion was made by Ms. Sredinski, seconded by Ms. Kenney, that this AHBR 
Application be approved with the applicant permitted to remove the 27 x 27 window if 
so desired. The motion carried by the following vote:

AHBR 8800 31 Church Street (Historic District) (Informal)
Addition (Covered Parking)
31 Church St - Informal AHBR PacketAttachments:
Ms. Coffman introduced the application by describing and displaying renderings of the 
Page 7City of Hudson, Ohio
August 12, 2026Architectural & Historic Board of Review Meeting Minutes - Final
project and reviewing the staff comments. 
Mr. Kurt Hartung, draftsman, stated: The existing home does not have a garage or covered 
parking, that the rear yard is not accessible for parking, and that this is the only location for 
covered parking. This project will widen the existing roof, with preservation and historical 
details a key part of the development of this plan. Mr. Hartung also noted the existing front 
porch is not covered and in poor condition and is part of the project. 
Mr. Hartung stated the house was built in 1853, that he believes the porch was a later 
addition, and that two other examples of this type of covered porch exist in the neighborhood.  
The Board and Mr. Hartung also discussed that the existing posts look to have been recently 
installed, that the existing steps are wood, that the foundation material is unknown, that the 
existing ceiling material is not plank material, that the proposed roof trim above the porch will 
not match the existing roof trim, that a window will be added to the front of the structure, that 
the front door and window may not be original to the house, that research may be available 
through Hudson Heritage Association, that the step material will either be painted wood or a 
composite tongue and groove porch flooring, that the existing steps and floor materials are not 
historic, that work will be done on the driveway to suit the new parking area, that the Board 
appreciates the degree of detail that supports the historic look of the property, and the Board 
is generally supportive of the plan as presented. 
The Board requested information on the history of the house and the materials to be used.
This matter was discussed
AHBR 8810 211 Aurora Street (Historic District) (Informal)
Addition (Saywell House)
211 Aurora Street - AHBR Informal PacketAttachments:
Ms. Coffman introduced the application by describing the project, displaying renderings, and 
reviewing the staff comments as well as the preservation briefs included in the staff report.
Ms. Allison and Mr. Charles Worthington were present to discuss the project.
Chair Caputo noted that this project represents an opportunity to preserve and save the 
historic house located on the middle school property.
The applicants explained that the two-story connector will allow access to the upper level of 
the house from both sides and it will be constructed for both function and form. 
The Board, applicants, and staff discussed their appreciation for the renderings and noted that 
the Saywell House will likely be raised up so that its floors align with those of the other 
house. It was noted that much of the existing siding from the Saywell House will be reused, 
and that the rear addition is a later addition that does not fit the historic character of the house. 
The Board also discussed that the two houses have different siding, but that the contrast may
