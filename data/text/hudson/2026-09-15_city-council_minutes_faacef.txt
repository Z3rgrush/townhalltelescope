Brief Description:   This legislation authorizes the City Manager to execute a 
Settlement Agreement and Release with Prestige Builder Group, LLC, George

Mr. Sutton left the dais at 7:54 p.m. to sit in the audience.
The public hearing opened at 7:54 p .m.
Mayor Anzevino described the order of events for the public hearing .  
Mr. Michael Cicero, outside legal counsel and legal counsel for the Northern Ohio Risk Management Agency 
(NORMA), of which the City of Hudson is a member, stated that the City's code has a unique provision for an 
administrative review of a Constitutional claim, which was made by the developer and is a precursor to a 
federal lawsuit.  Mr. Cicero said it was at that time that he was asked to review the claim and suggested that the 
City hire Planning Next, a community planning company .
Mayor Anzevino swore in Ms. Sarah Kelly and Ms. Delaney Sillman, consultants from Planning Next, who 
presented findings of the proposed plan and how it complies with the Land Development Code .  Their research 
indicated that the proposed plan complies with the Land Development Code (LDC) requirements, the LDC's 
Open Space Conservation Subdivision fits within the common definition of a Conservation Subdivision, the 
LDC's Open Space Conservation Subdivision fits within the spectrum of requirements in conservation 
subdivision ordinances, and the Open Space Conservation Subdivision requirements compel better design than 
what would be allowed with conventional single-family residential development in the Rural Residential Zone 
District.
Mr. Brezovec asked the consultants what reason a developer would choose a conservation plan instead of use by 
right.  They responded with reasons such as proximity to open space being beneficial to the property values, the 
allowable layout can reduce the amount of right-of-way and set-asides, the amount of money to align utilities, 
market demands, and other projects that developers may be involved in within the same community .  He later 
asked if there is anything in the LDC that would suggest that the proposed plan be altered or denied .  Ms. 
Sillman said no.
Mr. Brezovec and Dr. Goetz then asked Mr. Cicero to further explain how he was brought into the settlement 
discussion and to explain the Constitutional claim .  Mr. Cicero said that a hardship Constitutional claim was 
made pursuant to the City code, which triggered a potential claim for money damages in the future, therefore 
Mr. Pitchford contacted the insurance carrier for the pool of communities .  A local insurance company was 
Page 3City of Hudson, Ohio
September 15, 2026City Council Meeting Minutes - Final
hired to act as an adjuster for the pool and the pool's senior risk manager reached out to Mr . Cicero as chief 
legal counsel.  The process was triggered not by Council, but by the application for a hardship takings claim by 
the developer.  When a claim concerning a Constitutional violation is presented, a federal lawsuit could be 
triggered.
Dr. Weinstein asked Ms. Sillman what happens if the proposed development is considered a use by right instead 
of conditional use.  Ms. Sillman responded there is nothing that prohibits the same number of units going into 
the space, but that the units would cover the entire space not set aside as open space .  Dr. Weinstein later asked 
for a description of conditional use versus use by right .
Dr. Bird confirmed with Ms. Sillman that the City's code does not state a maximum lot size .  He then asked if 
there is a maximum square footage of the homes in other communities .  Ms. Sillman stated that either is 
possible.
