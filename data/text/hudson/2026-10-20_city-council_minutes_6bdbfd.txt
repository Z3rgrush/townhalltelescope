Public Comments6.
Ms. Melissa Jones, 2233 Ravenna St., spoke on the proposed Canterbury Meadows settlement with Prestige. She 
said her earlier concerns had not changed, stated that the proposed settlement relies on incomplete plans that 
could later be changed without public notice, and she argued that it disregards the Charter, the Land 
Development Code, and the Comprehensive Plan's rural residential conservation requirements . She compared 
the plan unfavorably to Prestige's Pine Ridge development on State Route 303 . She also objected, as a former 
Planning Commission member, to email exchanges between Dr. Weinstein and the Planning Commission chair 
attached to the prior night's Planning Commission agenda.
Ms. Caitlin Honniball, 2763 Middleton Road, addressed statements made by the Mayor at the previous Council 
meeting. She requested a correction of the record, and invited other residents with similar experiences to 
contact her.
Ms. Collette Schlemmer, 3080 Ravenna St.,  spoke on the Canterbury Meadows settlement agreement. She said 
the dense layout bordered by a small ring of open land is a weak example of the rural residential conservation 
and open space conservation subdivision concept. She argued that the Planning Commission's rejection should 
require the developer to revise the plan rather than remove the Commission's power . She urged Council to abide 
by the Charter and said approval would permanently alter Hudson's rural character .
Ms Kim Cosma Segedy, 2242 Ravenna St. spoke on behalf of Leonore Cosma, questioned the developer's 
hardship claim, citing two cash offers on the property and the developer's experience with conservation design 
at Pine Ridge. She raised a concern about a perceived conflict of interest involving Ms. D'Eramo, asked Council 
to reconsider the development as structured, require thorough review of drainage and wetlands, preserve public

oversight of future plan changes, and obtain a clear legal determination of the Planning Commission's 
authority. She asked whether the next public hearing would be new or a continuation of September's, and why 
surrounding landowners had to hire private counsel.
Ms. Cynthia Curtin, 1102 Cutler Lane, spoke on the Clinton Crossing concept plan. She said the proposed 
number of housing units is too high and would add traffic to the Owen Brown Street and Morse Road 
intersection and the one-lane railroad underpass. She asked about possible traffic controls and noted that traffic 
and infrastructure studies were forthcoming.
Mr. Todd Zedak, 16 Hudson Common, criticized recent Council actions, including the revocation of a former 
Council member's censure, the process for public input on the Phase 2 property disposition, and efforts 
regarding the Planning Commission's authority and a Planning Commission member . 
Ms. Maureen Hulbert, 2200 Ravenna St., stated her belief that Council Member D'Eramo has a conflict of 
interest in the Canterbury Meadows matter and should have recused herself from the outset . She also questioned 
the hardship claim and objected to bypassing the Charter, Land Development Code, and Planning Commission 
process.
Mr. Anthony Ravida, 1746 Edgar Dr., spoke on oversight and accountability.
Correspondence and Council Comments   7.
Dr. Goetz stated that Council had not been informed that Tucker Ellis represented Prestige until recently, which 
is why a recusal had not occurred earlier.  She also reported on a recent tour of the O'Brien Cemetery.
Ms. D'Eramo reported on the October 15th Board of Zoning and Building Appeals meeting .  She also stated that 
she would recuse herself from further discussions regarding the Prestige development due to her brother's 
employment at Tucker Ellis.  
Dr. Weinstein said the Charter guarantees residents the right to address Council but is silent on businesses and 
non-residents. She asked Council to consider a procedural change allowing Hudson business owners to speak .  
She also addressed concerns about Planning Commission attendance, bringing in an expert to provide training 
on the Land Development Code, and making changes to the Land Development Code in order for it to reflect 
what residents want.
Mr. Brezovec clarified that the public hearing on the Canterbury Meadows settlement agreement was a Council 
decision rather than a legal mandate, so no further hearing is required before the vote, though Council could 
choose to hold one. Regarding Clinton Crossing, he addressed the number of housing units and said staff is 
preparing a plan for the Planning Commission, to be accompanied by a full traffic study .
Mayor Anzevino congratulated Laurie Gross as the September winner of his kindness challenge, with one more 
kindness challenge planned for October. He also congratulated the inductees of the Hudson High School 
Distinguished Alumni Hall of Fame. He noted that he would be in attendance for the Clayton Rakes ALS 
fundraiser on Sunday, the Take a Second, Make a Difference Award luncheon, and the monthly area mayors' 
meeting later in the week.
Report of Manager8.
Mr. Griffith stated that the Hudson PTO will be sponsoring the annual storefront window painting event 
October 24 and 25, residential leaf pickup will commence October 26, Merchants Trick-or-Treat will occur on 
October 29 from 5:00-7:00 p.m., pumpkin composting will take place from October 31 to December 31 with a 
bin located in the back parking lot of Hudson City Hall, and citywide trick-or-treat will be October 31 from 
6:00-8:00 p.m.
