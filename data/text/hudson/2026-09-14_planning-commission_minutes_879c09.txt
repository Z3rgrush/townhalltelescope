A. DISC PRESTIGEPlanning Commission discussion of the Prestige Settlement Agreement
PacketAttachments:
Chair Norman noted that several Commissioners agreed it was important to hold this discussion early in the 
meeting.  She reviewed the language of the Settlement Agreement and the Planning Commission’s response 
resolution, which was included in full within the meeting agenda.  She noted the Planning Commission resolution 
was reviewed by legal Council.   The resolution; Requests independent legal counsel for the Planning 
Commission; Requests certain members of city staff and Council members be disqualified from further 
involvement in the matter; Requests that Council postpone the upcoming public hearing for the matter; Rejects 
the settlement agreement on multiple fronts; Requests City Council pursue remedies in the courts; and issues a 
resolution of "no-confidence" in the presently seated Council.    
The Commissioners discussed the need for greater transparency regarding the application, particularly related to 
traffic impacts at the nearby intersection and downstream water issues that may not have been fully addressed by 
the City Engineer. It was frequently mentioned that both a Planning Commission member and a City Council 
member live near the proposed development and had expressed concern about water issues, which may have 
caused the applicant to believe political factors were influencing the process. Commissioner Romano stated that 
he had received no communication from any Council member on this matter and acknowledged awareness that 
Mr. Vizmeg has been active in supporting members of the current City Council. He further observed that while 
the development could potentially be brought into compliance, the proposed settlement does not meet 
development standards. He expressed concern about what he viewed as political and possibly vindictive tensions 
between City Council members and Planning Commission members. He stated that he does not understand why 
Page 3City of Hudson, Ohio
September 14, 2026Planning Commission Meeting Minutes - Final
City Council is considering the proposed settlement, that he sees little risk to the City from an appeal judgment, 
and that approving such a settlement sets a troubling precedent. He added that the optics suggest City Council’s 
involvement may be politically motivated.
Commissioners directed numerous questions to staff regarding the legal process associated with the Settlement 
Agreement. Staff explained that, based on the advice of counsel, they were not able to respond. Commissioners 
also questioned why City Council is involved in this matter rather than directing the applicant to return to the 
Planning Commission to bring the development into compliance with the Land Development Code. Mr. Nystrom 
outlined additional concerns, including potential implications for future applicants, City Council’s encroachment 
on the Planning Commission’s decision-making role, the involvement of Council Members who were supported 
by the applicant, and the lack of disclosure of those relationships. He emphasized that Planning Commission 
members have not involved themselves in politics or favoritism and urged City Council members to do the same.
Ms. Smith read a prepared statement affirming her commitment to the Land Development Code and her 
responsibilities as a Commissioner. She noted that Commissioners should not involve themselves in the City’s 
legal matters and may not be aware of all information available to City Council. She stated she would comment 
only on applications formally before the Planning Commission.
Mr. Innamorato stated that he believes the appeal process should proceed in the normal manner. Ms. McCoy 
expressed her belief that Prestige is capable of developing the property through the standard Planning 
Commission process. Mr. Romano responded that he had participated in two meetings with the City Solicitor 
regarding the City’s strategy and believes that Ms. Smith’s comments were inaccurate.
Discussion followed, including acknowledgment that Planning Commission members may not agree with every 
comment contained within the recommendations.
Mr. Nystrom moved, seconded by Mr. Innamorato, that the resolution included in the agenda 
be forwarded to City Council prior to the next Council meeting. The motion was approved by 
the following vote:

Mr. Adam Fishman of Fairmont Properties was present for the discussion.
The Commissioners, staff, and the applicant addressed a resident’s question regarding why the proposed 
townhomes are three stories. Mr. Fishman explained that the three-story design reflects the quality and character 
of the community, allows for units with smaller footprints, makes the project economically viable, and is 
consistent with nearby buildings. The Commissioners also discussed the landscape plan and expressed concern 
about the long-term effects of road salt on plantings near the street.
Mr. David Rapp of Hudson Engineering described the type of basin planned for the development. Mr. Jeff 
Jardine of Riverstone Company added that the basins are designed to accommodate the development’s water 
needs and noted that they are dry basins. Further discussion addressed the use of retention basins, which conflict 
with the Land Development Code. Mr. Rapp explained the reasons for using retention basins in this situation, 
despite the Code’s preference for alternative types. He clarified that construction staging may occur in the valet 
lot and that a temporary sanitary pump station may be needed until the Summit County system is confirmed as 
adequate.
Mr. Brice Hamill, architect, explained that the proposed wood porch railings are intended to reflect the heritage 
of the surrounding area while balancing a willingness to use modern materials. Mr. Fishman added that the wood 
finish is appealing to many homeowners. The Commissioners noted a conflict between the elevations shown on 
page 24 and the landscape plan for the tree lawns. Mr. Hamill clarified that the elevations are not an exact 
representation of the final conditions. Mr. Fishman stated that the HOA documents have not yet been completed.

Mr. Sugar introduced the Site Plan Review, noting that the application was continued from the August meeting 
and highlighting the changes included in the updated staff report.
Mr. Jeff Durbon of TLE stated that the company is in agreement with the staff recommendations.
The Commissioners, applicant, and staff discussed the dry retention basin and related safety measures, including 
fencing and landscaping. Ms. Smith referenced LDC Section 1207.07, which cites guidance from the Center for 
Watershed Protection recommending fencing around ponds, safety benching, and specific side-slope standards.
Mr. Jeff Jardine of Riverstone Company explained that the retention pond meets the required slope standards, 
described the differences between retention and detention basins, the functional levels of the pond, and his 
intention to recheck applicable codes for full compliance. Commissioners discussed whether fencing should 
extend around the entire detention pond. Mr. Jardine noted the difficulties this may pose, and Commissioners 
acknowledged that full fencing would result in fencing along a public road at a gateway entry to the City. Mr. 
Durbon added that TLE, as a corporate entity with 460 sites, considers the pond design safe. Chair Norman 
recognized the challenge of balancing safety considerations with aesthetic and functional concerns. Mr. Durbon 
also reported that a 12-inch stormwater pipe from the 1950s, shown on records, could not be located during 
excavation. He stated that both a local general contractor and TLE’s internal estimators are confident in the 
project’s budget estimates.
Discussion continued on several additional topics. Regarding snow storage, Mr. Jardine noted that landscaping

has been placed farther from the parking lot to provide adequate room. Commissioners discussed the need for a 
walkway connecting the site to the public sidewalk. Noise levels at the playground were reviewed, with Mr. 
Durbon noting they are expected to remain under 60 decibels at the fence and that the playground will close at 3 
p.m. The Industrial Design Subcommittee previously suggested moderating the sign colors; Mr. Durbon 
presented an alternative sign featuring black-and-white lettering while retaining the mascot character.  
Commissioners reviewed the design of the playground doors, confirmed that a landscaping bond will be required,

The Commissioners discussed their differing views on whether fencing should be required around the retention

branding in some areas, shared her concerns about water conditions on the property, the potential convenience of 
the site for residents who need daycare choices, and the potential tax benefits to the citizens of Hudson.
The Commissioners further considered the option of requiring a fence and the possibility that the applicant might 
request a variance should fencing be desired by the applicant. They also discussed the impact such fencing could 
have on the appearance of a key gateway into the City.

Mr. Sugar introduced the application by reviewing the previous approval, the progress made to date, the 
revisions requested by the applicant, and the four staff recommendations.
Chair Norman and Mr. Sugar discussed the revisions to the previously approved plans.
Mr. Innamorato summarized the Industrial Design Subcommittee’s review of the project. He stated that he was 
unaware that the brick columns from the previous design were eliminated and noted that new ownership is now 
leading the development. He explained that the approved plans held by the Subcommittee differ from what was 
constructed on the first building. He reported that the Subcommittee discussed requiring additional bonding for 
the project and expressed concern about the extended phasing timeline. He concluded that the Subcommittee 
recommended continuing the application.
Mr. Charles Wolf, 3600 Chapelton Court, Richfield, Ohio, explained that the original investors now hold 
minority ownership, with majority ownership held by himself and another investor. He stated that he became an 
owner in April 2026, described the project as being in disarray at that time, and outlined the work completed 
since April. He identified a principal issue in Phase 1: each unit had effectively become a custom design, a 
practice he intends to discontinue moving forward. He noted that existing tenants are unhappy with the slow 
progress and expressed his desire to bring the project back on track. Mr. Wolf also stated his belief that the new 
setback requirements do not apply to the project because construction has been ongoing.
Regarding construction, Mr. Wolf stated that the project was always intended to be phased and described the 
upcoming construction phases. He further detailed the concerns raised by existing tenants over delays and 
reiterated his desire to resume timely development. He questioned the need for an additional variance.
Solicitor Thomas clarified that once a permit expires, the code requires any new permit to meet all current 
standards.

numerous times to engage the previous owners about the condition of the project. Staff confirmed that the new

Mr. Mark Crowder, Medina, Ohio, stated that the landscape budget is between $20,000 and $30,000, and

Mr. Mark Leszynski, project designer, stated he received a stamped, approved set of drawings that did not 
include rear doors and that he does not know when they were removed from the plans. The Board stated that 
complete copies of the approved plans need to be submitted to staff. Mr. Wolf confirmed that no rear doors are 
planned for any of the units and described the landscaping and screening efforts.
