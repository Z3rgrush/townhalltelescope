Dr. Bird and Mrs. Norman, on behalf of City Council and Planning Commission, respectively, made 
introductory remarks.  Mr. Hannan provided a brief orientation, described the purpose of the meeting, and 
timeline of the process.
Mr. Sugar commented on the Comprehensive Plan as the framework for the land uses at this site and 
emphasized its alignment in guiding future development .  He explained the definitions of a Concept Plan Review 
and a Planned Development Review, noting that a Planned Development Review may be a better fit for this 
project and allow for easier tracking by the public .  He further discussed the architectural design of the 
development.
Ms. McCoy noted her involvement as a member of the Comprehensive Plan Steering Committee and cautioned 
against building apartments at the site, as this type of housing was not preferred by the community .  She also 
had questions about what was categorized as "co-working space".  Responding to the comment about 
apartments, Mr. Sugar indicated that duplexes, quads, and apartments did not receive favorable public 
feedback.  Mr. Hannan commented on the intent to focus on a process discussion initially, but added that 
interest had been expressed in those uses by private entities which could be combined into a market hall 
concept.
Dr. Weinstein clarified that the public was able to select pieces of each plan they preferred and that then would 
help develop a final plan.  
Ms. Obert asked when a determination would be made if the application would be for a Concept Review Plan or 
Planned Development.  Mr. Sugar responded that the decision would be made when there is a single concept 
plan and it can be reviewed in a pre-application meeting .  Ms. Obert further stated that the subcommittee has 
met to discuss Planned Development language in the Land Development Code, and requested direction from 
Council to focus their work with a tight timeline.  She also asked Mr. Sugar to distribute an architectural pattern 
book to Council and Planning Commission .
Dr. Goetz asked if the Architectural and Historic Board of Review would be required to utilize an architectural 
pattern book. Ms. De’Eramo requested clarification on architectural pattern books and Mr . Sugar noted that it 
specifically guide architectural designs. Mrs. Norman added that Shaker Heights implemented this tool in the 
early 1900s. 
Further discussion followed regarding the Request for Qualifications ( RFQ) and Request for Proposals (RFP) 
process, when to engage a developer, and the process by which First & Main was completed .  Mr. Innamorato 
recommended the RFQ/RFP process require a conceptual design to evaluate how the developer's vision aligns 
with the City's.  
Responding to a question, Mr. Griffith stated that the final plan will be presented to Bricker Graydon Wyatt LLP 
for financial viability review.
Ms. Fernandez summarized comments on each of the three plans that were narrowed down from the previous 17 
plans, stating that for residential, the preference was for Plan #1, including single family homes, townhomes, 
and duplexes and that this was overall preferred use plan .  The plan also featured a market hall and community 
center.  Plan #3 was preferred for non-residential uses, which included a boutique hotel and market hall .  She 
also discussed residents' preferred amenities, future hopes for the space, and the importance of recovering the 
City's initial investment of $11 million.  Ms. Fernandez further stated that the final plan will include only single 
family and townhomes, market hall with reduced size, boutique hotel, flex space, and pedestrian and green

Mrs. Norman examined the allowable uses for District 5, which must include uses permitted by right or by 
conditional use, and stated that a "market hall" is not included or defined .  There was consensus that the code 
would need to be updated.  She further detailed the layers of review that the final plan could require .  Mrs. 
Norman then summarized the work of the subcommittee regarding Planned Developments, which included 
limiting Planned Developments to District 5, 7, 7 Overlay, 8 with conditions, and 11; re-draft the purpose 
statement, and upon Council's approval, develop three sets of Planned Development guidelines .  There was 
consensus to work on planned development and density as it relates to Clinton Crossing .
Mrs. Norman noted that Planning Commission requested legal review of the legislative and administrative 
decisions as it relates to approval process.
This matter was discussed.
