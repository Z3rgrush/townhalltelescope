Mr. Sugar introduced the application by Crosslands Companies for a 10,000-square-foot building in District 6 on 
2.7 acres. He noted that the project requires both a Conditional Use Review and a Major Site Plan Review. The 
13 items identified at the previous Planning Commission meeting were reviewed along with the applicant’s 
revisions and staff’s comments and recommendations. He also stated that the Industrial Design Subcommittee 
recommends approval and outlined the conditions that may be approved by staff. Staff additionally recommends 
approval with further conditions, which he summarized.
Ms. Kim Tatsch of Crosslands Companies and Jeff Jardine of Riverstone Companies were present. The 
applicants stated that comments from earlier reviews have been incorporated into the revisions, that the team will 
address the staff recommendations from the staff report but notes clarification is needed for some of the items.
The Commissioners, applicant, and staff discussed the State of Ohio licensing process, fencing, and landscaping. 
Page 3City of Hudson, Ohio
August 24, 2026Planning Commission Meeting Minutes - Final
Mr. Sugar reviewed the applicable parking and landscaping codes and requirements. The applicant noted that 
parking has been shifted farther from the road which lowered the parking area and allows more practical 
landscaping.
Discussion continued regarding a 12-inch HPD pipe that does not appear in any city or county records. The 
applicant explained that the stormwater plan will not route HPD water into their retention basin and that they 
have contingency plans should the pipe be located. Soil borings have been completed but were not provided to 
the Planning Commission. The site is not balanced and will require exporting approximately 576 cubic yards of 
soil. The applicant described options for relocating the 2-inch sanitary force main, installation of the water line 
under the road and anticipated traffic impacts, the water meter location, erosion-reducing rock channels at 
headwalls, underdrains with vertical curbs, property-wide water migration, grading, and storm sewer and 
underdrain use. Additional topics included brick screening at the dumpsters, lighting height and timing, 
coordination of emergency access and child safety, planning for 100-year storm events, fencing locations, traffic 
concerns regarding left turns, estimated site-work and project costs, feasibility of underground water detention, 
additional drawing details needed, landscaping adjustments based on pond relocation and fencing changes, new 
legislation affecting utility boring under roads, and willingness to work with an arborist on the 48-inch oak tree. 
An updated landscape plan will be submitted.
Chair Obert opened the floor for public comments from citizens with or without standing. No public comments 
were offered.
The Commissioners, applicants, and staff discussed security measures and noted a potential architectural drawing 
error involving one missing door.
The applicant stated that additional stormwater and cost information will be provided to the Commissioners and 
noted the possibility of obtaining a building permit by March 2027.
Owner, Ryan Kane, of 1689 East Saphire Drive, described his and his sister’s relevant experience in child-care 
operations. Ms. Kane stated that they are pursuing this particular business because it is the leading child-care 
educational provider in the country.
The Commissioners discussed the need for additional stormwater information before making a decision, 
expressed appreciation for the redesigned building and efforts to preserve trees, and reviewed comments from 
Engineering regarding stormwater capacity and flow. Mr. Dave Rapp, Assistant City Engineer, explained that the 
stormwater pond has sufficient storage capacity, described water paths on and off the property, addressed the 
uncertain status of the 12-inch pipe, and clarified that offsite water can pass through without detention. 
Snow-storage plans were also discussed.
Mr. Sugar noted that PC could approve the Conditional Use application and accept the Industrial 
Subcommittee’s recommendation for design.  The Major Site Plan request could be continued for the applicant to 
provide the requested updates.  
A motion was made by Mr. Nystrom, seconded by Mr. Innamorato, that the Conditional Use 
application be approved and accept the Industrial Design Subcommittee's recommendations.  
The Major Site Plan request shall be continued to the September 14, 2026 meeting. The motion

Chair Obert noted that the meeting will end at 10 p.m., and that the hearing will most likely need to be continued 
to a future meeting.
Mr. Sugar introduced the application, describing the proposed 18-unit townhome development and summarizing 
the staff report and recommendations.
Mr. Adam Fishman of Fairmont Properties presented a PowerPoint overview to the Commissioners, including 
previously granted variances and the project’s Open Space requirements.
Chair Obert opened the floor for public comments from individuals with standing.
Mr. Ron Stroble, of 24 Owen Brown Street, stated that he was unaware of the proposed development, expressed 
concerns that a three-story building is incompatible with Owen Brown Street, and objected to ingress to the 
development from Owen Brown. It was noted that Mr. Stroble does not technically have standing.
With no additional speakers with standing, Chair Obert opened comments to those without standing. No further 
comments were offered.
The Commissioners, applicant, and staff discussed appreciation for the project’s redesign, phasing of 
underground work, and construction sequencing. It was noted that six to nine homes are planned for the first

phase, though most underground work will occur during a single phase. Mr. Brice Hamill, Fairmount Properties, 
described potential equipment and material staging approaches for phase two. Staff confirmed that Fairmont 
owns the adjacent parking lot that will be used for staging. The group also discussed possible alternative ingress 
points onto Owen Brown Street, noting that original plans included options, and that adding nine homes is not 
expected to significantly impact traffic on Owen Brown.
Ms. Suzanne Meltzer of RDL Architects noted that previous Planning Commission feedback requested that 
homes along Owen Brown Street resemble individual houses and include greenspace, which required relocating a 
driveway away from Owen Brown.
Mr. Fishman stated that each unit can accommodate an elevator. Mr. Hamill explained architectural differences 
from the Clinton Street project, emphasizing that the proposed development sought alignment with surrounding 
streetscapes and increased open space. Mr. Fishman added that, with 23 years of experience since the Clinton 
Street project, the firm has improved its design approach. Additional discussion included the former use and age 
of the existing metal building, allocation of trail funds in place of unbuilt land, missing drawing details, the 
Geotechnical report and soil conditions, site preparation, the estimated $600,000 site-work cost, reliability of 
traffic-study assumptions, and the need for the Clinton Crossing traffic study to allow a holistic view of all the 
development that may take place in this area. Staff noted that because this project precedes Clinton Crossing, it 
must be considered independently. They also clarified that Clinton Street open-space standards were part of the 
broader First & Main development requirements.
At this time, because of time constraints, the application was continued to the following meeting.
