26-1052
5 Baldwin Street (Historic District)
Alteration (Siding, Windows & Awning)
Submitted by Nathan Zampelli
a) Appendix 1 (6) of the Secretary of Interior Standards state “deteriorated 
historic features shall be repaired rather than replaced. Where the severity 
of deterioration requires replacement of a distinctive feature, the new 
feature shall match the old in design, color, texture, and other visual 
qualities and, where possible, materials. Replacement of missing features 
shall be substantiated by documentary, physical, or pictorial evidence.” 
b) Staff suggests the applicant take this meeting to discuss the proposed 
project with the Board and verify the scope of work.
c) Staff suggests the Board utilize this meeting for questions for the applicant 
and to set a time for a site visit.
d) Staff notes the applicant is seeking approval for JELD-WEN Siteline series 
windows for the replacement of historic materials. Staff notes that the 
proposed window series is not currently included on the AHBR-approved 
materials list for replacement windows on historic structures. Suggest the 
applicant bring a sample window to the meeting for the Board to review.
e) Question the appropriateness of the proposed transom windows on the 
historic carriage house.

AHBR 26-643 2200 Middleton Rd
Addition (Living Room & Primary Suite)
Submitted by Quinn Miller, Peninsula Architects
a) Section IV-4 (d)(3) of the Architectural Design Standards state that the 
materials used in any mass must be applied consistently on that mass on 
all sides of the structure. Staff notes the applicant is proposing brick siding 
on the left and rear elevations that would not be applied around the entire 
mass. Revise elevations to meet this requirement. 
b) Section IV-4 (e)(4) of the Architectural Design Standards state that the 
building shall have a typical window used for most windows. Staff notes a 
mix of fixed and casement windows are proposed, while the main mass has 
double hung.
c) Section III-1(g)(3) of the Architectural Design Standards states exposed 
foundations and tie courses shall be consistent on all elevations. Revise 
elevations to depict a more consistently applied exposed foundation on all 
elevations.
d) Section III-1(g)(8) of the Architectural Design Standards state that large 
expanses of blank wall are to be avoided. Fenestration placement should 
be at a maximum of approximately every 12 feet. Revise left elevation to 
meet this requirement. 
e) Question proposed panel on right side elevation and how it relates to the 
main mass.

AHBR 
26-1143
6000 Pine Ridge Trail
New House (Single-Family Dwelling)
Submitted by Chris Brown
a) Verify grade line is accurately depicted on submitted elevations.
b) Verify proposed wall material for covered front porch.
c) Section IV-(d)(3) of the Architectural Design Standards states the 
materials used in any mass must be applied consistently on that mass on 
all sides of the structure. Staff notes that the applicant is proposing stone 
on the front and right-side elevations that would not be applied around the 
main mass. Revise elevations to meet this requirement. Suggest the 
proposed stone be reduced to depict a consistent water table around the 
structure.
d) Section IV-(e)(4) of the Architectural Design Standards states that the 
building shall have a typical window used for most windows. Revise 
elevations to depict a more consistent window style.
e) Section IV-(f)(1) of the Architectural Design Standards states that details 
in the main body must be consistently applied throughout all sides of the 
main body. Revise elevations to depict proposed shutters and grid pattern 
consistently applied around the entire mass.
f) Section III-1 (d) of the Architectural Design Standards states that large 
expanses of blank wall are to be avoided. Fenestration placement should 
be at a maximum of approximately 12 feet. Question if additional 
fenestration is needed on left-side elevation to better meet the requirement. 
g) Section IV-4 (c) of the Architectural Design Standards state that roofs on 
projections should match the roof material of the building (unless both 
roofs are flat) and to the extent possible, shall be same kind of roof. 
Natural finish metals such as copper, terne coated steel, or lead may be 
substituted for any roofing material. Staff notes a metal roofing material is 
proposed. Question if proposed shed roof and dormer on front elevation 
should be metal to match the proposed awning.
