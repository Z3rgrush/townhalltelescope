Mr. Hannan introduced Assistant City Manager Brian Griffith who noted that City Manager Thom Sheridan was 
attending a conference out of state. Mr. Griffith stated that staff views this project as a collaborative community 
effort. He described the City-owned property, the current proposal, and the City's approximately $11 million 
investment in the site. He also reviewed previous development plans that were never implemented, the history of 
the current proposal, the decision for the project to be City-led rather than developer-led, the selection of a 
development partner who may recommend modifications to the plan, and the project's status as in the second year 
of a five-year process.
Ms. Emily McKinley, City of Hudson, reviewed previous site studies, due diligence efforts, and their findings . 
She described the site's wetlands, soil conditions, and projected soil-bearing capacity . She also reviewed 
completed environmental studies, which identified no significant environmental concerns, and noted that 
well-monitoring data is expected by the end of the year. Additional topics included the ongoing traffic study, 
district-wide parking capacity that was generally found to be adequate, sanitary sewer service that remains 
contingent upon a memorandum of understanding currently being developed by Summit County, and plans by 
Hudson Public Power to relocate power lines underground.
Ms. McKinley also reviewed market feasibility studies related to a hotel and market hall, as well as financial 
projections addressing the project's tax base and long-term viability.
Ms. McKinley discussed the public engagement process, including outreach efforts and informational signage 
installed on the property. She reviewed how the Comprehensive Plan's land use recommendations were 
incorporated into the Clinton Crossing concept. She also described the August 20, 2026, meetings with City 
Council and the Planning Commission, during which anticipated development uses were presented, along with 
subsequent meetings with the public. Ms. McKinley explained how the community was kept informed throughout 
the planning process and reviewed public feedback regarding the proposal. She also described efforts to 
incorporate Hudson's history, scale, open space, and character into the development concept .
Ms. McKinley displayed the proposed land use map, showing the developed land, the wetlands and open space 
areas, which she stated is generally consistent with the Comprehensive Plan's recommendations .
Mr. Sugar reviewed the proposed development program, which includes 33 single-family homes, 44 townhomes, 
a market hall, a hotel, and community flex space. He then displayed the site plan and described its overall layout 
and organization.
The Commissioners and staff discussed the proposed privately operated market hall; survey results, which 
included approximately 145 responses; and whether vehicle queuing could become a concern when the project is 
fully developed. Staff noted that the ongoing traffic study and City engineering review will evaluate those issues.

The discussion also included expectations that the market hall will provide public restrooms and whether Hudson 
has sufficient demand for the public components of the project. Staff stated that public demand and consultant 
analyses support those uses.
The Commissioners and staff also discussed the potential impact of the market hall on existing restaurants and 
the possibility of attracting food types not currently represented in the community. Additional topics included 
potential limitations on lighting and noise, public engagement efforts centered on the Comprehensive Plan's 
medium-density land use recommendations, and the fact that participants were not directed toward any specific 
development intensity. Staff noted that public feedback did not approach the maximum density permitted under 
the Comprehensive Plan.
Staff explained that the traffic study will evaluate more than 30 intersections and is intended to align with 
previous traffic analyses for comparisons sake. The study will also incorporate the previously approved 18 
residential units. Several Commissioners expressed appreciation for the extensive public involvement associated 
with the project. Commissioners also commented that the residential and commercial portions of the 
development should be more closely integrated, that the proposed plaza may be too small to accommodate 
anticipated activities, and that a playground may be more appropriate within the residential area than near the 
hotel. The need for a parking study was also discussed. Commissioners expressed appreciation for the effort to 
engage the community and incorporate Comprehensive Plan recommendations. When asked about the schedule 
for the Summit County memorandum of understanding, Mr. Hannan stated that the County continues to work on 
the agreement.
The Commissioners and staff discussed ongoing groundwater and gas well testing intended to identify trace 
amounts of gas that are not expected to present significant concerns. Discussion also addressed whether portions 
of the Category 1 wetlands could be filled, potential homeowner insurance considerations, and lots that may 
present construction challenges because of roadway curvature. Staff stated that the City's consultant expects 
developer interest in the project and sees value in having a single developer construct the entire development . 
Staff also noted that hotel planning has focused primarily on room counts, with additional details to be developed 
later in the process. Commissioners identified traffic and density as primary concerns and also questioned how 
nearby property values, particularly on Owen Brown Street, may be affected.
The Commissioners requested examples of existing 60-foot-wide lots elsewhere in Hudson . Staff noted 
consultant estimates indicating townhome prices of approximately $700,000 and single-family home prices of 
approximately $900,000. Commissioners also expressed appreciation for the planning process and stated their 
hope that the project will be successfully completed.
Regarding soil remediation, staff stated that the future developer is expected to prepare and implement any 
required remediation plans. While additional studies remain necessary, staff indicated that available data does not 
suggest significant remediation challenges. Mr. Hannan noted that previous studies have not identified substantial 
concerns, although site-specific investigations will still be required. Several Commissioners expressed a 
preference for completing additional soil studies before the project advances.
The Commissioners and staff discussed the proposed sanitary sewer agreement with Summit County and whether

Administrative Rules DiscussionAttachments:
Chair Norman introduced the topic, noting email correspondence from City Council's Planning Commission 
liaison regarding discussions between Commissioners and applicants.  
Chair Norman noted that planning commissions are common throughout Ohio and are typically composed of 
citizens or a combination of citizens and elected officials, with no formal training requirements. Chair Norman 
emphasized the Citizen Nature of Planning Commissions.
Chair Norman reviewed the concerns raised in the liaison's emails, including suggestions that discussions with 
applicants should be more closely managed by the Chair, be shorter in duration, or involve fewer questions . The 
Chair stated that the Planning Commission's role is to ensure due process, consider the full body of evidence 
presented with an application, evaluate the credibility of evidence and testimony, and apply the law 
appropriately. She emphasized the importance of considering a variety of perspectives as part of the 
Commission's deliberative process.
Page 10City of Hudson, Ohio
October 19, 2026Planning Commission Meeting Minutes - Final
Chair Norman also expressed concern that the liaison's emails could be perceived as an attempt to influence or 
intimidate members of the Planning Commission. She reviewed the issues raised in the correspondence and 
discussed procedural changes implemented during her five years on the Commission . Chair Norman stated that 
she welcomes dissenting opinions, differing votes, and questions, including those that may not appear directly 
related to the immediate discussion. She also noted that both staff and the Planning Commission play important 
roles in the review process and that collaboration is essential, as staff recommendations may occasionally require 
further examination or discussion.
Chair Norman reviewed the Planning Commission's work during the past year, noting that approximately 75 
percent of its decisions resulted in approvals. She also discussed the reasons for the three applications that were 
denied and stated that all applicants are treated fairly.
Commissioners expressed concern that City Council may be seeking to limit or alter the role of the Planning 
Commission. They also noted that training opportunities had not been provided to Commissioners. Several 
Commissioners stated that each member contributes different experiences and perspectives to Commission 
discussions. Commissioners further noted that previous requests for information, support, or direction from City 
Council have not received responses. The Commissioners also thanked staff for their daily work and expressed 
concern that staff appeared to be included in some of the criticism directed toward the Planning Commission .
Commissioners expressed disappointment with what they viewed as a misunderstanding of the Planning 
Commission's responsibilities. They noted that review of civil plans, financing, project budgets, and 
construction-related matters can be relevant to understanding an application's feasibility and impacts . 
Commissioners stated that part of the Planning Commission's role is to support City Council; however, some felt 
that recent communications from Council were focused on criticizing the Commission rather than supporting its 
work.
Mr. Romano stated that his legal and accounting background provides him with a perspective that helps identify 
potential concerns in governmental processes. He expressed concern that some communications could be 
interpreted as attempts to discourage or limit discussion by Commissioners.
Mr. Innamorato noted that a recent daycare applicant thanked the Planning Commission for identifying an 
omission in its utility plans. He also stated that representatives of Fairmont Properties expressed appreciation for 
the Commission's suggestions regarding project phasing.
Chair Norman stated that she had not heard any Commissioner express support for amending the Administrative 
Rules to limit either the number or type of questions asked during meetings. She then asked Solicitor Thomas if 
he wished to comment. Solicitor Thomas offered no comments.
