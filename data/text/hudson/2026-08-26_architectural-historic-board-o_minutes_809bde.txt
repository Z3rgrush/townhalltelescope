Staff Memo
86 Owen Brown Street - AHBR Packet
86 Owen Brown Street - Site Plan Submittal

Attachments:
Mr. Sugar opened the discussion by noting that AHBR conducted an informal review on July 
22, 2026. He confirmed that the applicant had attended several Planning Commission 
meetings, that the site plan was publicly available, and that staff comments were under review.
Mr. Adam Fishman, Co-Principal, attended the meeting with Mr. Brice Hammel and Ms. 
Suzanne Meltzer of Fairmont Properties. Mr. Fishman presented Fairmont’s formal proposal, 
outlining existing development conditions, a site overview, and the previously reviewed 
conceptual design. He explained that the project had been reduced from 22 to 18 units, 
creating more breaks between buildings and fewer street-facing facades. He also noted that 
the BZBA granted two sets of variances. His presentation covered the landscape plan, the 
goal of creating a luxury living environment, the effort to ensure compatibility with 
Page 3City of Hudson, Ohio
August 26, 2026Architectural & Historic Board of Review Meeting Minutes - Final
surrounding neighborhoods, and Fairmont’s broader vision for Clinton Corners.
Renderings of the proposed development, photographs of nearby structures, and a summary of 
how AHBR’s informal comments were incorporated were included. Mr. Fishman also 
described how AHBR-recommended changes affected internal functionality, raised concerns 
about smaller and less usable outdoor decks, and responded to the LDC’s strict interpretation 
of townhome requirements. He stated that their design could outperform the LDC’s two-story 
interpretation by allowing a more functional top floor. He acknowledged the LDC’s 35-foot 
height limit and noted that AHBR could consider a height waiver. Mr. Fishman referenced the 
strong market performance of Fairmont’s previous Hudson townhomes, highlighted design 
continuity, and emphasized that the proposed homes aim to attract current Hudson residents. 
He explained Fairmont’s request for waivers to permit three-story units and how these waivers 
might meet the five required conditions. He also described Fairmont’s 25-year history 
developing $2.5 billion in townhome projects and the experience gained in maximizing 
interior usability. He said many features were requested by the Planning Commission and 
stressed that Fairmont would not reduce quality to lower costs. He cautioned that strict 
adherence to the LDC would result in uniform massing.
The Board, applicant, and staff discussed concerns about three-story massing, especially 
along Owen Brown Street, where most homes are one-and-a-half to two stories. It was 
suggested that three-story units might be better located farther from the street. Mr. Fishman 
noted the distance between the proposed buildings and Owen Brown Street and stressed that 
certain trade-offs may be necessary to reach a balanced solution. The Board pointed out that 
many nearby houses are smaller than those shown in Fairmont’s materials. Mr. Fishman noted 
that the project begins on Owen Brown Street with a two-story unit designed to fit the scale of 
the street. Mr. Fishman said the proposal would improve the site compared to the large 
commercial building that has stood there for 75 years. Additional discussion covered the 
placement of three-story buildings behind the street-facing units, how they would appear from 
different vantage points, and the fact that two-story units require larger footprints, whereas 
three-story units allow for more green space. Also noted was that the Planning Commission 
requested a reduction in total units, and the applicant attempted to schedule a joint PC-AHBR 
meeting, which did not occur. The two-story units placed at corners were intended to improve 
sight lines, and the overall design benefits from reduced asphalt and enhanced views of the 
creek and pedestrian paths.
The group also addressed concerns regarding limited fenestration on certain side walls and 
roofs, the potential use of hip-gable roof forms to soften the scale of units facing Owen Brown 
Street, and the possibility of applying similar treatments to the three-story units to reduce 
perceived height. Participants noted that the Planning Commission did not appear to seek 
major changes to the site plan. While the design was generally regarded as attractive, some 
felt it might not rise to the standard of exceptional. Discussion included ensuring consistent 
materials across units, how site constraints affect bedroom size in a ground-floor bedroom 
unit, and differing AHBR interpretations of the Planning Commission’s preference for taller, 
Page 4City of Hudson, Ohio

more articulated units. The 35-foot height limit was acknowledged, and concern was raised 
that the developer is receiving conflicting direction from AHBR and the PC. Mr. Fishman 
said he had attended eleven Hudson meetings without receiving clear guidance. Staff stated 
they did not know why three-story units were permitted in Fairmont’s earlier Hudson 
development; Mr. Fishman clarified that those units were intentionally designed and 
well-received. Board members noted that precedent for three-story units does exist and 
mentioned that some existing Clinton Street units have elevators, with the new units designed 
to allow future elevator installation.
Board members shared varying views on the three-story units, particularly the one proposed 
along Owen Brown Street. Some opposed it; others did not. Several agreed the proposal 
would improve the site compared to the existing building. The Board discussed how 
exceptions should be evaluated and whether the roof design facing Owen Brown Street should 
be reconsidered. The Clinton Street development was viewed as a more successful streetscape 
than the current Owen Brown proposal, and concern was expressed that the elevations do not 
accurately reflect a pedestrian-level perspective. Members disagreed on whether the Owen 
Brown design includes sufficient variation. Some noted that a three-story unit on Owen 
Brown would represent a more significant exception than elsewhere. Others felt the design did 
not yet meet the exceptional standard but agreed that the required exceptions could potentially 
be satisfied.
Responding to comments about exceptional design, Mr. Fishman stated that land-use 
considerations should factor into whether the project meets the exceptional threshold. He 
added that practical difficulties could also serve as grounds for approval. He noted that 
leasing the existing building remains an option for utilizing the property.
Mr. Sugar summarized potential guidance from the Board, including reducing the visual 
impact of the three-story Owen Brown unit, increasing variation among the three-story units, 
improving material consistency across the massing, and reconsidering the mirroring of units to 
create more variety.
The Board and staff discussed how the Planning Commission should be informed of the 
meeting’s outcomes and what revisions should be made before the next vote. An informal poll 
on allowing the massing and the three-story unit resulted in a 4-to-3 opposition.
Mr. Fishman stated that the only way to make the project profitable would be to reduce green 
space, which he believed would be unacceptable to the Planning Commission.
The group discussed moving the three-story Owen Brown unit elsewhere on the site and 
replacing it with a two-story unit. Mr. Fishman reiterated the need for clear direction from 
AHBR. Members emphasized that redesigning the three-story Owen Brown unit is the central 
issue and that there are too many design changes to approve the plan at this meeting.
