BZBA 26-906 The subject of this hearing is a request for an appeal, pursuant to Section 
1212.01(b), from the final decision made by the Architectural and 
Historic Board of Review (AHBR) on June 12, 2026, for a request for an 
exterior alteration, including the replacement of a commercial front door 
per AHBR case No. 2025-1478.
The appellant is Attorney Joseph Kernan of PO Box 1456, Norton, Ohio 
44203. The property owner is Declan Thomas LTD, 88 N Main Street, 
Hudson, Ohio 44236 for the property at 88 N Main St in District 5 [Village 
Core Gateway] within the City of Hudson.
26-906 N Main St - Staff ReportAttachments:
Ms. Rodack introduced the appeal by identifying the property’s location and explaining that the door in question 
was installed without a permit, which is required in the Historic District. She noted that the case appeared before 
the AHBR multiple times and resulted in a denial at the April 22, 2026 meeting. She also stated that a Findings 
of Fact document was included in the staff report.
Mr. Joe Kernan, attorney for the appellant, asked whether the application would be remanded back to the AHBR 
due to the Executive Session that occurred during the meeting. Chair Bronstein responded that the Board would 
determine whether a remand was appropriate. Mr. Kernan stated that he had no knowledge of what occurred 
during the Executive Session and referenced section 121.22 of the Ohio Revised Code, noting that public 
hearings must be open to the public except for specific, limited exceptions. He explained that he did not believe 
any applicable exception justified the Executive Session and reviewed the allowable reasons and requirements 
for conducting one. He added that the failure to follow these rules complicated his case.
The Board asked Mr. Kernan whether he preferred a decision based on the legality of the Executive Session or 
on the merits of the case. Mr. Kernan responded that if the Board wished to consider the merits, he would request 
additional time to prepare.
The Board discussed potential outcomes and agreed that the merits of the case should ultimately be heard. They 
also requested guidance from the City Solicitor and staff regarding the legality of the Executive Session. Solicitor 
Thomas stated that he believed the Executive Session was legally problematic.
Chair Bronstein clarified that the appellant was requesting either a remand or a continuance. The Board 
discussed whether new evidence could be submitted and whether the issues should be separated so that the 
legality of the Executive Session could be addressed before considering the merits of the case. The Board also 
discussed potential remedies for an improper Executive Session, and Mr. Kernan noted that payment of legal fees 
could be one such remedy. The appellant expressed concern that the Findings of Fact may have been influenced 
by discussions that occurred outside the public meeting.

Ms. Samantha Arnold, the property owner, stated that certain Findings appeared to have been based on matters 
not discussed publicly.
Mr. Kernan stated that, in his opinion, the most practical way to proceed would be to remand the case to the 
AHBR for a new hearing.
Mr. Scott moved that the Board deliberate on the question of remand; Mr. Jones seconded the motion, 
which was unanimously approved.
Solicitor Thomas stated that the AHBR is an administrative board rather than a quasi-judicial body, and its 
decisions are not appealed to the Court of Common Pleas. Staff noted that the City Solicitor was not present to 
provide advice at the AHBR meeting. The Board also observed that the Findings of Fact did not describe what 
occurred in the Executive Session. Mr. Kernan reiterated that he had not been informed of what transpired during 
that session. Additionally, it was noted that during the June 10, 2026, AHBR review of the Findings of Fact, no 
comments were made before the vote to approve them. The Board confirmed that this appeal was timely, as the 
applicant received the Findings on June 25, 2026, within the allowable period for filing an appeal.
The Board stated that it agreed with the appellant that the Executive Session was illegal because no roll call was 
taken, the purpose of the session was not stated, and the meeting did not comply with the Open Meetings Act.
Mr. Jones noted that the merits of the case had not been discussed and recommended that all relevant Ohio laws 
be followed during the remand. Chair Bronstein reviewed the applicable sections of the Ohio Revised Code and 
explained that a remand would require conducting a completely new hearing.
Council stated that the Board may include any details it deems necessary in the motion. The Board also 
emphasized that the AHBR must understand that its previous decision is null and void and that a new hearing 
will be required.
Mr. Scott made a motion to find the AHBR decision unlawful because Ohio Revised Code 
121.22(H) states that any formal action adopted in an open meeting is invalid if it results from 
deliberations not open to the public, unless those deliberations were for a purpose authorized 
under Section G. He further moved that the case be remanded to the AHBR with instructions to 
reconvene their hearing on the matter, comply fully with all Open Meetings Act requirements, 
adhere to all applicable Hudson Codes and their own administrative rules, and issue a formal 
written decision following the hearing. Mr. Jones seconded the motion. The motion was 
approved by the following vote:
Aye: Ms. Bronstein, Mr. Jones, Mr. Dyer, Mr. Kahrl and Mr. Scott5 -
