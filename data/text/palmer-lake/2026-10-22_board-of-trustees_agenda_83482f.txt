Police Staffing Questions .  Because some discussions occurred with staffing levels within PD, and because 
these updates are included in public packets, I wanted to republish the police staffing analysis again here for 
your review.   The Town has sought 24/7/365 coverage with 2 dee p for officer availability.   This is standard 
in many small Colorado communities and is practical and necessary not only for officer safety but also citizen 
safety.  Nationally, the number of both officer and citizen injuries goes down based on the number of officers 
available to respond to an incident.  This is particularly useful to keep in mind in the case of domestic calls for 
service.  Assuming an officer is expected to work a standard 2080 work year, this does not take into 
consideration vacation, hol idays, sick leave, training, mandatory time off based on critical incidents, court 
appearances, security at BOT meetings, responding to discovery requests, meeting with District Attorneys, 
special investigations, joint defense/mutual aid, administrative du ties, injuries/workman’s comp, and other 
things that reduce deployable time.   
 
Based purely on 24/7/365 double coverage, the number translates to about 17,520 coverage hours per year [2 
officers * 24 hours *365].   That equates to about 9 officers per year.   Adding in all the additional, non -
deployable time periods, a more realistic approach is about 1,600-1,650 hours of deployable coverage per year 
per officer.  At 1,600 deployable hours per officer, the number needed to cover the entire 17,520 hours requires 
about 10.95 full-time officers for complete coverage.  If this is translated to a work schedule, it is a minimum 
of 11 full-time officers per year for coverage.  Coverage can and is reduced on both a state and national average 
from these ideal numbers. 
 
Fire Staffing Questions .  I was asked a separate question by a resident in terms of Fire staffing.   As such, 
because you may hear the same questions, I am addressing it here.  The easiest way to look at staffing is “four 
firefighters minimum”.  This is not technically correct but is a merger of OSHA’s standard of 2 inside and 2 
outside for structure fires, with the NFPA 1720 which states a total of four firefighters on s cene before 
beginning an interior attack on a fire.   These two codes reflect the same standard – a minimum crew for 4 to 
establish the “safety floor” staffing for a structure fire.   
 
Merger of Public Works and Water.  During the budget discussions on Wednesday, I suggested a restructuring 
of Town operations to merge water and streets/parks under the title of “Public Works”.  This is an operational 
Page 24 of 59
 
change but is very common in smaller communities and is generally quite effective.   The key is that water 
operations are kept separate financially from the general fund budgets of parks and streets.    At this stage of 
the budget process, the transition cannot be made immediately for the 2027 budget, but I will be looking at the 
restructuring during the first quarter of 2027.   The key is that greater integration would permit cross -training 
and more flexible deployment of personnel among water, streets, parks , facilities, snow removal, and 
emergency work, subject to appropriate qualifications and certifications. Also shared personnel, equipment, 
facilities, purchasing, scheduling, and maintenance can improve efficiency without subsidizing General Fund 
operations with water-rate revenue.

MEMORANDUM 
 
BACKGROUND. During the October 8, 2026, Board meeting, Trustee Harris requested an analysis of the 
implications of a property tax mill increase for Palmer Lake. The following memo provides initial information 
available about this request. This proposal and discussions related to any increase in the mill levy cannot affect 
the Town’s 2026 budget deficit, or the projected deficit for 2027, but can be used to limit future deficits and 
create a more stable revenue stream for the Town of Palmer Lake 
 
BRIEF SUMMARY. A General Fund mill levy increase would require voter approval at the earliest practical 
TABOR election, November 2, 2027. Ballot content must be certified by September 3, 2027, following prompt 
Board action. A preliminary increase of 15 mills calls for evaluation for long-term stability; at the illustrative 
$634,580 median home price, which would add approximately $583 annually. Fire-related tax changes, 
whether for independent operations or participation in Monument Fire District, are excluded . This proposal 
cannot affect the Town’s 2026 or 2027 deficits; added collections could begin in 2028. 
 
ANALYSIS. Colorado Constitution article X, section 20(4)(a) generally requires advance voter approval for 
a property tax rate increase. A preliminary review of C.R.S. §§ 31-20-101, 29-1-301, 29-1-302 and 29-1-1705 
shows no general statewide maximum on a statutory town’s operating mills or combined ceiling on 
overlapping taxing districts. Subject to verification of Palmer Lake-specific authority, a proposed mill increase 
initially appears consistent with the statewide sta tutory and TABOR framework if approved by voters. The 
separate revenue-limit provisions and applicable exceptions must be checked before any election action. 
 
The first possible date to set a mill levy election is Tuesday, November 2, 2027 . Assuming a coordinated 
election, notice and certification of the ballot content must be completed by September 3, 2027. These dates 
are calculated based on C.R.S. 1-5-203(3)(a) but must be recalculated again prior to making any final decisions 
on proceeding with the election . Additionally, the calendar should be confirmed with the El Paso County 
Clerk. 
 
The working 2026 baseline  for mill levies analysis is 11.112 General Fund mills on $62,516,770 assessed 
valuation, excluding fire mills (Town 2026 levy certification, Resolution 71 -2025). Under this general 
calculation, one added mill would yield approximately $62,517 in gross annual revenue at that valuation . 
However, the application is not uniform due to factors such as assessment ratios, deductions, valuations, and 
other factors can intervene. However, the following table should be considered illustrative:

Subject:  Organizational Restructuring and Financial Sustainability 
 
 
PURPOSE AND FINANCIAL CONTEXT 
 
The Town is implementing an organizational restructuring to reduce recurring personnel costs, 
combine supervision, and align municipal operations with resources the Town can sustain. The 
Board of Trustees is familiar with the financial concerns identified through the current budget 
process, including the projected shortfall in 2026 and continuing pressure  on the 2027 General 
Fund. These conditions require action during the current fiscal year, not merely adjustments to a 
future budget. 
 
The restructuring follows an ongoing review of Town operations, personnel requirements, 
departmental coordination, and financial capacity. It is an administrative decision within the Town 
Administrator's responsibilities. This memorandum informs the Board of the changes and explains 
their fiscal and operational basis; it does not seek Board approval of individual personnel decisions. 
 
ORGANIZATIONAL CHANGES 
 
Public Safety. Following the departure of the Fire Chief, the Town will not refill that permanent 
position at this time. The current Police Chief will serve as Public Safety Supervisor, coordinating 
Police and Fire operations. Town Hall will oversee human resources and administrative functions, 
allowing the Public Safety Supervisor to concentrate on operational needs, coordination, and 
departmental accountability. In addition, an interim Fire Chief will be appointed from within the 
fire department to assist with operations and to provide technical assistance in the discussions 
surrounding the Monument Fire merger.  This arrangement combines oversight without treating 
the police and fire functions as interchangeable or reducing applicable operational and professional 
requirements. 
 
Public Works and Water. Following the departure of the Public Works supervisor, the Town will 
not refill that supervisory position. Public Works and Water will run as a combined department 
under the existing Water Supervisor on an interim basis. The combined structure is intended to 
improve scheduling, share equipment and personnel where appropriate, and avoid duplicate 
supervision. Qualified personnel will continue  to perform work requiring water -system 
Page 31 of 59
certifications, and the Town will separately account for Water Enterprise and General Fund labor, 
equipment, and other expenses according to actual use. 
 
Operational Staffing. The Town is also eliminating one position in Water and reducing one full-
time Public Works position to a part-time position of approximately 20 hours per week. The Water 
position is being eliminated, while the Public Works position will continue with reduc ed hours. 
The Town may consider a part -time or seasonal parks position for summer operations. An added 
labor position supporting the combined Water and Public Works department may also become 
necessary in the future, but neither position is presently commi tted. Employees affected by these 
staffing changes remain eligible to apply for any such future openings under the Town's normal 
hiring process. 
 
COST REDUCTION AND BUDGET TREATMENT 
 
The immediate and recurring savings arise principally from eliminating the Fire Chief and Public 
Works supervisory positions, eliminating one Water position, and reducing one full -time Public 
Works position to approximately 20 hours per week. Reduced employer costs extend beyond base 
salaries and include applicable health insurance, retirement and payroll contributions, workers' 
compensation, unemployment coverage, and other employment -related expenses, as applicable. 
Savings during the rest of 2026 depend on effective dates and actual payroll and benefit costs; the 
full-year effects will differ. 
 
The Town will incorporate updated personnel and benefit figures in the revised 2027 draft budget 
provided separately to the Board. This memorandum therefore does not present an unsupported 
savings total or suggest that the restructuring, by itself, closes the projected 2027 shortfall. The 
amended budget will provide the financial comparison and allow the Board to evaluate the 
continuing gap after these reductions. 
 
LONG-TERM FINANCIAL SUSTAINABILITY 
 
The organizational restructuring should not be viewed as a short -term solution to the Town's 
expected 2027 budget shortfall. Rather, these changes are the beginning of a systematic, long-term 
approach to municipal operations, staffing, and the delivery of services. The Town must set up an 
organizational structure that it can sustain with existing revenues, while keeping essential services 
and meeting its financial obligations. Unless and until the Town realizes substantial added revenue 
through increased retail sales and associated sales taxes, property taxes, or other sustainable 
revenue sources, staffing and operational expenditures must remain consistent with available 
resources. Future increases in revenue may provide opportu nities to reconsider staffing levels, 
restore services, or address deferred operational needs. However, the Town should not establish 
recurring expenditures based upon expected revenues that have not materialized. 
 
Reduced staffing will require the Town to prioritize work, coordinate resources, and adjust service 
schedules to available personnel. The administration will monitor operational demands, employee 
safety, essential public-safety coverage, water-system responsibilities, and service impacts as the 
combined structure develops. The Town cannot rely indefinitely on reserves to finance an 
operating model that recurring revenues do not support.

1. Purpose 
Social media can help Town officials share public information and hear from residents. It can also 
blur the lines between official and personal speech, create public records on private devices, 
permit unlawful deliberation outside a noticed meeting, compromise quasi-judicial fairness, or 
expose the Town and an individual official to legal claims. 
The purposes of this Policy are to identify those boundaries, preserve lawful individual 
expression, protect public participation, and provide practical rules that apply across Town 
public bodies without targeting any person, platform, body, or incident. 
2. Scope and Application 
This Policy applies to the Mayor and members of the Board of Trustees; members of the Planning 
Commission; members of the Parks and Trails Commission; and members of any other Town 
board, commission, committee, task force, or similar public body established now or in the 
future. These persons are referred to collectively as Town Officials. 
The Policy applies when a Town Official uses a Town Account, Town resources, an account 
associated with an official Town position, or any account or electronic medium to conduct or 
discuss Town business. It does not regulate lawful private speech unrelated to Town business or 
require any Town Official to create or maintain a personal social media account. 
Town employees remain subject to applicable personnel, technology, records, and 
communications policies. When an employee also serves as a Town Official, this Policy applies 
to conduct in the appointed or elected role. 
3. Definitions 
Authorized Administrator. The Town employee, contractor, or other person designated by the 
Town Administrator or Board to create, manage, post to, archive, or moderate a Town Account. 
Electronic Communication. Email, text message, direct message, group message, chat, 
comment, reaction, shared document, online forum, video conference, or other 
communication transmitted by electronic means. 
Official Town Position. A position adopted by the Board or another Town body acting within its 
authority, or an administrative statement issued by an authorized Town spokesperson within 
delegated authority. 
Personal Account. A social media account controlled by an individual and not created, required, 
funded, managed, or supported by the Town. Whether a particular post or action is private or 
governmental depends on applicable law and the facts, not only the account label. 
Public Business or Town Business. A matter relating to the affairs, operations, policy, programs, 
services, decisions, or legal responsibilities of the Town or a Town public body. 
Social Media. Any digital service or electronic medium that permits users to create, publish, 
share, view, react to, or exchange user-generated content, including social networks, video 
and photo platforms, blogs, podcasts, discussion forums, messaging services, and 
comparable technologies developed after adoption of this Policy.

Town Account. An account created, owned, authorized, funded, supported, or administered by 
the Town, or held out as the official account of the Town, a Town department, a Town public 
body, or a Town office. A Town Account is a Town asset. 
4. Governing Principles 
• Town public bodies act collectively through lawful public processes. An individual member 
does not speak for or bind the body unless authorized. 
• The same standards of role discipline, respect, confidentiality, impartiality, and 
noninterference that apply offline apply online. 
• The content and function of a communication matter more than the device, platform, account 
name, privacy setting, or disclaimer used. 
• Town-related electronic communications may be public records and may implicate open 
meetings requirements. 
• Rules for public participation on Town Accounts must be clear, reasonable, and applied 
without viewpoint discrimination. 
• No person may use a platform feature, private account, intermediary, or disappearing-
message tool to evade a duty imposed by law or Town policy. 
5. Town Accounts 
5.1 Creation and Ownership 
Only the Town Administrator or a designee acting under Board policy may authorize a Town 
Account. The account must be registered, where practicable, with Town contact information; use 
Town-approved branding; identify its public purpose; and remain accessible to the Town. A Town 
Official shall not create an account that appears to be an official Town, Board, Commission, 
department, or office account without authorization. 
The Town shall maintain an inventory of Town Accounts, authorized administrators, recovery 
information, and archiving arrangements. Account credentials shall be protected through 
reasonable security measures, including multifactor authentication when available. A departing 
official or administrator shall promptly return or transfer all account access and Town content. 
5.2 Authorized Content and Spokespersons 
Town Accounts shall communicate verified information concerning Town programs, services, 
meetings, events, public safety, decisions, and other authorized subjects. The Mayor normally 
communicates the official position of the Board unless the Board designates another 
representative. The Town Administrator or designee may communicate administrative and 
operational information within delegated authority. A chair or member of another public body 
may communicate for that body only when the body, Board, Town Code, or Town Administrator 
has provided authority. 
A Town Account shall not be used to advance an individual's personal, commercial, or campaign 
interests; attack a person or group; pressure Town staff; disclose protected information; or 
announce a policy, commitment, decision, or direction that has not been lawfully authorized.

5.3 Public Inquiries and Emergencies 
Town Accounts are supplemental communication channels and are not continuously monitored 
unless the Town expressly states otherwise. Each account should direct users to established 
Town contact methods. Emergency posts shall direct users to 911 or the appropriate emergency 
service, and service requests should be referred to the Town's normal intake process. A post or 
message does not constitute legal notice, filing, service, application, or an official request unless 
law or Town procedure expressly provides otherwise. 
6. Personal Accounts and Individual Speech 
Town Officials retain their rights as private citizens. A Town Official may maintain a Personal 
Account, express personal views about public affairs, control access to a qualifying private 
account as permitted by law, and choose not to use social media. For local elected officials, 
C.R.S. section 29-34-101 addresses private social media that is neither supported by local-
government resources nor required by law. 
A Town Official who uses a Personal Account for Town-related speech should keep the account 
clearly personal. The official should use a statement such as, 'This is my personal account. Views 
are my own and do not represent an official position of the Town of Palmer Lake,' when the 
context could otherwise cause confusion. The official should not use the Town seal or logo as a 
profile image, categorize the account as the Town or a Town department, use Town staff or funds 
to operate it, or make original announcements available only through that account. 
A disclaimer is helpful but not conclusive. Under applicable First Amendment law, an official may 
engage in governmental action on social media when the official has actual authority to speak for 
the government on the particular matter and purports to exercise that authority. Each post and 
use of platform features may be evaluated according to its content, function, and surrounding 
facts. 
7. Distinguishing Personal and Official Positions 
A Town Official may explain a personal position, including disagreement with a decision, but shall 
accurately distinguish that position from an Official Town Position. The official shall not state or 
imply that the Board, a Commission, Town staff, or the Town has taken action that has not 
occurred. 
When sharing an official Town release, notice, emergency message, or other verified 
communication, the safest practice is to share or link to the original Town source without 
changing its meaning. Questions about facts, legal matters, personnel, active investigations, or 
operational details should be referred to the Town Administrator, Town Clerk, Town Attorney, or 
other authorized source. 
No Town Official may use social media to direct an employee, assign staff work, approve an 
expenditure, promise a permit or service outcome, negotiate for the Town, commit Town 
resources, or exercise authority belonging to the full body or Town administration.

8. Open Meetings and Intermember Communications 
Colorado's Open Meetings Law applies to qualifying electronic discussions of public business. A 
meeting may occur through posts, comments, direct messages, group messages, emails, shared 
documents, or a sequence of communications. The applicable threshold is measured for each 
public body under current law; communications involving a quorum or three members of a local 
public body, whichever is fewer, require particular caution. 
A Town Official shall not use social media or Electronic Communications to deliberate, debate, 
exchange positions, build agreement, poll members, or decide the merits of public business 
outside a properly noticed public meeting when the communication would violate or evade open 
meetings requirements. The prohibition includes serial communications conducted directly, 
through an intermediary, or across multiple platforms. 
Members may receive one-way scheduling notices, agendas, publicly available information, or 
material distributed for later consideration, provided the exchange does not become substantive 
discussion. A member receiving a group communication concerning the merits of public 
business should not reply to all or continue the discussion and should contact the Town Clerk, 
Town Administrator, or Town Attorney when guidance or preservation is needed. 
A member should not comment in an online thread about Town business when participation by 
other members of the same public body may create a prohibited meeting. A reaction, emoji, 
endorsement, repost, or platform poll can communicate a position and should not be used to 
accomplish indirectly what may not lawfully be done through text. 
9. Public Records and Retention 
Town-related posts, comments, messages, photographs, recordings, edits, moderation records, 
and metadata may be public records regardless of whether they reside on a Town or personal 
account or device. A Town Official shall not promise confidentiality, delete a record, use auto-
delete or disappearing-message features, or move Town business to a private channel to avoid 
disclosure or retention duties. 
A Town Official who creates or receives substantive Town business on a Personal Account or 
device shall preserve it and promptly provide or forward it to the Town Clerk or designated Town 
repository in a form that preserves reasonably available context, attachments, dates, 
participants, and edits. When a records request, complaint, audit, investigation, claim, or 
litigation hold applies, relevant material shall be preserved until the Town Attorney or authorized 
custodian releases the hold. 
Town Accounts shall be archived or otherwise retained according to the Town's adopted records-
retention schedule. Before authorized content is hidden, removed, corrected, or deleted, the 
Town should preserve the content and record the date, decision-maker, and policy basis when 
practicable. Nothing in this Policy changes a statutory exception, privilege, or other lawful basis 
for withholding a record from public inspection.

10. Quasi-Judicial and Pending Matters 
A Town Official shall not use social media to advocate, prejudge, solicit support or opposition, 
gather facts outside the record, or communicate privately with an interested person about the 
merits of a pending or reasonably anticipated quasi-judicial matter. This rule applies to Planning 
Commissioners and to Trustees when a matter may come before the Board after Commission 
review. 
A Town Official who receives an unsolicited message or sees material concerning a pending 
quasi-judicial matter should avoid a substantive response, preserve the communication, and 
promptly provide it to the Town Clerk, Town Administrator, designated planning staff, or Town 
Attorney. Disclosure on the record, recusal, or another remedy may be required. The Town 
Attorney should be consulted as soon as the issue arises. 
General notice should be issued through neutral Town channels and established noticing 
procedures. Selectively publicizing one pending application, engaging with comments about its 
merits, or signaling a likely vote can create an appearance of bias and jeopardize the fairness or 
defensibility of the proceeding. 
11. Confidential and Protected Information 
A Town Official shall not post, transmit, confirm, hint at, or use information that is confidential by 
law, protected by attorney-client privilege, discussed in a lawful executive session, restricted by 
personnel or privacy requirements, related to protected public-safety or security information, or 
otherwise unavailable for public release. 
An individual member cannot independently waive the Town's privilege or authorize disclosure of 
executive-session information. When follow-up communication is appropriate, the body or 
authorized Town representative should identify what may be disclosed and who may speak. A 
Personal Account, closed group, direct message, or deleted post does not make an unauthorized 
disclosure permissible. 
12. Professional Conduct Online 
Town Officials shall follow the applicable Code of Conduct and Governance Handbook when 
communicating online about Town business. Vigorous disagreement is permitted. Misuse of 
office, threats, retaliation, unlawful harassment or discrimination, disclosure of protected 
information, knowing material misrepresentation of official action, interference with Town 
administration, and attempts to pressure another public body outside its lawful process are not 
permitted. 
Officials should direct service issues to staff through the Town Administrator rather than attempt 
to resolve them publicly. They should not publish protected personnel information, publicly 
evaluate individual employees, encourage online attacks on staff or volunteers, or imply that an 
employee must follow an individual member's direction.

13. Public Comments and Content Moderation 
13.1 Town Account Rules 
When a Town Account permits public comments, the account shall identify the subject and rules 
of the forum through posted terms or a readily available link. Authorized Administrators shall 
apply the rules consistently and without regard to whether a comment supports or criticizes the 
Town, a policy, an official, an employee, or a viewpoint. 
Subject to review by the Town Attorney, the Town may restrict content that falls outside the 
purpose of the forum or within a clearly stated, legally permissible category, such as: 
• Content unrelated to the topic of the specific post or stated purpose of the forum. 
• True threats, unlawful incitement, or content that facilitates criminal activity. 
• Obscenity as defined by law, or sexual content prohibited by the forum rules. 
• Malware, malicious links, phishing, or content that compromises system or public safety. 
• Unauthorized disclosure of personal identifying, confidential, or legally protected 
information. 
• Commercial spam or substantially repetitive content, when restricted without regard to 
viewpoint. 
• Content that infringes copyright, trademark, or another enforceable legal right after 
appropriate notice. 
• Content that a platform removes under its own rules without direction from the Town. 
Criticism, harsh language, unpopular opinions, political disagreement, or offensiveness alone is 
not a sufficient basis for removal when the speech is otherwise protected and within the forum's 
scope. The Town should use the narrowest effective moderation tool and should consult the 
Town Attorney when the legal status of content is uncertain. 
13.2 Removal, Blocking, and Review 
Only an Authorized Administrator may moderate a Town Account. Before removing or hiding 
content, the administrator should preserve it and document the applicable rule, unless 
immediate action is reasonably necessary to address a threat, unlawful content, disclosure of 
protected information, or system-security risk. Blocking or banning a user from a Town Account 
should be reserved for repeated or serious violations, be no broader or longer than reasonably 
necessary, and include notice and an opportunity for review when practicable. 
A person whose content is removed or whose access is restricted may request review by the Town 
Administrator or designee. The reviewer shall consult the Town Attorney when appropriate and 
may restore content or access when the restriction was inconsistent with the posted rules or 
applicable law. 
13.3 Personal Accounts 
The owner of a Personal Account ordinarily controls that account, subject to applicable law. No 
Town employee shall moderate a Personal Account as part of Town duties. A Town Official should 
seek legal guidance before deleting comments or blocking users when the account or relevant 
post could reasonably be treated as governmental action. Platform-wide blocking can affect 
access to both personal and potentially official posts and therefore creates added risk.

14. Political and Campaign Activity 
Town Officials retain their personal political rights. Campaign activity must remain separate from 
Town business. A Town Account, Town email address, Town logo, staff time, equipment, funds, 
mailing list, or other Town resource shall not be used to support or oppose a candidate or ballot 
measure except as expressly permitted by law. 
An official using a Personal Account for campaign activity should not imply Town endorsement or 
use confidential information or Town-created resources unavailable to the public. Nothing in this 
section prohibits an elected official from stating a personal position through private resources 
when permitted by law. 
15. Accuracy, Corrections, and Security 
Town Officials should verify factual claims before publishing Town-related information and 
should link to authoritative Town sources when available. A material error should be corrected 
promptly and transparently. A correction should preserve the original record and explain the

SUBJECT: Snow Removal Standards and 
Enforcement 
MEMORANDUM 
 
 
Purpose.  
The Town needs to adjust snow removal to what its staffing, equipment, and budget can support. The proposed 
resolution limits routine plowing and overtime while allowing Police and Fire to call for help when an accident or 
emergency requires it. 
 
Background.  
Staffing reductions needed to meet the budget will leave fewer employees available for snow removal and other 
Town work. Each plowing operation also uses fuel and adds wear to equipment that has already seen substantial use. 
More operating hours mean more maintenance and a greater chance of repairs. The Town needs to limit avoidable 
wear and repair costs because available funds may not be sufficient to cover a major equipment repair. These limits 
are intended to keep snow removal within the resources the Town can afford while preserving emergency response. 
 
Snow Removal Standard.  
Routine street snow removal will not begin until actual accumulation exceeds four inches, whether from one storm 
or a combination of storms. Combined accumulation includes snow remaining on the street plus new snow; melted 
or previously removed snow does not count. Public Works will decide accumulation from representative street 
conditions. 
 
Overtime, Weekend Work, and Cleanup.  
Overtime snow removal will be allowed only for emergency conditions. Weekend snow removal will occur only 
when accumulation exceeds four inches or an emergency exists. Exceeding four inches allows weekend work but 
does not independently authorize overtime. Added cleanup will occur during regular work hours as time, staff, and 
equipment are available. 
 
Emergency Response.  
Police and Fire may request snow removal for accidents, blocked emergency access, and other immediate threats to 
life or public safety, regardless of accumulation or day. The Town Administrator or designee may authorize 
emergency work and overtime. An incident commander may request an immediate callout when delay would impair 
emergency response, with prompt notification to the Administrator. 
 
Parking Enforcement and Property Owner Duties.  
The existing two-inch snow parking threshold remains separate from the proposed plowing threshold (applicable 
snowstorm parking provisions of Title 10 of the Municipal Code). Residents are encouraged to comply with existing 
Page 49 of 59
 
parking restrictions so that streets remain accessible to emergency vehicles and snow removal equipment. When 
vehicles obstruct streets or snow removal operations, the Town may use available enforcement measures, including 
citations or towing when circumstances warrant, in accordance with applicable law and procedures. Section 
12.20.010(h) also preserves the prohibition against parking that interferes with traffic or snow removal. Chapter 
12.22 requires clearing specified sidewalks and adjacent areas within 24 hours and prohibits placing private-property 
snow on public property or another person’s property without required consent. 
 
Fiscal Impact.  
The proposed limits will help control fuel use, overtime, and wear on existing equipment. With fewer staff and 
limited repair funds, the Town must use its equipment carefully and avoid unnecessary operating hours. Needed 
repairs can still arise, and the Town’s ability to pay for them is a concern. Savings have not been calculated; 
emergency work and necessary maintenance will continue to carry costs. 
 
Recommendation.  
Adopt the accompanying resolution to establish clear snow removal standards, preserve emergency response 
capabilities, and remind residents of existing parking and property maintenance responsibilities and the Town’s 
available enforcement options when necessary.

SUBJECT: Wildlife Attractant Ordinance 
MEMORANDUM 
 
 
Purpose.  
The Board of Trustees is asked to consider an ordinance amending Chapters 7.16 and 8.16 of the Palmer Lake 
Municipal Code to reduce conflicts between residents and bears and other wildlife by regulating access to food, 
refuse, and other wildlife attractants. 
 
Background.  
On October 12, 2026, the Board of Trustees and Planning Commission held a joint meeting to discuss bear activity 
and the management of wildlife attractants. Members of the public and a representative of Colorado Parks and 
Wildlife also participated. The proposed ordinance reflects the general consensus of those discussions and is intended 
as an initial step in addressing the issue. 
 
Proposed Ordinance  
The ordinance expressly prohibits feeding bears and other big game wildlife and maintains remedial obligations 
following bear encounters. It establishes year-round restrictions on outdoor access to attractants, including refuse 
containing food, pet food, bird seed, suet, and similar materials. Attractants may be outdoors where accessible to 
wildlife only from 6:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.; outside those hours, they must be secured. Refuse may be placed at its 
collection location only on the scheduled collection day, between 6:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m., and must be retrieved if 
collection is missed. 
Storage Requirements  
Acceptable storage includes a fully enclosed garage, shed, or comparable secure structure, or a properly closed bear-
resistant container meeting the specific requirements or certification standards of either the Wildlife Management 
Institute (WMI) or the Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee (IGBC). Straps, clips, bungee cords, and similar add-
ons alone do not establish compliance. 
Enforcement and Penalties  
The proposed fines are $100 for a first violation, $250 for a second, and $500 for a third or subsequent violation. 
The ordinance does not separately amend the Town’s master fee schedule. 
 
Future Considerations. 
The proposed ordinance represents a first step, and additional Municipal Code revisions may be considered as the 
Town evaluates implementation. Grant assistance may become available to help obtain or supply bear-resistant 
containers for residents who lack garages or other suitable enclosed storage; no such funding is assumed or 
committed by this action.
