2. Discussion, Recommendation, and Possible Action Regarding Amendments to Chapter 8 of 
The Town of Palmer Lake Municipal Code Concerning Bear-Resistant Trash Containers 
The Planning Commission discussed introducing a mandatory, town-wide bear-proof container ordinance 
covering residential, commercial, and short-term rental properties in Palmer Lake. To address rising human-
bear interactions, members emphasized requiring certified bear-proof receptacles, researching CPW grant 
funding to offset costs, evaluating fine structures, and resolving waste hauler logistics. The commission 
Page 3 of 61
agreed to discuss during an upcoming planning workshop before submitting a formal recommendation to the 
Town Attorney and Board of Trustees. 
 
Public Comment: 
Jennifer Rausch - Asked the commission to prioritize mandatory bear-resistant trash requirements and 
stricter license enforcement for short-term rentals, citing frequent issues with out-of-town guests leaving 
unsecured trash that habituates bears and creates neighborhood hazards. 
Marne Kendrick - Asked for a town-wide bear trash ordinance with strict enforcement, citing multiple 
dangerous encounters with habituated bears drawn to unsecured trash at nearby short-term rentals. 
Tati McAlister - Asked the commission to enact bear trash regulations immediately, explaining that local 
bears undergo torpor rather than continuous winter hibernation and will forage during warm spells 
whenever unsecured trash is available. 
Patrick McAlister - Asked the commission to immediately regulate short-term rentals as commercial 
businesses by mandating trash compliance backed by license revocation and fines, using them as an initial 
test case before rolling out broader community-wide regulations. 
Public Comment 
This time is reserved for the public to speak to items not on the agenda. Individuals must state 
name and limit comments to (3) minutes unless extended. 
Don Kendrick - Recommended posting informational flyers at the post office and sending direct compliance 
notices to short-term rental owners to prevent dangerous wildlife encounters. 
Nancy Reed - Expressed concern over town communications regarding the recent water restrictions, stating 
that relying solely on the town website and the post office bulletin board was insufficient.

PROJECT JUSTIFICATION 
Procedures for Plat Amendments are set out in Chapter 16.20.060 and 16.20.130 of the Town 
of Palmer Lake Municipal Code.  This request meets the criteria for a Plat Amendment as set 
out in Chapter 16.20.060, subsection (c), as follows: 
(1) The amendment involves minor lot line adjustments which do not increase the number of 
lots previously included within the area involved in the amendment, and which meet the 
requirements of the zoning district in which the lots are located 
Only one lot line is to be adjusted, and it will not create additional lots.  There will still be 
only three lots in this subdivision.  The property is zoned PUD, and this change does not 
impact the ability to implement the approved PUD Plan. 
(2) The lots must be under the same ownership and shall not be separated by non-owned lots 
The two lots impacted by this change are Lots 1 and 8.  Both lots are in the Applicant’s 
ownership under different entity names. Lots 1 and 8 are not separated by non-owned lots. 
Lot 17 has always been and will continue to be wholly surrounded by Lot 1. 
(3) No public improvements are required 
This Plat Amendment does not impact existing or require additional public improvements. 
(4) The plat amendment meets the requirements of the MS4 permitting process

existing stormwater detention pond will now be wholly contained within Lot 8 instead of

(5) The plat amendment meets the development standards and lot coverage standards 
of the stormwater quality control ordinance 
This Plat Amendment will not impact existing drainage patterns and will not affect lot 
coverage standards. The existing stormwater detention pond will now be wholly 
contained within Lot 8 instead of Lot 1. 
(5) The amendment affects minor adjustments in the boundaries of street right-of-way

PLATTING HISTORY  
The property was originally  subdivided  as Santa Fe Ridge on June 13, 2000 into  3 lots; Lots 1, 8 and 
17 (Reception No. 200068318) (EXHIBIT A) .  A handwritten note on the plat  gives an explanation as 
to why the lots are so numbered:  
“May 11,  2000 Board of Trustees approved the Final Plat for a Minor Subdivision within a 
PUD, Santa Fe Ridge, listing the lots as Lot 1 – Lot 2 – Lot 3.  
In order to be similar to the Final Plat for a PUD, Santa Fe Ridge , approved by the Board of 
Trustees on April 8, 1999:  
Lot 1 will remain Lot 1  
Lot 2 will be relabeled Lot 8  
Lot 3 will be relabeled Lot 17 ” 
On December 19, 2012 the Santa Fe Ridge subdivision was  replatted  as Amended Santa Fe Ridge  
subdivision  (Reception No. 212713280) (EXHIBIT B).  This replat subdivided lot 1 into a smaller Lot 1 
and a new Lot 4  was established .  Concurrently with this replat, a new subdivision was created to 
include additional land to the north purchased by the Applicant.  The new plat, Santa Fe Ridge II  
(Reception No. 212713281), included this additional land and the newly formed Lot 4 of Amended 
Santa Fe Ridge subdivision  (EXHIBIT C).  Both plats were recorded concurrently on December 19, 
2012. The result is that the Amended Santa Fe Ridge subdivision now only includes Lots 1, 8 and 
17, as Lot 4 was incorporated into Santa Fe Ridge II subdivision.
