8. Discussion and Update on 2027 Budget Ranking 
To assist Public Works with budget preparation, the parks and trails project list has been prioritized based on 
funding availability, favoring stable sources like the Conservation Trust Fund over prospective grants. As part

student-designed, kiosk-funded bridge to expand trail connectivity.  The Parks Commission agreed to discuss 
this item further in a future workshop.

cabin site cleanups are currently paused, and the town is seeking a permanent easement to complete

2. Awake the Lake Presenting on Ideas for Parks 
Jason Phillips and Jeff Hulsman from Awake the Lake presented a conceptual proposal to construct a 
reversible dirt pump track at the south end of the lake, utilizing approximately one-third of the open dirt 
area. Targeted at beginner and intermediate riders, the family-friendly project is estimated under $50,000 to 
be funded by Awake the Lake and volunteer labor, incorporating native, drought-resistant landscaping 
without impacting the adjacent disc golf course. 
 
The commission discussed an immediate, time-sensitive opportunity to secure 150 tons of free, clean fill dirt 
from a septic excavation in Monument toward the total 400 to 800 tons required. Following discussion 
regarding hauling logistics and long-term volunteer maintenance, the commission supported pursuing the 
administrative acquisition of the available fill dirt for site improvements, with formal plans and maintenance 
agreements to be presented to the Board of Trustees at a later date. 
 
Lauren Penner moved to proceed with the pump track at south end of the lake as a recommendation to the 
Board of Trustees. Carolyn Kincade seconded.

Town Administrator Patrick Davidson updated the Parks Commission on municipal operations and budget 
constraints, explaining that the vacant Public Works Director position will be addressed by merging Public 
Works and Water under Chaz Roubidoux, effectively eliminating the Public Works supervisor role as an 
interim measure that will transition into standard procedure. Town Administrator  Davidson outlined the 
town's critical financial position, noting a projected $1M deficit for year-end 2026 and a 2027 budget 
requiring $600,000 from reserves, putting current balances on a 1.3-year depletion track, while reassuring 
commissioners that January-approved park earmarks will remain protected and not be reallocated to general 
fund shortfalls. Additionally, Town Administrator Davidson reported that negotiations to finalize the El Paso 
County land transfer on the east side of the lake (excluding the trail) are expected to close before year-end, 
approved the use of Conservation Trust Fund monies for fall native grass and wildflower seeding at Glen Park, 
and confirmed that CIRSA playground inspections were passed with wood chip replenishment scheduled for 
late spring. 
Business Items 
4. Discussion on Mile High Youth Core Grant Request and Target Projects 
The Parks Commission is preparing a Mile High Youth Corps grant application, currently being drafted by 
Commissioner Nilson, to fund target projects such as Monument Creek tree clearing, trail extension, and 
completing retaining wall work on a new Elephant Rock education trail. Following a budget-driven hiatus in 
funding last year, commissioners are maintaining partner ties and coordinating administrative responsibilities 
with town staff to advance the proposal. 
5. Discussion on 2026 Playground Inspection report 
No discussion on this item 
6. Discussion on 2026 Service in the Parks Days 
Palmer Lake doubled its volunteer service days this year to four events, adding 30 new participants and 
featuring Master Gardeners and Fountain Creek Watershed representatives targeting invasive weed removal 
along the lake's south end and local trails. Meeting packet records highlight positive feedback, a Channel 5 
feature segment, and ongoing watershed collaboration, with an October 22nd debrief set to plan continued 
weed mitigation and creek maintenance. 
7. Discussion on American Discovery 250 Relay 
Palmer Lake hosted a fully sponsored, no-cost aid station for the American Discovery 250 relay as runners 
traveled from Larkspur to Colorado Springs. Building on existing fire and police partnerships, town officials 
discussed creating an annual inter-town event to strengthen ties between the two communities through their 
parks departments. 
8. Discussion on Donations to Parks 
The Parks Commission addressed $1,000-plus in event donations from 24 Hours of Palmer Lake, expressing 
concern over unallocated past funds. Commissioners proposed meeting with the donor to ensure future 
contributions are strictly earmarked for Centennial Park projects rather than absorbed into the general fund.
