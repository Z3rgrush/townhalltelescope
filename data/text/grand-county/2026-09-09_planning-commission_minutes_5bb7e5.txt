• Project Name: Ridge Point Townhomes (Amended Final Plat / Sketch Plan) 
• Staff Presenter: Hannah Mahorney Community Development, Planner I 
• Applicant: Diventures, LLC 
• Representatives: Daniel R. Rother (RAP Architecture), Lincoln Langhorst, and Jason 
Ahlers 
• Location: Lot 27, Block 1, Winter Park Ranch Second Filing Replat 
• Zoning: R – Residential District 
• Staff Recommendation: Approval with eight (8) conditions 
Project Overview & Background 
Hannah Mahorney presented an application for an expedited subdivision review for Ridge Point 
Condominiums. While structured similarly to an Amended Final Plat, the submittal is being 
processed as an expedited subdivision combining Preliminary and Final Plat reviews into a 
single concurrent submittal. All primary technical studies and agency reviews were completed 
during the original 8-unit approval in 2023. 
 
The current submittal reconfigures the site layout from an 8-unit multi-family condominium 
development into a 6-unit residential townhome subdivision, incorporating direct-access 
attached garages and modifying the overall building footprint and open space. 
Staff & Agency Findings 
• Master Plan Compliance: The proposal aligns with Element 3 (Infill development within 
Urban Growth Area No. 1), Element 4 (utilization of adjacent water, sewer, and electric 
infrastructure), and Element 5 (proximity to the seasonal Winter Park Lift transit 
corridor along U.S. Hwy 40). 
• Zoning Compliance: Multi-family residential use is permitted. Required setbacks (30-
foot front, 20-foot rear, 5-foot side) and minimum lot area standards (7,000 sq. ft. 
minimum for served lots) are met on the 0.989-acre site. 
• Road & Bridge / Access: Access is provided via a private 24-foot drive connecting to 
County Road 834. A single point of ingress/egress is supported under Section 3.4.2 of 
the Road and Bridge Standards (fewer than 9 units, length under 600 feet, and 
turnaround provided), subject to written agreement from the East Grand Fire Protection 
District and the Grand County Road and Bridge Department. Driveway and parking 
surfaces must be paved with asphalt. 
• Utilities & Infrastructure: The property is served by the Winter Park Ranch Water and 
Sanitation District. All utility extensions must be installed underground.

Staff recommends approval of the Ridge Point Townhomes / Sketch Plan, subject to the

3. Radon Mitigation Plat Note: A plat note shall be added stating: 
"A radon mitigation system shall be incorporated into the design of any occupied 
structure constructed in this subdivision if recommended by the soils report." 
4. Paving Design: Because the planned density exceeds 3 units per acre, the private access

pavement design. 
5. Engineering Documents: Engineered construction documents and associated cost

Preliminary/Final Plat submittal. 
6. Agency Approvals: Written letters of support for the proposed single access drive shall 
be secured from the East Grand Fire Protection District and the Grand County Road and 
Bridge Department. 
7. Hazard Delineation: All-natural slopes exceeding 30% shall be clearly delineated and 
labeled on the plat as "No Build Zones."

Commission Discussion 
Daniel Rother (RAP Architecture) and Jason Ahlers presented the revised design, explaining that 
reducing density from 8 units to 6 units and adding attached garages responded to current 
market demand. 
 
Key discussion points included: 
• Height Restrictions & Rooflines: The applicant explained that maintaining a flat roof 
design with internal drainage was chosen to comply strictly with the Winter Park Ranch 
covenants, which limit building height to 30 feet (measured 25 feet from the road). 
While Commissioners observed that surrounding properties have often exceeded this 
height, the applicant preferred avoiding covenant violations. 
• Architectural & Aesthetic Impact: Commissioners expressed concern regarding flat-roof 
aesthetics, retaining wall heights, and excessive exterior glazing that could create a 
visible "lantern effect" across the valley. 
• Drainage & Environmental Protection: Drainage strategies were reviewed, focusing on 
underground detention systems to manage stormwater quality despite steep terrain. 
Commissioners raised questions regarding runoff toward adjacent parcels, protection of 
the Fraser River corridor during construction, maintenance of snow-storage easements, 
and preservation of an existing bridle easement. 
• Landscaping Review: The Commission considered removing zero-landscaping 
requirements to afford the applicant flexibility under local site constraints. 
Action & Vote 
• Motion: Approve the Amended Final Plat / Sketch Plan for Ridge Point Townhomes, 
subject to the conditions recommended by Staff.

Presentation & Background 
Staff presented proposed comprehensive amendments to the Grand County Subdivision 
Regulations. The update consolidates single-family and multi-family standards into a unified 
regulatory framework, restructuring the code from eight articles down to five. While preserving 
the core intent of the 2025 regulations, the 2026 update modernizes technical standards and 
streamlines review timelines. 
 
Deletions & External Alignments 
• School Land Dedication: Replaced complex multi-page statutory formulas with direct 
compliance mandates tied to local school district resolutions and the March 2026 
Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA). 
• Road Paving & Fire Infrastructure: Eliminated county-specific numeric thresholds (e.g., 
ADT thresholds, pipe sizing, storage minimums) in favor of direct cross-references to 
Grand County Road and Bridge Standards and local Fire Protection District regulations. 
• Alleys & Landscaping: Removed the standalone alleys section, eliminated the 
mandatory Landscape Architect stamp, and removed the two-year landscaping warranty 
requirement. 
• Financial Securities: Restricted acceptable Subdivision Improvements Agreement (SIA) 
collateral strictly to irrevocable letters of credit or certified funds. 
• Sketch Plan Extensions: Formally removed the mechanism allowing sketch plan 
approval extensions. 
 
Additions & Procedural Expansions 
• Standardized Cost Estimates: Enacted Section 4.5, requiring an Engineer’s Opinion of 
Probable Cost (EOPC) certified by a Colorado-licensed Professional Engineer, with a 
mandatory 20% contingency and a 3-year shelf life. 
• Front-Loaded Submittal Requirements: Required surface and mineral ownership 
surveys, soil suitability tables, and title commitments issued within six months at the 
sketch plan phase. Added mandatory plat tables detailing EV charging infrastructure, 
dumpster enclosures, sign tracts, and dedicated park land. 
• As-Built Plats: Expanded mandatory as-built survey requirements to encompass all 
multi-family developments prior to preliminary acceptance.

• Legal Protections & Timelines: Introduced explicit plat dedication language disclaiming 
County maintenance and repair liability on private roads; restricted Planning 
Commission authority regarding variances that conflict with external County 
regulations; and expanded preliminary plat review periods from 55 to 65 days to 
incorporate a mandatory 10-day staff completeness review. 
 
Commission Discussion 
Commissioners reviewed the consolidation of single-family, linear-lot, and multi-family 
subdivisions into one section, and affirmed the elimination of alleys and the implementation of 
a 6-month recording requirement following final plat approval. 
Key discussion items included: 
• Calculation of Days: The Commission clarified that all procedural review timeframes, 
including the 10-day completeness review, shall be measured in calendar days rather 
than business days. 
• Architectural Review Discretion: Commissioners discussed architectural regulations—
specifically flat roof standards requiring a 3:12 minimum pitch up to a 35-foot height 
ceiling. Staff (M. LaBarre) indicated that County legal counsel will review the scope of 
the Planning Commission's discretion in enforcing architectural mandates versus private 
covenant enforcement (noting covenants such as Winter Park Ranch renew 
automatically every 10 years).

• Project Name: Planned Unit Development (PUD) Regulation Rewrite 
• Staff Presenter: Kristen Manguso Grand County Community Development, Director 
• Status: Discussion / Policy Direction (No formal action requested) 
Background & Procedural Context 
• Initiation: Grand County Community Development initiated a comprehensive rewrite of 
the County’s PUD regulations (unrevised since August 21, 2007) following development 
Grand County Planning Commission 
September 9, 2026 
 
 
 
 
Page 7 of 10 
 
 
inquiries regarding the Byers Peak Ranch property along County Road 721 near Fraser. 
Staff noted existing regulations are procedural relics and administratively burdensome. 
• Planning Commission Hearing (May 13, 2026): The Commission noted Grand County 
has processed only one PUD application in the last 25 years (which was ultimately 
withdrawn). Citing long-term tracking challenges experienced by neighboring 
jurisdictions (e.g., Town of Winter Park, Town of Fraser, and Weld County—which 
repealed PUDs in 2024), Commissioners concluded high-density developments requiring 
central water and sewer belong within municipal boundaries via annexation. The 
Commission recommended eliminating the PUD mechanism in its entirety, favoring 
updated residential zoning (R-1, R-2) in the Master Plan. 
• Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) Workshop (May 26, 2026): The BOCC reviewed 
the Planning Commission’s recommendation but directed Staff to retain and modernize 
the PUD mechanism, instructing them to prepare a revised regulatory framework. 
Proposed Draft Framework 
• Benchmarking: Modeled after the Colorado Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) 
Template Land Development Code and peer frameworks from Summit, Mesa, and Eagle 
counties. 
• Mechanism: Retains the PUD as a site-specific, negotiated "PUD Guide" reviewed by 
Staff and the Planning Commission and approved by the BOCC to provide dimensional 
relief (density, height, setbacks, and lot coverage). 
• Mandatory Public Benefits: In exchange for dimensional deviations, applicants must 
provide: 
1. Affordable Housing Component: Mandatory for any PUD incorporating a 
residential development component. 
2. Menu-Based Public Benefits (Minimum 1 Required): Permanent agricultural 
preservation, critical wildlife corridor protection, regional trail construction, 
historic resource preservation, advanced water conservation infrastructure, or 
public land dedication. 
Commission Discussion & Next Steps 
Staff requested initial guidance on the public benefit menu, code language adjustments, and 
alignment with Master Plan goals. 
 
During discussion, Commissioners reiterated reservations regarding the administrative 
feasibility of PUDs, questioning their long-term appropriateness for unincorporated Grand 
County. Following review, the Planning Commission reached a consensus to table further 
consideration of the PUD rewrite until after the comprehensive Master Plan update is 
completed and adopted.

Agenda Item 4: Grand County Master Plan Update 
• Project Name: Grand County Master Plan Comprehensive Update 
• Presenters: Logan Simpson (Consultation Company), Ben Osterling, and Kelly Nauman 
(Consulting Team) 
• Status: Presentation & Discussion 
Presentation Summary & Discussion Topics 
Public Engagement & Vision Statements 
Logan summarized community outreach outcomes, including feedback from seven in-person 
drop-in workshops and digital surveys. Public priorities centered on open space preservation, 
water resource protection, and conserving rural working landscapes. 
The Commission reviewed two draft vision statements: 
• Option 1: Emphasized interconnected, distinct communities and managed growth. 
• Option 2: Focused on enduring regional character, working landscapes, and growth 
balanced with natural resources. 
Commission Feedback: The Commission directed the team to consolidate both options into a 
single, concise, and aspirational statement that balances natural resource and water 
protections while recognizing both permanent residents and visitors. Detailed narrative 
elements will be housed in Chapter 2 of the final plan. 
Future Land Use Framework & Six Focus Areas 
Ben Nauman presented draft Future Land Use designations, emphasizing that designations 
serve as advisory, long-range policy guides rather than zoning boundaries. Land use mapping 
was structured around six regional nodes: Kremmling, Hot Sulphur Springs, Granby, Tabernash, 
Fraser/Winter Park, and Grand Lake. 
Proposed land use categories include: 
• Moderate-Density Residential 
• Rural Residential 
• Agriculture & Working Lands 
• Open Space & Conservation 
• Public, Civic & Institutional 
• Highway Commercial Corridor 
• Resort & Accommodations

• Mixed-Use Employment & Light Industrial 
 
Conservation, Agriculture, and Density 
The Commission examined how land use definitions intersect with private property rights and 
conservation easements: 
• Working Lands vs. Agricultural: Commissioners advised clear definitions, 
recommending "Agricultural" over broad terms like "Working Lands" to prevent 
ambiguity. 
• Rural Residential Lot Sizing: Questions were raised regarding minimum parcel sizes in 
rural zones to ensure development patterns do not exceed available groundwater 
supplies. 
Infrastructure & Municipal Coordination 
The Commission addressed growth boundaries adjacent to incorporated towns: 
• Service Responsibility: Commissioners emphasized that higher-density residential 
development adjacent to municipalities must be paired with clear municipal annexation 
strategies, ensuring developers and special districts—rather than County road crews—
remain responsible for ongoing infrastructure maintenance. 
• Map Adjustments: The team highlighted land use map adjustments emerging from 
regional workshops, including residential reclassifications near Fraser, mixed-use 
commercial and employment tracts near Granby Airport, and residential designations 
near Hot Sulphur Springs. A public web-based review tool will be released for 
community comment. 
Generational Framework & Document Structure 
The update introduces a "Generational Framework" organized around seven pillars: Water 
Conservation, Housing, Open Space, Wildlife, Resilience, Economy, and Tourism. The 
Commission supported this structure but stressed that policy recommendations must feature 
actionable implementation language and regulatory teeth. 
 
The proposed eight-chapter outline was accepted as presented:
