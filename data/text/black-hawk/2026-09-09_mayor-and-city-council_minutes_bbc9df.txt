Phil  Gibbs,  of  2107  Dory  Hill  Road,  Black  Hawk,  CO  80422,  signed  up
to speak  on  the Comprehensive  Plan.  He  had  prepared  his comments  to
read  into  the  record:
"l just  want  to make  sure  you  can  hear  me okay.
My name  is Phil Gibbs,  and my address  is 2107  Dory  Hill Rd, in Gilpin
County.
The  city's  2024  Comprehensive  Plan  envisions  Black  Hawk's  future
as a resort  with  new  casinos,  distilleries,  and  hotels  expanding  the
number  of hotel  rooms  from  1,500  to 3,000  over  the next  few  years
and  then  to 6,000  in the  following  years.
People  have  asked  whether  there  is a demand  study  for  a resort  of
this  scale.  They  ask  about  added  sanitation,  medical,  and law
enforcement  needs.  They  also  ask  about  water.
In May  of this  year,  city  council  approved  Resolution  33-2026,
authorizing  the contract  for  Four  Mile  Gulch  Well  #6 near  the  bottom
of Dory  Hill Road.  Well  #6 will be drilled  to a depth  of 1,000  feet. This
one  well  will deliver  up to 161 -acre  feet  of water  per  year,  or
approximately  52 million  gallons.  Enough  for  500  homes.  Aspen
Springs  and  Chalet  Park  currently  have  just  over  300  homes.
The  introduction  to the resolution  states  that  city  staff  is pursuing
drilling  a "well  or multiple  wells  while  sites  are still viable  in the  area";
the phrase  "while  still viable"  raises  a red flag  concerning  risks  to the
residential  water  supply.
The  city's  Alternatives  Analysis  Report  produced  by ERO  Resources,
states  that  the  maximum  annual  flow  rate  in the  city is currently  482-
acre  feet  per  year,  or approximately  157  million  gallons.
3
If the  resort  plan  is fully  realized,  the  report  forecasts  the  maximum
annual  flow  rate  in the  city  to be 3,070-acre  feet,  or approximately  one
billion  gallons.
ERO  Resources  recommends  ground  water  from  Aspen  Springs  and
new,  larger  reservoirs  in Pickle  Gulch  between  Missouri  Lakes  and
Paradise  Valley  as the  primary  sources  to meet  the  one-billion-gallon
tourist  needs.
The  city's  plan  will  likely  generate  a great  deal  of institutional  wealth.
However,  none  of  the  documents  that  I have  read  cite  potential  impact
to nearby  residential  neighborhoods.
In addition  to the  risks  to Aspen  Springs  and  Chalet  Park  water
supply,  residents  near  the  Gilpin  County  Schools  have  similar
concerns  while  seeing  no tangible  benefit  to the  community  at large.
I am  asking  you  to pause  plans  to drill  additional  wells  and  consider
impact  or  demonstrate  that  there  is no harm.
And  I thank  you."
Mayor  Spellman  stated,  "Okay,  your  comments  are a public  record
now."

B. Resolution  53-2026,  A Resolution  to Encourage  Diversification  of the City's  Local
Economy  by  Waiving  Use  Tax  on Renovation  Projects
Mayor  Spellman  read  the  title.
Acting  Finance  Director  Parsons  explained  that the resolution,  if
approved,  would  waive  the  use tax  owed  on renovation  and/or  upgrades
to existing  commercial  facilities  for  projects  with  a completed  permit
application  by April  4, 2028.
Mayor  Spellman  explained  that  is a similar  program  to a previous  one
used  in  the GOLD  District.  This  program  takes  in  a larger  area  including
businesses  in the HARD  District  and Artisans'  Point.
