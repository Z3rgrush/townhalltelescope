5. MAYOR  AND  BOARD
OF ALDERMEN
COMMENTS: Mayor  Spellman  stated  that  he learned  last week  that  Gilpin  County  is
discussing  placing  a countywide  sales  tax  on  the  November  2026  ballot.
On  Monday,  February  23, 2026,  Mayor  Spellman,  City  Manager  Cole,
and Finance  Director  Hillis  met  with  School  Board  President  Joe Marr
and Superintendent  David  MacKenzie.  During  their  meeting,  the school
representatives  confirmed  that  County  Manager  Rears  had  informed  the
School  District  that  the  Board  of  County  Commissioners  is considering
a sales  tax  proposal.
The  Mayor  emphasized  that  the County  is aware  that  if  such  a tax is
placed  on the ballot  and  approved  by Gilpin  County  voters,  the City  of
Black  Hawk's  1.5%  Education  Enhancement  Tax  would  automatically
terminate.  He  stated  that  if  the Commissioners  move  forward  with  a
countywide  sales  tax  that  results  in  the loss  of  funding  to the Gilpin  RE-
1 School  District,  they  should  be held  publicly  accountable  for  that
outcome.  At  that  point,  Mayor  Spellman  stated  the Commissioners
should  be known  as "School  Wreckers."
In  2008,  the School  District  faced  severe  financial  challenges,  and  there
were  serious  concerns  about  whether  it could  continue  operating
independently  or would  need  to be absorbed  by a neighboring  district.
At  that  time,  the City  of  Black  Hawk  proposed  an Intergovernmental
Agreement  (IGA)  with  the School  District  to establish  a 1.5%
Education  Enhancement  (Sales)  Tax.  This  tax  was  approved  by Black
Hawk  voters  and  has since  provided  critical  funding  to support  the
school.  Since  January  2009,  the Education  Enhancement  Tax  has
ge-nerated-$-1 €,948,577  tmoug-h December  2025.  Tbi-s tax  benefits  all
Gilpin  County  residents  and  businesses.
Mayor  Spellman  stated  that  Black  Hawk  stepped  in  to save  the School
District  once,  but  if  Gilpin  County  adopts  a countywide  sales  tax  that
causes  the Education  Enhancement  Tax  to terminate,  responsibility  for
replacing  that  funding  would  rest  with  the County.  "If  you  break  it, you
fix  it,"  he stated.
It has been  reported  that  at least  one County  Commissioner,  Jeff  Aiken,
believes  Black  Hawk  would  be pressured  to continue  financial  support
for  the  school  even  if  a countywide  sales  tax  is approved.  However,
both  the ballot  language  approved  by Black  Hawk  voters  and  the IGA
2
provisions  are self-executing.  They  clearly  state  that  the Educational
Enhancement  Tax  will  cease if  certain  future  taxes,  including  a
countywide  sales tax, are adopted  in Gilpin  County.
Accordingly,  if  Gilpin  County  voters  approve  a countywide  sales  tax,
the City  of  Black  Hawk  will  no longer  collect  or remit  the 1.5%
Education  Enhancement  Tax,  and the Gilpin  RE-1 School  District
would  lose  that  funding  source.
Alderman  Midcap  stated  that  if  the County  imposes  a 1.5%  sales  tax,  it
will  generate  approximately  $1.5  million  annually  for  the County,  while
the School  District  would  lose  the 1.5%  Educational  Enhancement  Tax.
He further  noted  that  if  the Board  of  County  Commissioners  had
accepted  Black  Hawk's  2024  multi-year  offer  of  $1 million  to support
the Recreation  Center,  the  County  could  have  secured  that  funding
without  jeopardizing  the School  District's  revenue.  In  his  view,  a
countywide  sales  tax  would  negatively  impact  residents  throughout  the
County.
