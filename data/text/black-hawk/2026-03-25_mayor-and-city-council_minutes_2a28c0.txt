Mayor  Spellman  said  we  are deeply  honored  to welcome  Mr.  Don  Eaton,
who  will  turn  102  in May.  He  is a World  War  II veteran  who  served  in
the  US  Marine  Corps  during  the  Battle  of  Iwo  Jima.  The  Marine  Corps
Hymn  was  then  played.  Mayor  Spellman  considers  him  a local  treasure
and recognized  Don  with  a plaque,  which  he read.  Mayor  Spellman
thought  it  would  be fitting  to have  this  recognition  today,  since  the  battle
l
took  place  from  February  19 to March  26, 81 years  after  the  battle.  He
said  on Don's  lOO'h birthday,  the  City  made  him  an honorary  citizen  of
Black  Hawk.  He  reminded  everyone  that  as we  celebrate  this
sesquisemiquincentennial  of  America  and  Colorado,  we  should
recognize  and honor  what  Don's  generation,  the Greatest  Generation,
sacrificed  and  endured  so we  can  enjoy  the  lives  that  we  live  today.  How

10.  ACTION  ITEMS:
B. Resolution  17-2026,  A Resolution  Reaffirming  that  the  Education  Enhancement  Tax  as
Approved  by  the  Voters  of  the  City  is Subject  to Certain  Self-Executing  Conditions,  the
Violation  of  Which  Automatically  Terminates  Said  Tax
Mayor  Spellman  read  the  title.
Mayor  Spellman  said  he invited  Gilpin  School  Board  President  Joe Marr
and  Superintendent  Dr.  Dave  Mackenzie  to attend.
Mayor  Spellman  referenced  a report  County  Manager  Rears  gave  to the
commissioners  at their  last  workshop.  He  then  read  his  comments  from
the first  check  presentation  on May  8, 2009,  and the 10th-year  check
presentation  on May  8, 2019.  What's  important  to see is what  has been
accomplished  and  how  successful  the  tax  has been,  he said. In surnrnary,
there  were  always  three  distinct  goals  the  City  had  for  this  tax:  1) to help
ensure  a reasonable  and responsible  tax  rate,  2) to provide  a revenue
source  for  the  school  that  increased  as Black  Hawk  prospered,  being  one
step  closer  to  a resort  community,  and 3) to  enhance  educational
programs  and  opportunities  for  students  at the school.  From  2009,  the
tax  produced  $481,326,  to 20l8's  amount  of  $978,811,  doubling  in less
than  10  years,  to the  current  day,  closing  in  on  20 million  dollars  provided
to the  school.
Dr.  Dave  MacKenzie  first  said  that  on behalf  of  students,  parents,  and
staff,  he wanted  to extend  a sincere  and  heartfelt  thank  you  to the  Black
Hawk  City  Council,  the  voters  of  the City  of  Black  Hawk,  and  Mayor
Spellman  and  his  staff  for  supporting  the  Educational  Enhancement  Tax
since  2008  for  the  Gilpin  County  School.  He gratefully  provided  a long
list  of  items  supported  by  the  tax.
Mayor  Spellman  remembered  that  at the original  check  presentation,
there  were  two  State  Representatives  who  were  supportive  of  this  tax,
knowing  the conditions;  there  were  also  representatives  from  the State
Department  of  Education.
School  Board  President  Joe Marr  said  it's been  amazing  for  the school
district.  Once  a year,  they  have  a get together  with  other  school  board
members  from  around  the state,  and  they  are always  tracking  us down
and  asking  how  we  did  this,  people  think  it's  amazing  from  the  school
side  of  things.  He said  there  is a plaque  hanging  in the  board  member
room  that  reads  "what's  best  for  kids",  and  what  the  City  did  was  best  for
kids,  and he would  ask the County  Commissioners  to ask that  same
question.
Mayor  Spellman  asked  the City  Clerk  to send  this  Resolution  to the
county  so that  they  understand  the City's  position.  He also  asked  the
School  Board  to look  at adding  a companion  resolution.
