COMMENTS: Mayor  Spellman  said,  in keeping  with  the season  of  Halloween  Street
and  the  fall  festivals,  he would  like  to give  a scream  out  to all  BlackHawk
team  members  who  participated  in making  the festival  on Saturday  a
spooktacular  and  frightfully  successful  event.  He  said  it  was  a monstrous
turnout,  truly  spectacular  and  beyond  expectations.
Alderman  Midcap  agreed  that  it  was  a huge  success  and  that  it was  the
most  people  he had  seen  in one  day.  The  parking  areas  were  filled  to
capacity  all the way  up, including  the Red  Dolly  and  Ed Smith's  lot.
Again,  thanks  to all  team  members  for  making  it happen.
Alderman  Torres  wished  to comment  on Ballot  Issue  IA.  He  said  he had
never  heard  of  something  worded  like  that  before.  To  him,  it sounds  like
the  County  Commissioners  want  bold  restraint  on  their  capacity  to levy
taxes  and  how  to spend  them  to be overthrown.  There  is little  promise
that  they  won't  raise  taxes,  but  they  could.  He  believes  they  plan  to raise
taxes  on homeowners  whose  property  taxes  just  went  up, so it would  be
a double  whammy  on homeowners  to be taxed  on  that  added  value.  He
said  all  they  can  think  about  is raising  taxes;  they  refused  the  money  we
offered  for  the  rec  center,  and  their  solution  always  seems  to be raising
taxes  on homeowners.  He  believes  the Commissioners  would  be much
better  served  if  they  came  up with  something  that  would  unite  the
County.  He  threw  the  idea  out  to bring  the  kids  from  the  northern  part  of
the  county  to the  Gilpin  school  to do something  where  the  county  can  get
together,  instead  of  continuing  to divide.  He  encourages  voters  to vote
against  IA  and that  this  is not  the time  to raise  property  taxes.  He
concluded  by  saying  that  the  Ballot  Issue  does  not  say  that  it would  raise
taxes,  but  he can't  imagine  it  would  have  any  other  purpose.
Alderman  Bennett  thanked  everyone  for  their  concem  for  him  while  he
was  injured.

B. Resolution  57-2026,  A Resolution  Approving  a Professional  Services  Agreement  with
Good  People  Tree  Service  in  an  Amount  Not  to Exceed  $250,000.00  for  the  Remainder  of
Calendar  Year  2026
Mayor  Spellman  read  the  title.
Fire  Chief  Woolley  introduced  this  item  as an  effort  to  continue
mitigation  and  forest  management.  He presented  a map  and  explained
priority  areas  1-3.  Alderman  Midcap  had  a few  questions.  Chief  Woolley
confirmed  that  they  cut down  old-growth  trees  only  when  they  are
affected  by disease  or bugs.  They  will  always  favor  mature  trees  to
reduce  competition  with  regenerating  trees,  so mature  trees  can  continue
to grow  without  stress.

C. Resolution  58-2026,  A  Resolution  Awarding  the  Bid  and  Approving  the  Contract  Between
the  City  of  Black  Hawk  and  Grapes  & Sons  Excavating  in an Amount  Not  to Exceed
$160,400.00  for  Construction  of  the  Bobtail  Wall  Excavation  Project
Mayor  Spellman  read  the  title.
City  Engineer  Reed  introduced  this item  and provided  an image  of  the
project  area.  He said the slope just  outside  the portal  needs to be
stabilized  before  additional  mining-related  amenities  can be displayed.
Stabilizing  the slope  would  be the main  goal,  with  the added  benefit  of
creating  more  usable  flat  space.  After  this  project  is finished,  he said,
they  would  survey  the area  to introduce  a separate  contract  to Council  to
create  a wood  cribbing  retaining  wall  fagade.  Grapes  and Sons  submitted
the lowest  bid among  14 bids  received,  and they  should  be able to
complete  this  project  by the end of  the year.
MOTION  TO
APPROVE Alderman  Bennett  MOVED  and  was  SECONDED  by Alderman  Torres
to approve  Resolution  58-2026,  a Resolution  awarding  the bid and
approving  the contract  between  the City  of  Black  Hawk  and Grapes  &
Sons  Excavating  in  an  amount  not  to  exceed  $160,400.00  for
construction  of  the Bobtail  Wall  Excavation  Project.

Special  Events  &  Marketing  Manager  Ryan  then  provided  an update  on
the  Fall  Festival  and  Halloween  Street,  noting  that  it was  a great  success,
with  far  more  people  than  anticipated.  Between  paid  and  earned  strong
social  media,  the event  had  over  2.2 million  views.  Also,  social  media
influencers  posted,  the  City  jumped  on those  coattails,  and  we  had  the
Pumpkin  Passport,  which  was an activity  of  receiving  stamps  at the
HARD  district  businesses  and  redeeming  them  for  a pumpkin  decorating
kit.  Within  the  first  10 days,  she said,  we  gave  away  240  kits,  which  was
the number  purchased  to last  for  the whole  month.  From  talking  with
visitors,  she said  there  was  a good  amount  of  new  visitors  and  record
sales  for  businesses.  She said  the candy  store  ran  out  of  candy.  There
were  close  to 5000  people  there,  and  it did  place  a heavy  burden  on  the
staff  working  the  event,  but  they  all  kept  smiles  on  their  faces  and  made
our  visitors  feel  warm  and  welcome  with  that  small-community  feel.  She
wanted  to personally  thank  all staff  who  worked  the event:  Steve
Martinez,  Chris  Vincent,  Thomas  Tabbert,  Jim  Eckert,  Tara  Lohman,
Chris  Keller,  Alex  Ponton,  Zack  Zeckster,  Carrie  Yerkman,  Corey
Colombin,  and  Tara  Sergo,  and  thanked  everyone  else  in Public  Works
who  made  it happen.  She  said  she advertised  in  the  local  newspaper,  but
social  media  was  the  biggest  form  of  advertising.  Everyone  gave  a round
of  applause  for  a job  well  done!
