ACTIVITY:  PL2026044 
APPELLANT: Kevin Rohnstock and Claudia Anderson
REQUEST:  Adjustment to the property line setback to allow an 
addition/reconstruction of the garage
Required:  50 ft. from the property line
Requested:  47.6 ft. from the west property line for an adjustment of 
2.4 ft.
LOCATION:  31650 Aspen Ridge Road; located 0.22 miles from the 
intersection of Aspen Ridge Road and Agate Creek 
Road
Ms. Sally Kavanagh, an architect representing the petitioner, reviewed the 
previous hearing. She stated that the original design for the garage expansion 
had been based on a survey provided by Four Points Surveying and 
Engineering. According to that survey the proposed garage only encroached very 
slightly into the setback. She said that during the homeowners’ association 
(HOA) review process, which took a very long time, the HOA had decided that it 
wanted to revisit the building envelopes in the subdivision. For this process, a 
new survey was done by D & D which was approximately 5 ft. different in most 
locations from the previous survey and resulted in the proposed garage 
encroaching into the setback by 5 ft. 9 in. This is the plan that was originally 
presented to the BOA in July. Ms. Kavanaugh said that the design had also 
reflected the desires of the HOA to pull the building as far as possible back into

the hillside. Mr. Rohnstock, the homeowner, said that following the July BOA 
meeting they had revised the plan and had moved the proposed garage several 
feet to the east and thereby reduced the adjustment request from 5 ft. 9 in. to 2 ft. 
6 in. 
Mr. Jaeger summarized the BOA discussion from the July meeting, noting that 
the BOA had suggested that the proposed building could be moved to the east 
and that they would be willing to entertain a reduced adjustment request. Mr. 
Jaeger presented diagrams of the original plan and the revised plan and 
indicated how the degree of encroachment had been reduced with the new 
design. He also provided aerial views of the neighborhood and mapping of the 
steep slopes in the area. He noted that the footprint of the proposed garage with 
residential space above had not changed, but had been moved several feet to 
the east. He stated that the location of the garage presented some engineering 
and operational challenges due to snow load and snowmelt concerns, as well as 
concerns regarding limiting the available natural light into and views from the 
residence. Mr. Jaeger noted that a Secondary Dwelling Unit would not be 
allowed on this property, so if plumbing is extended to the residential area above 
the garage, the applicant would need to sign a plumbing agreement that would 
limit the use of this space. This agreement would be processed in conjunction 
with the building permit. Mr. Jaeger presented elevations of the existing garage 
and the proposed expanded garage, as well as photos of the site and the 
surrounding area. Mr. Jaeger reviewed the criteria that must be met for an 
adjustment to be approved and the reasons that staff had determined that the 
revised proposal meets those criteria. He reviewed the suggested conditions of 
approval.
Mr. Blau commended the petitioner and staff for working on the redesign of the 
proposed structure to reduce the adjustment request.
In response to a question from Mr. Krug, Ms. Kavanaugh confirmed that only one 
corner of the garage would encroach into the required setback.
Mr. Hoffman asked if the discrepancy between the two surveys had been 
resolved. Ms. Kavanaugh stated that the D&D survey had employed newer 
technology and had used a different survey corner pin as its basis. Mr. Kelly 
noted that some pins are based on an older grid and some on a newer grid. This 
problem applies to all of Dakota Ridge, as well as some other areas in the 
County.
There was no public comment.

required property line setback from the west property line, for a setback of 47.6 ft. 
This approval is based on the following findings of fact:

ACTIVITY:  PL2026043 
APPELLANT: Tyson and Ashley Sweetser
REQUEST:  Adjustment to the property line setback for an 
agricultural outbuilding
Required:  50 ft. from the property line
Requested:  18 ft. from the west property line for an adjustment of 32 
ft.
LOCATION:  At the west intersection of County Road 69 and Highway 
40; located at 11635 County Road 69
Ms. Ashley Sweetser reviewed the history of the property, which was purchased 
by Tyson Sweetser’s grandparents in 1979. She stated that she and her husband 
purchased the property in 2022. When they applied for a building permit for a 
new home it was discovered that several outbuildings were not in conformance 
with the required setbacks. Some were determined to be legally non-conforming 
and others not. All of the illegal structures were removed except for the 
agricultural shed (3-sided pole barn) which is necessary to their agricultural 
operation. Ms. Sweetser stated that they had discussed applying for an 
adjustment for the shed at that time, but had been strongly discouraged from 
doing so by Planning staff, who suggested that an adjustment was unlikely to be 
approved. Ms. Sweetser reviewed the process through which they had originally 
thought they would have to demolish the end of the shed that encroaches into 
setback, but ended up getting approval to only remove the roof from the north
R.C.B.O.A. MINUTES August 10, 2026
5
end, leaving the walls, which were then considered to be an engineered fence. 
The remaining structure was determined to be structurally sound. She said that 
the roof was removed, but that without the roof the shed did not function well, so 
they put the roof back on. This led to the Notice of Violation and the code 
enforcement case. She said that they have been working with County staff on a 
solution. Ms. Sweetser said that it had been a mistake not to request an 
adjustment prior to building the house, and that they regret not having done so 
before the roof was removed. She presented photos of the structure with and 
without the roof.
Ms. Sweetser presented a diagram of the property with the uses of each area 
shaded. She noted that the location of the existing buildings, driveway, circulation 
pattern for agricultural equipment, septic system and leach field constrain the 
areas available for other uses, without taking away productive hay land. She 
noted that although the parcel is small (approximately 7.5 acres) it is 
agriculturally productive. She reviewed the agricultural operation that is 
conducted on the property. Ms. Sweetser discussed the possible alternative 
locations for the shed and provided the reasons that none of them would be 
practical, or in some cases even feasible. She emphasized that in order to 
function properly, the 3-sided shed must be oriented so that that the back is to 
the west, shielding the open bays from the prevailing wind and weather.
Ms. Sweetser stated that Planning staff agrees that criteria #4, #6 and #7 are met 
by the request. She added that there have been no complaints about the 
structure, the structure does not interfere with road maintenance or plowing, the 
neighbors support the adjustment, and many properties in the vicinity have 
similar structures to support their small agricultural operations. Ms. Sweetser 
added that moving the shed to another location would cause operational 
difficulties, reduce the usefulness of the shed, and move the impact elsewhere.
Regarding criteria #1, Ms. Sweetser stated that without the roof, the structure 
does not function as a pole barn to protect livestock, hay and equipment, that 
relocating the shed would consume productive agricultural land and would 
require additional disturbance. She said that having to relocate the shed would 
be an unnecessary hardship and would negatively impact their agricultural 
operation.
Regarding criteria #2 & #3, Ms. Sweetser stated that removing the roof again is 
not an option that is on the table, and did not work when it was tried. She said 
any of the alternatives to the current location would create new impacts and no 
further encroachment into the setback is being requested. She noted that the 
with the roofless walls, the encroachment into the setback was visually the same 
as with the roof. Ms. Sweetser said that stock/hay sheds are a common use, and 
that many sheds encroach into setbacks throughout the County.
R.C.B.O.A. MINUTES August 10, 2026
6
Regarding criteria #5, Ms. Sweetser stated that the structure was in its current 
location when they purchased the property. She said that restoring the roof was 
not when the encroachment into the setback began; it began with the 
construction of the shed. She acknowledged that the prior owners (Mr. 
Sweetser’s grandparents) had built the shed without a permit. She said that they 
were under the impression that because the shed was to be used exclusively for 
agricultural purposes, and is not served by electricity or plumbing, that it did not 
require a permit.
Regarding criteria #8, Ms. Sweetser quoted several policies and sections of the 
Routt County Master Plan that strongly support the preservation of agriculture 
and rural character. She also noted that the Master Plan encourages the 
continuing use and reuse of existing structures.
Ms. Sweetser stated that the least impactful outcome would be for the existing 
shed to be allowed to remain where it is. She asked that the BOA approve the 
adjustment request with reasonable conditions, if necessary, and allow the shed 
to remain in its current location.
Mr. Jaeger reviewed the history of the parcel and the timeline, as previously 
discussed by Ms. Sweetser. A detailed account of the dates is provided in the 
staff report. Mr. Jaeger presented an aerial view of the site and indicated the 
various structures and uses on the property. He also presented a map showing 
the steep slopes that encumber a portion of the parcel. He presented and 
reviewed the diagram indicating the uses of different parts of the parcel. He 
indicated the portion of the shed that encroaches into the setback, and that was 
subject to the demolition permit that led to the removal of the roof from the 
northern end of the shed. He also presented photos of the site from a variety of 
locations, including photos of the shed with and without the roof.
Mr. Jaeger reviewed the criteria that must be met for an adjustment to be 
approved and discussed the reasons that staff had determined that criteria #1, 
#2, #3, #5, and #8 cannot be supported. A detailed analysis of the criteria is 
provided in the staff report. He emphasized that in removing the roof, the 
applicant had demonstrated that compliance with the regulations was possible 
and added that there is room for the shed to be located elsewhere on the 
property.
In response to a question from Mr. Hoffman, Mr. Jaeger confirmed that parking 
campers, trailers, and equipment is allowed in the setback. Mr. Hoffman asked if 
the shed could be relocated to the south end of the older shed, where equipment 
is currently parked. The camper, trailer, and equipment could then be moved into 
the setback. Mr. Sweetser said that this would be possible, but would entail the 
hardship of demolition and reconstruction, including additional grading, 
excavation, etc.

Mr. Krug asked about the history of the use of the parcel. Mr. Sweetser reviewed 
the history, noting that it had been in continuous agricultural use since 1979. He 
stated that his grandfather had constructed the addition onto the shed. He said 
that shed is used for livestock, including his children’s 4-H lambs, as well as to 
store agricultural equipment and hay. He confirmed that the agricultural operation 
on the parcel was small and that both he and his wife have full-time jobs.
Mr. Pedersen asked when the addition to the shed was built. Mr. Jaeger clarified 
that the original shed as built in 1990 and the addition that encroaches into the 
setback was built sometime between 2009 and 2011. He reviewed the process 
through which all elements of a property are reviewed and must be brought into 
conformance with the regulations prior to the issuance of a building permit. He 
confirmed that if the Sweetser’s had not applied for a building permit, the shed 
would not have been flagged for enforcement. In response to a question from Mr. 
Pedersen, Ms. Sweetser said that they had not pursued an adjustment for the 
shed because Planning staff had strongly discouraged them from doing so. Mr. 
Sweetser added that it seemed like an expensive process to go through if they 
were just going to be denied.
Mr. Pedersen asked about operational changes that had occurred when the roof 
was removed. Mr. Sweetser confirmed that the haying equipment was not 
protected, that the hay, which is usually stored in the northern stalls due to the 
circulation pattern of equipment, had to be put elsewhere. He added that without 
the northern wall, snow blew into and would have accumulated in the rest of the 
shed. He noted that he shares the haying equipment with his father, and that it is 
important to protect it.
Mr. Pedersen asked about the other outbuildings that had been removed. Mr. 
Sweetser described them and said that there was little impact from their removal.
Mr. Kelly stated that he is bothered by applying the 50 ft. setback to agricultural 
structures. He offered that barns and sheds have traditionally be located near the 
property lines on agricultural properties for a reason: to preserve as much 
productive agricultural land as possible. He stated that the County is trying to 
preserve agriculture in Routt County, but that this 50 ft. setback regulation does 
not reflect this common practice. He noted that many outbuildings are located in 
the setbacks throughout the County. Mr. Kelly stated that the strict application of 
the setback regulation is punishing people for trying to improve their property. He 
acknowledged the tension between compliance with the regulation and the 
function of the building, and offered that he was torn regarding this application. 
Mr. Kelly stated that many policies of the Master Plan are designed to help 
agricultural operations, and suggested that common sense needs to be applied. 
He also noted that four letters of support for the adjustment that had been 
submitted.

Mr. Fitz reviewed the discussion that had led to allowing the applicant to remove

Mr. Blau said that he would like there to be a compromise. He asked if half of the

Public Comment 
Mr. Doug Monger, a neighboring property owner, stated that enforcing the strict 
setback on this property would be penalizing the Sweetsers for improving their 
property. He said that agricultural structures did not need building permits when 
this structure was built and that it was constructed prior to the purchase of the 
property. He stated his support for the adjustment.
Ms. Tegan Ebbert, a neighboring property owner, stated that the small 
agricultural properties along CR 69 are unique. She said that in order to maintain 
the agricultural function of the property, structures must be located to preserve all 
the productive agricultural land available. She acknowledged that removing the 
roof to allow the walls to become fences was a bad idea. She said that the 
applicant had put the roof back on out of necessity to restore the practical use of 
the shed. She urged the BOA to consider the most practical solution.
Ms. Karen Gilroy, a neighboring property owner, reviewed the history of the 
parcel. She stated that consideration should be given to these small, 
continuously functioning agricultural properties that were created when US 40 
was relocated. She said that the existing shed is not in the way of road plowing 
or maintenance and that no purpose was being served by strictly enforcing the 
setback requirement.
Seeing not further comment, Mr. Blau closed public comment.
In response to a question from Mr. Krug, Ms. Gilroy described how the small 
agricultural parcels between CR 69 and US 40 were created in the 1950s when 
the highway was re-aligned.

MOTION
Mr. Krug moved to approve item PL20260043, a request for an adjustment to the 
required property setback to allow all but one bay of the existing pole barn to 
remain in its current location, with the following findings of fact:
1. Peculiar and exceptional practical difficulties or an unnecessary and 
unreasonable hardship will be imposed on the property owner if the 
provisions of this UDC are strictly enforced 
2. The requested Adjustment is the minimum deviation necessary to enable 
reasonable use of the property. Removing one bay from the north end out 
the shed would reduce the encroachment.
3. The Adjustment is necessary to provide the property with comparable use 
rights and privileges permitted to other properties in the vicinity and in the 
applicable zone district. The structure’s integrity both structurally and 
functionally can be maintained if one bay is removed. Agricultural sheds 
are common on comparable properties in the AF zone district.
4. The Adjustment, if granted, will not confer a personal convenience or 
special privilege to the applicant because the size and use of the structure

are common in the area, with other parcels having similar structures in the 
area, making this not out of place in the area.
5. Circumstances creating the hardship were in existence on the effective 
date of the regulations from which an adjustment is requested or were 
created subsequently through no fault of the applicant. The structure was 
built by the prior property owner and was in existence when the applicant 
purchased the property.
6. The property for which an Adjustment is requested possesses a site-

This approval is subject to the following conditions;
1. The applicant shall remove one bay from the north end of the existing 
shed in its entirety.
2. All applicable permits related to the removal of the bay shall be obtained 
within six months of this approval (by March 10, 2027).
3. All work associated with the removal of one bay of the shed shall be 
completed within two years of this approval (by August 10, 2028).
Mr. Kelly seconded the motion.
Discussion and Friendly Amendments
Mr. Hoffman proposed allowing the applicant the option of either removing the 
walls from the end of the structure and leaving only the roof or removing one bay 
entirely. This friendly amendment failed. There was consensus that the applicant 
be required to remove one bay of the shed entirely.
There was discussion of conditions of approval. There was consensus to add a 
condition requiring the applicant to obtain all necessary permits for the removal of 
one bay within six months of this approval. There was also consensus to require 
that all work associated with the removal of the bay be completed within two 
years of this approval (August 10, 2028).
These amendments are reflected in the motion above.
