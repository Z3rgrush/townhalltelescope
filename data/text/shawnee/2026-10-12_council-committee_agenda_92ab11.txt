SUBJECT: Amendment to 10.04.126.2 - Use of Wireless Communication Devices 
 
BACKGROUND: 
Over the years, with changing and evolving technology and social media, Shawnee Police 
Department officers are routinely seeing concerning and dangerous driver behavior related to 
cell phone usage that is not covered by the language in our Shawnee Municipal Code (SMC). 
Some examples of concerning driving behavior that officers have recently observed are: a 
commercial through scrolling drivers truck, dump TikTok a in scrolling driver miniature 
Facebook, drivers online shopping while driving, etc.  
  
As currently written, SMC limits the behavior to text or email only (i.e. sending written 
communication), yet this is rarely the reason drivers are on their phones while driving. With 
everyone having a smart phone and internet and app access at their fingertips, the majority of 
drivers are engaging with their phones in ways that are not covered by our current SMC: 
internet surfing, Facebooking, engaging on other social media platforms (TikTok, Instagram, 
Snapchat, etc.). Without being able to hold distracted drivers accountable for this behavior, the 
roads will only grow more dangerous as drivers continue to use their cell phones while driving, 
and if unchecked, that behavior will only grow more common over time. 
 
DISCUSSION: 
The legal department recommends amending 10.04.126.2 Use of Wireless Communication 
Devices to read as follows:  
 
10.04.126.2 Use of Wireless Communication Devices. 
A. Except as provided in subsections B and C, no person shall operate a motor vehicle on a 
public road or highway while using a wireless communications device to write, send or read a 
written communication, engage in video communication or video calls, watch videos, or access 
or use phone applications or internet browsers. 
B. The provisions of subsection A shall not apply to:  
1. A law enforcement officer or emergency service personnel acting within the course and 
scope of the law enforcement officers or emergency service personnel's employment;  
2. A motor vehicle stopped off the regular traveled portion of the roadway;  
3. A person who reads, selects or enters a telephone number or name in a wireless 
communications device for the purpose of making or receiving a phone call;

SUBJECT: SMC Chapter 12.26 - Excise Tax On Platting Property Overview 
 
BACKGROUND: 
In August 1998, the Governing Body passed an ordinance establishing the collection of an 
excise tax on property that is platted. The purpose of the excise tax is to generate revenue to be 
used for general city projects or operations that are needed to support new development within 
the community. Shawnee Municipal Code (SMC) Chapter 12.26 regulates collection of the 
Excise Tax (SMC Chapter 12.26 Link).  
  
At the time of original adoption, the excise tax rate was 13 cents per square foot of property 
included in the plat. The excise tax increased over time, and the last increase in the excise tax 
was in May 2004 to the current 21.5 cents per square foot of platted land area. Between 1998 
and 2004, our peer cities also adopted an excise tax on development. Today Overland Park, 
Olathe, and Lenexa collect the same 21.5 cents per square foot of platted property. Prior to 
creation of the tax abatement / rebate policies, excise tax revenue was placed in the City's 
General Fund.   
  
The Kansas Legislature adopted K.S.A. 12-194 effective in July 2006 requiring any increase 
from a presently collected excise tax to be placed on the ballot at an election. The statute also 
prevented cities that did not have an excise tax from collecting such a tax. Additionally, if a city 
rescinds an excise tax adopted previous to July 2006, it may not be reinstated at a later date. 
  
By current ordinance, SMC 12.26.040 exempts right-of-way dedicated on the plat for any major 
(collector or arterial) street from the taxable area of the plat. This section also provides an 
exemption for land dedicated and accepted by the City as public parkland within a plat. SMC 
12.26.050 permanent for tax excise the against a dollar for dollar provides also credit 
improvements made to any major street within or adjacent to the platted area. These street 
classifications are identified on the City's approved street circulation plan.   
 
DISCUSSION: 
As a result of the great recession, development activity in the City slowed significantly. 
Construction of new residential and commercial properties was further hampered by the 
financial banking crisis and the lack of capital for investment. Several larger tracts of land that

had been preliminary platted and zoned within Shawnee prior to the recession reverted back to 
the original banking lenders.  
  
In response to that on March 5, 2013, the Governing Body passed an ordinance approving a 
two-year suspension of the City’s excise tax to stimulate the development of vacant land within 
the City. The Governing Body then passed ordinances extending the excise tax suspension 
program in December 2014, October 2016, and November 2017, March 2021, and March 2022.  
  
On October 27, 2025, the Governing Body altered Policy Statement 21  that changed the 
abatement program to a tax rebate program. The administration of the current program is non-
discretionary. Essentially, any entity that requests excise tax relief is granted the exemption. 
This has resulted in some very small projects gaining the exemption as well as non-property tax 
and/or non-sales tax generating projects. All exemptions are granted by the Governing Body in 
the form of an Excise Tax Rebate Agreement that sets performance standards and level of 
development that must be achieved to allow a rebate of excise tax funds that have been 
previously collected.  
  
Relieving potential developers of the excise tax expense has been successful at stimulating 
development. Residential, commercial, office, and warehouse construction volumes have 
recovered significantly since 2013, including times during the COVID pandemic. The amount of 
tax abated or now set for rebate has equaled $9,221,909.66 since 2013. 
  
Staff will provide additional excise tax policy information for other KC metro. communities at the 
Council Committee meeting.
