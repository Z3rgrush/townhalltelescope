MS. BELLMANN:  Good evening.  This is the annual time when we receive the updated 
Uniform Public Offense Code for Kansas.  For those of you who may not be as familiar 
with the UPOC, as we call it for short, essentially, this guides our municipal laws when it 
comes to criminal offenses.  Some jurisdictions opt out of utilizing the UPOC and write 
all of their own ordinances.  A lot of Kansas cities utilize the UPOC, which updates 
annually and keeps us kind of up to date with any state law changes that happen 
through Senate bills, new laws passing, things of that nature.   
 
There isn't anything too groundbreaking this year in the updates.  But we do like to go 
over each one of the items so that you can ask questions, or if there are any concerns 
or anything so that you're aware of what those updates look like.  In addition, we do also 
have ordinances that govern our traffic offenses predominantly, but we do have a few 
ordinances that cover some of our more criminal codes.  So, some examples would be 
our interference ordinance or animal cruelty, which the UPOC utilizes, but we opt out of

[Ordinance Amendments slide] 
The first section of this will just be to our ordinances.  The first ordinance amendment 
that I am proposing we consider would be updating our ordinance that handles 
obstructing license plates.  There was a new state law that came through this year that 
essentially added a requirement that a plate be free of anything that obstructs the 
frame, or sorry, free of a frame that obstructs the visibility or legibility of the plate.  This 
doesn't change what's already in effect, which is that the plate must be free of any 
material that would obstruct an officer from being able to read it.  It just adds additional 
requirements.  So, the recommendation would be to update our ordinance so that we’re 
consistent with state law.  
 
[Ordinance Amendments, continued slide] 
As to the second Ordinance, Animal Cruelty was updated in the UPOC and in the state 
law this year.  And since we opt out, my proposal would be that we consider updating 
our ordinance so that it stays consistent there as well.  The big change to this is 
essentially that there is generally a crime of abandoning an animal if you are not 
providing proper care for the animal.  But in this instance, if an individual were to pick up 
a feral cat and then provide vaccination or spaying or neutering services, they are then 
permitted to return the cat back to where they found the cat and leave the cat there.  So, 
there wouldn't be a criminal offense for abandoning a feral cat if those services were 
provided beforehand.

[42nd Edition UPOC Changes, continued - Section 6.1 Theft slide]

reason to know that property was stolen and were in possession of it.
