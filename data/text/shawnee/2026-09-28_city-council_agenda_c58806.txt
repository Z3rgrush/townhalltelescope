MR. ZIELSDORF:  Good evening.  This item is a Rezoning and Preliminary 
Development Plan for a new multi-family residential development.  The rezoning 
request is from CH (Commercial Highway) to Planned Unit Development Mixed 
Residential.  
The Preliminary Development Plan proposes a 168-unit apartment complex with five 2- 
to 3-story buildings, a 1-story clubhouse, surface parking, amenities, and other related 
site infrastructure.  
 
The subject site is designated as Attached Residential Neighborhood within the 
Achieve Shawnee Future Land Use Framework.  The Attached Residential 
Neighborhood supports primarily multi-family attached housing, including apartments.  
And the proposed use is in conformance with the attached residential district as shown 
in the Achieve Shawnee Comprehensive Plan.  
 
The property has been vacant under a commercial zoning designation since 1974.  
The site has steep topography that affords preservation of existing wooded areas at 
the east end of the site that encompasses approximately one-third of the property.  And 
adjacent properties include Tire Town to the southwest, Shawnee Mission Parkway to 
the north, Midland Drive to the south, and Trinity Lutheran Church to the west.  
 
A rezoning to PUDMR will not have a detrimental effect upon surrounding area, nor will 
it have a negative impact on the health and welfare of the community.  
 
Public Works has reviewed traffic information supplied by the applicant and has 
determined the development of 168-unit apartment building or apartment buildings will 
not overburden public service provisions or traffic circulation in the area.  
 
A public hearing was held on this item at the August 17th Planning Commission 
meeting.  Representatives from two nearby churches spoke in regard to the item.  
These comments centered around the condition of Midland Drive.  The Commissioners 
also had discussion about Midland Drive, site grading, and building architecture.  City 
Engineer Mark Sherfy was present to field questions about traffic and the future of 
Midland Drive.  And based on this information, the Planning Commission felt that it was 
a good project and appropriate for the site.   
 
An ordinance is required and no protest petition was filed in relation to this request.  An 
ordinance requires five votes to approve or remand the request, or six votes to deny 
the request by overriding the recommendation of the Planning Commission.  The 
Planning Commission, by a vote of 10 to 0, recommended the Governing Body 
approve PUD26-01 subject to the conditions listed within the staff report.   
 
And I believe, as you indicated, the City Manager indicated that Kevin Manning is here 
to discuss traffic on Midland Drive.  And I believe the applicant is here to answer any 
questions as well.
