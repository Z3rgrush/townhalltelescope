debris on their property, frightened pets, veterans, others who struggle with 
unexpected explosions, smoke, air quality, and people using fireworks irresponsibly.  
Those concerns should not be dismissed.   
 
But I respectfully believe there is an important question we still need to answer.  Were 
fireworks themselves the problem or irresponsible behavior the problem?  Because 
those require two very different positions.  If someone shoots fireworks after the 
permitted hours, they should be punished.  If someone uses prohibited fireworks, cite 
them.  If someone shoots fireworks recklessly towards another person's property, hold 
them accountable.  If someone leaves debris on someone else's property, enforce the 
law.  But when people violate the rules, the solution shouldn't automatically be to take 
the privilege away from every resident who followed them.   
 
That's why I'm not asking the Council to simply restore the previous ordinance.  I'm 
asking you to replace it with a better one.  Give Shawnee one controlled day, the 
Fourth of July.  Establish a clearly defined window with a firm end time such as 11 p.m.  
Increase fines for anyone who operates outside that window.  Aggressively enforce the 
prohibition of illegal fireworks and require cleanup.  Require every permanent fireworks 
seller to give customers clear information about Shawnee's hours, prohibited fireworks, 
penalties, and safety rules, and give the City authority to suspend fireworks when 
drought, extreme fire danger, weather conditions, or dangerous air quality make them 
unsafe.  That addresses many of the concerns brought to you without eliminating the 
tradition entirely.  
 
There's another reason I believe this deserves reconsideration.  Prohibition and 
compliance are not the same thing.  The people who are already shooting fireworks 
outside permitted hours were already violating Shawnee’s rules.  The people using

There is also the issue of air pollution.  Fireworks do create a particulate pollution.  I do
