 ____________________________________________________________________________ 
As directed by the BOCC, Land Use, Legal, and the Planning Commission have been working on 
proposed revisions to the Land Use Code as outlined in Resolution No. 2024- 029 (attached). 
Additionally, edits to code sections were made regarding the on-going discussions on a possible 
‘bonus adu’ concept (shown in blue text – other changes shown in red text). Staff made every 
effort to simply cut/paste these changes from previously circulated documents (also in packet) 
and does not comment in the proposed changes in this memo.  
The Land Use Department is  also recommending certain revisions to the Land Use Code that 
were not specifically addressed in the Board’s resolution. Those changes will be explained in this 
memo and noted as ‘SP ’ - Staff Proposed Change, or ‘LP ’ - Legal Proposed Change. Some 
changes are driven by gaps in the Code, potential regulatory loopholes, and typos or other errors.  
Note: Please see the public submitted comments at the end of the packet. 
Thank you.

 https://ouraycountyco.gov/DocumentCenter/View/146

- Section 3.10 (LP): This change is a simple starting point triggered by changes in state

referrals are certainly possible as this progresses. The new law is available at: 
https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-212  
- Section 3.11 (LP): These changes are being driven by top-down “water-wise” landscaping 
requirements in new state law, which establish a legal nonconforming use for already -
existing invasive plant species and thirsty non-native, grass species, but prohibit this type 
of landscaping going forward for new development projects. Please note the definition of 
invasive plant species is much broader than noxious weeds. Exceptions to the new water-
wise conservation laws exist for hybridized sod/grass seeds (e.g. buffalo grass hybrids), 
and artificial turf on “athletic fields of play” (which will need further clarification in our Code 
to avoid loopholes). ***Special Note: it may be advisable to simply include Sections 3.10 
and 3.11 in a new Section 25 for “State Mandates”, due to the continual frequency of top-
down measures from the State, which would avoid continually amending the critically -
important Section 3 on Zones, as new state mandates keep coming up.***  The new 
legislation on landscaping requirements is available at: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-
005   
- Section 3.12 (LP): Changes proposed, intended to clarify what is allowed by a Special 
Event Permit. These have been informally permitted by the BOCC for many years, and 
regulations should be on the books (see Section 2- definition of Special Event Permit). 
Specific regulations would clarify what has previously been an informal/courtesy process 
(outside the specific context of short-term rentals). The BOCC and PC may want to discuss 
appropriate thresholds for major events, and other limitations. Examples of previous 
special event permits have included car commercials, large gatherings of sports/car 
enthusiasts, and various races and community events.

SECTION 13 - ADMINISTRATION AND ENFORCEMENT: 
- 13.3B Summary (LP): Counties statewide have been struggling with code enforcement for 
land use and building codes, after the passage of the Misdemeanor Reform Act (Senate 
Bill 20 -271), which changed many misdemeanors to civil infractions. Previously, a 
summons and complaint could be issued by the Sheriff or deputies, although most DAs 
were reluctant to handle code enforcement and viewed it as a civil matter. Now, the 
issuance of code enforcement citations by law enforcement as a “civil infraction” is virtually 
impossible/impracticable under the Misdemeanor Reform Act and has led to adverse court 
rulings, since attorneys are largely prohibited from appearing in a civil infraction “R” docket 
case under court rules and statute, and county law enforcement officers ar e typically not 
building inspectors as well (or vice versa).  
For example, one county attorney was overruled in an appellate decision for attempting to 
prosecute a civil infraction zoning citation, instead of only using law enforcement for a civil 
infraction citation without an attorney present, as bizarre as that sounds. This leaves the 
only feasible option of non-law-enforcement filings into county court as a civil legal action 
through the normal process that seeks $100 in daily penalties under state statute after an 
NOV is issued and $500-$1000 in initial fines are sought via civil court order, and then if 
there is something highly serious or substantial, we can also request a civil 
injunction/abatement in district court. This new civil process, with its major quirks and 
inefficiencies, applies for both building code and Land Use code violations under state 
law, if authorized by the County Attorney, and the Board has also authorized expedited 
filing under County Resolution 2024-028.  
Expedited code enforce ment is especially critical for certain types of violations such as 
OWTS or septic issues that threaten public welfare, which may warrant an immediate 
restraining order in district court as opposed to an administrative hearing or $100 in daily 
penalties which may not be adequate deterrence. Some legislative lobbying is underway 
in this area, to potentially allow building inspectors to issue immediate citations with fines 
without having to obtain civil court relief through an attorney, service of a complaint , and 
judge’s order, and without having to ask law enforcement to issue a civil infraction citation 
(who do not have experience with building and land use code).   
- 13.6 Summary (LP): The County does not have authority to enforce private covenants or 
private easements, outside of very special situations specified in Code such as abandoned 
open space maintenance. Land Use Staff and the County Attorney are continually 
requested to engage in these kinds of issues, which are typically HOA-related and private 
civil disputes outside the County’s scope. It would be useful to have this specific language 
in Code, instead of always sounding like a broken record when we are inevitably asked to 
enforce private covenants or easement disputes.  
- 13.10E (SP): Puts the burden of recording a Site Development Permit on the Applicant 
rather than Staff. 
- 13.11.A.1.a (SP): Typo correction. 
- 13.11.A.2.a (SP): Typo correction. 
- 13.11.H.3 (LP): CDPHE and the State AG’s Office had reached out to Land Use and Legal 
last year to discuss a p ossible MOU or IGA with the County, to have materials 
management plans and CDPHE referrals for certain types of excavation/ development in 
the high country, especially those over 5 cubic yards. This was requested due to the 
sensitive nature of the high alpine area, CERCLA considerations under at least one 
consent decree, and the amount of historical mining activity and engineered features to 
prevent contamination from tailing piles and similar.  
However, it could be simpler to have this CDPHE referral process hard -wired in Code, 
instead of in an informal MOU/IGA that could be changed and would need to be drafted 
(nothing has been drafted yet with an MOU – just preliminary discussions with state and 
local regulators as brain-storming for presentation to the policy-makers). This draft Code 
language has also been provided to the State, and CDPHE has now requested to work 
directly with the County Planner on any proposed changes here, to discuss possible 
mandatory CDPHE referrals for certain types of high-country development.  
This Code change, if palatable to the BOCC and/or PC, may also warrant further 
discussion with other local stakeholders and/or engineers such as Idarado. It would be 
helpful if the BOCC could authorize Planning and Legal to discuss this further with any 
local stakeholders such as Idarado, following such extensive discussions with CDPHE. 
 It is unclear whether other institutional forms of local control for the high alpine area, 
similar to the Panoramic Heights Excavation Ordinance, could be implemented in the high 
alpine area, due to the fact that other remedial decisions with state control in the high 
alpine have pre-dated the enactment of the State Environmental Covenants Law in 2001 
(that allowed local control ordinances for remedial consent decree areas at CERCLA 
superfund sites). A mandatory CDPHE referral process in the Land Use Code could be 
the simplest and cleanest option to protect engineered features and other sensitive areas 
from development-related disturbance.  
- 13.11.I.5 (LP): Change  proposed to ensure consultation with Ouray County Historical 
Society or other historic preservation organization (such as SHPO).  
- 13.11.I.11 (LP): Changes made for minor clarification with recorded statements.  
- 13.11.J (LP): Changes made to shift the burden for recording documents to the Permittee; 
not Land Use Department, which results in a small amount of revenue for filing fees.  
- 13.12.A (LP): Changes made due to the International Code Council Codes (IBC, IRC, 
IECC, IEBC, etc.) being use, not the Uniform Building Codes. 
- 13.12.A.1 (LP): Generally the scope of the IBC/IRC or other building codes applies to both 
building codes and “structures.” This is intended to close potential sought-after loopholes 
with structures that may not be defined as “buildings,” such as underground structures 
and similar. Of course, Land Use does not have time to regulate every single mailbox or 
tree-house “structure:, but additional regulatory flexibility is needed here.  
- 13.12.B (LP): This is just a starting point to require expert reports, safety measures, and 
alternative excavation measures before blasting occurs for residential and commercial 
development. This is a special concern as development “crawls” up the hills in certain 
areas with extreme terrain features. Blasting regulations could also be parked in a stand-
alone blasting/fire ordinance, but that project has been somewhat delayed due to the 
development of a potential county -wide Fire & EMS Authority with potential fire code 
authority. It may also be advisable to consider a stand- alone sub-section at the end of 
Section 13 of the Code, with regulations similar to what Pima County (AZ) has 
done:https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/pimacounty/latest/pimacounty_az/0-0-0-
4624   
- 13.13 (LP): Change made to state the obvious and clarify what has previously been a 
common-sense item– the building inspector has discretion to determine when to go for an 
inspection with incomplete construction, instead of just being called for a final.  
- 13.14 (LP): Changes made to clarify move to electronic permit systems (CloudPermit or 
others).  
- 13.15.B (SP): This change is intended to address the on -going difficulty in appointing all 
7 Planning Commission members. 
- 13.17.A (SP): This change in intended to reduce the difficulty in maintaining all 5 members 
of the VIRC.  
- 13.17.B (SP): Same as above. Just trying to make it easier to maintain a full body of the 
VIRC. 
- 13.18.B.1 and 2 (SP): Change intended to reduce the difficulty in establishing a quorum 
for the JPB. Staff notes that several times in 2024 and 2025 JPB hearings were held when 
there was not an established quorum.

3.1 PURPOSE AND INTENT: 
A. The zoning provisions that follow have been adopted to achieve the purposes set forth 
in Section 1. 
B. To allow gradual, long- term population and economic growth in Ouray County in a 
manner that does not harm the County’s irreplaceable scenic beauty, wildlife, air and 
water resources, and other environmental qualities and that does not unduly burden 
the County’s residents or its governments. 
C. The intent of the County zones is to achieve across  the zones, the overall goal of the 
Master Plan.  This goal includes, in alphabetical order, specific goals for agricultural 
lands, c ounty/municipal relationships, economic development, housing, natural 
resources, rural character, touris m, transportation, utilities, visually significant areas, 
and wildlife and plant habitats. 
D. The specific intent of each of the zones that follow shall be read in conjunction with 
the combination of the Master Plan’s overall and specific goals, and provide general 
guidance with regard to specific uses within each zone. 
 
3.2 ZONING DISTRICTS, MAPS AND BOUNDARIES: 
A. The zones established by the Code are identified on the basis of the physical character 
of the County combined with the pertinent information about existing land use and 
ownership patterns and the needs of a stable and growing economy. 
B. All Zones shall be designated on the “Official Zoning Map of Ouray County” which 
is on file in the records of the County Clerk and Recorder.  A copy of the map is 
attached to this Code and, in the event of any conflict between the copy and the map 
on file in the County records, the latter shall be conclusively deemed to prevail. A 
courtesy copy of the map is also located on the County website (currently located 
on the Land Use Department section of the website), and available for inspection 
upon request to the Land Use Department. 
 
3.3 USES BY RIGHT AND SPECIAL USES: 
A. In each zone there are uses permitted by right and special uses which may be allowed 
on a site specific basis though a permitting process.  These uses have been determined 
in each zone according to the unique characteristics of the zone. 
B. Uses allowed by right are allowed automatically, although construction of new 
structures may require a Site Development Permit or High Alpine Site Development 
Permit and a building permit pursuant to Sections 13 and 24 of this Code.

any more offensive noise, vibration, dust, heat, smoke, odor, glare, or other 
objectionable influences, or more traffic, hazards, or alterations to the zone than 
the minimum amount normally resulting from the other uses permitted in the 
zone.

3.9 OVERLAY DISTRICTS: 
A. Intent and purpose:  Due to continued growth pressures, t here is an increased desire 
for coordination between the Municipalities and the County to promote the efficient 
use of services and protection of open lands, agricultural lands, alpine lands and 
community identities. It is therefore the intent and purpose of the Overlay Districts to 
establish districts and create a process to jointly review development on 
unincorporated property surrounding the Town of Ridgway and the City of Ouray. 
This Section may be further interpreted and administered pursuant to 
Intergovernmental Agreements with the Municipalities and , shall automatically 
lapse with respect to any Municipality if an Intergovernmental  Agreement is 
terminated with regards to the Overlay Districts.   
B. D efinitions:  
(1) Area of Influence ( “AOI”).  An area of unincorporated land wherein 
development or use of land has an impact upon the adjoining municipality. 
(2) Urban Development.  Development that  conforms to the standards of moderate 
and high density residential, commercial/industrial or tourist land use categories, 
which is typical to urbanized areas.   Urban development also includes the types 
of services that are generally required to support t hat development such as central 
potable water, storm water systems, central sanitary sewer systems, quick -
response fire and police protection, urban level street design and maintenance, 
parks and recreation programs, open space and undeveloped parks, urban level 
retail and commercial development and other similar services that are typically 
provided by cities and towns. 
(3) Urban Growth Management Area (“UGMA”).  An area of unincorporated land 
adjacent to a municipality in which urban development may be allowed  when 
annexed by the municipality.  The Urban Growth Management Area includes an

(2) Installation of grass seed or sod that is a native plant or has been 
hybridized for arid conditions. 
(3) Installation of artificial turf on bona fide athletic fields of play, which are 
available for public or community use for regular sports activities.
