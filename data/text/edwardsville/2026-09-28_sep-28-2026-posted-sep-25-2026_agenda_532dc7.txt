6. Conduct a public hearing to consider exceeding the revenue neutral rate (RNR) for 
the Fiscal Year 2027 Annual Budget. 
 
Motion: Councilmember Adcox motioned to open the public meeting.
 Second: Councilmember Moulin seconded the motion. 
 Vote: Passed, 5-0.  
 
Steve Johnson - 507 High St– He stated that he has been looking into revenue neutral 
and the mil levy and was leaning more toward raising it because of the concerns 
residents continue to bring to the council regarding their expectations for the city’s 
expenses. Steve stated that, like everyone else, he received a notice indicating that his 
taxes will increase by 31%. He said that if he were to rely solely on his Social Security 
income, the increase would amount to approximately 13% of his Social Security check. 
He also stated that, in his experience, the city will not take action against someone for 
failing to pay their property taxes for as long as five years. 
 
Richard Cosgwell- 1834 S 98th St – He stated that his taxes would not be raised by 31%, 
but rather by 15%, and that as a retired individual, it's hard. He stated that he had a 
Wyandotte County appraiser come out to look at his four-room house. He asked the 
appraiser why his taxes were increasing, and the appraiser stated that he was not sure 
why. Richard stated that he is protesting the tax rate and will continue to do so. 
 
Debra Sweeten- 10330 Swartz – she stated she doesn’t think it matters what size of 
home you have when it comes to the property taxes being raised, but questioned why it is 
going up 36%. She said she does not understand why the increase is so significant and 
stated that she would understand a small increase each year, but believes a 36% 
increase is outrageous. She stated that such an increase is unsustainable for citizens 
and questioned whether it could result in people being driven out of Wyandotte County. 
She said she wants answers regarding the reasons for the increase. 
 
 Motion: Councilmember Adcox motioned to close the public hearing. 
 Second: Councilmember Moulin seconded the motion. 
 Vote: Passed, 5-0.  
 
The mayor explained that the City does not have any control over property appraisals but 
does have control over the budget. She encouraged residents to continue protesting their 
property appraisals, as the City has no authority over the appraisal process. However, 
the City does have control over the Edwardsville portion of the property tax bill.
 
The mayor would like to attain a motion of not exceeding revenue neutral  
 
City manager Doug Gerber explained that if the governing body adopts the revenue-
neutral rate, the city will collect the same amount of property tax as the previous year. He 
stated that if the governing body doesn’t adopt the revenue-neutral rate, no matter what 
the governing body sets it up, the mil levy rate will not go up.  
 
Josh Ney explained property taxes in more detail and what would happen if they adopt a 
revenue-neutral rate. Discussion ensued.
