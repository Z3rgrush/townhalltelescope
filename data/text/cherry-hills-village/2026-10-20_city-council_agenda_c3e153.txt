TAXPAYER RELIEF 
Included in the 2027 draft budget is a temporary reduction of the Village’s property tax mill levy to 
0 mills for 2027, providing one -time property tax relief to Village taxpayers while preserving the 
Village’s ability to restore the adopted mill levy beginning in 2028. The temporary reduction is 
expected to provide approximately $8.5 million in total taxpayer relief, including approximately $4 
million in reduced General Fund property tax revenue and $4.5  million in reduced Parks Fund 
property tax revenue. For the average Cherry Hills Village homeowner, this represents an estimated 
reduction of approximately $3,800 in the 2027 property tax bill. The exact revenue impact will be 
finalized as 2027 assessed valuation and property tax collections are certified. 
 
The 2027 property tax reduction will have a direct impact on the General Fund budget, reducing 
projected General Fund property tax revenue by approximately $4 million. Based on the Village’s 
current Long-Range Financial Forecast, the General Fund has sufficient financial capacity to absorb 
this one-time reduction while maintaining compliance with the adopted Fund Balance and Reserve 
Policy. The Village’s strong projected fund balances and revenues exceeding expenditures through 
2041 provide capacity to fund the reduction without creating an ongoing structural imbalance. As 
approved by Council, the Parks Fund will receive an approximately $4.5 million transfer from the 
General Fund to offset its property tax revenue reduction and maintain required fund balances.

COP Debt Payment and Interfund Transfer to the Capital Fund 
One budget highlight is related to the 2017 Certificates of Participation (COP) debt service.  The 
General Fund pays 49.6% of the annual principal and interest payments as well as receiving that 
amount of the total annual property tax collections as shared  with the Parks and Recreation Fund.  
In 2027 there is $395,000 in principal due and $352,050 in interest. These are split at 49.6% to be 
paid by the General Fund and 51.4% from the Parks Fund. 
 
In utilizing a ‘pay-as-you-go’ capital funding strategy to eliminate the accrual of excess Capital Fund 
ending fund balances, there is $1M  planned transfer in 2027 for General Fund projects and 
equipment.

Long-Range Financial Forecast (LRFF) 
The Long-Range Financial Forecast (LRFF) has been updated and included in the Council’s 2027 
Proposed Budget materials. The Major Assumptions are also provided. 
 
As reflected in the updated forecast, the projected 2037 General Fund ending fund balance is $30.67 
million, a decrease of approximately $7.83 million from the balance projected for 2037 in the 2026 
LRFF presented at Council Retreat. This represents no material change from the LRFF presented 
during the August 4, 2026, taxpayer relief discussion. 
 
The total projected decrease in General Fund balance associated with the updated assumptions is 
approximately $7.83 million. This includes the impact of the one-year property tax reduction, which 
reduces 2027 property tax revenues and requires related transfers to the Parks Fund, as well as the 
addition of a full-time Community Development Planner I position to maintain current service levels 
amid continued increases in building permit activity. The forecast also incorporates a projected 1% 
year-over-year increase in building permit revenues to reflect anticipated revenue growth associated 
with ongoing permit activity. 
 
The projected expenditures on the updated LRFF are mostly unchanged from August .  The largest 
stand out is the addition of a full -time Planner I position in Community Development, which has a 
$413k impact on the ten-year financial forecast and a $1.1M impact on the 15-year financial forecast.  
The other larger stand out is increase in fuel prices expected for 2027, with the hope and expectation 
fuel will go back down in 2028.   
 
The purpose in presenting the LRFF with the Proposed Budget is to determine whether, under current 
budget assumptions and conditions, this Budget is sustainable into the future.

NEW BUSINESS 
Resolution 32, Series 2026; Reappointing Members to the Planning and Zoning 
Commission 
Clerk Gillespie presented Resolution 32, Series 2026 for Council consideration. She 
explained three members of the Planning and Zoning Commission (P&Z) ended their 
terms on September 15; two desired reappointment and one did not; the two desiring 
reappointment were also running unopposed for City Council; staff had already begun a 
recruitment process for those two positions with the plan to conduct interviews in 
December and appoint new members at the January 5, 2027 Council meeting; staff 
provided options for Council on how to handle the current terms; staff recommended 
reappointing the two members to partial terms and leaving the third position vacant until 
January when Council would appoint new members to all three positions. 
Councilor Robinson asked why staff recommended that option. 
Clerk Gillespie replied staff believes it will help with continuity on P&Z and be a more 
efficient use of Council’s time. 
Mayor Pro Tem Hoellen asked why staff recommended partial terms instead of full 
terms. 
Clerk Gillespie replied that staff was asking Council to depart from the normal procedure 
of recruitment and had recommended partial terms to account for that. 
Mayor Brown noted the soonest Council would be able to appoint a new member would 
be October 20, which would only be two regular P&Z meetings before January 5. 
Councilor Maguire moved, seconded by Councilor Robinson, to approve Resolution 
32, Series 2026, Reappointing Members to the Planning and Zoning Commission. 
The motion passed unanimously.

Historically, the City Manager has used a work plan on and off over the years, and this work plan 
has had a wide range of formats and levels of sophistication.  Starting in 2023, the work plan 
format: 
• Includes projects that are large in scale and might have a duration that covers multiple 
years, while also including smaller projects that cover some operational elements of staff 
time. 
• Is organized by department. 
• Is annual, starts at the beginning of each year, is sent to City Council at the end of the 
year as a part of the City Manager’s performance review.   
• Includes a review of the performance of the prior year’s work plan for each item. 
 
Further, in 2023, the following timeline was established to allow City Council to provide input 
early in the work plan creation process and create more vertical alignment between City Council 
and staff: 
1. February/March Retreat: City Council discusses potential projects for the following year 
using the Implementation chapter of the Master Plan as a guide (see Chapter 9, available 
on the City website at www.cherryhillsvillage.com/MasterPlan)  
2. Spring/Summer: City Manager and Department Directors use direction from Council 
Retreat to translate Council priorities into a draft budget and draft work plan 
3. October: The following year’s draft budget and draft work plan are presented to City 
Council at the budget study session 
4. December: City Manager sends final work plan for following year and review of 
performance for current year’s work plan to City Council as part of evaluation process

In late 2025, after receiving feedback from City Council regarding the status of various projects, 
staff made several changes to the work plan (Exhibit A): 
• Changed the organization format of the work plan: Staff created two categories of 
tasks and projects, “Strategic” and “Operational”.  Splitting the municipal work plan into 
two distinct parts creates greater clarity and focus for both City Council and staff.  The 
Operational Plan captures the essential projects and recurring tasks led by staff that keep 
the city running efficiently. In contrast, a separate Strategic Plan allows staff and City 
Council to collaboratively focus on longer-term initiatives that often span multiple years, 
require deliberate policy direction, and carry significant budget implications. This 
distinction helps ensure routine operations are not overshadowed by major initiatives, 
supports more effective prioritization and resource allocation, and strengthens alignment 
between Council goals, staff capacity, and long-term financial planning. 
• Updated the list of tasks and projects: Since the last version of the work plan that was 
provided to City Council, a number of projects have emerged that need to be added to the 
work plan.  For example, the Nexus Study for Impact Fees was not on the previous 
version of the 2026 work plan but now needs to be reflected.  A separate list that shows 
all the added and deleted projects and tasks is included in the packet (Exhibits B and C).   
 
DISCUSSION 
The purpose of tonight’s discussion is to review the current 2027 Strategic Plan and incorporate 
any changes Council would like to make in advance of the first reading of the 2027 Budget in 
November. As a reminder, this is the second time Council reviews the following year’s plan. The 
first time is during the retreat, the second is during the budget process, and the third is during the 
City Manager’s performance evaluation. Due to a combination of external factors, staff capacity, 
and additional City Council input, there have been a number of changes to the 2027 Strategic 
Plan since the Council Retreat that the City Manager will review during tonight’s meeting. Staff 
hopes to receive Council direction on the updated plan.
