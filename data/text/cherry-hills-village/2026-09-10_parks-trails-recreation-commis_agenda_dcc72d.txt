Item 7b 2027 Work Plan 
 
Manager Black reviewed each item in the draft 2027 Work Plan with the Commission; she 
informed the Commission that there are two items that are rolling over from the 2026 Work 
Plan, one being Park Signage which the Commission can start working on in 2026 and the 
other Reviewing Ebike and Emoto Regulations which she said she hoped to begin in early 
2027. Manager Black went over additional possible projects for 2027; Trail Passport 
Program, Recreation Reimbursement Program, Activation of Volunteer Park, Conducting a 
Bike and Pedestrian Study which Manager Black recommended waiting for the traffic study to 
conclude before working on that item because there may be some suggestions that the 
Commission may be able to implement from the first study.  
 
Chair Presecan asked if the Special Events budget was sufficient for all Parks events and if 
working on the Ebikes/Emoto issue and the Recreation Reimbursement Program was too 
heavy of a lift. Manager Black replied that the special events budget has been sufficient, but 
the Commission can look at it in the fall and see if higher participation numbers warrant a 
higher budget; she added that the Commission can work on both Work Plan items but 
suggested spacing them out within the year. Chair Presecan also asked if the traffic study 
would potentially address Ebike/Emoto issues and Manager Black replied that it would likely 
not. 
 
Vice Chair Leviton suggested that Parks & Trails Visioning be added to the 2027 Work Plan 
as it had appeared in previous years. The Commission discussed the idea and agreed to add 
the item. 
 
Commissioner Greer asked if the Bike and Pedestrian Study item would need to be moved to 
2028 and Manager Black replied that the discussion can begin in 2027, but the actual study 
would be scheduled for 2028. Commissioner Greer added that the trails along Quincy Ave. 
and Colorado Blvd. were of concern as far as safety goes. The Commission agreed and 
added that Mansfield Ave is also dangerous for pedestrians and cyclists. 
 
Commissioner Darch asked about who was in charge of maintaining the new bollards that 
were hit in the crosswalks by the High Line Canal.  Manager Black replied that the Streets 
Division was and she would speak to the Public Works Director about street improvements 
being suggested by the Commission. 
 
Chair Presecan suggested adding Safety Identification and Improvements to the Parks & 
Trails Visioning item and asked if safety items could fall under the Trail & Parks Improvement 
budget item. Manager Black replied that they would. 
 
Commissioner Darch stated that she was very interested in reintroducing the Trail Passport 
Program and possibly having either a kickoff or end celebration. The Commission really liked 
the idea and agreed to add it to the 2027 Work Plan. Vice Chair Leviton asked if the Cherry 
Hills Land Preserve would be interested in running the program and Manager Black replied 
that they may not have the capacity and it would be better to keep it as a Commission event; 
CHLP may be interested in participating in a more limited capacity.

MEMORANDUM  
TO: MEMBERS OF THE PARKS, TRAILS, AND RECREATION COMMISSION  
 
FROM: EMILY BLACK, PARKS PROJECT & OPERATIONS MANAGER 
 
SUBJECT: DRAFT 2027 WORK PLAN  
 
DATE: SEPTEMBER 10, 2026 
 
ISSUE 
Finalizing the 2027 Work Plan.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
In 2025, City Council reviewed and updated their Liaison Program Policy. The two most 
important updates for PTRC are as follows:  
 
“…b. To allow enhanced and direct communication between City Council and the boards, 
commissions, and committees:  
i. Each City board, commission, and committee prepare an annual Work Plan or report 
to be reviewed by the City Council.  
ii. Each City board, commission, and committee provide periodic and no less than annual 
state reports, which may include recommendation to the City Council, to the City Council 
at a City Council meeting.”  
PTRC’s 2026 Work Plan is attached as Exhibit A. Based on this year’s Work Plan and 
discussions at previous PTRC meetings this year, staff drafted a 2027 Work Plan. PTRC 
reviewed the first draft at the July 9, 2026 meeting and suggested changes. Staff has incorporated 
most suggestions into this version of the plan (Exhibit B). In consultation with the Public Works 
Director, biking improvements to specific streets were not included in PTRC’s work plan; staff is 
awaiting the results of the traffic analysis and Council direction based on those findings. 
However, there is $100,000 currently earmarked in the draft 2027 budget for possible project 
designs that come out of the traffic analysis.  
 
Presenting the plan is currently scheduled for the Oct. 20th, 2026 City Council meeting to 
correspond with the City budget process.

Parks, Trails, and Recreation Commission  
2026 Work Plan  
 
In January 2025, City Council updated the City Council Liaison Program Policy to 
include the requirement that each City board, commission, and committee prepare an 
annual Work Plan or report to be reviewed by the City Council. The Parks, Trails, and 
Recreation Commission will present this 2026 Work Plan to Council in mid-2025.  
 
Per PTRC’s bylaws and the Municipal Code, the responsibilities of the Commission are 
below:  
 
Purpose of the Commission 
 
a) The Parks, Trails and Recreation Commission has been established to advise City 
Council on matters related to parks, trails, recreation and open space within the 
community. The Commission shall meet at the direction of City Council for the following 
specific reasons:  
(1) To make recommendations with the purpose of improving and maintaining the 
City's park and open space system consistent with the provisions of Chapter 11, 
Article III of the Code;  
(2) To evaluate and make recommendations concerning the acquisition of 
additional trails, trail connections, pocket parks, wildlife refuge areas, protected 
view areas, open space parks and recreational parks;  
(3) To make recommendations with the purpose of protecting and maintaining the 
streams and water features that exist in the City;  
(4) To review and make recommendations on the Recreation Reimbursement 
Program to meet the recreation needs of the community;  
(5) To make recommendations to the Planning and Zoning Commission 
concerning the adoption of that portion of a Master Plan concerning park and trail 
development;  
(6) To recommend annual budget priorities to the City Council for capital projects 
related to parks, trails, open space and recreation;  
(7) To develop and recommend an annual recreation and event program and 
budget to the City Council; and  
(8) To recommend long-range financing plans for open space acquisition; 
b) The Commission shall meet in accordance with the provisions of the Municipal Code 
to:  
(1) Review and make comments and recommendations on preliminary plat 
subdivision development applications concerning the dedication of land, in 
accordance with Chapter 17 of this Code; and  
(2) Act in accordance with any other duties as required by other provisions of this 
Code as enacted by the City Council from time to time.  
c) In addition, the Parks, Trails and Recreation Commission shall have such other duties 
as assigned by the City Council, by motion, from time to time.

In accordance with PTRC’s responsibilities, the following projects are anticipated for 
2026:  
 
Improvements in City Parks and Trails ($10,000)  
 
This item appears annually on PTRC’s Projects and Priorities list. It is a $10,000 line 
item budgeted in the Arapahoe County Open Space Fund. Funds can be used for various 
Park or Open Space improvements, including: play equipment, shelter improvements, 
horse arena upgrades, etc. or for programming in City parks. The use for the funds is 
typically determined  during the calendar year they are budgeted, for something that  
comes to PTRC’s attention or to contribute towards an already budgeted project in order 
to improve it. For example, in recent years, this line item has been used to contribute to 
additional signage around the City when it was requested by residents and PTRC 
members and to contribute additional funds to the Dahlia Hollow playground project. It 
gives PTRC the flexibility to respond to Parks needs during the year.  
 
Special Events ($21,500)   
 
This is an annual item. PTRC’s annual special events include:  
 
• Scavenger Hunt (May) 
• Movie Night (August) 
• Winter Celebration (December) 
 
Last year, PTRC added hosting a table for Bike to Work Day (typically June) . This is a 
very minimal cost that did not require adjusting the budget. Part of this line item ($5,000) 
also contributes toward the Spring Clean Up, but that event is planned and run primarily 
by Public Works.  
 
Reviewing Applications and Requests from Residents ($0)  
 
PTRC will continue to review applications and requests from residents and make 
recommendations to the Planni ng & Zoning Commission and City Council. The most 
recent example of this type of application is the request for a gate on a public trail by the 
Cherry Hills Park HOA. Another type of review PTRC has provided in recent years 
include making recommendations regarding the placement of sculpture in parks and open 
spaces (whether on loan or permanent acquisitions) in coordination with the Cherry Hills 
Village Art Commission. The number of applications that PTRC reviews has declined in 
recent years due to subdivis ion requests becoming less frequent, as well as  the recent 
ballot initiative that changed the process for trail vacations.

Reviewing Bench and Tree Donation Applications ($0; costs are offset by donations)  
 
PTRC will continue to review and make recommendations to City Council regarding 
requests for bench and tree donations from residents. This program has been going on for 
at least 25 years for benches, and PTRC recently expanded the program to include trees 
to provide another option for residents. O n average, PTRC reviews approximately three 
requests per year.  
 
Park Signage ($10,000) 
 
New Parks & Trails maps are being developed in 2025 and will be available by 2026. 
PTRC will use the new maps to create permanent signs for parks to help residents 
navigate the trail system. PTRC will consider whether to add only one sign at John 
Meade Park and consider increasing signage in subsequent years based on resident 
feedback.  
 
Review E-bike Regulations ($0)  
 
PTRC will learn more about current ebike regulations in the City, local region, and state 
level, types of ebikes, and enforcement of the current City code.  The Commission will 
consider whether or not to revisit the current rules in the Municipal Code.
