SCUSSION 
Background 
The Cherry Hills Village Art Commission (CHVAC) presented Calaveras en Mi Ciudad at City Hall 
from October 19 through November 2, 2026. The exhibition featured eight hand-painted papier-
mâché calaveras and their bases on loan from art curator Alexis Newton. City Council approved the 
artwork loan at its August 18 meeting for an amount not to exceed $10,000. 
A complementary community celebration was held on Saturday, October 24, from 2 to 4 p.m., 
providing residents and families an opportunity to experience the artwork and learn about Día de los 
Muertos traditions through music, storytelling and hands-on activities.  
Event Overview and Participation 
Approximately 88 visitors attended the October 24 event, based on a manual count excluding 
Commission members and City staff working the event. The count is approximate and may include 
minor counting discrepancies. The event featured: 
• Live guitar music performed by Stephen Bondy.
• Día de los Muertos story time and educational presentation by children's author Jolene
Gutiérrez.
• Face painting provided by two artists from Starburst Face Painting.
• Paper marigold crafts and sugar-skull coloring activities.
• A themed photo area using the Commission's existing backdrop and decorations.
• Food and refreshments provided by Chile con Quesadilla.
Exhibition curator Alexis Newton and participating artists Juls Mendoza and Jennifer Gomez also 
attended the celebration, providing visitors with an opportunity to meet individuals involved in 
creating and presenting the artwork. 
P
articipation and material distribution included: 
Item Distribution 
Meals 87 of 100 available meals served 
Paper marigold craft kits Approximately 80 assembled; 22 remaining after the event 
Sugar-skull coloring pages Approximately 128 available; 26 remaining after the event

aterials distributed do not necessarily represent individual participants, as attendees could take 
activities home or collect more than one. 
Exhibition Engagement and Outreach 
The exhibition and community event were promoted through the Village Crier, City website, social 
media and other City communication channels. Commissioners also helped share information 
through their personal networks, community connections and affiliated organizations. 
The exhibition remained accessible during regular City Hall hours throughout its two-week display. 
Although a formal visitor count outside the October 24 event was not available, staff observed that 
individuals visiting City Hall for other business also stopped to view the calaveras and expressed 
appreciation for the artwork. 
Following the community event, remaining craft kits and coloring pages were made available to 
exhibition visitors. Approximately six additional kits and six coloring pages were taken home during 
the final days of the display. 
Commissioner Scottie Iverson photographed the October 24 event and contributed an article 
highlighting the celebration and ongoing exhibition for the October 29, 2026, edition of The Villager. 
Attendee Feedback 
A QR-code survey was offered to attendees to gather feedback about the event and future arts 
programming. Five responses were received. While the response rate was limited, the feedback was 
positive: 
• All five respondents rated the event as Excellent.
• Four respondents indicated they loved the exhibition; one indicated they liked it.
• Three respondents would definitely recommend future CHVAC events or exhibits, while two
indicated they probably would.
• Respondents identified crafts, face painting, storytelling and the combination of activities and
artwork among their favorite aspects.
 
 
• Family arts activities received the most interest for future programming (four responses), 
followed by sculpture and public art (three responses). Art exhibits, artist talks and cultural 
celebrations each received two responses. 
• Three respondents reported learning about the event through the Village Crier, one through 
the City website and one through friends or family. 
Written comments expressed appreciation for the activities, educational opportunities and overall 
experience. Given the small, voluntary sample, responses should be considered individual feedback 
rather than representative of all attendees.

DISCUSSION 
Background 
The Commission finalized its 2027 Work Plan on July 27, 2026, and presented it to City Council 
on August 18. Council requested no revisions to the plan, which serves as the Commission's 
framework for the coming year. 
With the Calaveras en mi Ciudad exhibition and October Día de los Muertos event completed, 
the Commission can begin identifying project schedules, responsibilities and early coordination 
needs to support implementation in January 2027. 
2027 Sculpture-on-Loan Program 
The current Sculpture-on-Loan installation is scheduled for replacement in 2027, with loan 
periods typically beginning and ending in July. 
At its September 15 meeting, the Commission discussed beginning artist and artwork research 
earlier to allow additional time for identifying available pieces. Members supported researching 
potential artists and sculptures during the fall and bringing preliminary ideas forward in 
November.  
 
The Commission also discussed inviting art consultant Chris Stevens to its December meeting, 
subject to his availability, to review preliminary research and assist with developing the project 
brief. Staff will coordinate his potential participation, including a cost estimate and any 
necessary contracting or project-specific approvals. 
The Commission should discuss any initial research, anticipated selection milestones and next 
steps to support the 2027 installation. 
Art in Public Places Master Plan Update 
Updating the City's Art in Public Places Master Plan is a primary goal of the Commission's 2027 
Work Plan. Work to update the Master Plan was previously initiated but subsequently deferred, 
leaving the revised draft incomplete and unadopted. 
The Commission is invited to provide initial direction on the approach and timing for resuming 
the update, including any considerations staff should incorporate into a proposed timeline and 
process for Commission review. 
2027 Events and Programming 
The 2027 Work Plan includes $6,000 for events and identifies several potential activities, 
including the postponed Quincy Farm Plein Air Painting event, artist talks, art exhibitions and 
other community arts programming. 
The Commission should begin considering which activities it wishes to pursue, their 
approximate timing and any early coordination requirements. This will help staff plan around 
other City activities, facility availability and anticipated resources. 
  
COMMISSION ACTION 
Staff requests preliminary Commission direction regarding: 
• Sculpture-on-Loan Program: Initial artist research, anticipated selection and 
installation milestones, and potential consultant involvement. 
• Art in Public Places Master Plan Update: Priorities for resuming the update, 
anticipated timeline and considerations for community engagement. 
• 2027 Events and Programming: Activities the Commission wishes to pursue, preferred 
timing and initial coordination needs.

requirement that each City board, commission, and committee prepare an annual Work Plan or 
report to be reviewed by the City Council. The Cherry Hills Village Art Commission will present 
this 2027 Work Plan to City Council on August 18, 2026. 
Purpose of the Commission: 
(excerpt from Resolution 06-11, the resolution creating the Art Commission): 
“
The (CHVAC) shall act as an advisory commission on the arts to the City Council and fulfill 
those responsibilities set forth in the Art in Public Places Master Plan, including, but not limited 
to, providing recommendations to the City Council regarding the selection, location, purchase, 
and maintenance of public art within the City of Cherry Hills Village; and presenting an annual 
report on its work to the City Council. The (CHVAC) shall also perform such other duties and 
responsibilities as may be designated by the City Council.”

Each year at the beginning of the summer, staff hire a contractor to clean the permanent bronze and 
coated steel pieces in the City’s collection (Charlo, Chainsaw, Rubric, Gusto, Resilience, and
