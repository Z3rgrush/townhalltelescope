PURPOSE 
 
This Administrative Review serves to update the Pueblo County Planning Commission on the status 
of Special Use Permit HSUP-07-6. The permit allows natural deposits extraction and processing; a 
hot mix asphalt plan; stockpiling, crusher, and screening plan; concrete batch plant; scale houses and 
an office on a 1,030± acre parcel. The application includes a proposal to reclaim 783± surface acres 
to water storage reservoirs. 
 
LOCATION 
 
A portion of Sections 35 and 36, Township 20 South, Range 63 West; Section 31, Township 20 
South, Range 62 West; and Section 6, Township 21 South, Range 62 West, of the Sixth P.M. The site 
carries a physical address of 42500 East State Highway 96 and is located both east and west of 
Nyberg Road, south of and adjacent to East State Highway 96 at the intersection of Highway 96 East 
and Nyberg Road (Exhibit 1).  
 
STAFF COMMENTS / STATUS OF CONDITIONS 
 
Special Use Permit HSUP-07-6 was originally approved by the Pueblo Count Planning Commission 
on April 24, 2007. It was reviewed yearly from 2008 to 2013, then every three (3) years up to 2022, 
then again yearly to present. The most recent administrative review of this special use permit was 
approved by the Pueblo County Planning Commission on August 20, 2025, with the existing thirteen 
(13) conditions noting Condition 13 was updated to reference the new Pueblo County Unified 
Development Code (UDC) and a Directive to Staff to conduct another review in a year.   
 
Staff requested an update from the previous representative on July 10, 2026 (Exhibit 2). The new 
representative responded on July 28, 2026, providing status of the conditions of approval, Minerals 
Reclamation Permit Annual Report, site maps, and a parcel map. The representative stated that 
mining was not anticipated during the next twelve month; however, operational and business needs 
may change; therefore, requesting continuance of this special use permit to preserve the approved 
land-use framework while future mining, reclamation, property ownership, and operational needs are 
evaluated. (Exhibit 3).
