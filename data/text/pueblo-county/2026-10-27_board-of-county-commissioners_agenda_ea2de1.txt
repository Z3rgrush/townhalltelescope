A RESOLUTION AMENDING THE CHIEF EXECUTIVE MODEL TO DELEGATE 
ORGANIZATIONAL AND PERSONNEL AUTHORITY TO COUNTY CHIEFS  
 
          WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of Pueblo County, Colorado (Board), 
has authority under C.R.S. § 30-11-107(1)(e) to manage the business and concerns of the 
County, subject to applicable law; and 
 
          WHEREAS, Resolution No. 26-039, dated February 3, 2026, established the Chief 
Executive (C-Suite) model, with peer chief officers reporting directly to the Board; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board desires to authorize the chiefs to implement organizational and 
personnel changes within their assigned areas without a separate resolution for each 
administrative action; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board intends to retain ultimate oversight and reserved decision-making 
authority, with the Chief Human Resources Officer responsible for bargaining-unit matters 
arising from these actions. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that: 
 
1. Amendment and Scope: 
Section 6 of Resolution No. 26-039, dated February 3, 2026, is supplemented by this delegation 
to the Chief Administrative Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Human Resources Officer, 
Chief Technology Officer, and Chief Legal Officer, including duly appointed interim officers. 
Each chief may exercise this authority only within that chief’s Board-assigned area. 
 
2. Delegated Authority: 
Subject to this Resolution, each chief may reorganize assigned functions; create, modify, 
reclassify, consolidate, or abolish subordinate positions; revise job descriptions and internal 
reporting relationships; adjust staffing allocations and individual compensation within the 
Board-approved classification and compensation framework; and recruit, hire, promote, transfer, 
reassign, demote, discipline, suspend, or lay off employees, or implement a reduction in force. 
For the sake of clarity, a “reduction in force” shall not have the same meaning as a “lay off” and 
occurs when there is an desire to reduce staffing levels with no intention of recalling the 
employee(s) back to their original working status. A reduction in force shall not be implemented 
on a discriminatory basis. Examples of criteria used to implement a reduction in force could 
include, but is not limited to mission needs, efficiency, essential skills, seniority, and subject 
matter expertise. Reductions in force must be based strictly on organizational, operational, or 
Page 7 of 9
budgetary necessity and shall not serve as a pretext for or alternative to employee discipline or 
other adverse personnel action. Decisions made pursuant to a reduction in force are non-
appealable and constitute the final administrative action of the County. 
 
Changes affecting more than one chief’s assigned area require the affected chiefs’ concurrence. 
The Chief Administrative Officer shall decide how approved changes are coordinated and 
implemented across departments. Unresolved disputes shall be decided by the Board.  
 
No separate resolution is required for an action within this delegation, including changes to 
subordinate positions or job descriptions previously established by resolution. Prior requirements 
for separate Board approval of those actions are superseded only to that extent. This provision 
does not override a hiring freeze or other substantive Board restriction. 
 
All personnel actions shall comply with applicable law, County personnel policies, collective 
bargaining and employment agreements, and employee notice, representation, due-process, and 
appeal rights. This delegation does not authorize a chief to waive employee rights. 
 
All delegated actions contemplated herein shall be subject to prior review and approval by the 
County Attorney’s Office and the Human Resources Department, solely as to legal sufficiency 
and compliance with applicable policies and procedures.  
 
3.Board Restrictions and Budget Limits: 
All actions shall remain within the Board-adopted budget, lawful appropriations, available 
funding, and authorized staffing limits. Chiefs shall coordinate with the Chief Financial Officer 
to account for ongoing, separation, and transition costs before implementation. 
 
4. Authority Retained by the Board: 
The Board retains ultimate oversight; authority over County policy, appropriations, chief 
positions, and departments established by the Board; and appointment, compensation, discipline, 
and removal authority over employees reporting directly to the Board. Changes to Board 
reporting relationships, classification and compensation plans, salary ranges, and budget 
transfers requiring Board action remain subject to Board approval. 
 
5. Continuing Effect and Effective Date: 
Except as expressly amended, prior Board resolutions, appointments, and reporting assignments 
remain in effect. If any provision is held invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain effective 
to the extent they can operate independently. This Resolution takes effect upon passage and 
adoption. 
    PASSED AND ADOPTED this October 27, 2026, in Pueblo County, Colorado.
