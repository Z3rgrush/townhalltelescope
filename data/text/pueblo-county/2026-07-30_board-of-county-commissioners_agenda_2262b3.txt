A RESOLUTION CONTINUING THE AUTHORIZATION OF A DECLARATION OF 
LOCAL DISASTER EMERGENCY REGARDING THE ASPEN ACRES AREA FIRE  
 
          WHEREAS, Pueblo County has sustained severe damage from an ongoing fire that 
started in Aspen Acres area on the border between Pueblo County and Custer County; and  
 
          WHEREAS, on June 29th, 2026, the fire necessitated the evacuation of people and 
livestock from the Aspen Acres Area, Beulah, San Isabel, Rye, and surrounding areas, all 
located wholly or partially within Pueblo County, Colorado; and  
 
          WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709, which provides that a local disaster may 
be declared” only by the principal executive officer of a political subdivision,” the Chair of the 
Pueblo County Board of County Commissioners issued a Disaster/Emergency Declaration on 
June 29th, 2026; and 
 
          WHEREAS, by Resolution 26-202, dated July 2nd, 2026, this board authorized a 
declaration of Local Disaster Emergency; and 
 
          WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709(1), the declaration of a local disaster “shall 
not be continued or renewed for a period in excess of seven days except by or with the consent 
of the governing board of the political subdivision”; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the cost and magnitude of responding to and recovering from the impacts of 
this event continues to be far in excess of Pueblo County’s available resources; and 
 
          WHEREAS, an imminent threat continues to exist for Pueblo County citizens due to fire 
and smoke; and  
 
          WHEREAS, as of July 28th, 2026, approximately 101,921 acres within the County have 
been affected by the fire, and approximately 240 homes and 9 businesses have been destroyed in 
Pueblo County, requiring the support of over 1900 fire personnel from across the County at 
various times; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the purpose of the Continued Authorization of Declaration is to proceed 
with the response and recovery aspects of any and all applicable disaster emergency plans and to 
authorize the furnishing of aid and assistance; and  
 
          WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners enacted the continued resolution on 
July 9th, 16th, and 23rd 2026, and again desires to enact this Continued Authorization of the 
Emergency Declaration to ensure that financial aid is made available to affected individuals, 
Page 3 of 26
business and governmental entities as applicable and pursuant to state law.  
 
  NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that 
1.  The Declaration Continuing Authorization of the Local Disaster Emergency, as provided 
for herein, is hereby approved, and shall remain in full force and effect until further 
action is taken by the Chair or the Board to terminate or modify the Declaration or until 
the declaration expires pursuant to state statute; and 
2. The Chair of the Board of County Commissioners is authorized to execute said 
Declaration on behalf of Pueblo County. 
  
 
 
 
          PASSED AND ADOPTED this July 30, 2026, in Pueblo County, Colorado.

APPROVING THE AGREEMENT FOR ADMINISTRATION OF SCHOLARSHIP 
FUNDS BETWEEN THE PUEBLO HISPANIC EDUCATION FOUNDATION AND 
PUEBLO COUNTY  
 
          WHEREAS, in 2015, Pueblo County established the Pueblo County Scholarship Fund 
that is funded by marijuana excise tax revenue in accordance with Resolution 15-203 and 
amendments thereto; and  
 
          WHEREAS, the Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation (“PHEF”) is a 501(c)(3) 
nonprofit organization that has been awarding scholarships in Southern Colorado since 1988; 
and  
 
          WHEREAS, to date, PHEF has administered the Scholarship Fund on behalf of Pueblo 
County in prior years; and  
 
          WHEREAS, Pueblo County shall provide PHEF $502,221.10 in scholarship funds 
resulting from the Pueblo County Retail Marijuana Excise Tax imposed by Resolution No. 15-
203; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board has determined that it is in the best interests of the County of 
Pueblo to enter into this Agreement with PHEF to administer and manage the Pueblo County 
Scholarship Fund for the academic year 2026-2027. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that the Agreement for the Administration of Scholarship Funds 
between the Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation and Pueblo County is hereby approved, the 
terms and conditions of which are incorporated herein by this reference and the Chair of the 
Board of County Commissioners and the Director of the Pueblo County Office of Budget and 
Finance are hereby authorized to sign said Agreement on behalf of Pueblo County. 
  
 
 
 
          PASSED AND ADOPTED this July 30, 2026, in Pueblo County, Colorado.

Agreement for the Administration of Scholarship Funds 
 
This Agreement, entered into this  30th day of July 2026, by and between the County of 
Pueblo, Colorado (hereinafter referred to as the "County") and  
 
Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation 
215 S Victoria Street, Suite J 
Pueblo, CO 81003 
          
(hereinafter also referred to as "Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation," “PHEF,” “Central 
Administrator,” or “Contractor”): 
 
WITNESSETH THAT: 
 
 WHEREAS, on September 2, 2015 through the adoption of Resolution No. 15-203, 
Pueblo County certified a ballot issue to the eligible voters of the County at the November 
2015 Coordinated Election requesting approval of an excise tax of up to five percent (5%) 
on the first sale or transfer of unprocessed retail marijuana by a retail marijuana cultivation 
facility; 
 
 WHEREAS, on November 4, 2015 the electorate approved an excise tax on the 
first transfer of unprocessed retail marijuana by a retail cultivation facility, which taxes, in 
part, have been used to create the Pueblo County Scholarship Program; and  
 
 WHEREAS, Resolution No. 15- 203 requires that at least half of the marijuana 
excise taxes shall be spent on scholarships for residents of Pueblo County to attend 
colleges and universities; and  
 
 WHEREAS, consistent with Resolution No. 15 -203, the Board of County 
Commissioners has passed resolutions  to expand the category of students eligible to 
receive scholarship awards; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the Pueblo County Commissioners has determined that a central 
administrator could award the scholarship funds  in the most efficient and effective 
manner; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the Pueblo Hispanic Education  Foundation has contracted with the 
County to allocate some of the scholarship funding since the program’s inception and has 
proven to be a fair, equitable, reliable and responsible steward of the scholarship funds ; 
and 
 
 WHEREAS, the County has determined that it is in the best interest of the citizens 
of Pueblo County to designate the Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation as the Central 
Administrator of the Pueblo County marijuana excise scholarship funds to be distributed 
Page 7 of 26
                             Page 2 
to eligible students for the 2025 Fall Semester and 2026 Spring Semester consistent with 
the terms of this Resolution; and  
  
 WHEREAS, authority exists in the Law, and Funds have been budgeted, 
appropriated and otherwise made available for payment in Account Number 303-419430-
4355.00 Administrative Services and 303-419430-4720.10 Scholarship Program. 
  
 NOW, THEREFORE , in consideration of the above recitals , and subject to the 
terms and conditions hereinafter stated, it is hereby understood and agreed by the parties 
hereto as follows: 
 
1. PROJECT DESCRIPTION AND SCOPE OF SERVICES:   The Contractor agrees 
to serve as the C entral Administrator of the Pueblo County marijuana excise tax 
scholarship funds. As Central Administrator, the Contractor shall advertise the Pueblo 
Scholarship Pr ogram, r ecruit applicants, review and vet applications,  ensure that 
applicants meet the eligibility criteria required by Pueblo County, select award recipients, 
and distribute the scholarship funds to students and/or schools consistent with the terms 
and limitations of Pueblo County Resolution No. 15- 203 and under programs including 
but not limited to: 
 
• Career and Technical Education Program where students are enrolled in CTE, 
apprenticeship or vocational programs within the continental United States.  
 
• Colorado Proud Program where students are enrolled in a school within the state 
of Colorado.  
 
• Red, White & Blue Program  where students are enrolled in a school within the 
United States, not within Colorado.  
 
• Graduate Studies Program  where students are enrolled in a master ’s level 
program for education, social work or business.  
 
• Pueblo Merit Scholarship is designed to support students who will be attending 
one of the two local institutions within Pueblo, Pueblo Community College or 
Colorado State University Pueblo.  
 
• Veterans Program provides financial support to students engaged in the Veterans 
Upward Bound (VUB) Program.  
 
Distribution of funds is to be consistent with the terms of this Agreement. PHEF shall not 
discriminate on the basis of race, color, religion (creed), gender, gender expression, age, 
national origin, marital status, sexual orientation, disability, military status, or any other 
restricting classification in its selection of scholarship recipients.  Pueblo Hispanic 
Education Foundation may, but  is not required to, utilize up to 5% of the award for 
reimbursement of administrative costs associated with distributing the scholarships.  The 
Page 8 of 26
                             Page 3 
Contractor must spend at least 95% of the amounts provided on scholarships for the 2026 
Fall Semester and/or the 2027 Spring Semester.   
 
2. ELIGIBILITY REQUIREMENTS FOR STUDENTS RECEIVING SCHOLARSHIP 
FUNDS:  Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation shall determine the allocation of the 
scholarship funds  by program  and ensure that students  who are selected to receive 
scholarships fulfill the scholarship eligibility criteria described in Pueblo County Resolution 
No.15-203 which is attached hereto and incorporated herein as Exhibit A.  For applicants 
that meet the Pueblo County resident eligibility criteria, PHEF must verify that they have 
been residents of Pueblo County for 12 continuous months prior to applying for a 
scholarship by obtaining a copies of a lease, rent r eceipts, proof of home ownership in 
Pueblo County or a notarized statement from a landlord that the applicant has resided in 
Pueblo County for 12 continuous months.  PHEF shall make the documents evidencing  
an awardee’s  proof of citizenship in Pueblo County available to Pueblo County upon 
request. 
 
A. Reporting Requirements:   Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation shall 
submit a report  to the County, specifically to Melissa Spengler, Marijuana 
Tax and Revenue Specialist, Pueblo County Office of Budget and Finance, 
for monitoring purposes, no later than  June 30, 2027.  Such report shall 
indicate by program, the students to whom scholarships were awarded, the 
high school, online school, or home school in Pueblo County from which the 
student graduated.  If the awardee did not graduate from a Pueblo County 
high school , PHEF must state which documents the student provided 
demonstrated 12 months of continuous residence in Pueblo County . The 
report must also state where each scholarship recipient attended college or 
university, and the amount of scholarship awarded.   
 
 
3. TIME OF PERFORMANCE:   Distribution of the scholarships are to commence as 
soon as practicable after the execution of this Agreement and shall be undertaken 
and completed in such a manner and sequence as to assure their expeditious 
completion in the light of the purpose of this Agreement, but in any event all of 
the services required hereunder shall be completed by June 30 , 2027.  Any 
scholarship funds provided to Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation pursuant to 
this Agreement that are not spent on tuition for eligible Pueblo Hispanic Education 
Foundation students for the 2026 Fall Semester and/or the 2027 Spring Semester 
shall be refunded to Pueblo County on or before June 30, 2027. 
 
4. METHOD OF PAYMENT:   County will pay Pueblo Hispanic Education Foundation 
$504,221.10 of marijuana excise tax funds specifically designated for scholarships 
for disbursement through  the programs as outlined above  within t hirty (30) 
business days of the execution of this Agreement.

APPROVING THE AGREEMENT BETWEEN CROSSROADS TURNING POINTS, 
INC. AND THE PUEBLO COUNTY DEPARTMENT OF HUMAN SERVICES, ACTING 
AS FISCAL AGENT FOR THE COLLABORATIVE MANAGEMENT PROGRAM  
 
          WHEREAS, the Collaborative Management Program (“CMP”) is established through 
Colorado House Bill 04-1451 with the purpose of enabling families and community agencies to 
engage collaboratively in order to access more effective and efficient services, improve 
outcomes, and encourage family preservation; and  
  
          WHEREAS, C. R. S. § 24-1.9-101, et seq., authorizes the County Department of Human 
Services to enter into a Memorandum of Understanding (“MOU”) with specific agencies for the 
purpose of promoting a collaborative system of local-level interagency oversight groups and 
individualized service and support teams to coordinate and manage the provision of services to 
children and families who would benefit from integrated multi-agency services; and 
  
          WHEREAS, the Pueblo County Department of Human Services desires to purchase the 
services of Contractor, at the direction of the House Bill 04-1451-Collaborative Management 
Project, locally named the Pueblo Interagency Oversight Group (PIOG); to administer and to 
assure that the mission, intent, and purpose of PIOG is consistent with HB 04-1451 and the 
related MOU to effectively execute policies, as approved by the Interagency Oversight Group; 
establish and effectively manage a case tracking process to assure measurement of outcomes and 
reporting processes; to assure coordinated care for children, youth and families involved in one 
or more of the community’s systems; and advocate for the provision of services that promote the 
wellbeing, safety and permanency of child and youth in Pueblo County while remaining family 
focused; and 
  
          WHEREAS, Pueblo County, acting by and through its Department of Human Services, 
desires to act as the fiscal agent for the CMP and provide funding of up to $210,000.00 which 
has been allocated through its FY 2026-2027 funding.  
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that the Agreement between Crossroads Turning Point, Inc. and 
Pueblo County, on behalf of the Pueblo County Department of Human Services, acting as fiscal 
agent for the CMP, the terms and conditions of which are incorporated herein by this reference, 
is hereby approved nunc pro tunc July 1, 2026, the Chair of the Board of County Commissioners 
and the Director of the Pueblo County Department of Human Services (or designee), are hereby 
authorized to execute the Agreement on behalf of Pueblo County.

AGREEMENT  
FOR  
COLLABORATIVE MANAGEMENT PROGRAM SERVICES  
THIS AGREEMENT is entered into and is effective this 1st Day of July, 2026, nunc pro tunc, (“Effective Date”) by and between 
Crossroads’ Turning Point, Inc., (“Contractor”) and Pueblo County, Colorado, on behalf of the Pueblo County Department of Human 
Services (“County”). 
 
 WITNESSETH: 
  
 WHEREAS, C. R. S. § 24-1.9-101, et seq., authorizes the county department of human services to enter into a memorandum 
of understanding with specific agencies for the purpose of promoting a collaborative system of local-level interagency oversight groups 
and individualized service and support teams to coordinate and manage the provision of services to children and families who would 
benefit from integrated multi-agency services; and  
 
 WHEREAS, the County and Contractor are partners in the Collaborative Management Program as set forth in House Bill 04-
1451 and the relevant Memorandum of Understanding (MOU); and 
 
 WHEREAS, the Collaborative Management Program would be enhanced by contracting for coordination of services as outlined 
in the MOU between the eleven (11) community agencies involved; and 
 
 WHEREAS, County desires to purchase the services of Contractor, at the direction of the House Bill 04-1451-Collaborative 
Management Project, locally named the Pueblo Interagency Oversight Group (PIOG); to administer and to assure that the mission, 
intent, and purpose of PIOG is consistent with HB 04-1451 and the related MOU  to effectively execute policies, as approved by the 
Interagency Oversight Group; establish and effectively manage a case tracking process to assure measurement of outcomes and reporting 
processes; to assure coordinated care for children, youth and families involved in one or more of the community’s systems; and advocate 
for the provision of services that promote the wellbeing, safety and permanency of child and youth in Pueblo County while remaining 
family focused; and 
 
WHEREAS, the County acts as the fiscal agent to the PIOG and maintains the incentive funds as distributed according to HB 
04-1451.   
 
 WHEREAS, Contractor is qualified, experienced, and has satisfactorily provided services to the County in the past; and 
 
 WHEREAS, in order to set forth clearly the rights and responsibilities of each of the parties, the parties desire to enter into this 
Agreement. 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the foregoing and the mutual promises and covenants contained herein, the parties 
agree as follows:

 Contractor agrees: 
  
A. To utilize its expertise to provide services to County as described herein and:  
 
1. To perform the specific services set forth in Attachment “A” of this Agreement attached hereto and 
incorporated herein.

payment of wages, health benefits, sick/leave time and all other benefits provided by Contractor and shall be

Contractor shall ensure all income tax, social security, Medicare, workers compensation, unemployment and other 
applicable taxes are properly withheld in accordance with all applicable state and federal laws/regulations.  
 
3. Although County’s appointed Child Welfare Program Administrator can make recommendations to 
Contractor concerning the individual’s performance, such individual shall ultimately report to Contractor in the 
performance of services under this contract and, therefore, all decisions with regard to supervising, discipline, and 
termination are ultimately the responsibility of Contractor.

THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS

A RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING DOTTED-LINE REPORTING RELATIONSHIPS 
WITHIN THE PUEBLO COUNTY ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE  
 
          WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of Pueblo County, Colorado has 
established a Chief Administrative Officer position and a C-suite model of enterprise leadership 
in which the Chief Administrative Officer serves as the County’s enterprise integrator 
responsible for cross-department coordination and implementation of Board priorities while 
respecting the independent authority of the Chief Financial Officer, Chief Human Resources 
Officer, Chief Technology Officer, Chief Legal Officer, and other designated officials over their 
respective domains; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board has determined that certain positions may benefit from a 
secondary, or dotted-line, reporting relationship in addition to a primary solid-line reporting 
relationship in order to facilitate cross-functional coordination, project oversight, and efficient 
use of specialized expertise without altering primary supervisory authority, performance 
evaluation, or employment decisions; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the use of dotted-line reporting relationships is a recognized feature of 
matrix organizational structures that supports collaboration across departmental boundaries 
while preserving clear primary accountability; and 
 
          WHEREAS, any amendments to the County’s Organizational Structure shall be made by 
separate resolution of the Board consistent with existing County practice. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVEDby the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that: 
1. The Board hereby authorizes the establishment of dotted-line reporting relationships 
within the Pueblo County organizational structure. A dotted-line reporting relationship 
constitutes a secondary reporting relationship in which an employee receives functional 
guidance, project direction, or specialized coordination from a manager other than the 
employee’s primary (solid-line) supervisor, while the solid-line supervisor retains 
primary authority over performance evaluation, work prioritization outside the secondary 
scope, compensation recommendations, and employment decisions. 
2. The purpose of authorizing dotted-line reporting relationships is to enable matrix-style 
coordination that supports cross-functional projects, enterprise initiatives, and efficient 
deployment of specialized expertise consistent with the Chief Administrative Officer’s 
role as enterprise integrator and the independent authority of C-suite peers and 
department directors. 
3. Specific dotted-line reporting relationships shall be designated by subsequent resolution 
Page 25 of 26
of the Board, or by written recommendation of the Chief Administrative Officer 
submitted to and approved by the Board via resolution. Each designation shall identify 
the employee or position, the solid-line supervisor, the dotted-line manager, the scope of 
the secondary relationship, and the expected duration if temporary. 
4. Upon designation of any dotted-line reporting relationship, the Chief Administrative 
Officer shall ensure that the relationship is documented in the applicable organizational 
chart, position description if appropriate, and communicated in writing to the affected 
employee, solid-line supervisor, and dotted-line manager, including clear delineation of 
decision rights and escalation paths for priority conflicts. 
5. This Resolution does not alter existing solid-line reporting relationships established by 
prior Board resolutions, including those governing the C-suite model and direct reports 
to the Chief Administrative Officer, except as expressly modified by subsequent 
designation under paragraph 3. 
6. The provisions in this Resolution shall apply only to employees under the administrative 
authority of the Board of County Commissioners.  
7. This Resolution shall be effective upon passage and adoption of this Resolution.
