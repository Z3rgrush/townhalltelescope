SUBJECT:  SLI-25-3 Three Corners Connector, Energy Transmission Project 
Executive Summary: 
Three Corners Connector LLC requests approval of a Pueblo County 1041 permit for the Pueblo County 
portion of an approximately 290-mile, up-to-525-kilovolt high-voltage direct current transmission 
project connecting Pueblo, Colorado, and Guymon, Oklahoma. Within Pueblo County, the project 
includes approximately 29.8 miles of HVDC transmission line, approximately 10.3 miles of 345-kV 
alternating-current transmission line, and a converter station east of the intersection of Doyle Road and 
Bush Road. The system is designed to transfer up to 3,600 megawatts between the Western and Eastern 
Interconnections. 
The approximately 55-acre converter station is a substantial permanent component of the project. The 
facility will include valve halls, DC halls, converter transformers, AC yards, cooling equipment, a control 
building, a generator, stormwater facilities, security fencing, and nighttime lighting. Because of the size 
and nature of the facility, staff has evaluated this facility separately from the temporary and permanent 
impacts associated with the transmission corridor.

Location Zoning and Existing Land Use: 
The Pueblo County segment begins near the existing Comanche Substation southeast of the City of 
Pueblo. The AC line extends from the Comanche area to the proposed converter station; the HVDC line 
extends from the converter station generally south and east to the Otero County line. The converter 
station is proposed on applicant-owned land east of the intersection of Doyle Road and Bush Road. The 
route crosses predominantly rural agricultural and undeveloped lands and is near scattered residences, 
existing electrical infrastructure, renewable-energy development, the Broad Acre Landfill, and oil and 
gas activity. (See Exhibit 1.) 
Zoning and Parcel Data: See Appendix K  for a list of all participating parcels. 
Project Components: 
Transmission Facilities 
• Approximately 29.8 miles of up-to-525-kV HVDC transmission line and approximately 10.3 miles of 
345-kV AC transmission line within Pueblo County. 
• A 200-foot-wide right-of-way for both the AC and HVDC segments. 
• Weathering or non-reflective tubular steel monopoles generally ranging from 115 to 195 feet in 
height, with two-pole configurations at angles and selected crossings. 
• Drilled-pier concrete foundations, preliminary dimensions ranging from approximately 8 to 12 feet 
in diameter and 32 to 54 feet deep depending on structure type. 
• Permanent access roads up to 25 feet wide and temporary workspaces, pulling sites, laydown yards, 
and potential helicopter fly yards. 
• A future Phase II conductor on the same structures and within the same right-of-way; additional 
structures may be installed at heavy-angle or dead-end locations. 
Pueblo Converter Station 
The converter station converts electricity between AC and DC and is the terminal facility connecting the 
HVDC system to the existing Comanche Substation. The preliminary plan places the fenced facility on 
approximately 55 acres and shows cooling equipment, spare-parts storage, AC yards, valve halls, DC 
halls, a control building, generator, converter transformers, AC and DC line entries, and a detention 
basin. The applicant states that the submitted plot plan, grading plan, drainage report, visual 
simulations, and elevations are preliminary and assume the greatest potential impacts; updated 
materials will be submitted as design advances. (See Exhibit 2.) 
Two permanent driveways from Bush Road are proposed, each approximately 50 feet wide with turning 
radii up to 75 feet to accommodate large equipment. The site would be enclosed by a 12-foot chain-link 
fence. The applicant states that approximately 300 trees have been installed on nearby private 
properties through its Community Investment Plan to screen views from individual residences. 
Development Schedule Construction and Operations 
Construction is anticipated to begin in 2027 and continue for approximately three to four years, with 
commercial operation as early as 2031. Construction sequencing includes mobilization, laydown yards, 
surveying, right-of-way clearing, access construction, erosion controls, grading and excavation,

Comprehensive Plan Conformance 
The 2022 Pueblo Regional Comprehensive Plan supports reliable and resilient infrastructure and the 
responsible siting of energy facilities while directing development to minimize impacts on residential 
neighborhoods, agricultural lands, sensitive natural areas, water resources, wildlife habitat, and scenic 
quality. The project would create a new interregional connection capable of improving grid reliability 
and transferring power between the Western and Eastern Interconnections. Those public benefits are 
consistent with the Plan only when balanced against the project’s local impacts through avoidance, final-
design controls, construction management, restoration, and long-term operational mitigation. 
The converter station will permanently convert approximately 55 acres of rural land to an industrial 
utility use and will be visible from nearby residences and roads. The transmission corridor crosses land 
classified as prime farmland if irrigated, water resources, and CPW-mapped habitat. With the conditions 
recommended below, staff finds the project generally consistent with the Plan. This conclusion depends 
on final plans demonstrating that the project has avoided sensitive resources where feasible and 
minimized or mitigated impacts where avoidance is not feasible. 
Impact Assessment; 
Converter Station Visual Character and Lighting 
The converter station will introduce a large, visually dominant industrial form into a broad, generally 
open rural landscape. The applicant’s simulations acknowledge that the facility may obscure a portion of 
the mountain horizon from some viewpoints and that the greatest visual change will occur for nearby 
residents and travelers. A color treatment is proposed to reduce contrast. The applicant concluded that 
effective onsite vegetative screening would be difficult to achieve because of the facility’s scale, the 
area’s climate, and the irrigation needed to establish and maintain substantial vegetation. Instead, the 
applicant reports that approximately 300 trees have been installed on nearby properties through its 
Community Investment Plan. These plantings may provide localized benefits but are not expected to 
fully screen the converter station. Accordingly, visual-impact mitigation should focus on the facility’s 
final colors, reflectivity, lighting, equipment arrangement, and approved height and development 
envelope. (See Exhibit 3.) 
Permanent lighting will increase visible nighttime light near the converter station. The applicant 
proposes DarkSky-compliant, fully shielded, downward-directed, warm-toned fixtures. A photometric

Noise Nuisance and Electromagnetic Fields 
Construction will create temporary noise, dust, vibration, traffic, and disruption along the route and at 
the converter station. During operation, the transmission lines may produce low-level corona noise, and 
converter-station transformers and cooling equipment will operate for extended periods. The applicant 
predicts that converter-station sound at the nearest residential property line will not exceed 55 dBA. 
Because the final equipment has not been selected, the applicant should provide final acoustic modeling 
prepared by a qualified professional demonstrating that the final design is consistent with the noise 
levels represented in the application. The project shall comply with the Pueblo County Noise Ordinance, 
Pueblo County Code Chapter 10.06. Noise complaints and alleged violations shall be investigated and 
enforced pursuant to Section 10.06.110. 
The application also includes modeled magnetic-field levels based on the preliminary transmission-line 
configuration and states that the project will comply with applicable National Electrical Safety Code 
requirements. If the final design materially differs from the modeled configuration, updated modeling 
should be provided by the applicant. 
Water Wastewater Drainage and Floodplain 
The route study identified 76 mapped water resources within the study area and indicates that the 
centerline crosses 71, including 29 mapped wetland features. The Saint Charles River, Huerfano River, 
Doyle Arroyo, Sixmile Creek, and Chicosa Creek are among the major features. The applicant proposes to 
span water resources and site structures outside wetlands, waters of the United States, and regulated 
floodplains where practicable, use construction BMPs, and obtain applicable floodplain, federal and 
state permits. 
The converter station will use approximately 45,000 gallons of ethylene glycol in a 50-percent mixture 
with deionized water in a closed-loop cooling system. The maintenance building is expected to use 
approximately 1,400 to 2,000 gallons of potable water annually. The applicant has initiated discussions 
with St. Charles Mesa Water District and proposes either a wastewater utility connection or a permitted 
onsite wastewater treatment system. Final service commitments and permits have not been submitted. 
The final converter-station drainage report, grading plan, detention design, drainage easements, 
maintenance agreement, and operations manual remain subject to Public Works review. 
Wildlife and Habitat

Vegetation Agriculture and Reclamation 
Most of the right-of-way is grassland or herbaceous cover. The applicant reports that approximately 28 
percent of the right-of-way and approximately 46.8 percent of the converter-station site are classified as 
prime farmland if irrigated. Temporary disturbance will include access roads, structure pads, pulling 
sites, and laydown yards. The applicant proposes topsoil segregation, erosion controls, native seed, 
noxious-weed control, post-construction monitoring, and restoration of areas not needed for 
operations. These commitments should be incorporated into the permit and secured by the Public 
Works revegetation bond. 
Traffic Roads and Access 
Construction traffic will use numerous County and local roads and will include worker vehicles, heavy 
trucks, concrete deliveries, oversized equipment, and station-component deliveries. Public Works 
requires a Road Use Agreement, access and excavation permits, route and traffic data, drainage and 
stormwater submittals, and a revegetation bond. CDOT commented that the submitted trip-generation 
data are insufficient because they do not include laborer vehicle data or distribution at highway 
connections and requested haul-route and labor-route files in KMZ format. CDOT further advised that 
access permits may be required for each state-highway connection used during construction, including 
indirect connections through local roads. 
The applicant removed Doyle Road as a proposed haul route in response to public feedback. Final haul 
routes, construction traffic volumes, and measures addressing potential impacts to County roads will be 
established through the Road Use Agreement. Because construction of the converter station will require 
large and potentially oversized deliveries, the applicant will also be responsible for coordinating with 
CDOT regarding the use of state highway connections and obtaining any permits or approvals CDOT 
determines are required. 
 
Fire Emergency Response and Security 
The project crosses the Pueblo Rural Fire District and the Pueblo County Sheriff’s Office Emergency 
Services Bureau service area. The applicant submitted an Emergency Response Plan and proposes 
remote monitoring, de-energization capability, onsite fire-suppression equipment during construction, 
crew training, and vegetation management. The converter station includes energized equipment, large 
oil-filled transformers, cooling systems, batteries, a generator, and a 12-foot security fence. Final plans 
should be reviewed with the responding fire district, Sheriff’s Office Emergency Services Bureau, and 
County Building and Fire officials, with documented responder access, gate/key arrangements, water 
supply, shutoff and isolation procedures, hazardous-material information, training, and emergency 
contacts before energization.

Hazardous Materials and Spill Prevention 
The preliminary project information identifies sulfur hexafluoride, transformer and equipment oils, 
combustible non-PCB fluids, and an ethylene-glycol solution among the materials expected at the 
converter station. Final materials and quantities will depend on equipment selection. The applicant will 
be responsible for complying with all applicable federal, state, and local requirements governing 
hazardous-material storage, secondary containment, spill prevention and response, stormwater 
protection, fire safety, and emergency planning. Technical review, permitting, and enforcement will be 
conducted by the agencies having jurisdiction over those requirements. 
Geology Soils and Cultural Resources 
The Colorado Geological Survey found no unusual geologic or geotechnical constraints that would 
preclude the transmission line or converter station and did not object to approval.  
Economic and Public Service Effects 
The applicant projects construction employment and regional economic benefits and states that the 
project will not require permanent increases in County staffing or public services. Staff notes that the 
converter station creates ongoing inspection, emergency-response, and compliance demands even 
without permanent onsite employees. Conditions requiring cost reimbursement, third-party review, 
access for inspection, and emergency-responder coordination are necessary to prevent an undue 
burden on County services. 
Facility Retirement and Reclamation 
The application states that the checklist’s decommissioning requirement applies only to utility-scale 
solar and therefore provides no full decommissioning plan for the transmission and converter facilities. 
The proposed infrastructure nevertheless has a finite service life and includes large foundations, 
buildings, transformers, oils, SF6-containing equipment, roads, fencing, and permanent drainage 
improvements. Staff recommends requiring a facility retirement, removal, and reclamation plan, 
including financial assurance, before construction. This closes a material gap without assuming that 
near-term retirement is expected.

Staff Findings: 
The following findings are based on the current application, referral comments, applicant commitments, 
and recommended conditions.  
Technical and financial feasibility. The application describes established HVDC technology, an 
experienced development team, procurement and financing strategies, and a multiyear development 
schedule. Final engineering and supporting technical reports will be completed and submitted before 
construction in accordance with applicable permit conditions and agency requirements. 
Natural hazards. CGS identified no unusual geologic constraints. Compliance with NESC design 
standards, geotechnical recommendations, drainage controls, vegetation management, and emergency-
response requirements can reduce identified risks to an acceptable level. 
Land use and visual quality. The transmission line and converter station will change the rural landscape. 
The converter station’s permanent scale and nighttime lighting are the principal localized visual impacts. 
Non-reflective exterior finishes, shielded lighting, and adherence to the approved facility footprint, 
equipment layout, and height envelope are necessary to minimize those impacts.

Public services and fiscal burden. Public services and fiscal burden. The project does not create 
residential population or school demand, but it may require road, inspection, fire, emergency, and 
enforcement services. Road agreements, cost reimbursement, responder coordination, and inspection 
access are necessary to prevent an undue public burden. The developer will be responsible for project-
specific review, inspection, mitigation, road repair, and other costs required by the permit conditions, 
applicable agreements, and County regulations. 
Water, wetlands, and habitat. The applicant proposes to span aquatic features where practicable and 
to use erosion-control and aquatic-crossing best management practices. CPW recommended buffers 
around identified aquatic High Priority Habitat. The applicant indicated that many of those buffers can 
be achieved but that some may conflict with landowner requests, cultural or Tribal resources, or 
engineering constraints. CPW requested additional site-specific information regarding those locations, 
and coordination remains ongoing. This status is provided for the Board’s consideration in evaluating the 
project’s potential effects on water resources and wildlife habitat. 
Nuisance. Construction will create temporary noise, dust, vibration, traffic, and other disturbances, 
particularly near residences. The converter station will also introduce permanent operational noise and 
nighttime lighting. Final plans should demonstrate compliance with the Pueblo County Noise Ordinance, 
Pueblo County Code Chapter 10.06, and the outdoor-lighting standards of UDC Section 17.04.040.  
Hazardous materials. The converter station will contain substantial volumes of oil, glycol solution, and 
equipment containing sulfur hexafluoride. The applicant is responsible for complying with all applicable 
federal, state, and local requirements governing hazardous-material storage, secondary containment, 
spill prevention and response, fire protection, and emergency planning. Compliance will be 
administered and enforced by the agencies having jurisdiction over those requirements. 
Plans and need. The project advances regional grid reliability and interregional transfer capability and is 
generally consistent with the 2022 Plan when its localized impacts are mitigated. The County’s finding 
concerns local land-use approval and does not substitute for any state or federal determination of 
system need. 
Property rights. The applicant reports that it has secured voluntary options along approximately 97 
percent of the Pueblo County alignment. Approval of the 1041 permit does not grant access to private 
property or convey any easement or other property right. The applicant is solely responsible for 
obtaining all property rights and lawful access necessary to construct, operate, and maintain the project. 
Overall finding. Based on the application, referral comments, applicant responses and commitments, 
and the conditions recommended below, staff finds that the project can meet the applicable criteria in 
UDC Sections 17.07.050 and 17.07.060.

Recommended Conditions of Approval 
1. General compliance. This permit does not exempt the permit holder from zoning, building, fire, 
floodplain, stormwater, access, road, health, environmental, or other applicable approvals. 
Construction and operation shall comply with all applicable local, state, and federal requirements. 
2. Approved plans and impact envelope. The project shall be constructed and operated in substantial 
conformance with the SLI-25-3 application, supplemental materials, applicant commitments, final 
approved plans, and these conditions. Where a condition conflicts with an applicant commitment, 
the more protective requirement applies unless the County approves otherwise in writing. 
3. Final plans. Before any building permit or construction activity, the permit holder shall submit final 
site plans, profiles, structure schedules, access plans, converter-station plans and elevations, 
equipment list, grading and drainage plans, lighting plan, landscaping and screening documentation, 
and updated legal descriptions and GIS data for County review and approval. 
4. Construction management. Before ground disturbance, the permit holder shall submit an updated 
Construction Management and Mitigation Plan addressing construction phases, hours, workforce 
transportation, haul routes, oversized loads, staging and laydown yards, helicopter use, dust, noise, 
vibration, waste, sanitation, complaints, emergency procedures, restoration, and responsible parties. 
5. County contact. The permit holder shall designate a single project manager and provide the County, 
affected agencies, adjacent owners, and occupants with 24-hour contact information for construction 
and operational complaints and emergencies. Complaints and responses shall be logged and provided 
to the County upon request. 
6. Temporary facilities. Temporary construction areas, laydown yards, helicopter fly yards, and similar 
facilities shall obtain any required County approvals before use. If a concrete batch plant is 
established in Pueblo County, it shall obtain all applicable County approvals. Temporary facilities shall 
be removed and disturbed areas reclaimed after construction unless their continued use is separately 
approved. 
7. Vegetation and noxious weeds. Before construction, the permit holder shall submit the final Noxious 
Weed Management Plan and revegetation plan to the County. Only EPA-approved herbicides may be 
applied by licensed applicators. No herbicide shall be applied within 150 feet of an approved 
groundwater well. The County shall be notified before pesticide or fertilizer application. 
8. Revegetation monitoring. Disturbed areas shall be restored with locally appropriate native seed and 
monitored until approved success criteria for stabilization, native establishment, and noxious-weed 
control are met. Poorly established or eroded areas shall be repaired and reseeded. 
9. Converter-station design. The final converter station shall remain within the approved approximately 
55-acre site and the maximum equipment, building, and impact envelope shown in the approved 
plans. Exterior finishes shall use non-reflective colors and treatments consistent with the approved 
visual simulations. 
10. Outdoor lighting. Lighting shall be limited to that necessary for safety, security, and maintenance 
and shall comply with UDC Section 17.04.040. Before a building permit, the permit holder shall 
submit a photometric plan showing fixture type, color temperature, mounting height, shielding,

shall be fully shielded and directed downward. Temporary construction lighting shall be shut off 
when work ceases except where required for safety. 
11. Operational noise. Construction and operation shall comply with the Pueblo County Noise 
Ordinance, Pueblo County Code Chapter 10.06. Noise complaints and alleged violations are subject to 
investigation and enforcement pursuant to Section 10.06.110. 
12. Water and wastewater. Before construction of the converter station, the permit holder shall 
provide evidence of an approved potable-water source and approved wastewater service or onsite 
wastewater treatment permit. No County approval constitutes a commitment of service by St.

converter station is energized, the permit holder shall provide the responding fire district and the

Safety Data Sheets, and emergency-response information needed for responder planning.

and inspection costs authorized by the UDC or required by these conditions.
