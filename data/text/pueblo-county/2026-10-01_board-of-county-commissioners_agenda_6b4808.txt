A RESOLUTION CONTINUING THE AUTHORIZATION OF A DECLARATION OF 
LOCAL DISASTER EMERGENCY REGARDING THE ASPEN ACRES AREA FIRE 
AND SUBSEQUENT FLOODING  
 
          WHEREAS, Pueblo County has sustained severe damage from the fire that started in 
Aspen Acres area on the border between Pueblo County and Custer County and further damage 
from flooding in the area of the burn scar; and  
 
          WHEREAS, on June 29th, 2026, the fire necessitated the evacuation of people and 
livestock from the Aspen Acres Area, Beulah, San Isabel, Rye, and surrounding areas, all 
located wholly or partially within Pueblo County, Colorado; and  
 
          WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709, which provides that a local disaster may 
be declared” only by the principal executive officer of a political subdivision,” the Chair of the 
Pueblo County Board of County Commissioners issued a Disaster/Emergency Declaration on 
June 29th, 2026; and 
 
          WHEREAS, by Resolution 26-202, dated July 2nd, 2026, this board authorized a 
declaration of Local Disaster Emergency; and 
 
          WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709(1), the declaration of a local disaster “shall 
not be continued or renewed for a period in excess of seven days except by or with the consent 
of the governing board of the political subdivision”; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the cost and magnitude of responding to and recovering from the impacts of 
this event continues to be far in excess of Pueblo County’s available resources; and 
 
          WHEREAS, an imminent threat continues to exist for Pueblo County citizens due to fire 
and smoke and flash flooding; and  
 
          WHEREAS, over 101,965 acres have been affected by the fire, and approximately 240 
homes and 9 businesses have been destroyed in Pueblo County requiring the support of over 
1900 fire personnel from across the County at various times, and with the continuing and 
developing risk of flash flooding in the area of the burn scar; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the purpose of the Continued Authorization of Declaration is to proceed 
with the response and recovery aspects of any and all applicable disaster emergency plans and to 
authorize the furnishing of aid and assistance; and  
 
          WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners enacted the continued resolution on

2026, and again desires to enact this Continued Authorization of the Emergency Declaration to

entities as applicable and pursuant to state law.  
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of

          WHEREAS, under the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) Program, 
funds are provided to local governments to support a broad range of activities to prevent and 
control crime based on local needs and conditions; and 
 
          WHEREAS, under the disparate allocation provision included as part of the original 
Local Law Enforcement Block Grant (LLEBG) legislation, a state’s Attorney General is 
authorized to certify a county as a “disparate jurisdiction,” making the county eligible to receive 
a fair share of federal funds that had been allocated to jurisdictions that share certain law 
enforcement responsibilities with such counties; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Colorado State Attorney General’s Office has certified Pueblo County as 
a disparate jurisdiction for FY 2026 JAG funding; and 
 
          WHEREAS, federal law requires that a certified disparate county and the city or cities 
within the county agree on how to divide available funds to their mutual satisfaction, and that if 
the parties are unable to reach an agreement, neither the county nor the city are eligible to apply 
for or receive funds; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the City of Pueblo intends to apply for FY 2026 JAG funding in the amount 
of $138,742.00 of which $33,298.08 will be available to Pueblo County; $6,659.62 to the 10th

no matching funds required; and 
 
          WHEREAS, and the District Attorney of the Tenth Judicial District, Pueblo County 
Sheriff, and Chief of the Pueblo Police Department have agreed upon the division of funds to 
their mutual satisfaction as set forth in a Memorandum of Understanding among the parties; and 
 
          WHEREAS, this Board supports programs which promote public safety and reduce crime 
and appreciates the joint effort by the County and City to reach a swift and mutually satisfactory 
disbursement agreement. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of

Disbursement of Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) Funds for FY 2026,

Proposal Narrative 
Description of the Issue 
Pueblo County, Colorado, maintains a population of 168,162, with its county seat, the 
City of Pueblo, operating as a majority-minority community composed of a broad diversity of 
residents with unique linguistic, racial, ethnic, and cultural backgrounds. The community faces 
an alarming poverty rate of 24.4%, which severely strains local resources and significantly 
drives criminal activity. The effective delivery of public safety and law enforcement services 
remains a critical factor affecting economic development, business investment, population 
retention, and the county's overall fiscal well-being. 
Pueblo's total crime rate of 49 per 1,000 residents stands as one of the highest in the 
nation. Furthermore, the city’s violent crime rate of 6.49 per 1,000 residents is 70% higher 
than the Colorado state average (3.81 per 1,000) and 62% higher than the national median 
(4.00 per 1,000). Gun violence and serious offenses remain concentrated in areas that directly 
mirror low-income household distributions, underscoring the urgent need to implement 
targeted infrastructure, technology, and equipment upgrades across all facets of the criminal 
justice system. 
To quantify local public safety trends and measure institutional impact, the Pueblo 
Police Department (PPD) monitors Part I crime datasets compiled under the FBI's Uniform 
Crime Reporting (UCR) guidelines. These figures reflect serious, regularly reported violent and 
property crimes impacting the community:

Disparate Jurisdiction Governance and Fiscal Administration 
The City and County of Pueblo have been actively engaged as a certified "disparate 
jurisdiction" under the Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) program since its inception in 2005. To 
ensure collaborative equity, the three primary components of the local justice system—the 
Pueblo Police Department (PPD), the Pueblo County Sheriff’s Office (PCSO), and the Tenth 
Judicial District Attorney's Office—have established a long-standing allocation formula 
executed via a formal Memorandum of Understanding (MOU). Under the FY 2026 formula 
award, the total aggregate allocation of $138,742.00 will be divided as follows: 
 Pueblo Police Department (Primary Grantee/Fiscal Agent): Retains 76% of the total 
award, equal to $105,443.92. 
 Pueblo County Sheriff's Office (Sub-Grantee): Receives 80% of the County's 24% share, 
equal to $26,638.46.

PPD serves as the primary applicant and administrative fiscal agent for this grant. Upon 
official award approval, PPD will draw down the full balance and disburse the exact sub-

Grant Compliance Manager within the Finance Department, maintains robust review 
procedures and internal financial controls. Sub-grantees are continually monitored for strict 
compliance with all applicable Department of Justice (DOJ) guidelines, accounting practices, 
and procurement policies. No administrative or compliance issues have occurred during 
previous fiscal funding cycles, ensuring reliable oversight for the FY 2026 program. 
Project Design and Implementation 
 
The Pueblo Police Department’s (PPD) strategic planning approach relies on an 
exhaustive analysis of localized Part I crime statistics balanced against pressing, operational 
infrastructure deficiencies. PPD, the Pueblo County Sheriff’s Office (PCSO), and the Tenth 
Judicial District Attorney’s Office selected the proposed FY 2026 initiatives to directly maximize 
officer safety, reduce technological downtime, and optimize criminal prosecution capabilities. 
Beyond internal data monitoring, PPD actively captures community safety feedback by 
engaging daily with citizens on multiple social media platforms, including Facebook (84,596 
followers), X (9,501 followers), and YouTube (836 subscribers). Public comments, safety 
requests, and neighborhood concerns are cross-referenced with regional agency demands and 
coordinated with State JAG applications and external funding sources to avoid duplication of 
effort. Furthermore, the City hosts an open public meeting regarding the intended use of

          WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of Pueblo County, Colorado ("Board") 
is authorized to appoint persons to County administrative offices to serve at the pleasure of the

          WHEREAS, the Board appointed Craig McBrain to the position of Chief Financial 
Officer pursuant to Resolution No. 26-100; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board desires to remove Craig McBrain from that appointment and 
retain the Chief Financial Officer position. 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board believes Mr. McBrain can serve the Pueblo County Budget and 
Finance Department in a different capacity. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVEDby the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that: 
1. Craig McBrain is hereby removed from the position of Chief Financial Officer (CFO) of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, effective upon passage of this Resolution. His appointment 
under Resolution No. 26-100 is terminated. The Chief Financial Officer position shall 
remain established. 
2. Craig McBrain remains an employee of the Budget and Finance Department and he is 
expected to compete for a lower level position in that department. 
3. The Chief Human Resources Officer is hereby authorized and directed to implement this 
action in consultation with the County Attorney and in accordance with applicable law, 
County policies, and contractual obligations.
