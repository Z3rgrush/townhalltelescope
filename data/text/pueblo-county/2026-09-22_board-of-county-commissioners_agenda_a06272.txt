A RESOLUTION APPOINTING A COUNTY ATTORNEY FOR PUEBLO COUNTY  
 
          WHEREAS, the position of County Attorney is required by Colorado law and is essential 
to the effective operation of all aspects of Pueblo County government; and  
 
          WHEREAS, by Resolution 26-171, this Board selected an Interim County Attorney to 
ensure continuity in Pueblo County government leadership; and 
 
          WHEREAS, Brendan Hursh has served in the County Attorney’s office for over eight 
years, and has been serving as Interim County Attorney since his appointment on June 9, 2026; 
and 
 
          WHEREAS, Brendan Hursh possesses the legal education, litigation and supervisory 
experience, and management skills necessary to serve effectively as County Attorney; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board finds that the appointment of Brendan Hursh as County Attorney 
is in the best interest of Pueblo County Government and Pueblo residents. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVEDby the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that: 
1. Brendan Hursh is hereby appointed to the position of County Attorney for Pueblo 
County, Colorado, effective nunc pro tunc, Friday, September 11, 2026.  
2. The County Attorney shall exercise all powers, duties, and responsibilities associated 
with the position of County Attorney and Chief Legal Officer (CLO) for the County and 
shall report directly to the Board of County Commissioners. 
 
 
    PASSED AND ADOPTED this September 22, 2026, in Pueblo County, Colorado.

A RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING A LETTER OF SUPPORT OF THE PROPOSED 
LOWER SOIL LEAD LEVELS AT THE COLORADO SMELTER SUPERFUND 
CLEANUP SITE  
 
          WHEREAS, Pueblo was once home to five ore smelters and the Bessemer, Eilers, and 
Grove neighborhoods are adjacent to the former smelter site; and  
 
          WHEREAS, the Colorado Smelter Superfund Site was designated by the Environmental 
Protection Agency (EPA) in December of 2017 when it was listed on the National Priorities List 
due to the high potential for human exposure to metals; and 
 
          WHEREAS, in January 2024, EPA announced updated national guidance for detecting 
and screening for lead in soil at residential properties in Superfund sites and in October 2025, 
EPA lowered screening levels for lead at residential Superfund sites nationwide; and 
 
          WHEREAS, EPA is requesting feedback on this proposed cleanup level change for lead 
and written comments will be accepted from September 1st to October 1st, 2026; and 
 
          WHEREAS, lowering soil lead levels is a practical and necessary step to address long-
standing contamination and reduce community health risks; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Pueblo County Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) fully supports 
EPA’s proposal to lower the residential soil lead cleanup level from 350 ppm to 240 ppm and 
the indoor dust level from 275 ppm to 110 ppm; and 
 
          WHEREAS, this adjustment will also provide clearer, more protective standards for 
ongoing revitalization efforts and help restore confidence among residents who have lived with 
the uncertainty of legacy pollution for decades; and 
 
          WHEREAS, to that end, this Board had elected to authorize and transmit a letter of 
support for the EPA’s proposed lower residential soil lead cleanup levels.  
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVEDby the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that: 
1. This Board is in support of the EPA’s proposed lower residential soil lead cleanup levels 
and wishes to provide Public Comment on said proposal; and 
2. The Chief Administrative Officer shall transmit this letter to the appropriate contact at 
the Environmental Protection Agency.

Beth Archer 
Community Involvement Coordinator 
U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, Region 8 
 
Re: Support for Proposed Lower Lead Cleanup Levels – Colorado Smelter Superfund Site 
 
Dear Ms. Archer, 
 
The Pueblo County Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) fully supports the EPA’s proposal to 
lower the residential soil lead cleanup level from 350 ppm to 240 ppm and the indoor dust level 
from 275 ppm to 110 ppm. These updated thresholds reflect current national guidance and 
strengthen protection for families living in the impacted neighborhoods. 
 
Lowering these levels is a practical and necessary step to address long-standing contamination 
and reduce community health risks. This adjustment will also provide clearer, more protective 
standards for ongoing revitalization efforts and help restore confidence among residents who have 
lived with the uncertainty of legacy pollution for decades. 
 
We appreciate EPA’s continued collaboration with our community and encourage residents to 
participate in the public comment process and upcoming open houses. The BOCC remains 
committed to supporting efforts that safeguard public health and ensure a thorough and equitable 
cleanup. 
 
Sincerely,
