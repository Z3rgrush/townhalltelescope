AMENDING RESOLUTION 26-252 APPOINTING A MEMBER TO THE PUEBLO 
WEST ROADS SALES TAX OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE  
 
          WHEREAS, on November 8, 2022, the Pueblo West Metropolitan District voters 
approved Ballot Question 6C, which allows for a temporary 1% sales tax to fund roads 
maintenance in Pueblo West, as well as transfer responsibility of Pueblo West roads 
maintenance from the Pueblo West Metropolitan District to Pueblo County Government; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Pueblo West 2022 Ballot Question 6C also created an independent 
Oversight Committee composed of District residents to ensure accountability and annual review 
and report to the public on the use of the revenues collected; and 
 
          WHEREAS, by Resolution 24-175, the County and Pueblo West Metropolitan District 
adopted Bylaws for the Oversight Committee to provide further clarification to the members 
regarding their role and responsibilities; and  
 
          WHEREAS, the Oversight Committee shall consist of five (5) members, two (2) shall be 
appointed by the BOCC, two (2) shall be appointed by the BOD and one (1) shall be a joint 
appointment; and  
 
          WHEREAS, by Resolution 26-252, Cabree Cleveland was appointed to the Pueblo West 
Road Sale Tax Oversight Committee to fulfill a term expiring June 30, 2027 as a County 
member to the Pueblo West Road Sale Tax Oversight Committee; and 
 
          WHEREAS, this vacancy is for a joint appointment to the Pueblo West Road Sale Tax 
Oversight Committee. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that Resolution No. 26-252 is hereby amended and that Cabree 
Cleveland is hereby appointed as a joint member to the Pueblo West Road Sale Tax Oversight 
Committee to fulfill the term expiring on June 30, 2027 and that the staff to the Board is 
authorized and directed to notify the aforementioned of their appointment by providing them 
with a copy of this Resolution. 
 
 
    PASSED AND ADOPTED this September 8, 2026, in Pueblo County, Colorado.

1. Article 1: General Administration 
1.1. Overall Goal. The Behavioral Health Administration is providing State Opioid 
Response grant funding to support services targeting opioid use disorder and/or 
stimulant use disorder and/or polyuse disorder for individuals currently in 
custody. 
1.2. Participation. Any jail that is currently participating in the Jail Based 
Behavioral Services program administered by the Colorado Behavioral Health 
Administration is eligible to receive this grant funding. Inmate or participation 
is voluntary. 
2. Article 2: Definitions and Acronyms 
2.1. MAT: Medication Assisted Treatment 
2.2. SUPRT A/C: SAMHSA's Unified Performance Reporting Tool (SUPRT) - A/C.  
2.3. SOR: State Opioid Response grant 
2.4. JBBS: Jail Based Behavioral Services 
2.5. TEC: The Evaluation Center, a contracted provider to provide SUPRT A/C 
support 
3. Article 3: Activities and Services  
3.1. Cost Reimbursement / Allowable Expenses. As with JBBS, this contract is also 
paid by cost reimbursement. Monthly invoices will be sent to JBBS Program 
Managers at cdhs_jbbs@state.co.us for review and processing. 
3.2. Allowable expenses for this funding include but are not limited to SUD related 
assessment, intake and/or counseling, peer services, FDA approved medications 
for opioid use disorder, naloxone, and release kits (materials preapproved as

3.3. Future Spending. Jails are encouraged to create a long term spending plan for 
their individual MAT programs. BHA encourages jails to seek additional grants 
whenever available.  
4. Article 4: Data Reporting 
4.1. Data Reporting. All SAMHSA’s discretionary grants (including SOR) are required 
to collect and complete SUPRT A/C surveys. This will be required for each 
inmate or patient who receives services with this funding. The contractor will 
be responsible for reporting, when applicable: the patient count, medication(s) 
dispensed, and supplies purchased for clients receiving services by way of these 
funds and this data within the monthly invoice. No invoice shall contain any 
private health information or identifying information for the individual 
receiving services 
4.2. SAMHSA’s Unified Performance Reporting Tool (SUPRT) – Administrative 
(A)/Client: The SUPRT-A baseline assessment must be collected within 30 days 
before or after the client first receives services. Staff should only complete the 
Demographics section of SUPRT-A if the client, proxy, or caregiver/parent 
declined the SUPRT-C baseline assessment. The SUPRT-A reassessment is due 3 
months or 6 months (that is, 90 or 180 days) after the recorded SUPRT-A 
baseline date, depending on program requirements. Each reassessment has a 
60-day window during which grantee staff should ensure the reassessment is 
collected, which opens 30 days before and closes 30 days after the 3- or 6-
month anniversary of the SUPRT-A baseline. The SUPRT-A annual assessment is 
due 12 months (or 365 days) after the recorded SUPRT-A baseline date, and 
every 12 months thereafter for the duration of the client’s enrollment. Each 
client has a 60-day annual assessment window during which grantee staff 
should ensure the annual assessment is collected, which opens 30 days before 
the anniversary of the recorded SUPRT-A baseline date and closes 30 days

7.4. SOR funds for treatment and recovery support services shall only be utilized to 
provide services to individuals that specifically address opioid or stimulant

addressed. Individuals who have no history of or no current issues with opioids 
or stimulants misuse shall not receive treatment or recovery services with SOR 
grant funds.

through the BHA deliverables inbox as outlined in the deliverables, scope of

participate in any SAMHSA-sponsored evaluation of the SOR grant program. The 
submission of these data in the form required by SAMHSA is a requirement of 
funding. Noncompliance with this requirement may result in restricted access 
to funding for this year or limited or no access to funding in the future grant 
year.

XECUTIVE SUMMARY 
Crossroads' Turning Points, Inc. (CTP) respectfully submits this proposal to continue 
serving as the case management services provider for Pueblo County's Pre-Arrest 
Evaluation, Intervention and Linkage (PREVAIL) pre-booking diversion program under 
RFP No. 26-16. With eight consecutive years of demonstrated performance beginning 
with the inception of the Pueblo Law Enforcement Assisted Diversion (LEAD) Project in 
2018, CTP brings unmatched institutional knowledge, established community 
partnerships, and a proven model of person-centered, harm reduction-based care.  
For this project, CTP intends to transition existing LEAD participants to PREVAIL case 
management services, and shall expand the project to include individuals beginning at 
age 15. CTP shall continue to meet with low-level drug users referred by law enforcement 
and community stakeholders and shall conduct evaluations for additional services. 
Examples of services may include comprehensive behavioral health services (substance 
use disorder and mental health treatment), physical health services, transitional housing, 
assistance gaining employment and other relevant services, which the PREVAIL team 
will coordinate and refer participants to.  
For over four decades, CTP has demonstrated its ability to develop partnerships and 
provide linkages to local community-based providers who can help meet participant’s  
basic needs and support their recovery paths, thus reducing criminal justice 
involvement. Since CTP’s initial engagement as a LEAD case management provider, 
CTP has embodied harm reduction practices, always ensuring participants understand 
that the program is voluntary and that abstinence from substance use is not requisite to 
use services or participate in the project. 
CTP is fully prepared to deliver all required services as specified in the RFP No. 26-16 
Scope of Work for the 2026–2027 contract period with the same commitment and 
fidelity that has defined our partnership to date.
