A RESOLUTION CONTINUING THE AUTHORIZATION OF A DECLARATION OF 
LOCAL DISASTER EMERGENCY REGARDING THE ASPEN ACRES AREA FIRE 
AND SUBSEQUENT FLOODING  
 
          WHEREAS,  Pueblo County has sustained severe damage from an ongoing fire that 
started in Aspen Acres area on the border between Pueblo County and Custer County; and  
 
          WHEREAS, on June 29th, 2026, the fire necessitated the evacuation of people and 
livestock from the Aspen Acres Area, Beulah, San Isabel, Rye, and surrounding areas, all 
located wholly or partially within Pueblo County, Colorado; and  
 
          WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709, which provides that a local disaster may 
be declared” only by the principal executive officer of a political subdivision,” the Chair of the 
Pueblo County Board of County Commissioners issued a Disaster/Emergency Declaration on 
June 29th, 2026; and 
 
          WHEREAS, by Resolution 26-202, dated July 2nd, 2026, this board authorized a 
declaration of Local Disaster Emergency; and 
 
          WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709(1), the declaration of a local disaster “shall 
not be continued or renewed for a period in excess of seven days except by or with the consent 
of the governing board of the political subdivision”; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the cost and magnitude of responding to and recovering from the impacts of 
this event continues to be far in excess of Pueblo County’s available resources; and 
 
          WHEREAS, an imminent threat continues to exist for Pueblo County citizens due to fire 
and smoke and flash flooding; and  
 
          WHEREAS, over 101,965 acres have been affected by the fire, and approximately 240 
homes and 9 businesses have been destroyed in Pueblo County requiring the support of over 
1900 fire personnel from across the County at various times, and with the continuing and 
developing risk of flash flooding in the area of the burn scar; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the purpose of the Continued Authorization of Declaration is to proceed 
with the response and recovery aspects of any and all applicable disaster emergency plans and to 
authorize the furnishing of aid and assistance; and   
 
          WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners enacted the continued resolution on 
July 9th, 16th, 23rd, 30th, August 6th, 13th, 20th, 27th, and September 3rd, 10th,  and 17th 
Page 4 of 403
2026, and again desires to enact this Continued Authorization of the Emergency Declaration to 
ensure that financial aid is made available to affected individuals, business and governmental 
entities as applicable and pursuant to state law.  
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that: 
1. The Declaration Continuing Authorization of the Local Disaster Emergency, as provided 
for herein, is hereby approved, and shall remain in full force and effect until further 
action is taken by the Chair or the Board to terminate or modify the Declaration or until 
the declaration expires pursuant to state statute; and 
2. The Chair of the Board of County Commissioners is authorized to execute said 
Declaration on behalf of Pueblo County.

RE: Letter of Support — Controlled Environment Agriculture Project in Pueblo County 
 
To Whom It May Concern: 
 
As the Pueblo County Board of County Commissioners, we are pleased to express our strong support 
for the controlled-environment agriculture (CEA) development proposed by Quantum Energy, LLC 
and its partners in Pueblo County. This project represents exactly the type of forward-looking, 
community-focused investment that aligns with Pueblo County’s economic priorities and long-term 
transition planning. The integration of high-tech greenhouse operations, sustainable energy systems, 
and modern agricultural production mirrors the County’s commitment to job creation, food security, 
and environmentally responsible development. 
 
The proposed project incorporates advanced greenhouse technology designed to operate 
year-round, producing more than 50,000 tons of fresh, nutritious produce annually, with a portion 
dedicated to serving local and statewide food banks. The facility is envisioned to utilize waste heat, 
innovative power systems, and recovered resources to support a sustainable growing environment. 
These features reinforce Pueblo County’s long-standing identity as a hub for agricultural innovation 
and its growing reputation as a center for clean-energy leadership.  
 
The project also presents meaningful economic beneﬁts for Pueblo County. Plans include the 
creation of approximately 450 high-quality, full-time agricultural jobs , along with substantial 
construction-related employment. The capital investment associated with the project is anticipated 
to reach several hundred million dollars, supporting new tax revenues, business activity, and long-
term economic diversiﬁcation for the region. This aligns with Pueblo County’s needs as a Tier One 
“Most Impacted” coal transition community preparing for the retirement of the Comanche 
Generating Station.  
 
Given the project’s strong alignment with Pueblo County’s economic, environmental, and 
agricultural priorities, the Board of County Commissioners encourages all prospective partners, 
agencies, and institutions to give this project full and favorable consideration as it proceeds through 
the necessary development, permitting, and ﬁnancing stages. We look forward to continuing 
collaboration with all stakeholders involved and to the substantial beneﬁts the project will bring to 
Pueblo County.

A RESOLUTION CERTIFYING COMPLIANCE WITH C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122(3)(A)(III) 
REGARDING THE PEACE OFFICER TRAINING AND SUPPORT FUND  
 
          WHEREAS, Colorado voters approved Proposition 130, and the Colorado General 
Assembly enacted Senate Bill 25-310 establishing the Peace Officer Training and Support Fund 
codified at C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122; and 
 
          WHEREAS, C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122(3)(a)(III) requires a local government seeking 
distributions from the Peace Officer Training and Support Fund to adopt a resolution or 
ordinance stating that monies awarded by the Colorado Department of Public Safety will be 
expended in accordance with C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122(3)(c); and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Pueblo County Sheriff's Office is an eligible law enforcement agency 
under C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122; and 
 
          WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of Pueblo County, Colorado, desire to 
satisfy the statutory requirements necessary for eligibility under the Peace Officer Training and 
Support Fund. 
 
          NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of 
Pueblo County, Colorado, that 
 
Section 1. Statutory Certification 
Pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122(3)(a)(III), the Board of County Commissioners hereby 
certifies that Pueblo County shall only budget and expend monies awarded from the Peace 
Officer Training and Support Fund in accordance with C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122(3)(c). 
 
Section 2. Permissible Purposes 
Funds received from the Peace Officer Training and Support Fund shall be accounted for and 
exclusively used for permissible purposes authorized by C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122, for the initial and 
continuing education and training of peace officers and the compensation of peace officers, 
including authorized hiring, rehiring, merit, and retention incentives. 
 
Section 3. Supplement Not Supplant 
Money received from the Peace Officer Training and Support Fund shall be for law enforcement 
training and not replace, reduce, offset or supplant funds to available or otherwise be provided 
from other state or local sources. 
 
Section 4. Budgetary and Financial Administration 
Page 15 of 403
Funds received pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-122 shall be appropriated, budgeted, expended, 
accounted for, and audited in accordance with Colorado law, the Pueblo County budget, and 
applicable County financial policies and procedures. 
 
Section 5.  Reporting and Audit Compliance 
Pueblo County Budget and Finance Director shall obtain from the Sheriff's Office such records 
and provide such reporting, audit documentation, and certifications as may be required by C.R.S. 
§§ 24-33.5-122, 29-1-603, and any rules or guidance issued by the Colorado Department of 
Public Safety. 
 
Section 6. Effective Date 
This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon adoption.

The community needs identified in the Community Needs Assessment survey will be 
addressed through the following programs administered through the PCDHS Housing 
Division associated with the CSBG purview.   
 
• Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP) The WAP provides free energy efficiency 
and health and safety upgrades to existing residential dwellings for qualified Colorado 
families and individuals. PCDHS's WAP covers ten counties within Southeastern 
Colorado.  WAP services emphasize cost-effective measures to reduce energy burden, 
increase comfort, and improve health and safety for program participants. These 
services are beneficial to improving the dwellings of low-income individuals by reducing 
seasonal and baseload utility costs. 
 
• Low-Income Energy Assistance Program (LEAP) & Energy Outreach Colorado 
(EOC) – LEAP provides utility assistance by giving credit to reduce heating fuel costs 
for low-income Colorado individuals, families, and seniors. This benefit is during the 
heating season running from November through April. In addition to assisting with the 
cost of primary heating fuel, LEAP participants are eligible for additional situational-
based assistance through the EOC. The bill assistance program provided is to help 
individuals and families avoid electric disconnect who are past due or have a current 
disconnect notice. Individuals experiencing a heating emergency can receive aid 
through the Crisis Intervention Program. 
 
• Crisis Intervention Program (CIP)– CIP addresses emergency no-heat situations for 
service for LEAP participants. These services aim to maintain safe living conditions 
during the heating season for individuals and families without heat. Services may 
include repair(s) or replacement of heating equipment to relieve heating emergencies.  
 
• Emergency Repair and Essential Services Program ERESP- ERESP provides older 
adults, 60 and over, with roofing repair(s), electrical problem(s), plumbing, heating, and 
accessibility modifications who may be experiencing an emergency or essential service 
necessary to remain in their home safely. The program aims to assist individuals to 
maintain independent living in a safe dwelling.  
 
• Everyday Eats (EE) and Temporary Emergency Food Assistance (TEFAP) – EE 
provides monthly food commodities to qualified older adults 60 years and over. TEFAP 
provides monthly food commodities to qualified individuals and families over the age of

3. Strategic Plan Goals 
What long-term family, agency and/or community goals are addressed by the strategic

The long-term family, agency, and community goals include: 
• Preventing the prevalence of homelessness by helping community members maintain

critical repairs. 
• Ensuring all community members have access to nutritious foods. 
 
4. Customer Satisfaction and Input 
How is customer satisfaction information and customer input included in the strategic 
planning process? 
We gathered customer satisfaction information and input through our Community Needs 
Assessment survey, distributed broadly throughout Pueblo County.  We utilized this 
information to identify programming that would best help us alleviate the causes and 
conditions of poverty in our community. Additionally, we conducted key informant 
interviews with our Tripartite Board members while analyzing the Community Needs 
Assessment survey results, which helped us develop a strategy to guide our work over 
the next five years.
