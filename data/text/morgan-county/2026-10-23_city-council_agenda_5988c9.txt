Dear Morgan County Commissioners: 
 
 We respectfully submit this letter to you on behalf of our clients,  Fortress Solar I LLC, 
Fortress Solar II LLC, and Fortress Solar III LLC (collectively, “ Fortress”) in advance of the 
October 23rd1 hearing.  Fortress appreciates and thanks Morgan County (the “ County”) for this 
opportunity to address the concerns set forth in  the September 23rd Notice of Intent to Take 
Enforcement Action and Order to Show Cause (the “NOI Letter”).  As described in further detail 
below, Fortress takes these concerns very seriously and has taken immediate and diligent action to 
address them. Fortress rema ins committed to being a good pa rtner to the County and has 
established a plan to ensure clear, timely, and open communication going forward. 
 
 By way of background, Fortress was grante d multiple special use permits (the “Permits”) 
in 2024 for a three-phase so lar and battery project.  In connection with the Permits, Fortress and 
the County entered into a Public Improvements Agreement dated July 21st (the “PIA”) regarding 
road improvement work (the “Road Improvement Work”) that Fortress would make to County 
Roads Q and R (the “ Roads”). In connection with these obligations, Fortress retained Kimley-
Horn (“KH”) as project engineer an d Elevated Paving to perform  the paving work. The County 
brought a number of concerns related to Elevated Paving’s performance to Fortress’s attention on 
or around September 21st, which were then form alized in the NOI Lette r. Fortress directed 
Elevated Paving to stop all wo rk on September 22nd, and has now  terminated Elevated Paving. 
All future engineering and paving work will be led by M.A. Mortenson Company (“Mortenson”), 
an experienced construction company that is awar e of the prior issues and is committed to both 
clear communication and a high sta ndard of paving work. Mortenson is to engage only qualified 
subcontractors with preference to companies that are familiar with Morgan County. 
 
 The NOI Letter alleges violations of certain spec ific provisions in the Permits and the PIA.  
Those provisions fall within one of the following broader categories: (1) notice and communication 
obligations, including review, inspection, and monitoring rights; (2) CDOT permit authorizations;

NOTICE AND COMMUNICATIONS 
 
As set forth in detail in the NOI Letter, the C ounty alleges that Sections 2(c) and 2(d) of

Elevated Paving provided on the prior Friday di d not provide the County with the reasonable

commence paving prior to the County’s represen tatives arriving to observe the Roads on 
September 21st. To remedy this, the Mortenson Sc ope of Work, defined below, includes a clear

(3) business days’ notice in all instances to allow for on-site engagement by the County and the 
County’s engineering consultant HDR.

written confirmation, which may be provided by email, before proceeding from one stage of work 
to the next (for example, from proof rolling to paving).  The prior mix-design documentation, as

NEIGHBORING PROPERTIES 
 
Next, the NOI Letter alleges violations of the Permits’ conditions related to obtaining 
permission from landowners to use private roads. Fortress believes that this allegation may arise 
from confusion over whether a portion of County Road Q was previously vacated and is now 
privately owned. The County approved a driveway permit on this former segment of County Road 
Q and the PIA requires that paving extend up to that driveway. To the extent that it is privately 
owned, it exists pa rtly on land owned by a Fo rtress affiliate, Fortress Land I LLC, and partly on 
land owned by Matthew and Stacy Pollart (“Pollart”). Fortress first became aware of this potential 
issue on September 21st and immediately contacted Pollart to  negotiate an easement. Fortress 
provided Pollart with an initia l draft easement on October 7 th, after productive discussions, and 
anticipates that a final version will be executed before the end of October. Fortress also identified 
Ginther Holdings, LLC (“Ginther”) as a potential owner of the vacated County Road Q and sent 
a draft easement document on October 17 th for review after discussing the situation with both 
Pollart and Ginther. Further, since Fortress Land I LLC now owns the northern portion of vacated 
County Road Q, Fortress plans to grant reciprocal  easement rights for the benefit of Pollart and 
Ginther at no cost.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY  
County Road Q and County Road R Public Improvements  — Fortress Solar I Project, Morgan County, Colorad o  
This scope of work is for the design and construction of improvements to County Road Q and County Road R in Morgan 
County, Colorado, in connection with the Fortress S olar Project (“Owner”). The design, construction, t esting, County 
coordination and acceptance requirements for the Wo rk are summarized below and set out in detail in th e Articles that 
follow.  
Owner has engaged M. A. Mortenson Company (Mortenso n) as Contractor for the improvements. Mortenson is  one of 
the leading builders of renewable energy projects i n the United States, with an established presence i n Colorado that 
includes the Rocky Mountain Solar + Storage project in Avondale, Arroyo Solar in Pueblo County, and the Pawnee Solar 
project currently underway in Morgan County. That e xperience gives Mortenson direct familiarity with t he County’s 
processes, local conditions, and expectations. Mortenson will take the lead on compliance with the Road Use Agreement 
(RUA) and the Public Improvement Agreement (PIA), w orking alongside Owner throughout.  
The improvements are a condition of the Special Use Permits for the Fortress Solar Project and are to be constructed and 
conveyed by bill of sale to the County under the PI A, executed July 21, 2026. Together they cover appr oximately 1.7 
miles of County roadway . County Road Q, approximately 1.1 miles in length, is the Project’s primary construction access. 
The existing asphalt will be milled and reutilized as subgrade with a new paved section at least four inches thick, with the 
roadway widened to 24’, but at minimum keep existin g width. County Road R, approximately 0.6 miles in length, is the 
secondary commuter access and will be graded, surfa ced with compacted gravel base, and treated for dus t control. Work 
on both roads includes drainage, ditch and culvert improvements, signage, and re-seeding of disturbed areas within County 
right-of-way. 
The Work will proceed in a defined sequence. Before  design begins, Mortenson will document the existin g condition of 
both roads, sample the subgrade and locate utilitie s. A hydrology study will be completed to ensure de sign reflects the 
current site drainage conditions. The design will b e stamped by a professional engineer registered in Colorado and 
submitted to the County for review and approval. A detailed schedule will show each phase of the Work from design and 
site investigation through pre-construction submittals, construction and inspection hold points, to completion, acceptance 
and close-out, including the time allowed for Count y review.  
Quality will be confirmed through a written testing  and quality assurance plan based on Colorado Depar tment of 
Transportation (CDOT) and County standards. Pavement mix designs and aggregate gradations must be approved by the 
County before Mortenson paving equipment is brought  to site. Third-party testing of compaction, materi al quality, and 
pavement thickness will be performed throughout con struction, and all final test reports will be share d with the County. 
Owner and Mortenson will involve the County at every stage of County Road Q and R improvements to ensure the County 
reviews and approves the design and submittals, is invited to witness testing, and will have the opportunity to inspect the 
Work at three hold points before work proceeds - ma terial verification, roadbed preparation, and paving.  
The obligations arising under the RUA and the PIA are consolidated in a single register at Article 7, which identifies what 
is required, when it is due, and the responsible pa rty. During construction, Mortenson will comply and  cause its 
subcontractors to comply with the terms of the RUA. On completion, Mortenson will coordinate the County’s inspections 
for preliminary acceptance, restore any County road  used by the Project to its post-PIA final acceptan ce condition, and 
warrant the improvements for two years, inspecting and maintaining them until final acceptance and con veyance to the 
County.  
Owner and Mortenson are committed to an open and co llaborative process with the County so that the com pleted roads 
meet County standards and serve the community well beyond construction.
