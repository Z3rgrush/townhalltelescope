Data centers are physical facilities that house IT infrastructure and are considered the backbone of 
the internet and digital technology. The facilities include networked computers, servers, 
networking equipment and storage systems. Support facilities include cooling systems, dedicated 
power supply with possible backup generators and fire suppression systems. 
 
Data center types range from small to large scale. Aside from building and lot size, the types of 
data centers are similar from the land use perspective.  
 
The Planning Commission held a work session on March 9, 2026 to discuss new supplementary 
regulations for Data Centers to be added to Chapter 4 of the Zoning Regulations. The Planning 
Commission then held a public hearing on April 21, 2026 and recommended approval with some 
changes. 
 
The Board of County Commissioners held a public hearing on May 5, 2026 and continued the 
hearing to June 16th, June 23rd, July 21st and August 4th to give staff time for requested revisions. 
 
This summary outlines the draft regulations along with the changes recommended by the Planning 
Commission. Included in the packet are the draft regulations with the changes and the draft 
regulations the Planning Commission reviewed. The redlines are suggested revisions based on the 
May 5th and June 23rd meetings. Also included in this packet is an email from Tyler Purvis with 
the City of Brush and suggested language and information from Richard Miller, a consultant with 
the Sun & Wind Group, LLC.

4-925 Definitions 
Closed Loop Cooling System: A cooling system that recirculates a coolant (liquid or air)  within 
a sealed circuit to remove heat from information technology equipment.  
 
Data Center Facility (DCF): A use consisting of buildings or structures specifically designed or 
modified to house networked computers and data and transaction processing equipment and related 
infrastructure support equipment, including power and cooling equipment. A DCF may be used to 
provide data and transaction processing services, outsource information technology services 
and/or computer equipment colocation services. A DCF may also include buildings or structures 
that support the operation of the DCF including retrofit building s, warehousing for logistics, 
independent network buildings, buildings for support staff, and emergency generators.  A DCF 
shall not include an EDCF or an Edge DCF.  
 
Edge Data Center Facility (Edge DCF ): A data center which is the primary use on a propert y 
and is no larger than 2,500 square feet or smaller in size. A use consisting of buildings or structures 
specifically designed or modified to house networked computers and data and transaction 
processing equipment and related infrastructure support equipment, including power and cooling 
equipment. An Edge DCF may be used to provide data and transaction processing services, 
outsource information technology services and/or computer equipment colocation services. A n 
Edge DCF may also include buildings or structures that support the operation of the Edge DCF 
including retrofit buildings, warehousing for logistics, independent network buildings, buildings 
for support staff, and emergency generators.   
  
Enterprise Data Center Facility (EDCF):  A data center used by a single organization or business 
to contain that organization or business’s data processing and storage needs.  
 
Large Data Center Facility (LDCF): A data center which is the primary use on a property and is 
larger than 2,500 square feet in size. A use consisting of buildings or structures specifically 
designed or modified to house networked computers and data and transaction processing 
equipment and related infrastructure support equipment, including power and cooling equipment. 
A LDCF may be used to provide data and transaction processing services, outsource information 
technology services and/or computer equipment colocation services. A LDCF may also include 
buildings or structures that support the operation of the LDCF including retrofit buildings, 
warehousing for logistics, independent network buildings, buildings for support staff, and 
emergency generators.   
 
Project Area: The total boundary encompassing all project related activities , structures and 
improvements including parking, laydown yards, private access roads, and construction zones.

of the LDCF or Edge DCF shall notify the County Planning Department and the Board in writing

a void use permit will be subject to the same review and approval processes for new applications.  
4-955 Approval Time Frame and Abandonment 
The use permit for a LDCF or Edge DCF shall be valid for a period of three (3) years from the 
date of approval. The operator may seek an extension of the approval period from the appropriate 
decision-making authority by demonstrating a need for an extension and filing an application with 
the County Planning Department. Each renewal application shall be accompanied by the 
appropriate fee, as established by the Board. Requested renewals may be in one (1) year, two (2) 
year, or three (3) year terms. However, no extensions shall extend the approval period beyond six 
(6) years from the date of the original approval of the use permit. If the operator fails to achieve 
substantial construction and installation of the LDCF or Edge DCF  within the approved period, 
including any extensions, the approval shall expire and a new application shall be required. The 
applicant shall be responsible for applying for and obtaining any necessary extensions for any 
building permit issued for the LDCF or Edge DCF.

4-925 Definitions 
Closed Loop Cooling System: A cooling system that recirculates a coolant (liquid or air) within a sealed 
circuit to remove heat from information technology equipment.  
 
Data Center Facility (DCF): A use consisting of buildings or structures specifically designed or modified

support equipment, including power and cooling equipment. A DCF may be use d to provide data and

colocation services. A DCF may also include buildings or structures that support the operation of the DCF

staff, and emergency generators.

access roads, and construction zones.

4-945 Decommissioning Requirements 
(A) General Requirements.  
(1) The operator shall provide a final decommissioning plan in accordance with this Section 
as a part of the submission of any construction permit application related to the approved 
land use permit or earlier if required by the County.  
(2) If a DCF  ceases to  perform its originally intended function for more than twelve (12) 
consecutive months, the operator and/or property owner shall initiate the decommissioning 
of the DCF within sixty (60) calendar days after the end of the twelve (12) month period 
during which the DCF ceased to perform and remove the system, foundation and associated 
equipment and facilities by no later than twelve (12) months after the end of the initial 
twelve (12) month period.  
(3) If the operator notifies the County of the termination of operations, decommissioning shall 
be initiated within sixty (60) calendar days of the written notification and completed within 
twelve (12) months from the date of the notice. 
(4) Upon completion of decommissioning activities to remove the system, founda tion and 
associated equipment and facilities, the property shall be restored to the condition prior to 
development of the system or as expressly agreed to in writing by the County Planning 
Department and property owner.  
(5) The operator shall update the decommissioning plan every three (3) years and provide a 
copy of the updated plan to the County Planning Department. If there are no updates to the 
plan, the operator shall notify in writing the County instead of submitting an updated plan. 
However, the ope rator must submit an updated plan every six (6) years. The amount of 
surety, described below in subsection C, for decommissioning shall be reevaluated and if 
necessary, due to increased decommissioning costs, a new surety or additional surety in the 
required amount must be submitted with any updated decommissioning plan.  
(B) Decommissioning Plan. The decommissioning plan shall include:

4-950 Ownership Changes 
If the ownership of a DCF  changes or the owner of the property changes, the use permit shall remain in 
effect, provided that the successor owner or operator assumes in writing all of the obligations of the use 
permit and decommissioning plan. A new operator of the DCF shall notify the County Planning Department 
and the Board in writing of such change in ownership or operator within thirty (30) days of the ownership 
change. The use permit and all other local approvals for the DCF  will be voided if a new operator fails to 
provide written notification as provided herein in the required timeframe. Reinstatement of a void use 
permit will be subject to the same review and approval processes for new applications.  
4-955 Approval Time Frame and Abandonment 
The use permit for a DCF  shall be valid for a period of three (3) years from the date of approval. The 
operator may seek an extension of the approval period from the appropriate decision- making authority by 
demonstrating a need for an extension and filing an application with the County Planning Department. Each 
renewal application shall be accompanied by the appropriate fee, as established by the Board. Requested 
renewals may be in one (1) year, two (2) year, or three (3) year terms. However, no extensions shall extend 
the approval period beyond six (6) years from the date of the original approval of the use permit. If the 
operator fails to achieve substantial construction and installation of the DCF  within the approved period, 
including any extensions, the approval shall expire and a new application shall be required. The applicant 
shall be responsible for applying for and obtaining any necessary extensions for any building permit issued 
for the DCF.

DATA CENTERS 
 
The County needs to create new supplementary regulations for Data Centers in Chapter 4 of the Zoning 
Regulations.  
 
Data centers are physical facilities that houses IT infrastructure and are considered the backbone of the 
internet and digital technology. The facilities include networked computers, servers, networking 
equipment and storage systems. Support facilities include cooling systems, dedicated power supply with 
possible backup generators and fire suppression systems. 
 
Data center types range from small to large scale. Aside from building and lot size, the types of data 
centers are similar from the land use perspective.  
 
Different Definitions: 
 
Logan County: 
 
Data Center Facility (DCF): A use consisting of buildings or structures specifically designed or 
modified to house networked computers and data and transaction processing equipment and related 
infrastructure support equipment, including power and cooling equipment. A DCF may be used to 
provide data and transaction processing services, outsource information technology services and/or 
computer equipment colocation services. A DCF may also include buildings or structures that 
support the operation of the DCF including retrofit buildings, warehousing for logistics, 
independent network buildings, buildings for support staff, and emergency generators.  
 
Weld County proposed: 
 
Data Center: A building or buildings used to house information technology or telecommunications 
equipment with which digital information is processed, transferred, and/or stored. A Data center 
may include associated ancillary structures, including, but not limited to, offices, security buildings, 
cooling water tanks, and backup power systems with a total capacity of less  than fifty (50) 
megawatts 
 
Linn County, Iowa: Also include small and large data centers – Page 47 
 
Data Center: A facility consisting of one or more buildings (whether constructed simultaneously or 
in separate phases) used primarily for storage, management, processing, and transmission of digital 
data, which houses computer or network equipment, systems, servers, appliances, and other 
associated components related to digital data storage and operations. Data center includes 
commercial cryptocurrency mining operations and processing.
