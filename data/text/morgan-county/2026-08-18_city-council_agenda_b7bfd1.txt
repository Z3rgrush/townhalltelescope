   Fiscal Year 2025–2026 marked the first year that MCPCF operated independently following its transition to a 501(c)
(3) organization, without the support of a fiscal sponsor.
   During the fiscal year, MCPCF increased its approved indirect cost rate from 10% to 15%. However, this
adjustment was not reflected in the State invoicing and reimbursement system until the August invoice cycle. As a
result, the organization was unable to access the full indirect cost reimbursement for July, contributing to a year-end
budget surplus.
   Additionally, State funding was not fully expended due to approximately six weeks of Family and Medical Leave
Insurance (FAMLI) usage. The employee's leave resulted in unspent salary and wage funds that could not be fully
reallocated to other allowable budget categories before the close of the fiscal year, creating a surplus in the wage line
item.
    EQIT funding was returned when no Portfolio options for college credit were completed, and the planned number
of facilitators to attend the state meeting was not met.
   E&E Base funding was placed in reserve to help offset costs in the first half of FY 2026-27.  This decision was
made as a result of a forecasted State budget reduction MCPCF will be facing.

My Experience as a Spanish-Speaking Child Care Provider with Morgan Partnership for Children and Families 
As a Spanish-speaking child care provider, beginning the process of becoming a licensed provider in a country where I did not
speak the language was a significant challenge. At first, I felt uncertain and worried because I was not fluent in English. However,
from the very beginning, I found tremendous support from Morgan Partnership for Children and Families. 
Even though most of their staff do not speak Spanish, that was never a barrier to helping me. They always found ways to
communicate with me, answer my questions, and guide me step by step throughout the entire licensing process. Their patience,
dedication, and willingness to help made what seemed like a difficult process much more manageable. 
I would especially like to recognize Sandy and Carri, who was always kind, respectful, and patient with me. She consistently made
me feel heard and supported, showing a genuine willingness to help and find solutions despite the language barrier.
  In addition to providing guidance, they donated materials and resources to my child care program, Tiny Tesoros, helping me
create a safer, more welcoming, and enriching environment for the children in my care. Every donation has had a positive impact
on both my program and the families I serve. 
Another meaningful way they supported me was by guiding me through the process of applying for an emergency grant. Thanks to
their assistance, I was able to complete the application and obtain funding to improve my playground. 
My experience shows that a true commitment to serving the community goes beyond language. Morgan Partnership for Children
and Families has demonstrated that with dedication, respect, and inclusion, it is possible to empower providers from different
cultures and backgrounds to achieve their goals and strengthen the quality of early childhood care in our community. 
I am deeply grateful for all the support I have received since the beginning of my journey as a child care provider.
