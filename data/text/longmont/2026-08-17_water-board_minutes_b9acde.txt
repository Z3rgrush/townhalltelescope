8. GENERAL BUSINESS 
A. Water Rights Exchange Agreement with St. Vrain & Left Hand Water Conservancy District 
Vice Chair Holwick recused himself from this item and left the room for the discussion. 
Staff Member Wes Lowrie explained a proposed short-term, one-year water exchange 
agreement with the Saint Vrain and Left Hand Water Conservancy District (SVLHWCD or 
District). The agreement would allow SVLHWCD to exchange 148.8 ac.-ft. of water from their 
Colorado-Big Thompson (C-BT) shares for the same amount of fully consumable water from 
Longmont's other sources that can be used for augmentation. Lowrie said staff supported the 
exchange to help out-of-priority diverters remain in compliance with their obligations. 
Scott Griebling of SVLHWCD shared a presentation with the Board detailing the history of their 
water augmentation program. In the early 1990s, the District created an umbrella 
augmentation plan to help cover small depletions across the basin. He noted that Copeland 
Lake and Lake 4 were their primary water sources. After the 2013 Flood, the City of Longmont 
helped SVLHWCD make depletion replacements in exchange for District's C-BT water during 
reconstruction of Lake 4, which was heavily damaged during the flood. The District's 
Augmentation Program served 214 members in the St. Vrain Creek and Left Hand Creek Basins, 
and SVLHWCD was working to firm up their water supplies. Unfortunately, with the limited 
water in 2026, SVLHWCD's supply likely would be exhausted in 2028, if drought conditions

continued. If the exchange agreement was approved, the District planned to provide the City of 
Longmont with monthly scheduled releases of their C-BT water in exchange for the water 
Longmont would provide the District. This exchange would not require any changes to 
Longmont's water rights but would afford them the opportunity to bring in more C-BT directly 
to the water treatment plant and therefore allow the City to keep more of its storage in Ralph 
Price Reservoir later into the year. Discussion with the Board followed related to the terms of 
the agreement, whether the District continued accepting applications for new members, and 
prior history precedent for such an agreement. Staff Member Lowrie explained how the City 
would handle the exchange along with other exchanges already included in water delivery for 
the year. 
Board Member Tom Duster moved, seconded by Board Member Davis, to recommend that 
City Council approve the short-term water exchange agreement with St. Vrain & Left Hand 
Water Conservancy District. The motion carried 4-0. 
Griebling left the meeting at 3:27 pm. Also, Vice Chair Halwick returned and participated fully 
in the remainder of the meeting. The Board asked a few more questions about other water 
exchange requests the City received each year. Lowrie said multiple requests came in yearly 
and staff considered each one uniquely and carefully, ensuring that the City of Longmont 
continued to provide sufficient supply to the community first. 
9. ITEMS FROM STAFF 
A. 2026 Water Fair Impact Report 
Staff Member Hope Bartlett reported on the success of the 2026 Water Fair. The theme for the 
event was "Where Our Water Goes" and included nine stations of interactive learning stations 
related to the interdisciplinary, multicultural goals of the event's organizing partners, of which 
the City of Longmont was one. Stations intentionally offered attendees the opportunity to 
travel through a water cycle related to the theme for the year; this year's event began with the 
water in homes and followed it through the treatment plant, streams and rivers, to the ocean. 
The Keep It Clean Partnership displayed their art contest submissions by area students from 
elementary to high school age, and the event also included basic information about Colorado's 
water law and appropriations. 
Bartlett noted that participation increased by approximately 20% in 2026 over 2025, with 425 
participants. This was the third year for the event and the Watershed Center (also one of the 
organizers) recently applied for a large grant to continue with it in future years, with hopes to 
potentially add an additional event in the winter.
