9. ITEMS FROM STAFF 
A. Ralph Price Reservoir Water Right Management 
Staff Member Wes Lowrie shared about the City of Longmont's water rights associated with 
Ralph Price Reservoir (also known as Button Rock) and how the Water Resources team 
manages those rights. He reviewed the history of this reservoir that is fully owned by 
Longmont, noting it was constructed between 1967 to 1969 and holds 16,197 acre-feet of 
storage. Lowrie explained that Longmont has four different types of storage in Button Rock, 
including direct flow decrees, high mountain dam decrees, storage decrees, and decrees 
specific to Ralph Price Reservoir. Dates from original decrees and change case decrees range 
from 1862 to 1964, which allows flexibility in how the reservoir is used for storage and water 
movement in both irrigation and non-irrigation seasons. Water in Ralph Price Reservoir is 
typically used for potable water treatment in winter months, storage during runoff season, and 
water replacement with senior change decrees during irrigation season. When managing the 
water flows into and out of Button Rock, staff also considered recreational usage and changes 
in conditions that might help with mitigating high inflows or short runoff seasons. 
Board members were interested to learn about Button Rock's design resiliency as discovered 
during the 2013 Flood in how it withstood the conditions and the likelihood of potential 
expansion of the reservoir in the future. 
B. Beckwith Diversion Structure Plan 
Lowrie reviewed the water rights history of the Beckwith Ditch, located near the Golden Ponds 
area in Longmont. He explained that the City of Longmont owned all but two shares, and their 
very senior rights meant that the ditch was in priority most of the time. He said about half the 
shares were changed through water court in the 1990s, and the other half were in process for 
changing their decree from irrigation only use to municipal use. Lowrie noted that staff was 
working with the State to find a way to allow Longmont to measure the flow at the Beckwith 
diversion for instream credit. At the same time, they were working with other entities to 
address issues related to physical constraints in the creek corridor, impacts from the 2013

Flood, allowing fish passage, and mitigating other ecological impacts. These were all concerns 
from change case objectors, so working with them to address the issues was important to 
Longmont's ability to get approval in the change cases. He said the plan included multiple 
phased improvements between 2026 and 2031 that would address these concerns and would 
give Longmont the ability to use more of its Beckwith water rights. Ultimately, Longmont 
wanted options to use these water rights for municipal use and storage early in the season, as 
well as for bypass credits during the later irrigation season. Lowrie said staff hoped to get the 
change case decree through water court in the next year, which would make this possible. 
C. Cash-in-Lieu of Water Rights Received 
Lowrie informed the Board that City Council approved their recommendation to set the fee for 
cash-in-lieu of water rights received to the increased cost of $63,000 per acre-foot, effective 
January 1, 2027. He said he had not received complaints about the increase from developers 
but only their expressed appreciation for the recent change to make the new fee effective 
January 1 instead of essentially when City Council acted. Lowrie noted that Council also 
seemed to appreciate and agree with the Board's revised approach to include the selected 
indices in evaluation when considering a recommendation to adjust the fee for cash-in-lieu.

A. Review of Major Projects Listing and Items Tentatively Scheduled for Future Board

A review of major project listing was provided in the agenda packet. 
Chair Lange and Board Member Renee Davis asked what would be included in the water rates 
conversation at the October meeting. Lowrie explained that the topic for the October meeting 
was specific to 2027 rates that were previously approved by City Council. The members asked 
about future water rate studies and said they would be particularly interested in understanding 
any capital improvement plans that might be included to inform proposed rates. 
Vice Chair Halwick asked for an update at a future meeting on how the City of Longmont did 
with water conservation through the summer months given the move to a higher drought 
response level with mandatory restrictions for the City and only voluntary ones for residents. 
Staff Member Hope Bartlett shared information about the City of Longmont's autumn 
campaign encouraging residents to make a water conservation plan. This plan included 
commitments to turn off their irrigation system by October 1, as well as a list of other water 
saving measures. She said that residents who chose to fill out and turn in a "My Water Plan" 
pledge form could be eligible to receive a $SO utility bill credit, and that the form was available 
on the City's website. The rebate was available to the first hundred customers who submitted 
the form. Promotional yard signs were also available to residents to encourage their neighbors 
to conserve water as well.
