SUMMARY  
The consultant team will begin the work session with a short introduction to Safe Streets Leawood, 
the Safe Streets for All (SS4A) Program, and the project goals. The presentation will explain how the 
project is focused on Leawood through data collection, policy reviews, community input, and 
identifying safety projects.  
  
The team will review crash data, including the High Injury Network (HIN) and High Risk Network 
(HRN), along with feedback gathered through online and in-person community engagement. The 
team a lead then recommendations and project safety introduce then will countermeasures 
discussion with the group. The work session will end with a review of the next steps and the project 
timeline for the next few months.

HIGH INJURY / HIGH RISK 
NETWORKS
• Identifying the street segments and intersections 
with the greatest quantitative safety concerns 
• High Injury Network = based on crash history
• High Risk Network = based on future crash risk
• Locations on both networks = highest priority!
Network Calculated Based On: How It’s Used
HIN Past crashes (especially fatal / serious 
injury)
Target known problem areas – 
capital improvements, short-term 
interventions
HRN Statistical risk for future crashes based on 
attributes correlated with crashes (e.g., 
traffic volume, speeds, pedestrian activity)
Prevent new problem areas 
(proactive investment)

Key Countermeasures Definitions
Intersection Safety Improvements 
Right turn on red prohibition Drivers must stop and wait for a green light to turn right, rather than proceeding after a full stop as is often allowed
4-Way Stop Control STOP signs at each intersection approach
Dedicated Left-Turn Lanes / Signal Separate space for vehicles waiting to turn left and protected left turn signal
Raised Intersections Sidewalk-level intersections that defer to pedestrian traffic
Traffic Calming Measures
Advanced Warning Signs Signs that warn drivers of imminent conflicts or stops
Curb Extensions Narrowing of street width at intersections or the midblock
Speed Feedback Signs Permanent or portable sign to capture and display vehicle speed
On-Street Parking Angled, reverse-angled, or parallel space for vehicles
Vertical Deflectors Speed tables, cushions, and raised crosswalks
Chicanes Series of alternating curves or lane shifts that are located in a position to force a motorist 
to steer back and forth out of a straight travel path

Key Countermeasures Definitions
Pedestrian and 
School Zone 
Improvements
Lighting Streetlamps or masts that illuminate the intersection area
Leading Pedestrian Interval WALK signal that activates before vehicles are permitted to turn
Raised Crosswalk Crosswalk that is the level of the sidewalk to slow down vehicles
RRFBs Rapid Rectangular Flashing Beacon
PHB/HAWK Signals Pedestrian Hybrid Beacon or High-Intensity Activated Crosswalk
Median Refuge Islands Curbed or painted safe zones for crossing pedestrians
High Visibility Crosswalks Continental bar markings of high reflectivity thermoplastic
Access and 
Corridor 
Management
Driveway Access Control Closing or restricting vehicle movement at access driveways
Raised Median Curbed median that restricts vehicle turning movement
Road Diet / Lane Reduction Reallocating street width (e.g., converting a 4-lane undivided roadway to 2 travel lanes + 1 center turn lane) to reduce speeding, crash severity, and conflict points.
Bicycle Safety 
Improvements
Sidewalk Off-street facility for pedestrian use
Shared Path Off-street facility for bicycle and pedestrian use, at least 10’ wide
Sharrow In-lane marking indicating shared vehicle and bicycle use
Painted Bike Lane Bike lane adjacent to or laterally buffered from vehicles, marked with paint
Bike Detection Induction loops or cameras that identify bicycles at signals
Protected Bike Lane Bike lane separated from vehicles by a physical barrier
