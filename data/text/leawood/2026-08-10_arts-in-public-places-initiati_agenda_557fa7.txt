APPI Meeting
Monday, March 23, 2026
4:00 p.m. – Main Conference Room in City Hall and via Zoom
Present: Kim Hinkle (Chair), Anne Blessing, Amy Girard, Burton Dunbar, Julia Steinberg, 
Michael Shirley, Stann Tate (LAC Member), Julie Cain (Councilmember), Melissa Duggan 
(Staff Liaison), Chris Claxton (Staff)
CALL TO ORDER
Kim Hinkle called the meeting to order at 4:01 p.m. A quorum was present. 
Ms. Hinkle introduced Stann Tate. 
APPROVAL OF MINUTES
Anne Blessing made a motion to approve the APPI minutes of June 20, 2024. Julia Steinberg 
seconded the motion. The minutes were approved unanimously, by a vote of 6-0. 
Ms. Girard made a motion to approve the APPI minutes of December 2, 2025. Ms. Steinberg 
seconded the motion. The minutes were approved unanimously, by a vote of 6-0. 
OLD BUSINESS
Melissa Duggan reported that the Public Art Fund is at a total of $513,429.68. A total of 
$222,998 is dedicated to the maintenance of the city’s art collection. A total of $290,431 is 
dedicated to procuring new acquisitions into the city’s art collection. The Public Art Impact Fee 
Fund is currently around $10,000 with projected growth from upcoming projects, including the 
Hallbrook North development.  
Ms. Duggan reported that damage occurred to the lighting components at Growing along 
Tomahawk Creek Parkway. It is appropriate to consider using some of the maintenance funds to 
cover the costs of the repair. 
PLAID Sculpture Site 17/18 Addition
Chris Claxton shared that the original concept and thought process behind this proposed addition 
reflects similar site concepts used in memorials around Washington, D.C. This general concept 
would activate the trail from the temporary Art on Loan site by City Hall to Dancers (Tango). 
Ms. Hinkle clarified that if the 17/18 site addition gets approved into the Public Art Master Plan, 
that further details will need to be clarified on PLAID’s concept of an art trail. This site 
consideration would be an opportunity to collaborate with Parks and Recreation Advisory Board. 
It was recommended that the Sculpture site 17/18 addition be tabled until further research and 
consideration can be made. 
NEW BUSINESS
Naming Tomahawk Creek Parkway Public Art Collection
Agenda Item #2.A.
Page 3 of 4
Ms. Hinkle reported that there is a need to propose a name for the public art that is displayed 
along Tomahawk Creek Parkway corridor. Stann Tate is familiar with strategic marketing and 
has 
been invited to give his professional opinion on the matter. 
Stann Tate shared that focusing on Leawood Arts’ branding would be effective in creating a 
name that reflects the themes of exploration, imagination and celebration. 
The group brainstormed various ideas and discussed several before narrowing down the options. 
The group reached consensus that Art Line Leawood reflected the fluid nature of the parkway, 
the inclusion of the road, trails and sidewalks as places to view the art and the inclusive acronym 
ALL. 
Ms. Blessing suggested a call for artists to interpret the theme for permanent signage as an 
entryway to Tomahawk Creek Parkway in the future. Temporary signage could be created and 
reviewed to weigh in on branding input that aligns with the city. 
Ms. Blessing made a motion to approve the new name proposal, Art Line Leawood, to be 
designated for the public art collection along Tomahawk Creek Parkway and the corresponding 
trail system. Julia Steinberg seconded the motion. The name recommendation was approved 
unanimously, by a vote of 6-0. 
ADJOURN
Ms. Blessing made a motion to adjourn. Ms. Girard seconded the motion. The meeting was 
adjourned at 5:32 p.m.
