Proclamation
WHEREAS, the Monarch butterfly (Danaus plexippus) is one of the most recognizable 
and beloved pollinator species in North America, known for its extraordinary multi-generational 
migration spanning thousands of miles between Canada, the United States, and Mexico; and 
 
WHEREAS, Monarch butterfly populations have declined by an estimated 80 to 90 
percent over the past two decades due to habitat loss, pesticide use, climate change, and the 
disappearance of milkweed, the sole host plant for Monarch caterpillars; and 
 
WHEREAS, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has recognized the Monarch butterfly as 
a species warranting conservation attention, and local communities play a vital role in providing 
safe habitat corridors for its survival; and 
 
WHEREAS, human health ultimately depends on well-functioning ecosystems and that 
biodiverse regions can better support food production, healthy soil and air quality and can foster 
healthy connections between humans and wildlife; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City of Leawood recognizes that municipal and community action — 
including planting native milkweed and nectar plants, reducing pesticide use in public spaces, 
and educating residents — can meaningfully contribute to the recovery of this iconic species; and 
 
WHEREAS, citizens, schools, garden clubs, and conservation organizations throughout 
Leawood have shown strong interest in supporting pollinator-friendly practices and habitat 
restoration.
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Marc E. Elkins, Mayor of the City of Leawood, Kansas, do 
hereby proclaim the City's commitment to the protection and conservation of the Monarch 
butterfly, and I call upon all residents, businesses, schools, and community organizations to: 
- Plant native milkweed and nectar-rich flowering plants in gardens, parks, and public 
spaces; 
- Reduce or eliminate the use of pesticides and herbicides harmful to pollinators; 
- Support local and regional Monarch habitat restoration efforts; 
- Educate our youth and communities about the importance of pollinator conservation; 
 
IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand and caused the Seal of the City 
of Leawood to be affixed this 21st day of September, 2026.
   
   Marc E. Elkins, Mayor

Reaching Comprehensive Plan (RRCP)

through expectations for the conversation tonight. The first topic had to do with 
areas in the community they anticipated to facilitate change and the evolutions of 
neighborhoods. She said that development and redevelopment is going to be the 
way to increase the sales tax base and then opened the discussion for thoughts 
on redevelopment. 
Mayor Elkins said that it is critically important that they continue policies that will 
continue to diversify the tax base. He also thought they should pursue policies that 
encourage reinvestment in North Leawood but still preserve its character.
