Improvements – 85th Terrace to 86th Terrace

Julie Cain, David Ley, Brian Scovill, and Spencer Osborne (TREKK Design Group) speaking:
• Staff provided background on the State Line Road project scope: a mill and overlay from 
the north city limits to 89th Street, narrowing of 89th Street for a 10-foot trail, signal 
replacement at 85th Terrace, and geometric improvements. The focus area for this study 
was 85th Terrace, 86th Street, and 86th Terrace between Lee Boulevard and State Line 
Road, encompassing 93 residential properties.
• Staff summarized the 30-year history of traffic concerns in this area, including prior 
traffic calming efforts (raised intersections/speed tables paid for by 85th Terrace 
residents), multiple prior committee appearances, and the 2001 median closure at 86th 
Street/Ward Parkway.
• TREKK Design Group presented findings from the traffic study, including crash history 
(2022–2024), traffic volumes, and level of service analysis. Crash rates at 85th Terrace 
and 85th Street fell below the elevated threshold of 1.0, though safety remains a concern. 
Eight of nine crashes at 85th Street involved the westbound-to-southbound movement. 
Current traffic counts on 85th Terrace exceed 2,500 vehicles per day, with the majority 
identified as cut-through traffic.
• A third option (traffic signal at 85th Street) was reviewed and determined not feasible 
from an engineering standpoint due to queuing concerns, U-turn constraints, commercial 
cut-through impacts, and failure to reduce neighborhood cut-through traffic.
• Two viable options were presented:
◦Option 1 – Modified Existing: Protected left turns added at the 85th Terrace signal; 
86th Street median closure included. Reduces crash risk at the signal but may increase 
traffic on 85th Terrace between Sagamore and State Line.
◦Option 2 – Movement Restrictions: Restricts northbound left turns and westbound 
through movements at 85th Terrace; closes 86th Terrace median. Most directly 
addresses cut-through traffic. Recommended by staff and TREKK.
• A resident survey was conducted (93 households notified; 52 responded). Results from 
Question 5 (direct comparison): Option 1 – 30%; Option 2 – 42%; Neither – 28%. Staff 
and TREKK recommended Option 2 based on greater public support, stronger safety 
benefits, and best alignment with project goals.
• Survey results broken down by street: Households on 85th Terrace and High Drive 
preferred Option 2 (representing 33 of 52 respondents). Households on 86th Street 
preferred Option 1. Sagamore respondents did not support either option. 86th Terrace 
respondents were equally split among all three choices.
• Staff noted that 85th Terrace residents paid for their own traffic calming devices — one 
of very few instances in the city — and that this history was considered in evaluating the 
options.

AGENDA ITEM  
Construction of Sidewalks as part of the 2027 Residential Reconstruction Project 
 
SUMMARY  
This item is a discussion and recommendation regarding sidewalks as part of the 2027 Residential 
Street Reconstruction Program (see attached map). The residential streets included in the project 
are located north of Brookwood Elementary School and currently do not have sidewalks. 
  
Prior to the 2023 Residential Street Reconstruction Project, which included 97th Place (Mission 
Road to Ensley Lane), Public Works surveyed residents within the surrounding neighborhood 
because several streets identified for future reconstruction could potentially accommodate 
sidewalks. The 2022 survey was sent to residents along 97th Place (Mission Rd to Ensley Ln), 
Pawnee Lane (97th Pl to 101st St) and Mohawk Lane (101st St to 103rd St) to determine support for 
constructing sidewalks as part of planned street reconstruction projects. Survey results indicated 
support was slightly below 50 percent. 
  
Following the 2022 survey, Public Works received feedback from several residents expressing 
concerns that the survey did not clearly communicate the level of resident support needed for 
sidewalks to move forward. Residents also indicated they were not fully aware that all property 
owners on the street would be responsible for the cost of sidewalk construction, and that the 
adjacent property owner was responsible for the long-term maintenance and repair of the sidewalks. 
  
Although Public Works conducted a survey in 2022, staff elected to survey residents included in the 
2027 Residential Street Reconstruction Program again to determine whether opinions had changed 
and to provide the additional information listed above. The new survey results indicate overwhelming 
opposition to constructing sidewalks within the project area. 
  
The proposed layout of the sidewalk indicating which trees would be removed and the  resident 
survey, conducted by ETC Institute, is included with this report. Staff will present the review the 
proposed sidewalk layouts and survey results.  
  
Residents who received the Sidewalk survey were also notified of the Public Works Committee 
meeting and invited to attend. 
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION  
Based on the limited support from the residents who would be directly impacted by the construction 
and long-term maintenance of the sidewalks, Staff recommends the Public Works Committee 
forward a recommendation to the Governing Body that sidewalks not be included as part of the 2027 
Residential Street Reconstruction Program.

Executive Summary 
Purpose  
In July 2026, ETC Institute conducted a neighborhood resident survey in partnership with the City of 
Leawood and GBA to assess resident interest in a proposed sidewalk project that would directly affect 26 
nearby households. 
Methodology  
A letter was  mailed to  each of the  26  households  that would be  directly  impacted by  the  proposed 
sidewalk. The letter explained the purpose of the survey and invited residents to participate. A total of 19 
completed surveys were received, resulting in a response rate of 73%.
