 LEAWOOD PLANNING COMMISSION 
 
  
The Leawood Planning Commission is a nine-member non-partisan body whose members are 
appointed by the Mayor and confirmed by the Governing Body.  
  
The Planning Commission prepares the Comprehensive Plan that is used as a general guide for the 
development of the community.  The Comprehensive Plan is reviewed and updated annually as part 
of the commission's ongoing process of evaluating trends and patterns.  The Commission also 
reviews all zoning, special use permit, and site plan and plat applications prior to making 
recommendations to the governing body for final action.     
  
The regular scheduled public meetings of the Planning Commission are held at 6:00 PM on the fourth 
Tuesday of each month in the City Council chambers, 4800 Town Center Drive.  The Commission 
may also conduct a study session followed by a meeting on the second Tuesday of each month.     
  
Anyone wishing to appear on the Planning Commission agenda or study session agenda should 
contact Planning Services at (913) 339-6700.     
  
REZONING AND SPECIAL USE PERMIT PROCEDURES FOR LEAWOOD, KANSAS 
  
 Newspaper publications: The city will be responsible for publishing the notice of public hearing in the 
official City newspaper not less than 20 days prior to the end of the public hearing.     
  
Posting of the sign: Upon submission of the application, the City will supply the applicant with a sign 
to be posted on the property.  The sign must be posted not less than 20 days prior to the public 
hearing. 
  
Letters of notification: The applicant will be responsible for mailing notices by certified mail, return 
receipt requested, of the proposed zoning change to all land owners located within 200 feet of the 
area proposed to be altered.  These notices must be sent a minimum of 20 days prior to the Planning 
Commission hearing.

Public hearing: The Planning Commission hears all zoning requests, hearing from the applicant and 
anyone in the audience wishing to speak for or against the proposal.  The Commission will then make 
a recommendation for approval or denial to the City Council or continue the application to another 
Planning Commission agenda.  The following is an outline of the public hearing process.     
1. Staff summarization of comments and recommendations.   
2. Applicant presentation and response to staff comments and recommendations.   
3. Public Hearing    
o Anyone wishing to speak, either in favor or in opposition has an opportunity to speak.   
o It is appreciated if the speakers keep repetition to a minimum.  
4. The applicant will have an opportunity to respond to points raised during the hearing.  
5. Planning Commission discussion.  
6. Motion and second by the Planning Commission.   
7. Planning Commission discussion of motion.  
8. Planning Commission vote on the motion.     
  
Protest period: Certain property owners may file a petition protesting the application within 14 days 
after the close of the Planning Commission public hearing. The petition must be signed by the owners 
of record of 20% or more of any real property proposed to be rezoned, or by the owners of record of 
20% or more of the total real property within the area required to be notified in Article 16-5-4.1 of the 
proposed zoning of specific property, excluding streets and public ways and property excluded 
pursuant to 165-4.3.     
  
City Council Action: After the protest period has concluded, the application will be placed on an 
agenda for a City Council meeting.  The Council may then take action on the proposal.  The Council 
may approve the Planning Commission’s recommendation, or it may amend and approve or remand 
the proposal to the Planning Commission for further consideration.  
  
Public Accommodation:  If you require any accommodation (i.e. qualified interpreter, hearing 
assistance, etc.) in order to attend this meeting, please notify this office at (913) 339-6700 or email at 
cityclerk@leawood.org no later than 96 hours prior to the scheduled commencement of the meeting.

Introduction 
 
The project site is located at the Northeast corner of Mission Boulevard and 135th Street.  The site 
is bounded by 133rd Street on the North and Pawnee Street to the East.  The overall Market

undeveloped acres.

seven development strategies.  The following narrative describes how specific elements and 
goals of the plan “fit” in with the City’s goals for development of this key site located at the 
principal southern gateway to the City of Leawood.

Section 1: 
Design Narrative 
 
Creating Sense of Place 
The intersection of Mission Boulevard and 135th Street has been identified as a primary 
intersection along the 135th Street corridor in the City of Leawood. Sense of place is provided in 
this development by large panels of green space containing walks, plantings, special paving, 
and architectural elements along the way. There is an existing pedestrian plaza at Mission and 
135th Street that provides special columns, planters, ironwork, and landscape plantings to 
provide a visual statement and a pedestrian focal point.  This proposal will serve to improve this 
feature with a signature sign for the development and additional plantings to further create 
identity and “sense of place”.   
 
A secondary gateway or intersection feature is proposed at the intersection of Pawnee and 
135th Street. This feature will consist of a proposed seating area with brick pavers, a low 
curvilinear stone seat wall, benches and colorful, low landscape plantings. The element will 
primarily relate to the motorist but also provide a pedestrian-level scale by locating the sidewalk 
along this pedestrian node and providing place to sit and relax. In addition, ironwork will be 
incorporated behind the seating wall and will provide an opportunity to place internally lit sign 
letters noting the name of the development. The ironwork matches the materials and design at 
the Mission Road intersection.   
 
Additional landscape plantings along 135th Street will also contribute to “sense of place” by 
providing a continuous canopy of shade trees for the pedestrian to walk beneath and the 
motorist to drive along side. Special pedestrian nodes through out the development are 
connected by walks and consist of seating areas, concentrated plantings of ornamental trees, 
shrubs, perennials and will serve to create a “linear park”. The pedestrian walks will connect this 
development at strategic points to the proposed Gezer Park north of this development and to 
the walks along the adjacent public streets.  
 
The development will adopt a traditional architectural design style that people are comfortable 
with and denotes a distinctive level of quality. Amenities include open-air courtyard spaces, 
seating areas, and low-level stone walls to create a series of outdoor “rooms”. These rooms are 
linked with wide landscaped walks and, at times, specially treated paving to enhance the 
pedestrian experience. 
 
Transition within the Corridor 
This development lies in the center of the 135th Street corridor and is designated as a mixed-use 
retail and office center that will serve the regional market.  The development will also serve local 
residents by providing convenient access for pedestrians or bicyclists from adjacent residential 
areas. For example, the retail shops at the southeast corner of the site will face inward to the 
parking field with the backs of the buildings treated with “four-sided” architecture along 135th 
Street will be designed to invite the pedestrian by creating pedestrian scale architectural 
building features and orientating special café style courtyards to the street with connecting 
sidewalks. 
 
The existing larger scale building in the center of the site accommodates the Price Chopper.  
The proposed buildings will be developed at a smaller scale with inc reased architectural 
detailing to provide visual interest. These mixed-use buildings will be marketed to smaller retail 
type shops, office space and restaurant/café venues.

Defining Urban Space 
Buildings are located near the periphery of the site to clearly define the street edge.  Landscape 
plantings of large trees will assist in creating consistent edges as well as enclosure.  Large 
overstory trees will “soften” the buildings. The proposed one-story buildings will have a height of 
about 23 feet to the parapet wall and a maximum of 30 feet to the tower elements.  Building 
setbacks are per city guidelines. 
 
The site is organized to orient building front doors toward each other and sometimes a 
landscape focal point. Walks consisting of special paving will serve to link the buildings. The 
access road is proposed to have brick paver crosswalks. The service points and trash enclosures 
all buildings are internalized to the development and are attached to the buildings to provide 
inherent screening of these activities. 
 
Landscape plantings serve to provide additional screening, definition of edges, and provide 
orientation for the pedestrian throughout the site. Low plantings of shrubs and groundcover 
serve to visually contain the sidewalks. Tree plantings between the walk and the street or parking 
areas provide a safe environment for the pedestrian. In addition, pedestrian nodes are provided 
throughout the site consisting of special pavers, low stone walls, landscaping and benches.  
 
Creating Pedestrian Scale 
Ornamental lighting, pavers, low walls, and plantings will delineate a “market -style promenade” 
and café areas adjacent to the buildings. The result will be a unique shopping experience and a 
distinctive appearance for this development. Sidewalk linkages throughout the site will connect 
each of the buildings and the public street areas. The site design and landscape architecture 
will provide a high level of detail to create interest, relate to the human scale, and 
accommodate pedestrian and vehicular circulation. Site lighting will be scaled down along 
walkways and near the buildings. 
 
Controlling Bulk 
The architectural concept provides a series of “village-type” facades utilizing a variety of 
materials, windows, building heights, color and undulations to relate to a human scale. The 
façade on the back and sides of each building will also be treated with similar materials and 
undulations to enhance each building’s appearance on all sides and will provide an aesthetic 
appearance from adjacent commercial and residential areas. 
 
The architecture of the buildings provides interplay of materials and color. Increased 
architectural detail at the pedestrian level includes use of: stone columns, window lintels, 
additional window mullions and awnings. Extensive detailing with materials such as brick 
combined with cast stone sill/caps and undulating stucco surfaces will create charming 
pedestrian environment with interest and appeal. Tower structures serve to provide covered 
portico areas and location for shop signing. Canopies and awnings provide interest, color, and 
serve to shelter patrons from weather at all entries. An overall festive environment is proposed 
with colorful umbrella tables in the café areas, colorful seasonal plantings, and a high level of 
building detail at eye-level.

Creating Pedestrian Space within Parking Lots 
Continuous pedestrian connections are made within the parking lot areas. Connections consist 
of concrete or special paving and planted with low shrubs. Vehicles are separated from the 
pedestrian by plantings and green space. Wheel stops will be provided where the accessible 
spaces abut flush curbs and sidewalks. Pedestrian scale lighting, seating areas and special 
paving systems serve to enhance pedestrian safety, provide a sense of place and increase 
beauty within the site. 
 
Integration of Greenways and Open Space 
Extensive landscaping of the site’s periphery combined with a six-foot wide sidewalk along 135th 
Street will serve to provide a visual link to other developed areas in the corridor. The walkway 
accommodates bike and pedestrian travel along the corridor. Well-defined walkways extend 
into the site at key locations. The city requested a pedestrian link from the existing portion of this 
development to Gezer Park north of 133rd Street. This link will occur north of the existing Price 
Chopper and will provide a crosswalk in 133rd Street to the existing pedestrian/bike path on the 
south side of the park along 133rd Street. 
 
The resulting combination of plantings throughout the parking areas, pedestrian amenities and 
architectural elements will provide an enjoyable shopping experience and quality services for 
the City of Leawood.

To Whom It May Concern: 
On behalf of our customer, Leawood Family Eye Care, Excel Sign Co. is formally requesting 
consideration and approval of a deviation from the established Leawood Commons sign 
criteria for the proposed exterior building signage at 11401 Ash Street, Leawood, Kansas. 
The specific deviation being requested is to allow exterior building signage to be installed 
on the west side of the building tower rather than on the south side of the tower, where a 
building sign is already permitted under the current sign criteria. 
The proposed west-facing sign location will provide appropriate identification and visibility for 
Leawood Family Eye Care while utilizing the architectural tower element as an intentional and 
suitable location for the tenant’s signage. We believe the proposed placement is compatible with 
the architecture of the building and the overall character and appearance of the Leawood 
Commons development. 
We respectfully request the opportunity to present the proposed signage, drawings, and 
supporting information at the August 25, 2026 meeting. At that time, we would be available to

CASE PC-155-2026 - LEAWOOD DEVELOPMENT ORDINANCE AMENDMENT TO SECTION 16-
4-6 - SIGNS DURING CONSTRUCTION ACTIVITIES - Request for approval of an amendment to 
the Leawood Development Ordinance pertaining to Construction Signs. PUBLIC HEARING 
 
SUMMARY  
This proposed LDO amendment addresses construction site signage as a specific type of temporary 
signage.  Currently, construction site signage must adhere to general temporary sign regulations 
which limit the total square footage for temporary signs on any lot to 48 square feet, with no 
individual sign exceeding 16 square feet. 
  
It is common practice for construction sites to display temporary signage as part of screening 
materials to enhance aesthetics and safety of the site during construction, as well as for clear project 
identification.  As a specific type of temporary signage with unique circumstances, many jurisdictions 
include language in their respective development regulations to address construction site signage.  
Staff reviewed regulations related to construction site signage from the neighboring jurisdictions of 
Overland Park, Lenexa, and Olathe in developing the proposed language.

16-4-6.15 Temporary Signs 
A)  Purpose and Findings. 
The Governing Body enacts this Ordinance to establi sh reasonable, narrowly tailored regulations 
for the posting of Temporary signs on public and pr ivate property.  Temporary Signs left 
completely unregulated, can become a threat to publ ic safety as a traffic hazard, and a detriment 
to property values as an aesthetic nuisance.  By im plementing these regulations, the City intends 
to: 
1)  Balance the rights of individuals to convey their m essages through temporary signs and 
the right of the public to be protected against the unrestricted proliferation of signs; 
2)  Further the objectives of the City’s comprehensive plan; 
3)  Protect the public health, safety and welfare; 
4)  Reduce traffic and pedestrian hazards; 
5)  Protect property values by minimizing the possible adverse effects and visual blight 
caused by signs; 
6)  Promote economic development; and  
7)  Ensure the fair and consistent enforcement of the temporary sign regulations. 
This ordinance is not intended to, and should not b e read to include, content based regulations.  If 
for any reason any portion or part of this Ordinanc e set out in this Section, or the application 
thereof to any person or circumstances is declared to be unconstitutional or invalid, such decision 
will not affect the validity of the remaining portions of this Section. 
B)  Regulations.

City, subject to the following requirements: 
1)  The total square footage for temporary signs on any  lot in any district, in the aggregate, 
shall not exceed forty-eight (48) square feet, with  no individual sign exceeding sixteen 
(16) square feet.  The total square footage of a si gn is measured to include all of the
