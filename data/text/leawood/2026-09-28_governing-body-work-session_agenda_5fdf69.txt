Purpose of This Discussion 
 
Olsson prepared the draft Leawood: Rooted & Reaching Comprehensive Plan, which was distributed to the 
Governing Body and Planning Commission for review and comment in June 2026. Since its release, the project 
team has received more than 150 comments from elected officials and planning commissioners. Those comments 
can be reviewed using the following link: https://olsson.konveio.com/leawood -rooted-reaching-plan-draft-june-
2026 
Those comments are currently being reviewed and will help inform the next round of revisions to the plan to 
prepare it for stakeholder and public review. The purpose of this work session is to help establish a shared 
understanding of several key policy issues that emerged repeatedly throughout the planning process and plan 
review. These issues represent areas where additional discussion, clarification, and direction from leadership 
would strengthen the final plan and provide a clearer foundation for future policy decisions.  
Leawood is a mature, highly established community that is approaching build- out. Unlike many growing 
communities, Leawood's future will be shaped less by large-scale greenfield development and more by 
redevelopment, reinvestment, infrastructure stewardship, neighborhood preservation, and managing change over 
time. As a result, many of the decisions facing the community are not questions of whether growth will occur, but 
rather what form that change should take and how it should be guided to reflect communit y values. Several 
recurring themes have emerged throughout the process related to redevelopment, housing, transportation, 
preservation, open space, fiscal sustainability, and community character. These topics often involve competing 
priorities, differing viewpoints, and legitimate tradeoffs. The objective of this discussion is not necessarily to reach 
unanimous agreement on every issue, but rather to identify where common ground exists and where additional 
policy direction is needed. 
During this session, we will discuss six major policy questions that rose to the top through community 
engagement, stakeholder discussions, technical analysis, and review of the draft plan. These topics represent the 
issues most likely to influence Leawood's future growth, development patterns, quality of life, and co mmunity 
identity over the coming decades. The direction provided during this discussion will help shape revisions to both 
the Comprehensive Plan and the accompanying updates to the Leawood Development Ordinance, ensuring that 
future recommendations are aligned with the collective vision of the Gov erning Body and Planning Commission. 
Thank you for your continued participation, feedback, and thoughtful consideration throughout this process. 
The time invested by City leadership has been instrumental in creating a comprehensive plan that reflects the 
values, aspirations, and long-term vision of the Leawood community.

Topic 1: Redevelopment, Economic Vitality, and Fiscal Sustainability 
 
Why This Matters 
Leawood is approaching full build-out, meaning future growth will occur primarily through redevelopment and 
reinvestment rather than development of vacant land. As commercial centers, office parks, and other properties 
age, they will periodically require substantial upgrades, repositioning, or redevelopment to remain competitive.  At 
the same time, the City must continue maintaining public infrastructure, parks, utilities, streets, and other services. 
Some communities address these long-term costs through new commercial growth and redevelopment that 
expands the tax base. Others focus more heavily on preserving existing character and limiting change. Throughout 
this planning process, differing perspectives emerged regarding the degree to which tax base growth should be a 
long-term objective for Leawood. Some participants emphasized the importance of strengthen ing economic 
activity and supporting redevelopment, while others questioned whether the City needs significant additional 
commercial growth at all. 
 
Key Considerations 
 Future growth opportunities will be increasingly tied to redevelopment rather than greenfield 
development. 
 Aging commercial and office properties may require reinvestment to remain viable.  
 Redevelopment can generate additional tax revenue and economic activity but may also result in changes 
to community character. 
 Fiscal sustainability may become increasingly important as public infrastructure ages.  
 Leawood may need to determine whether future redevelopment should primarily focus on economic 
performance, community character, or balancing both objectives. 
 
Discussion Questions 
1. Does the Governing Body and Commission have a clear understanding of how the Growth Strategy and 
Future Land Use Map applies during development review?  
2. How important is tax base diversification as a long-term objective? 
3. What is your expectation for the defined Adaptive Reuse & Redevelopment Sites? Do they align with the 
defined Land Use categories?  
4. If increased sales and property taxes are not gained through redevelopment (particularly nonresidential 
sites) would residents be willing to pay more to support city operations and maintenance as inflation and 
cost escalation occurs over time?  
5. The plan recommends limited application of mixed-use buildings, while gearing towards “horizontal” 
mixed-use development patterns. Does Leawood need additional commercial or mixed -use development, 
or should the focus be on maintaining existing assets? 
6. What outcomes should the City prioritize when evaluating redevelopment opportunities?

Topic 2: Neighborhood Preservation, Historic Resources, and Property Rights 
 
Why This Matters 
Many of Leawood's established neighborhoods are entering a period of transition. Homes built several decades 
ago are reaching an age where significant reinvestment, renovation, expansion, and redevelopment are becoming 
increasingly common. Rising land values have also created market pressure for tear-down and rebuild activity in 
certain neighborhoods. 
Throughout plan review, questions emerged regarding preservation, neighborhood character, and the role of 
historic recognition. There appears to be differing perspectives regarding whether preservation efforts should 
remain primarily educational and honorary or whether the City should explore stronger tools to influence 
redevelopment outcomes. At the same time, any discussion of preservation must be balanced against private 
property rights and homeowners' ability to reinvest in their properties.  
 
Key Considerations 
 Existing historic recognition programs do not prevent demolition or redevelopment.  
 Additional preservation strategies could range from incentives and education to more formal regulatory 
tools. 
 Design standards may influence redevelopment outcomes without restricting property owners from 
reinvesting. 
 Different neighborhoods may have different expectations regarding preservation and redevelopment.  
 The City may need to determine whether certain areas warrant a different policy approach than others.  
 
Discussion Questions 
1. What should preservation mean in Leawood? 
2. Should historic designation remain primarily honorary, or should additional tools be considered?  
3. To what extent should the City seek to influence tear-down and rebuild activity? 
4. How should the City balance neighborhood character with property rights?  
5. Should North Leawood and other established neighborhoods be treated differently than newer areas  on 
the Future Land Use Map (currently, they are in the same category)? 
6. Are the proposed neighborhood categories and place type distinctions appropriate?

Topic 3: Housing Choice and Residential Growth 
 
Why This Matters 
Demographic trends suggest that many existing Leawood residents wish to remain in the community as they age 
but may eventually seek different housing options. At the same time, attracting future generations may require a 
broader range of housing formats than currently exists in some portions of the city. The planning process revealed 
relatively broad support for certain housing types such as villas, townhomes, and other lower -maintenance 
ownership options. However, less consensus exists regarding apartments , multifamily housing, accessory dwelling 
units, and future residential density. Recent state legislation may also affect how communities regulate certain 
housing types moving forward. 
 
Key Considerations 
 The draft Comprehensive Plan is limited in its recommendations for new housing types, limiting variety to 
designated areas on the Future Land Use Map.  
 State Legislation may impact the City’s ability to ban Accessory Dwelling Units, and legal review of new 
state laws are recommended. If ADUs are required by-right, we would recommend addressing this in the 
Leawood Development Ordinance updates.  
 Housing needs change throughout different stages of life, and some residents have expressed a lack of 
options for downsizing. 
 Housing diversity can support aging in place, workforce housing, and household transitions.  
 State legislation may limit local discretion regarding some housing types , such as accessory dwelling units. 
 There appears to be a distinction in public perception between ownership- oriented housing and 
traditional multifamily apartment development. It is important to note that plans and regulations focus 
on the use, not the user, and Federal Fair Housing prevents discrimination based on occupancy status.  It is 
also important to note that many single-family homes in existing neighborhoods are actually renter 
occupied.  
 
Discussion Questions 
1. What is the desired approach for enabling downsizing in Leawood for aging residents who are seeking 
independent living options? 
2. What housing types should be encouraged over the next twenty years?  
3. Has Leawood reached an appropriate level of apartment development? 
4. Should future growth focus primarily on ownership-oriented housing options? 
5. What role should accessory dwelling units play? 
6. How should Leawood balance neighborhood character with changing housing needs?

Topic 4: The Future of 135th Street 
 
Why This Matters 
The 135th Street Corridor represents one of the last significant opportunities for Leawood to shape future growth 
and development. Decisions regarding roadway design, transportation, land use, housing, open space, and 
commercial development will influence the corridor for generations. The corridor also represents a broader 
question about Leawood's identity. Some envision a corridor that continues to function primarily as a regional 
transportation and commercial route. Others envision a corridor with a stron ger emphasis on placemaking, 
walkability, housing diversity, and village-style development patterns. 
 
Key Considerations 
 Land use decisions and transportation decisions are closely linked, and there appears to be a mismatch 
between planned roadway expansion and community feedback in this process.  
 Different corridor visions produce different economic, mobility, and character outcomes.  
 Community members have expressed a desire for the corridor to maintain a distinctly Leawood character.  
 Future zoning updates will need to align with the vision ultimately selected.  
 
Discussion Questions 
1. What role should 135th Street serve within the community? 
2. Should the corridor prioritize mobility, placemaking, or a balance of both?  
3. What development intensity is appropriate? 
4. Should growth focus on village-style development, mixed-use nodes, commercial centers, or another 
model? 
5. How important is maintaining a distinct transition between Leawood and adjacent communities?

Topic 5: Transportation, Mobility, and Community Character 
 
Why This Matters 
Transportation discussions throughout the planning process frequently extended beyond traffic and 
engineering. Residents and stakeholders consistently connected mobility decisions to quality of life, 
safety, walkability, and community character. Questions emerged regarding roadway widening, bicycle 
and pedestrian safety, trail connectivity, neighborhood traffic concerns, and the balance between 
moving vehicles efficiently and creating comfortable places for people. 
 
Key Considerations 
 Transportation projects often require tradeoffs between capacity, speed, safety, and 
placemaking. 
 Community input consistently emphasized pedestrian and bicycle improvements. 
 Shared-use trails are serving a growing variety of users. 
 Future transportation investments will influence development and redevelopment patterns. 
 Different corridors may warrant different design approaches.  
 The in-progress Safety Action Plan will identify the high injury network and may position 
Leawood for implementation grant funding opportunities.  
 
Discussion Questions 
1. When tradeoffs occur, should safety or vehicle throughput take precedence? How do we 
balance safety versus vehicle speed/congestion? Are there certain roadways or roadway types 
to prioritize vehicle throughput? (arterials, for example) 
2. The current Self-Propelled Leawood plan allows striped bike lanes on major streets, while 
current guidance generally favors separated facilities that serve a wider range of riders. Would 
Leawood like the Comp Plan to encourage a low-stress bicycle network on these streets or 
continue focusing on facilities for more confident riders? 
3. What level of roadway congestion is acceptable in exchange for improved walkability and 
community character? (fewer lanes, fewer/shorter turn lanes, narrower lanes, shorter 
intersection crossings all help walkability, but slow traffic down) 
4. Should trail facilities increasingly separate different user types? Is there a preference for fully 
separate facilities, or would signage or pavement markings help manage safety between user 
types? See signage and pavement marking examples.

Topic 6: Open Space, Golf Courses, and Environmental Stewardship 
 
Why This Matters 
Parks, trails, mature trees, golf courses, and open spaces are among Leawood's defining community 
assets. These spaces contribute not only to recreation and environmental quality, but also to property 
values, community identity, and overall quality of life. Several comments raised questions regarding the 
future of private golf courses, preservation of open space, native landscaping, stormwater management, 
and environmental stewardship. While these issues are often discussed independently, they are 
fundamentally related to how the community values and manages its landscape over time. 
 
Key Considerations 
• Private golf courses represent some of the largest remaining open spaces in the city. 
• Open space can provide recreational, environmental, and aesthetic benefits. 
• Redevelopment pressure may eventually affect existing open space assets. 
• Environmental practices must be balanced with resident expectations regarding aesthetics and 
maintenance. 
• Long-term resilience may require new approaches to landscaping and stormwater management. 
 
Discussion Questions 
1. Should preservation of major open spaces be an explicit priority? 
2. If a private golf course ceases operation, what future outcomes would be acceptable? 
3. How should environmental goals influence future development standards? 
4. What role should native landscaping and green infrastructure play in Leawood? 
5. What open space assets should be preserved regardless of future growth pressures?

areas in the community they anticipated to facilitate change and the evolutions of

way to increase the sales tax base and then opened the discussion for thoughts 
on redevelopment. 
Mayor Elkins said that it is critically important that they continue policies that will 
continue to diversify the tax base. He also thought they should pursue policies that 
encourage reinvestment in North Leawood but still preserve its character.
Councilmember Sunkel said the way to bridge the concern of reinvestment while
