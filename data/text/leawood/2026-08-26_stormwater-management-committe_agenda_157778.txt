SECOND ITEM OF BUSINESS: Indian Creek Floodplain Master Plan Update – Olsson 
Associates 
David Ley, Brian Scovill, and Teddy Gillespie (Olsson Associates) speaking: 
• The City retained Olsson Associates in February 2026 to review the Indian Creek flood 
model and develop the Indian Creek Master Plan for the corridor between Mission Road 
and State Line Road. The study scope includes evaluating watershed improvement 
concepts to reduce flood risk, preparing concept-level cost estimates, coordinating with 
stakeholders, conducting public engagement, and delivering a final sub-watershed study 
summary memorandum. 
• Staff noted this is a planning study only. Alternatives identified may include property 
buyouts, drainage improvements, and other watershed management strategies with

conceptual costs. No funding is currently programmed in the City’s Capital Improvement 
Program (CIP) to implement any recommended improvements. 
• Johnson County previously hired Olsson to produce an updated hydraulic model of 
Indian Creek using more recent, larger rainfall conditions. The updated model produces 
a more conservative floodplain than the current effective FEMA model and has been 
approved by FEMA, though it is not set to replace the effective model at this time. The 
City hired Olsson to use this updated model to investigate seven specific locations 
around Leawood City Park and advise design alternatives. 
• Olsson presented findings for all seven sites. The following is a summary of each: 
 
Site 1 – Berm West of Mission Road and I-435 
• The effective Zone AE floodplain crosses Mission Road north of Lee Boulevard. The 
proposed berm is mostly located within the City of Overland Park and will require 
coordination with their engineering department. 
• Proposed design: Install a berm parallel to Mission Road to fully block 100-year flow. 
Primary purpose is to protect emergency access to and from the wastewater treatment 
plant up to the 100-year storm. Will require compensatory storage on the south side of 
Indian Creek to offset floodplain impacts. 
 
Site 2 – Future Sculpture and Garden Area 
• Proposed design: Raise the future community garden (located between the Reaching 
Sculpture and cell tower) by 1 to 3 feet of fill to remove it from the 100-year floodplain. 
Water surface elevation would drop nearly 1.5 feet across the park area. 
• This outcome depends on whether the Mission Road berm (Site 1) is also constructed; 
without it, additional fill and cost would be required. Includes a proposed walking path 
and review of impacts to the adjacent Indian Creek recreational trail. 
 
Site 3 – Raising Lee Boulevard 
• Lee Boulevard currently floods during common storm events, cutting off access for park 
visitors, emergency vehicles, and the wastewater treatment plant — even during a 2-
year storm event. 
• The project area is almost entirely within the Zone AE Floodway, which is highly 
regulated by FEMA for placing fill. Raising the road in place would likely require re-
routing Lee Boulevard to the north. The baseball park, parking lot, and eastern 
intersection would all need to be reworked. 
 
Site 4 – Leawood Estates Flood Control (West) 
• Residential homes west of Lee Boulevard and north of I-435 reported flooding in 2017. 
The area was removed from the mapped Zone AE floodplain in 2009, but Olsson’s 
updated model closely matches reported flood conditions. 
• Proposed engineering solution: Storm sewer extension to route and detain stormwater, 
construct a pump house above the existing 5.5-foot by 5-foot concrete box culvert, and 
pump water northward toward Indian Creek to alleviate flooding. Estimated cost

approximately $10 million. Buyout of affected homes is the alternative if the engineering 
approach is not pursued. 
 
Site 5 – Leawood City Park Flood Control at Future Park Areas 
• Three potential sports court locations were proposed by the city for Olsson to model. 
Two of the three fall within the effective Zone AE Floodway, which prohibits amenity 
projects under city regulation. 
• Selected viable location: Between the two existing parking lots north of Constitution 
Court. Requires approximately 8,700 cubic yards of fill and raising the existing grade up 
to 7.25 feet to clear the 100-year flood elevation. Adjacent land provides sufficient area 
for compensatory storage. The site is not flooded in 10- or 100-year events. 
 
Site 6 – Erosion Control Measures West of Leawoof Dog Park 
• Olsson evaluated three alternatives for creek bank stabilization: a stacked stone wall, a 
regraded riprap slope, and a secondary channel east of the existing alignment. 
• Recommended solution: Stacked stone wall, selected for its lower impact to trails and 
park fields, smaller footprint, and reduced cost. Approximately 830 linear feet of wall at 
elevation 828 feet, with 4:1 grading behind and riprap to prevent undercutting. A no-rise 
condition is achievable. Estimated cost: approximately $1.6 million. Described by Olsson 
as the most straightforward and viable of all seven sites. 
 
Site 7 – Stateline Road Flood Control 
• Stateline Road is approximately 6 feet underwater during the 100-year storm event and 
has flooded at least 3 times in the last 10 years. When it floods alongside Mission, 
Holmes, and Warnell, north-south access is effectively cut off across a large portion of 
the corridor, pushing all emergency vehicle traffic to Roe. 
• The primary challenge is not Indian Creek alone. James Branch is independently 
undersized for its own drainage and overtops before Indian Creek reaches its banks, 
flooding the indoor pickleball facility and adjacent city buildings first. 
• Olsson modeled multiple alternatives including raising State Line Road, flood walls, re-
routing James Branch to its original alignment, and a long-span bridge concept. All 
approaches either caused significant upstream flooding, required buyouts of 
approximately 17 commercial parcels, or were not feasible without major additional work. 
Rough planning-level cost estimate for full improvements in this area: $30–$50 million. 
• Leawood Estates East (Sagamore area) is protected by an uncertified levee built in the 
early 2000s. Olsson’s updated model shows the levee overtopping in the 100-year event 
by over a foot. The engineering solution would require raising and certifying the levee 
(approximately 3 additional feet), removing approximately 550 feet of existing storm 
infrastructure, and installing an underground detention basin with a pump station. 
Certification would likely also require buying out 1–2 homes along the levee alignment. 
Estimated cost: approximately $10 million. 
• Staff noted the city has proactively maintained the uncertified levee through biannual 
inspections and deficiency remediation. Certification would provide flood insurance 
benefits to homeowners and make the levee eligible for FEMA repair funding after flood 
events.

• No single intervention at Site 7 effectively resolves the full problem area. Staff and 
Olsson identified State Line Road access as the most critical issue; a dedicated

KEY QUESTIONS/CONCERNS/REMARKS: 
▪ Steven Kaster asked whether the raised garden/bed would make a difference in the 
floodplain analysis. 
o Staff/Olsson advised that the impact would not be significant enough to affect the 
analysis or require separate consideration. 
▪ Debra Filla asked whether the wastewater treatment facility had concerns about flooding 
when it was originally constructed and why the facility would not be responsible for 
addressing the issue. 
o Brian Scovill advised that the conditions being discussed relate to approximately a 50-
year storm event and that the current modeling incorporates newer rainfall data that 
may not have been available when the facility was originally designed. 
o Tom Kimes added that when the wastewater facility was constructed, it did not include 
mitigation measures for the flooding conditions now being evaluated. 
▪ Jim Rawlings asked whether crews have an alternate route when the roads are flooded and 
how long the flooding typically lasts. 
o Teddy Gillespie and David Ley advised that flooding generally lasts a matter of hours. 
Lee Boulevard can remain flooded for up to approximately six hours during a 100-year 
event, while Mission Road may remain flooded for approximately two to three hours. 
They noted that the amount and intensity of rainfall have the greatest effect on duration. 
▪ Debra Filla asked how many homes or properties could potentially be offered a buyout and 
what would be involved if the City wanted to avoid construction of a pump system. 
o Tom Kimes advised that FEMA has guidelines governing property buyouts and that 
participation is voluntary. Property owners who choose to remain may have options to 
make modifications to their properties depending on the circumstances. 
o David Ley noted that the City also follows Johnson County requirements when 
evaluating floodplain improvements. 
▪ Lisa Harrison asked whether other jurisdictions have completed similar property buyouts. 
o Teddy Gillespie (Olsson Associates) advised that Shawnee Mission had completed 
approximately six buyouts and that Overland Park and Olathe have participated in more 
than 20 buyouts. 
▪ Debra Filla asked how many properties could potentially be involved in a Leawood buyout. 
o Teddy Gillespie and Brian Scovill advised that the number could potentially be 
approximately 17 properties. 
▪ Debra Filla asked what the primary issue is that the City is trying to resolve in the State Line 
Road area. 
o Teddy Gillespie explained that the concern is not limited to commercial properties. 
Flooding also affects residential areas and transportation access, with multiple flooding 
conditions occurring at approximately the same time. State Line Road access was 
identified as the most critical issue, while any proposed improvements would also need 
to minimize impacts to surrounding residential and commercial properties. 
▪ Jim Rawlings asked what the City is hearing from commercial property owners located 
within the floodplain.

o Staff advised that contact generally occurs when property owners seek to redevelop or 
make improvements to their properties. At that time, additional floodplain permitting and

▪ A committee member asked whether funding associated with the Lockton/Holbrook North 
development could potentially be used for the proposed creek-bank stabilization

▪ A committee member asked whether the Holbrook North development and wastewater

o Teddy Gillespie confirmed that both were incorporated into the model. 
▪ A committee member asked whether more natural creek-bank stabilization methods, such as 
willow plantings, had been considered for the area west of the dog park. 
o Teddy Gillespie advised that willow stakes are sometimes incorporated with riprap 
stabilization. More natural solutions would generally require a flatter slope, 
approximately 3:1 to 4:1 rather than the existing 2.5:1 condition, which could extend the 
project footprint an additional 30 to 40 feet into the soccer field area. A hybrid approach 
could be considered. 
▪ A committee member asked whether James Branch had been modeled using both its historic 
alignment and its current channel to create separate high-flow and low-flow paths. 
o Teddy Gillespie advised that the specific dual-channel scenario had not been modeled. 
He noted that providing another path could potentially reduce overtopping from James 
Branch, but Indian Creek would still flood the area shortly afterward, so the larger 
flooding problem would remain. 
▪ A committee member asked whether a long-span bridge could be constructed along State 
Line Road to allow additional floodwater to pass beneath the roadway. 
o Teddy Gillespie advised that the concept could be worth further study but could not 
confirm its effectiveness without additional modeling. 
o Staff also noted that bridge freeboard requirements would require the structure to be 
raised even higher than the roadway elevation needed simply to prevent overtopping. 
▪ A committee member asked whether State Line Road flooding is primarily the result of newer 
rainfall data or whether the roadway was originally constructed at an inadequate elevation. 
o Staff advised that the roadway was realigned as part of an earlier KDOT project and that 
the floodplain mapping available at that time would have been based on substantially 
older data. Olsson's updated model also corrects differences that previously existed 
between Kansas and Missouri flood elevations at State Line Road. 
▪ A committee member asked why the City could not simply deepen the creek channels to 
increase capacity. 
o Teddy Gillespie and staff explained that excavated channels would fill back in with 
sediment over time and that lowering the channel would require carrying the elevation 
change progressively downstream. 
o Staff noted that a 2018 estimate to widen and deepen Indian Creek from State Line 
Road toward Holmes was approximately $100 million. 
▪ A committee member asked who constructed the uncertified levee protecting Leawood 
Estates East and why it was not federally certified.

o Staff advised that the City constructed the levee around 2000 during a residential 
reconstruction project using available fill. At the time, it was approximately three feet 
above the mapped flood elevation and has successfully protected the homes from creek 
flooding, although ponding can remain in backyards for several hours. 
o Staff further explained that the City voluntarily follows many federal levee-maintenance 
practices, including biannual inspections and correction of identified deficiencies, but 
the levee is not technically FEMA-certified. 
▪ A committee member asked about Johnson County's compensatory storage requirement and 
how it differs from FEMA's no-rise requirement. 
o Teddy Gillespie explained that Johnson County has a separate compensatory-storage 
requirement for floodplain development. FEMA permitting generally focuses on 
demonstrating that a project does not cause a rise in flood elevation, while the County 
also requires consideration of displaced storage volume. 
 
Key Discussion Points 
• Committee asked about natural bank stabilization alternatives (willow stakes, 
bioengineered slopes) for Site 6. Olsson noted natural solutions require a flatter slope 
(3:1 to 4:1 vs. current 2.5:1), which would push the footprint 30–40 feet further west into 
the soccer fields. A hybrid riprap-with-willow-stakes approach is commonly used and 
could be explored. 
• Funding question raised: Whether the Lockton/Holbrook North development project 
funding could apply to applicable improvements. Staff confirmed the masterplan was 
incorporated into that evaluation and that funding from that source is a possibility. 
• Committee asked whether Holbrook North development and the wastewater treatment 
plant were accounted for in the model. Confirmed: both are included in Olsson’s updated 
model. 
• Deepening creek channels was raised as an alternative approach. Olsson explained 
channels refill with sediment quickly and the excavation would need to chase the change 
all the way to the Missouri and Mississippi Rivers. A 2018 estimate for widening and 
deepening Indian Creek from State Line Road to near homes was approximately $100 
million. 
• Compensatory storage requirements under Johnson County regulations were discussed. 
The county’s requirement differs from FEMA’s no-rise standard; some committee 
members were not previously familiar with the distinction. 
 
NEXT STEPS:

study area to present findings, gather input, and answer questions. Location and date to

• Following the public meeting, Olsson will present the final Indian Creek Study to the 
Stormwater Committee for recommendation to the Governing Body. 
• Staff to continue coordinating with Overland Park on the Mission Road berm (Site 1) 
alignment and approvals. 
• If the committee or council wishes to pursue Site 7 (Stateline Road corridor), a dedicated 
engineering study would be required prior to any design or construction.
