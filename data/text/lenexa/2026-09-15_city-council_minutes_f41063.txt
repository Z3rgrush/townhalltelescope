ACTION NEEDED: 
Approve Change Order No. 2 to the agreement with CM Concrete, Inc. ("CM Concrete") for the 2026 
Sidewalk & Trail Repair Program ("Program"). 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
The Program funds the replacement of aging asphalt trails with the City's current concrete standard to 
provide a more resilient surface and longer useful trail life. The Prairie Star Parkway Trail from I-435 
Bridge to Ridgeview Road was initially identified for reconstruction under the 2026 Program. During 
Program development, priorities shifted after the necessary closure of a pedestrian bridge along the 
Hickory Ridge Trail near 79th Street. Because the bridge closure had already affected trail use in that 
area, staff prioritized reconstructing the Hickory Ridge Trail to better coordinate improvements and 
minimize disruptions to trail users.

work, and staff recommends adding the Prairie Star Parkway trail reconstruction to CM Concrete's existing 
agreement. The work will remove the existing asphalt trail and replace it with a 10-foot-wide, 6-inch-thick 
concrete trail and also includes finish grading and seed and sod restoration. 
 
Staff solicited pricing from two contractors currently mobilized and under contract with the City to 
reconstruct approximately 1.42 miles of the Prairie Star Parkway Trail from I-435 to Ridgeview Road. CM 
Concrete submitted a lower price of $822,875.00 compared to Realm Construction's $836,157.70. If 
approved, the work is anticipated to be completed by December 15, 2026. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS/FUNDING SOURCES:

Trail Reconstruction Program (Project No. 60225).

SUBJECT: Approval of a cooperative agreement with Johnson County, Shawnee, Olathe, and Overland 
Park for the distribution of funds under the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant 
Program 
    
CONTACT: Eric Schmitz, Police Chief 
    
DATE: September 15, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Approve a cooperative agreement with Johnson County, Shawnee, Olathe, and Overland Park for the 
distribution of funds under the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) Program. 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
For several years, the Cities of Lenexa, Shawnee, Olathe, and Overland Park, and Johnson County have 
agreed to apply jointly for the JAG award from the Office of Justice Programs within the U.S. Department 
of Justice. The parties each receive a different amount of the total award, determined by a formula based 
primarily on community crime levels. 
  
The City of Olathe is the group's facilitator and will distribute the grants. The funds can be used to further 
and support each community's efforts to prevent or reduce local crime and violence through: 
• state and local initiatives,  
• technical assistance,  
• training,  
• personnel,  
• equipment,  
• supplies,  
• contractual support,  
• information systems for criminal justice, and 
• criminal justice-related research and evaluation activities. 
The Lenexa Police Department (LPD) has been allocated $12,722 for the 2026 JAG Program. The specific 
use of the grant funds for this cycle has not yet been determined. Staff is evaluating needs and identifying 
an appropriate project that meets the grant requirements. With past grant allocations, the LPD has 
purchased electric pedal-assist bicycles for the Bicycle Patrol Unit and a laser reconstruction scanner, 
which provides fast, accurate reconstruction of accident scenes. 
 
Approval of this cooperative agreement will allow the City to accept the 2026 JAG Program funds. This 
agreement is available for review in the City Clerk's office.

ITEM 5  
    
SUBJECT: Resolution amending the Volumetric Rate Calculation Form used in determining the fee paid 
for natural gas transported within the city in accordance with natural gas franchise 
ordinances 
    
CONTACT: Steven Shrout, Assistant City Attorney 
    
DATE: September 15, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Adopt a resolution amending the Volumetric Rate Calculation Form ("Form") used in determining the fee 
paid for natural gas transported within the city in accordance with natural gas franchise ordinances. 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
On July 15, 2008, the City passed Ordinance 5047 establishing the Form and allowing future amendments 
to be made by resolution. The Form allows the City to collect a franchise fee for natural gas transported, 
but not sold, within the city. The volumetric rate to be used for a given year is based upon the average 
settlement prices for natural gas during the preceding year. The City has received franchise fees for 
transport gas from the natural gas providers since January 1, 2009. 
 
Pursuant to the City’s natural gas franchises, the Form must be updated annually. The current Form took 
effect on January 1, 2026, and this resolution amends the Form to allow continued collection of this fee in 
2027. The Form has been reviewed by both natural gas providers without any objection. Under this 
resolution, the volumetric rate will increase from $0.1805 to $0.1821 per Mcf (thousand cubic feet) in 2027. 
Following adoption of this resolution, the amended transport gas fee will be effective January 1, 2027. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS/FUNDING SOURCES: 
The City currently has franchise ordinances with Kansas Gas Service Company and Atmos Energy 
Corporation. The 2027 franchise fees are expected to increase because of the increase in the volumetric 
rate. 
  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION: 
Adopt the resolution.

ACTION NEEDED: 
a. Adopt a resolution authorizing the assignment and assumption of and amendment of Resolution of 
Intent 2025-057 and the associated payment in lieu of taxes (PILOT) agreement; and 
 
b. Pass an ordinance authorizing the issuance of industrial revenue bonds (IRBs) in the principal amount 
not to exceed $20 million. 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
On December 2, 2025 the City Council adopted Resolution 2025-057, stating the City's intent to issue 
approximately $20 million in IRBs to help finance the acquisition, construction, and equipping of an 
office/warehouse/manufacturing project located at the northwest corner of Eicher Drive & Renner 
Boulevard ("Project"). The City Council also approved a fixed tax abatement with an effective abatement of 
71% over 10 years, as well as a PILOT agreement.  
 
Since approving the IRBs and tax abatement, Williams Equity Investments, LLC ("Original Developer") has 
created WEI Renner9, LLC ("Developer"), which will own and develop the Project on behalf of the Original 
Developer. The Original Developer has requested the City consent to the benefits and obligations arising 
under the resolution of intent being assigned to the Developer, as well as the PILOT agreement, which 
actions are authorized in this proposed resolution. Additionally, after finalizing design of the Project, the 
size of the building has decreased to 129,370 square feet and the Developer has requested Resolution 
2025-057 and the PILOT agreement be amended to reflect the actual building size. The PILOT amount will 
be adjusted to reflect the revised building size. All other terms of the amended resolution and PILOT 
agreement remain the same as the original documents, including the total amount of IRBs. 
 
The Developer is also ready to issue IRBs and has requested the City consider an ordinance issuing up to 
$20 million in IRBs. If approved, the IRB issue is anticipated to close in fall 2026. 
 
The IRB issuance uses the City's standard IRBs documents, which are available for review in the City 
Clerk's office. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS/FUNDING SOURCES: 
IRBs are not backed by the full faith and credit of the City. The applicant is responsible for repayment of 
the bonds and all fees related to the bond issue, including an origination fee. The Developer will begin 
making PILOT Payments in 2027. The property is currently paying approximately $50 in annual property 
taxes and the net impact of the PILOT payments is $595,921 over the 10-year abatement period.

LAND USE REVIEW  
The proposed plan includes two industrial buildings within the BP-2, Planned Manufacturing District. The 
buildings may accommodate multiple tenants or a single large tenant, subject to the permitted uses identified in 
Section 4-1-B-19 of the Unified Development Code (UDC). The BP-2 District is intended to accommodate high-
quality planned industrial parks, well-designed business cent ers, and single-tenant facilities. Uses may range 
from moderate to high intensity and are predominantly light industrial in nature, including wholesaling, 
manufacturing, warehousing, research, and office uses. Specific tenants have not yet been identified and will be 
reviewed for compliance with the BP-2 District requirements as the site develops.
The property’s BP-2 zoning is consistent with its Business Park classification on the Comprehensive Plan’s 
Future Land Use Map. Staff recently identified a minor parcel mapping discrepancy affecting a small portion of 
the northeast corner of the site, resulting in the Business Park classification not aligning with the corrected parcel 
boundary. Staff recommends correcting the Future Land Use Map as part of the annual Comprehensive Plan 
amendment process so the Business Park designation encompasses the entirety of the parcel. This is a technical 
mapping correction and does not represent a change in the intended future land use of the property.
The proposed development is also compatible with surrounding land uses. Properties to the north and west are 
primarily developed with office, wholesale, and warehousing uses. I-35 borders the site to the south and east, 
with properties beyond the interstate located within the City of Olathe.

DEVIATIONS 
The applicant requests one deviation for the setback of the white vinyl screening fence for the outdoor storage 
area. Section 4-1-B-24-F-5 of the UDC states that a fence cannot be located closer to the front property line than

3. Santa Fe Commerce Center — Consideration of a preliminary plan for an industrial development 
on property located at the northeast corner of Santa Fe Trail Drive & Lakeview Avenue within the 
BP-2, Planned Manufacturing District. PL26-10P 
 
APPLICANT PRESENTATION 
Dan Finn, civil engineer with Phelps Engineering, introduced himself and Brandon Becker, the owner and 
developer. He stated that the approximately 18-acre project is located off Santa Fe Trail Drive, just north 
of 119th Street. The project was previously approved in February 2024, but the approval lapsed when a 
final plan was not submitted before February 2026. The applicant is now seeking approval of the 
preliminary plan with the intent to proceed immediately with the final plan and building permits. The project 
includes two buildings totaling approximately 222, 550 square feet, stormwater detention and treatment 
facilities, and an outdoor storage lot at the northeast corner. Improvements include new right-turn lanes 
at the northern and third entrances, striping of the existing southern right -turn lane, and installation of 
sidewalk, curb and gutter, and stormwater infrastructure along the frontage.  Mr. Finn noted that the 
buildings would incorporate concrete panels, aluminum, and glass storefront. Berming, landscaping, and 
a screen wall would be provided along the southern edge to screen the truck dock from view. The project 
includes a deviation to allow fencing to extend to the 50- foot build line rather than the required front 
setback established from the building. Because the building is set back approximately 100 feet, this would 
provide an additional 50 feet of storage area. The outdoor storage area would be fully screened with solid 
vinyl fencing, including sections with masonry columns. Mr. Finn noted that Staff recommends masonry 
columns along Santa Fe Trail Drive and the north property line. The applicant instead proposes wrapping 
the columns around the northeast and southeast corners, citing the existing trees along the north property 
line that would largely obscure the columns. He stated that this approach would provide a better visual 
result while preserving the existing trees. Mr. Finn concluded by stating that the applicant was available 
for questions and was excited to move the project forward. 
STAFF PRESENTATION  
Noah Vaughan presented the preliminary plan for Santa Fe Commerce Center, located at the northeast 
corner of Lake view Avenue and Santa Fe Trail Drive near I -35 and the southern city limits. The 
approximately 18-acre development consists of four existing parcels, all zoned BP -2 with a Business 
Park Future Land Use designation. The proposal includes two industrial buildings totaling approximately 
222,550 square feet. Mr. Vaughan noted that a similar preliminary plan and final plat were approved in 
2024, but the preliminary plan expired before a final plan was submitted, and the final plat has not yet 
been recorded. The current proposal is generally consistent with the previously approved plan, with minor 
changes including a slight relocation and three-foot increase in height for Building 1, bringing it to 45 feet, 
the maximum permitted height in the BP-2 District. Other revisions include adjustments to the stormwater 
detention area, landscaping, and parking. The proposed final plat would establish two development lots 
and two tracts. Tract A would contain the stormwater detention area, while Tract B contains Lakeview 
Avenue, which currently serves as a private drive. Mr. Vaughan noted that the sidewalk easement should 
be renamed as a trail easement on the final plat, while maintaining the proposed five-foot sidewalk. The 
project also includes an outdoor storage area enclosed by an eight -foot vinyl fence, with portions 
incorporating masonry columns. The fence would be located 50 feet from the front property line, requiring 
a 50-foot deviation because it would extend beyond the building's 100- foot front setback. Mr. Vaughan 
stated that Staff recommends approval of the preliminary plan with two conditions: installation of the five-
foot public sidewalk in conjunction with construction of the first building, and provision of internal sidewalk 
connections between Lots 1 and 2 with the final plan. The preliminary plan is scheduled to be considered 
by the Governing Body on September 15th.

Commissioner Woolf asked about the applicant’s proposed fencing, noting that the applicant had 
indicated the fencing would not wrap around the north side and that the pillars would be omitted in an 
effort to retain the existing trees as much as possible. Commissioner Woolf stated that the City’s report 
showed the pillars continuing along the entire north side and asked if that was correct. Mr. Vaughan stated 
that the City’s report reflected the information provided to Staff. He explained that he was unaware of the 
proposed fencing modification until the applicant presented it and therefore deferred to the applicant for 
clarification. Scott McCullough said that he recalled the fencing design being revised, likely between the 
Planning Commission and Governing Body meetings. He believed the applicant’s representation 
reflected where the plan had ultimately landed.  
 
Commissioner Katterhenry stated that he had nothing further to add and felt the proposal closely matched 
what had been previously approved and had no problem with moving forward that same way. 
 
Commissioner Burson stated that he supported the project and plan but requested clarification regarding 
access to the properties west of the parcel. He asked whether the private drive shown on the plans would 
provide access to the northern building west of the site and whether Frey Wagner would continue to have 
ingress and egress through the private drive. Noah Vaughan clarified that the existing Lakeview Avenue, 
which is labeled as a private drive, would continue to provide access. He explained that the proposed 
additional access point would primarily serve Building 1 and the truck and trailer parking area, while 
connections would remain available to the property west of the site. Commissioner Burson noted that the 
plans appeared to show an access connection extending onto another property and asked whether the 
applicant also owned that property. Brandon Becker, the developer, explained that Lakeview Avenue had 
been abandoned and incorporated into the development as a private drive. He stated that Frey Wagner 
and the two proposed buildings were part of the same business park and that a circular vehicular access 
system would provide connectivity throughout the development. He further explained that an easement 
would allow access along the north side of Building 1 and provide egress through the north side of Lot 2. 
Commissioner Burson clarified that his concern was simply ensuring that the development was not 
proposing construction on property owned by someone else without their approval. Stephanie Sullivan 
provided an additional clarification, noting that when the project was reviewed in 2024, the project 
included a request to vacate Lakeview Avenue and “abandoned” was an incorrect term. The street was 
vacated . She clarified that Lakeview Avenue was formerly a public street but is now a private drive. 
 
Commissioner Horine asked for clarification regarding the outdoor storage and whether the proposed 
fence would be eight feet tall. Noah Vaughan confirmed that the fence would be eight feet tall. 
Commissioner Horine also asked whether the outdoor storage would remain below the top of the fence, 
which Mr. Vaughan confirmed the outdoor storage would be below the top of the fence. 
 
Commissioner Horine stated that he wanted to ensure the outdoor storage would not be highly visible 
from the development to the north at Sout hlake Office Park, noting that the development had been 
carefully designed and that he did not want the buildings to look out onto piles of stored materials. 
 
Chairman Poss stated that he agreed with Staff’s assessment and recalled that the project had previously 
generated extensive discussion regarding the fence along the north property line. He agreed with the 
applicant’s representation that the fence would wrap around the corner as proposed.
