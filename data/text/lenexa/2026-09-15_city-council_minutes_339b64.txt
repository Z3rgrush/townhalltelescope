funding for projects, training, staff, equipment, supplies, support, information systems

5. Resolution amending the Volumetric Rate Calculation Form used in determining the 
fee paid for natural gas transported within the city in accordance with natural gas 
franchise ordinances 
  Franchise ordinances with natural gas providers allow the City to receive 
compensation based on the volume of natural gas transported within the city. These 
ordinances require the Volumetric Rate Calculation Form to be updated annually. 
Adoption of this resolution will allow the City to continue collecting this fee in 2027.

building in the Lenexa Logistics Centre North Phase II development and authorized a

REGULAR AGENDA 
  
   
3. Approval of Change Order No. 2 to the agreement with CM Concrete, Inc. for the 
2026 Sidewalk & Trail Repair Program 
  This change order covers additional work to reconstruct the Prairie Star Parkway 
Trail from the I-435 Bridge west to Ridgeview Road, replacing 1.42 miles of asphalt 
trail with the City's current standard 10-foot-wide concrete trail, at a total cost of 
$822,875. 
   
Nick Arena, Municipal Services Director, said this is a change order to reconstruct 
1.42 miles of trail along Prairie Star Parkway, from the I- 435 bridge to the bridge 
just west of Ridgeview Road. He said the project would replace the failed asphalt 
trail with 10-foot-wide concrete. He explained that reconstruction of this trail had 
been scheduled for this year but was deferred to prioritize the Hickory Ridge Trail 
project because of a failed pedestrian bridge.  
 
Mr. Arena said staff obtained pricing from two contractors currently mobilized in the 
area and recommended CM Construction. He said the project would be funded by 
the Mining Tax Increment Financing District. 
 
Councilmember Nicks commented that trails are a good investment and repairing 
existing trails is a good use of funding. 
 
Councilmember Nicks made a motion to approve Item 3 and Councilmember Bell 
seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously. 
  
 
 
  
BOARD RECOMMENDATIONS 
  
 
   
9. Consideration of a preliminary plan for an industrial development with two buildings 
known as Santa Fe Commerce Center located at the northeast corner of Santa Fe 
Trail Drive & Lakeview Avenue in the BP-2, Planned Manufacturing District 
  The applicant proposes an industrial development consisting of two buildings totaling 
222,550 square feet of floor area. The buildings are speculative and potential uses 
include wholesale/warehousing, manufacturing, office, and basic industry. 
   
Stephanie Sullivan, Planning Manager, said this is a preliminary plan for Santa Fe 
Commerce Center, consisting of two industrial buildings totaling more than 222,000 
square feet along Santa Fe Trail Drive, north of 119th Street near the Olathe border. 
She said the site’s BP-2 zoning and business park future land use designation 
support the proposed development. She talked about how the plan closely resembles 
the preliminary plan approved in 2024, which expired earlier this year, with minor 
changes to Building 1, parking, and stormwater improvements. She noted that the 
private street, Lakeview Avenue, would provide access to the site. 
 
Ms. Sullivan reviewed the proposed concrete buildings, landscaping, and screening. 
She said staff supported a deviation allowing the outdoor storage area and fencing to 
extend beyond the building’s front wall, with a 50-foot setback, upgraded fencing, 
and masonry columns, and that existing trees along the northern property line would 
be maintained. She noted the same deviation had been approved previously. 
 
Ms. Sullivan said staff and the Planning Commission recommended approval subject 
to two conditions: a 5-foot-wide public sidewalk along Santa Fe Trail Drive, included 
in the final plan and constructed with the first building, and internal sidewalk 
connections between the buildings. 
 
Councilmember Denny asked why a 10-foot sidewalk was not being constructed at 
this time and Ms. Sullivan explained that the public sidewalk could be widened by five 
feet when future trail connections are developed.  
 
Councilmember Eiterich asked if the previous plan was for a commerce center. Ms. 
Sullivan confirmed that it was and that no tenants had been identified yet and that 
anticipated uses would be permitted under BP-2 zoning.  
 
Dan Finn, Phelps Engineering, said the developer was ready to proceed with 
speculative construction and intended to move forward immediately with the final 
plan and construction documents. 
 
Councilmember Bell made a motion to approve Item 9 and Councilmember Herron 
seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously. 
  
 
   
10. Consideration of a preliminary plan for an office, wholesale and warehousing, general 
and wholesale and warehousing, limited use development known as Meritex Lenexa 
Executive Park located at the northeast corner of 99th Street & Britton Street in the 
BP-1, Planned Business Park, and the BP-2, Planned Manufacturing Districts 
  The proposed development consists of four buildings totaling 233,587 square feet of 
floor area. The initial building will house mechanical equipment serving Meritex's 
subsurface tenants. 
   
Ms. Sullivan said this is a preliminary plan and four-lot preliminary plat for Meritex 
Lenexa Executive Park, a 30-acre business park with four buildings totaling more 
than 230,000 square feet northeast of 99th Street & Britton Street. She said the site is 
zoned BP-1 and BP-2 and designated for office, research, and development uses in 
the Comprehensive Plan. She said staff identified future rezoning needed to align 
zoning boundaries with Lots 1 and 2. Access would be provided from Britton Street 
and 99th Street and a new public street, Scarborough Street. 
 
Ms. Sullivan said both staff and the Planning Commission recommended approval 
with no conditions. 
 
Ms. Sullivan said the first construction phase would include Building 4, an 
approximately 27,000-square-foot equipment enclosure housing chillers and standby 
generators to serve underground tenants. She said staff classified the use as limited 
wholesale and warehousing under BP-1 zoning. She explained that the structure 
would resemble other Meritex surface buildings, be largely screened from Renner 
Boulevard by existing topography, and comply with City noise and performance 
standards. 
 
Councilmember Nicks asked about the business’ operational hours and Ms. Sullivan 
said the applicant could answer that. 
 
Councilmember Bell asked about the agriculturally zoned property east of the site on 
the zoning map and Ms. Sullivan confirmed the future land use of that area is office, 
research, and development. 
 
Mark Roseman, Meritex, said the equipment stored in the building would support the 
expansion of Lightedge, an existing Meritex underground tenant. He said the cooling 
equipment would operate continuously, while generators would run during power 
outages and maintenance testing.  
 
Councilmember Avery asked about sound modeling for the project and Mr. Roseman 
said that had not been done but affirmed that the project would comply with City 
requirements.  
 
Councilmember Herron talked about a visit he made to the site recently, saying he 
went to a larger generator yard in the area with Mr. Roseman and they did not have 
to raise their voices to talk over the noise so that is not of concern to him. 
 
Councilmember Charlton asked if this was considered a data center. Mr. Roseman 
explained that this operation is different in how it operates compared to data centers 
that have been in the news recently. He said this operation has a less dense power 
usage and is more of a colocation facility. 
 
Councilmember Charlton asked if there are state regulations for generator emissions.  
  
Councilmember Bell responded saying if they are running as emergency standby, it 
falls under the EPA standard tier 2 classification. He said the Department of Health 
and Environment regulates for air quality, but he believed it was still under the EPA’s 
purview for standby. 
 
Councilmember Herron said he met with someone at Lightedge and that the 
environmental impacts are minimal, saying they use closed water cooling systems. 
He said he saw the space where the servers will be located underground and noted 
that they will have the same performance specifications as the servers that are 
already in the underground. He said he views this as needed economic development. 
 
Council President Williamson confirmed that Lightedge is a current 20-year tenant in 
Meritex underground. She asked how many chillers and generators there would be. 
Mr. Roseman said the number of chillers and generators had not been finalized and 
that the building would connect to the underground facility through a shaft extending 
approximately 80 feet below the surface.  
 
Councilmember Eiterich asked about the intended uses of the other three proposed 
buildings and Mr. Roseman said those will be spec buildings similar to those already 
in the Meritex surface development.  
 
Councilmember Charlton asked if the building would not have a roof and Mr. 
Roseman said that was correct. 
 
Councilmember Bell made a motion to approve Item 10 and Councilmember Eiterich 
seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously.

   11. Consideration of action items regarding the Central Green Playground Project 
   
 
a. Resolution amending the fiscal year 2026-2030 Capital Improvement Program 
to increase the budget for the Central Green Playground Project to $1.7 million 
   
 
b. Bid award to Sands Construction, Inc. to construct the Central Green 
Playground for a total of $704,172.50 
   
 
c. Approval of an agreement with Crouch Recreation for the purchase and 
installation of new playground equipment for a total of $596,751 
   
 
d. Approval of an agreement with Greenfields Outdoor Fitness for the purchase of 
the Ninja Course and Fitness X-Rig for a total of $188,998 
  Updated cost estimates for the Central Green Playground Project, including 
construction of the park restrooms, associated site work, as well as the 
purchase of playground and fitness equipment, increased significantly. Staff 
recommends amending the 2026-2030 CIP to increase the project budget by 
$300,000 to $1,700,000. Additionally, staff recommends awarding a bid to 
Sands Construction, Inc. to construct the project for $704,152.50 and 
approving agreements with Crouch Recreation and Greenfields Outdoor 
Fitness to purchase the playground and fitness equipment for $596,751.00 and 
$188,998.00, respectively. 
   
Mandy Danler, Assistant Parks and Recreation Director, presented the 
proposed Central Green playground and outdoor fitness project and introduced 
Doug Pickert with Indigo Design, the project designer. She said the project 
would create a destination recreation space in City Center for users of various 
ages and abilities and fulfill priorities in the Parks Master Plan. The 
improvements would be on approximately 1 acre of the 11.6-acre park and 
include a playground, ninja obstacle course, fitness equipment, year-round 
restrooms with two family stalls, and trail connections. She noted that a trail 
surrounding the playground would allow parents to supervise children who are 
using different areas. She said a new eastern trail connection would also 
improve access for AdventHealth users and connect with the existing trail 
system. 
 
Ms. Danler outlined the $1.7 million project budget, which includes a $400,000 
AdventHealth donation approved in March. She said staff requested approval 
of a $300,000 Capital Improvement Program (CIP) budget amendment funded 
through the City Center tax increment financing (TIF) fund, a construction 
contract with Sands Construction (“Sands”) and agreements with Crouch 
Recreation (“Crouch”) and Greenfields Outdoor Fitness (“Greenfields”). The 
additional funding would cover costs associated with the year-round restrooms, 
playground features, and the eastern trail connection. 
 
Ms. Danler described the proposed playground, including an infinity tower that 
would be nearly 23 feet tall, hillside slides, climbing features, a seesaw, 
spinners, an accessible ground-level merry-go-round, and accessible swings. 
She said Crouch would supply and install the playground equipment and 
Greenfields would supply a 57-by-46-foot ninja course and an X-Rig fitness 
station designed for multiple users. She said two Apollo multi-gyms and one 
Helios unit from Outdoor-Fit would provide additional exercise options, 
including weight-assisted exercises. She noted that the Outdoor-Fit purchase 
was within existing budget authority and did not require Council approval. 
 
Ms. Danler said construction is anticipated to begin in the fall, with most 
construction in spring 2027. She said staff would establish a more detailed 
schedule after contracting with Sands and that staff also was exploring bringing 
an “American Ninja Warrior” participant to the ribbon-cutting ceremony. 
 
Councilmember Eiterich asked whether signs would explain safety rules, 
equipment use and etiquette, particularly for the hillside slides and ninja 
course. Ms. Danler said the ninja course and fitness equipment packages 
would include rules, etiquette signs, and QR codes with instructions. 
Playground supervision would primarily rely on parents, but she said staff 
would add signs if needed after opening. 
 
Councilmember Eiterich said she liked the layout’s visibility between activity 
areas and the surrounding trail’s potential use by children learning to ride 
bicycles. Ms. Danler noted that the connected trails would provide additional 
recreation opportunities throughout the park. 
 
Councilmember Charlton asked about parking and Ms. Danler said staff had 
contacted neighboring property owners about the project but had not 
designated specific parking locations. She anticipated visitors would find 
available parking as they do for swim meets and other City Center events.  
 
Councilmember Charlton asked whether the hillside slides could become 
dangerously hot and Ms. Danler said the slides were concrete and that staff 
observed no temperature concerns while testing similar slides during hot 
weather. 
 
Councilmember Bell asked whether the additional $300,000 would fund 
features excluded from the original project. Ms. Danler explained that the 
restrooms and playground features were already planned, but preliminary 
estimates did not fully reflect their costs. She said the eastern trail connection 
was added, and equipment selections, including the infinity tower, contributed 
to the additional expense.  
 
Councilmember Bell asked how children would reach the tower’s top and Ms. 
Danler said interior climbing elements would provide access between levels. 
 
Councilmember Bell asked about equipment warranties and ongoing 
maintenance. Ms. Danler said Greenfields warranties ranged from one year for 
certain ropes and suspension components to 10 years for structural 
components. Outdoor-Fit equipment and playground components also would 
carry warranties, including 10-year structural coverage. She said staff had 
discussed replacement needs with the parks facilities manager, and 
maintenance funding was available. She added that staff inspects playgrounds 
three to four times annually and would include the new fitness equipment and 
ninja course in those inspections. 
 
Councilmember Nicks asked Sands’ responsibilities and Ms. Danler said the 
contractor would complete site work, curbs, restrooms, and trail connections 
but would not install playground or fitness surfacing or equipment. She said 
City staff would install the ninja course and three fitness units, with Greenfields 
providing on-site oversight and inspection for the ninja course. Crouch would 
install the playground equipment.  
 
Councilmember Nicks asked about the difference between Sands’ bid and the 
other bids. Ms. Danler said she had verified the scope with Sands, whose 
ability to perform its own concrete work contributed to the savings. Sands’ bid 
was also closest to the engineer’s estimate. 
 
Councilmember Denny said he was happy to see Central Green transforming 
from a passive park into an active recreation destination and anticipated 
increased use by children and adults. 
 
Council President Williamson asked whether the ninja course would have an 
age restriction and said she would like to try it herself. Ms. Danler said she 
believed the signs would recommend ages 12 and older because of the 
equipment’s height, but she would verify that information with Greenfields. She 
said staff would continue discussing appropriate guidance and supervision with 
the vendor.
