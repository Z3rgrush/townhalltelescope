2. Approval of an agreement with Kimley-Horn to perform a Lenexa City Center 
Parking Study 
  As development at City Center progresses and the area becomes more heavily 
trafficked, a parking study is necessary to engage key stakeholders, inventory 
existing parking and curbside facilities, evaluate current usage, and forecast future 
parking needs. Staff proposes to contract with Kimley-Horn to develop 
recommendations for parking management, wayfinding, and future parking 
improvements, along with a phased implementation plan to guide the City’s short- 
and long-term parking strategies for a total maximum cost of $138,424. 
   
Scott McCullough, Community Development Director, said this is a proposed 
agreement with Kimley-Horn to conduct a comprehensive parking study of Lenexa 
City Center. He noted that City Center’s physical development began about 15 
years ago and continues to evolve. He said conducting the study before significant 
problems arise would help the City manage existing parking and prepare for future 
development. 
 
Mr. McCullough explained that City Center relies on a combination of public and 
private parking, including privately constructed garages with public spaces and 
private parking shared for festivals and large swim meets. Business hours, changing 
uses, and special events create varying demand across locations and times. He 
said understanding private-sector needs and opportunities for partnerships would 
help the City operate an efficient parking system. Because parking structures are 
expensive, he said the City must balance adequate capacity with the cost of building 
spaces that could remain unused. 
 
Mr. McCullough said City Center currently has sufficient parking, including during 
periods of high demand, although visitors may need to use upper garage levels or 
park farther from their destinations. Those conditions can create a perception of 
insufficient parking compared with suburban developments. He explained that City 
Center’s designation as a high-intensity downtown area supports a shared parking 
approach. The Unified Development Code (UDC) exempts City Center from the 
numerical parking requirements applied to typical suburban developments, and staff 
evaluates each project’s parking needs in the context of the surrounding district. 
He said the study would establish a baseline of existing parking conditions, forecast 
demand as vacant land develops, and identify areas with potential deficiencies or 
excess capacity. He said it would examine driver behavior, on- and off-street 
parking, regulations, enforcement and wayfinding, while considering pedestrians, 
bicyclists, people with disabilities, transit, and truck loading. Recommendations 
would include strategies and implementation steps to accommodate growth, with an 
emphasis on lower-cost management options where appropriate. 
 
Mr. McCullough said a project team representing Community Development, 
Communications, Municipal Services, and Police would coordinate with Kimley-
Horn. He added that stakeholder engagement would help identify business and user 
needs. He said the consultant would bring specialized expertise, current technology, 
national best practices, and an independent assessment of previous parking 
decisions. He said staff anticipated completing the study in 14 to 16 weeks, which 
would be followed by a presentation to the City Council. He said staff recommended 
approval of the agreement. 
 
Councilmember Herron asked how the proposed three-day occupancy count would 
account for seasonal differences, including festivals and weather. Mr. McCullough 
said parking studies generally establish an average baseline rather than focus on 
special events, which create unusually high demand and may require specific 
arrangements. He said the study would help identify areas experiencing parking 
pressure, anticipate where demand could increase, and determine opportunities to 
shift parking use through management changes. 
 
Mayor Sayers asked whether overlapping farmers market activities and swim meets 
on busy Saturdays would be examined. Mr. McCullough said staff would discuss 
festivals and overlapping events with the consultant and could adjust event 
arrangements if excessive demand occurred during particular periods. 
 
Councilmember Bell asked how the parking study would relate to traffic studies in 
City Center. Mr. McCullough said the street network was designed to accommodate 
higher-intensity development, with improvements such as planned traffic signals 
implemented when development and traffic conditions warrant them. He said 
parking needs follow a similar pattern, with development triggering additional 
demand in specific areas. He added that vehicle counts are used in both traffic and 
parking reviews. 
 
Councilmember Handley said he supported the independent review to assess 
whether perceptions about parking and decisions involving parking deviations align 
with actual conditions. He asked whether the study would be limited to the City 
Center zoning district. Mr. McCullough confirmed the study boundaries. 
Councilmember Handley said it would be important to include a weekend day in the 
occupancy count and said the findings would be valuable in evaluating future 
development. 
 
Mayor Sayers requested clarification about funding. Beccy Yocham, City Manager, 
said the study was an eligible expense under the City Center tax increment 
financing (TIF) fund and would be paid for with available, unencumbered money 
from that fund. She said no general tax dollars would be used.

8. Resolution determining the advisability of authorizing certain municipal improvements 
and establishing a special benefit district to finance them and waiving the sealed bid 
process (99th Street & Woodsonia Drive SBD) 
  The City received a petition to establish a special benefit district (SBD) to finance the 
construction of 99th Street & Woodsonia Drive, as well as related public 
improvements. The maximum estimated cost for the SBD is $10.6 million. This 
resolution will establish an SBD for this project and waive the sealed bid process for 
construction of the improvements. 
   
Sean McLaughlin, City Attorney, reviewed the process for establishing a special 
benefit district (SBD), which allows the City to levy property assessments to pay for 
public improvements. He said property owners submit a petition, the City establishes 
the district by resolution, and assessments are levied by ordinance. He explained 
that the City uses a maximum assessment approach, establishing a budget with 
sufficient contingency and reducing assessments to reflect final project costs if those 
costs are lower. He said the City holds the design and construction contracts, 
manages the project, and accepts the completed improvements. 
 
Mr. McLaughlin presented the proposed 99th Street & Woodsonia Drive SBD 
associated with the Solera mixed-use development southeast of K-7 Highway & 
Prairie Star Parkway. He said the SBD encompasses approximately 57 acres, and all 
property owners signed the petition. The estimated project cost is $10.6 million, with 
80% assessed to properties in the SBD and 20% paid by the City, consistent with 
City policy for eligible arterial and collector road improvements. Assessments would 
be paid over 20 years. He said staff recommended establishing the district and 
waiving the sealed bid process. He added that design and construction contracts and 
an ordinance levying assessments could come before the City Council as early as 
October 20th, with construction potentially beginning late in the year. 
 
Councilmember Denny said waiving the sealed bid process appeared reasonable 
because contractors already working on the development could reduce mobilization 
costs and use established unit prices. He asked whether the City had prepared an 
estimate for the public improvements and would use it to evaluate the contractor’s 
proposed pricing. Mr. McLaughlin confirmed that staff had prepared internal 
estimates, including construction costs within the $10.6 million budget. He said using 
contractors already on-site would improve coordination, while the City would retain 
the option to solicit bids if proposed costs did not meet expectations. 
 
Tim Green, City Engineer, added that Emery Sapp was performing work on the 
northern portion of Woodsonia Drive. He said cost proposals had been obtained from 
several contractors and the lower proposal was selected. He said staff reviewed the 
estimate and found it reasonable. 
 
Councilmember Denny asked whether the arrangement would resemble a 
construction manager at risk contract. Mr. McLaughlin said the City would use its 
standard public improvement contract and follow its usual design-bid-build approach. 
He explained that contractors familiar with City requirements could help expedite the 
work, while the developer would determine the contracting approach for its portion of 
the project. 
 
Mayor Sayers said the project had been a long time coming and described the 
proposed arrangement as an efficient way to complete a public improvement while 
sharing the cost. 
 
Councilmember Charlton made a motion to approve Item 8 and Councilmember Bell 
seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously. 
  
 
   
9. Approval of amendments to the Governing Body Rules of Procedure 
  The Governing Body has decided to amend its rules of procedure to allow for the 
livestreaming of City Council meetings. Additionally, the proposed amendments 
correct typographical errors and clarify a few portions of the Rules of Procedure to 
align with current practices. 
   
Mr. McLaughlin presented proposed updates to the Governing Body’s Rules of 
Procedure, which were last adopted in 2023. He said the revisions were intended to 
keep the rules clear and concise, support efficient Council business, accommodate 
livestreaming, and align the document with current practices. Other changes included 
minor edits and typographical corrections. 
 
Mr. McLaughlin said regular meetings held on the first and third Tuesdays of each 
month would be livestreamed, including the business from the floor portion. Video 
and audio recordings would continue to be retained for two years, and written 
minutes would remain the Governing Body’s official record. He said if the revisions 
were approved that evening, livestreaming would begin October 20th. 
 
Mr. McLaughlin said additional revisions would clarify that two continuances are 
allowed by right, replacing separate provisions for applicants and the City and 
accounting for items without applicants. The rules would also specify expected 
agenda packet materials, including staff memorandums, resolutions, ordinances, and 
other legislative documents. A three-minute public comment limit would be included, 
with the chair retaining discretion to adjust it during a meeting. 
 
Mr. McLaughlin explained that adopting the revisions upon their first presentation 
required a three-fourths majority of the Governing Body, or seven votes. If the 
revisions were not adopted that evening, approval at the October 20th meeting would 
require five votes. 
 
Mayor Sayers addressed emails concerning the placement of public comment on the 
agenda and said business from the floor would remain at the end of the meeting. 
Using the parking study discussion as an example, she said receiving concerns by 
email before a meeting gives staff time to prepare responses and incorporate them 
into presentations. She supported keeping that process separate from business from 
the floor, which provides an opportunity for members of the public to express their 
views. 
 
Mayor Sayers also said the City would continue allowing public comment on 
significant agenda items when appropriate, even when a public hearing is not legally 
required, consistent with past practice. 
 
Councilmember Denny made a motion to approve Item 9 and Councilmember 
Williamson seconded the motion. Motion passed 7-0.
