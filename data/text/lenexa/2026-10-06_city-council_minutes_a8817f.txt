SUBJECT: Approval of an encroachment agreement with WaterOne for the Lenexa Logistics Centre

CONTACT: Tim Green, Deputy Community Development Director 
    
DATE: October 6, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Approve an encroachment agreement with WaterOne for the Lenexa Logistics Centre North Phase II 
Britton Street Special Benefit District Project ("Project"). 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
The Britton Street Special Benefit District (SBD) was approved by the Governing Body on December 2, 
2025. The Project includes construction of 1,638 linear feet of 40-foot wide asphalt pavement with 
concrete curb and gutter, drainage facilities, streetlights, and sidewalks. Work also includes construction of 
a westbound right turn lane on College Boulevard at Britton Street, and signalization of the intersection. 
The Project will serve the new Lenexa Logistics Centre North development.  
 
WaterOne has an existing 48-inch waterline along the north side of College Boulevard. To accommodate 
the proposed Britton Street, stormwater facilities will be constructed within the private WaterOne 
easement. Therefore, an encroachment agreement is necessary.  
 
WaterOne has agreed to permit construction over its private easement to allow necessary stormwater 
construction work for the Project. General provisions of the encroachment agreement include: 
• The City is solely responsible for performing the operation, maintenance, and repair work at no cost 
to WaterOne and shall coordinate all maintenance and repairs to minimize disruption. 
 
• WaterOne shall provide no compensation to the City for any damage to the structure caused in 
connection with WaterOne's lawful use of the easement.  
 
• The City shall indemnify, defend, and hold WaterOne harmless from any claims directly or indirectly 
arising out of the City's occupation and use of the easement.  
 
• WaterOne reserves the right to request that the City make reasonable changes to the structure at 
any time during the agreement. 
This agreement is available for review in the City Clerk's office. 
  
STAFF RECOMMENDATION: 
Approve the agreement.

ITEM 2  
    
SUBJECT: Approval of an agreement with Kimley-Horn to perform a Lenexa City Center Parking Study 
    
CONTACT: Tim Green, Deputy Community Development Director 
    
DATE: October 6, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Approval of an agreement with Kimley-Horn to perform a Lenexa City Center Parking Study. 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
The success of Lenexa City Center has led to a need to better understand parking demand and 
opportunities as the area continues to develop. The City issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) to solicit 
qualified consultants to conduct a parking study for Lenexa City Center and received eight proposals. A 
selection committee comprised of staff from Community Development, the Police Department, and the City 
Manager's office evaluated the proposals and shortlisted Kimley-Horn, Walker Consultants, and DESMAN 
Parking Consultants for interviews. The selection committee recommends Kimley-Horn based on its 
experience, responses to interview questions, and familiarity with Lenexa. 
 
Staff proposes entering into an agreement with Kimley-Horn to conduct a comprehensive parking study for 
Lenexa City Center, beginning with stakeholder engagement involving City departments, partner agencies, 
major tenants, and merchants to identify parking issues and opportunities. The study will inventory existing 
on- and off-street parking, curbside facilities, amenities, signage, and regulations, and assess parking 
usage through a three-day occupancy study. Working with the City, Kimley-Horn will evaluate projected 
growth and development over two-, five-, and 10-year planning horizons to identify current and future 
parking needs, opportunities to optimize existing capacity, and potential locations for additional parking.  
 
Based on these findings, Kimley-Horn will develop recommendations to improve parking management, 
wayfinding, technology, enforcement, shared parking, and the overall parking experience for residents, 
employees, businesses, and visitors. The study will conclude with a phased implementation action plan 
outlining recommended improvements, priorities, responsible parties, resource requirements, and 
timelines to guide the City’s parking system enhancements over the short, intermediate, and long term. 
  
The anticipated goal is to complete the analysis within 14 weeks of the project kickoff meeting. The 
consultant will present final results to the City Council.  
 
The agreement uses the City's standard form and is available for review in the City Clerk's office. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS/FUNDING SOURCES: 
The maximum not-to-exceed fee is $138,424.00, which includes all labor and fees to complete the study. 
Available funds from the City Center Tax Increment Financing (TIF) District will pay for the analysis.

ITEM 3  
    
SUBJECT: Approval of an addendum with Kimley-Horn for final engineering design and construction 
documents for the Little Mill Creek Park Improvements Project 
    
CONTACT: Nick Arena, Municipal Services Director 
    
DATE: October 6, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Approve an addendum with Kimley-Horn for final engineering design and construction documents for the 
Little Mill Creek Park Improvements Project ("Project"). 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
The Project is needed to enhance safety, improve long-term infrastructure performance, and meet current 
operational and public-use demands at Little Mill Creek Park and the Municipal Services Center. Key 
reasons the Project is necessary are: 
• Improves safety and durability by reconstructing Cottonwood Street with long-lasting concrete 
pavement, reducing future maintenance needs. 
 
• Addresses high park demand by constructing a new parking lot at the north end of Little Mill Creek 
Park, increasing capacity, improving traffic flow, and alleviating frequent parking shortages. 
 
• Supports City operations by expanding the south parking lot at the Municipal Services Center, 
providing additional parking for employees and visitors. 
 
• Enhances public infrastructure with upgraded street lighting, stormwater management features, 
sidewalks, curbs, and other safety improvements. 
In April 2026, the City Council approved an agreement with Kimley-Horn for preliminary engineering 
services for the Project. At that time, staff advised that upon approval and completion of the preliminary 
design, a separate agreement for final design and construction documents would be presented to the City 
Council for consideration. 
 
Preliminary design evaluated alternatives for the parking lot configurations and Cottonwood Street 
roadway width and alignment. Based on this work and coordination with Parks & Recreation staff, the 
selected improvements include an additional 84-space parking lot serving Little Mill Creek Park, 33 
additional parking spaces at the Municipal Services Center, and roadway and stormwater improvements 
along Cottonwood Street. The Project will also upgrade several utility service lines, including gas, water, 
and electrical services, serving the Municipal Services Center. 
 
The proposed amendment will advance the Project through final engineering design and construction 
document preparation. Services will include roadway and parking lot design, grading and stormwater 
Page 11
 
 
improvements, utility coordination, lighting, landscaping, pavement markings and signage, pedestrian 
accessibility, mulch and topsoil bin relocation, traffic control, and construction sequencing. 
 
Final design is anticipated to be completed in early 2027, followed by bidding and construction beginning 
in spring 2027. 
 
This amendment uses the City's standard form and is available for review in the City Clerk's office. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS/FUNDING SOURCES: 
This Project is budgeted in the 2026-2030 Capital Improvement Program (Project No. 60032). The total fee 
for final engineering design, bidding, and related services is $205,500. The previously approved 
preliminary engineering agreement was $94,500.00, bringing total engineering services authorized for the 
Project to $300,000. While not final, the Project's construction cost is currently estimated at $3.5 million.

Approve the addendum.

SUBJECT: Approval of an addendum to the Master Services Agreement with ConvergeOne, Inc. for 
Information Technology Services 
    
CONTACT: Jerry Swingle, Information Technology Director 
Anthony Sitzes, Assistant Information Technology Director 
    
DATE: October 6, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Approve an addendum to the Master Services Agreement (MSA) with ConvergeOne, Inc. ("ConvergeOne") 
for Information Technology (IT) Services. 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
The City's IT systems are crucial to conducting business quickly, efficiently, and securely. Protecting these 
systems from cybersecurity threats requires continuous monitoring, proactive maintenance, and rapid 
response capabilities. By outsourcing these functions to a specialized provider, the City strengthens its 
security posture and responds more effectively to evolving cybersecurity risks. 
 
In 2023, the City entered into an MSA with ConvergeOne to purchase hardware, software, and 
professional services related to IT equipment and systems. Like cooperative purchasing agreements the 
City regularly uses for other products and services, ConvergeOne brokers and implements major IT 
services, then provides support or manages them on the City's behalf. The MSA establishes baseline 
terms and conditions for future transactions, streamlining procurement and reducing the need to 
repeatedly negotiate standard provisions within each subcontract. The MSA automatically renews and has 
no direct cost; expenses arise only when the City orders products or services. 
 
Purchases are made using individual Solution Quotes, which function as addenda under the MSA by 
identifying the service-specific pricing, terms, and conditions of use. Each Solution Quote operates as an 
add-on issued under the MSA framework to authorize and document each hardware or service purchase 
from ConvergeOne. 
 
This proposed addendum covers the purchase, replacement, and improvement of the City's existing 
managed cybersecurity services. The addendum is for an initial term of one year, with up to four additional 
one-year extensions. Importantly, this addendum provides consistent pricing with no annual increase 
during the five-year term. 
 
This addendum is available for review in the City Clerk's office. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS/FUNDING SOURCES: 
The cost of the managed cybersecurity services is $147,299.71 in fiscal year 2027 and $143,519.71 
annually for each of the four optional one-year extensions. The Information Technology Department’s 
annual operating budget includes sufficient funds to cover the addendum's projected costs.

CONTACT: Scott McCullough, Community Development Director 
Angel 
Whitaker, Community Standards Supervisor 
    
DATE: October 6, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Adopt a resolution creating the Neighborhood Beautification Grant Pilot Program. 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
City staff often receives inquiries from homeowners associations (HOAs) about commonly owned physical 
improvements, such as neighborhood identification signs, fencing, retaining walls, etc., in need of repair or 
replacement. These issues occur in neighborhoods with established, active HOAs and in many 
neighborhoods without an HOA. The City has a popular and beneficial Exterior Grant Program to help with 
repairs for individual homes, but it does not have a program for neighborhood-wide improvements.  
 
In response to inquiries about whether the City could assist in these situations, City staff reviewed 
neighborhood beautification grant programs implemented locally and nationally to determine whether such 
programs could benefit Lenexa neighborhoods. Many of these programs provide modest City-funded 
grants for public-facing improvements that enhance neighborhood identity and encourage community pride 
and engagement.  
 
At its August 11, 2026 Committee of the Whole meeting, the City Council reviewed the proposed 
framework for a Neighborhood Beautification Grant Pilot Program ("Pilot Program"), which incorporates 
successful elements from the programs researched, along with additional features to best support 
Lenexa’s neighborhoods and residents. The proposed Pilot Program incorporates direction provided at 
that meeting and generally includes: 
• $1,000 minimum project investment 
 
• $2,000 maximum grant award per neighborhood with eligibility occurring once every three years 
(i.e. 2027, 2030) 
 
• Required 50% neighborhood match in funding and/or labor 
 
• Funding the repair or replacement of existing physical neighborhood assets, but not new 
improvements 
 
• Prioritizing highly visible public-facing improvements 
 
Page 21
 
 
• Excluding individually owned physical assets and neighborhood pools from grant eligibility 
Procedurally, the Pilot Program would mirror the Exterior Grant Program's timelines and administrative 
process, with applications opening in spring 2027. 
 
Staff recommends approving the Pilot Program for one year, including a review of initial results and 
recommended changes before funding the program in future years. 
  
FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS/FUNDING SOURCES: 
The proposed Pilot Program budget for 2027 is $20,000, with individual grants capped at $2,000.

b. The age of neighborhood, with older neighborhoods receiving priority.

Development Director or their designee.

written notice that their application has been pre-approved or denied for the 
program. 
4. The applicant or their contractor shall obtain a building permit (if applicable) and 
pay any associated plan review and/or permit fees. 
 
5. The applicant will have 120 days to complete the work, complete the necessary 
inspections, and provide the required documentation to process the grant. 
 
a. The Community Development Director or their designee may waive the 120-
day deadline for completion of work for good cause and at their sole 
discretion. 
b. Once the work is complete the applicant shall contact the Community 
Development Department for an inspection to ensure conformity with 
applicable Lenexa City Code and regulations.

PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
The City received a petition from the property owners of 100% of the land within the proposed special 
benefit district (SBD) to construct 99th Street and Woodsonia Drive in the southeast corner of K-7 Highway 
& Prairie Star Parkway, as well as related public improvements. The proposed SBD includes 57.37 acres. 
The proposed improvements are described in more detail in the petition and resolution, but generally 
include the construction of Woodsonia Drive commencing at Prairie Star Parkway and extending south 
approximately 2,562 feet, the construction of 1,125 feet of 99th Street along the southern border of the 
SBD, and construction of associated landscaping, stormwater facilities, and similar municipal 
improvements (referred to as the “Improvement”). The SBD is part of the Solera Mixed-Use Development 
which includes retail, commercial, and residential uses over a 75 acre area at the southeast corner of K-7 
Highway & Prairie Star Parkway. 
 
The maximum cost of the Improvement is estimated to be $10.6 million. In accordance with City policy for 
arterial and collector road SBD assessments, the City is participating in the cost of the Improvement, which 
will be assessed 20% to the City and 80% to the property within the SBD. The assessments will be divided 
equally per square foot over the assessable area in the SBD and excludes right of way, easements, parks, 
and other common area. 
 
The term of the SBD shall be 20 years. Because this is a maximum assessment SBD, the maximum 
assessments will be levied before construction commences and then adjusted downward (if applicable) 
and certified after construction is completed. After construction of the Improvement is completed, if the 
cost of the Improvement is less than the estimated maximum cost of $10.6 million, then the savings will 
accrue: 80% to the property within the SBD and 20% to the City. 
 
A portion of the City's share of the Improvement costs will be reimbursed through excise tax generated 
from property within the SBD. The petitioner will benefit from the Improvement, but the Improvement will 
also benefit other property which is not included in the SBD. In accordance with state law, the petitioner 
acknowledges this and agrees to be 100% responsible for the assessments on the property.

hold both the engineering and construction contracts. Construction is estimated to start later this year.

ITEM 9  
    
SUBJECT: Approval of amendments to the Governing Body Rules of Procedure 
    
CONTACT: Sean McLaughlin, City Attorney 
    
DATE: October 6, 2026 
     
  
ACTION NEEDED: 
Approve amendments to the Governing Body Rules of Procedure. 
  
PROJECT BACKGROUND/DESCRIPTION: 
The Governing Body adopts its own Rules of Procedure to assist in efficiently conducting the City's 
business and to provide general information on the roles and responsibilities of staff. The Governing Body 
last updated its Rules of Procedure in 2023. The Governing Body has decided to allow livestreaming of 
Regular City Council meetings, which requires revisions to the Rules of Procedure. Additionally, staff has 
corrected some typographical errors and clarified a few portions of the Rules of Procedure to align with 
current practices. 
 
The amendments include the following: 
• Livestreaming - Regularly scheduled City Council meetings and Special Meetings (when practical) 
may be livestreamed and recorded. Livestreaming will cease when the meeting closes for executive 
session and when the meeting ends. Video recordings will remain available on the City's website 
after the meeting concludes and will be retained for two years. 
 
• Continuances - Instead of specifically assigning one by-right continuance each to the City and an 
applicant, each item may now simply be continued twice in total before the City Council must take 
specific action to do so, which matches the City's current practice. 
 
• Packet Materials - Identified the common documents that should be included in the Meeting Packet, 
including staff memos, resolutions, ordinances, and other legislative documents. 
 
• Public Comment - Sets a three-minute time limit per individual for public comment, with the Chair 
having discretion to determine a different time limit. 
Pursuant to the Rules of Procedure, to adopt the amendments at its October 6 meeting, a three-fourths 
majority of the Governing Body (7 members) must vote in favor because it is the first Regular City Council 
meeting at which the new Rules of Procedure are formally considered. Otherwise, the Governing Body 
may adopt the new Rules of Procedure by a simple majority (5 members) at a subsequent Regular City 
Council meeting. If the Governing Body adopts the Rules of Procedure at its October 6 meeting, the 
updated Rules of Procedure, including the livestreaming provisions, will take effect at the October 20, 2026

3. Approval of Change Order No. 2 to the agreement with CM Concrete, Inc. for the 
2026 Sidewalk & Trail Repair Program 
  This change order covers additional work to reconstruct the Prairie Star Parkway 
Trail from the I-435 Bridge west to Ridgeview Road, replacing 1.42 miles of asphalt 
trail with the City's current standard 10-foot-wide concrete trail, at a total cost of 
$822,875.

  The applicant proposes an industrial development consisting of two buildings totaling

She said the site’s BP-2 zoning and business park future land use designation 
support the proposed development. She talked about how the plan closely resembles 
the preliminary plan approved in 2024, which expired earlier this year, with minor

to two conditions: a 5-foot-wide public sidewalk along Santa Fe Trail Drive, included 
in the final plan and constructed with the first building, and internal sidewalk 
connections between the buildings.

feet when future trail connections are developed.

anticipated uses would be permitted under BP-2 zoning.

speculative construction and intended to move forward immediately with the final 
plan and construction documents.

10. Consideration of a preliminary plan for an office, wholesale and warehousing, general 
and wholesale and warehousing, limited use development known as Meritex Lenexa 
Executive Park located at the northeast corner of 99th Street & Britton Street in the 
BP-1, Planned Business Park, and the BP-2, Planned Manufacturing Districts 
  The proposed development consists of four buildings totaling 233,587 square feet of 
floor area. The initial building will house mechanical equipment serving Meritex's 
subsurface tenants. 
   
Ms. Sullivan said this is a preliminary plan and four-lot preliminary plat for Meritex 
Lenexa Executive Park, a 30-acre business park with four buildings totaling more 
Page 48
than 230,000 square feet northeast of 99th Street & Britton Street. She said the site is 
zoned BP-1 and BP-2 and designated for office, research, and development uses in 
the Comprehensive Plan. She said staff identified future rezoning needed to align 
zoning boundaries with Lots 1 and 2. Access would be provided from Britton Street 
and 99th Street and a new public street, Scarborough Street. 
 
Ms. Sullivan said both staff and the Planning Commission recommended approval 
with no conditions. 
 
Ms. Sullivan said the first construction phase would include Building 4, an 
approximately 27,000-square-foot equipment enclosure housing chillers and standby 
generators to serve underground tenants. She said staff classified the use as limited 
wholesale and warehousing under BP-1 zoning. She explained that the structure 
would resemble other Meritex surface buildings, be largely screened from Renner 
Boulevard by existing topography, and comply with City noise and performance 
standards. 
 
Councilmember Nicks asked about the business’ operational hours and Ms. Sullivan 
said the applicant could answer that. 
 
Councilmember Bell asked about the agriculturally zoned property east of the site on 
the zoning map and Ms. Sullivan confirmed the future land use of that area is office, 
research, and development. 
 
Mark Roseman, Meritex, said the equipment stored in the building would support the 
expansion of Lightedge, an existing Meritex underground tenant. He said the cooling 
equipment would operate continuously, while generators would run during power 
outages and maintenance testing.  
 
Councilmember Avery asked about sound modeling for the project and Mr. Roseman 
said that had not been done but affirmed that the project would comply with City 
requirements.  
 
Councilmember Herron talked about a visit he made to the site recently, saying he 
went to a larger generator yard in the area with Mr. Roseman and they did not have 
to raise their voices to talk over the noise so that is not of concern to him. 
 
Councilmember Charlton asked if this was considered a data center. Mr. Roseman 
explained that this operation is different in how it operates compared to data centers 
that have been in the news recently. He said this operation has a less dense power 
usage and is more of a colocation facility. 
 
Councilmember Charlton asked if there are state regulations for generator emissions.  
  
Councilmember Bell responded saying if they are running as emergency standby, it 
falls under the EPA standard tier 2 classification. He said the Department of Health 
and Environment regulates for air quality, but he believed it was still under the EPA’s 
purview for standby.

   11. Consideration of action items regarding the Central Green Playground Project 
   
 
a. Resolution amending the fiscal year 2026-2030 Capital Improvement Program 
to increase the budget for the Central Green Playground Project to $1.7 million 
   
 
b. Bid award to Sands Construction, Inc. to construct the Central Green 
Playground for a total of $704,172.50 
   
 
c. Approval of an agreement with Crouch Recreation for the purchase and 
installation of new playground equipment for a total of $596,751 
   
 
d. Approval of an agreement with Greenfields Outdoor Fitness for the purchase of 
the Ninja Course and Fitness X-Rig for a total of $188,998 
  Updated cost estimates for the Central Green Playground Project, including 
construction of the park restrooms, associated site work, as well as the 
purchase of playground and fitness equipment, increased significantly. Staff 
recommends amending the 2026-2030 CIP to increase the project budget by 
$300,000 to $1,700,000. Additionally, staff recommends awarding a bid to 
Sands Construction, Inc. to construct the project for $704,152.50 and 
approving agreements with Crouch Recreation and Greenfields Outdoor 
Fitness to purchase the playground and fitness equipment for $596,751.00 and 
$188,998.00, respectively. 
   
Mandy Danler, Assistant Parks and Recreation Director, presented the 
proposed Central Green playground and outdoor fitness project and introduced 
Doug Pickert with Indigo Design, the project designer. She said the project 
Page 50
would create a destination recreation space in City Center for users of various 
ages and abilities and fulfill priorities in the Parks Master Plan. The 
improvements would be on approximately 1 acre of the 11.6-acre park and 
include a playground, ninja obstacle course, fitness equipment, year-round 
restrooms with two family stalls, and trail connections. She noted that a trail 
surrounding the playground would allow parents to supervise children who are 
using different areas. She said a new eastern trail connection would also 
improve access for AdventHealth users and connect with the existing trail 
system. 
 
Ms. Danler outlined the $1.7 million project budget, which includes a $400,000 
AdventHealth donation approved in March. She said staff requested approval 
of a $300,000 Capital Improvement Program (CIP) budget amendment funded 
through the City Center tax increment financing (TIF) fund, a construction 
contract with Sands Construction (“Sands”) and agreements with Crouch 
Recreation (“Crouch”) and Greenfields Outdoor Fitness (“Greenfields”). The 
additional funding would cover costs associated with the year-round restrooms, 
playground features, and the eastern trail connection. 
 
Ms. Danler described the proposed playground, including an infinity tower that 
would be nearly 23 feet tall, hillside slides, climbing features, a seesaw, 
spinners, an accessible ground-level merry-go-round, and accessible swings. 
She said Crouch would supply and install the playground equipment and 
Greenfields would supply a 57-by-46-foot ninja course and an X-Rig fitness 
station designed for multiple users. She said two Apollo multi-gyms and one 
Helios unit from Outdoor-Fit would provide additional exercise options, 
including weight-assisted exercises. She noted that the Outdoor-Fit purchase 
was within existing budget authority and did not require Council approval. 
 
Ms. Danler said construction is anticipated to begin in the fall, with most 
construction in spring 2027. She said staff would establish a more detailed 
schedule after contracting with Sands and that staff also was exploring bringing 
an “American Ninja Warrior” participant to the ribbon-cutting ceremony. 
 
Councilmember Eiterich asked whether signs would explain safety rules, 
equipment use and etiquette, particularly for the hillside slides and ninja 
course. Ms. Danler said the ninja course and fitness equipment packages 
would include rules, etiquette signs, and QR codes with instructions. 
Playground supervision would primarily rely on parents, but she said staff 
would add signs if needed after opening. 
 
Councilmember Eiterich said she liked the layout’s visibility between activity 
areas and the surrounding trail’s potential use by children learning to ride 
bicycles. Ms. Danler noted that the connected trails would provide additional 
recreation opportunities throughout the park. 
 
Councilmember Charlton asked about parking and Ms. Danler said staff had 
contacted neighboring property owners about the project but had not 
Page 51
designated specific parking locations. She anticipated visitors would find 
available parking as they do for swim meets and other City Center events.  
 
Councilmember Charlton asked whether the hillside slides could become 
dangerously hot and Ms. Danler said the slides were concrete and that staff 
observed no temperature concerns while testing similar slides during hot 
weather. 
 
Councilmember Bell asked whether the additional $300,000 would fund 
features excluded from the original project. Ms. Danler explained that the 
restrooms and playground features were already planned, but preliminary 
estimates did not fully reflect their costs. She said the eastern trail connection 
was added, and equipment selections, including the infinity tower, contributed 
to the additional expense.  
 
Councilmember Bell asked how children would reach the tower’s top and Ms. 
Danler said interior climbing elements would provide access between levels. 
 
Councilmember Bell asked about equipment warranties and ongoing 
maintenance. Ms. Danler said Greenfields warranties ranged from one year for 
certain ropes and suspension components to 10 years for structural 
components. Outdoor-Fit equipment and playground components also would 
carry warranties, including 10-year structural coverage. She said staff had 
discussed replacement needs with the parks facilities manager, and 
maintenance funding was available. She added that staff inspects playgrounds 
three to four times annually and would include the new fitness equipment and 
ninja course in those inspections. 
 
Councilmember Nicks asked Sands’ responsibilities and Ms. Danler said the 
contractor would complete site work, curbs, restrooms, and trail connections 
but would not install playground or fitness surfacing or equipment. She said 
City staff would install the ninja course and three fitness units, with Greenfields 
providing on-site oversight and inspection for the ninja course. Crouch would 
install the playground equipment.  
 
Councilmember Nicks asked about the difference between Sands’ bid and the 
other bids. Ms. Danler said she had verified the scope with Sands, whose 
ability to perform its own concrete work contributed to the savings. Sands’ bid 
was also closest to the engineer’s estimate. 
 
Councilmember Denny said he was happy to see Central Green transforming 
from a passive park into an active recreation destination and anticipated 
increased use by children and adults. 
 
Council President Williamson asked whether the ninja course would have an 
age restriction and said she would like to try it herself. Ms. Danler said she 
believed the signs would recommend ages 12 and older because of the 
equipment’s height, but she would verify that information with Greenfields. She 
Page 52
said staff would continue discussing appropriate guidance and supervision with 
the vendor.

1. AUTHORITY AND DEFINITIONS:  
1.1 Authority:  
The Governing Body shall establish its own Rules of Procedure to assist them in the general conduct of 
City business. The following rules shall be in effect upon adoption by the Governing Body and until such 
time they are amended, or new rules are adopted in the manner provided by this policy.  
1.2 Application:  
The Rules of Procedure shall be applicable to all members of the Governing Body and pertinent staff 
members in their role of conducting City business. It is the City's intent to comply with the Americans with 
Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA) and all applicable federal, state, and local laws. If any provisions of this policy 
conflict with the ADA, the City will provide reasonable accommodation for all qualified persons. 
1.3 Definitions and Interpretation:  
(a) Definitions and Rules of Construction: 
(1) Chair: When the term Chair is referred to herein, it shall mean the Mayor, Council 
President, or Acting Council President pursuant to Lenexa City Code Section 1-2-A-3. 
(2) Governing Body: The eight (8) City Council members-elect plus the Mayor, collectively. 
(3) City Council: The eight (8) City Council members-elect. 
(4) Regular Meeting: When the term Regular Meeting is referred to in this document, it 
shall mean City Council meetings held pursuant to Lenexa City Code Section 1-4-B-1. 
(5) Special Meeting: When the term Special Meeting is referred to in this publication, it 
shall mean Special City Council Meetings held pursuant to Lenexa City Code Section 1-4-
B-2. 
(6) Committee of the Whole Meeting: When the term Committee of the Whole is referred 
to in this publication, it shall mean Committee of the Whole Meetings held pursuant to 
Lenexa City Code Article 1-4-D. No binding action shall be taken at a Committee of the 
Whole Meeting. The Committee of the Whole may make recommendations to be 
considered by the Governing Body at a future Regular Meeting or Special Meeting.

other non-social gatherings of the Governing Body where business of the City will be 
discussed.

mean a Regular Meeting, Special Meeting, Committee of the Whole, or Work Session 
unless otherwise provided for by these Rules of Procedure.

2. GENERAL RULES:  
2.1 Applicability:  
This document shall govern all Regular Meetings, Special Meetings, Committee of the Whole proceedings, 
and Work Sessions.  
2.2 Meetings to be Public:  
All Meetings of the Governing Body shall be open to the public in accordance with K.S.A. 75-4317, et seq. 
as amended (the “Kansas Open Meetings Act”), except for the purpose of having an Executive Session 
pursuant to applicable law.  
2.3 Quorum:   
A majority of the members-elect of the Governing Body shall constitute a quorum and be necessary for 
the transaction of business at a Meeting. 
2.4 Attendance:  
Governing Body members should inform the City Clerk and City Manager if they are unable to attend any 
Meeting. 
2.5 Attendance via Electronic Participation Prohibited:  
Members of the Governing Body shall not be allowed to use telephonic or other electronic means, such 
as video conferencing, to establish attendance to conduct Meetings. 
2.6 Cancellation of a Meeting:  
The Mayor may cancel a Regular Meeting when it falls on a federal holiday or local election day.  
Cancellations may also be made by the Mayor or at the request of three (3) City Council members when 
special circumstances exist. The City Manager may, due to inclement weather or other emergency 
situation, cancel a Regular Meeting or Special Meeting. Cancellation of Committee of the Whole and Work 
Sessions may be made by the City Manager. 
2.7 Executive Sessions:  
Executive Sessions are closed to the public and are held in accordance with the provisions of the Kansas 
Open Meetings Act. A motion must be passed to recess into Executive Session. The duration of the 
Executive Session must be stated, and discussion must be limited to the subjects identified in the motion.
