3. Approval of an addendum with Kimley-Horn for final engineering design and 
construction documents for the Little Mill Creek Park Improvements Project 
  Approved 
  The project will reconstruct Cottonwood Street with concrete pavement, add 
parking at Little Mill Creek Park and the Municipal Services Center, and 
complete associated utility, stormwater, lighting, and site improvements. 
Preliminary engineering is complete, and staff recommends approval of an 
amendment with Kimley-Horn for final engineering design and preparation of 
construction documents for a total cost of $205,500. 
    
 
   
4. Approval of an addendum to the Master Services Agreement with 
ConvergeOne, Inc. for Information Technology Services 
  Approved 
  The City's Information Technology Services provide core operational 
functions and services to staff and the public. The proposed addendum is to 
purchase, replace, and improve the City’s existing managed cybersecurity 
services. The addendum for these services is for an initial one-year term, 
with up to four additional one-year renewal terms. The initial term costs 
$147,299.71.

6. Resolution creating the Neighborhood Beautification Grant Pilot Program 
  Resolution 2026-053 
  Homeowners associations (HOAs) often inquire about repairs for shared 
items like signs, fences, and walls. Staff has developed a pilot program to 
address this. Key features include a focus on repairing existing assets, 
prioritizing visible improvements, excluding pools, and requiring a 50% cost 
match from neighborhoods. The 2027 budget for the program is proposed at 
$20,000, with individual grants capped at $2,000.

2. Approval of an agreement with Kimley-Horn to perform a Lenexa City Center 
Parking Study 
  Approved 
  As development at City Center progresses and the area becomes more 
heavily trafficked, a parking study is necessary to engage key stakeholders, 
inventory existing parking and curbside facilities, evaluate current usage, and 
forecast future parking needs. Staff proposes to contract with Kimley-Horn to 
develop recommendations for parking management, wayfinding, and future 
parking improvements, along with a phased implementation plan to guide the 
City’s short- and long-term parking strategies for a total maximum cost of 
$138,424.

8. Resolution determining the advisability of authorizing certain municipal 
improvements and establishing a special benefit district to finance them and 
waiving the sealed bid process (99th Street & Woodsonia Drive SBD) 
  Resolution 2026-054 
  The City received a petition to establish a special benefit district (SBD) to 
finance the construction of 99th Street & Woodsonia Drive, as well as related 
public improvements. The maximum estimated cost for the SBD is $10.6 
million. This resolution will establish an SBD for this project and waive the 
sealed bid process for construction of the improvements. 
    
 
   
9. Approval of amendments to the Governing Body Rules of Procedure 
  Approved 
  The Governing Body has decided to amend its rules of procedure to allow for 
the livestreaming of City Council meetings. Additionally, the proposed 
amendments correct typographical errors and clarify a few portions of the 
Rules of Procedure to align with current practices.
