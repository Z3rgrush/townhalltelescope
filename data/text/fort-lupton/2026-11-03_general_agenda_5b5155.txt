AM 2026-158 An Ordinance Imposing a Temporary Suspension on the Submission, 
Acceptance, Processing, and Approval of Applications for the Establishment or Expansion 
of Data Center Facilities with A Demand of Five (5) Megawatts (MW) or Greater of 
Electrical Capacity Within the City of Fort Lupton and Declaring an Emergency 
 
Data centers are specialized facilities for storing, managing, and disseminating data and 
information, including housing IT infrastructure, computer systems and associated components, 
and training artificial intelligence models. Data centers vary considerably in purpose 
(telecommunications, cloud computing, AI model training, cryptocurrency mining, etc.), 
customer structure (colocation vs. hyperscale), and scale (small-scale to utility-scale). A data 
center facility comprises digital infrastructure as well as physical structures, including cooling 
equipment, power and backup power generation facilities, energy transmission infrastructure, 
stormwater facilities, administrative offices, and parking. Potential community impacts vary

For the purposes of this Ordinance, a "Data Center" is broadly defined as any facility meeting 
any of the following criteria:

information technology (IT) equipment necessary for the provision of off-site or on-site 
digital services that has a demand of five (5) megawatts (MW) or electrical capacity or

(b) A facility where the primary use is the storage, processing, management, or

HDR biologists will conduct a field delineation of waters of the U.S. within the project’s limits

threatened and endangered species (including species listed as candidates, proposed for listing,

Drilling Methods 
PDI boreholes will be advanced using hollow stem augers (4.25-inch inside diameter) with split 
spoon sampling (2-foot sample length) every 5 feet through the upper unconsolidated soils to 
bedrock. If auger refusal occurs prior to target depths in bedrock, an additional borehole will be 
advanced approximately 5 feet away from the refusal location. If auger refusal still occurs, 
drilling using solid stem augers or air-rotary methods will be contemplated.  
An on-site HDR geologist or geotechnical engineer will log each boring using the Unified Soil 
Classification System (USCS) and select sample collection intervals. Select samples will be 
submitted to a laboratory for geotechnical testing depending on subsurface conditions, sample 
quality, and the judgement of the on-site geologist or geotechnical engineer.  
Cuttings from drilling will either be used to backfill the boreholes/temporary wells or be spread 
near finished borings or around the site as needed to create minimal disturbance of existing 
established grades.  
SPT and Soil Sampling Methods 
As each boring is advanced, split-spoon samplers will be driven into the various soil strata every 
five-feet using a 140-pound hammer falling 30 inches. The number of blows required to advance 
the sampler in each 6-inch increment will be recorded. When using the SPT (ASTM D1586 
2022) split-spoon sampler, the total number of blows required to advance the sampler in the 
second and third 6-inch increments is the penetration resistance (N-value). The N-value gives a 
relative density (coarse grained soils) or stiffness (fine grained soils) and correlates SPT values 
to number of blows, N60 as developed by Terzaghi and Peck (Terzaghi, Peck, and Mesri 1967). A 
larger diameter modified California split spoon sampler may be used in lieu of the SPT split 
spoon sampler to allow for collection of relatively undisturbed samples with brass columnar 
inserts in the sampler. Corrections will be made for the larger diameter modified California 
sampler as applicable to allow for consistency of N-values. Following the field test, the sampler 
will be removed, and the subsurface material will be logged. 
For each boring location, the N-value data; soil and geology characteristics; total and refusal 
depths; sample location, type, and depth; and observed groundwater depths (if applicable); and 
other notable observations (fractures, discoloration, sediment deposits, etc.) will be documented 
on field forms. Photos of samples will be taken to document each boring. 
Samples selected for laboratory testing will be labelled with project name, sample ID, boring ID, 
date, and depth (as per ASTM D2488). The samples will then be collected into moisture-proof 
plastic bags or containers and transported to Advanced Terra Testing in Lakewood, Colorado. 
The exact depth location of samples to be laboratory tested, as well as the exact tests to be 
performed on each sample, will be determined by the on-site geologist or geotechnical engineer

Aquifer Testing 
Following installation of the temporary monitoring wells, HDR will conduct aquifer testing for 
each of the two proposed extraction trench alignments. Temporary pressure transducers will be 
installed in up to eight temporary monitoring wells and existing BURNCO piezometers to 
continuously monitor groundwater levels throughout the testing period. Exact monitoring 
locations will be selected based on geology and hydrogeology encountered during PDI boring 
drilling and temporary well installation. Groundwater elevations will be recorded for a minimum 
of 12 hours prior to the start of pumping to establish baseline conditions. Groundwater elevation 
monitoring will continue throughout the pumping test and for an extended recovery period 
following completion of pumping activities. Groundwater levels will be monitored for up to 6 
hours during active pumping and for up to 12 hours after pumping ceases, resulting in a total 
monitoring duration of up to 30 hours.  
Pumping tests will be performed using a temporary well equipped with a pump capable of 
producing flow rates greater than 50 gpm. The pumping test will consist of up to three 
progressive pumping-rate steps conducted over a period of up to 6 hours. Pumping rates, 
drawdown responses, and recovery characteristics will be evaluated to estimate aquifer 
properties and assess the anticipated performance of the proposed extraction trench system. 
Groundwater produced during testing will be managed on site and will be discharged directly 
into the existing wellfield conveyance system.  
To further evaluate potential interactions between the proposed extraction trench and the existing 
water supply infrastructure, additional pumping of nearby production wells may be conducted 
for up to 4 hours during testing. Groundwater levels measured within the temporary monitoring 
wells and available observation points will be used to assess interference effects and determine 
the degree of hydraulic connectivity between the proposed extraction system and existing 
pumping wells.  
Upon completion of aquifer testing activities, pressure transducers will be removed from the 
monitoring wells and observation points. Temporary monitoring wells will subsequently be 
abandoned in accordance with applicable DWR requirements. Well materials will be removed, 
and boreholes will be backfilled with native materials and sealed with bentonite at the ground

conditions.
