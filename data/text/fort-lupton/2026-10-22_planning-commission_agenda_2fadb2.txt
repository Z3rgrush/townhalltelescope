P2026-001 A RESOLUTION OF THE PLANNING COMMISSION OF FORT LUPTON RECOMMENDING TO CITY

Planner Zachary presented the item submitted by Western Midstream for a Special Use Permit for two 
buried gathering pipelines. Both pipelines, one gas gathering pipeline and one oil gathering pipeline, will 
carry material to existing infrastructure. He explained that the lines will originate outside of city limits at 
the O&G facility and showed an aerial of the pipeline routes, which will run through seven parcels within 
city limits. The project runs through parcels zoned I-2 (Heavy Industrial) and covers agricultural and 
industrial uses, including the Basalite plant, the Intermountain trailer project, mining operations, water 
reservoir, and agricultural parcels. He stated that the Comprehensive Plan designates the general area as 
industrial but pointed out that the project will be underground and not visible to the public.

• The length of the underground project

• Accountability for any pipe bursts requiring repair and the necessary cleanup

DRAFT Future Land Use Category Descriptions 
1. Agriculture 
a. This designation allows for agricultural and related uses, as well as existing rural 
residential homes associated with larger lots. This designation aims to preserve existing 
productive agricultural properties for the purposes of farming, grazing and raising 
animals, while also promoting agritainment, agritourism, and agriculture-supported 
commercial operations like community supported agriculture (CSA’s), larger farmstands, 
and farm markets. By aiming to preserve agricultural uses, this designation also aims to 
direct development to other areas to promote a consistent agricultural character 
throughout the broader Fort Lupton area. 
i. This category is expanded to more greatly identify and emphasize commercial 
agricultural uses. Staff intends to separate “Agriculture” and “Rural 
Residential” to better preserve and identify key agricultural areas and to also 
identify existing rural residential areas.  
2. Rural Residential

a. This designation identifies existing rural residential subdivisions and aims to allow low-
impact commercial and industrial uses that are clearly sub-ordinate to the principal 
residence. Fort Lupton should restrict the construction of new rural residential 
subdivisions that are not part of a more cohesive mixed-use and mixed-density 
subdivision, to promote efficient expansion of utilities and services, and promote a 
resilient tax base. 
i. The identification of “low-impact commercial and industrial uses that are 
clearly subordinate to the principal residence” aims to put further and clearer

Staff still aims to promote home-occupancy type businesses and

and less industrial storage yard type uses. 
ii. Staff also aims to reduce the proliferation of rural residential subdivisions as 
they often do not result in the “rural charm” character that they are thought 
of, and they place an excessive burden on city resources compared to other 
uses. Rural residential lots may be allowed as a part of a master subdivision 
that has a variety of housing types and lot sizes, to promote diverse housing 
types across the city.

1 INTRODUCTION 
Purpose of Housing Action Plan 
This Housing Action Plan serves as Fort Lupton's roadmap for addressing identified local housing 
needs and expanding access to housing across all income levels. Required under Senate Bill 24-
174 and developed in accordance with the requirements of C.R.S. 24-32-3705, the plan builds on 
the findings of Fort Lupton’s Housing Needs Assessment (HNA) by translating data and analysis 
into concrete goals, strategies, and implementation actions.  
The HNA identifies the housing Fort Lupton needs and the households those needs affect. This 
Housing Action Plan defines how the community will respond through targeted strategies to 
produce and preserve affordable housing, mitigate displacement pressures, and update local 
zoning to better support housing development. The plan also incorporates input gathered through 
an inclusive public engagement process so that community priorities and local knowledge shape 
implementation. Measurable goals and a clear framework for implementation and monitoring will 
guide local decisions about housing policies, programs, and investments throughout the planning 
period. 
Prior to adoption, the proposed plan was posted publicly on Fort Lupton’s website for a minimum 
of 30 days before the public hearing, providing residents and stakeholders with the opportunity to 
review and comment on the plan. Following approval by the City Council at a public hearing, the 
plan was submitted to the Colorado Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) within 60 days of adoption. 
DOLA reviewed the plan for completeness and acceptance and posted it publicly on the 
department's website.  
Planning Horizon and Update Requirements 
This Housing Action Plan covers a six-year planning horizon, consistent with statutory 
requirements. Over this period, Fort Lupton commits to working towards implementation of the 
goals, strategies, and actions outlined in this plan, with progress tracked against the goals and 
metrics established in Chapter 5.  
Three years after adoption, Fort Lupton is required to submit a progress report to DOLA 
documenting the adoption of strategies and any changes to local laws identified in the plan. DOLA 
will review this report to determine whether the City has made a good faith effort to advance the 
plan's commitments or has identified alternative strategies to pursue.  
Summary of Public Engagement Process and Key Findings 
Public engagement for the Housing Action Plan was designed to gather input while creating 
opportunities for residents to participate in ways that were convenient and accessible. The project 
team used a combination of community events, targeted stakeholder outreach, surveys, and 
virtual engagement opportunities to collect feedback on local housing needs and to discuss the 
goals and strategies.

Key Findings  
Several consistent themes emerged throughout the community engagement process.  
• Housing Affordability: Residents and stakeholders identified housing affordability as a 
significant community concern.  
• Housing Diversity: People emphasized the need for additional housing options for a range 
of income levels, including workforce households, families, first-time homebuyers, and 
seniors. 
Page 16 of 129
 
5 | P a g e  
 
• Preservation and Rehabilitation: Participants highlighted the importance of preserving 
existing housing stock and reinvesting in older housing to maintain affordability and 
neighborhood stability. 
• Senior Housing: Housing for older residents was identified as a critical need. They 
emphasized that multiple options are needed, including affordable senior apartments, 
independent living communities, assisted living facilities, and smaller, lower-maintenance 
types that allow seniors to stay in the community and age in place. 
• Thoughtful Growth: While participants recognized the need for additional housing, they 
emphasized that future growth should be thoughtfully planned and compatible with Fort 
Lupton's identity, neighborhoods, and long-term vision. 
• Partnerships: Discussions with the Housing Authority staff and Board highlighted the 
importance of collaboration among the City, Housing Authority, developers, funding 
partners, and other stakeholders to preserve existing housing, develop new affordable and 
workforce housing, pursue strategic acquisitions, and secure funding resources.

implementation strategies.

stock shaped Goal 1, which focuses on rehabilitation, infill, and redevelopment.  
• Goal 2: The community's desire for additional housing choices and housing types informed 
Goal 2, which encourages greater housing diversity.  
• Goal 3: The identified need for affordable, workforce, and senior housing influenced Goal 3, 
which seeks to reduce barriers to these housing types and support their development.

Housing Needs Assessment Highlights 
The 2026 Fort Lupton HNA provides the foundation for the goals, strategies, and implementation 
actions in this Housing Action Plan. This chapter summarizes the community’s demographic and 
housing characteristics, affordability challenges, current and projected housing needs, and gaps 
within the ownership and rental markets. 
Community Snapshot  
Chapter 3 of the HNA (pp. 20–41) examines the demographic, economic, and housing trends 
shaping Fort Lupton’s current and future housing needs. Key findings include: 
• 9,219 residents: Fort Lupton’s population grew by 17% between 2018 and 2023. (pp. 20–21) 
• Smaller households are increasing: Two-person households grew from 27% to 34%, 
increasing demand for modest-sized homes and rentals. (pp. 24–25) 
• 14% of residents are age 65 or older: The growing senior population increases demand for 
accessible, lower-maintenance, and age-friendly housing. (pp. 22–23) 
• $64,850 median household income: Median income increased by only 2% between 2018 
and 2023, while 44% of households earned less than $50,000. (pp. 25–27) 
• 36% of households rent: The renter share increased from 28% to 36% between 2018 and 
2023, while median rent rose to $1,296. (pp. 34 and 40) 
• 77% of housing is single-family detached: Multifamily housing represents only 12% of the 
housing stock, limiting choices for smaller households, renters, seniors, and first-time 
buyers. (pp. 35–36) 
• More than 50% of homes were built before 1980: The median year built is 1978, indicating 
growing rehabilitation, maintenance, accessibility, and energy-efficiency needs. (p. 37) 
• Housing costs have risen faster than incomes: The median home value more than doubled 
between 2013 and 2023, reaching $372,400, while rental units priced below $1,000 
declined substantially. (pp. 38–40) 
Looking ahead, Fort Lupton’s projected population growth is expected to slow from 24% between 
2020 and 2030 to approximately 13% between 2040 and 2050. Based on State Demography Office 
employment projections for Weld County, employment in Fort Lupton could increase by 
approximately 21% between 2030 and 2050. Continued local and regional growth is expected to 
place additional pressure on housing demand.

Risk of Displacement 
The HNA, community engagement process, and stakeholder discussions did not identify a specific 
neighborhood or geographic area within Fort Lupton currently experiencing significant 
displacement pressures. However, several factors suggest that certain households may be 
vulnerable to future displacement if housing costs continue to increase faster than incomes. These 
households include lower-income renters, seniors on fixed incomes, residents living in older 
housing stock requiring substantial maintenance, and households seeking housing at affordable 
price points. 
The HNA identified limited rental housing options, increasing housing costs, and a shortage of 
affordable and workforce housing as ongoing challenges that may increase housing instability for 
some residents over time. The 2023 Fort Lupton displacement indicators provide a high-level 
snapshot of populations that may be more vulnerable to housing instability or displacement (HNA, 
p. 48). Feedback from community engagement activities and discussions with the Housing 
Authority further highlighted housing stability concerns for seniors and lower-income households, 
including rising property taxes, utility costs, maintenance expenses, and the limited availability of 
affordable senior housing options. Collectively, these findings suggest a need to proactively 
preserve housing affordability and support residents who may be vulnerable to future housing cost 
increases. 
The Housing Action Plan includes several strategies intended to reduce displacement risk and 
maintain housing stability for existing residents. These strategies include preserving and 
rehabilitating existing housing, expanding affordable and workforce housing opportunities, 
supporting senior housing and aging-in-place initiatives, encouraging housing diversity, and 
strengthening partnerships with the Fort Lupton Housing Authority to preserve and expand 
affordable housing opportunities. Collectively, these actions seek to increase housing supply,

Key Findings 
The HNA found that Fort Lupton’s housing supply is not keeping pace with changes in household 
incomes, demographics, and housing preferences. Housing costs have risen faster than incomes, 
contributing to growing affordability pressures for both renters and prospective homebuyers. 
Between 2018 and 2023, renter households increased by 43%, and the share of cost-burdened 
renters increased to 54%. 
Housing choices also remain limited. The community’s housing stock is dominated by single-family 
detached homes, leaving fewer options for young adults, smaller households, local workers, 
seniors, and first-time buyers. At the same time, the growing senior population and aging housing 
stock are increasing the need for accessible housing, lower-maintenance options, and investment 
in existing homes. (HNA, pp. 3–4) 
Identified Housing Needs 
Chapter 5 of the HNA identifies a housing market with strong demand but significant mismatches 
between available housing and what residents need and can afford. Although the overall supply of 
homes for sale appears adequate, most listings are concentrated within a narrow range of 
approximately $400,000 to $600,000. Very few homes are available at prices affordable to lower-
income households, creating an estimated need for approximately 35 to 60 ownership units 
affordable to households earning 80% of Area Median Income (AMI) or less. (HNA, pp. 52–57) 
Fort Lupton’s most immediate shortage is rental housing. The effective rental vacancy rate is 
approximately 1%, well below the 5% to 7% range associated with a balanced market. The city 
currently needs an estimated 31 to 51 additional rental units, with gaps at lower rent levels and 
across different unit sizes and housing types. (HNA, pp. 58–60) 
Population growth will increase pressure on the housing market. Over the next five years, Fort 
Lupton is projected to need approximately 157 to 261 additional ownership units and 191 to 319 
rental units. Over ten years, these needs are projected to increase to approximately 328 to 546 
ownership units and 376 to 626 rental units. New housing will be needed across income levels, 
with an emphasis on attainable ownership opportunities, affordable rentals, and a broader variety 
of housing types. (HNA, pp. 57 and 61) 
Additional housing needs identified include: 
• Accessible and visitable housing for seniors and residents with disabilities, who represent 
approximately 16% of the population. 
• Rehabilitation, maintenance, and possible replacement of older homes, particularly the 
28% of the housing stock built before 1959. 
• A limited number of supportive housing units and stronger partnerships with organizations 
serving residents experiencing homelessness or housing instability. (HNA, pp. 62–66)

Methodology 
Because the Fort Lupton Development Code regulates residential development intensity through 
permitted building types and associated lot standards rather than maximum dwelling units per 
acre (DU/AC), DU/AC were calculated for each housing type using the minimum lot size and unit 
standards established in Article 5, Table 5-1, of the Development Code. 
• For housing types with fixed lot sizes, density was calculated using the minimum lot area 
required for a single dwelling unit.  
• For housing types with variable lot sizes, lot size ranges, or unit counts, minimum and 
maximum permitted densities were calculated to establish a range of potential 
development outcomes.  
• For accessory dwelling units (ADUs), density was calculated by assuming one additional 
dwelling unit on a lot meeting the minimum lot size requirement for the principal dwelling.  
• Manufactured housing and small format housing projects were evaluated using the project 
intensity standards specified in the code.  
Residential development capacity was then estimated by applying the calculated lowest- and 
highest-density housing types to the vacant, developable acreage within each residential zoning 
district. This approach provides a range of potential housing outcomes under current zoning

Regulatory Tools and Constraints 
Density Bonus Opportunities 
The Fort Lupton Development Code includes limited opportunities to increase residential density 
beyond the base standards established by zoning districts. These incentives are tied to specific 
development patterns and open space preservation requirements rather than being broadly 
available throughout the city. As a result, density bonuses function as targeted tools that can 
increase housing capacity while advancing community goals related to open space preservation, 
neighborhood design, and efficient land use. 
Conservation Pattern Development 
In the Agriculture (A), Single-Family Residential (R-1), and Single- and Two-Family Residential (R-
1A) districts, the Conservation Pattern option allows residential development to be clustered on a 
portion of a site while preserving larger areas of open space, agricultural land, or natural features. 
Depending on the percentage of land preserved, projects may qualify for density bonuses ranging 
from 15% to 100% above the base density otherwise permitted by the zoning district. This provision 
provides one of the few opportunities within lower-density districts to increase housing yield while 
preserving open space and rural character. 
Manufactured and Small-Format Housing 
The Manufactured and Small-Format Housing (M-H) district includes a density bonus that allows 
projects to increase density from the base intensity of 7 dwelling units per acre to as much as 15 
dwelling units per acre when more than 30% of the project area is designed as usable open space. 
This incentive can substantially increase housing capacity while encouraging the provision of 
shared amenities and open space. It can also support the development of higher-density, 
attainable housing types that can help address the identified housing needs.

Development Standards Affecting Housing Capacity 
 
Several zoning and development standards influence the City's ability to accommodate the 
housing types identified in the HNA. Minimum lot size requirements are among the most influential 
regulations because they directly affect the density that can be achieved and the range of housing 
products that may be developed. Larger lot requirements in lower-density districts support 
traditional detached housing patterns but limit opportunities for smaller and more attainable 
housing options. 
The analysis also found that opportunities for duplexes, row houses, apartments, manufactured 
housing, and other alternative housing types are concentrated within a limited number of zoning 
districts. While the Development Code permits a variety of housing products, much of the City's 
residential land base remains oriented toward detached housing. As a result, future housing 
diversification will likely depend on continued development and redevelopment within districts 
that permit a broader range of housing types. 
At the same time, the Development Code includes several provisions that support housing choice 
and residential capacity. Accessory dwelling units are permitted in districts that allow detached 
housing, providing opportunities for incremental residential growth. The Conservation Pattern 
provisions and Manufactured and Small-Format Housing District density bonuses also provide 
mechanisms to increase housing yield while preserving open space and neighborhood amenities.

4 HOUSING GOALS AND STRATEGIES 
The HNA and community engagement process identified several housing priorities for Fort Lupton, 
including maintaining affordability, expanding housing choices, preserving existing housing, and 
providing options that meet the needs of residents at various stages of life. These findings provide 
the foundation for the goals and strategies of this Housing Action Plan. 
The Housing Action Plan is organized around three goals: (1) incentivize rehabilitation, infill, and 
redevelopment; (2) encourage housing diversity; and (3) support affordable, workforce, 
multifamily, and senior housing opportunities. Together, these goals focus on preserving existing 
housing, expanding housing choices, reducing regulatory and development barriers, and creating 
opportunities to meet current and future housing needs. 
A summary table of the goals and strategies is provided below. Each goal and strategy is described 
in more detail in this chapter, and an implementation matrix with actions and metrics is provided in 
Chapter 5.

Goal 1: Incentivize Rehabilitation, Infill, and Redevelopment 
Goal Overview 
As Fort Lupton continues to grow, maintaining and improving existing housing is an important 
complement to new housing development. Community engagement identified concerns related to 
housing maintenance costs, aging housing stock, and preserving affordable housing opportunities. 
At the same time, vacant and underutilized properties provide opportunities to accommodate 
additional housing within established areas of the City. 
This goal focuses on preserving existing homes, encouraging reinvestment in established 
neighborhoods, and supporting infill and redevelopment opportunities. Partnerships, financial 
resources, and targeted programs can help Fort Lupton extend the life of its existing housing while 
creating additional housing opportunities in areas already served by infrastructure and community 
services. 
Why This Goal Matters 
Preserving existing homes can help maintain naturally occurring affordable housing and allow 
residents, particularly seniors and households with limited financial resources, to remain in their 
homes as housing needs change. Rehabilitation assistance can also address deferred 
maintenance, accessibility, energy efficiency, and life-safety needs before homes deteriorate 
further. 
Infill and redevelopment complement preservation efforts by creating opportunities for new 
housing within established areas. Together, these approaches can make efficient use of existing 
infrastructure, encourage neighborhood reinvestment, and support broader community 
revitalization and economic development objectives. 
Strategy 1.1 Conduct a Home Rehabilitation Assistance Program Feasibility 
Study 
The City will evaluate the feasibility of establishing a home rehabilitation assistance program 
focused on low-income and senior homeowners. A future program could provide grants, low-
interest loans, or other forms of assistance for critical repairs, accessibility improvements, energy-
efficiency upgrades, and life-safety needs. 
The feasibility study will evaluate potential program structure, eligibility requirements, funding 
sources, administrative capacity, and implementation partners. This initial evaluation will help 
determine whether and how a rehabilitation program could support housing preservation, reduce 
the impacts of deferred maintenance, and help residents remain in their homes. 
Strategy 1.2 Partner with the Housing Authority for Future Affordable Housing 
Development 
The City will strengthen coordination with the Housing Authority to identify and pursue 
opportunities for future affordable housing development and redevelopment, with particular 
Page 39 of 129
 
28 | P a g e  
 
consideration given to opportunities within the downtown core. Collaboration can help align 
housing priorities, identify potential sites, coordinate resources, and position future projects for 
funding and implementation. 
Affordable housing located near services, employment, and transportation can improve access to 
community resources while supporting broader downtown reinvestment objectives. Continued 
collaboration between the City and Housing Authority can help ensure that affordability remains 
part of future redevelopment and housing growth. 
Strategy 1.3 Pursue Grant Funding for Brownfield Redevelopment 
Assessments 
The City will pursue grant funding and partnerships to support environmental assessments of 
properties with redevelopment potential. Environmental uncertainty can create a barrier to 
investment, particularly on older or previously developed properties where potential contamination 
is unknown. 
Completing environmental assessments can help identify site conditions, reduce uncertainty for 
property owners and potential developers, and position appropriate properties for future 
residential or mixed-use redevelopment. These efforts can help return underutilized properties to 
productive use while supporting housing and economic development objectives. 
Goal 2: Encourage Housing Diversity 
Goal Overview 
Fort Lupton's housing needs extend across household sizes, income levels, ages, and stages of 
life. Expanding the range of housing types available in the community can provide more options for 
first-time homebuyers, working households, seniors, smaller households, and residents whose 
housing needs change over time. 
This goal focuses on removing regulatory barriers and creating opportunities for a broader range of 
housing types, including smaller-lot homes, missing-middle housing, accessory dwelling units, and 
other residential options appropriate to different areas of the community. 
Why This Goal Matters 
Housing choice is inextricably connected to housing attainability. When the housing supply is 
concentrated within a limited range of housing types and price points, households may have fewer 
options that fit their needs and budgets. 
Allowing a greater variety of housing types can create opportunities for smaller and potentially 
more attainable homes, support incremental infill, and help residents find appropriate housing as 
their household needs change. Thoughtful changes to development standards can expand these 
opportunities while considering existing neighborhood patterns, infrastructure capacity, and 
community character.

Strategy 2.1 Evaluate Regulatory Requirements for Housing Type Diversity in 
Subdivision Standards 
The City will evaluate regulatory approaches that encourage or require a mix of housing types 
within appropriate residential developments. This evaluation may consider requirements for larger 
new residential developments as well as opportunities within infill zone districts that allow 
residential uses. 
Potential approaches could establish expectations for a mix of lot sizes, housing types, or 
residential products rather than relying predominantly on a single housing type. Any future 
requirements would be developed through additional analysis, public review, and the City's 
ordinance adoption process. 
Strategy 2.2 Evaluate Residential Lot Standards to Support Missing-Middle 
Housing 
The City will review residential lot size and dimensional standards to identify opportunities for 
smaller lots and a broader range of housing types. The review should consider where changes 
could support infill and missing middle housing while remaining responsive to existing 
development patterns and infrastructure conditions. 
More flexible lot standards can create opportunities for smaller housing products and make more 
efficient use of available land, potentially expanding the range of housing choices available to Fort 
Lupton residents. 
Strategy 2.3 Streamline the Accessory Dwelling Unit Development Process 
Accessory dwelling units (ADUs) can provide flexible, small-scale housing options for family 
members, caregivers, seniors, renters, and other households while adding housing incrementally 
within existing neighborhoods. 
The City will evaluate the existing ADU review and development process to identify unnecessary 
barriers and opportunities for greater predictability. This may include simplifying requirements and 
developing clear guidance or informational resources for property owners interested in 
constructing an ADU. 
Strategy 2.4 Evaluate Increased Allowable Densities in Appropriate Zone 
Districts 
Using the findings of the zoning capacity analysis, the City will evaluate opportunities to increase 
allowable residential densities in locations where infrastructure, services, surrounding 
development patterns, and community goals can support additional housing. 
Targeted adjustments to allowable density can increase housing capacity and improve 
development feasibility without applying the same approach throughout the community. This 
strategy is intended to direct additional housing opportunities toward locations where increased 
density is appropriate and can be effectively served.

Goal 3: Support Affordable, Workforce, Multifamily, and 
Senior Housing 
Goal Overview 
Fort Lupton's current and future housing needs include options for working households, seniors, 
renters, and households that cannot find housing within their budget. Meeting these needs will 
require both new housing production and development conditions that allow a wider range of 
housing projects to be financially and physically feasible. 
This goal focuses on reducing regulatory and cost barriers and strengthening incentives for 
affordable, workforce, multifamily, and senior housing. The strategies are intended to improve 
development predictability and feasibility while creating opportunities for housing that responds to 
identified community needs. 
Why This Goal Matters 
The availability and cost of housing affect residents as well as the employers, services, and 
institutions that depend on the local workforce. Expanding workforce and affordable housing 
opportunities can help provide more options for households that may otherwise have difficulty 
finding housing within Fort Lupton. 
Senior housing is also important to providing residents with options as their housing needs change. 
Smaller, accessible, lower-maintenance, and other senior-oriented housing can help older 
residents remain connected to the community. Multifamily housing provides another important 
component of the housing supply by expanding rental and smaller-unit options. 
Reducing unnecessary regulatory and development barriers does not guarantee that these housing 
types will be constructed, but it can improve the conditions necessary for projects to move 
forward. 
Strategy 3.1 Expand By-Right Multifamily Housing Opportunities 
The City will evaluate amendments to zoning regulations that allow multifamily housing by right in 
appropriate residential, commercial, and downtown zone districts. Expanding by-right 
opportunities can provide greater predictability in the development process and reduce the time 
and costs associated with discretionary approvals. 
Potential amendments should consider locations where multifamily housing is compatible with 
surrounding uses and can be adequately served by infrastructure, transportation, and community 
services. This approach can expand opportunities for multifamily housing while directing 
development toward appropriate locations. 
Page 42 of 129
 
31 | P a g e  
 
Strategy 3.2 Evaluate Building Height Increases to Support Multifamily and 
Senior Housing 
The City will evaluate opportunities to increase allowable building heights for multifamily and 
senior housing in appropriate locations. Where compatible with surrounding development and 
infrastructure capacity, additional height can allow more efficient use of available land and 
improve project feasibility. 
The evaluation should consider where increased heights could support additional housing 
opportunities while remaining consistent with the desired scale and character of surrounding 
areas. 
Strategy 3.3 Reduce Parking Barriers by Expanding Director’s Administrative 
Authority 
The City will evaluate expanding the Community Development Director's administrative authority 
to approve reductions in required parking for affordable, workforce, and senior housing projects 
when project characteristics support a lower parking demand. 
Greater administrative flexibility can allow parking requirements to respond to factors such as 
housing type, unit size, resident population, location, and access to nearby services. Reducing 
unnecessary parking requirements can lower development costs and allow more land to be used 
for housing while maintaining adequate parking for residents. 
Strategy 3.4 Evaluate Permit Fee Waivers for Housing Authority Projects 
The City will evaluate permit fee waivers, reductions, or similar incentives for qualifying Housing 
Authority projects. Reducing eligible development costs may improve project feasibility and allow 
limited affordable housing resources to be directed toward construction and long-term 
affordability. 
Any future incentive program should establish clear eligibility criteria, identify applicable fees, and 
define the approval process. This strategy can strengthen the City's partnership with the Housing 
Authority while providing a targeted tool to support future affordable housing development.

5 IMPLEMENTATION AND MONITORING  
Implementation Approach 
Identifying housing goals and strategies is a key step, but meaningful progress depends on 
sustained implementation. This chapter translates the goals and strategies of this Housing Action 
Plan into a practical work plan for Fort Lupton. An implementation plan is a critical tool for 
converting the plan into tangible results, bridging the gap between planning and action. For each 
strategy identified in this plan, the implementation plan provides additional detail on what will be 
done, who is responsible, when it will happen, and how it will be carried out. This includes 
clarifying timelines, identifying responsible departments and partners, and defining success 
metrics.  
Sequencing strategies thoughtfully will also allow Fort Lupton to coordinate with complementary 
planning efforts, budget cycles, and other local priorities to maximize feasibility and impact. 
Together, these details elevate each housing action from a policy statement into a concrete work 
item that can be assigned, tracked, and advanced by staff and partners over the life of the plan.  
The implementation plan is intended to remain flexible as community conditions, funding 
opportunities, and priorities change. Fort Lupton may update actions, responsibilities, timelines, 
and metrics as needed while maintaining progress toward the plan’s adopted goals over the six-
year planning horizon. 
Intergovernmental Coordination 
Addressing housing needs in Fort Lupton will require ongoing collaboration among local, regional, 
state, and housing-focused partners. Throughout the development of the Housing Action Plan, the 
City coordinated with the Fort Lupton Housing Authority to better understand local housing needs, 
existing housing resources, and opportunities to preserve and expand affordable housing options. 
Discussions with Housing Authority staff and Board members identified opportunities for 
continued partnership related to affordable and workforce housing development, preservation of 
existing housing, strategic property acquisition, and pursuit of funding opportunities. 
Moving forward, the City will continue to coordinate with the Fort Lupton Housing Authority, the 
Colorado Department of Local Affairs (DOLA), the Colorado Housing and Finance Authority (CHFA), 
and other regional and housing partners to implement Housing Action Plan recommendations. 
Opportunities for intergovernmental coordination include pursuing grant funding, supporting 
affordable and workforce housing development, identifying redevelopment opportunities, 
conducting housing preservation programs, and sharing technical resources and expertise. These 
collaborative efforts will help leverage limited local resources, align housing investments with 
community needs, and address housing challenges that extend beyond municipal boundaries.

APPENDIX A: ENGAGEMENT PROCESS AND 
FINDINGS 
Public engagement was a foundational component of the Housing Action Plan and was designed to 
ensure the plan reflects the needs, experiences, and priorities of Fort Lupton residents and 
stakeholders. Recognizing that housing challenges affect residents differently based on factors 
such as age, income, tenure, household size, and housing type, the engagement plan sought to 
gather input from a broad cross-section of the community throughout the planning process. 
The engagement strategy was structured to provide multiple opportunities for participation using a 
variety of formats, locations, and communication methods. This approach allowed residents to 
engage in ways that were convenient and accessible to them while helping the project team reach 
individuals who may not typically participate in community planning efforts. Outreach activities 
included presentations to local boards and committees, participation in community events, 
meetings with housing and community stakeholders, targeted engagement with seniors, 
community surveys, and online engagement opportunities. 
Engagement activities were designed to accomplish several objectives: 
1. They provided an opportunity to educate residents and stakeholders about local housing 
conditions and trends.  
2. They allowed community members to share their experiences with housing affordability, 
availability, quality, and accessibility. 
3. They helped identify housing challenges affecting specific populations, including seniors, 
workforce households, renters, homeowners, and lower-income residents.  
4. They provided an opportunity to test potential solutions and gather feedback on the goals, 
strategies, and actions being considered for inclusion in the Housing Action Plan. 
Input received through the engagement process directly informed the development of the Housing 
Action Plan's goals, strategies, and implementation actions.  
Key Themes 
Feedback collected throughout the engagement process revealed broad agreement around several 
housing priorities. While perspectives varied across participants, the input consistently highlighted 
the importance of increasing housing opportunities, supporting affordability, preserving existing 
neighborhoods, and planning for future growth in a way that reflects Fort Lupton's unique 
character. The following themes emerged most frequently: 
• Residents expressed a need for additional housing choices across a range of price points to 
accommodate households with different incomes, life stages, and housing preferences. 
• Participants identified workforce and affordable housing as a critical need to support local 
residents, employees, and families. 
Page 47 of 129

• Community members emphasized the importance of preserving and rehabilitating existing 
housing stock to maintain affordability and neighborhood stability. 
• Residents highlighted the need to support seniors through both new senior housing 
opportunities and programs that help residents remain in their homes as they age. 
• Participants expressed a desire for housing growth that is thoughtfully planned and 
aligned with Fort Lupton's character, community values, and long-term vision. 
Inclusive Outreach 
A key objective of the Housing Action Plan engagement process was to ensure that participation 
opportunities were accessible to a broad cross-section of the community, including residents who 
may face barriers to participating in traditional public meetings. Recognizing that work schedules, 
transportation limitations, caregiving responsibilities, mobility challenges, language barriers, and 
other factors can limit participation, the project team utilized a variety of engagement methods and 
meeting formats to increase accessibility and encourage broader community involvement. 
To reach traditionally underrepresented populations and residents potentially vulnerable to 
housing instability or displacement, outreach extended beyond standard formal public meetings. 
Engagement activities were integrated into existing community events, conducted at locations 
already frequented by residents, and supplemented with a paper survey option. Targeted outreach 
efforts included engagement with seniors through the Recreation Center senior luncheon, 
collaboration with the Housing Authority, bilingual activities and project team members at 
community events, and opportunities for residents to provide feedback both in person and 
virtually. By offering multiple ways to participate and meeting residents where they already gather, 
the City was able to collect input from a diverse range of households and ensure the Housing 
Action Plan reflects community needs, experiences, and priorities across different ages, incomes, 
and housing situations. 
Engagement Activities
