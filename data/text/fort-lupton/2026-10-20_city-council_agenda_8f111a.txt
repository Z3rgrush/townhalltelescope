FORT LUPTON CITY COUNCIL/ENTERPRISE BOARDS 
October 6, 2026 
  
 
The City Council of the City of Fort Lupton met in a regular session at the City Complex, 130 
South McKinley Avenue, the regular meeting place of the City Council, on Tuesday, October 06, 
2026. Mayor Chris Ceretto called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. and invited everyone to join 
him in the Pledge of Allegiance. 
 
ROLL CALL 
 
Mari Peña, City Clerk, called the roll. Those present were Mayor Chris Ceretto, Councilmembers, 
Bruce Davis, Bruce Fitzgerald, Valerie Blackston and Claud Hanes. Councilmember David Crespin 
participated remotely. Councilmember Michael Sanchez was absent. 
 
Also present were City Administrator, Chris Cross, City Clerk, Mari Peña, Public Works Director, 
Roy Vestal, Chief of Police, William Carnes, Planning Director, Todd Hodges and Finance Director 
Leann Perino.  
 
PROCLAMATION 
 
Mayor Ceretto proclaimed the month of October 2026 as Domestic Violence Awareness Month. 
Sarah Ball with Brighton Office of Victim Advocacy was in attendance and received the 
proclamation.  
 
PERSONS TO ADDRESS COUNCIL 
 
There was no one to address the Mayor or Council. 
 
APPROVAL OF AGENDA 
 
It was moved by Claud Hanes and seconded by Bruce Fitzgerald to approve the agenda as 
presented. Motion passed unanimously on voice vote.  
 
REVIEW OF ACCOUNTS PAYABLES

SUBJECT FOR DISCUSSION 
 
Approving a Motion to Direct the Planning Department to File the Local Government Affordable Housing 
Commitment in Order to Participate in Funding Programs Associated with Proposition 123 – Colorado 
Statewide Affordable Housing Fund (Prop 123-C.R.S. 29-32-101 – 29-32-106). 
            
SUMMARY STATEMENT/BACKGROUND DISCUSSION 
 
Prop 123 created the State Affordable Housing Fund which will provide funding for affordable housing 
programs in Colorado.  Municipalities and counties can opt in to this program if they so choose. In order  
for affordable housing development projects within the city to be eligible for funding  provided through 
Prop 123 , Fort Lupton must file a commitment with the Colorado Department of Housing (DOH).  This 
commitment establishes affordable housing cost limits for the City along with the current/baseline number 
of affordable housing units in Fort Lupton, which the City  intendeds to increase by no less than three 
percent each year for three years, totaling nine percent by the end of 2029. If this targeted growth is not 
met, the City would be ineligible for funding for Year 7 (2030) and could make a new 2-year commitment 
in Year 5 (2031). Additional requirements for the commitment could come after the renewed 3-year period. 
 
The updated baseline calculation (upon which the commitment to increase affordable housing stock 
growth is measured)  is established through a calculation  that looks at the average number of annual 
residential new construction building permits from 2023-2026, multiplies it by a calculated job growth rate 
bracket (15% for Fort Lupton), and multiplies it by three years. This calculation equals the City’s new three-
year unit growth commitment. The calculation for Fort Lupton is 200 (average new residential construction 
permits) x 15% x 3 years, equaling 90 total units to be committed by the renewal of the City’s Proposition 
123 commitment. 
 
In the previous three -year period (2023 -2026), “Naturally Occurring Affordable Housing” (NOAH) was 
eligible if it met certain cost and income standards relative to “Area Median income” (AMI). The next three-
year cycle does not allow NOAH units to count and  requires all units to have some form of a “formal 
affordability restriction”. Depending on the incentive or restriction used for different units, an incentive 
bonus can be applied, counting each unit as 1.1 -1.2 units instead. Meaning the City may not ulti mately 
need 90 total units depending on what method is used. 
 
Once the new  commitment is filed and accepted, individual projects can continue to pursue funding  
through a variety of programs established by Prop 123. Entities that can apply for loans and grants include 
the City of Fort Lupton, regional non-profits, private developers, individuals, and others. 
 
If the City intends to file a commitment, the deadline is November 1, 2026.

Approving a Police K-9 Program Supported by Donation and Sponsorship Funding     
           
SUMMARY STATEMENT/BACKGROUND DISCUSSION 
The Police Department intends to establish a Police K -9 program supported by donation and sponsorship 
funding. The program would initially consist of one dual trained and certified handler and canine.  The K-9 
program supports the City’s mission and vision, enhances the public safety capabilities of the Police 
Department, and increases community engagement opportunities. 
 
The program would be managed with existing staff and vehicles, requiring some additional equipment be 
installed in one vehicle. The canine is owned by the handler, who is currently working for the Police 
Department. The canine has an estimated four to five years of working life left before retirement.  
 
FINANCIAL CONSIDERATIONS 
Initial start-up costs are anticipated to be under $10,000. This would cover refitting an existing patrol fleet 
vehicle. This entire cost is expected to be covered through donations. Annual operating costs are expected 
to average $6,500, with the highest anticipated cost being less than $9,000. The Department expects 
donations and sponsorships will cover all operating costs. 
 
Any other known costs (annual training, salary and benefits, vehicle maintenance) are already included in 
the Police Department operating budget and would not require any change to funding. Donations and 
Sponsorships will be used to supplant costs associated with the program. 
 
Unexpected costs or donation shortfalls can be covered by the existing operating budget, if donations and 
sponsorships are insufficient. 
 
Funds would be maintained by the Finance Department, by establishing a budget line for donations and 
expenses, and using established policies for spending.  
 
LEGAL/POLITICAL CONSIDERATIONS 
Police K-9 programs carry inherit liability, which is mitigated through training, certifications, and record -
keeping. Liability is managed through an experienced handler, the Colorado Police Canine Association, and 
Department Policies (LEXIPOL). 
 
Insurance coverage is included in the City’s CIRSA policy and would not require any cost changes.

SUBJECT FOR DISCUSSION 
 
An Ordinance Imposing a Temporary Suspension on the Submission, Acceptance, Processing, and 
Approval of Applications for the Establishment or Expansion of Data Center Facilities with  A Demand of 
Five (5) Megawatts (MW) or Greater of Electrical Capacity Within the City of Fort Lupton and Declaring an 
Emergency 
 
SUMMARY STATEMENT/BACKGROUND DISCUSSION 
 
Data centers are specialized facilities for storing, managing, and disseminating data and information, 
including housing IT infrastructure, computer systems and associated components, and training artificial 
intelligence models. Data centers vary considerably in purpose (telecommunications, cloud computing, AI 
model training, cryptocurrency mining, etc.), customer structure (colocation vs. hyperscale),  and scale 
(small-scale to utility -scale). A data center facility comprises digital infrastructure as well as physical 
structures, including cooling equipment, power and backup power generation facilities, energy 
transmission infrastructure, stormwater fac ilities, administrative offices, and parking. Potential 
community impacts vary greatly depending on the facility’s size, design, resource demands, and 
technology. 
 
For the purposes of this Ordinance, a "Data Center" is broadly defined as any facility meeting any of the 
following criteria: 
(a) A facility for housing networked servers, computers, and networking infrastructure 
information technology (IT) equipment necessary for the provision of off- site or on -site digital 
services that has a demand of five (5) megawatts (MW) or electrical capacity or greater, or 
(b) A facility where the primary use is the storage, processing, management, or transmission of 
digital data and/or support for the delivery of cloud computing or artificial intelligence (AI) 
services that has a demand of five (5) megawatts (MW) or electrical capacity or greater 
Currently, Chapter 16 of the Fort Lupton Municipal Code does not contain regulations specifically 
addressing the permitting, development, or performance standards of data center facilities. During the 
suspension period, Planning staff will survey and analy ze data center regulations across Colorado and 
nationally to inform appropriate amendments to the Municipal Code, including zoning regulations, water 
and energy management requirements, and design standards.

AN ORDINANCE IMPOSING A  TEMPORARY SUSPENSION ON THE SUBMISSION, ACCEPTANCE, 
PROCESSING, AND APPROVAL OF APPLICATIONS FOR THE ESTABLISHMENT OR EXPANSION OF DATA 
CENTER FACILITIES WITH A DEMAND OF FIVE (5) MEGAWATTS (MW) OR GREATER OF ELECTRICAL 
CAPACITY WITHIN THE CITY OF FORT LUPTON AND DECLARING AN EMERGENCY 
 
WHEREAS, data centers can have significant impacts on the City’s infrastructure and water supply, other 
infrastructure that serves the City, such as electrical power and telecommunications networks, as well as 
potential impacts on surrounding land uses, natural resources, public services and the character of the 
surrounding community; and 
 
WHEREAS, at the C ity’s October 13, 2026 Town Hall , Planning staff requested Council’s direction  on 
whether or not to propose a temporary suspension on the submission, acceptance, processing, and 
approval of applications for the establishment or expansion of data center facilities; and 
 
WHEREAS, after presenting to  Council, staff was directed to propose an ordinance authorizing a 
temporary suspension of the submission, acceptance, processing, and approval of applications for the 
establishment or expansion of data center facilities; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Fort Lupton Municipal Code does not currently include comprehensive code provisions or 
regulations that adequately address data centers; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Colorado General Assembly may consider legislation regarding statewide data center 
facilities regulations during the 2027 legislative session that may impact local regulation, which impact 
will not be known until after the legislation is passed; and 
 
WHEREAS, temporary suspension would provide the City with time to evaluate whether additional 
regulations are appropriate to address the potential impacts of data centers; and 
 
WHEREAS, based on the information provided, Council has determined that it is necessary to temporarily 
suspend acceptance of applications for data center facilities while appropriate regulations, standards, and 
definitions can be developed through a comprehensive public process; and 
 
WHEREAS, during the suspension, staff intends to propose a development code update and the 
suspension would provide an opportunity to obtain community input regarding data centers and any 
potential amendments to the City’s Comprehensive Plan; and 
 
WHEREAS, a temporary suspension on the submission, acceptance, processing, and approval of all 
applications and requests for a City permit, license, land use approval, or other approval for a data center 
is reasonable in time and scope and would not work an unnecessary hardship upon or unreasonably 
prejudice any party; and 
 
WHEREAS, it is appropriate for Council to establish a 12-month suspension during which land use 
applications for new or expanded data center facilities will not be accepted or approved in order to survey 
current data center facilities locally and throughout the nation, to draft amendments to the municipal
