4. Reports 
Mayor Dixon brought up what the State has done and raise awareness of the impacts of regulation 
43, specifically in regard to Transfer and Title. Mayor Dixon brought up Senate Bill 22 and got reports 
from Colorado Springs Utilities that they really need this Bill to pass  as i t has to do with our 
environmental requirements on a power generation company.  If this Bill does not pass Mayor Dixon 
will be recommending everyone to obtain generators as we may have rolling blackouts.   
Marshal Goings reported 38 calls of service, which was up from 33 calls of service in January. Marshal 
Goings reported 253 citations were issued in January, which was down from 325 that were issued in 
December.  Of those citations issued 217 have been paid. 
Kelley Hakes Public Works Supervisor reported that Maple and Foster were picked for ice breakup 
and that the new plow blade for the skid steer was acquired.  Kelley Hakes also reported that the 
gas-powered sander is out of commission.  
Town Clerk Nicole Woods reported that most of the STR’s were submitted for this coming year and 
there is currently a waitlist for any STR spots.   
Caleb Patterson Town Manager reported that the vault designs for the Marshal’s building and the 
Park restrooms will be done in March.  The Ballot box between the Town and El Paso will be getting 
installed at Town Hall.  Mr. Patterson reported that two members of the Planning Commission 
recently resigned.  Mr. Patterson also introduced our new Town Planner Natalie.  
 
5. Public Comment 
Shelly Scott-Nash, 7095 Iona Ave, reported the information about Bronc Day and how the events are 
all free for the kids.  She also requested that the Town provides volunteer Law Enforcement,  a

Rocco Blasi, 10955 Belvidere Ave, expressed concerns about how the Planning Committee has been

8) CORRESPONDENCE 
           a. Septic Permits – Town Manager presented that the pumping for the Marshal’s building and 
the park is not going to be cheap, it will be around $16,000.00. 
           b. Camp Decision- Town Manager read how The Colorado Supreme Court considered whether 
the state M isdemeanor Reform Act  of 2021 preempts municipal code provisions with their  own 
maximum sentences for non-felony offenses.  In particular, the court considered whether home rule 
municipalities can impose penalties  in excess of certain caps set by state statute for identical 
conduct under C.R.S.18-1.3-501(1)(a.5) and C.R.S. 18-1.3-503(1.5).  Basically new decision from the 
Colorado Supreme Court and we must check our municipal code and our fines and make sure they 
line up and don’t go over the state limit.  
           c. Ongoing Task List – Was presented by Town Manager Patterson on what has been 
completed and what is still being worked on.
