Site Summary & Significance 
The historic rectangular wood-frame Swedish-Finn Hall is located on the prominent southeast corner 
of West Pacific Avenue and Townsend Street, near the historic depot. Constructed between 1898 
and 1899, the building has served as a prominent social gathering place for the Telluride community 
throughout its history—first for Swedish-Finn immigrants and their cultural organizations, and today 
as the home of the Telluride Elks Lodge (B.P.O.E. No. 692). Aside from modifications to the roof and 
the construction of a non-historic wood deck that spans the width of the south elevation and wraps 
around the southeast corner onto the east elevation, the building retains a high level of historic 
integrity. The historic privy located at the rear of the property also retains a high level of integrity. Both 
structures are eligible for listing on the National Register of Historic Places and the Colorado State 
Register of Historic Properties.

STAFF ANALYSIS AND RECOMMENDATION  
The pergola structure was constructed without the required permits and design review and is 
currently in violation. Staff do not find sufficient justification to support approval, given the 
pergola's highly visible and prominent location. The pergola is out of context and not compatible 
with the Contributing primary structure and Contributing shed. Accordingly, staff recommends 
denial of the application. As an alternative, the Chair could consider allowing the pergola to 
remain only as a temporary structure, with the applicant required to obtain a separate permit for 
temporary use. 
 
Should the Chair not concur with staff recommendation, alternatively staff would suggest 
minimizing the visual impact of the pergola by reconstructing the roof pitch of the pergola gable 
to match the roof pitch of the Contributing primary structure. In addition, painting the exposed 
wood posts to match the painted wood siding of the primary structure would help the pergola 
blend more cohesively with the existing historic character.

21. General architectural description: 
Swede-Finn Hall is a tall, rectangular-shaped, wood frame building located at the southeast corner of West Pacific 
Avenue and Townsend Street.  It is supported by a low concrete foundation, which is considerably higher at the 
building's south (rear) end than at the north (front) end, as the ground slopes upward from south to north.  The 
building's exterior walls are clad with painted red horizontal weatherboard siding, with painted green 1" by 4" 
corner boards topped by wood capitals.  The front gable roof is steeply-pitched, covered with corrugated metal 
roofing material, and with painted green boxed eaves.  A false front wall on the façade (north elevation), covers 
most of the upper gable end, except for the topmost portion of the roof peak.  Here, the dated "1899" appears in 
gold numerals, on a painted green belt course, in the upper gable end.  A simple wooden gable ornament appears 
in the uppermost part of the upper gable end, while a tall wooden finial extends above the roof peak.  The 
symmetrical façade fronts almost directly onto the concrete sidewalk which parallels Pacific Avenue on the north 
elevation.  A set of paired, painted green with red and pale blue wood trim, wood-paneled doors enter the center 
of the façade.  These doors are topped by a two-light transom, beneath a slightly projecting wood cornice with a 
dentil course.  A set of elk antlers and a light fixture are affixed to the façade wall over the entry doors.  Single 1/1 
double-hung sash windows, with painted blue wood frames, painted green wood surrounds, and wood cornices 
with dentil courses, penetrate the façade wall on either side of the entry doors.  The building's east elevation is 
penetrated by five 1/1 double-hung sash windows, and the west elevation is penetrated by three 1/1 double-hung 
sash windows.  These windows feature painted blue wood frames, painted green wood surrounds, and wood 
cornices.  A non-historic wood deck, with a wood railing extends across the south (rear) elevation, and wraps 
around to cover the south end of the west elevation.  A painted green wood-paneled door, with red and pale blue 
wood trim, enters the east end of the south elevation.

35. Historical background:  
Construction on the Swedish-Finn Temperance Hall was begun in late 1898, and the building was completed early 
the following year.  It was built under the auspices of the Swedish-Finn Temperance Association which had been 
formed, and acquired this property, during the summer of 1898.   
 
Immigrants from Scandinavia represented Telluride's largest ethnic population, and were divided culturally into 
three groups: "pure" Swedes, who generally settled in the vicinity of Tomboy Road, Finns (from Finland), and 
Swede-Finns, or Swedish-Finns, who generally settled in this area along West Pacific Avenue.  Immigrants of 
Swede- Finn descent had been born in southern Finland, near the Swedish border, and spoke Swedish, not 
Finnish.  Although they were born in Finland, Swede-Finns identified culturally more with Sweden than Finland.  
 
After it was completed, this building became the focal point for Swede-Finn culture in Telluride, with dances, 
weddings, masquerade balls, parties, dinners, and any and all other social affairs held here on a regular basis.  
The following advertisement offering to lease the building for such events appeared in the Telluride Daily Journal 
as early as December 1898: 
 
"New Temperance Hall 
 
With stage, kitchens and all appurtenances can now be rented for balls, parties and 
entertainments at reasonable rates.  Enquire of 
John Sundquist, Proprietor 
Located near Depot." 
 
Organizations that used the building early on included the "Ninety-Nine Social Club" (described in the Journal as 
a "Telluride dancing club"), and the Swedish Literary Club.  The first wedding to take place in the building may 
have been that of Miss Minnie Johnson and Mr. Ernest Freeman, officiated by Rev. H.E. Coleman on May 20, 1899. 
 
In more recent years, the building has served as the meeting place for the Telluride Elks Club (B.P.O.E. No. 692).

42. Statement of significance: 
The Swedish-Finn Temperance Hall is historically significant, under National Register Criterion A, because it 
served as the social focal point for Telluride's citizens of Swedish-Finn descent.  The building is also 
architecturally significant, under National Register Criterion C, as a representative example of a wood frame 
building constructed as a meeting hall, and for such features as its false-front façade.  Meeting halls such as this 
represent an important property type in Telluride, and mining towns generally, and were of singular importance to 
the community's ethnic and social history.  As such, this building may be considered individually eligible for 
inclusion in the National and State Registers, and it rates as a contributing resource within the Telluride National 
Historic Landmark District.  The privy at the rear of the lot (THAS #184) also rates as a contributing resource 
within the landmark district. 
 
43. Assessment of historic physical integrity related to significance: 
This building displays a reasonably high standard of physical integrity, relative to the seven aspects of integrity 
as defined by the National Park Service and the Colorado Historical Society, Office of Archaeology and Historic 
Preservation - setting, location, design, materials, workmanship, feeling and association. A non-historic wood 
deck on the rear elevation diminishes its integrity to a small degree.
