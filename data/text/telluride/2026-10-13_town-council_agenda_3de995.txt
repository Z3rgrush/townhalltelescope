Purpose. This memo summarizes the FY27 budget requests for 12 Town funds, 
typically referred to as miscellaneous funds, that are smaller in revenues, expenditures 
and/or scope of services. Together they request $12.11 million in revenue and $16.11 
million in expenditures. The $4.01 million net draw on fund balance comes almost 
entirely from the $4.79 million Open Space set-aside in Fund 60, which budgets the 
fund's full remaining balance. 
Council direction requested. (1) Acceptance of the 2027 budget requests for the 12 
funds, including a combined $4.01M use of fund balance. (2) Acceptance of the one-
year suspension of $348,000 in RETT dollars to the Open Space Fund. (3) Acceptance 
of the $290,000 increase in the transfer to the Transportation Fund. (4) Approval of the 
Town Lodgers' Tax Fund position moving from part time to full time. 
2027 Miscellaneous Funds at a Glance 
The 12 funds request $12.11M in revenue and $16.11M in expenditures for 2027, a combined 
$4.01M use of fund balance. The Open Space Fund accounts for $4.25M of the draw; the other 
11 funds together add $244,119 to fund balance.

Key Issues for Council 
1. Open Space set-aside drives the draw. The $4.79M Open Space Set Aside budgets the 
fund's full remaining balance so it is available for Council-approved acquisitions or 
projects, drawing $4.25M on a budget basis and leaving a $0 ending balance. Tax 
revenue falls to $639,556 with the one-year suspension of $348,000 in RETT dollars. 
2. Transportation relies on a larger transfer. The Transportation Fund is projected to end 
2026 with $11,165. The transfer in rises $290,000 to $1.19M, producing a $59,897 
surplus. 
3. Lodgers' tax assumes a rebound. 2026 lodgers' tax is projected at $919,075, down 
18.8% from 2025. Staff recommends a 2027 budget of $1.14M, 24.1% above the 2026 
projection and about equal to 2025; the fund still draws $97,737 from fund balance. 
4. Child Development relies on a growing General Fund transfer. Tuition is held at $78 
per day, and a larger vacancy allowance holds budgeted tuition 7% below the 2026 
projection. The General Fund transfer rises to $182,000 to keep the fund balance positive. 
5. Health Reserve continues to build. The fund adds $401,622 in 2027, ending at $1.58M, 
in anticipation of higher premium and claims costs.

24 — Energy Mitigation Fund 
The Energy Mitigation Fund accounts for green energy fees the Town charges through building 
permits, mainly the Telluride Energy Mitigation Program (TEMP) fee and the Green Building 
Fee. These fees pay for energy conservation projects within the Town and offset the Town's 
carbon footprint. The fund supports the Sustainability Division, including the Eco Action Partners 
professional services contract, the Green Grants program with Eco Action Partners, Climate 
Action Plan projects, capital energy projects and the grant-funded community-wide building 
energy retrofit program through benchmarking and performance standards. 
Fee changes. The 2027 fee schedule calculates TEMP fees using a new methodology, so 2027 
rates are not directly comparable to 2026. TEMP revenue is budgeted at $250,000, up from 
$170,000. The Green Building Fee remains 20% of the building permit fee, so the 10% building 
fee increase raises budgeted Green Building Fees 10% to $46,750. 
The 2027 request grows to $1.57 million in expenditures, up from $216,882 projected for 2026, 
and draws $124,586 from fund balance. The increase is mostly $1.21 million for the 
Sustainability Program (Materials & Equipment), offset by a $1.13 million grant from the 
Colorado Energy Office Local IMPACT Accelerator program (recorded as Miscellaneous 
revenue), plus a $262,000 transfer out to the Capital Fund.

25 — Child Development Fund 
The Child Development Fund accounts for the Town's childcare program, funded by childcare 
fees and a transfer in from the General Fund. The 2027 request is essentially balanced: 
$447,460 in revenue against $446,296 in expenditures, adding $1,164 to fund balance. Labor is 
96% of spending at $430,416. 
No Fee changes and tuition. The 2027 fee schedule is seeing a 0% increase from 2026 to 
2027 $78 per day ($36 to $39 for Town employees). Budgeted tuition starts at full capacity of 14 
children, five days a week for 47 weeks ($78 x 14 x 5 x 47 = $256,620), less a $36,660 vacancy 
allowance. The allowance reflects typical enrollment of 11 children five days a week and three 
children three days a week ($32,994), plus a buffer of one more vacant day per week ($3,666). 
Even with the rate increase, Charges for Services are budgeted at $220,460, 7% below the 
2026 projection, because the vacancy allowance nearly doubles from $18,640. 
The General Fund transfer keeps the fund balance positive. It has grown from $165,000 in 2025 
to $182,000 in 2027; without it, the fund would run a deficit of about $180,836. The ending 
balance of $9,813 covers about eight days of operating costs. The Town Manager will offer a 
discussion on requiring full-time enrollment.

58 — Parking Fund 
The Parking Fund accounts for parking meter, permit and fine revenue, which pays for parking 
operations and debt service and is intended to eventually fund parking structures. The 2027 
request nearly balances, drawing $14,570 from fund balance. Revenue rises 10% to $904,450, 
led by Licenses & Permits (meters, permits and the parking facility) up 18% to $602,450, while 
fines and parklet fees dip 3% to $302,000. 
Fee changes. Parking rates, permit fees and violation fines are unchanged in the 2027 fee 
schedule, so revenue growth reflects activity assumptions: meter and parking facility revenue 
each rise 10%, and permits rise to $178,350. The temporary summer parklet use certificate 
rises 10% to $22 per space per day, though parklet revenue is budgeted lower at $27,000. 
Expenditures fall 19% to $919,020, mainly because the Lot L feasibility study (Capital Outlay) 
drops from $500,000 to $280,000. Debt service on the 2018 parking revenue bonds holds near 
$410,000, 45% of 2027 spending. Ending available resources are $139,524, down from 
$722,801 at the start of 2025, which also reflects a $67,249 restricted revenue adjustment in 
2025.

59 — Virginia Placer 2A Fund 
The Virginia Placer 2A (VP2A) Fund accounts for the Town's nine-unit municipal employee 
housing building at 1200 Black Bear Road, financed with Certificates of Participation (COP). In 
2025, the fund received $5.47 million in COP proceeds and $1.95 million in transfers in and 
spent $1.58 million on construction.  
The 2027 budget is the building's first full year of operation. Rent (Charges for Services) rises to 
$333,363 from $78,000 in 2026, and miscellaneous revenue falls to $15,000. Expenditures total 
$294,688: $259,225 in debt service and $35,462 in operating costs for labor, purchased 
services, materials and utilities. With no transfer in budgeted, revenue covers debt service and 
operating costs with a $53,676 surplus, and ending available resources are $879,018. 
 
*Note: Fund 59 blends capital construction with operational costs, meaning 2025 audited 
actuals and 2026 projections reflect both types of costs. Because project subsidies were 
transferred upfront while actual construction spending spanned across multiple fiscal years, a 
temporary timing mismatch was created. Ending Available Resources in 2027 reflect remaining 
project capital funds that will be reconciled and corrected once final construction costs are fully 
separated from ongoing operational costs, with any minor variance due to lower-than-
anticipated bond interest earnings.

60 — Open Space Fund 
The Open Space Fund pays for acquiring and maintaining open space. Historically, the fund 
received 20% of sales, property, use and motor vehicle tax, business and building license and 
real estate transfer tax (RETT) revenue. Voters approved this dedication when open space 
preservation was a high community priority, and it needed to stay in place while debt was 
outstanding. At Council's direction, the Town paid off the Excise Tax bond ($6.265 million 
principal) in November 2022 and the COP in February 2023, freeing this source for other high-
priority capital projects. Effective August 1, 2023, 5% of these revenues are dedicated to open 
space and the remaining 15% goes to the new Reserve Capital Improvement Fund. 
The Open Space Set Aside is a budgeting line, not a planned purchase. All revenue not spent 
during the year is budgeted to the set-aside so it is available for open space acquisitions or 
projects Council approves; amounts not spent remain in the fund. In 2026, $462,500 of the $4.6 
million set-aside is projected to be spent. The 2027 request sets aside $4.68 million, the full 
remaining fund balance, drawing $4.35 million on a budget basis and leaving an ending balance 
of $0. Tax revenue falls to $639,556 because of a one-year suspension of $348,000 in RETT 
dollars to the fund.

74 — Airline Guarantee Fund 
The Airline Guarantee Fund collects a 2% excise tax on lodging and on restaurant and bar sales 
to support airline service at the Telluride and Montrose airports. It passes that revenue straight 
through, holding a $1 balance. The 2027 request budgets $2.52 million in taxes, 4% above the 
2026 projection of $2.42 million. Of that, $2.47 million goes to airline guarantee payments 
(Capital Outlay) and $50,416 (2% of revenue) is transferred out as the administrative fee. The 
guarantee pays only the actual revenue received, so a shortfall reduces payments rather than 
fund balance.

76 — Town Lodgers Tax Fund 
The Town Lodgers' Tax Fund accounts for a 2% tax on stays of 29 days or less, used for 
tourism marketing, managing tourism's effects and other purposes Council designates. 
Revenue. Lodging tax is projected at $919,075 for 2026, down 18.8% from 2025 actual 
collections of $1.13 million and 26.9% below the 2026 budget of $1.26 million. For 2027, staff 
recommends budgeting $1.14 million, a 24.1% increase over the 2026 projection that returns 
revenue to about the 2025 level (up 0.8%). 
Expenditures. Labor rises 44% to $236,375 as one position moves from part time to full time, in 
line with Council's goal of more outreach. Purchased services remain the largest cost at 
$818,125: Marketing Telluride ($736,125), website maintenance and hosting ($35,000), paid 
media ($25,000), translation and interpretation ($15,000), software ($5,000) and travel and 
training ($2,000). Community Support of $177,000 covers community engagement and events 
($175,000) and media outreach ($2,000). 
With $1.24 million in expenditures against $1.14 million in revenue, the fund draws $97,737 
from fund balance to meet expenditure demand.
