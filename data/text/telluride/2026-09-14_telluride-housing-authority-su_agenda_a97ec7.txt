FROM: Alexandra Slaten, Special Counsel to the Telluride Housing Authority  
DATE: August 30, 2026 
RE: Worksession to discuss Intent and Potential Guidelines/Policies Changes with Respect to 
Exceptions for Occupancy of Subpar Bedrooms  
BACKGROUND: 
SMRHA  has staff requests  Exception seen an uptick in   Households by  owning multi-bed units 
seeking to leave bedrooms empty that do not meet the current standards of Guidelines for Mitigation 
Units or which do not meet the Building Code standards for a bedroom. At its last meeting on August 
5, 2026 the Subcommittee requested that this topic be placed on the next agenda for discussion. 
 
Standards  for  and Construction Size  the Section 210 of  in  exists  Mitigation Units of Affordable 
Housing Guidelines (“Guidelines”)  “  that  include  standards . These  in part Areas for  designated 
sleeping and/or Bedrooms must be a minimum of one hundred (100) square feet, with a minimum 
width of ten (10) feet.” Mitigation Units must also of course “comply with applicable building codes 
and with rules, regulations and codes of all governmental bodies and agencies having jurisdiction.” 
The International Residential Code provides that a bedroom must have at least 70 square feet of floor 
area, a 7 -foot minimum ceiling height across half the room, an emergency egress window, and a 
permanent heat source to legally qualify as a bedroom. For reasons unknown, several Mitigation Units 
currently exist which do not meet these standards – particularly several Element 52 units which have 
bedrooms that lack windows in their entirety, not just emergency egress windows.  
 
Under the Guidelines, owners of such units who cannot or who do not wish to rent such rooms can 
seek an Administrative Exception under Section 903 – “Approval of Exceptions for Household falling 
below the Section 105.4F Continuing Household Size Standard by a maximum of one (1) person per 
Household.” However, staff is reluctant to grant any permanent Exceptions under the Administrative 
Exception process and would forward any such permanent  for request  to the  Subcommittee 
consideration. Staff also cannot approve any such request if it would lead to a Household falling 
below the standard by two or more persons, an any such request must go before the Subcommittee.  
 
The  question before  the  Subcommittee  today is  whether  the  existing processes  for  seeking an 
Exception  in  this  situation  are  sufficient,  or  if  the  Subcommittee  desires  to  make  it easier  for 
Households  in this  situation to not  be  required to rent  a  room  by  granting such Households  a 
preemptive and blanket Exception, or offering a different administrative review process . In making 
this decision, please consider:  
 
• At what point does a sub-par bedroom warrant being exempt from the occupancy requirement 
– if there is only no egress due to safety? If the room is too small, even if there are other factors 
that may make up for this? If the ceiling heights do not strictly match the Code?

• What if the bedroom met Code when it was issued a Certificate of Occupancy, but is now 
grandfathered in? Will new rooms that meet Code now, but later fail to meet Code due to 
Code changes, also be exempt from occupancy in the future?   
• Are there any unintended consequences by making non-occupancy of such rooms the new 
“rule”?  
 
DISCUSSION:  
The Subcommittee should consider the information above and current practices of staff in making 
these determination to decide if any changes are warranted to the Guidelines to better accommodate 
these situations, or if current Exception practices are sufficient to address these situations. Staff would 
caution the Subcommittee against over -legislating for unique situations. If this current practice and 
the availability of Exceptions is sufficient, no changes to the Guidelines should be directed. However, 
if the Subcommittee desires these situation to be more easily accommodated with less process for 
review, changes may be made to the Guidelines.  
RECOMMENDED MOTION: 
As this is a work session, no formal action may be taken. However, if after discussing this matter the 
Subcommittee determines more information is needed or that a change to the Guidelines is desired, 
the Subcommittee should direct staff to return at the next meeting to move forward with the discussion 
or amendments as appropriate.
