VII. ADJOURN 
  
Values Statement 
Our work will be guided, and decisions adjudicated, by our beliefs and commitment to: 
  
Preservation – We are the stewards of, and committed to the preservation of, the historic properties 
and the historic character of Telluride’s National Historic Landmark District. 
Efficiency – We value time and expediency, and come to each meeting prepared and understanding 
each application to the best of our individual ability. 
Respect – We are committed to conducting our meetings with respect for the opinions and 
contributions of applicants, staff, members of the public, and each other. Our discussions and 
deliberations are civil and considerate. 
Consistency – Our review of applications and decisions will be based on the Master Plan, Land Use 
Code, and the Design Guidelines and Standards for Building in Telluride to provide an outcome that 
is as fair, reasonable, and predictable. 
  
More complete information is on file and available at Rebekah Hall, 113 W. Columbia Ave., (970)-728-2170. If 
you would like to comment on the proposed development, please forward your comment letter to "Attention: 
Historic & Architectural Review Commission" by mail to P.O. Box 397, Telluride, CO, 81435, by fax to (970)- 
728-3078, by email to preservation@telluride.gov, or by person to Rebekah Hall at the address listed above.  
Written public comment must be submitted prior to 9 a.m. on the day of the HARC meeting to guarantee 
distribution to the HARC members and to be considered part of the meeting record. After 9 a.m. on the day of 
the HARC meeting, you must print and bring 10 copies to the HARC meeting and/or testify in person. In 
the interest of managing the agenda and time, the HARC Chair may request public comment to be kept to 3 
minutes per person.

The Payment in Lieu of Parking application is the aspect of the concurrent review being brought before HARC. 
 
In the Historic Residential Zone District, one off-street parking space is required per dwelling unit. Pursuant to 
LUC 3-205.E.2., the Planning Director may authorize, upon recommendation of HARC, payment-in-lieu of one 
required on-site parking space on any lot which: 
a. contains a rated and a non-rated structure; and 
b. contains a rear yard through which vehicle access to required parking is obstructed by either such structure; or  
c. in the case of a corner lot, contains a side yard through which access is so obstructed. 
 
This is an administrative level review and approval process. A recommendation from HARC allows this 
administrative process to proceed, based on the Applicant having met the criteria above, as demonstrated in the 
staff findings and analysis below.

Columbia Ave and N Townsend St. Brazy Townhouses is

STAFF FINDINGS AND ANALYSIS

one parking space was designated for each of the two dwelling units: one unenclosed space for the North Unit 
and one enclosed parking space to be located within the historic shed for the South Unit.  
 
The parking space within the Contributing shed is not a viable option in its current orientation and location. The 
historic shed, as situated, is too narrow and not tall enough to accommodate a vehicle. Access would have to be 
from Townsend, not from the alley but directly adjacent to it. The parking space in the shed is not a practical or 
viable option; as such, no parking has existed or been used by the owners of the North or South Units. The

Project Overview
Primrose Lane 785, LLC - Bret and Judy Stanley and their family - (“Owner”) is the owner of Lot 9, The 
Overlook at Telluride Subdivision/PUD (“Overlook Subdivision”) that has an address of 785 Primrose Lane 
(“Property”) as shown in Figure 1. The Property contains 8,281 sq. ft. (0.19 acres) and is located within the 
Hillside Developing II (“HD 2”) Zone District and the Developing Hillside Treatment Area. The Property is 
located outside of the Telluride Historic Landmark District with no historic structures in the area. The Property 
is identified by the Town Geohazard Map with “Generalized Slopes Over 50%” and “Rock Fall Hazard Zone A”. 
The Property’s building envelope has a low USGS elevation of 8888 feet in the southeast corner and a high 
elevation of 8942 feet in the northwest corner for an elevation gain of 54 feet with an approximate 44 percent 
grade. 
Tommy Hein and his team designed the home on the Property with the following key design principles:
• Respect for Topography. The home is stepped into the hillside, following the natural grade to minimize 
excavation, reduce perceived height, and respond to the site rather than alter it.
• Reduced Massing. The residence is broken into smaller horizontal volumes with recessed upper levels, 
reducing visual bulk and overall prominence.
• Landscape Integration. Architecture and landscape are conceived as a single composition through 
terraces, planters, native vegetation, and limited retaining walls, allowing the building to blend into its 
surroundings over time.
• Natural Materiality. Stone, patinated copper, and dark metal elements are selected to reflect the colors 
and textures of the surrounding mountain environment.
• Visual Restraint. Recessed glazing, deep overhangs, muted materials, and strong horizontal lines 
reduce glare, light spill, and visual impact from public viewpoints.

• HARC Alignment. Every design decision is guided by the goal of minimizing visual impact and creating a

DH 15 Utilities. The Property’s utilities were designed and installed per DH 15 as a part of the Overlook 
Subdivision development. Building ventilation will be located in the least visible areas to not detract from the 
design.
DH 16 Mass, Scale and Form. The proposed building is designed with low building forms and elements that 
blend into, evolve from and step down the hillside. 
DH 17 Roof Form. The flat and shed roof forms reinforce the slope of the hillside. The roofs do not impact 
views to significant natural features. Matte medium gray bonderized metal roofing is proposed for the shed 
roof forms; concrete terraces; green roofs and planters, and a low slope flat seam copper and flat seam 
bonderized roof proposed for the upper flat roof form. The roof lines are simple in form to blend with the 
natural environment and follow the topography.
DH 18 Architectural Character. The architectural form does not replicate historic buildings. Architectural 
character and design direction is further discussed under the background section.
DH 19 Building Components and Details. DH 19 Intro states:
“Because this area is establishing its own character and context, there will be greater latitude in terms 
of detailed architectural design. Designs that blend with the character of the natural hillside in material, 
color and texture are encouraged. Architectural details that suggest building styles found historically 
in Telluride are discouraged in this area, in order to help maintain a clear definition of the edge of the 
historic district.”
The building design and roof form blend into the natural hillside with muted colors and stepped architectural 
form. 
DH 20 Architectural Details. Architectural details are simple in design. 
DH 21 Pattern of Building Materials. The pattern of the exterior materials and their method of application is 
similar to those materials used historically in the town. 
DH 22 Building Materials. The building materials were chosen to blend into both the natural environment 
and also because they maintain the range of building materials that have been used historically. The proposed 
building materials are composed of natural colors. 
23 Windows. DH 23 Intro states: 
“HARC does not review window design, divisions or frames in the Developing Hillside Treatment Area. 
HARC will review the impacts of light spill from windows that can be easily seen from other parts of 
Telluride.”
DH 23.C reinforces this statement by stating “...nontraditional window patterns may be considered in some 
parts of this treatment area”. There are some conflicting window provisions in DH 23.B and D that state 
“windows should be of a traditional size and relate to a pedestrian scale” and “the placement and grouping of 
windows should be similar to that seen historically”. 
The proposed windows are designed with predominantly traditional, rectangular window groupings and 
pattern and appropriate window-to-wall ratio.
DH 24 Doors. DH 26 Intro states:
HARC does not review door design, divisions or frames in the Developing Hillside Treatment Area. HARC 
will review the impacts of light spill and concentration of glass in doors.”
Doors have been designed with the requisite 2/3 lites.
DH 25 Accessibility. Not applicable.
DH 26 Snow Shedding. Any roofs shedding snow or ice onto pedestrian areas will have snow guards installed. 
DH 27 Energy Conserving Design. The building is designed with strong passive solar design via the southern 
windows. The home will not block solar access for Lot 10 to the west due to the low profile home design.
