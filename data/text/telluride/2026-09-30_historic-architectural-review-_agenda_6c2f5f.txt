Values Statement 
  
Our work will be guided, and decisions adjudicated, by our beliefs and commitment to: 
Preservation – We are the stewards of, and committed to the preservation of, the historic properties and the historic 
character of Telluride’s National Historic Landmark District. 
Efficiency – We value time and expediency, and come to each meeting prepared and understanding each application to 
the best of our individual ability. 
Respect – We are committed to conducting our meetings with respect for the opinions and contributions of applicants, 
staff, members of the public, and each other. Our discussions and deliberations are civil and considerate. 
Consistency – Our review of applications and decisions will be based on the Master Plan, Land Use Code, and the 
Design Guidelines and Standards for Building in Telluride to provide an outcome that is fair, reasonable, and predictable.

PROJECT OVERVIEW 
 
The Project Site is comprised of two lots owned by 
the Town of Telluride which total 4.11 acres within 
the AC-2 Zone District. It is currently primarily a large 
public parking area.  
  
The Project stems from community values outlined in 
Telluride’s Master Plan and Southwest Area 
Conceptual Plan, adopted by Town Council in 2021, 
which outlines a comprehensive vision for revitalizing 
the southwest quadrant of Telluride, including the 
Carhenge property.  
 
Consistent with the goals and objectives of the 
Telluride Master Plan, the Project aims to: provide 
affordable housing; reduce car dependency; increase 
walkability; promote a sense of community; and 
contribute to a year-round economy that supports 
locals and visitors alike.

COMPLIANCE WITH DESIGN GUIDELINES 
 
Overall precepts of the Design Guidelines are: 
1. Keep it simple.  
2. Keep it in scale.  
3. Respect historic resources.  
4. Make all new design compatible with the existing context.  
 
The Accommodations Treatment Area (AC), River Park Corridor Overlay (RPC), and Exterior and Site Lighting 
(ES) Standards and Guidelines apply.  
 
Staff’s complete analysis of the 7/15/26 application’s compliance with relevant Design Guidelines was included 
in the Staff Memorandum for the 7/15/26 HARC Meeting. At that meeting, HARC Continued the public hearing 
with Conditions. Staff analysis of the Applicant’s compliance with the 7/15/26 HARC Conditions follows. 
Recommended Conditions based on the staff analysis are included in the proposed Motions. The staff 
presentation during the 9/30/26 Continued Public Hearing will supplement the analysis below.  
 
BACKGROUND 
 
The Design Guidelines for the AC Treatment Area recognize that large-scale projects on large parcels may be 
built in this area and provide specific guidance in these cases. Special importance is placed on the design of 
larger-scale projects to ensure that they are visually integrated in mass, scale, and character with the 
neighborhood and the Town. As seen from a distance, projects should appear to be a part of the community, 
with essentially the same perceived street/alley arrangements, building forms, and scale of development.  
 
The Applicant has reduced the buildings’ massing by establishing smaller modules, incorporating roof and wall 
plane articulation, and stepping down the structures towards the river. Larger buildings are divided into smaller, 
articulated modules, reducing the perceived scale and breaking up the massing. The Project is designed to 
step up from the river to the mountains; this is contextually appropriate in this part of Telluride. 
 
That said, as the Project’s design continues to evolve, there are specific aspects of the Project that could 
benefit from further refinement in order to demonstrate substantial compliance with the Design Guidelines.

SUMMARY 
This HARC Preliminary Large Scale Development application is submitted by Design 
Workshop on behalf of the T own of T elluride to advance a vision for the Carhenge 
Redevelopment project site (Lot 34 and 34B). 
The Carhenge Redevelopment Project represents a once-in-a-generation 
opportunity to advance some of T elluride’s most important community priorities: 
providing attainable housing for local residents, strengthening community 
connections, and investing in public-serving infrastructure that supports the 
town’s long-term vitality. In response to critical housing shortages and affordability 
challenges identified through extensive community planning efforts, the T own of 
T elluride is transforming an underutilized surface parking lot into a vibrant, mixed-
use neighborhood designed to serve the people who live and work in T elluride. This 
project represents a unique opportunity to address some of the community’s most 
urgent and long-standing needs, including affordable housing, expanded public 
amenities, and public-serving infrastructure. Guided by the vision and objectives 
established in the T elluride Master Plan, Southwest Area Conceptual Plan (SACP), and 
San Miguel County Housing Needs Analysis, the site is envisioned as a community 
hub where housing and community come together to support a resilient future for the 
T elluride community.  
The project transforms an existing surface parking lot into a mixed-use, pedestrian-
oriented neighborhood with affordable housing, public uses, open space, and 
structured parking. The site design strengthens connections between Lift 7 , the San 
Miguel River corridor, and greater T elluride.
The materials presented herein balance the project’s public objectives with the 
character, scale, and architectural values that define T elluride of the project in a 
manner consistent with the applicable review criteria, fulfilling a critical community 
need and advances the long-term vision of T elluride.  The proposal has been shaped 
through ongoing collaboration with T own staff, public stakeholders, and feedback 
received during HARC work sessions conducted throughout 2025 and spring 2026.

The Carhenge Redevelopment Project reflects the established development pattern 
of the T own through building scale, massing, rhythm, and materials that are compatible 
with the surrounding context. The buildings for the project are broken into smaller 
components that relate to the historic lot and block pattern, with roof forms, facade 
treatments, and architectural details that reduce perceived mass and create visual 
interest. The architecture will follow the HARC practices to; keep it simple; keep it in 
scale; respect the historic resources; and make all new designs compatible with the 
existing context.
Site planning should preserve important views, enhance pedestrian connections, 
and create high-quality public spaces that contribute to the vitality of the community. 
Successful projects within the treatment area demonstrate a thoughtful balance 
between historic compatibility and modern functionality, resulting in development that 
complements T elluride’s unique cultural, architectural, and environmental character.
The following demonstrates how the proposed development has been designed to 
meet these guidelines.
1.  Policy: Context Compatibility. The architecture responds to the local context 
of T elluride, incorporating forms that mirror the region’s historic attributes and utilizing 
materials that reflect the natural character of the T own, while remaining distinctly 
contemporary in style.
2.  Policy: Relationship to Site Context. The surrounding treatment area includes 
other multifamily accommodations, specifically Cimarron Lodge, Mountain Village Inn, 
and Element 52, similar in mass and scale to the proposed architectural elements.
3.  Policy: Natural Resources.  Existing wetlands on the site will be protected and 
restored to comply with regulations. Protecting existing vegetation along the River 
Corridor will be preserved.. 
4. Policy: On-Site Hazards. The project incorporates considerations for hazards, 
including floodplain and rockfall. 
5.  Policy: Relationship to the T own Grid. The project mirrors the town grid in 
Buildings A, B, C, and D. Building E follows the hillsides and frames view corridors to 
the east and west, creating a pedestrian-oriented park space.
6.  Policy: Pedestrian Scale. The site plan is organized around several buildings to 
create a connected network of open spaces. A central gathering plaza offers ground-
floor activating uses, with shaded outdoor seating areas, and captures views of the 
mountains. These ground-floor establishments, such as restaurants and retail, create 
a vibrant pedestrian realm.
7 .  Policy: Bicycle Systems. Bike parking is provided at key locations around the site. 
8.  Policy: Views. The orientation of the buildings and primary rooflines reinforces 
view corridors to and from the Lift 7 base area and mountain views to the east.
9.  Policy: Site Furniture. Site furnishings will be simple and reflect the natural 
aesthetic of T elluride, using stone and wood elements.

10.  Policy: Public Art. Public art may be considered in plaza and park spaces in the 
future. 
11.  Policy: Site Drainage.  Drainage will be directed away from buildings and public 
spaces, captured in a storm drain system. 
12.  Policy: Cut and Fill of Steep Slopes. The site is generally flat, minimizing cut-
and-fill. Building E integrates with the steep slopes to minimize overall mass and scale 
while maintaining views of the mountain. As needed, retaining walls will be of natural 
stone materials.
13.  Policy: Positive Open Space.  The project emphasizes a network of open 
spaces, including a central green and play area, community gathering and seating 
areas, and recreation spaces along north-to-south and east-to-west corridors.
14.  Policy: Site Planning. The site responds to the existing context, with access 
across the existing T omboy bridge and a simple circulation pathway directing 
vehicular traffic to sub-grade parking.
15.  Policy: Building Orientation. Buildings A, B, C, and D are oriented east-west, 
aligning with the grid and following the River’s directional flow. Building E follows the 
natural contours of the hillside topography.
16.  Policy: Building Setbacks. The AC2 Zone District outlines several dimensional 
limitations in section 3-210.C. This project will maintain a 25’ setback from the San 
Miguel River. No high-intensity uses are proposed along the San Miguel River. 
17 .  Policy: Plant Materials. Plant materials feature a simple palette, using native or 
adaptive water-wise species. Planting will align with the current regulations outlined in 
the 2026 Colorado Fire Resiliency code. Screening of adjacent properties and utilities 
will be provided as needed. 
18.  Policy: Fences and Walls. No fencing is proposed on the site. Low retaining 
walls will be made of natural materials and utilize terracing when needed. 
19.  Policy: Automobile Circulation. The circulation network prioritizes pedestrians 
and bicycles with safe, connected pathways while limiting vehicular access and 
strengthening connections to Lift 7 and surrounding neighborhoods.
20.  Policy: Parking Design. The parking strategy prioritizes safety and minimizes 
visual impact while aligning with the T own’s goals for walkability, reduced car 
dependence, and neighborhood compatibility. The parking layout is intentionally 
compact, efficient, and located within buildings, either as tuck under parking in a 
basement level grade parking, minimizing visibility and presence of vehicles on the 
site.  The project is designed to meet the parking requirements of the underlying zone 
district.
21.  Policy: Service Areas. Service areas will be accessed along the primary 
roadways between Buildings B and D. Fire truck access has been provided to the east 
and west of the site, including vehicular access on the plaza for maintenance and fire 
trucks. Loading for commercial will be accommodated on the north side of Building D. 
22.  Policy: Utilities. Utilities will be upgraded across the site to accommodate this 
development, including storm, water, and electric services.  Utilities will be located 
outside the pedestrian realm while maintaining access for maintenance and being 
appropriately screened from view.  
23.  Policy: Mass and Scale. Articulated massing, varied setbacks, and separation 
between structures preserve views, improve solar access, and create positive open 
spaces throughout the site. Building heights and massing step down toward the river 
corridor to create a sensitive transition to surrounding properties and natural features.
24.  Policy: Building Form. The development is organized as several buildings 
rather than a single large structure to reduce perceived scale and reflect the 
established development pattern of T elluride. 
25.  Policy: Directional Emphasis. Buildings are oriented to align with the town grid 
and the San Miguel River. Building E is oriented to align with the mountain. 
26.  Policy: Roof Form. Roof forms emphasize simple/rational gabled and 
shed forms with steep pitches, dormers, and secondary roof elements, inspired 
by the surrounding hillsides and mountains. Varied rooflines help break down the 
development’s scale while maintaining compatibility with traditional roof proportions 
and forms found throughout town. Roof forms are oriented to provide a human-scaled 
pedestrian experience.
27 .  Policy: Architectural Character. Architectural character is simple and honors 
the vernacular of the town of T elluride while being appropriate to the current design 
context. No elaborate design elements are proposed. 
28.  Policy: Building Components. Elements such as dormers are used to provide 
visual interest and reduce the perceived scale of the buildings. Decks and balconies 
provide an outdoor living space. Awnings are used along the commercial frontage to 
emphasize the spaces and create respite for outdoor seating. 
29.  Policy: Architectural Details. Architectural details remain simple and clean, 
supporting the architecture’s visual lines and creating interest without overwhelming or 
creating monotonous facades. 
30.  Policy: Pattern of Building Materials., Building materials are vertically oriented 
to reflect the verticality of the natural surroundings, using natural materials when 
possible, to fit into the natural setting of T elluride and the base mountain area. 
31.  Policy: Building Materials. The project incorporates a durable and context-
sensitive palette of materials consistent with historic development patterns in T elluride, 
including fire-resistant stained wood siding and standing-seam metal roofing. 
Material selection, colors, and textures are designed to integrate the buildings into the 
surrounding river corridor and mountain environment.
32.  Policy: Windows. Windows and doors are organized to provide quality access 
to light and connect residents to nature. Vertically oriented windows, recessed 
openings, and appropriate window-to-wall ratios are incorporated into the design. 
33.  Policy: Doors. Doors are located to support an active pedestrian experience. 
Ground-floor entries and storefronts are oriented toward pedestrian streets, plazas, 
and open spaces to support neighborhood activity.
34.  Policy: Accessibility. Accessibility will be provided internally in each building.  
An accessible route will be provided along the eastern and western portions of the site.
35.  Policy: Snow Shedding. Roof design responds to environmental 
considerations such as snow shedding, drainage, and solar access, integrating 
functional performance with T elluride’s architectural character. Roofs are oriented to 
avoid snow shedding in pedestrian circulation areas between buildings.
36.  Policy: Energy Conserving Design. Energy conservation aligns with the 
current standards of the T elluride Building Code. No solar is proposed for this project.

LEGEND
Pedestrian circulation will be through building cores to access 
residential units. An accessible route will circulate through the 
community plaza and park spaces. 
Vehicular access is secondary, vehicles on site will turn left upon 
entering the site to access underground parking structures.  These 
pathways will provide access for drop off, waste trucks, and other 
back of house uses, away from the main pedestrian travel ways. 
This minimizes the land dedicated to vehicular circulation without 
compromising function or safety. Drive aisles are purposefully 
narrow and shared surfaces have been introduced where 
appropriate to create a more efficient and pedestrian-friendly public 
realm. 
Parking is concentrated in discrete and enclosed locations to 
reduce overall pavement area, creating opportunities for meaningful 
open space. 
Emergency vehicle access occurs through appropriately sized 
fire lanes and turning radii in accordance with local fire code 
requirements.

The following memorandum includes a response to comments received at the Preliminary Large-
Scale Carhenge Redevelopment Project at 700 W Pacific Ave for three separate applications, 
including (1) Buildings A, B, and C; (2) Buildings D1 and D2; (3) Buildings E1, E2, and E3.   
 
Buildings A, B, and C   
 
1. Significantly reduce the perceived mass and scale of Buildings A, B, and C by: incorporating 
additional variations in vertical wall planes, increasing articulation of horizontal wall planes, 
stepping down facades towards the roads and pedestrian pathways, breaking up the roof 
ridges, varying the building heights, and/or increasing the number of building modules, 
consistent with AC1 – A.3., AC23 – C, C.1, C.2, C.3, D, E, G, I, I.1, I.2, I.3, I.4, J, and J.1; AC24 – 
A.3; and AC26.  
 
Friendly Amendment to add the citations in the AC district to Condition #1 to include AC.6, 
AC.13, and AC.14.

into the south façade to create a step down toward the adjacent pedestrian plaza/street 
(AC23.D) and increases wall plane variation (AC23.C.1) along the eastern, western, and 
southern facades. This modulation gives the appearance of a cluster of smaller buildings 
(AC23.C.3, AC23.I, AC24.A.3) and follows the proportions and scale of buildings in Telluride
