Telluride Ecology Commission’s 2026 Work Plan 
 
This work plan has been compiled as a tool to help the Ecology Commission address issues of ecological concern, 
as identified within the Town’s Municipal Code. These issues shall include, but not be limited to, the following: 
 
• address issues that impact the quality, health, and welfare of natural ecosystems within the urban core and 
at the boundaries between the urbanized environment and adjacent wildlands 
• address human-wildlife interactions of concern. Such work shall address mechanisms that reduce or 
eliminate interactions that may pose a threat to wildlife and/or the safety of the general public. 
•  conduct research and strive to educate the community about methods to mitigate and minimize everyday 
adverse impacts on the environment, including but not limited to invasive flora and fauna, landscaping 
practices, and de-icing practices. 
• stay apprised of issues that may impact the Town’s air quality and develop and advocate for solutions to 
such issues. 
• stay apprised of issues that may impact water quality in the Town’s streams, rivers, and wetlands, as well 
as its drinking water and develop and advocate for solutions to such issues 
• work cooperatively with regional organizations, as appropriate, to facilitate community sustainability.  
 
The charge to the commission is to provide the following: 
 Research and Inform 
• Work in a coordinated fashion, respect all points of view, and, in carrying out its duties and tasks, shall 
adhere to the Town’s municipal code as well as the values and mission of the Town of Telluride. 
• Review and understand the Town’s current Climate Action Plan and provide recommendations to Town 
Staff regarding required 5-year update to the plan.  
• Research and advise on other tools that could be used to encourage greenhouse gas reduction and 
ecological concern mitigation. 
 
Educate and Advocate 
• To provide a means for dialogue between the Town and community stakeholders. 
• Be knowledgeable environmental professionals and stewards who have unique insights into 
environmental best practices and the best ways to promote said practices in the Town of Telluride. 
• To promote intergovernmental and public/private cooperation on environmental policies

it  the  from support  with together flexibility, identified. Having  not projects  other  work on preclude this  Town 
Council and other stakeholders, helps the Commission best serve the community.

ORDINANCE NO. ____, SERIES OF 2026 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE TOWN OF TELLURIDE, COLORADO, 
AMENDING TELLURIDE MUNICIPAL CODE CHAPTER 7, HEALTH, SANITATION AND 
ANIMALS, BY THE ADDITION OF A NEW ARTICLE 10, “PHASE-OUT OF COMBUSTION 
LAWN AND GARDEN EQUIPMENT” 
 
RECITALS 
WHEREAS, gasoline-powered lawn and garden equipment — particularly handheld equipment using two-
stroke engines — produces air pollution grossly disproportionate to its size, with the California Air 
Resources Board estimating that operating a commercial gas leaf blower for one hour emits smog-forming 
pollution comparable to driving a modern passenger car more than 1,000 miles; and 
WHEREAS, such equipment routinely produces noise of 90 to 110 decibels at the operator’s ear and carries 
low-frequency noise across long distances — effects amplified within Telluride’s box canyon and compact 
residential fabric, where no yard is far from a neighbor’s window — harming the hearing of operators and 
degrading the quiet enjoyment of property; and 
WHEREAS, emissions from combustion lawn and garden equipment are inconsistent with the Town’s 
adopted climate and greenhouse -gas-reduction goals, while battery -electric equipment is now widely 
available, comparable in performance for the small-parcel conditions typical of Telluride, quieter, cleaner, 
and cheaper to operate and maintain; and 
WHEREAS, more than two hundred local governments across the United States have restricted or phased 
out gas-powered lawn equipment, including the District of Columbia and Montgomery County, Maryland; 
the State of California has required new small off -road engines s old in the state to be zero -emission 
beginning with model year 2024; and the Colorado Air Quality Control Commission has prohibited state 
agencies, and certain Front Range local governments, from operating small gas-powered equipment during 
the summer ozone season; and 
WHEREAS, the transition is substantially subsidized today: Colorado law (HB23 -1161) provides a 30% 
point-of-sale discount on electric lawn mowers, leaf blowers, trimmers, and snow blowers at participating 
retailers, including within the Town, and the San Miguel Pow er Association’s Beneficial Electrification 
program provides additional rebates of up to 25% on electric outdoor equipment, such that a resident or 
business replacing equipment during the phase-in period may offset roughly half of its cost; and 
WHEREAS, a phase -out schedule extending to 2028 for leaf blowers and 2030 for all other covered 
equipment, together with the incentives described above, the extended -use defense, and the exception for 
pre-adoption dual -use enclosed -cab tractors provided herein, giv es current owners and operators a 
reasonable period to obtain full use of existing equipment and to plan replacement purchases, and ensures 
that no equipment purchased before adoption of this ordinance is stranded; and 
WHEREAS, the Town Council finds that this ordinance is necessary for the preservation of the public 
health, safety, and welfare;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE TOWN OF 
TELLURIDE, COLORADO: 
 
Section 1. Amendment. 
Chapter 7 of the Telluride Municipal Code, Health, Sanitation and Animals, is hereby amended by the 
addition of a new Article 10, “Phase-Out of Combustion Lawn and Garden Equipment,” to read as follows: 
 
Sec. 7-10-10. Purpose. 
The purpose of this Article is to phase out the use of combustion -powered lawn and garden equipment 
within the Town in order to reduce noise, protect local air quality and public health, and reduce greenhouse 
gas emissions, while providing owners and opera tors reasonable lead time and directing them to available 
financial incentives for replacement with electric equipment. 
Sec. 7-10-20. Definitions. 
equipment garden and lawn Combustion  spark a by powered equipment any means -or ignition 
compression-ignition internal combustion engine and designed or used for lawn or garden maintenance, 
including without limitation: lawn mowers (walk-behind or ride-on), leaf blowers and leaf vacuums, string 
trimmers and weed whackers, edgers, hedge trimmers, brush cutters, lawn aerators, dethatchers, and power 
rakes. 
Combustion lawn and garden equipment  does not include: (a) snow removal equipment of any kind, 
consistent with Section 7 -2-20(g) of this Code; (b) chainsaws; (c) portable generators; (d) construction 
equipment; or (e) agricultural equipment used in bona fide agricultural operations. 
Leaf blower means any portable machine, whether handheld, backpack, or wheeled, used to blow, vacuum, 
or disperse leaves, grass clippings, dirt, or other debris by means of moving air. 
Sec. 7-10-30. Town Procurement and Operations. 
(a) Effective immediately upon the effective date of this ordinance, the Town shall not purchase, lease, or 
otherwise acquire any new or used combustion lawn and garden equipment. 
(b) The Town’s use of combustion lawn and garden equipment shall cease no later than the dates set forth 
in Section 7 -10-40, subject to the exception set forth in Section 7 -10-40(d), and Town departments are 
encouraged to complete the transition earlier as existing equipment reaches the end of its service life. 
Sec. 7-10-40. Phase-Out of Use. 
(a) Leaf blowers. On and after January 1, 2028, no person shall operate a combustion-powered leaf blower 
within the Town. 
(b) All other covered equipment.  On and after January 1, 2030, no person shall operate any combustion 
lawn and garden equipment within the Town. 
Page 9 of 12
(c) This Section applies to all persons, including residents, property owners, commercial landscaping and 
property-maintenance operators, and the Town, on both public and private property within the Town limits. 
(d) Exception for pre -adoption dual-use tractors.  Subsection (b) of this Section shall not apply to the 
operation of a tractor equipped with an enclosed operator cab that (1) was acquired by the operator, or by 
the operator’s employer, prior to the date of adoption of this ordinance, and (2) is used seasonally for both 
lawn or garden maintenance and snow removal. The burden of establishing that equipment qualifies under 
this subsection rests with the operator. This subsection does not authorize the acquisition of combustion 
equipment otherwise prohibited by Section 7-10-30(a). 
Sec. 7-10-50. Extended-Use Defense for Pre-Adoption Purchases. 
(a) It shall be an affirmative defense to a citation issued under Section 7 -10-40 that the equipment in 
question was purchased new by the cited person, or by the cited person’s employer, prior to the date of 
adoption of this ordinance, and that the citatio n was issued: (1) for a leaf blower, before January 1, 2030; 
or (2) for any other covered equipment, before January 1, 2032. 
(b) The defense under this Section shall be established by presentation of a dated proof of purchase, invoice, 
or equivalent documentation identifying the equipment. The burden of establishing the defense rests 
entirely with the cited person. No applicatio n to, registration with, or approval by the Town is required or 
available under this Section. 
Sec. 7-10-60. Exemptions. 
This Article shall not apply to: (a) equipment operated in connection with an emergency affecting public 
health or safety, or at the direction of the Town, the Telluride Fire Protection District, or another public 
safety agency; (b) wildfire mitigation, hazard tree removal, or defensible-space work performed by or under 
the direction of a public agency or fire district; or (c) any use during a Town-declared emergency. 
Sec. 7-10-70. Enforcement and Penalties. 
(a) This Article shall be enforced by the Town’s code enforcement officers and the Town Marshal’s 
Department, primarily on a complaint basis. 
(b) The first violation of this Article by any person in a calendar year shall result in a written warning, 
together with written information regarding available electric-equipment incentives. Subsequent violations 
within the same calendar year shall be punishable by a fine of not more than one hundred dollars ($100.00) 
for a second violation, two hundred fifty dollars ($250.00) for a third violation, and the maximum penalty 
set forth in Section 1-4-10(a)(2) of this Code for each violation thereafter. 
(c) For commercial operators, both the equipment operator and the business entity may be cited. 
Sec. 7-10-80. Incentive Outreach. 
Through December 31, 2030, the Town shall, at least annually and at the start of each summer season, 
publicize the availability of the State of Colorado point -of-sale discount on electric lawn and garden 
equipment, the San Miguel Power Association Beneficial Electrification rebate program, and any successor 
or additional incentive programs, and shall coordinate with San Miguel Power Association, EcoAction 
Partners, and local retailers to facilitate resident and business participation.

Section 2. Severability. 
If any section, phrase, clause, or portion of this ordinance is held invalid by a court of competent 
jurisdiction, such invalidity shall not affect the validity of the remaining portions of this ordinance. 
Section 3. Effective Date. 
This ordinance shall become effective upon the date of publication of notice of its adoption as required by 
the Telluride Home Rule Charter, provided that the prohibitions in Section 7-10-40 shall take effect on the 
dates stated therein.

COUNCIL MEMORANDUM 
To: Mayor and Town Council, Town of Telluride

Date: ____________, 2026 
Re: Ordinance Phasing Out Combustion Lawn Equipment (new TMC Art. 7-10) 
 
RECOMMENDATION 
Adopt the attached ordinance, which (1) immediately bars the Town government from purchasing combustion lawn 
and garden equipment; (2) phases out the operation of gas leaf blowers for all users, public and private, by January 
1, 2028; and (3) phases out all remaining combustion lawn equipment by January 1, 2030. 
WHY 
• Noise. Gas equipment runs 90–110 dB at the operator and carries far in the box canyon; electric equivalents are 
dramatically quieter for neighbors, visitors, and workers. 
• Local air quality. CARB estimates one hour of commercial gas leaf blower use emits smog-forming pollution 
comparable to driving a modern car 1,000+ miles — in lab testing, a single two -stroke blower emitted more 
smog-forming pollution than a full-size pickup. 
• Climate. Nationally, gas lawn and garden equipment emits ~30 million tons of CO₂ per year (EPA 2020 
emissions inventory); elimination of small-engine combustion supports the Town’s adopted emissions goals. 
• Precedent. Over 200 U.S. local governments have restricted gas lawn equipment (e.g., Washington, D.C.; 
Montgomery County, MD); California requires new small off -road engines to be zero -emission; Colorado 
restricts government use on the Front Range. 
WHAT THE ORDINANCE DOES 
• Immediate: Town may not buy or lease combustion lawn equipment (self-enforcing procurement rule). 
• Jan. 1, 2028: no combustion leaf blowers — the worst offenders and cheapest to replace. 
• Jan. 1, 2030: no combustion mowers, trimmers, edgers, or similar equipment, townwide. 
• Carve-outs: snow removal (consistent with TMC 7 -2-20(g)), chainsaws, generators, wildfire mitigation and 
defensible-space work, and emergencies. 
• Dual-use tractors: enclosed-cab tractors acquired before adoption and used seasonally for both mowing and 
snow removal (e.g., Parks & Recreation’s plow -equipped cab tractor) may serve out their working lives. The 
procurement ban still ensures their eventual replacements are electric. 
FAIRNESS WITHOUT BUREAUCRACY 
Rather than a discretionary waiver program, the ordinance provides a self -executing affirmative defense: anyone 
cited may defeat the citation with dated proof that the equipment was purchased new before adoption, extending 
lawful use up to two years past t he applicable deadline (to 2030 for blowers; 2032 for other equipment). Together 
with the dual-use tractor exception, the principle is simple: no machine bought before this ordinance is stranded. No 
applications, no registrations, no staff review — the burden of proof sits with the owner, and the cost to the Town is 
zero unless a citation is contested. 
TRANSITION SUPPORT ALREADY IN PLACE 
Colorado’s HB23-1161 provides a 30% point -of-sale discount on electric lawn equipment at participating retailers 
(including in Town), and SMPA’s Beneficial Electrification program adds rebates of up to 25%. Combined, a 
replacement purchase during the phase -in window can be roughly half price. Because the state discount is not 
guaranteed indefinitely, residents should be encouraged to buy early. The ordinance directs annual Town outreach 
on these incentives through 2030. 
ENFORCEMENT AND FISCAL IMPACT 
Complaint-based, via Code Enforcement and the Marshal’s Department; the first violation each year draws a written 
warning packaged with incentive information, then escalating fines ($100 / $250 / Code maximum). Fiscal impact is 
minimal: no new staff, no wa iver administration, and Town equipment turns over on the same public schedule as 
everyone else’s.
