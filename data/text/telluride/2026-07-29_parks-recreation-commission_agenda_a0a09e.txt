Title: Consideration of a Request by the Telluride Humane Society for 
Use of Elks Park and the River Trail from Maple St. to Gold Run 
for Telluride Tails on the Trail on October 10, 2026 
To: Parks & Rec. Commission 
Meeting Date: July 29, 2026 
Submitted By: Amanda Baltzley, Parks & Recreation 
Attachments: Humane Society 5K Application 
 
Introduction: 
  
The Telluride Humane Society is requesting use of Elks Park and the River Trail from 
Maple St. to Gold Run for Telluride Tails on the Trails on October 10, 2026. The 
planned 5k will start at Elks Park and run down Colorado Ave. to the River Trail 
entrance at Maple St. and return back from Idarodo to the River Trail, then back on 
Colorado Ave. from Maple St. to Elks Park. Street use is pending staff review and 
approval.  
 
Please see the attached Special Event Application.   
  
Background: 
  
Event Description: Event organizers are looking to organizing a 5K fun run to raise 
funds for the Telluride Humane Society. The event would welcomes locals, visitors, and 
dog lovers who want to get outside and support a great cause. All participants would 
complete a 5K course, followed by a community celebration with music, coffee, food, 
and socializing. The event will temporarily utilize sections of the river trail and the trail to 
the Idorado Mine, and will occupy Elks Park for a limited time. We would love to have a 
vendor at the park afterward from a pet store, and a coffee shop. 
  
The proposed schedule is as follows: 
 Set-up: 7:00 a.m. - 8:30 a.m. 
 Event: 8:30 a.m. - 12:00 p.m. 
 Teardown: 12:15 p.m. to 2:15 p.m.  
 Amplified Sound: 9:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. 
Event organizers expect to set-up a couple of 10x10 tents and tables in Elks Park.  
Page 5 of 159
  
The event organizer is expecting approximately 100 total attendees for the event. 
  
There will be a fee charged to run the 5K, but Elks Park will be open to the public for 
free. 
  
Analysis/Financial Analysis: 
  
Events 
  
The proposed event dates will not overlap with any other special events using Elks Park 
or the River Trail. 
  
Financial 
  
Each race registration sold (maximum of 100) per ordinance, requires an admissions 
fee of $.85 per person paid to the Town of Telluride. The admissions fee collected from 
the proposed event (less up to 15 complimentary tickets) would result in maximum 
revenue of $72.25.   
Recommendation: 
Review Criteria         (adopted by the P&R Commission on October 19, 2011)  
  
The Parks and Recreation Commission should use the review criteria listed below when 
considering new or changed event requests for use of Town Park facilities.  The review 
criteria are not weighted or prioritized and include the following:  
1. Venue availability 
2. Impacts to the following: 
a. Town Park facilities 
b. Parks and Recreation Department programs 
c. Parks and Recreation Department staff resources 
d. The environment within Town Park 
e. The neighborhood surrounding Town Park 
f. Existing special events in Town Park 
3. Financial impacts to Town Park 
4. Past performance (resume for new applicant) 
5. Crowd size

Introduction: 
The purpose of this worksession is to review and discuss the Town Park Oval Improvements 
Project and specifically the Oval surfacing plan with regards to the project goals, design and 
uses, management, facility scheduling and community impacts.  Improvements focus on active 
and passive recreation, infrastructure, and public gathering space that serves residents and 
visitors year-round. 
 
Staff will provide an overview of the project followed by public comments and potential P&R 
Commission direction on next steps regarding project goals, preferred design option, and 
preliminary input on facility uses and management strategies.  
 
Extensive outreach occurred in advance of this meeting to encourage community participation 
and input, including an email blast (Exhibit A) sent to 3,754 people on July 18 in addition to 
direct emails to project stakeholders.

Background: 
  
The Oval Improvements Project is the third phase of implementation as part of the Core Area 
Improvements Project, as outlined in the 2020 Town Parks Master Plan: 
  
  Phase 1: Skatepark 
  Phase 2: Warming Hut and Washstand Facility 
  Phase 3: Oval Improvements & Warner Field Safety Netting  
Public engagement occurred through all phases of the Core Area Improvements Project from 
2020 – 2025 as outlined in the attached Core Area Improvements project timeline summary 
(Exhibit D). 
  
The current phase of this project received all required design approvals from the Parks & 
Recreation Commission and Historic and Architectural Review Commission (HARC): 
  
 P&R Commission approved schematic designs in September 2021 and reviewed 
subsequent plan updates in June 2025 
 HARC issued the Certificate of Appropriateness in December 2025 
Additionally, construction documents and permitting are complete. 
  
Town Council determined this project a priority for year 2026 on the Town Council Goals & 
Objectives and appropriated capital funds for the project across two budget years: 
  
 2026: $800,000 for Phase 1, including Warner Field safety netting, storm drainage, and 
as much sitework as weather allows. 
o Phase 1 work will continue this Fall as proposed as it does not directly relate to 
the Oval surfacing plan (phase 2) which will be further discussed and evaluated 
in the upcoming months. The bid process for Phase 1 work is currently 
underway. No construction contracts have been executed at this time. Phase 1 
construction is anticipated to proceed this Fall. 
 2027: $1,334,300 for Phase 2, including remaining sitework, concrete court surfaces, 
equipment installation, landscaping, and revegetation.

Dear Council Members & Parks & Rec. Department, 
 
I am writing to express my strong opposition to the plan by the Town of 
Telluride and Parks & Recreation to pave the grassy oval in Telluride 
Town Park. 
 
While I agree that additional facilities for pickleball, tennis, and 
basketball are needed, this specific grassy area is one of the few 
remaining open green spaces within the park. It is frequently utilized 
by locals and is often the only area available for public use when the 
rest of the park is closed for festivals. Paving this section would create 
a "concrete jungle" feel at the entrance of Town Park, which does not 
align with the atmosphere Telluride aims to convey. 
 
For a long time, locals have enjoyed this area for birthday parties, 
kids' events, and picnics. The lack of advanced planning for larger 
court facilities during the previous pool and Hanley Pavilion redesign 
should not result in the loss of a lovely grassy area that has always 
been cherished in its current form. 
 
There are several other locations where these courts could be 
situated, such as behind the Hanley Park pavilion/hockey rink on the 
south side, or closer to the skateboard park. Paving the oval would 
mean that the stretch from the "bra" to the festival grounds would be 
entirely pavement. This is particularly concerning as the festival 
grounds are frequently closed to taxpayers, while this grassy area has 
traditionally remained accessible. 
 
I urge you to find another location for the new courts and preserve this 
grassy area for lawn games, gatherings, and the community traditions 
it supports.
