EXTENSION OF THE CERTIFICATE OF APPROPRIATENESS 
Background.  For a development approved under the Historic and Architectural Review Commission 
provisions of Article 7 of the Land Use Code (LUC) of the Town of Telluride, a “Certificate of 
Appropriateness” (“CA”) is granted for a period of 3 years. The LUC authorizes HARC to grant an 
extension for up to three years. Extensions are granted by the reviewing body which granted the initial 
final approval if all of the criteria are met.  
 
Approval Criteria for the Extension of a C.A. Approval of the extension is based on the following 
two criteria, pursuant to LUC Section 7-211.J: 
 
7-211.J.1. H.A.R.C. may grant an extension of the activity approval for an additional period of up to 
three (3) years if H.A.R.C. determines the activity is consistent with current Design 
Guidelines and Design Considerations, as stated in Section 7-105, and the purposes of 
Article 7, Division 1, and if the applicant demonstrates a legitimate need to delay the start of 
construction, including inclement weather, financing difficulties, or similar factors.  
7-211.J.2. An application for extension of a CA shall be submitted to the Planning Director no less 
than two (2) months prior to the expiration of the CA and H. 
 
STAFF ANALYSIS  
There are only two criteria that must be met to grant an extension to a Certificate of Appropriateness.  
 
Criterion #1 is for the HARC Chair to determine if the approval is still consistent with the current 
Design Guidelines and Design Considerations; and if the applicant has demonstrated a legitimate 
need to delay construction.  
 
The applicant has provided a narrative (attached) that describes the proceeding adjacent construction 
which has delayed construction of this project. Staff find the project to be consistent with the 2025 
Design Guidelines. The HARC Chair must determine if the applicant has demonstrated a legitimate 
need to delay construction.  
 
HARC Chair may not make changes to the design at this time. The Chair may either approve the CA 
extension with the previously approved site-specific development plans, or deny the CA extension, 
which will cause the CA to expire on unless a building permit application is submitted 30 days prior to 
the expiration date.   
 
Criterion #2 has been met as the request for a CA extension was submitted at least two months prior 
to its expiration. Staff recommend approval of the request as it meets both criteria for CA extensions.

Rockfall Hazard Evaluation 
 
  Rockfall hazard exists wherever rock has the potential to dislodge and move downhill by forces 
of gravity.  This process is usually associated with a weathering of formational material.  Freeze-
thaw cycles and availability of free water promote rockfall; therefore, spring is the most active 
season for rockfall.  The steep topography and fractured rock outcrops that are common to this 
region make rockfall a common hazard.  
 
  Rockfall can occur without warning and can be destructive to both life and property.  Rockfall 
frequency is very difficult to predict, but modeling techniques allow us to estimate the trajectory 
and intensity of rockfall events.  Simulation of rockfall events to provide an analysis of the 
potential destructive properties are typically performed using field mapping and observations in 
addition to computer modeling analysis.  
 
Field Reconnaissance and Analysis   
 
  The outcrops of the Entrada Formation are the main potential source areas for rockfall north of 
the project site.  Based on our experience in the area rockfall from the Entrada Formation 
impacts much of the north side of Telluride.  We performed a site reconnaissance of the property 
and slope above the site on August 13, 2019.  Photographs from our site reconnaissance are 
provided below.
