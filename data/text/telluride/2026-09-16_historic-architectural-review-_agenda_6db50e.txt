VIII. ADJOURN 
  
Values Statement 
Our work will be guided, and decisions adjudicated, by our beliefs and commitment to: 
  
Preservation – We are the stewards of, and committed to the preservation of, the historic properties 
and the historic character of Telluride’s National Historic Landmark District. 
Efficiency – We value time and expediency, and come to each meeting prepared and understanding 
each application to the best of our individual ability. 
Respect – We are committed to conducting our meetings with respect for the opinions and 
contributions of applicants, staff, members of the public, and each other. Our discussions and 
deliberations are civil and considerate. 
Consistency – Our review of applications and decisions will be based on the Master Plan, Land Use 
Code, and the Design Guidelines and Standards for Building in Telluride to provide an outcome that 
is as fair, reasonable, and predictable. 
  
More complete information is on file and available at Rebekah Hall, 113 W. Columbia Ave., (970)728-2170. If 
you would like to comment on the proposed development, please forward your comment letter to "Attention:

PROJECT OVERVIEW 
 
This Work Session is an opportunity for the Applicant to have a preliminary conversation with HARC regarding 
a potential demolition of an existing municipal building and construction of a new municipal building at 113 W 
Columbia Ave. The new building is proposed to be occupied by the Telluride Marshal’s Department. Work 
Sessions provide an opportunity for the Applicant to seek guidance from HARC prior to preparing and 
submitting a formal HARC application.  
 
HARC is requested to provide general feedback on the information presented by the Applicant during the Work 
Session. HARC should focus their review on the areas within which HARC has decision-making purview. 
HARC’s preliminary evaluation during a Work Session is not intended to, and shall not, vest the Applicant or 
public with any reliance as to the outcome of the formal development review process, per LUC 5-201.C.5.

PROJECT BACKGROUND/CONTEXT 
 
The below background on the Town of Telluride Facilities Master Plan provides context for the proposed 
redevelopment of the 113 W Columbia site. It is not intended to be used as part of the design review process. 
 
In December 2023, the Town initiated a partnership with Hellmuth, Obata & Kassabaum, Inc. (“HOK”) and 
Charles Cunniffe Architects (“CCA”) to formulate a comprehensive facilities needs assessment and plan. The 
primary objective was to devise a future-oriented solution that optimizes service delivery to the community, 
streamlines staff efficiencies, and accommodates growth and requirements over the next decade and beyond.  
 
As part of the project scope, an evaluation of Town Departments and existing facilities was conducted. This 
assessment involved on-site inspections, in-depth programming interviews with department heads, and review 
of existing documentation and plans, including the Town’s Master Plan. Subsequently, the consultants devised 
a Space Needs Program and a 20-year Capital Plan for the identified properties, including the potential for a 
new municipal building at the 113 W Columbia site. In March 2024, HOK and CCA presented three distinct 
scenarios to Town Council, each accompanied by estimated costs, to address the identified space needs. 
 
Town Council provided guidance to staff and consultants and selected a scenario for further development 
which includes a new municipal facility at 113 W Columbia to address the space needs of the Telluride 
Marshal’s Department. The next phase of the project entails taking the Town Council approved approach 
through the preliminary design, cost estimation, and financing approach steps.  
 
SITE AND CONTEXT 
 
Existing: The existing primary structure, Rebekah Hall, is non-historic. It currently serves as a municipal 
building with offices for staff from multiple Town of Telluride Departments. The primary meeting space for Town 
Council and other Town Commissions is currently located in the basement of the building. 
 
Proposed: The proposal includes demolition of the existing primary structure and replacement with a new 
primary structure. The new primary structure will be a municipal building for the Telluride Marshal’s 
Department. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Context: 113 W Columbia is located within the Residential/Commercial Treatment Area and Zone District.  
The Town-owned property is situated between the Telluride Fire Protection District Station 1 and the Western 
Federation of Miners Hospital (“Miner’s Union Hospital”) (Contributing – 5SM1756) which was completed in 
1902. This historic building was converted to other uses in the late 1910s, after serving its primary purpose as 
a hospital as well as housing a miner’s union hall in the basement. It currently contains condominiums.

Residential/Commercial (RC) Treatment Area – Introduction 
 The RC Treatment Area north of Main Street is a mix of residential, commercial, and institutional buildings. 
 The scale of projects in the area should be compatible with the overall residential scale of the Town.   
 Pedestrian amenities are encouraged; natural assets, including views, should be protected and enhanced.  
 
RC1. Historic Context 
Intro: The Residential/Commercial Treatment Area includes some of the town’s most important historic 
buildings, such as Town Hall and the Miner’s Union in the north portion. These historic buildings establish the 
scale and character of the area. 
A. New construction adjacent to historic resources should be especially respectful of their context. 
 
RC2. Context Compatibility 
A. New interpretations of traditional building types are encouraged, such that they are seen as products of their 
own time yet compatible with their historic neighbors. 
3. Historic proportions of height, width and depth are very important to be compatible with the historic mass 
and scale of Telluride. 
 
RC3. Relationship to Site Context 
Intro: The Residential/Commercial neighborhoods of Telluride have distinctive identities that result from 
common ways of building. This sense of setting is a product of the historic context that should be preserved. 
A. All projects shall respect the traditional context of the town and the Residential/Commercial Treatment Area. 
1. In all cases, consideration should be given to the broader historic context of the vicinity, the treatment 
area and the town at large. 
 
RC8. Pedestrian Scale 
Intro: The traditional scale of buildings found in Telluride’s historic core is considered to be at a pedestrian, or 
human, scale. That scale should be maintained to promote use of the area by pedestrians. Variety in color and 
texture is also desired to enhance the pedestrian experience and provide visual interest. Pedestrians should 
find walking along sidewalks and in alleys a comfortable and pleasant experience. The scale of buildings and 
the architectural treatments used should enhance this pedestrian-oriented experience. 
A. Develop the ground floor level of all projects to be at a pedestrian scale. 
1. As seen up close, buildings should express human scale through materials and forms that are familiar 
buildings elements in town.

RC17. Building Setbacks 
A. Maintain the pattern of alignment for building fronts in the treatment area. 
1. Where similar front setbacks are characteristic, maintain the alignment of uniformly setback primary 
façades. The front setback shall not be less than the average of the block front. 
2. Where a variety in building setbacks is a part of the historic context, locating a new building within the 
range of setbacks seen historically is appropriate. 
E. Maintain the general alignment of building fronts. 
1. New construction should be set back to match the average alignment of historic buildings on the street 
and to maintain the traditional front yard. This includes porches, bays and other building elements. 
2. Landscaping and fences that help define the yard’s front edge are encouraged. 
 
RC20. Parking Design 
B. Design parking areas to be accessed from alleys or rear drives rather than from the street. 
2. If parking is located within a garage, minimize the size of the driveway. 
C. Locate parking facilities such that they are subordinate to other site features. 
1. An on-site parking area should be located inside or behind a building, where its visual impacts will be 
minimized, unless site conditions (such as steep slopes) prevent this arrangement. 
 
RC22. Mass and Scale 
A. New construction should relate to the existing historic buildings in mass and scale. 
1. The historic mass and scale are of primary importance. Where a new project abuts a designated historic 
structure, step the building down at the property edge to minimize abrupt changes in scale, or add to side 
yard setbacks to reduce the impact. 
2. Maintain the traditional proportions of building height, width and depth found in existing historic buildings. 
C. Maintain the similarity of building heights. 
1. The apparent height of the primary façade should not exceed two stories. This includes additions and 
new construction. 
2. Limit the height of foundation walls to those seen historically. 
G. New construction should appear similar in mass and scale to historic structures found historically in the 
neighborhood and in similar areas throughout the town. 
H. A larger building may be divided into modules that reflect the traditional scale of construction. 
1. Modules should be expressed three-dimensionally, by having significant architectural changes, 
throughout the entire building. A single form should remain the dominant element, such that the overall 
mass does not become too fragmented. 
2. Step down the mass of larger buildings to minimize the perceived scale at the street.

STAFF COMMENTS FOR WORK SESSION 
 
The Design Guidelines that are particularly pertinent to the proposed project at 113 W Columbia in the early 
stages of project design are listed above. Many aspects of the proposed project are compliant with the Design 
Guidelines for the Residential/Commercial Treatment Area, including a number of the Design Guidelines which 
will be the focus of this first Work Session. Overall, the proposed project is compliant with the Design 
Guidelines related to Mass and Scale. That said, there are some aspects of the proposed project design that 
warrant further discussion/consideration by HARC. Staff would suggest that there are opportunities for 
modification/further refinement to bring the project into compliance with the Design Guidelines.  
 
HARC might consider discussing the following during the Work Session: 
 
Please note that while references to Design Guidelines are included below, these are not intended to be 
comprehensive; they are provided for quick reference during the Work Session discussion. 
 
Architectural Character/Pedestrian Scale: HARC may want to consider whether the ground floor level on the 
south façade is at pedestrian scale and whether to recommend that this façade be refined to provide more 
visual interest, especially to a pedestrian approaching the southwest corner from the west. HARC may also 
want to consider whether the height of the board-formed concrete/transition to the brick material should be 
lower on the westerly module. HARC may consider recommending more extensive landscaping and stonework 
to add visual interest and provide a year-round vegetative buffer. (RC8 – A2 and E; RC22 – C2; RC26 – D, D2) 
 
Windows: While the windows on the primary module of the south façade do have vertical emphasis consistent 
with the Design Guidelines, HARC may want to consider whether the size of the openings/height of the 
windows/placement could be modified to: be more consistent with that seen historically; increase visual 
interest; and better relate to the pedestrian scale. (RC31 – A, B, B2, D, D1, E, E1) 
 
Building Setback: HARC may want to consider whether it is appropriate for the front façade of the western 
module of the new building to project beyond the front façade of the Contributing structure to the east (Miner’s 
Union). Per the RC17 – E.1.: “New construction should be set back to match the average alignment of historic 
buildings on the street and to maintain the traditional front yard. This includes porches, bays and other building 
elements.” (RC17 – A, E, E1)  
 
Garage Door: Although the preference within the Design Guidelines is to access parking areas from alleys or 
rear drives (RC20 – B), due to the slope of the site, in this case the only option for enclosed parking (also 
preferred) is to enter from the front, off Columbia Ave. The Applicant has designed the garage door to minimize 
the apparent width of the opening (consistent with RC32 – A3). The parking facilities are subordinate to other 
site features by being enclosed and with an entrance designed to minimize visual impact by setting the garage 
door back/incorporating this element into the easterly, less prominent module (consistent with RC20 – C, C1). 
The detailing is also less utilitarian given the location on the front (consistent with RC32 – A6). In addition, the 
adjacent Fire Station to the west has very large garage doors facing Columbia Ave, making the proposed 
garage door on the new structure diminutive by comparison. Staff would suggest that in this particular case, 
given the site slope challenges and the preference for enclosed parking, siting the garage door facing 
Columbia Ave is appropriate. HARC may want to discuss these site-specific points in making a determination. 
 
Roofs: HARC may want to consider the low slope of the roof on the east façade and the length of the primary 
roof ridge. (RC22 – I and I1; RC25 – A and A8)  
 
Materials: HARC may want to consider whether the extent of the brick on the south façade is appropriate in 
the context of the adjacent structures to the east and west which are both also brick. The mass of the new 
building could be reduced with more varied and refined materials versus the perceived mass of the board-
formed concrete and brick currently comprising a significant proportion of the front façade. Some variation in 
materiality may also be beneficial in setting the new building apart from the Contributing structure to a certain 
extent. (RC8 – B; RC22 – G; RC29 – Intro)

EXTENSION OF THE CERTIFICATE OF APPROPRIATENESS 
 
Background.  For a development approved under the Historic and Architectural Review Commission 
provisions of Article 7 of the Land Use Code (LUC) of the Town of Telluride, a “Certificate of 
Appropriateness” (“CA”) is granted for a period of 3 years. The LUC authorizes HARC to grant an 
extension for up to three years. Extensions are granted by the reviewing body which granted the 
initial final approval if all of the criteria are met.  
 
Approval Criteria for the Extension of a C.A.  Approval of the extension is based on the following two 
criteria, pursuant to LUC Section 7-211.J: 
 
7-206.J.1. H.A.R.C. may grant an extension of the activity approval for an additional period of up to 
three (3) years if H.A.R.C. determines the activity is consistent with current Design Guidelines and 
Design Considerations, as stated in Section 7-105, and the purposes of Article 7, Division 1, and if 
the applicant demonstrates a legitimate need to delay the start of construction, including inclement 
weather, financing difficulties, or similar factors.  
 
7-206.J.2. An application for extension of a CA shall be submitted to the Planning Director no less 
than two (2) months prior to the expiration of the CA and H.A.R.C. shall act on such extension

STAFF ANALYSIS  
There are only two criteria that must be met to grant an extension to a Certificate of Appropriateness.  
 
Criterion #1 is for HARC to determine if the approval is still consistent with the current Design 
Guidelines and Design Considerations; and if the applicant has demonstrated a legitimate need to 
delay construction. The applicant has provided a narrative (attached) that describes concerns. HARC 
must determine if the applicant has demonstrated a legitimate need to delay construction.  
 
HARC may not make changes to the design at this time. The board may either approve the CA 
extension with the previously approved site-specific development plans, or deny the CA extension, 
which will cause the CA to expire on October 18, 2026 unless a building permit application is 
submitted 30 days prior to the expiration date.   
 
Criterion #2 has been met as the request for a CA extension was submitted at least two months prior 
to its expiration. Staff recommend approval of the request as it meets both criteria for CA extensions.

PROCESS – EXTENSION OF CA VESTED PROPERTY RIGHTS 
 
An extension of the vested property rights is also being sought for the CA. Per LUC Section 5-208.K., “a 
vested property right shall remain vested for a period of three (3) years from the effective date of the site 
specific development plan.” As per LUC Section 7206.J.1., “extensions of a vested property right may only 
be granted by the Town Council in accordance with Section 5-208 of this Title …”  
 
LUC Section 5-208.K.2. outlines the process for extending vested property rights:  
 
“Upon approval by the Town Council, a property right originally vested may be extended for a period 
exceeding an initial three (3) year vesting period when deemed in the public interest and when warranted in 
light of all relevant circumstances including, but not limited to, the size and phasing of the development, 
economic cycles, and market conditions. Town Council shall receive a recommendation from the P&Z 
Chairperson and/or the HARC Chairperson, as applicable, before granting an extension of any vested right.” 
 
The HARC Chair will be asked to make a determination as to whether to recommend to Town 
Council that the vested right also be granted an extension. If the HARC Chair makes this 
recommendation, then this request will be considered by Town Council at a publicly noticed Town 
Council public hearing per LUC Section 5-204.  
 
Notes: 
 A vested property right “means the right to undertake and complete an approved development and use 
for property under the terms and conditions of a site specific development plan.” (LUC Section 5-208.B.)  
 Extension of the vested property right for this project precludes “any zoning or land use action by the 
Town of Telluride which would alter, impair, or diminish the development or use of the property as set 
forth in an approved site specific development plan.” (LUC Section 5-208.C.)  
 “Establishment of a vested property right shall not preclude the application of ordinances or regulations 
which are general in nature and applicable to all property subject to land use regulation by the Town, 
including, but not limited to, fee assessments and building, fire, plumbing, electrical, mechanical, water 
and sewer codes.” (LUC Section 5-208.C.3.)  
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
 
The request for an extension of a vested property right by Town Council is based on a number of factors 
outlined by the Applicant in their Narrative, including: delays due to high interest rates, high construction 
costs, a regional shortage of qualified contractors, and higher costs for all goods and services throughout 
the US. The property owners are seeking an extension to ensure the project is completed correctly and with 
the highest standards possible. Extension of the vesting in concert with a CA extension will provide the 
Applicant with the time needed to move this large-scale project forward.  
 
A recommendation by the HARC Chair is required in order for Town Council to consider this request.

Appropriateness #10263 for 221 W Colorado Ave by an additional three years [or another timeframe to be 
determined by the HARC Chair] be considered by Town Council at a publicly noticed Town Council public 
hearing, due to the large size of the project and challenging market and economic conditions.
