Introduction: 
Town Council held a work session at the August 5, 2026 Council meeting at which it 
directed Town staff to prepare a resolution authorizing the amendment of the deed 
restrictions for FINO II units 1A and 2A, with the intent of converting these units to 
Employee Dwelling Units ("EDUs") from Town Constructed Units ("TCUs"), thereby 
removing the income tier cap and Maximum Sale Price ("MSP") provisions of the deed 
restrictions. The Units will remain subject to the Telluride Affordable Housing Guidelines 
(the “Guidelines”). Additional information regarding these Units and the need for such a 
change is included in the materials for the work session. Town Council as co-beneficiary 
will also be considering a resolution at its regular meeting of September 22, 2026 to 
authorize amendment of the deed restrictions.   
Background: 
This is not an Exception, Appeal, or Grievance to the Guidelines, but a request to 
amend a contract (the deed restrictions) as allowed by that contract. Here, the Town as 
current owner of the Units proposes removal of the Units from the TCU Program and re-
deed restricting the Units as EDUs. Both types of units are subject to regulation by the 
Guidelines but follow different requirements for ownership qualification and resale. Most 
notably, owners of EDUs have no income limit and EDUs likewise have no MSP 
limitations. Rather, the value of such units fluctuates with the demands of the market for 
Qualified Owners similar to many units in both the Mountain Village and San Miguel 
County affordable housing programs. While Telluride has historically geared its program 
towards creation of price-capped units to maintain affordability for an income-diverse set 
of Qualified Owners who would otherwise be priced out of other local affordable housing 
programs, there are compelling reasons to convert the Units to EDUs which reasons 
have been discussed by Town Council at length, and this request comes as a last resort 
Page 4 of 15
following extensive negotiations with the FINO II HOA seeking to protect the affordability 
of the Units with respect to HOA dues and special assessments.  
  
The Town of Telluride and the Telluride Housing Authority (“THA”) are the beneficiaries 
of any deed restriction executed in connection with the Guidelines. The deed restrictions 
for the Units require that modification be in writing, signed by the parties and recorded 
with the Clerk and Recorder of San Miguel County. Likewise, Section 108.9 of the 
Guidelines provides that “No modification or amendment to the Deed Restriction shall 
be effective unless agreed to in writing by THA and any other beneficiary.” Section 
108.8 of the Guidelines also provides that “Deed Restrictions may not be transferred off 
a Housing Unit unless permitted by the governing Housing Program. Transfers require 
the express consent of the THA Board which must find that the Section 102 General 
Affordable Housing Program Goals are met by such transfer, and such transfer 
promotes the provision of Affordable Housing.” Emphasis added.  
  
The THA Board must also consider whether such release would meet the Section 102 
General Affordable Housing Program Goals: 
  
102. 1  Provide affordable Employee housing. The primary goal of the Affordable 
Housing Programs is to provide Affordable Housing for residents and their 
families who make a living primarily from Presence Required Employment. 
This is accomplished primarily by regulating and restricting occupancy of 
Housing Units to Qualified Households and Ownership to Qualified 
Owners 
102.2   Promote economic diversity within the Telluride community. Diversity may 
be or ownership for limiting Eligibility example, for encouraged, by 
occupancy to Qualified Households that meet specific economic means 
criteria which may include both Household Income and Household Net 
Assets. 
102.3   Ensure ongoing affordability of Housing Units. Most Housing Units are 
subject to price and appreciation limitations for Sale, resale and/or rental. 
These limitations are intended to ensure affordability for both the current 
Household and for the long-term affordability of the Housing Unit. 
for 102.4 Provide clear, fair, and consistent administration of Housing 
Programs. These Guidelines establish standards and procedures for the 
Town's Affordable Housing Programs. There are circumstances however, 
that may not fit clearly within the specific provisions of the Guidelines but 
still meet these General Affordable Housing Program Goals. For these 
cases, Exception and appeal processes have been established as set 
forth in Guidelines 109. 
  
As relevant for the THA Board’s consideration of release, Section 301.2 Town 
Constructed Ownership Program – Purpose and Applicability, provides: 
  
Application of the provisions of this Part 3 to Town or THA built or financed

Introduction: 
This Policy Statement is being presented to the Telluride Housing Authority Board 
(THA) the at worksession a during staff direction on based consideration for to 
September 1, 2026, Town Council meeting.   
Background: 
The current Telluride Employee Rental Housing Policies (Policies) clearly outline how 
annual rent rates at Town owned rental properties will be established. Those targeted 
rent rates are calculated using the prior year Area Median Income (AMI) model for San 
Miguel County.  
  
Based on staff presentations from the past two years, anticipating Town Council may 
have concerns with such a drastic change in the 2026 AMI numbers for San Miguel 
County, and needing clear direction to inform the 2027 Budget discussions, Staff 
presented three options for determining the 2027 Rental Rates to the Town Council for 
consideration: 
1. Apply the standard rent increases based on direction in the Policies, which would 
result in a 9.7% increase to the current rent rates; 
2. Consider zero increase to the current 2026 rent rates; 
3. Consider a change to the multipliers for the calculation of targeted rent rates. 
  
Page 9 of 15
The Town Council consensus on September 1, was to keep the rent rates at the 2026 
levels for 2027 - 0% AMI shift.  
Analysis/Financial Analysis: 
Adopting no increase to 2027 rent rates - maintaining the current 2026 rent rates - will 
result in lower potential rent revenue at all properties. All rent revenue models operate 
under the assumption that existing household incomes remain static. These scenarios 
can fluctuate if new or existing tenants have higher or lower household income and 
rental Tiers.  
Recommendation: 
Based on Town Council direction at the September 1, 2026, meeting, staff reccomends 
approval of Policy Statement 2026-06.  
Motion: 
Move to approve Policy Statement 2026-06 as presented.
