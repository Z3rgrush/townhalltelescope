4) GENERAL AGENDA 
A) AGENCY INTERVIEWS: SEPCIAL ALCOHOL FUND APPLICANTS 
Chair Sisley and the board conducted interviews with multiple agencies, including USD 383, 
Wonder Workshop, Thrive, Riley County Community Corrections-Adult and Juvenile, Manhattan 
Emergency Shelter, USD 378, Sunflower Children's Collective, Big Brothers Big Sisters, Be Able, 
Boys and Girls Club, Pawnee Mental Health, and UFM Learning Center, to review their 
 
 
2 
applications for special alcohol funding, focusing on prevention and intervention programs, 
challenges, accomplishments, and budget clarifications.  
o Interview Process and Structure: Chair Sisley outlined the interview process, specifying 
that each agency would have five minutes to present, followed by a two-minute period 
for board questions. Board members were encouraged to ask applicants to elaborate on 
significant challenges or accomplishments if not addressed during their presentation. 
o Applicant Presentations: Each agency representative provided an overview of their 
programs, described their use of special alcohol funds, and addressed specific questions 
from the board regarding their services, methodologies, and impact on the community. 
o Board Member Questions: Board members asked applicants for clarification on 
enrollment numbers, budget details, program reach, and the relationship between their 
services and alcohol prevention, ensuring comprehensive understanding of each 
application. 
o Documentation and Data Provided: Some applicants provided handouts and others 
spoke to detailed data on student enrollment, service reach, and methodology for 
reporting numbers, supporting their funding requests with concrete evidence. 
• Prevention and Intervention Program Highlights: Agency representatives from USD 383, 
Wonder Workshop, Thrive, Big Brothers Big Sisters, and Boys and Girls Club described their 
evidence-based prevention and intervention programs, emphasizing services such as counseling, 
peer education, social-emotional learning, and skill-building to reduce substance abuse among 
youth and families.  
• Challenges and Accomplishments Reported by Agencies: Applicants shared significant 
challenges such as funding delays, staff turnover, increased demand, and program closures, as 
well as accomplishments like successful program launches, increased service reach, and 
resilience in maintaining operations.  
o Funding and Staffing Challenges: Several agencies, including Manhattan Emergency 
Shelter and Riley County Community Corrections, reported challenges with federal 
funding delays, staff turnover, and the need to maintain services despite resource 
constraints. 
o Program Expansion and Successes: Sunflower Children's Collective expanded its board 
and reach, Wonder Workshop and Thrive saw increased participation and skill-building, 
and Be Able launched a nationally accredited men's recovery housing program, all cited 
as major accomplishments. 
o Service Demand and Trends: Agencies noted rising numbers of clients affected by 
substance abuse, increased youth homelessness, and higher demand for prevention and 
intervention services, with Manhattan Emergency Shelter reporting 66% of clients under 
age 25 and Boys and Girls Club facing waitlists. 
 
 
3 
o Resilience and Adaptation: Organizations adapted to challenges by leveraging 
community partnerships, revising budgets, and focusing resources on core programs, as 
seen in Sunflower Children's Collective's decision to close a visitation program and 
strengthen CASA and CAC services. 
• Budget Clarifications and Funding Requests: Board members questioned applicants about 
budget discrepancies, excess funds, and the allocation of special alcohol funds, with agencies 
such as Boys and Girls Club, Pawnee Mental Health, and Sunflower Children's Collective 
providing explanations and committing to submit further clarifications as needed.  
o Budget Errors and Corrections: Jennifer Rogers (Boys and Girls Club) addressed clerical 
errors in membership dues and program fees, provided revised budget figures, and 
explained the use of excess funds for scholarships and operational needs. 
o Salary and Expense Allocations: Michelle Sink (Big Brothers Big Sisters) and Diane 
Hendricks Tiburean (Pawnee Mental Health) clarified that requested funds would be 
used for salaries of match support specialists and therapists, respectively, and explained 
the breakdown between prevention and treatment expenses. 
o Funding Sources and Use: Megan Lewis (Riley County Community Corrections) detailed 
the sources of agency funding, including state grants and opioid settlement funds, and 
specified that special alcohol funds are used for drug testing and intervention services 
not covered by other grants. 
o Requests for Additional Documentation: Board members requested further justification 
and documentation from agencies regarding budget overages, allocation of excess 
funds, and clarification of service numbers, with agencies agreeing to provide follow-up 
information to Kristen King for board review. 
• Methodologies for Reporting Service Numbers: Applicants, including Kari Quinton Humes (USD 
383), Dr. Erin Flair (USD 378), and Rachel Fanno (Sunflower Children's Collective), explained their 
methodologies for determining the number of individuals served, using enrollment data, district 
boundaries, and service tracking systems to ensure accurate reporting for funding applications.  
o Enrollment Data Usage: USD 383 and USD 378 representatives described using end-of-
year enrollment numbers and district GIS systems to report student counts, 
distinguishing between students residing in Manhattan and those outside city limits. 
o Service Tracking and Reporting: Sunflower Children's Collective and Big Brothers Big 
Sisters used tracking systems and surveys to report the number of children and families 
served, including those affected by parental substance misuse. 
• Community Partnerships and Collaboration: Several agencies, including Manhattan Emergency 
Shelter, Be Able, and Pawnee Mental Health, described collaborative efforts with other 
organizations, such as case management, transitional housing, and referral systems, to provide 
comprehensive support for clients affected by substance abuse and homelessness.

o Transitional Housing Initiatives: Emily Wagner (Manhattan Emergency Shelter) 
discussed working with Be Able and other agencies to develop transitional housing for 
youth and older adults, addressing gaps in community resources. 
o Case Management Collaboration: Be Able and Pawnee Mental Health described their 
approach to case management, connecting clients to treatment professionals and 
providing layered support through peer mentors, directors, and recovery groups. 
o Referral and Outreach Programs: Manhattan Emergency Shelter and Sunflower 
Children's Collective detailed their outreach and referral programs, ensuring clients are 
connected to appropriate services and safe environments. 
• Trends in Substance Abuse and Service Needs: Board members and agency representatives 
discussed observed trends, including increased substance abuse among youth, rising 
homelessness, and higher demand for mental health and prevention services, with agencies 
adapting their programs to address these evolving needs.  
o Youth Substance Abuse Trends: USD 383 and Sunflower Children's Collective reported 
changes in youth perceptions of harm, increased marijuana normalization, and higher 
rates of substance misuse among clients under age 25. 
o Mental Health and Co-occurring Issues: Riley County Community Corrections and 
Pawnee Mental Health noted the growing prevalence of co-occurring mental health and 
substance abuse issues, requiring integrated intervention and treatment approaches.
