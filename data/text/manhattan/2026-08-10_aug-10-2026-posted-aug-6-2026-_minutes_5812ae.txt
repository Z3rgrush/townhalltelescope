Present were the following members of the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board: Mr. Nick Lander, Mr. 
Joe Knopp, Mr. Matt Enoch, Mr. Bruce Snead, Mr. Lew Faust, Mr. Jerry Lawrence, and Mr. Chaz Mailey.

Mr. Faust shared he attended several of the Arts in the Park concerts and felt those were very well received 
and is a great opportunity for the community and gave kudos to the people involved in organizing them. 
 
MINUTES 
It was moved and seconded to approve the minutes of the special meeting held on July 20, 2026. On vote, 
motion carried 7-0.  
 
STAFF REPORTS 
Mr. Lander as if the Board could get a reminder of what the cost recovery targets are and how close we are 
to hitting those. Mr. Stewart shared the overall goal is to get 35-40% cost recovery. That goal may need to 
be changed due to bringing on Building Services to the Department. In 2025, if you count the Building 
Services piece, cost recovery finished at about 27%. If Staff can hit 29-30% this year, they’ll be doing good. 
 
NEW BUSINESS 
1. Mr. Lander stated the Board needs to elect a Chair and Vice-Chair. Mr. Stewart shared that Mr. Lander has 
termed out of Chair, so the Board needs to elect a new Chair. Mr. Lawrence nominated Mr. Lew Faust to 
serve as Chair. On 7 -0 vote, Mr. Lew Faust will be the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board Chair. Mr. 
Snead nominated Mr. Matt Enoch to serve as Vice-Chair. On 6-0-1 vote, Mr. Matt Enoch will be the Parks 
and Recreation Advisory Board Vice-Chair. 
 
2. Mr. Thomas presented a Summer Programs Review for Recreation Programs to the Board. He shared 
information from the past 5 years for Total Body Fitness, Total Body Strong, Special Populations programs, 
and Senior Trips.  
 
 
Mr. Knopp asked if the banks still offer trips and activities for customers  and if there were other 
opportunities for Seniors in the community  besides Parks and Rec. Mr. Thomas shared he hasn’t worked 
with banks, but he has worked with the Senior Center  on trips. He hasn’t seen or heard if there are other 
opportunities for Seniors but can look into it to see about partnerships.   
 
Ms. Micheals presented a Summer Programs Review for Instructional Programs to the Board. She shared 
some highlights from the new programs that occurred this year  as well as Summer Camp and Summer 
Theatre registration numbers. One of the major takeaways from the Summer is the need to expand more. 
Staff is going to look into  licensing one of the Recreation Centers to house more Little Apple Day Camp 
kids. One of the challenges that will continue is transportation. Hy -Vee was a big partner allowing bulk 
orders at a discounted price to offer healthy snacks for campers.  
 
Mr. Enoch commented he would recommend including in the presentation the objectives for skill 
development and then measure for those throughout the course of the program to get some metrics. Also 
including financial metrics would be great.  
 
Mr. Faust shared to look into the school district for partnerships on transportation. Ms. Micheals commented 
she uses USD 383 primarily for out-of-town field trips and plans to continue with that partnership.  
 
Ms. McCarter presented a Summer Programs Review for Athletics to the Board. She shared information on 
Youth Athletics including Baseball/Softball, Optimist T -Ball, My First T -Ball, Flint Hills Summer 
Volleyball, NIL Volleyball Clinic, and 3v3 Basketball. She also shared information on Adult Athletics 
including Pickleball and Sand Volleyball.  
 
Mr. Knopp asked what the cost of Sponsorship was and the fee for participation for Baseball/Softball. Ms. 
McCarter shared it varies from leagues and seasons. To sponsor for Baseball/Softball it’s $400 per team 
and the business gets their name on the back of the jerseys. There is also an annual sponsor of De Hart for 
Youth Athletics. The participation fee varies for each age group and is anywhere between $50 -$90. Mr. 
Knopp asked if these reports are being created as new or updating previous reports and how much time it 
takes to create them. Ms. McCarter answered the reports are create d as new with the help of Ms. De Hart 
creating templates and takes anywhere from 2 -4 hours depending on the season. Mr. Knopp asked if the 
reports are only for the board or if they benefit other departments. Ms. McCarter answered it’s for both the 
Board and for the Department to reflect on. Ms. De Hart commented the Supervisors run similar programs 
year over year so the data is being tracked and can be pulled from CivicRec. Mr. Stewart shared over the 
time Staff is looking at a program life cycle, they are identifying trends. When programs begin to decline, 
that’s when changes need to be made to either reinvigorate or have it go away. This information is not just 
for the Board’s consumption, it’s used for data analytics all the time.  
 
Mr. Mailey asked if Staff has thought about how Parks and Rec could potentially impact some of these 
trends. Ms. De Hart shared one of those Staff is working on is Women’s Flag Football which is now 
sanctioned in Kansas. Staff is making some adjustments to how programs are being run to anticipate that 
coming to town as well as marketing NFL Flag to the community. Mr. Mailey asked if there was a free 
agent system or a way for people to get involved in the community. Ms. McCarter answered yes, there is a 
free agent list for all Adult programs and teams are encouraged to use it if they need players or substitutes. 
 
Mr. Lander commented the investment in quality facilities is crucial in order to  provide the programs 
needed. Also filling the gaps between club sports and school teams is an opportunity for Parks and Rec so 
he appreciates all the efforts and adjustments Staff is putting into programs.  
 
Mr. Knopp asked who Parks and Rec benchmarks with. Mr. Stewart stated we mostly benchmark with 
ourselves because there’s no other community like us as this community behaves differently than others. 
 
 
Staff does look at trend data nationally in order to  bring new programs in. Mr. Knopp asked if looking at 
other community’s programs is helpful or provides incentive. Mr. Stewart answered yes, Staff 
communicates with other communities about successes and challenges, that’s why Staff attends conferences 
so they can talk to colleagues and learn about new programs.  
 
3. Mr. Bridenstine presented a Flint Hills Discovery Center update to the Board. He shared numbers on 
attendance to the center, revenues, the Sunflower Summer Program, new programs and events to include 
adult only programs and movies in the park, new initiati ves and partnerships, exhibits, projects to include 
Vigilance Bison Sculpture and the FHDC Outdoor Classroom, the Bison, Beef, and Bourbon event which 
is the Foundation’s fundraiser, and the impact the Discovery Center has on the community. He also shared 
this fall, a new 5-Year Strategic Plan will be developed followed by a Comprehensive Master Plan with an 
end goal of presenting the Discovery Center in 2032, which is the 20th Anniversary Celebration, looking 
as good as the day it opened. 
 
Mr. Enoch commended Mr. Bridenstine on his presentation and stated it’s exactly what he is looking for. 
He shared the Discovery Center is better than it was when it opened because of the fact that Mr. Bridenstine 
has a vision, the foundation support, and hopes he continues to improve to provide this amazing resource 
for the community.  
 
Mr. Knopp asked what percentage of funding is coming from the transient guest tax. Mr. Bridenstine 
answered zero, the support comes through the Special Parks and Rec Fund and General Fund. Mr. Knopp 
asked where the Special Parks and Rec Fund gets its resou rces. Mr. Bridenstine stated it comes from the 
alcohol drink tax at restaurants. Mr. Knopp asked how these two funds compare and how much the Center 
was getting from the transient guest tax. Mr. Stewart stated it’s $260,000 a year. Mr. Knopp asked how 
many visits of the regular visitors are spontaneous from people staying in nearby hotels. Mr. Bridenstine 
commented it would be hard to guess as there is a spontaneous visits demographic, but the Discovery Center 
is more of a destination of choice. Mr. Stewart shared based on placer data, 18.2% of people come from a 
hotel and visit the Discovery Center.
