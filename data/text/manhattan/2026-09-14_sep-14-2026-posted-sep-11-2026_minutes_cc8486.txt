PUBLIC COMMENT 
Mr. Rex Fowles spoke about the potential formation of a Friends group for the Cemeteries. 
 
BOARD MEMBER COMMENTS 
Mr. Knopp asked clarifying questions about the upcoming sales tax and the impact on the existing Quality 
of Life Sales Tax. Mr. Stewart explained that the upcoming ballot sales tax is a streets only sales tax at .50 
cent. The existing .25 cent QOL sales tax will be up for renewal at the end of 2027.  
 
Mr. Snead asked about the new financial system and staff’s ability to get reports from it. Mr. Stewart 
explained that Staff is using it to generate the information that is provided in the reports. One draw back is 
that Staff needs to improve the use of AI in generating a consistent report from month to month.  
 
Mr. Knopp asked about the financials of the specialty funds, particularly the Special Sunset Zoo Fund. He 
asked about the revenues associated with that fund. Mr. Stewart answered that those were all revenues, 
including memberships, admissions, concessions,  and gift shop sales. Mr. Knopp asked if the expenses 
within the General Fund account for the Zoo. Mr. Stewart stated if you add the expenses of the Special 
Sunset Zoo and General Fund, you will get the full expense for the Zoo.  
 
Mr. Lander asked what Staff was tracking in the financials. Mr. Stewart answered primarily cost recovery. 
The Parks and Rec revenues and expenses are like a bell curve with most of the expenditures being in the 
late spring and early summer. As of now, we are sitting at approximately 31% cost recovery, and our goal 
is 29-30% for the year. This will reduce over the rest of the year since our revenues are reduced by our 
fixed costs, such as Staff.

NEW BUSINESS 
1. Mr. Leyva presented an update on Park Projects to the Board. He shared information on the CiCo Park 
Project, Trail improvements, the Flint Hills Discovery Center Outdoor Classroom, the Sunset Zoo Lemur 
and Americas Project, playgrounds at Douglass, Goodnow Par k, and Longs Park. He shared how these 
 
 
projects were and will be funded through the Recreation and Trails Sales Tax for the Recreation Centers, 
CiCo Project, and trails. He added that approximately $2.4 million dollars had been spent out of the 
Recreation and Trails Sales tax and that was paire d with approximately $900,000 in grants, bringing the 
total amount to be spent on trails  to $3.3 million. Additionally, he covered the partnership with Planning 
and Development and the use of Community Development Block Grants (CDBG) to fund the restoration 
of the playground in the Lower to Moderate Income (LMI) areas. 
 
Mr. Knopp  asked questions about the project occurring at Ft. Riley Blvd and Martin Luther King, 
specifically about the sidewalk connection. Mr. Leyva responded that the project would include widening 
and connecting the sidewalk/trail back to Longs Park in addition to the road work.  Mr. Knopp also asked 
who to contact if someone wanted to bid on the used playground equipment. Mr. Leyva explained that the 
company replacing the playground equipment is required to remove the existing equipment. Individuals 
would need to contact the company that was awarded the work.   
 
2. Staff presented a Winter Programs Preview to the Board for Aquatics, Recreation Programs, and Athletics. 
Ms. De Hart covered the changes in the court schedules this coming winter as it is the Department’s intent 
to have a court open at each location as much as possible. Additionally, she also noted a new development 
is a partnership with Kansas State University Physical Educati on students. These students will be the 
instructors for the Homeschool P.E. program that MPRD offers.   
 
Mr. Lawrence asked if the schedules in the system are correct this year . Ms. De Hart responded yes, they 
should be. Mr. Lawerence asked if staff would continue Friday night basketball. Ms. De Hart said yes. 
 
3. Mr. Stewart presented a review of the Sunset Cemetery historic designation. The presentation reviewed 
what was presented to the Board in July 2024. Since there is an indefinite pause in any construction at the 
Cemetery, Staff recommends moving forward with the Historic Resources Board recommendation that the 
Cemetery be listed on the National and State Historical sites, however the Sexton’s house should be omitted 
from this nomination. He reviewed and presented a memo from Katie Jackson, City Attorney, which 
highlights the questions surrounding the deed and ownership of the Sexton’s house based on the restrictions 
listed in the deed. The recommendation from Staff is to direct Staff to quiet the title so a judge can officially 
rule on proper ownership of the house.   
 
Mr. Lander asked questions about the joint facility between the Zoo and the Cemetery that is in the Master 
Plan. Mr. Stewart stated this is still the main priority for Staff, but due to some other issues that have arisen 
with other facilities, we would look to redesign that facility and are not sure if it would stay in the location 
it was designed.  
 
The floor was opened for public comment. 
 
Mick Charney spoke to the importance of the Sexton’s House being the most west historic Sexton’s House 
in the US. He additionally provided an article that he wrote about the Cemetery to the Board. 
 
Kathy Dzewaltowski spoke to the properties around the Cemetery, which are as important in a historic 
district theme and should be included. Additionally, the Landscape Water Tower was included in the 
Historic Resources Board and recommended that being included in the designation. She also stated that she 
didn’t hear a reason why the Sexton’s House shouldn’t be included. 
 
Colene Lind recommended voting for both the Cemetery and Sexton’s House in the designation. She 
explained the importance of the designation and what that meant for the future care and use of the facilities.  
Additionally, this would provide avenues and resources to care for these facilities. 
 
 
 
Linda Glasgow stated the legal information is not correct and suggested that the Board get someone here 
who can help Staff understand the ownership of the house. 
 
Mel Borst spoke about the importance of including the Sexton’s House. He also stated as someone who 
lives in the neighborhood, that he was speaking on their behalf and they wanted to ensure that the Sexton’s 
House, Cemetery, and Water Tower are preserved as is. 
 
Rex Fowles suggests the Board recommend the entire area, so the paperwork is done.   
 
Mr. Snead asked Mr. Stewart to clarify about the house. Mr. Stewart referenced the memo provided by the 
City Attorney, and that it was his understanding that the owner had to be the one who designated the house.  
Since the ownership was in question, the Staff recommendation was to let a judge rule on the ownership to 
bring clarity and then figure out the next steps. 
 
Mr. Lander asked what happens if the house is included and the ownership turns out to not be the City. Mr. 
Stewart stated he didn’t know what the ramifications of that would be. 
 
It was moved by Mr. Snead, seconded by Mr. Hoyt, to recommend the designation of Sunset Cemetery to 
the State and National Historic Register and have Staff quiet the title to the Sexton’s house. Mr. Hoyt asked 
if the Landmark Water Tower should be included , to which the Board agreed. Mr. Snead amended the 
motion to include the water tower and proceed with the designation of the Sexton’s House once a judge had 
ruled on ownership. Mr. Hoyt seconded the amended motion. On vote, motion carried 7-0. 
 
Mr. Knopp asked about deposing of City assets and what the renovation of that house could be. Mr. Stewart 
answered if it is on the register, they would have to follow those guidelines, but as outlined in public 
comment, there are additional resources that assist with that.   
 
Meeting adjourned.
