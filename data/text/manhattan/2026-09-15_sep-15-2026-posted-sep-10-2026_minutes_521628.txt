4. Public Hearing 
A. Conduct public hearings for the Revenue Neutral Rate and proposed 2027 
Budget; adopt Resolution No. 091526- F, levying a property tax rate exceeding 
the revenue neutral rate; approve Ordinance No. 7839 adopting the 2027 City 
Budget and approve Ordinance No. 7840 establishing the salaries for 2027. 
 
Danielle Dulin, City Manager,  presented the item and answered questions from the 
Commission. 
 
Jason Hilgers, Deputy City Manager, and Rina Neal, Director of Finance , answered 
questions from the Commission.  
 
Mayor Adamchak opened the public hearing for the Revenue Neutral Rate. 
 
Colin Cooper, 3802 Emerald Circle, stated that rising City revenues and property taxes 
were placing strain on younger homeowners and families. He compared Manhattan 
property taxes with those in New Hampshire and questioned why Manhattan could not 
provide comparable municipal services with greater fiscal restraint. He encouraged the 
Commission to avoid exceeding the Revenue Neutral Rate and expressed concern about 
continued growth in City debt and the projected budget. 
 
Gary Olds, 3308 Frontier Circle, encouraged Mr. Cooper to comment during the budget 
public hearing and stated that the Revenue Neutral Rate hearing focused on the mill levy. 
He believed the City could achieve revenue neutrality, estimating that the difference was 
approximately $460,000. He stated that the City had sufficient reserves and that achieving 
the Revenue Neutral Rate was a matter of Commission priorities. 
 
John Ball, 3107 Harahey Ridge, expressed appreciation for the Commission’s efforts to 
reduce the proposed levy. He stated that his total property -tax bill, across five taxing 
City Commission Minutes September 15, 2026 
  
jurisdictions, had increased by approximately $1,000. As a retiree, he noted that 
retirement income did not increase at the same rate as inflation and property taxes. He 
encouraged the Commission to maintain the Revenue Neutral Rate if possible. 
 
Anthony Tiroch, 1000 Brierwood Drive, expressed concern about the impact of tax 
increases on retirees, low -income households, and young families. He encouraged the 
City to evaluate departmental budget requests, identify cost-saving opportunities, pursue 
grants, and connect budget expenditures to measurable public benefits. He stated that 
the City should seek revenue-neutral budgets over the next five years. 
 
Mayor Adamchak stated that City staff regularly considered cost -saving measures. She 
cited the City’s planned transition to a cloud -based phone system as an example, noting 
that the system would reduce costs and replace outdated equipment. She stated that the 
Information Technology Department had evaluated more than 30 vendors during the 
selection process and that staff considered similar efficiencies throughout City 
operations. 
 
Patricia Cassinelli, 2124 Fox Meadows, expressed concern that her property valuation was 
inflated and did not reflect the amount she believed she could receive if she sold her 
home. Mayor Adamchak responded that real property valuations in Manhattan were 
established by the County Appraiser. 
 
Commissioner McCulloh encouraged residents who believed their valuations were 
inaccurate to contact the County Appraiser’s Office and review the comparable properties 
used in the valuation. Sh e noted that property -tax bills included levies from the City, 
County, and school district. Sh e further stated that school mill levies were affected by 
State funding decisions, including funding for education and special education. 
 
Commissioner Fox stated that the State Legislature had established the current property 
tax system and that the County was responsible for determining property values. He 
encouraged residents who believed their properties were overvalued to appeal their 
valuations through the County Appraiser’s Office. 
 
Alex Young, 3016 Conrow Drive, stated that rising costs were becoming overwhelming for 
her middle-class family of four, which did not qualify for assistance programs. She stated 
that her family may need to consider more affordable places to live. 
 
Commissioner Morrison stated that the Commission should discuss property-tax impacts 
more frequently throughout the year. He expressed satisfaction that the Commission had 
attempted to reduce the levy but noted that the city continued to face significant needs 
related to streets, public facilities, fire stations, and other infrastructure. He stated that 
while the City could potentially provide property -tax relief, additional sales -tax revenue 
may be needed to address maintena nce and capital needs. He stated that he could

within the overall budget, and he thanked staff for their work. 
 
Commissioner Von Lintel expressed appreciation for the residents who provided 
comment. He stated that concerns about taxes and inflation had been raised frequently 
during the campaign and that the city  should continue working to improve operational 
efficiency.

Mayor Adamchak opened the public hearing for the proposed 2027 City Budget. 
 
Gary Olds, 3308 Frontier Circle, stated that the budget hearing was intended to address 
how, where, and when City funds would be spent. He referenced the City Manager’s 
comments regarding staffing reductions and noted that the budget notice reflected 
authorized spending of approximately $262 million, including approximately $82 million 
in carryover funds from the prior year. He stated that the budget’s impact would vary 
among residents and expressed particular concern for middle -income households that 
may b e affected by the City’s spending decisions. He encouraged the Commission to 
carefully monitor expenditures during the budget year and in preparation for its January 
retreat. 
 
John Ball, 3107 Harahey Ridge, expressed appreciation for the work completed on the 
budget. He stated that, as a member of the Community Support Funds Advisory Board, 
he was concerned that the amount allocated for social- service funding had remained 
unchanged for the previous three years. He stated that inflation had reduced the 
purchasing power of that allocation by approximately $40,000. He noted that the City was 
the only local government in the area that used General Fund resources to support social-
service agencies and encouraged the Commission to consider adjusting the allocation for 
inflation in the following year. He noted that local agencies provided meals and other 
essential services to residents. He suggested that the City consider reducing reserv es or 
limiting cost -of-living increases for certain higher- paid City staff in order to increase 
funding available for social services. 
City Commission Minutes September 15, 2026 
  
Colin Cooper, 3802 Emerald Circle, thanked Mr. Olds for highlighting the effect of budget 
decisions on middle -income and younger residents. He referenced an earlier City 
Manager recommendation to increase the mill levy to 54.506 mills in order to capture 
additional revenue from rising property valuations for the Bond and Interest Fund. He 
expressed concern that relying on future valuation growth to address debt obligations 
represented a spending issue rather than a revenue issue. He questioned whether the 
City should instead shift existing resources and reduce discretionary spending to address 
debt and budget needs. He acknowledged that certain expenditures, including Riley 
County Police Department funding, were not readily negotiable, but stated that the Ci ty 
should identify other areas for reductions. He encouraged the Commission to make 
budget reductions now rather than deferring them. 
 
Mayor Adamchak clarified that nearly two mills had been shifted from the General Fund 
to the Bond and Interest Fund. She  stated that City staff reviewed the budget in detail 
and made adjustments among budget line items as opportunities became available. 
 
Commissioner Fox addressed Mr. Olds’ comments regarding the approximately $262 
million in authorized spending. He stated that he had initially shared concerns about the 
amount but learned that a portion of the total was designated for cash reserves. He stated 
that, excluding funds intended for reserves, the City’s anticipated expenditures were 
approximately $186.6 million. He gave direction to city staff to reduce the mill levy to 52.5 
mills which is a $236,000 reduction . He stated that the reduction would be made from 
the General Fund and funded through the cash reserve balance.  
 
Hearing no other comments, Mayor Adamchak closed the public hearing. 
 
Motion By: Commissioner Fox. 
Action: Approve Ordinance No. 7839 adopting the 2027 Budget.
