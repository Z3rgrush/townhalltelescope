From  Anne Schneider, Director   
Subject Proposition 123 C.R.S. § 29-32-105  
 
Background 
In 2023, Lake County and the City of Leadville elected to participate jointly in Proposition 123, 
Colorado’s statewide affordable housing initiative. Through that commitment, the community agreed to 
pursue two primary objectives: increase the inventory of affordable housing and establish expedited 
review processes for qualifying affordable housing development applications. 
The commitment was formalized through an Intergovernmental Agreement between Lake County and 
the City of Leadville, executed and recorded in August 2025. 
Under the current three-year commitment cycle, the County and City committed to achieving a 
cumulative 9 percent increase in the community’s affordable housing inventory through a combination 
of newly constructed affordable housing units and existing housing units converted to qualifying 
affordability. 
The County, City, and the Regional Housing Authority (RHA) are currently working with the 
community’s Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) Proposition 123 case manager to document and 
validate the new and converted units that will be credited toward the current commitment. 
Expedited Development Review 
Participation in Proposition 123 also required the community to establish regulatory mechanisms that 
facilitate the timely review and processing of qualifying affordable housing proposals. 
Through the coordinated efforts of the Lake County and City of Leadville Community Planning & 
Development Departments, the Land Development Codes were amended to establish the required 
expedited review processes and development standards. 
As a result of completing these regulatory changes ahead of the statutory deadline, Community Planning 
& Development received a $50,000 Proposition 123 implementation incentive. 
This represents an important policy outcome of the program: Proposition 123 has not simply provided 
access to funding; it has also supported local regulatory modernization and greater predictability in the 
development review process for qualifying affordable housing.

Financial Return to the Community 
The most substantial direct benefit of the community's participation has been access to Proposition 123 
funding. 
To date, the community has been awarded approximately $980,000 for the Housing @ 10,200 project, 
demonstrating the ability of Proposition 123 participation to leverage significant state resources for 
locally identified housing priorities. 
Taken together, the regulatory incentive and project funding demonstrate a meaningful return on the 
community's participation in the program and provide an important funding mechanism for advancing 
local housing objectives. 
 
 
 
 
Topic of Presentation: 
2027–2029 Commitment 
Under C.R.S. § 29-32-105(1)(d), a local government seeking to remain eligible for Proposition 123 
funding must make and file its next commitment with DOLA by November 1, 2026. The new 
commitment will govern the January 1, 2027 through December 31, 2029 three-year cycle. 
The statutory framework for the next cycle differs from the current 9 percent commitment. 
Beginning with the 2027–2029 cycle, the required affordable housing target will be established using a 
locally responsive formula that considers the jurisdiction's recent housing production and applicable job-
growth factor. The calculation is generally based upon the jurisdiction's average annual number of 
permits for new housing units, multiplied by the applicable percentage associated with the county's job-
growth rate and the three-year commitment period. 
Based on current information regarding Lake County's job-growth rate, the applicable factor is 
anticipated to fall within the 10 percent to 15 percent range, depending upon the County's relationship to 
the statewide median. The significantly higher job-growth category does not appear to be applicable 
based on current information. 
This revised methodology is important because the 2027–2029 commitment will be based upon the 
community's actual recent housing production rather than a uniform 3 percent annual increase.

Development Pipeline and Capacity to Meet the Commitment 
The County and City Planning Departments, in coordination with the RHA, have been evaluating 
housing projects currently in the development pipeline to understand the community's capacity to satisfy 
the next commitment. 
Of particular significance is the Railyard Planned Unit Development, where current development 
interest across the three phases appears capable of generating affordable housing production 
substantially in excess of the anticipated Proposition 123 commitment threshold. 
This emerging development pipeline provides an important basis for considering continued participation 
in Proposition 123. It also demonstrates the value of coordinating the County's and City's housing 
policy, development review, and funding strategies rather than evaluating these efforts independently.
