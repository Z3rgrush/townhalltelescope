1. Union President Presentation: Captain Bo Knickman, Union President, a line staff captain and 
Wildland Coordinator, briefed the Fire Management Board to provide a "boots on the ground" 
perspective on wages and staffing, emphasizing a community-first approach. He characterizes the 
department as highly respected at state and national conferences for its skill and tactical 
performance, which makes it a point of pride for its members. Primary Concern: Wages are 
insufficient to retain staff, jeopardizing the department's strong culture, operational performance, 
and the non-monetary value built over years. Despite strong pride and positive external feedback, 
the department risks losing experienced personnel. Pay Disparity: A major issue is the pay 
disparity in Lake County, where firefighters earn approximately 25% less than law enforcement, a 
gap not seen elsewhere in the state where the average is 95%. This makes employees feel 
undervalued. Proposed Solution: A new percentage-based step-pay program is proposed, tying 
firefighter pay to 90% of the sheriff deputy's pay. This is considered a reasonable compromise to 
improve retention, even though it remains below the state average ratio. This 90% figure is a non-
binding guideline and can be renegotiated in future 1-2 year union contracts. Merit-Based 
Progression: The step program requires one year of service and an acceptable annual 
performance review to advance; a structure the union supports to incentivize performance and 
prevent automatic raises for underperforming staff. Implementation Options: To provide budget 
flexibility, the union proposed two implementation options: grandfathering all staff into their 
accrued steps or starting everyone one step back. The union finds the second option reasonable. 
Fiscal and Operational Impacts of Poor Retention: Financial Costs: Training and Equipment: 
It costs approximately $30,000 to train a new firefighter, and a past loss of staff led to a notable 
increase in apparatus damage costs due to inexperience. Overtime: A lack of qualified staff to 
“bump up” into senior roles forces costly overtime, with a 24-hour shift costing $1,500 versus a 
few hundred for a “bump up”. Wildfire Revenue: Gross revenue from wildland deployments, 
which helps fund apparatus, has increased from $50-150k annually to a projected $700,000 this 
year due to better retention. Operational & Safety Impacts: Experience Timeline: It takes 2.5 
years for a firefighter to be ready to act as an engineer and 5 years for an officer or engine boss. A 
straw poll indicated that under current pay trends, 75% of staff would leave within 5 years, a 
figure that significantly improves (23%) with the proposed plan. Public Safety: Inexperience can 
impact service quality and has placed young firefighters in high-stakes situations, causing 
significant mental health strain. There is a risk of "pencil whipping" qualifications to fill roles, 
compromising operational integrity. Swaner wanted mention something too, in this vein. When

Station II Staffing & Utilization: Bullock asked Chief if he was comfortable with Station II – how 
much it cost, how much to maintain, how to staff it. Two additional engineers were hired, but the 
station is not fully staffed due to deployments and five injuries. The plan is to staff it ~20 days per 
month in the fall, which is expected to reduce response times by 15-20 minutes for southern 
residents. Station II also provides temporary housing for sheriff's employees, storage for Search & 
Rescue, a bay for Public Works, and has served as an incident command post. Its classroom is 
heavily used for multi-agency training. The station's existence, along with apparatus acquisitions 
funded by wildland revenue, improved the area's ISO rating from 10 to 3. A union analysis 
estimated this saves the community ~$3.5 million in annual insurance premiums. There is a 
tremendous amount of value to Station II. It's been utilized twice during wildland fires for incident 
command teams. CBI uses it quite frequently. Historical context of a former volunteer presence in 
Twin Lakes and current use of the old garage. A two-bay garage in Twin Lakes, formerly used by a 
volunteer organization, is now used by Public Works. The volunteer model ended around 2000 
 
when the organization was professionalized with certification requirements. It was stated that a 
volunteer station "wouldn’t work" today due to the high cost, rapid turnover, and demanding 
training requirements of modern fire service. As the southern part of the county grows, the goal is 
to build a heated structure housing an apparatus to meet minimum ISO requirements, although a 
more comprehensive facility is desired. Tharp suggested her and Chief Dailey to explore, in the 
next year, the costs of using the county to provide desired services and assess what a fire district 
would require financially; consider engaging a consultant. If the fire department became a district, 
a proposal to absorb ambulance services was noted, but this could be detrimental to fire 
operations. Fiscal pressures necessitate new taxing mechanisms for the County since service costs 
are rising faster than revenues. Based on two prior attempts to form a district, stakeholders are 
aware of the significant overhead costs associated with creating a new bureaucracy, including 
finance, HR, and facilities. Tharp to meet with Birch Barron (Eagle County Emergency Manager) on 
Monday, 09-28-2026 at 9:00 AM to discuss pitfalls of fire districts, what could have been done 
differently, and cost comparisons between municipal/county service provision versus districts. 
The goal is to learn from Eagle County's decades-long (and unsuccessful) efforts to consolidate fire 
districts to avoid similar trial-and-error locally. Dailey: Since 2012, the department has pursued 
grants, raising approximately $6.2 million in external funds to cover positions and equipment 
without drawing from the general fund. While successful, this strategy had the unintended 
consequence of budget decreases when grants ended, creating ongoing funding challenges. 
Speaker 5 feels this creative funding may have "shot ourselves in the foot." Dailey confirms the 
“shot in the foot” refers to making do via alternate resources (grants) instead of steadily 
increasing the base budget.

Strategic Plan

Mission
• Reducing community risk through excellence in 
emergency service.
• PRIORITIES TO ACHIEVE OUR MISSION
• Emergency Response; To be mission ready at all costs
• Apparatus; To have all apparatus in service and safe for response
• Training; Train like your life depends on it, because it does
• Workout; Stay in great physical, and mental shape
• Projects; Stay current with your projects and have a plan
Vision
• We will be appropriately equipped, excellently trained, and 
eminently capable of meeting the community’s need for fire 
protection and emergency services.
• We will be a national example of a fire-rescue organization 
dedicated to providing excellent customer service
• We will provide effective staffing patterns throughout our service 
area by creating a fire-rescue department that draws and retains 
high-caliber, strongly motivated members.  We will be the kind of 
fire department to which people far and wide want to belong.
• We will be an aggressive fire department; renown for our assertive 
and effectual response, uncompromising in our commitment to 
responder safety and unquestionably dedicated to our reason for 
existing.
• We will be known as a smart fire department; one that studies, 
examines and learns and does not fear evolving.  People will come 
from miles around to train with us.
• We will be the model that other fire departments copy.
Values
• Excellence: In all things we will strive to achieve 
excellence, whether in the provision of our 
numerous services of in the working relationships 
we have with each other.
• Tradition: We will honor and live up to the finest 
traditions of Fire Service, particularly those that 
speak to faithful service to those in need brother and 
sisterhood and the trust of the community whom we 
serve.
• Attitude: We will know and fulfill our jobs,  We will 
never stop learning, improving or achieving.  We 
will present our selves to the public and to each 
other in the most professional of lights.
Ethics
The responsibility for ethical behavior rests with each individual 
member of the Department.
• Each member will remain accurate in their communications, never 
misrepresenting the facts or shading the truth.
• Each member will commit to not participating in firehouse rumors, 
denigrating other members or stripping other members of their 
dignity.
• Each member will commit to continuous learning and self-
improvement, always striving to fulfill the ideal of a “professional” 
fire fighter.
• Each member will commit to never violating the public trust 
through act, word, or deed.
• Each member will commit to the continuous development of on 
team, one fire department, one mission.
• Each member will look-out for the welfare of other members and 
help other members overcome their weaknesses while learning from 
their strengths.
Dignity Statement
LLCFR respects the uniqueness of all individuals and recognizes that 
having people with divers backgrounds, creeds, points-of-view and 
ethnicities brings strength to the organization.
• We will ensure equal opportunity to all members
• We will encourage all members to communicate and behave in a 
manner which is sensitive to and acknowledges the viewpoints of 
others.
• We will regard diversity as a resource that enriches the 
organizational environment through the sharing of differing 
perspectives, experiences, and ideas.
• We will removed barriers to teamwork through collaboration, 
problem solving, and the constructive resolution of conflicts.
• All members of the organizations are expected to treat each other 
with respect at all times.  Inappropriate behavior will not be 
tolerated and may result in disciplinary action at the discretion of 
the Department.
Core Ideology
• “First in-Last out” is the core ideology of LLCFR.  In this 
simple statement lives the standard of excellence to which we 
measure ourselves, the fines traditions of the fire service to 
which we commit ourselves and the altitude to which we daily 
aspire.  We are the community’s all-risk response agency to 
which people will turn to in time of need.  We therefore pride 
ourselves on being the first in – never shirking our 
responsibility to protect the public whom we serve or serving 
the public whom we protect – and last out – never leaving 
until the job is finished.  This is the ideology of the entire 
organization, from management to line firefighter, which 
drives our daily efforts, whether conducting vehicle checks, 
helping with a community event, fighting fire or rescuing 
people from danger.
• It is the faith we instill in those who call 911.

purpose for existing is to save lives and protect 
property.

Hazmat
The purpose of LLCFR’s Hazmat Program is to ensure 
the Department’s readiness for hazmat events that 
could reasonable affect Leadville, Lake County and 
the South Central All Hazards Region, particularly 
those which may be of low frequency but high 
consequence; and to ensure the proper working 
order of LLCFR’s hazmat and respiratory protective 
equipment.
• Problem Statement: FF’s have insufficient direction 
with regard to Hazmat responses
• Goal Statement: We will improve FF’s safety by 
providing comprehensive plans for responding to 
Hazmat incidents.

Rescue
The purpose of LLCFR’s rescue program is to provide 
adequate first-responder rescuers at the Engine Company 
level, and through the Technical Rescue Team, lead the 
effort to provide technician level rescuers who are capable of 
responding to the various types of rescue incidents that are 
likely to occur in Leadville and Lake County.
• Problem Statement: The public, FF’s and members of the 
TRT face unsafe conditions during rescue incidents because 
LLCFR has not adequately determined the priority of needs 
of training and preparation.
• Goal Statement: Building on what we already know, we will 
develop a prioritized list of training, certification 
requirements, and capability needs for LLCFR for local 
response & to support SAR when needed.
Rescue
• Objective 1: By 12/31/23 complete a capability 
needs assessment for LLCFR 
• Objective 2: By 12/31/23 develop a prioritized 
list of training/certification needs for LLCFR
• Objective 3: By 08/15/21 Complete an inventory 
of equipment and have all other needs for 
budget purposes. (Done)

Communications
The purpose of this program is to produce effective and 
efficient operation communications and promote 
effective and efficient inter-agency operation 
communications through comprehensive planning, 
appropriate equipment acquisition and cooperation in 
the development of common operation communication 
procedures.
• Problem Statement: FF safety is compromised by the 
lack of hand-held radios and poor transmissions.
• Goal Statement: We will implement procedures for 
maximizing the use of Department hand-held radios 
and acquire an adequate supply of appropriate hand-
held radios.

the Department to safely, effectively, and efficiently 
meet the emergency service needs of the community.

consistently earn certifications by the required 
deadlines. (JPR’s Completed)
• Goal Statement: We will implement a process by which 
training requirements are tracked and sufficient 
opportunities are given to ensure that certifications are 
acquired in a timely fashion.

Vehicle Maintenance
The purpose of LLCFR’s vehicle maintenance program is to ensure 
that the organizations apparatus are ready to respond to fires and 
other emergencies and that each vehicle functions safely, efficiently 
and effectively when operated appropriately.
• Problem Statement: With an aging fleet there are many 
maintenance issues that arise each budget year
• Goal Statement: To set a replacement schedule for all apparatus on 
a 10-15 yr. rotation. (hired a consultant 2022) 
• Replacement plan set in place, but is not being implemented due to 
lack of funds. It will be used as a priority list as capital items need to 
be replaced
