INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT BY AND BETWEEN 
THE CITY OF LEADVILLE AND 
THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF LAKE COUNTY, COLORADO 
THIS INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT is entered into on this ____ day of 
______________, 202 6, (the “Effective  Date”) by and between the CITY OF LEADVILLE, 
COLORADO, a Colorado municipal corporation (the “City”) and the BOARD OF COUNTY 
COMMISSIONERS OF LAKE COUNTY, COLORADO (the “County” or the “BOCC”) (each a 
“Party” and together the “Parties”). 
RECITALS 
WHEREAS, the City is authorized to establish and maintain a fire department and to 
acquire firefighting equipment pursuant to Colorado Revised Statutes (“C.R.S.”) § 31-15-601(1); 
WHEREAS, pursuant to such statutory authority, the City has established and operates 
and maintains a fire department; 
WHEREAS, the County is authorized pursuant to C.R.S. § 30-25-106(1) and C.R.S. § 30-
10-513, to appropriate money for the purpose of controlling fires in the County and to allow 
municipal fire departments such compensation and other expenses as the County deems necessary; 
and 
WHEREAS, the City and County are authorized pursuant to C.R.S. § 29 -1-203 to 
cooperate and contract with one another to provide any function, service or facility lawfully 
authorized to each of the cooperating or contracting units. 
NOW THEREFORE, the Parties agree that the purposes, powers, rights, obligations, and 
the responsibilities of the contracting Parties are as follows: 
1.0 PARTIES.  The Parties to this agreement (the “Agreement”) are the City of Leadville, a 
Colorado municipal corporation and the Board of County Commissioners of Lake County, 
Colorado, a political subdivision of the state of Colorado. 
2.0 PURPOSE.  The City and County desire to enter into an agreement to provide for adequate 
fire protection for the incorporated and unincorporated areas of Lake County.  The purpose of this 
Agreement is to allow the Parties to annually identify, establish and mutually agree upon priorities 
for fire protection, medical, hazardous materials response, and any other services and to equitably 
apportion the costs of those services between the Parties.  Finally, this Agreement provides 
fallback funding provisions if the Parties cannot reach an agreement regarding services and cost 
distribution. 
3.0 TERM.  The initial term of this Agreement shall be for twelve (12) months, 
commencingcommence on  as of ________________, 2026 and terminat eing on December 31, 
2026.  The parties shall renegotiate this Agreement for the 2027 once more comprehensive 
 
reporting is provided pursuant to Section 6.0 that allows the Parties to have more information to 
evaluate the fiscal impact related to the current fund ing obligations of the Parties.  ; provided, 
however, that this Agreement shall be automatically renewed for successive terms of one year each 
unless notice of termination is given by one Party to the other not later than June 30th of any given 
year to effect termination on the first day of January of the succeeding year .. Either pParty may 
terminate this Agreement without cause by providing the other pParty with ninety (90) days’ prior 
written notice. Upon such termination, the pParties shall fulfill any obligations accrued through 
the effective date of termination, and all provisions that by their nature should survive termination 
shall remain in effect. 
4.0 TERMS AND CONDITIONS. 
4.1. Service, Facilities.  The City shall operate, employ personnel, maintain, and equip 
a fire department (“Fire Department”) to serve both the incorporated and unincorporated areas 
of Lake County.  The Fire Department currently operates two fire stations: 816 Harrison 
Avenue in the City of Leadville (the “Leadville Station”), and 141 County Road 10 (the “Fire 
Station II”).  The Fire Department may operate additional fire stations in the future, that shall 
be located in the City or the County.  Funding for the Fire Department shall be determined by 
the procedures in Section 5.0 below. 
4.1.1 Resident/Reserves/Volunteers. Resident Reserves, as known by industry 
standards, are an essential part of providing fire protection in the incorporated and 
unincorporated areas of Lake County, the Fire Department shall also provide adequate 
fire protection  training and equipment to all fire department Resident/Reserves and 
volunteers as determined necessary by the Leadville Fire Chief.  Such Resident/Reserve 
and volunteer training shall be in conformity with National Fire Protection Association 
(“NFPA”) standards. The Management Board, shall be responsible for recommending 
Resident/Reserve solicitation programs and evaluating compensation methods for 
Resident/Reserves. The Fire Department shall continue with the solicitation and 
recruitment of new Resident/Reserves including without limitation using the Colorado 
Mountain College (CMC) fire academy program for individuals who have decided to 
obtain Firefighter 1 Certification.  The Fire Department will also train jointly with Lake 
County Search and Rescue and other agencies on activities including, but not limited to, 
swift and ice water rescue, low angle rescue, lost and missing persons rescues, hazardous 
materials, and training set forth by the Office of Emergency Management. 
4.1.2 Vehicle Maintenance.  The County agrees to assist the City with any labor to 
perform general maintenance on certain Fire Department vehicles by City and County 
mechanics, and the City and the County shall agree to a standard, pre -arranged cost of 
such labo r.  Routine maintenance that is not required to be done by an Emergency 
Vehicle Technician will be performed by City or County mechanics in a timely manner.  
Emergency repairs will be handled at the discretion of the Fire Chief.  Any non-routine 
maintenance work on fire engines shall be completed only by mechanics that have 
obtained the Emergency Vehicle Technician Certification and shall be completed to 
meet or exceed NFPA 1901 Standards.  If or when the City or County has a mechanic 
 
with Emergency Vehicle Technician Certification, then maintenance and repairs by such 
certified mechanic will be considered. 
4.2 Leadville Station.  The City is the fee owner of the Leadville Station property.  If 
the City sells or conveys legal title to the Leadville Station to any third party for any use other than 
providing fire protection to the City and County, the net proceeds (exclusive of transactional costs 
and fees) from the sale of the Leadville Station property shall be split 50/50 with the City and 
County, and such proceeds shall be used toward future fire protection costs or as otherwise agreed 
to by the Parties.  If the Leadville Station property is conveyed to a fire district established under 
Title 32, Colorado Revised Statutes, then this provision shall not apply; it being the intent of the 
Parties that all fire protection facilities and equipment owned by the Ci ty and/or the County will 
be conveyed to such fire district at no cost. 
4.3 Fire Station II.  The County is the fee owner of the Fire Station II property.  If the 
County sells or conveys legal title to the Fire Station II to any third party for any use other than 
providing fire protection to the City and County, the net proceeds (exclusive of transactional costs 
and fees) from the sale of Fire Station II property, shall be split 50/50 with the City and County, 
and such proceeds shall be used toward future fire protection costs or as otherwise agreed to by 
the Parties.  If the Fire Station II property is conveyed to a fire district established under Title 32, 
Colorado Revised Statutes, then this provision shall not apply; it being the intent of the Parties that 
all fire protection facilities and equipment owned by the City and/or the County will be conveyed 
to such fire district at no cost. 
4.4 Any Future Stations.  Decisions about funding, location, design and construction of 
future stations will be subject to future negotiations and agreement between the City and 
County. Additional fire stations will be located, designed, constructed, and acqui red 
through each Party's capital budgeting and planning process, and will be operated under 
the terms of this Agreement, as may be modified between the Parties. 
4.5 International Fire Code.  The Fire Chief is authorized to appoint a Fire Marshal or 
similar position to implement a prevention and inspection program and enforce the 
International Fire Code, as amended, and adopted by the Parties’ respective governing 
bodies, within the incorporated and unincorporated areas of Lake County.  Permit and 
inspection fees collected through the prevention and inspection program shall be 
distributed to a designated revenue account within the City Fire Department budget.  The 
Fire Marshal may use these funds for professional development courses as authorized to 
do so by the Fire Chief.   
4.6 Liability for Expenses.  To the extent permitted by law, the City waives all rights 
to collect expenses from private property owners within unincorporated areas of Lake 
County as may be allowed pursuant to C.R.S. § 30 -10-513.5.  However, neither the City 
nor the County waives the right to collect expenses or any other remuneration from any 
source as may be provided by any other statute, contract or common law. 
4.7 Management Board.  The Management Board consisting of the Mayor of 
Leadville, the City Administrator, one Lake County Commissioner, the Lake County 
Manager, and a member chosen at large by consensus of the four other members (the 
 
“Management Board”) shall be responsible for advisory guidance of the Fire Department 
through communication with the Parties’ respective governing bodies.  Management 
Board duties include without limitation review and approval of operational policies and 
new contracts in accordance with City and County procurement policies, and providing 
direction to the Fire Chief on emergent issues that affect budget and staff. The 
Management Board shall make recommendations to the City and County for long range 
planning including without limitation one- and five-year plans, plans for a potential future 
fire protection district, future additional stations, and equipment needs.  The Management 
Board shall submit a Preliminary Fire Budget, which shall include both operating and 
capital budgets, to the City Council and the Board of County Commissioners according 
to the schedule and Section 5.  The Management Board shall review any changes to pay 
structure and compensation, including review of proposed changes to the union contract.  
The Management Board shall make decisions by majority vote.  The City retains the 
exclusive right and responsibility to hire, discipline, fire, assign or otherwise manage Fire 
Department employees, including the Fire Chief.  Day -to-day management of  the Fire 
Chief, consistent with decisions made by the Management Board and the approved 
Budget (as determined under Section 5), will be conducted by the City Administrator, or 
in the event of that position being vacant or eliminated, by the Mayor.  The Fi re Chief 
shall be responsible for making recommendations to the Management Board, including 
without limitation annual capital and operational budget, five year plans, plans for a fire 
protection district if requested by the Management Board, future or addi tional stations, 
and other items as requested by the Management Board. 
5.0 FUNDING PROCEDURES 
5.1 Budget Process.  The Management Board shall meet as necessary, but at least 
quarterly, and shall work in good faith to review, revise, refine and finalize a preliminary 
Joint Fire Budget, which shall include an Operational Budget and Capital Expenditures 
Fund (as set forth in subsections 5.1.1 and 5.1.2).  The Management Board shall submit 
the preliminary Joint Fire Budget to the governing bodies of each Party for consideration 
on a timeline and in a format requested by each Party, and each Party shall work 
cooperatively with the Fire Chief to assist with preparation of budget material in their 
desired formats.   
The Fire Chief, and Management Board if desired, shall attend at least one work session 
each with the City Council and Board of County Commissioners each calendar year to 
assess year-to-date expenditures against the current year budget and identify early i n 
each Party’s budget process any emerging issues that have significant budget 
implications. 
 As part of their respective budgets, the governing body of each Party shall approve the 
Joint Fire Budget and appropriations for the same for the next budget year, subject to 
and in compliance with the Local Government Budget Law of Colorado (C.R.S. § 29-1-
101, et seq.). 
5.1.1 Operational Budget.  The Joint Fire Budget shall include a budget for 
operational expenses.  Operational expenses shall be apportioned as follows: the County’s 
proportionate share equals 70% of the budgeted expenditures and the City's proportionate 
share equals 30% of budgeted expenditures of the Fire Department in any given year (the 
 
“Operational Budget”).  The Management Board will inform their respective governing 
bodies of unexpected expenses on a monthly basis, thus ensuring both Parties are aware of 
any potential overages.  Previously unbudgeted expenses will then be split 70/30 as all 
other o perational expenses.  If supplemental appropriation of funds is necessary, the 
Management Board shall initiate supplemental appropriation requests for consideration 
and approval by the Parties' respective governing bodies in accordance with C. R.S. § 29-
1-109. 
5.1.2 Capital Expenditures Fund.  The Joint Fire Budget shall include a fund for 
capital expenditures that is consistent with the priorities outlined in a capital apparatus and 
equipment worksheet, prepared and updated at least annually by the Fire Chief and 
reviewed by the Management Board (the " Capital Expenditure Fund ").  The Capital 
Expenditures Fund shall be sufficient for approved capital expenditures in the budget year 
while also containing additional funds to be held for anticipated expenditures in future 
years, the intent being to accumulate in this fund monies sufficient to avoid emergency 
appropriations or large single-year appropriations.  The Capital Expenditure Fund shall be 
apportioned as follows: the County's proportionate share equals 70% of  the budgeted 
expenditures and the City's proportionate share equals 30% of budgeted expenditures of 
the Fire Department in any given year.   
5.1.3 Fire Fund.  The Fire Fund shall exist for any monies collected by the Fire 
Department specifically for wildfire fighting or aviation standby services and other contract 
services performed by the Fire Department (the "Contract Services").  This revenue shall 
first be used by the Fire Department to pay for employee wages, training, and other costs 
directly associated with providing Contract Services.  Thereafter, up to 30% of the 
remaining Contract Services revenues from Contract Services may be allocated by the Fire 
Chief to programs identified in the Fire Department's supplemental program budget for the 
following year.  The balance of the remaining Contract Services revenues shall be held in 
the Fire Fund and may be allocated to the Capital Expenditure Fund. 
5.2 Impasse.  If an agreement as to the Joint Fire Budget cannot be reached by December 1 for 
the next budget year, the Joint Fire Budget for the next budget year shall default to the prior year's 
Joint Fire Budget plus an adjustment for inflation equal to the  prior year's annual percentage 
change in the Consumer Price Index for the Denver-Boulder area (using the first half year estimate) 
or 5.5%, whichever is less. Sale of Capital assets shall be deposited into the Fire Fund. The Fire 
Management Board will advise on allocation of the Fire Fund. 
5.3 Payment.  The City shall issue, based on a calendar year, monthly invoices to the County 
for the County's proportionate share of the Joint Fire Budget.  The County shall pay in full such 
monthly amounts within 30 days of receipt of the invoice, unless the County protests an invoice in 
writing within the 30 -day period.  In the event of such a protest, the disputed amount shall be 
subject to the dispute resolution procedures found in Section 8.0 of this Agreement and the 
undisputed amount shall be paid. 
The City shall maintain all financial records related to the Fire Department in accordance with 
C.R.S. § 29-1-114 and shall make such records available for County inspection upon request. 
 
5.4 Adjustment for Actual Joint Fire Budget Costs.  As part of the City's annual audit 
process, the City shall cause an audit to be performed of the Joint Fire Budget.  The audit shall 
be conducted according to generally accepted accounting principles for gove rnmental entities.  
A copy of that portion of the City's audit including the audit work papers, pertaining to the Joint 
Fire Budget shall be furnished to the County.  Based on the City's annual audit, the Parties agree 
to negotiate any needed adjustments in the Joint Fire Budget. 
The County may, upon reasonable notice, cause an independent audit or performance review of 
the Fire Department’s use of County funds once per fiscal year at the County's expense.

6.2 The Fire Chief shall provide the BOCC and City Council with a quarterly 
brief on the status of the Fire Department at the second regularly scheduled monthly BOCC and 
council meetings following the end of each quarter.Bi-Annual Service and Activity Reports. 
The Fire Chief shall prepare and deliver to both the City Council and the Board of County 
Commissioners, within 30 days after the close of each calendar quarter, a written report detailing 
Fire Department operations and activity levels. Each bi-annual report shall include, at a 
minimum: 
a. Call Volume and Service Area Breakdown. A summary of all incident responses during the 
reporting period, categorized by: 
(i) total number of calls within the City limits; 
(ii) total number of calls within unincorporated areas of Lake County; 
(iii) mutual aid or automatic aid calls outside City and County boundaries. 
 
b. Incident Type and Classification. Calls shall be broken down by NFIRS (National Fire 
Incident Reporting System) categories or comparable codes, including but not limited to: 
structure fires, wildland fires, vehicle fires, medical/EMS calls, hazardous materials incidents, 
rescues, false alarms, and other public-service responses. 
c. Response Time and Staffing. Average and median response times for City and County calls, 
average on-scene duration, and the number and classification (career, resident/reserve, volunteer) 
of responding personnel. 
d. Labor Hours and Resource Allocation. Estimated total personnel hours and apparatus hours 
expended on City versus County incidents, sufficient to assess the proportional use of resources 
and fiscal impact to each jurisdiction. 
e. Training, Prevention, and Inspections. Summary of training hours conducted, inspections 
performed, and code enforcement activities, specifying the number conducted within the City 
and within unincorporated County areas. 
f. Financial Summary. A year-to-date comparison of actual versus budgeted expenditures and 
revenues, highlighting any variances greater than 5% and identifying any unanticipated 
operational or capital costs. 
6.2 Annual Performance Report. 
No later than March 31 following the end of each fiscal year, the Fire Chief shall provide an 
Annual Performance Report to both governing bodies. The Annual Report shall consolidate the 
quarterly data and include: 
a. Annual totals and percentages for City versus County service calls by type; 
b. Trends in call volume, response times, and resource allocation; 
c. Narrative summary of major incidents, interagency coordination, training achievements, and 
prevention activities; and 
d. Recommendations for operational improvements, staffing levels, or capital planning for the 
upcoming year. 
6.3 Strategic Planning. 
The Management Board, in consultation with the Fire Chief, shall develop and maintain strategic 
plans for Fire Department operations, staffing, and capital needs. These plans shall include one-
year operational goals and a five-year capital improvement and staffing plan. The plans shall be 
reviewed and updated annually and presented to the City Council and the Board of County 
Commissioners as part of the Joint Fire Budget process. 
6.4 Format and Data Access. 
Reports shall be submitted in both electronic and written form, in a mutually agreed-upon format 
that allows for data sorting and analysis (e.g., Excel or equivalent). Upon request, the County 
shall be granted reasonable access to the underlying call data and NFIRS or CAD (Computer-
Aided Dispatch) reports used to generate the summaries. 
 
6.4 Failure to Report. 
Failure to provide the required reports in a timely or complete manner shall constitute a material 
breach of this Agreement if not corrected within 30 days after written notice from either Party. 
The Management Board shall review any such deficiencies and recommend corrective action. 
 
7.0 DEFAULT.  If either Party materially defaults in the performance of any of the 
material covenants or agreements to be kept, done or performed by it under the terms of this 
Agreement, the non-defaulting Party shall notify the defaulting Party in writing of the nature of 
such default.  Within ten days following receipt of such notice the defaulting Party shall correct 
such default or, in the event of a default not capable of being corrected within the ten days, the 
defaulting Party shall inform the non -defaulting party of such and shall take reasonable steps to 
commence correcting the default within ten days of receipt of notification thereof, thereafter 
completing correction of the default with due diligence.  If the defaulting Party fails to correct the 
default as provided in this Agreement, the non-defaulting Party, without further notice, shall have 
the right to declare that this Agreement is terminated effective upon such date as the non-defaulting 
Party shall designate and the non-defaulting Party shall have the right to recover such damages as 
it shall be entitled to by law or as provided in the Agreement.  The rights and remedies provided 
for in this Agreement may be exercised singly or in combination.  
8.0 DISPUTE RESOLUTION.  The Parties agree that it is in the best interest of both 
Parties to attempt to resolve disputes that arise under this Agreement in an efficient and 
inexpensive manner.  To that end, the Parties commit to use their best efforts to attempt to resolve 
disputes informally.  For disputes that arise under this Agreement as to any action, decision or 
performance of either Party, the Parties promptly through their designated representatives from the 
Management Board shall begin negotiation s with one another in good faith in order to reach 
resolution of the dispute.  If the Parties representatives from the Management Board cannot agree 
to a resolution of the dispute within fourteen days after commencement of negotiations, written 
notice of the dispute (the "Dispute Notice") shall be delivered within 24-hours after such 14-day 
period by each of the Parties' Management Board representatives to each of the Parties' respective 
legislative bodies.  Within 30 business days after receipt of the Dispute Notice, the Parties, through 
their respective legislative bodies, shall determine whether other alternative dispute resolutions, 
such as mediation or arbitration, may be appropriate.  If the Parties are unable to agree on the 
manner in which to resolve the dispute, either Party may pursue its rights or remedies in accordance 
with Section 7.0 of this Agreement. 
9.0 TERMINATION.  In addition to termination of this Agreement pursuant to Sections 
3 and 7 of this Agreement, and without limitation upon any rights and remedies that may be 
available to a non-defaulting Party, the following apply: 
9.0.1     If the Leadville Station ceases to be used for fire protection purposes, disposition 
of the fire station shall be in accordance with Section 4.2 of this Agreement. 
 
9.0.2 If the Fire Station II ceases to be used for fire protection purposes, disposition of 
the fire station shall be in accordance with Section 4.3 of this Agreement. 
9.0.3 If a special fire protection district is formed pursuant to C.R.S. § 32-1-101, et seq., 
and the district's service plan includes the use of the Leadville Station, Fire Station II, 
and/or any other acquired or developed fire stations, neither Party shall transfer or dispose 
of its interests, if any, in any station, fire equipment or fire pr otection vehicles except to 
the fire protection district, subject to and in compliance with applicable State and local 
laws and regulations. 
9.0.4 In the event of termination, the Parties shall work toward an equitable resolution 
based on the principle of the 70 -30 (County/City) split of operating expenses of accrued 
and vested pension obligations existing at the date of termination in conformance wi th 
applicable law.
