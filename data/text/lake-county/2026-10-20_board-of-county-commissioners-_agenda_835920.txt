Subject Courthouse Grounds, Monument, Use, and Display Policy (FAC-01) 
 
Background 
The courthouse lawn has historically been used for private organized activities, such as voter 
registration drives, and the County has allowed domestic violence prevention signage on the lawn 
with approval at the sta; level. That practice creates legal risk. When the County opens property 
to private signs and displays, it may create a public forum in which it must accept every message 
on equal terms, including messages the County would not choose to endorse. Monuments and 
displays the County accepts and controls are the County's own speech, which the County may 
select freely (Pleasant Grove City v. Summum, 555 U.S. 460 (2009)). The County can say what it 
wishes and select the views that it wants to express. However, a program the County does not 
actively control is treated as private speech, even when the County intended otherwise and the 
County is prohibited from discriminating against speakers based on their viewpoint. (Shurtle5 v. 
City of Boston, 596 U.S. 243 (2022)). 
The proposed policy is intended to bring the Board's control of the courthouse grounds in line with 
that framework by: 
• Closing the courthouse lawn and building exterior (“Restricted Grounds”) to private signs 
and displays. Expression on Restricted Grounds is limited to monuments the Board 
accepts, County-sponsored seasonal displays, and County-sponsored public awareness 
displays, each adopted as the County's own speech. The closure applies to all subjects 
and speakers alike; 
• Recognizing the perimeter sidewalks as a traditional public forum. Individuals and groups 
may protest, demonstrate, or hold signs there without a permit, subject only to keeping 
entrances and accessible routes clear. A permit is required only for tables, canopies, 
freestanding signs, or other equipment, and is decided by the County Manager against 
objective, content-neutral criteria within ten business days; 
• Creating a new facility use permit for the Parking Lot, where organized activities such as the 
Leadville Race series or other groups can be directed. Permits are decided on viewpoint-
neutral criteria (scheduling, ﬁre lane and accessible parking, capacity, insurance or 
indemniﬁcation, cleanup, and disruption to courthouse operations), with any fee limited to 
the County's actual costs;

• Allowing commercial or exclusive use of the Parking Lot, such as races and similar events, 
with a substitute parking and access plan for full closures and a fee reﬂecting the fair value 
of the exclusive use, consistent with Colo. Const. art. XI, § 2; 
• Moving approval of public awareness displays, such as domestic violence prevention 
signage, from the County Manager to the Board. Each display requires a Board motion or 
resolution approving its content, design, and display period; must be attributed to the 
County; and, if recurring, must be reauthorized each time. These requirements document 
the Board's active control so the displays remain government speech and do not open the 
lawn to competing private displays; and 
• Retaining the Board's sole discretion over monuments, including a public hearing for new 
monuments, historical signiﬁcance criteria, limits on religious displays, and the Board's 
ability to remove a monument at any time. 
The County Manager decides only sidewalk equipment permits and Parking Lot facility use 
permits, each appealable to the Board. All decisions concerning Restricted Grounds rest with the 
Board. If adopted, groups that have used the lawn for organized activities should be directed to the 
sidewalks or the Parking Lot, and any public awareness display the Board wishes to continue will 
need Board approval under the new process.

Subject Intergovernmental Agreement with the City of Leadville for Fire Department 
Services 
 
Background 
The City of Leadville operates the Leadville/Lake County Fire Rescue Department (the “Fire 
Department”), which serves both the City and unincorporated Lake County from the Leadville. The 
County funds a share of the Fire Department’s costs under an intergovernmental agreement with 
the City. 
The most recent City/County ﬁre agreement on ﬁle is the 2015 IGA, which had a one-year term and 
has renewed automatically each year since. 
County and City staff and counsel have negotiated a replacement agreement. The proposed IGA 
keeps the basic structure of the 2015 agreement — the City operates and staffs the Fire 
Department and the County pays its share through a jointly developed budget — but updates the 
term, governance, cost-sharing, capital planning, audit, and reporting provisions to give both 
governing bodies clearer budget predictability and more visibility into Fire Department operations.

Other Key Provisions 
• Union Agreement (§§ 5.5, 15.0). The City may not modify its collective bargaining agreement 
with The Leadville Union Paid Fireﬁghters in a way that materially increases the County’s costs 
until the Joint Fire Budget for the upcoming year has been agreed. If the IGA and the Union 
Agreement conﬂict, the Union Agreement controls. 
• Fire stations (§§ 4.2–4.4, 9.0). The City owns the Leadville Station and the County owns Fire 
Station II. If either station is sold for a non-ﬁre use, the net proceeds are split 50/50 and used 
for future ﬁre protection. Stations transfer at no cost to any Title 32 ﬁre protection district that 
is formed. Future stations require separate negotiation and agreement. 
• Fire code and prevention (§ 4.5). The Fire Chief may appoint a Fire Marshal to run the 
prevention and inspection program and enforce the International Fire Code as adopted by 
each Party throughout the County. Permit and inspection fees, including those under the 2015 
Fire Prevention Program fee schedule, go to a designated revenue account in the Fire 
Department budget. 
• Operations (§§ 4.1.1–4.1.2). The IGA continues resident reserve and volunteer recruitment 
and NFPA-conforming training, including through the CMC ﬁre academy and joint training with 
Lake County Search and Rescue. It also provides for City and County mechanics to perform 
routine vehicle maintenance at a pre-arranged labor cost, with non-routine ﬁre engine work 
limited to EVT-certiﬁed mechanics. 
• Cost recovery (§ 4.6). The City waives its right under C.R.S. § 30-10-513.5 to collect ﬁreﬁghting 
expenses from private property owners in unincorporated Lake County. Both Parties keep all 
other cost recovery rights. 
• Default, disputes, and termination (§§ 7.0–10.0). Default requires notice and a 10-day cure 
period. Disputes go ﬁrst to the Parties’ Management Board representatives, then to the 
governing bodies, then to optional mediation or arbitration. The prevailing Party in litigation 
recovers attorneys’ fees. On termination, accrued and vested pension obligations are resolved 
under the 70/30 principle. 
• Risk allocation and appropriation (§§ 12.0, 23.0, 24.0). There is no indemniﬁcation: each 
Party is responsible for its own negligence, and both Parties keep the protections of the 
Colorado Governmental Immunity Act. Each Party’s obligations are subject to annual 
appropriation and do not create multiple-ﬁscal-year debt. 
• Supersession (§ 15.0). The IGA replaces the Parties’ prior cooperative ﬁre protection 
agreement. 
Legal Authority 
C.R.S. § 29-1-203 authorizes governments to cooperate or contract with one another to provide 
any function, service, or facility lawfully authorized to each of them, if the contract is approved by 
each party’s governing body.

Subject New IGA with the City of Aurora 
 
Background 
The City of Aurora is our largest and most substantial partner for Lake County’s Water Resources. 
Portions of Lake County’s water rights come from the City of Aurora system and rely on Aurora’s 
coordination and cooperation, outlined in our IGA, to fully utilize. Our existing IGA has expired, but 
we continue to operate under its conditions/agreements. This new IGA will change and add some 
elements to allow for the successful integration and implementation of the Lake County Water 
Projects Enterprise. 
Topic for Presentation  
The new Aurora IGA adds and changes several elements to make the Water Project Enterprise 
operations implementation possible: 
• Lake County’s water delivered from Aurora’s system will be available 365 days a year for 
augmentation and return ﬂow augmentations. This is changed from the existing IGA’s 
limitation on water delivery from March 15th through November 15th of a given year. 
• Adding more delivery points for water: we can request Aurora’s delivery of our IGA water 
from several more points to optimize its availability for storage and Enterprise use. 
• Hayden Rec Pond coordinated use: by allowing Aurora to use Hayden Pond for storage, we 
can coordinate use to A) make Hayden Pond available for Lake County storage as well and 
B) not endanger or impact the existing recreational use and ﬁsh habitat of the pond. 
• Coordination on shared infrastructure: De Lappe Ditch, Upper River Ditch, and the future 
Box Creek Reservoir. 
IGA is meant to be comprehensive and amends, restates, and replaces all prior agreements and 
IGAs with Aurora. The intent is to reduce confusion, provide historical context for our relationship, 
and clarify operations, implementation, and compliance by successor StaZ and leadership.
