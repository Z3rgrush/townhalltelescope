INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT BY AND BETWEEN 
THE CITY OF LEADVILLE AND 
THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF LAKE COUNTY, COLORADO 
THIS INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT is entered into on this ____ day of 
______________, 2026, by and between the CITY OF LEADVILLE, COLORADO, a Colorado 
municipal corporation (the “City”) and the BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF LAKE 
COUNTY, COLORADO (the “County” or the “BOCC”) (each a “Party” and together the 
“Parties”). 
RECITALS 
WHEREAS, the City is authorized to establish and maintain a fire department and to 
acquire firefighting equipment pursuant to Colorado Revised Statutes (“C.R.S.”) § 31-15-601(1); 
WHEREAS, pursuant to such statutory authority, the City has established and operates 
and maintains a fire department; 
WHEREAS, the County is authorized pursuant to C.R.S. § 30-25-106(1) and C.R.S. § 30-
10-513, to appropriate money for the purpose of controlling fires in the County and to allow 
municipal fire departments such compensation and other expenses as the County deems necessary; 
and 
WHEREAS, the City and County are authorized pursuant to C.R.S. § 29- 1-203 to 
cooperate and contract with one another to provide any function, service or facility lawfully 
authorized to each of the cooperating or contracting units. 
NOW THEREFORE, the Parties agree that the purposes, powers, rights, obligations, and 
the responsibilities of the contracting Parties are as follows: 
1.0 PARTIES.  The Parties to this agreement (the “Agreement”) are the City of Leadville, a 
Colorado municipal corporation and the Board of County Commissioners of Lake County, 
Colorado, a political subdivision of the state of Colorado. 
2.0 PURPOSE.  The City and County desire to enter into an agreement to provide for adequate 
fire protection for the incorporated and unincorporated areas of Lake County.  The purpose of this 
Agreement is to allow the Parties to annually identify, establish and mutually agree upon priorities 
for fire protection, medical, hazardous materials response, and any other services and to equitably 
apportion the costs of those services between the Parties.  Finally, this Agreement provides 
fallback funding provisions if the Parties cannot reach an agreement regarding services and cost 
distribution. 
3.0 TERM AND TERMINATION.    
3.1 Initial Term; Renewal. The initial term of this Agreement shall be for three (3) 
years, commencing on January 1, 2027, and terminating on December 31, 2029 (the "Initial 
 
Term"). Upon expiration of the Initial Term, this Agreement shall automatically renew for 
successive one (1) year terms (each a "Renewal Term"), unless either Party provides written notice 
of non-renewal at least ninety (90) days prior to the expiration of the then-current term. 
3.2 Termination. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Parties may renegotiate the terms 
of this Agreement at any time , and either party may terminate this Agreement without cause by 
providing the other party with ninety (90) days' prior written notice. Upon such termination, the 
Parties shall fulfill any obligations accrued through the effective date of termination, and all 
provisions that by their nature should survive termination shall remain in effect. 
4.0 TERMS AND CONDITIONS. 
4.1. Service, Facilities.  The City shall operate, employ personnel, maintain, and equip 
a fire department (“Fire Department”) to serve both the incorporated and unincorporated areas of 
Lake County.  The Fire Department currently operates two fire stations: 816 Harrison Avenue in 
the City of Leadville (the “Leadville  Station”), and 141 County Road 10 (the “Fire  Station II”).  
The Fire Department may operate additional fire stations in the future, that shall be located in  the 
City or the County.  Funding for the Fire Department shall be determined by the procedures in 
Section 5.0 below. 
4.1.1 Resident/Reserves/Volunteers. Resident Reserves, as known by industry standards, 
are an essential part of providing fire protection in the incorporated and unincorporated 
areas of Lake County, the Fire Department shall also provide adequate fire protection  
training and equipment to all fire department Resident/ Reserves and volunteers as 
determined necessary by the Leadville Fire Chief.  Such Resident/Reserve and volunteer 
training shall be in conformity with National Fire Protection Association (“NFPA”) 
standards. The Management Board, shall be responsible for r ecommending 
Resident/Reserve solicitation programs and evaluating compensation methods for 
Resident/Reserves. The Fire Department shall continue with the solicitation and 
recruitment of new Resident/Reserves including without limitation using the Colorado 
Mountain College (CMC) fire academy program for individuals who have decided to obtain 
Firefighter 1 Certification.  The Fire Department will also train jointly with Lake County 
Search and Rescue and other agencies on activities including, but not limited to, swift and 
ice water rescue, low angle rescue, lost and missing persons rescues, hazardous materials, 
and training set forth by the Office of Emergency Management. 
4.1.2 Vehicle Maintenance.  The County agrees to assist the City with any labor to 
perform general maintenance on certain Fire Department vehicles by City and County 
mechanics, and the City and the County shall agree to a standard, pre-arranged cost of such 
labor.  Routine maintenance that is not required to be done by an Emergency Vehicle 
Technician will be performed by City or County mechanics in a timely manner.  
Emergency repairs will be handled at the discretion of the Fire Chief.  Any non- routine 
maintenance work on fire engines shall be completed only by mechanics that have obtained 
the Emergency Vehicle Technician Certification and shall be completed to meet or exceed 
NFPA 1901 Standards.  If or when the City or County has a mechanic with Emergency 
Vehicle Technician Certification, then maintenance and repairs by such certified mechanic 
will be considered. 
 
4.2 Leadville Station.  The City is the fee owner of the Leadville  Station property.  If 
the City sells or conveys legal title to the Leadville Station to any third party for any use other than 
providing fire protection to the City and County, the net proceeds (exclusive of transactional costs 
and fees) from the sale of the Leadville Station property shall be split 50/50 with the City and 
County, and such proceeds shall be used toward future fire protection costs or as otherwise agreed 
to by the Parties.  If the Leadville Station property is conveyed to a fire district es tablished under 
Title 32, Colorado Revised Statutes, then this provision shall not apply; it being the intent of the 
Parties that all fire protection facilities and equipment owned by the City and/or the County will 
be conveyed to such fire district at no cost. 
4.3 Fire Station II.  The County is the fee  owner of the Fire Station II property.  If the 
County sells or conveys legal title to the Fire Station II to any third party for any use other than 
providing fire protection to the City and County, the net proceeds (exclusive of transactional costs 
and fees) from the sale of Fire Station II property, shall be split 50/50 with the City and County, 
and such proceeds shall be used toward future fire protection costs or as otherwise agreed to by 
the Parties.  If the Fire Station II property is conveyed to a fi re district established under Title 32, 
Colorado Revised Statutes, then this provision shall not apply; it being the intent of the Parties that 
all fire protection facilities and equipment owned by the City and/or the County will be conveyed 
to such fire district at no cost. 
4.4 Any Future Stations.  Decisions about funding, location, design and construction of 
future stations will be subject to future negotiations and agreement between the City and County. 
Additional fire stations will be located, designed, constructed, and acqui red through each Party's 
capital budgeting and planning process, and will be operated under the terms of this Agreement, 
as may be modified between the Parties. 
4.5 International Fire Code.  The Fire Chief is authorized to appoint a Fire Marshal or 
similar position to implement a prevention and inspection program and enforce the International 
Fire Code, as amended, and adopted by the Parties’ respective governing bodi es, within the 
incorporated and unincorporated areas of Lake County.  Permit and inspection fees collected 
through the prevention and inspection program shall be distributed to a designated revenue account 
within the City Fire Department budget.  The Fire Marshal may use these funds for professional 
development courses as authorized to do so by the Fire Chief.   
4.6 Liability for Expenses.  To the extent permitted by law, the City waives all rights 
to collect expenses from private property owners within unincorporated areas of Lake County as 
may be allowed pursuant to C.R.S. § 30- 10-513.5.  However, neither the City nor the County 
waives the right to collect expenses or any other remuneration from any source as may be provided 
by any other statute, contract or common law. 
4.7 Management Board.  The Management Board consisting of the Mayor of 
Leadville, the City Administrator, one Lake County Commissioner, the Lake County Manager, 
and a member chosen at large by consensus of the four other members (the “Management Board”) 
shall be responsible for advisory guidance of the Fire Department through communication with 
the Parties’ respective governing bodies.  Management Board duties include without limitation 
review and approval of operational policies and new contracts in accordance with City and 
County procurement policies, and providing direction to the Fire Chief on emergent issues that 
 
affect budget and staff. The Management Board shall make recommendations to the City and 
County for long range planning including without limitation one- and five-year plans, plans for a 
potential future fire protection district, future additional stations , and equipment needs.  The 
Management Board shall submit a Preliminary Fire Budget, which shall include both operating 
and capital budgets, to the City Council and the Board of County Commissioners according to 
the schedule and Section 5.  The Management Board shall review any changes to pay structure 
and compensation, including review of proposed changes to the union contract.  The Management 
Board shall make decisions by majority vote.  The City retains the exclusive right and 
responsibility to hire, dis cipline, fire, assign or otherwise manage Fire Department employees, 
including the Fire Chief.  Day- to-day management of the Fire Chief, consistent with decisions 
made by the Management Board and the approved Budget (as determined under Section 5), will 
be conducted by the City Administrator, or in the event of that position being vacant or 
eliminated, by the Mayor.  The Fire Chief shall be responsible for making recommendations to 
the Management Board, including without limitation annual capital and operational budget, five 
year plans, plans for a fire protection district if requested by the Management Board, future or 
additional stations, and other items as requested by the Management Board. 
5.0 FUNDING PROCEDURES 
5.1 Budget Process.  The Management Board shall meet as necessary, but at least 
quarterly, and shall work in good faith to review, revise, refine and finalize a preliminary Joint 
Fire Budget, which shall include an Operational Budget and Capital Expenditures Fund (as set 
forth in subsections 5.1.1 and 5.1.2).  The Management Board shall submit the preliminary Joint 
Fire Budget to the governing bodies of each Party for consideration on a timeline and in a format 
requested by each Party, and each Party shall work cooperatively with the Fire Chief to assist with 
preparation of budget material in their desired formats.   
 
The Fire Chief, and Management Board if desired, shall attend at least one work session each with 
the City Council and Board of County Commissioners each calendar year to assess year -to-date 
expenditures against the current year budget and identify early in each Party’s budget process any 
emerging issues that have significant budget implications.   At a minimum, the Fire Chief shall 
discuss with each governing body any material funding increases, if any, regarding the collective 
bargaining agreement between the City and The Leadville Union Paid Firefighters (the "Union 
Agreement").   
 
As part of their respective budgets, the governing body of each Party shall approve the Joint Fire 
Budget and appropriations for the same for the next budget year, subject to and in compliance with 
the Local Government Budget Law of Colorado (C.R.S. § 29-1-101, et seq.). 
 
5.1.1 Operational Budget.  The Joint Fire Budget shall include a budget for operational 
expenses.  Operational expenses shall be apportioned as follows: the County’s 
proportionate share equals 70% of the budgeted expenditures and the City's proportionate 
share equals 30% of budgeted expenditures of the Fire Department in any given year (the 
“Operational Budget”).  The Management Board will inform their respective governing 
bodies of unexpected expenses on a monthly basis, thus ensuring both Parties are aware of 
any potential overages.  Previously unbudgeted expenses will then be split 70/30 as all 
other operational expenses.  If supplemental appropriation of funds is necessary, the 
 
Management Board shall initiate supplemental appropriation requests for consideration 
and approval by the Parties' respective governing bodies in accordance with C.R.S. § 29-
1-109. 
5.1.2 Capital Expenditures Fund.  The Joint Fire Budget shall include a fund for capital 
expenditures that is consistent with the priorities outlined in a capital apparatus and 
equipment worksheet, prepared and updated at least annually by the Fire Chief and 
reviewed by the Management Board (the " Capital Expenditure Fund").  The Capital 
Expenditures Fund shall be sufficient for approved capital expenditures in the budget year 
while also containing additional funds to be held for anticipated expenditures in future 
years, the intent being to accumulate in this fund monies s ufficient to avoid emergency 
appropriations or large single-year appropriations.  The Capital Expenditure Fund shall be 
apportioned as follows: the County's proportionate share equals 70% of the budgeted 
expenditures and the City's proportionate share equa ls 30% of budgeted expenditures of 
the Fire Department in any given year.   
5.1.3 Fire Fund.  The Fire Fund shall exist for any monies collected by the Fire 
Department specifically for wildfire fighting or aviation standby services and other contract 
services performed by the Fire Department (the "Contract Services").  This revenue shall 
first be used by the Fire Department to pay for employee wages, training, and other costs 
directly associated with providing Contract Services.  Thereafter, up to 30% of the 
remaining Contract Services revenues from Contract Services may be allocated by the Fire 
Chief to programs identified in the Fire Department's supplemental program budget for the 
following year.  The balance of the remaining Contract Services revenues shall be held in 
the Fire Fund and may be allocated to the Capital Expenditure Fund. 
5.2 Impasse.  If an agreement as to the Joint Fire Budget cannot be reached by 
December 1 for the next budget year, the Joint Fire Budget for the next budget year shall default 
to the prior year's Joint Fire Budget plus an adjustment for inflation equal to the prior year's annual 
percentage change in the Consumer Price Index for the Denver -Boulder area (using the first half 
year estimate) or 5.5%, whichever is less. Sale of Capital assets shall be deposited into the Fire 
Fund. The Fire Management Board will advise on allocation of the Fire Fund. 
5.3 Payment.  The City shall issue, based on a calendar year, monthly invoices to the 
County for the County's proportionate share of the Joint Fire Budget.  The County shall pay in full 
such monthly amounts within 30 days of receipt of the invoice, unless the County protests an 
invoice in writing within the 30- day period.  In the event of such a protest, the disputed amount 
shall be subject to the dispute resolution procedures found in Section 8.0 of this Agreement and 
the undisputed amount shall be paid. 
The City shall maintain all financial records related to the Fire Department in accordance with 
C.R.S. § 29-1-114 and shall make such records available for County inspection upon request. 
5.4 Adjustment for Actual Joint Fire Budget Costs.  As part of the City's annual audit 
process, the City shall cause an audit to be performed of the Joint Fire Budget.  The audit shall be 
conducted according to generally accepted accounting principles for governmental entit ies.  A 
copy of that portion of the City's audit including the audit work papers, pertaining to the Joint Fire 
 
Budget shall be furnished to the County.  Based on the City's annual audit, the Parties agree to 
negotiate any needed adjustments in the Joint Fire Budget. 
The County may, upon reasonable notice, cause an independent audit or performance review of 
the Fire Department’s use of County funds once per fiscal year.  Any such audit or review shall be 
at the County's expense. 
5.5 Timing of the Union Agreement.  The City shall not modify the Union Agreement, 
or enter into or modify any other collective bargaining agreement with The Leadville Union Paid 
Firefighters, in a way that materially increases the County’s financial obligations under this 
Agreement until the Joint Fire Budget has been agreed to by the Parties for the upcoming fiscal 
year; provided, however, that nothing contained herein shall be construed as limiting the City's 
ability to negotiate the Union Agreement  or any other collective bargaining agreement with The 
Leadville Union Paid Firefighters.

6.2 The Fire Chief shall provide the BOCC and City Council with a quarterly brief on 
the status of the Fire Department at the second regularly scheduled monthly BOCC and council 
meetings following the end of each quarter. 
6.3 Quarterly Service and Activity Reports.  In satisfaction of the Quarterly Reports 
deadlines set forth in Section 6.1, the Fire Chief shall prepare and deliver to both the City Council 
and the Board of County Commissioners, no later than January 1, April 1, July 1, and October 1 
of each year, a written report detailing Fire Department operations and activity levels. The specific 
 
data elements, categories, and metrics required in each Quarterly Report shall be governed by the 
Fire Department Reporting Policy adopted and maintained pursuant to Section 6.8, and shall at 
minimum address call volume and service area breakdown by jurisdiction, incident classification, 
response time and staffing, labor hours and resource allocation, and training, prevention, and 
inspection activity. 
6.4 Annual Performance Report.  No later than March 31 following the end of each 
fiscal year, the Fire Chief shall provide an Annual Performance Report to both governing bodies, 
consolidating the data submitted in the preceding Quarterly Reports. The content and format of 
the Annual Perf ormance Report shall likewise be governed by the Fire Department Reporting 
Policy and shall at minimum include annual service call trends by jurisdiction, a narrative summary 
of major incidents and interagency coordination, and recommendations for operational 
improvements, staffing, or capital planning for the upcoming year. 
6.5 Strategic Planning.  Consistent with the Strategic Plans deadline set forth in Section 
6.1, the Management Board, in consultation with the Fire Chief, shall develop and maintain 
strategic plans for Fire Department operations, staffing, and capital needs, including one -year 
operational goals and a five -year capital improvement and staffing plan. These plans shall be 
reviewed and updated annually and presented to the City Council and the Board of County 
Commissioners as part of the Joint Fire Budget process. 
6.6 Format and Data Access.   All reports required under this Section 6.0 shall be 
submitted in a mutually agreed -upon format that allows for data sorting and analysis. Upon 
request, the County shall be granted reasonable access to the underlying call data and NFIRS or 
CAD (Computer-Aided Dispatch) reports used to generate the summaries. 
6.7 Failure to Report.  If any report required under Section 6.1 is not provided in a 
timely or complete manner, the Management Board shall notify the responsible Party in writing. 
If a deficiency remains uncured more than 30 days after the original written notice, the 
Management Board may withhold or defer action on the Preliminary Joint Fire Budget, or any 
requested funding increase, until the deficiency is cured, and shall report the uncured deficiency 
to the City Council and Board of County Commissioners ; provided, however, that any failure to 
provide any report required under Section 6.1 shall not be construed as a breach of this Agreement. 
6.8 Fire Department Reporting Policy. The Management Board shall adopt, and may 
from time to time amend by majority vote, a Fire Department Reporting Policy establishing the 
specific data elements, formats, and financial variance thresholds required for the reports described 
in Sections 6.3 and 6.4. The Reporting Policy is incorporated herein by reference for informational 
purposes but is not a term of this Agreement, and amendment of the Reporting Policy shall not 
require amendment of this Agreement; provided, however, that no amendment to the Reporting 
Policy shall (a) alter the  reporting deadlines fixed in Section 6.1, or (b) eliminate the financial 
summary or labor -hours/resource-allocation components described in Section 6.3 without the 
written consent of both Parties. 
 
7.0 DEFAULT.  If either Party materially defaults in the performance of any of the material 
covenants or agreements to be kept, done or performed by it under the terms of this Agreement, 
the non-defaulting Party shall notify the defaulting Party in writing of the nature of such default.  
Within ten days following receipt of such notice the defaulting Party shall correct such default or, 
in the event of a default not capable of being corrected within the ten days, the defaulting Party 
shall inform the non- defaulting party of such and shall take reasonable steps to commence 
correcting the default within ten days of receipt of notification thereof, thereafter completing 
correction of the default with due diligence.  If the defaulting Party fails to correct the default as 
provided in this Agreement, the non -defaulting Party, without further notice, shall have the right 
to declare that this Agreement is terminated effective upon such date as the non- defaulting Party 
shall designate and the non-defaulting Party shall have the right to recover such damages as it shall 
be entitled to by law or as provided in the Agreement.  The rights and remedies provided for in 
this Agreement may be exercised singly or in combination.  
8.0 DISPUTE RESOLUTION.  The Parties agree that it is in the best interest of both Parties 
to attempt to resolve disputes that arise under this Agreement in an efficient and inexpensive 
manner.  To that end, the Parties commit to use their best efforts to atte mpt to resolve disputes 
informally.  For disputes that arise under this Agreement as to any action, decision or performance 
of either Party, the Parties promptly through their designated representatives from the Management 
Board shall begin negotiations wi th one another in good faith in order to reach resolution of the 
dispute.  If the Parties representatives from the Management Board cannot agree to a resolution of 
the dispute within fourteen days after commencement of negotiations, written notice of the dispute 
(the "Dispute Notice") shall be delivered within 24-hours after such 14-day period by each of the 
Parties' Management Board representatives to each of the Parties' respective legislative bodies.  
Within 30 business days after receipt of the Dispute Notice, the Parties, through their respective 
legislative bodies, shall determine whether other alternative dispute resolutions, such as mediation 
or arbitration, may be appropriate.  If the Parties are unable to agree on the manner in which to 
resolve the dispute, either Party may pursue its rights or remedies in accordance with Section 7.0 
of this Agreement. 
9.0 TERMINATION.  In addition to termination of this Agreement pursuant to Sections 3 and 
7 of this Agreement, and without limitation upon any rights and remedies that may be available to 
a non-defaulting Party, the following apply: 
9.0.1 If the Leadville Station ceases to be used for fire protection purposes, disposition 
of the fire station shall be in accordance with Section 4.2 of this Agreement. 
9.0.2 If the Fire Station II ceases to be used for fire protection purposes, disposition of 
the fire station shall be in accordance with Section 4.3 of this Agreement. 
9.0.3 If a special fire protection district is formed pursuant to C.R.S. § 32-1-101, et seq., 
and the district's service plan includes the use of the Leadville Station, Fire Station II, 
and/or any other acquired or developed fire stations, neither Party shall transfer or dispose 
of its interests, if any, in any station, fire equipment or fire pr otection vehicles except to 
 
the fire protection district, subject to and in compliance with applicable State and local 
laws and regulations. 
9.0.4 In the event of termination, the Parties shall work toward an equitable resolution 
based on the principle of the 70- 30 (County/City) split of operating expenses of accrued 
and vested pension obligations existing at the date of termination in conformance wi th 
applicable law. 
10.0 ATTORNEYS' FEES.  If any action is brought in a court of law by either Party to this 
Agreement concerning the enforcement, interpretation or construction of this Agreement, the 
prevailing Party, either at trial or upon appeal, shall be entitled to reasonable attorneys' fees and 
costs, including without limitation expert witness fees, incurred in the prosecution or defense of 
such action.

12.0 GOVERNMENTAL IMMUNITY.  The Parties understand and agree that each of the 
Parties is relying on and does not waive or intend to waive by any provision of this Agreement, 
the monetary limitations or any other rights, immunities, and protections provided by the Colorado 
Governmental Immunity Act, C.R.S. § 24- 10-101, et seq., as amended, or otherwise available to

of this Agreement and shall be enforceable until all claims are precluded by statutes of limitation. 
13.0 WAIVER.  The failure of either Party to exercise any of its rights under this Agreement 
shall not be a waiver of those rights.  A Party waives only those rights specified in writing and 
signed by the Party waiving its rights. 
14.0 APPLICABLE LAW.  This Agreement shall be interpreted in all respects in accordance 
with the laws of the State of Colorado. 
 
15.0 ENTIRE AGREEMENT.  This Agreement constitutes the entire Agreement and 
understanding between the Parties.  Without limiting the generality of the foregoing, this 
Agreement specifically supersedes the Cooperative Fire Protection Agreement between the Parties 
dated as of January 2003.  Notwithstanding any provision of this Agreement to the contrary, 
nothing in this Agreement supersedes the Union Agreement.  The Parties' intent is that any Union 
Agreement in which the City is a party would not conflict with th is Agreement.  To the extent 
practical, this Agreement and the Union Agreement shall be construed in harmony with each other.  
To the extent there is a conflict between the City's obligations under this Agreement and the Union 
Agreement, the Union Agreemen t shall prevail.  Consequently, any non- arbitrary or non-
capricious action taken by the City without intent to evade its obligations under this Agreement, 
which the City determines in its reasonable discretion to be necessary for it to abide by its 
obligations under the Union Agreement, shall not constitute a breach of this Agreement.  Nothing 
in this Agreement shall obligate the City to continue to recognize any collective bargaining 
representative or agent, nor shall anything in this Agreement obligate the City to enter into any 
future agreement with The Leadville Union Paid Firefighters or any other union.  
 
16.0 MODIFICATION.  This Agreement may be modified or amended only by a duly 
authorized written instrument executed by the Parties.

the application thereof shall not in any way be affected or impaired thereby.

no way limit or define the interpretation to be placed upon this Agreement. 
20.0 BINDING EFFECT.  This Agreement shall be binding upon execution, and shall

It is the express intention of the Parties that any person or entity other than the Parties shall be 
deemed to be an incidental beneficiary only.
