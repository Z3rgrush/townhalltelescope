i. Awnings, Canopies, and Marquees 
1) Awnings and canopies shall be canvas with a matte finish.  Awnings with high gloss 
finishes are prohibited.  Marquees may be constructed in metal but shall be 
painted or shall have a matte finish. 
2) Rigid frame awnings and canopies may be used but shall stop at the top section 
and shall not be included in the valance. 
3) Illuminated plastic awnings and canopies are prohibited 
4) Awning and canopy colors shall be compatible with the overall color scheme of the 
facade.  Solid colors or subtle striped patterns shall be used. 
5) Awnings and canopies for rectangular openings shall be simple shed shapes.  
Rounded or bull-nose awnings and canopies shall only be allowed over arched 
openings. 
6) Marquees shall be located only over the primary public entrance into a building. 
7) Awnings, canopies, or marquees that span continuously across more than one (1) 
structural bay or storefront shall not be allowed. 
8) Poles or posts supported awnings, canopies, or marquees shall not be allowed 
9) Awnings, canopies, or marquees shall not project more than five (5) feet from the 
building facade to which they are attached. 
10) Awnings, canopies, or marquees shall maintain an eight-foot height clearance 
above a public sidewalk or other public owned space.  Additional height clearance 
may be required if an awning, canopy, or marquee is located over a public right-of-
way, including an alley way. 
 
The existing structure and the proposed replacement are both best classified as awnings 
because they are shelter devices composed of a rigid framework covered by another 
material.  They lack the support poles that would characterize a canopy, and the 
framework does not structurally resemble a roof as you would see with a marquee.  For 
this reason, the proposed awning, which is to be rounded instead of a shed shape, metal 
instead of canvas, and span multiple storefronts requires three design variances.

provides a timeless look that works with different architectural styles.  Delta Metals uses

070.060.060 Subdivision Procedures. 
(a) Minor Subdivision. 
(1) Purpose. The minor subdivision procedure is used to evaluate proposed subdivisions that will create 
four or fewer lots or involve minimal adjustments to approved final plats. The minor subdivision 
procedure also provides an administrative process to correct plat errors and adjust property 
boundaries.   
(2) Applicability. 
a. The minor subdivision procedure shall apply to:  
1. Properties within an previously approved preliminarysubdivision plat;  
2. Subdivisions creating three (3) four (4) or fewer lots or ten (10) or fewer condominium 
units within a single plat; and  
3. Condominiumizations within an existing building that do not include land dedication .  
.4.  Legally created properties having a metes and bounds description. 
b. The following are eligible to be processed as a minor subdivision, provided that Subparagraph a. is 
met: 
15. Consolidation of two (2) or more lots into a single lot on a single previously recorded final 
plat; or  
26. Boundary or lot line adjustments to an approved final plat; or 
37. Correction of errors on an approved final and recorded plat, or. 
8. Any other subdivision not considered a Preliminary or Final Plat. 
bc. Any subdivision requiring dedication of right-of-way, other public tracts, or public improvements 
to the City shall be submitted to the City Council for acceptance or denial of the dedication.
