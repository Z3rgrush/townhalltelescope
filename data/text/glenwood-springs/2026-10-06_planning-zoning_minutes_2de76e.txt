3.  New Items 
a.  SUB-000115-2026: Preliminary Plat Application for 51993 Hwy 6 & 24 
Presenter: Senior Planner Watkins Fulk-Gray.   
Senior Planner Watkins Fulk-Gray presented the preliminary plat application for the 
Canyon Vista affordable‑housing project, an 80‑unit multifamily development previously 
approved through the site/architectural plan process. The purpose of the plat is to 
consolidate three unplatted, commonly owned parcels currently described by 
metes‑and‑bounds into a single platted lot. The Commission must make a 
recommendation to City Council, and the application must meet seven required approval 
criteria, all of which staff finds satisfied. Six conditions of approval are recommended, and 
no public comments were received. 
 
Key points Included:  
• Canyon Vista is an 80‑unit affordable multifamily project on Highway 6, previously 
approved about a year ago. 
 
Page 2 of 3 
 
• Site consists of approximately 4 acres between Highway 6 and Donegan Road; 
zoned M1 Mixed-use Corridor. 
• The land is an unplatted lot having a metes-and-bounds legal description; 
preliminary plat is required to consolidate the parcels into one lot. 
• Preliminary plat requires a Planning and Zoning Commission recommendation; final 
plat will be administrative once conditions are met. 
• Staff finds the project meets all seven preliminary‑plat approval criteria, including 
consistency with the Comprehensive Plan and zoning standards. 
• Infrastructure: Water and sewer systems are available adjacent to the site; utility 
will‑serve letters provided. 
• Natural hazards: Only hydrocompactive soils identified; addressed through building 
design. 
• Public Works requested a condition relating to fire‑hydrant access; no public 
comments received. 
• Six conditions of approval are recommended; Commission may recommend 
approval, approval with conditions, or denial. 
Commission Questions & Discussion: 
• Commissioner Jim Frale: What are the required features that were missing on the 
retaining walls?  
o Staff response: The project must meet detailed visual design requirements 
for retaining walls, including offsets and landscaping. Most requirements 
were met, but some walls could not fully comply. City Council approved 
those exceptions using flexibility allowed for affordable‑housing projects, 
and overall the project was determined to meet the standards. 
• Commissioner Jim Frale: What sort of utilities will the City be providing to these 
lots? 
o Staff response: We will be providing water and electric. That area is part of 
the West Glenwood Sanitation District for sewer.  
 
Applicant Presentation 
Presenter: Kyle Ervin Cohen Esrey Development Group 
Kyle Ervin of Cohen Esrey Development Group explained that the development team has 
held the site under contract since 2022 and has been securing low‑income housing 
tax‑credit financing for the 80‑unit affordable housing project. Although funding delays 
occurred late last year, the project is now positioned to move forward. He explained that 
the preliminary plat is needed to consolidate the existing parcels and establish easements 
required for utilities, long‑term site maintenance, and a planned pedestrian stair 
connection between Donegan Road and the project site. The plat also formalizes a 
shared‑use area with the adjacent Elks property. He described the plat as primarily a 
technical step to clear the way for the housing development. 
Commission Questions & Discussion:

• Commissioner Jim Frale: Can you clarify if our actions here today are purely 
procedural or if they relate at all to monumentation? 
o Staff response: The Commission’s action concerns only the preliminary 
plat. Physical monumentation is a technical step managed by the project 
surveyor and will occur after City Council approval, alongside other technical 
revisions. City staff including Community Development and Public Works 
reviews and confirms compliance. Staff noted that preliminary plats require 
public hearings, but most technical details are handled administratively. 
 
Public Comment:  
• Rob Anderson 1405 Donegan: Mr. Anderson, a nearby resident on Donegan Road, 
expressed opposition to having an 80‑unit low‑income housing development 
adjacent to his home. He stated he has lived in the area for 30 years and is unhappy 
with the project. He noted he will bring further questions to City Council and 
expects to return for future discussions.
