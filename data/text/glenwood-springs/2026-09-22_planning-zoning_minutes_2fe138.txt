4.  Old Items 
a.  CDA-000049-2026 Code Amendment Short Term Rental and Accessory Tourist 
Rental 
Presenter: Senior Planner Emery Ellingson.   
Senior Planner Emery Ellingson presented a continuation of the proposed code 
amendment (CDA‑000049‑2026) concerning short‑term rentals (STRs) and accessory 
tourist rentals (ATRs). The amendment aims to clarify renewal requirements; specifically, 
that permit holders must rent at least once and remit lodging tax during any given permit 
period to renew. Ellingson also provided follow‑up information requested by the Planning & 
Zoning Commission, including data on STR activity, comparisons to other communities, fee 
structures, occupancy rules, compliance trends, and how other municipalities regulate 
rentals. 
 
Key points Included:  
• Clarification on permit requirements for renewal. 
o That permit holders must rent at least one night and remit lodging tax greater 
than zero dollars in order to renew STR or accessory tourist rental permits. 
• Glenwood Springs currently has 126 STRs and 11 ATRs. 210 is the cap on STR’s 
which is based on 5% of housing units in the city and there is no cap on ATRs. 
o For all of the STRs and ATRs in the renewal cycle there were 47 police or 
emergency responses. Most of these responses were for noise complaints, 
trash, and parking with zero permits being revoked. 
• Comparisons to similar communities including Steamboat Springs, Durango, 
Salida, Breckenridge, Crested Butte, Grand Junction, and Carbondale were 
provided. 
• Commission Questions & Discussion 
• Commissioner Joy White: What was the reason for the continuation? 
o Staff response: There was a recent appeal that went to Planning and Zoning 
and then City Council that was ultimately not renewed. The reason they were 
not renewed was because they did not collect any accommodation tax. In 
our code we could make this requirement clearer and as a curtesy we 
decided to wait until a final decision was made on their appeal. 
• Commissioner Joy White: Can you clarify the minimum night requirement 
stipulation? 
o Staff response: I am not sure about the specific talk that led to the exact 
number. However, a lot of thought was given to whom they were trying to 
incentivize and in which area. A higher minimum number of days was given 
where commerce is encouraged and a maximum number of days given 
where primary ownership is encouraged.  
• Commissioner Connie Geiman: Besides the minimum nights listed in Crested 
Butte did any other jurisdictions have a minimum number of nights?

o Staff response: Not that I could find through my research and direct 
outreach to these communities.  
• Commissioner Connie Geiman: While STRs are not necessarily bad, I don’t believe 
we need a cap on nights if we can establish limitations in other ways that promote 
housing. 
• Commissioner Amy Connerton: Are there any administrative costs with a 
stipulation that includes a number of nights? 
o Staff response: Adding a minimum number of nights would only require the 
collection of different data. Companies like VRBO and Airbnb already create

• Commissioner Amy Connerton: Is there a reason that we do not have annual 
renewals? 
o Staff response: Administratively these permits take a toll on the entire 
system, requiring a lot of staff time and inspections. Changing renewals to 
annually would mostly just increase workload and cost without offering 
much else in return.  
• Commissioner Kyle Jones: During the original discussion on short term rentals 
what did the city decide? Are we trying to encourage or discourage the use of these 
permits? 
o Staff response: I was not here for the original discussion on STRs and the 
City’s goals. However, the overall goal appears to be to encourage ownership, 
housing, and affordable alternatives to traditional rentals. The 250’ buffer 
sets a good balance of location and dispersion throughout the community 
while keeping the number of rentals at a reasonable number. While our 
requirements are rather inclusive, allowing for many different levels of use.  
• Alternate Jim Frale: Is the proposed amendment of at least one night and greater 
than zero dollars just a starting point? 
o Staff response: That is correct, whatever number you guys decide on will 
establish the new minimum requirement; and whatever requirement is 
decided on will always have some level of extenuating circumstance that can 
arise. 
• Commissioner Joy White: Did any other community have buffer zones? 
o Staff response: No, most are regulated through established zones like our

• Commissioner Amy Connerton: I believe that the intent of the code and

• Commissioner Connie Geiman: I don’t believe that the intent of the code is to

• Commissioner John Houghton: Does the 126 out of 210 permit number include the

5. New Items: 
a. Work Session: Economic and Community Development Year in Review 
Presenter: Community Development Director Trent Hyatt  
 
Community Development Director Trent Hyatt provided an overview of his first year back 
with the City and outlined the department’s operations, improvements, and ongoing 
initiatives. He explained the broad responsibilities of the Community Development 
Department, strategic changes made to improve customer service, process efficiency, 
training, technology use, succession planning, and public communication. He also 
presented data on building, planning, and enforcement activity, and discussed internal 
goals, permit turnaround timelines, staffing challenges, and the rationale behind the City's 
four‑day work schedule. The session served primarily as a transparent update on 
departmental performance and improvements, and an opportunity for commissioners to 
ask questions. 
Key Points: 
• The Community Development Department handles planning, development review, 
building permits and inspections, code enforcement, economic development 
support, and housing program management. 
• A strategic plan was created focusing on staff development, process improvements, 
and better use of technology. 
• Customer service improvements include 24‑hour response expectations, 
consistent front‑counter coverage, and a “planner of the day” for walk‑in questions. 
• Planners now act as project managers for building permits to ensure clear 
communication with applicants. 
• Staff development and succession planning are underway, including professional 
training and preparing internal candidates for future inspector roles. 
• Staff report formats were improved for clearer legal defensibility and consistent 
documentation. 
• Stakeholder outreach was conducted with builders, designers, and contractors to 
identify process issues and make targeted improvements. 
• Public feedback surveys were added to the permitting system to collect customer 
experience data. 
• Review processes emphasize identifying needed corrections early to reduce 
multiple rounds of comments. 
 
Page 7 of 8 
 
• Technology updates include improvements to Enterprise Permitting &Licensing 
permitting software and encouraging more efficient meeting and presentation 
practices. 
• Active applications and recently approved projects are now listed online; a new city 
website is planned for next year. 
• The department plans to increase public communication and encourage residents 
to seek guidance on permitting. 
• Building activity remains high: approximately 1,500 inspections last year and nearly 
1,100 so far this year; inspections are typically completed within 24 hours. 
• Internal review goals include two‑week turnaround for single‑family permits and 3–5 
days for mechanical, plumbing, and similar permits (excluding electrical, which is 
handled by the state). 
• Planning application volume remains strong, with 128 applications processed last 
year and 105 so far this year. 
• Neighborhood services opened 31 enforcement cases last year and 19 so far this 
year. 
• The City's four‑day work schedule was adopted primarily to support employee 
retention due to regional school schedules. 
• The front‑counter position sees turnover because it is an entry‑level role; 
succession planning is in place to train future replacements.
