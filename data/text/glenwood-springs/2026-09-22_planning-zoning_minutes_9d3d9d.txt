4.  New Items 
a.  L&E-000086-2026 Location and Extent Application and 1041-000085-2026 Matters of 
State Interest 1041 Application - Xcel Electric Transmission Line Rebuild  
Presenter: Long-Range Principal Planner Jim Hardcastle.   
Long‑Range Principal Planner Jim Hardcastle presented two related applications from Xcel 
Energy for the rebuild of an existing 69‑kiloVolt electric transmission line originally 
constructed in the 1940s and partially rebuilt in the 1960s. One application (L&E-000086-
2026) is here for commission approval, while the second (1041-000085-2026) requires a 
 
Page 2 of 7 
 
recommendation of approval to City Council. Hardcastle noted several staff‑report errors 
identified prior to the meeting, which were corrected in the presentation and will be 
addressed as necessary before City Council review. The project replaces aging poles and 
conductors, adds a lightning‑protection shield wire, meets modern design standards, and 
increases easement width to reduce wildfire risk. 
 
Key points Included:  
• 1.5-mile stretch of already existing Xcel Right-of-Way (ROW). 
• Will continue to be 69 Kilovolt or KV operation 
• New weathered‑steel monopoles reduce wildfire risk. 
• Average pole height increases from 42.5’ to roughly 80’. 
• 20 poles removed, 13 poles replaced, 6 via helicopter, 7 via bucket truck and crane. 
• Easement changes from 30’-50’ to a uniform 60’ easement.  
Commission Questions & Discussion: 
• Commissioner John Houghton: From my understanding a Location and Extent or 
L&E permit can be amended by the governing body that is applying. In the case of a 
quasi-public entity who does this authority fall onto? 
o Staff response: That authority would fall onto the board of directors. 
• Commissioner John Houghton: Is the proposed timeline and schedule just for the 
Glenwood Springs portion of the project? 
o Staff response: That is correct, and the applicant can give more details 
during their presentation on the timeline of the whole project later. 
• Commissioner Connie Geiman: If the final wording is not yet set why are we seeing 
the application today and not when the wording has been finalized? 
o Staff response: The application was deemed completed by staff. The 
changes that are being made are not changes to materials but purely 
administrative in nature to conditions. Such as changing multiple conditions 
into one, or wording on certain line items to be clearer.  
• Commissioner Patrick Corcoran: Will any land or property be taken by the city? 
o Staff response: The City is not taking any land. That might be a good 
question for the applicant since they possess the right of eminent domain 
same as us as a utility company in. 
• Commissioner Amy Connerton: Can you summarize what an L&E a 1041 are so we 
can better understand the decisions we are able to make today. 
o Staff response: A Location and Extent (L&E) permit is a state‑mandated 
process requiring government or quasi‑government agencies to notify and 
present the location and scope of a project within a jurisdiction. The Planning 
Commission makes the formal decision on the permit, but the initiating 
agency retains the authority to override that decision. 
o Staff response: A 1041 permit is a state‑authorized process allowing a city 
to review projects in “areas of state interest,” such as major utility

infrastructure. The statute requires applicants to meet specific criteria, 
which are evaluated through detailed findings. The 1041 process is primarily 
used to mitigate project impacts through conditions. The Planning 
Commission provides a recommendation, but City Council makes the final 
decision. 
 
Applicant Presentation 
Presenter: Cory Miller Xcel Energy Principal Agent, Sitting land rights. 
Corey Miller, representing Xcel Energy, presented the proposed rebuilding of a segment of 
the 6584 transmission line within Glenwood Springs. The project is part of Xcel’s 
wildfire‑mitigation effort to replace aging wood poles with modern steel monopoles, 
upgrade safety standards, and reduce infrastructure vulnerability. The Glenwood Springs 
portion includes approximately 1.5 miles of line, temporary construction needs, a 
laydown/landing yard, and both ground‑based and helicopter pole installation. Xcel has 
already secured required permits from other jurisdictions and seeks the Planning 
Commission’s approval of the Location & Extent permit and recommendation of approval 
for the 1041 permit. 
 
Key Points Included: 
Xcel Energy seeks approval for L&E permit and recommendation of approval for the 1041 
permit for the Glenwood Springs portion of the 6584 line. 
• Total line is 25 miles; Glenwood segment is roughly 1.5 miles from the west city 
boundary to Mitchell Creek Substation. 
• Project purpose: wildfire‑mitigation upgrade replacing decades‑old wood poles with 
steel monopoles, updating conductor clearance and safety standards. 
• Existing 69‑KV operation will remain 69‑KV after the rebuild. 
• Other jurisdictions (Rifle, Newcastle, Garfield County) have already issued required 
land‑use permits, and construction is underway west of Glenwood. 
• Glenwood portion planned for late 2027; site restoration expected to continue into 
2028. 
• Pole count will decrease from 19 existing poles to 13 new poles. 
• Seven poles require helicopter installation due to steep terrain; six poles will be 
installed using traditional ground construction. 
• Temporary facilities needed: construction easements and one laydown/landing yard 
(Cardell Yard). 
• Line will use steel monopoles (some with foundations), updated conductor sag 
requirements, and lightning‑protection static line. 
• Right‑of‑way widths increased where needed to meet safety standards and allow 
vegetation management. 
• Xcel held two community meetings in Glenwood Springs (Dec 2023, Feb 2024) and 
maintains public communication channels.

Commission Questions & Discussion: 
• Commissioner John Houghton: Can you explain why the project anticipates 
helicopter operations exceeding Glenwood Springs’ allowed construction hours? 
o Cory Miller: Construction likes to ask for the largest amount of time for 
flexibility. However, I can confirm that they will not fly helicopters outside of 
daylight hours and we are willing to abide by the City’s construction hours. 
The number of poles in the city is seven, and they should not take very long to 
complete.  
• Commissioner Amy Connerton: Your application stated that not all locations have 
been sampled for soils analysis. How will this issue be handled before foundation 
construction? 
o Cory Miller: That is a process issue. Without an approved application and 
plan we cannot get onto these properties for analysis.  
• Commissioner Amy Connerton: Your plans state that the odds of encountering the 
geothermal aquafer as low and contingency plans are incorporated. Who will be 
reviewing these plans to ensure this doesn’t happen?  
o Cory Miller: There will be final plans review and several preconstruction 
meetings as well as right-of-way permits, and grading permits for each 
location. There is also a condition of approval for the permit that involves if 
water is encountered a plan to be coordinated. This plan would then have to 
be approved by City staff and the Public Works Director. 
• Commissioner Amy Connerton: What is the additional amount of vegetation 
easement that will be required for each site?  
o Cory Miller: Anything above 10 to 12 feet near the lines must be cleared out. 
Anything else that is cleared out would be part of a post 1041 reclamation 
plan that would look at each site, and the technical details of what 
reclamation would look like.  
• Commissioner Amy Connerton: Will operations be similar to the laydown yard 
project near transfer trail? 
o Cory Miller: Yes, helicopter operations would be similar to that project in 
operation. However, there would be much less need for evacuations since 
there will be few if any habitable structures.  
• Alternate Jim Frale: what does coordination for the helicopter look like with other 
helicopters in the area?  
o Cory Miller: That is regulated through an FAA (Federal Aviation 
Administration) permit that is submitted by the helicopter operator prior to 
construction. On a similar project, from Rifle to De Beque, we had to 
coordinate with the hospital on operations but that is also a part of the FAA 
permitting.  
• Alternate Jim Frale: What does the circle around the poles represent in the 
application? 
 
Page 5 of 7 
 
o Cory Miller: That represents a 300-foot buffer. Anything inhabited within that 
buffer would have to be evacuated while the pole is suspended from 
helicopter. 
• Alternate Jim Frale: In past meetings with Planning and Zoning upgrading the line 
from 69KV to 115KV was discussed. What might trigger that upgrade? 
o Cory Miller: We have planners that flag that sort of improvement off growth 
and use. To my knowledge there has been no reason to believe that an 
improvement of that caliber is projected any time soon. The exact 
configuration of an upgrade to 115KV would requires various improvements 
to poles and substantial improvements to the substations.  
• Chair Peter Waller: How does this transmission line fit into the larger grid? 
o Cory Miller:  This transmission line (6584) runs down to Rifle and then 
another circuit (6670) runs down to De Beque where it branches in lines that 
lead to Fruita, Grand Junction, and the San Luis Valley.  
• Chair Peter Waller: What sort of lessons did you learn or feedback had you gotten 
from the public since the 2024 project? 
o Cory Miller: Some of the feedback we received was related to not giving 
enough notice before evacuations, potentially over saturating residents with 
notices and alerts. Along with that making sure procedures are understood 
and communication is clear with the public.  
• Chair Peter Waller: Have you received any negative feedback from the public on 
easements? 
o Cory Miller: No, we have not. What helps is that a large portion of this project 
is located on City owned property and not Privately owned.  
• Commissioner Amy Connerton: The CPW (Colorado Parks and Wildlife) gave you a 
large list in 2022. Will these items be revisited with the CPW for accuracy before 
construction?  
o Cory Miller: That is correct we have coordinated with CPW a lot on these 
applications. The typical procedure is they give us a large list of what’s known 
in the area during planning and then prior to construction they indicate any 
sensitive wildlife and what to look out for. One of the biggest is that if larger 
amounts of wildlife are present, we get someone from CPW present to 
monitor them who also has the authority to shut down construction.  
• Alternate Jim Frale: Do you feel your team understand how seriously the city takes 
any work near the Geothermal layer? 
o Cory Miller: Our engineers are professionally accredited and take this very 
seriously and have been working directly with the Public Works Director. This 
specific engineer has worked on many projects in this area for several years.  
• Commissioner Kyle Jones: In the report it states that these improvements would 
increase reliability. Since there are less poles would this also reduce down times in 
the event of an outage?

o Cory Miller: That is correct after an outage we have to inspect every pole 
before we can reenergize the line. These being modern poles would also take
