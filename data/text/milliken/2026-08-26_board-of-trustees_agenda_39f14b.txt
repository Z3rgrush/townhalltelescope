  b. WES DJ Gathering, LLC Pipeline Removal Agreement 
Jon Rabas , Streets and Facilities Directors  
Jon Rabas, Streets and Facilities Director, requested the Board of Trustees’ consideration of a 
Pipeline Removal Agreement with WES DJ Gathering, LLC related to the Ehrlich Lake 
Drainage Improvements Project and authorization for the Mayor to execute the agreement in 
the amount of $35,000. Mr. Rabas explained that the elevation of an existing abandoned WES 
DJ Gathering pipeline conflicts with the proposed 48-inch and 36-inch storm drainage 
improvements and does not provide the necessary separation. Lowering the existing pipeline 
was considered but would result in the pipeline being located below the water elevation at 
Ehrlich Lake. 
 
Trustee Wakeman asked Mr. Rabas to explain the location of the improvements and whether 
Town staff could perform the pipeline removal work. Mr. Rabas described the improvements 
on the south side of Ehrlich Lake, including the 36-inch storm drain entering the lake and the 
associated drainage infrastructure near Hillsborough. He explained that completing the work in-
house would require the Town to rent a large excavator and shoring equipment and purchase 
necessary materials. Based on the equipment, materials, and staff resources required, Mr. Rabas 
stated that staff did not believe the Town could complete the work for less than the proposed 
Page 7 of 31
cost. 
 
Trustee Wakeman expressed concern with provisions of the agreement requiring the Town to 
pay the $35,000 prior to the commencement of work, particularly given the lack of a firm 
completion commitment and other potentially open-ended provisions. Town Attorney Matthew 
Gould and Mr. Rabas discussed revisions that had already been made to the agreement, 
including changes related to indemnification and open-ended pricing, and indicated that staff 
was confident the work would be completed. 
 
Trustee Cummins asked whether the $35,000 represented a flat-rate or estimated cost and 
whether the agreement allowed WES DJ Gathering to seek additional payment. Attorney Gould 
explained that the amount was an estimated cost and discussed the agreement's provisions 
regarding additional funds. The Board continued to express concerns regarding advance 
payment, the absence of a firm completion date, and language providing that work would not 
begin until full payment was received. Attorney Gould and Mr. Rabas indicated that additional 
revisions could be proposed to address the Board's concerns while recognizing the need to 
complete the work as part of the overall drainage project. 
 
It was noted in the agenda memo that $150,000 was appropriated in the 2026 Stormwater Fund 
for the Ehrlich Lake Drainage Improvements Project. GLH Construction was previously 
awarded the project in the amount of $131,103, leaving an available project balance of $18,897. 
If the $35,000 WES DJ Gathering pipeline removal cost is approved, an additional $16,103 will 
be required, and staff will present a budget amendment at a future Board meeting to appropriate 
the additional funds necessary to fully fund the project. 
 
Trustee BJ Cummins motioned to approve and authorize the Mayor to sign a pipeline removal 
agreement with WES DJ Gathering, LLC., for the Ehrlich Lake Drainage Improvements 
Project in the amount of $35,000.00.  Trustee Dan Dean seconded. 
 
Voting Yes: BJ Cummins, Michael Orcutt, Dan Dean, Amanda Nix, Elizabeth Austin, Riki 
Rarick 
Voting No: Mindy Wakeman 
Motion passed 6-1.  Motion Carried. 
  
  c. Consideration and Approval of an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) 
Authorizing Initial Engineering and Design Services and Support Costs 
and Expenses 
Don Stonebrink , Water/Wastewater Director  
Don Stonebrink, Water/Wastewater Director, requested the Board of Trustees’ consideration in 
approving an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) authorizing initial engineering and design 
services, support costs, and expenses related to the potential formation of a Regional Water 
Authority. Mr. Stonebrink explained that the Town of Milliken, Central Weld County Water 
District, and several other municipalities and entities are exploring the formation of a Regional 
Water Authority and potential regional water system projects. The proposed IGA is limited to 
preliminary engineering and evaluation intended to determine which available water sources 
may be treatable and to provide the participating entities with information necessary to 
Page 8 of 31
determine whether continued participation in the regional project is feasible. Milliken’s 
proportionate share of the initial engineering services is $50,000, which has been appropriated 
in the Town’s 2026 Capital Budget. 
 
The Board discussed the potential treatment of Hillsborough water and other native water 
sources. Mr. Stonebrink explained that participating entities are motivated to evaluate options 
for treating various sources, potentially including pretreatment alternatives. The preliminary 
process is anticipated to include extensive water sampling and testing over approximately the 
next year due to seasonal variations in water quality. He noted that Hillsborough water quality 
has improved over time as agricultural and dairy operations in the area have changed, but 
additional testing is necessary to determine its suitability for treatment. 
 
Trustee Nix questioned whether the Town’s investment in the regional project would be more 
beneficial than pursuing an independent treatment system. Mr. Stonebrink explained that the 
Town has considered and continues to evaluate independent treatment options; however, 
significant challenges include obtaining sufficient water to treat, the location and delivery of 
available water supplies, regulatory requirements, and the expense associated with treatment 
alternatives. He noted that the Town currently utilizes approximately 1,000 acre-feet of water 
annually and that the approximately 900 acre-feet and 300 acre-feet associated with potential 
sources would not independently provide sufficient supply, particularly because Hillsborough 
is seasonal. 
 
Trustee Dean expressed support for the preliminary work and asked what specific deliverables 
the Town would receive from its investment. Mr. Stonebrink explained that the work is 
anticipated to include water testing and evaluation, a conceptual design report, and a final 
conceptual report addressing treatment feasibility. The resulting information will be presented 
to the Board and should provide a clearer understanding of which water sources can be treated 
and whether participation in a regional treatment facility is feasible. Jacobs Engineering is 
performing the engineering work, and additional information regarding the anticipated schedule 
is expected as the evaluation progresses. 
 
Trustee Orcutt asked what the best-case outcome of the preliminary work would be. Mr. 
Stonebrink explained that the process should help the Town determine whether it can afford to 
participate in the Regional Water Authority, whether participation is in the Town’s best 
interest, and what viable treatment options may exist. He noted that Milliken is currently the 
smallest of approximately eight potential participants. The Board discussed that there is no 
guaranteed return from the preliminary investment; however, the information obtained 
regarding the Town’s water sources could have value even if Milliken ultimately does not 
participate in the regional project. Discussion also included the potential use of Hillsborough 
Reservoir and approximately 300 acre-feet of storage for irrigation purposes, which could 
preserve other water supplies for treatment. 
 
Trustee Rarick expressed concern regarding Milliken’s position as one of the smaller 
participants, representing approximately eight percent of the overall project, and questioned 
whether the Town would have an equal voice as the project progresses. The Board and staff 
discussed the advantages and disadvantages of participation, the percentage-based allocation of 
Page 9 of 31
costs, and the importance of completing the engineering evaluation before committing to 
potentially significant future expenditures. Staff emphasized that the $50,000 preliminary 
investment is intended to provide the information necessary for the Board to make future 
decisions before substantially larger project costs are considered. 
 
Trustee Wakeman requested that staff continue to provide the Board with regular updates, 
including anticipated timelines, testing results, and project milestones. Mr. Stonebrink advised 
that Jacobs Engineering is expected to attend an upcoming meeting, at which time additional 
information regarding the project and anticipated timeline should be available. The Town’s 
$50,000 share will not be paid in advance but will be invoiced as engineering work progresses. 
 
Trustee Amanda Nix moved to approve and authorize the Mayor to sign the Intergovernmental 
Agreement for the initial engineering and design services, support costs and expenses related to 
possible Regional Water Authority formation.  Trustee Dan Dean seconded. 
 
Voting Yes: Elizabeth Austin, Dan Dean, Amanda Nix, BJ Cummins, Michael Orcutt, Mindy 
Wakeman, Riki Rarick 
Voting No: None 
Motion passed 7-0.  Motion Carried.

10. Discussion Agenda   
  a. Discussion and Direction Regarding the Town Hall Rental Program 
Caree Rinebarger, Town Clerk  
Caree Rinebarger, Town Clerk, requested Board discussion and direction regarding the Town 
Hall Rental Program, including whether the current fee structure adequately recovers the 
Page 11 of 31
Town’s costs and whether operational changes or restrictions should be considered.  
 
Staff explained that Town Hall is currently available for public and private rentals at rates 
ranging from $35 to $60 per hour; however, the existing fees do not fully account for 
administrative and staff time, overtime or compensatory time, utilities, facility maintenance, 
setup and cleanup, and general wear and tear. Staff also discussed ongoing staffing challenges 
associated with evening and weekend rentals following the loss of the Town’s dedicated Hall 
Monitor.  
 
The Board discussed the importance of Town Hall as a community amenity and generally 
expressed a desire to continue offering the facility for public use rather than discontinuing the 
rental program.  
 
Discussion focused on developing a hybrid approach that would better recover the Town’s 
actual costs while keeping rental rates reasonable and competitive. The Board discussed 
establishing a tiered fee structure based on factors such as event type, number of attendees, and 
level of staff support required; increasing rates and deposits; identifying and training 
appropriate Hall Monitors; improving pre- and post-event inspections and documentation, 
including photographs; and ensuring adequate oversight to address damages or other issues.  
 
The Board also discussed establishing blackout dates and limiting availability when rentals 
could interfere with Town operations or regularly scheduled uses, particularly activities at the 
Senior Center, which should remain a priority. The Board recognized that some municipal cost 
is associated with providing a community amenity but indicated that taxpayers should not be 
expected to substantially subsidize private events, particularly larger events, business uses, or 
rentals by individuals or organizations outside the community. The Board generally supported 
moving forward with a hybrid model that maintains public access to Town Hall while revising 
the fee structure and rental requirements to improve cost recovery, facility protection, staffing, 
and overall program sustainability. 
  
  b. Discussion and Direction Regarding the HWY 257 Noise Complaint 
Steven Krolczyk, Interim Town Administrator  
Steven Krolczyk, Interim Town Administrator, requested Board discussion and direction 
regarding ongoing noise concerns along Highway 257 near the Mad Russian neighborhood. Mr. 
Krolczyk explained that a resident petition regarding traffic and truck noise had recently been 
submitted to the Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT), requesting consideration of a 
sound barrier. The matter was also recently highlighted in an article in the Milliken Breeze. 
Town staff had not been contacted regarding the petition prior to its submission or the Breeze 
article; however, the concerns were brought back to the Board for discussion and possible 
direction. Staff provided background regarding previous efforts to address concerns in the area, 
including signage and speed trailers, and noted that Highway 257 is within CDOT’s 
jurisdiction. Previous Town-installed signage was required to be removed from CDOT right-of-
way.  
 
Staff and Police Chief Benito Garcia also discussed the significant safety concerns associated 
with positioning officers along this portion of the highway due to roadway elevations, curves, 
Page 12 of 31
traffic speeds, and limited safe locations for enforcement. It was further noted that the 
roadway’s grades naturally contribute to truck noise as drivers accelerate, brake, and downshift 
to safely navigate the area. A sound wall would present additional challenges because of the 
difference in elevation between the highway and surrounding properties and is not currently 
included in CDOT’s capital plans. 
 
The Board acknowledged and expressed understanding of residents’ concerns while 
recognizing the Town’s limited authority over a state highway. Discussion included the 
possibility of continuing to work with CDOT, encouraging commercial drivers to minimize 
unnecessary noise when possible, utilizing social media or trucking-related communication 
channels to increase awareness of neighborhood concerns, and exploring whether additional 
noise monitoring or other data collection could be helpful.  
 
The Board also discussed whether additional landscaping or vegetation could provide some 
noise mitigation, although improvements on private or HOA property would generally be the 
responsibility of the applicable property owners or association. The possibility of requesting a 
CDOT speed study was discussed; however, staff noted that such a study could potentially 
support maintaining or even increasing the existing speed limit rather than reducing it. The 
Board emphasized that traffic enforcement efforts should not place officers in unsafe situations 
and recognized that some vehicle noise, including noise from trucks and motorcycles 
accelerating, braking, or changing gears, is inherent to living adjacent to a major state 
highway.  
 
The Board generally supported continuing to explore reasonable opportunities to communicate 
residents’ concerns to CDOT and the trucking community while recognizing the Town’s 
jurisdictional and safety limitations.

for Preparation of the Water Supply Element 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director  
Community Developement Director Kevin Koelbel discussed Senate Bill 24-174 requires 
municipalities to adopt a Water Supply Element as part of their Comprehensive Plan by the 
state-established deadline. To meet this requirement, staff recommends contracting with Ayres 
Associates to prepare the Water Supply Element. 
 
Ayres Associates has an established understanding of the Town's existing conditions, growth 
projections, and long-term planning goals through their completion of the Town's Housing 
Needs Assessment and their ongoing work preparing the Strategic Growth Element of the 
Page 15 of 31
Comprehensive Plan. This familiarity provides continuity between the required planning 
documents, reduces duplication of effort, and allows the consultant to efficiently develop a 
Water Supply Element that is consistent with the Town's overall planning framework and state 
requirements. 
 
Utilizing Ayres Associates will allow the Town to build upon previously completed analyses, 
streamline the planning process, and maintain the project schedule necessary to meet the 
statutory deadline established under SB24-174.

1.   Proposed Ordinance Amending Section 2-2-90 of the Municipal Code 
2.   Depiction of Change to Existing Code 
PURPOSE 
The purpose of this proposed ordinance is to require regular board input into future 
board meeting agendas. 
BACKGROUND 
Section 2-2-90 of the Milliken Municipal Code currently provides in pertinent part that 
board meeting agendas are to be prepared "by the Town Clerk, acting at the direction of 
the Mayor."  The board as a whole has indicated a desire for consultation by the town clerk in 
assembling future meeting agendas.  Recognizing that some items that call for inclusion on a 
meeting agenda arise when it is too late for the board to be consulted in advance, such as 
matters that arise from the day-to-day work of town staff, the ordinance calls for board 
consultation "to the extent feasible" to allow the town clerk to exercise judgment and accept 
staff input in preparing final agendas.
