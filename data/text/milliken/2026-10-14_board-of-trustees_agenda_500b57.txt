Katie VanDeraa (9102 CR 44, Milliken) addressed the Board regarding the potential paving of 
County Road 44. Ms. VanDeraa acknowledged the Town’s efforts to maintain its streets and 
recognized that residents in the area chose to live along a county road. She expressed concern 
that paving CR 44, particularly due to its proximity to CR 19, could result in increased traffic 
volumes and vehicle speeds.  Ms. VanDeraa noted that the roadway is regularly used by 
cyclists, runners, pedestrians, and families and asked the Board to consider the potential long-
term impacts of increased traffic on those users. She also discussed the importance of traffic-
count and average daily traffic data in future decision-making and encouraged the Town to 
consider seasonal agricultural traffic when evaluating that data, given the agricultural character 
of the surrounding area.  Ms. VanDeraa stated that she has not observed significant access 
concerns for emergency vehicles or school buses under the road’s current conditions. She 
encouraged the Board to consider the long-term impacts and costs associated with paving CR 
44 and invited Board members to personally travel the roadway as part of their consideration. 
 
Ysabel Botello (1471 S. Cora Ave., Milliken) thanked the Board for taking the time to listen to 
residents and expressed his appreciation to Town Clerk Caree Rinebarger for her prompt 
response and assistance with his recent Colorado Open Records Act (CORA) request.  Mr. 
Botello asked when the Town published the next year's water rates. Finance Director Krolczyk 
explained that rates are generally adopted by the Board in December and become effective 
January 1.  Mr. Botello concluded by again thanking the Board and Town staff for their work 
and assistance and expressed his appreciation for the service being provided to the community.

8. Town Administrator's Report 
  Greg Brinck, Town Administrator   
Town Administrator Greg Brinck provided an update on current Town activities and discussed 
the format of future administrative reports. Beginning in October, a comprehensive written staff 
report will be provided at the first regular Board meeting of each month, with a verbal update 
provided at the second meeting. Department information, including Police and Community 
Development reports, will be incorporated into the comprehensive report and made available

Administrator Brinck recognized Finance Director Krolczyk's birthday and acknowledged 
employee service anniversaries for Tami Burns, eight years, and Courtney Diller, 18 years. He

Administrator Brinck also reminded the Board of the upcoming Budget Work Session 
scheduled for October 14. 
 
Administrator Brinck reported that call volumes related to utility billing and water rates have 
decreased significantly. Finance staff continue to spend considerable time assisting customers 
by telephone and through individual in-person meetings.

final rollout of WaterScope, which will include mailed postcards, utility bill information, a 
dedicated webpage and FAQ, and social media communications.  Staff provided additional 
information regarding the WaterScope rollout. The new webpage and comprehensive FAQ are

11. Discussion Agenda   
  a. Facility Rental Program Update  
Town Clerk Caree Rinebarger presented an updated Hybrid Model for the Town Hall Facility 
Rental Program following previous Board discussion. The proposed model would continue 
public rental of the Town Hall Community Room while discontinuing rental of the Activity 
Room. Proposed changes included a tiered fee structure for nonprofit/community organizations, 
funerals and memorials, Milliken residents, non-residents, and business/for-profit users; a two-
hour minimum rental period to assist with recovery of administrative and operational costs; and 
a requirement that setup, event, and cleanup time be included within the reserved rental period. 
The proposal also addressed rental overages, after-hours staffing costs, damage deposits, 
qualifying uses during normal business hours, and designated rental blackout dates. 
 
Clerk Rinebarger also presented a proposed employee facility rental benefit that would allow 
employees two complimentary Community Room rentals and two complimentary park rentals 
per calendar year, with additional qualifying rentals charged at the organizational rate. 
Employees would remain subject to the Town's standard application and approval process, 
damage deposit requirements, alcohol and facility policies, and cleaning and damage 
responsibilities. The employee would also be required to remain present throughout the rental. 
Failure to comply with applicable rental policies could result in suspension or loss of the 
employee rental benefit. 
 
The Board discussed the proposed Hybrid Model and expressed general support for continuing 
to make the Community Room available for public use while balancing accessibility with 
staffing demands, operational costs, security, and employees' time away from work. Discussion 
included whether the proposed blackout periods could be reduced to provide additional rental 
opportunities while still allowing adequate time for staff following holidays and major Town 
events. 
 
The Board discussed after-hours and blackout-period rentals and the possibility of using 
designated staff or other individuals willing to provide rental coverage. It was noted that 
previous efforts to secure reliable outside facility monitors had been unsuccessful and that 
coverage often ultimately fell to Town staff. The Board discussed providing incentives for 
employees who voluntarily provide coverage during holidays or designated blackout periods. 
 
The Board also discussed the circumstances under which rental fees should apply, with 
direction to focus fees on rentals that create additional Town expenses, particularly those 
occurring outside normal business hours. Staff was directed to further clarify how rentals 
occurring during regular Town Hall business hours would be handled within the fee structure. 
 
IT Specialist Mundy discussed security concerns associated with facility rentals, including 
individuals accessing or tampering with secured areas and the Town Hall keypad. Recent 
Page 8 of 109
incidents have resulted in numerous security notifications and required staff follow-up. The 
Board discussed potential security improvements, including limiting access to designated 
entrances, keeping nonessential doors secured, protecting access-control keypads, evaluating 
additional physical security measures, clearly identifying the individual responsible for each 
rental, and exploring options for monitoring security cameras from an accessible staff area 
rather than solely from an individual office. 
 
The Board expressed support for the proposed employee rental benefit, provided employees 
remain responsible for compliance with all applicable rental requirements and for the conduct 
of their rental party. 
 
Town Administrator Greg Brinck noted that staff would incorporate the Board's direction into 
the administrative policy and related rental documents to ensure the program meets the policy 
goals established by the Board. 
  
  b. Social Media and Communications Discussion  
Town Attorney Gould asked that the Board provide a list of the changes and guidance they 
would like incorporated into the revised policy so that staff and legal counsel can evaluate the 
requested changes, identify any legal concerns, and return with language that addresses the 
Board's objectives. He also noted that additional educational resources regarding social media 
risks for elected officials may be available and offered to share those resources with the Board. 
 
The Board discussed the need for clearer guidance regarding what constitutes current Board 
business versus matters that have already been considered or decided by the Board. Members 
expressed concern that the existing policy may be interpreted too restrictively and indicated 
that elected officials should have the ability to communicate factual information, provide 
clarification, and help educate residents regarding matters already addressed through the 
Town's public process, while avoiding deliberation, debate, or discussion that could implicate 
Colorado Open Meetings Law or other legal requirements. 
 
The Board requested that the revised policy include practical examples illustrating when an 
elected official may appropriately respond to a resident and when a response should be avoided 
or referred to Town staff. Discussion included providing factual information already contained 
in the public record, explaining previously approved decisions or processes, responding to 
questions from residents, and distinguishing those communications from ongoing matters that 
may come before the Board for consideration. The Board also discussed the importance of 
recognizing additional restrictions applicable to quasi-judicial matters. 
 
The Board discussed social media as an increasingly important tool for communicating with 
and educating residents. Members requested additional guidance regarding participation by 
multiple Trustees on the same social media post or topic and when such participation could 
create legal concerns. The Board also discussed whether comments on Town social media 
pages should be limited or disabled, the use of disclaimers and published commenting 
standards, and whether individual Town committees should have some flexibility in managing 
comments within an established Town policy. 
 
Page 9 of 109
The Board further discussed its role as a liaison between the community and Town staff. 
Members expressed concern that directing every resident question back to staff may 
unnecessarily limit communication between elected officials and the residents they represent. 
The Board indicated a desire for a policy that allows members to acknowledge residents, 
provide factual information when appropriate, and assist with directing questions or concerns to 
the proper resource without engaging in prolonged debate or discussion. 
 
Citizen comments received during Board meetings were also discussed. Members expressed a 
desire to establish a clearer process for acknowledging resident comments and ensuring 
appropriate follow-up when a response from staff is warranted. The Board discussed the 
importance of allowing residents to feel heard while maintaining the distinction between 
acknowledging or following up on a concern and engaging in debate during the citizen 
comment portion of a meeting. 
 
Town Administrator Greg Brinck indicated that staff would review the existing policies, 
including provisions governing official Town social media posting and commenting, and work 
with legal counsel to develop revisions that provide clearer guidance while maintaining 
appropriate legal safeguards. The citizen comment and staff follow-up process may be 
addressed separately to establish a clear communication flow between the Board, staff, and 
residents. 
 
The matter will return to the Board for additional discussion at the October 28, 2026, Work 
Session.

What Are The Rates?
2026 Water Rates
After an analysis of the Water Fund in fall 2025, it was
determined that the existing 2025 water rates would not
be sufficient to cover the Fund’s ongoing operational
needs and necessary capital infrastructure investments.
As a result, a 35% average water rate increase was
adopted and implemented beginning in January 2026. The
rate adjustment included a 15% increase to the base rate
and a 45% increase to consumption rates.
The rate adjustment was necessary for several key
reasons:
Capital infrastructure needs: Repairs, replacements,
upgrades, and other capital projects that help maintain
the reliability and long-term sustainability of the water
system.
Operations and maintenance: Additional funding is
needed for the ongoing operation, maintenance,
repair, and management of the water system to ensure
continued safe and reliable service.
Increased cost of treated water: Milliken purchases
treated water from Central Weld County Water
District and City of Greeley
The impact of the rate adjustment varies depending on
water consumption. For example, an average household
using 2,200 gallons during the winter would see an
estimated 23% increase in its monthly water bill. For that
same household using approximately 22,000 gallons
during a month of outdoor irrigation, the bill would
increase by approximately 41%.
For the example above, if they irrigate outdoors for six
months of the year and do not the other six months, the
estimated annual water bill increase would be
approximately 38%.
The rate adjustment is intended to ensure that water
revenues cover the actual costs of providing service and
that the Water Fund has sufficient resources to address
both immediate operational needs and the long-term
infrastructure investments necessary to maintain a reliable
water system.
The 2026 rates took effect the
January 2026 billing cycle. The first
bill received with the new rates was
the beginning of February 2026. Rate
increase notifications were included
in the December 2025 and January
2026 utility bill newsletters.

effect?

the Town budget including the
proposed rate increase. A Public

held for the 2026 budget on

1.   Encroachment Agreement 
PURPOSE 
To consider approval of an Encroachment Agreement with Meadow Farms HOA to 
allow the HOA to construct and maintain a trail connection within a portion of the Town-
owned County Road 44 right-of-way. 
BACKGROUND 
The Meadow Farms Homeowners Association (HOA) has requested permission to 
utilize approximately 10 feet of the Town's right-of-way along County Road 44, adjacent 
to the Meadow Farms subdivision. The purpose of the encroachment is to connect the 
existing equestrian trails to a new trail along the subdivision frontage, creating a more 
continuous trail system throughout the development.  
 
Because the proposed improvements would extend into Town-owned right-of-way, an 
Encroachment Agreement is necessary to establish the permitted use and outline the 
responsibilities of both parties. The agreement would grant the HOA a temporary, 
nonexclusive license to utilize the identified portion of the right-of-way without 
conveying ownership or permanent property rights. 
 
Under the agreement, the HOA would be responsible for the construction, 
maintenance, repair, and any necessary removal of the trail improvements at its sole 
expense. The Town would retain responsibility for curb and gutter maintenance 
immediately adjacent to County Road 44. 
 
The agreement also preserves the Town's ability to utilize the right-of-way for future 
Page 27 of 109
public improvements, utility work, or other municipal purposes. Should the 
improvements interfere with these activities or create a safety concern, the Town may 
require their removal at the HOA's expense. 
BUDGET IMPLICATIONS 
There is no anticipated direct financial impact to the Town associated with approval of 
the agreement. The HOA would be responsible for all costs associated with 
construction, maintenance, and removal of the improvements, except for the Town's 
existing responsibility for curb and gutter maintenance.

Staff recommends approval of the encroachment agreement with the Meadow Farms
