5. Citizen Comments  
Kayla Hopkins (960 S Rachel) addressed the Board with questions regarding the Town’s recent 
water meter replacement project. She asked who manufactured the new meters, the total project 
cost, and whether the software was included. Staff explained that the meters were manufactured 
by Metron and that the approximately $1.25 million project cost included both the meters and 
associated software. 
 
Ms. Hopkins also questioned the age of the previous meters and why meters in newer 
neighborhoods were replaced. Staff explained that the previous meters ranged from 
approximately 10 to 20 years old and that the replacement project began in 2024. Staff clarified 
that approximately 400–500 meters had previously been replaced as they failed, while 
approximately 2,450 meters were replaced during the current phase, including some newer 
meters. Staff explained that the previous system required drive-by data collection, while the 
Page 4 of 432
new system provides more advanced technology. Maintaining two separate meter systems 
would not have been cost-effective. Staff also confirmed that the removed meters were sold to 
help offset project costs. 
 
Katie VanDeraa (9102 CR 44) addressed the Board requesting that the proposed paving of 
County Road 44 be removed from the 2027 budget. She explained that the road is partially 
paved near Meadow Farms, with dirt road sections remaining on both ends, including where 
she resides. While acknowledging that dust and muddy road conditions can be inconvenient, 
she expressed greater concern regarding increased traffic volumes and vehicle speeds that could 
result from paving. She emphasized that the preferences of a few residents should not outweigh 
the concerns of others who may be impacted by the project. 
 
Shannon Farmer (9626 CR 44) addressed the Board in opposition to the proposed paving of 
County Road 44. She disputed previous statements suggesting that the road poses safety 
concerns for emergency medical services, sharing that her father has been transported by 
ambulance on two occasions, including once during a blizzard, without issue. She described CR 
44 as one of the best-maintained dirt roads in the county and questioned concerns regarding 
emergency vehicle accessibility. Ms. Farmer encouraged the Board to consider the broader 
impacts of paving, particularly the potential for increased vehicle speeds and associated safety 
concerns. She also expressed concern regarding the significant cost of the project and suggested 
that town's funds would be better allocated toward other priorities, including public safety, 
parks, and infrastructure.

  a. Meadow Farms Encroachment Agreement 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director  
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director, presented an Encroachment Agreement 
with the Meadow Farms Homeowners Association (HOA) requesting permission to utilize 
approximately 10 feet of Town-owned right-of-way along County Road 44 to construct and 
maintain a soft-surface trail connecting existing equestrian trails within the subdivision. The 
agreement would grant the HOA temporary, nonexclusive use of the right-of-way, with the 
HOA responsible for all construction, maintenance, and removal costs. Director Koelbel 
explained that the Town would retain full rights to the property and could require removal of 
the improvements if the right-of-way is needed for future municipal purposes. There would be 
no anticipated direct financial impact to the Town. Staff recommended approval of the 
agreement.

  b. Acknowledgment of Receipt by the Town Board of the 2027 Preliminary 
Budget 
Steven Krolczyk, Interim Town Administrator  
Steven Krolczyk, Finance Director, presented an overview of the Town's preliminary 2027 
budget, explaining that the purpose of the presentation was for the Board to review and 
acknowledge receipt of the proposed budget, not to approve or adopt it. He emphasized that the 
Page 6 of 432
budget remains a working document and will continue to be refined through upcoming work 
sessions and the public hearing scheduled for November 23, 2026, with final budget adoption 
and mill levy certification anticipated for December 9, 2026. 
 
The presentation outlined the Town's budget process, financial strategies, General Fund 
revenues and expenditures, enterprise funds, proposed utility rates, and five-year capital 
improvement plan. Staff emphasized the importance of maintaining healthy fund balances, 
addressing aging infrastructure, planning for future capital expenses, and developing a balanced 
budget. 
 
General Fund: Staff reviewed projected 2027 revenues of approximately $8.9 million and 
expenditures of $10.2 million, comparing the proposed budget with 2026 adopted figures. 
Discussion included departmental expenses, revenue projections, and the importance of 
evaluating actual expenditures as additional financial information becomes available and 
creating a balanced budget. 
 
Enterprise Funds and Utility Rates: Staff reviewed the financial outlook for the Water, 
Wastewater, Stormwater, and Trash Funds, including long-term capital needs and potential debt 
obligations. Several financial scenarios were presented, including the impacts of future water 
infrastructure projects, such as the Hicks Water Treatment Project and Central Weld 
improvements. Staff discussed the importance of maintaining sufficient revenues to cover 
operating expenses while preparing for significant future capital investments. 
 
The Board and Staff discussed the proposed 4% increases to water, wastewater, and stormwater 
rates and the contractual 5% increase for trash services. Trustees expressed concerns regarding 
the financial impacts on residents, the number of existing water usage tiers, and the need to 
avoid substantial rate increases in future years. Staff explained that the proposed increases 
would help cover ongoing operational costs but would not fully address future capital needs. 
Administrator Brinck advised that the Town is pursuing a comprehensive fee and rate analysis 
to evaluate the sustainability of current rates, the existing tier structure, and long-term financial 
requirements. The Board emphasized the importance of transparency, affordability, and 
ensuring that future rate adjustments are supported by sound financial planning. 
 
Wastewater Treatment Plant: The Board and Staff discussed the potential re-rating of the 
Wastewater Treatment Plant as an alternative to immediate expansion. The re-rating could 
increase the plant's permitted treatment capacity and potentially delay the need for a costly 
expansion. Board members asked questions regarding the feasibility of re-rating, anticipated 
growth, regulatory requirements, and the Town's ability to qualify for future debt financing. 
Staff explained that additional evaluations and discussions would be necessary before 
determining the most appropriate approach. 
 
Capital Improvement Plan: Staff presented the proposed five-year capital improvement plan, 
identifying projects related to streets, public safety, technology, facilities, parks, water, 
wastewater, and stormwater infrastructure. Projects discussed included Board Room 
audiovisual upgrades, IT infrastructure improvements, police equipment, Town Hall 
maintenance, park equipment, roadway maintenance, the Transportation Alternatives Program 
Page 7 of 432
(TAP) grant, North Frances Avenue reconstruction, and the proposed County Road 44 chip seal 
project. Staff also reviewed major utility infrastructure projects, including waterline 
replacements, wastewater treatment improvements, and stormwater drainage projects. 
Additional information regarding the TAP grant and associated funding requirements will be 
presented at a future meeting. 
 
Throughout the presentation, the Board discussed the importance of prioritizing essential 
infrastructure, maintaining adequate reserves, evaluating potential debt obligations, and 
balancing the Town's long-term needs with the financial impacts on residents. Staff reiterated 
that project timelines, funding scenarios, and proposed expenditures remain subject to change 
as the budget is further developed. 
 
Administrator Brinck and Director Krolczyk concluded by explaining that staff will continue 
refining revenue projections, expenditures, capital priorities, and proposed rates before bringing 
a balanced budget back for additional Board review. Board members thanked staff for the 
comprehensive and informative presentation.

11. Discussion Agenda   
  a. Food Truck Park Discussion 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director  
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director, presented a proposal to explore a monthly 
food truck rally pilot program during the warmer months of 2027 before committing to a 
permanent food truck park. Potential locations included Lola Park and the Town-owned 
property at 106 Josephine Avenue. Director Koelbel explained that the pilot would allow staff 
to evaluate community participation, vendor interest, operational requirements, and potential 
costs before investing in permanent infrastructure. He also discussed considerations involving 
ADA accessibility, floodplain restrictions, site improvements, utilities, maintenance, and 
staffing responsibilities. 
 
The Board discussed the original intent of providing food truck operators with a safer 
alternative to parking along Highway 60 while encouraging residents to dine locally and 
support food truck businesses. Several Trustees expressed concern that developing a permanent 
location could create unnecessary expenses and regulatory requirements when the primary goal 
was simply to provide additional parking options. Discussion included the existing amenities at 
Lola Park, the visibility and proximity of the Josephine Avenue property to downtown, and the 
possibility of allowing food trucks to utilize designated on-street parking areas. 
 
Page 8 of 432
Board members expressed differing opinions regarding restrictions on food truck parking, 
emphasizing the importance of addressing safety concerns without unnecessarily limiting 
business opportunities. Staff explained that operators often select locations based on visibility, 
customer traffic, and existing business relationships, and that providing an alternative location 
alone may not encourage relocation. 
 
Following discussion, Administrator Brinck indicated that staff would conduct additional 
research regarding potential parking restrictions, alternative locations, and seek input from 
existing vendors and return to the Board with additional information and recommendations. 
  
  b. 1398 Irene - Student Home Construction Partnership Discussion 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director  
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director, presented a potential partnership with Weld 
RE-5J School District and its Career and Technical Education (CTE) construction program to 
construct a single-family home on Town-owned property at 1398 Irene. The project would 
provide students with hands-on construction experience while creating an opportunity for 
attainable housing within the community. Director Koelbel explained that the District, Housing 
Authority, and construction industry partners have begun preliminary discussions regarding the 
project, including potential funding, materials, labor, and construction responsibilities. 
 
Staff outlined several options for the completed home, including housing for local teachers, 
workforce housing, income-qualified homeownership, or a traditional market sale with 
proceeds potentially supporting future projects. Director Koelbel noted that the Town's primary 
contributions could include the property and a required water share, with additional financial 
responsibilities and partnerships still to be determined. Habitat for Humanity was also 
identified as a potential partner. 
 
The Board expressed strong support for further exploring the partnership, recognizing the 
benefits of workforce development, student education, community partnerships, and providing 
attainable housing opportunities. Trustees emphasized the importance of minimizing the 
Town's financial commitment, identifying outside funding and resources, and considering 
opportunities to prioritize housing for income-qualified individuals or local workers. The Board 
also discussed the value of utilizing an existing Town-owned lot with infrastructure already 
available. 
 
Staff indicated that additional discussions would continue with the School District, Housing 
Authority, and potential partners to evaluate project feasibility, funding opportunities, and long-
term ownership options. The project would likely be considered as part of the 2028 budget 
process, allowing time to establish partnerships and determine financial responsibilities. Staff 
anticipates returning to the Board with additional information toward the end of the year. 
 
The Board expressed consensus to support continued exploration of the partnership.

for design and construction of the Milliken Sidewalk and ADA project.

kickoff design meeting was held. 
• Fall 2025: CDOT requested us to re-design for 5-foot sidewalks or place bump outs 
every 200 feet. A change order was issued to Kimley-Horn in January 2026 to design for 
5-foot sidewalks which reduced the amount of the Tap grant available for construction. 
• August 2026 an RFP went out to contractors and bid opening was held on September 4, 
2026. Because this is a CDOT funded project, the lowest responsive bidder is awarded 
the project. The lowest bid came in above the grant amount so the Town can choose 
between two different options.
