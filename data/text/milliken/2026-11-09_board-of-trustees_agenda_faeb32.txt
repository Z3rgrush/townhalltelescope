5. Citizen Comments  
Randy Helzer (9481 Meadow Farms Drive) addressed the Board regarding the condition of 
Weld County Road (WCR) 44 east of WCR 19 in relation to the upcoming 2027 Budget. Mr. 
Helzer noted that WCR 44 serves as the sole access point for nearly 50 Milliken homes, and 
functions as an informal bypass for heavy commercial and passenger vehicle traffic. He 
described severe surface deterioration, washboarding, deep ruts, standing water, and excessive 
dust impacting resident health and property. He countered arguments regarding potential 
speeding on a paved surface by emphasizing that the Town retains full law enforcement 
authority to enforce the posted 30 MPH speed limit. Additionally, he referenced a 2008 
Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) and Service Plan for Meadow Farms Filing 1 that allocated 
developer funding to pave WCR 44, urging the Town Board to address the long-standing 
Page 3 of 138
oversight and fund the necessary paving improvements. 
 
Kayla Hopkins (690 S Rachel) inquired about the decision-making process for the Town's new 
water meters, referencing statements from the prior Board meeting indicating the selected 
system was less expensive than Badger meters. Ms. Hopkins noted that she had requested a copy 
of the bid submitted by Badger and received four (4) emails from Town staff confirming that all 
records responsive to her request had been provided, before later being informed that the Town 
does not possess that specific record. She questioned whether the Town followed its proper 
procurement policy during the selection process. Mayor Austin noted that the Town 
Administrator would follow up with her regarding the matter.

11. Discussion Agenda   
  a. Communication & Social Media Policy Discussion 
Caree Rinebarger, Town Clerk, Matthew Gould, Town Attorney  
Town Attorney Matthew Gould presented proposed revisions to the Town’s Social Media Policy 
(Appendix F) and Public Comment Procedures (Appendix C), following previous Board 
direction. 
 
The Board discussed concerns regarding the proposed social media restrictions, including 
potential limitations on transparency, communication with residents, and First Amendment 
rights. Attorney Gould reviewed legal considerations under the Colorado Open Meetings Law 
Page 5 of 138
(COML), particularly regarding social media interactions among Board and committee 
members. Trustees expressed a desire for clearer, more flexible guidelines identifying 
permissible communications while maintaining legal compliance. 
 
The Board also discussed proposed revisions to public comment procedures, emphasizing the 
importance of allowing appropriate responses and clarification following public comments while 
maintaining meeting decorum and avoiding debate. 
 
Following discussion, the Board directed staff to schedule a Work Session on December 9, 2026, 
for further review and refinement of both policies. 
 
  b. Budget Work Session Continued  
Town Administrator Greg Brinck and Finance Director Steven Krolczyk continued discussions 
regarding the proposed 2027 General Fund, Capital Improvement, and Enterprise Fund budgets. 
 
Staff reported that the projected General Fund deficit had been reduced to approximately 
$718,175, primarily due to one-time capital expenditures, with operating expenses fully covered 
by anticipated revenues. Proposed capital improvements were reviewed, including audiovisual 
upgrades, technology and security enhancements, and roadway projects. Trustees expressed 
concerns regarding the cost of the proposed meeting room audiovisual upgrades and directed 
staff to explore more cost-effective options. Discussion also included proposed improvements to 
Weld County Road 44 and the future scheduling of Green Street improvements. 
 
Also reviewed the Enterprise Fund capital projects and proposed utility rate increases of 4% for 
Water, Sewer, and Stormwater and 5% for Refuse, resulting in an estimated 4% overall increase 
for an average residential customer. 
 
Staff outlined the remaining budget schedule, including further discussion on November 9, a 
public hearing on November 23, and anticipated final adoption on December 9, 2026.

Current Development Applications 
• Homestead Grove Subdivision: This is a Final Development Plan application 
for 899 single-family residential lots on roughly 232 acres. The proposal will also 
include roughly 53 acres of open space which will include a neighborhood park 
and various pocket parks. This application is currently under review. 
• Sunfield Filing 3:This is a Preliminary Plat application for 36 single-family 
residential lots on roughly 11 acres. The proposal will complete the Sunfield 
Subdivision. This application is currently set for public hearings with both the 
Planning Commission on November 4th and Town Board on November 9th. 
• Brookstone Administrative Plat: This is an Administrative Plat application to 
shift a lot line within the Brookstone 3rd Filing Subdivision. This application is 
currently under review. 
• Prairie Sky Preliminary Plat: This is a Preliminary Plat application for 626 
single-family residential lots on approximately 366 acres. The development will 
also include approximately 48 acres of open space, including a neighborhood 
park, pocket parks, and trails throughout the development. This application is 
currently under review. 
Department Updates 
• Staff has continued working on the Water Supply Element update to the 
Comprehensive Plan with Ayres Associates, as required by SB24-174. The 
project is currently in the initial draft phase and the draft will be completed in the 
coming weeks. 
• American Planning Association Colorado Conference: Staff attended the APA 
Colorado conference last week. Sessions covered housing, community growth, 
current trends impacting municipalities, and water planning. Topics included 
ways to build community and elected official support for housing, how cities are 
adapting to changing economic and development trends, and how neighborhood 
centers and mixed-use development can help communities grow while 
maintaining their character. Other sessions focused on the connection between 
water availability and future growth, including new requirements under SB24-174 
and how communities are incorporating water planning into development 
decisions. Overall, the sessions offered useful examples of how other Colorado 
communities are approaching similar challenges and planning for the future.

Facilities Maintenance 
The Facilities Maintenance Foreman has focused on reducing operating costs, completing 
repairs in-house when feasible, and improving the safety and reliability of Town facilities. Recent 
accomplishments include: 
AED and First Aid Supply Cost Reduction 
Conducted extensive research into automated external defibrillators (AEDs) and first aid station 
costs to evaluate potential savings through in-house management. This effort resulted in a 
successful negotiation that reduced the Town's CiNTAS AED contract cost by more than 60%. 
Streetlight Repairs 
Investigated and corrected multiple instances of improper streetlight wiring that had caused 
outages. Completing these repairs internally avoided the expense of hiring outside electrical 
contractors and resulted in cost savings for the Town. 
Police Department Garage Door Repairs 
Identified and secured a local contractor to replace the cables on the east garage door at the 
Police Department. The work was completed in half the time and at a cost of $400 less than the 
comparable work performed by Front Range Raynor Garage on the west door. 
Garage Door Roller Replacement 
Replaced garage door rollers at the Police Department and Public Works facilities, generating 
substantial savings on parts and installation costs. 
• Police Department: Personally replaced most of the garage door rollers at $3.80 per 
roller, compared with Front Range Raynor's price of $20 per roller, plus an estimated 
installation fee of $400–$700 per door. 
• Public Works: Replaced 15 of the 16 rollers on one garage door at $2.30 per roller, 
compared with Front Range Raynor's price of $15 per roller, plus an anticipated 
installation fee of $300–$500.

PURPOSE 
To consider a development application of a Preliminary Plat of 11.3 acres known as 
Sunfield FIling 3. 
BACKGROUND 
The applicant is requesting approval of a Preliminary Plat to subdivide approximately 
11.3 acres into 36 single-family residential lots. The proposed lots have an average size 
of approximately 8,224 square feet, resulting in a density of 3.19 dwelling units per acre, 
consistent with the low-density residential designation established through the 
Centennial Crossing PUD. The proposal also includes 2 tracts for open space totaling 3 
acres or 26% of the site which meets the required 15% for PUD subdivisions.  
 
The Sunfield Subdivision was originally approved by the Town of Milliken in 2019 as 
part of the Centennial Crossing Planned Unit Development (PUD). The original 
subdivision included Tracts D and E, which were designated for future residential 
development. Sunfield Filing No. 3 represents the final phase of the previously 
approved development. The proposed development is located generally between 
Moonflower Lane to the north and the Little Thompson Ditch to the south. The 
Preliminary Plat maintains the previously approved subdivision layout, with extensions 
of existing roadways and utilities to serve the additional lots.

Project Information 
Sunfield Subdivision was originally platted and approved by the Town of Milliken in 2019, with Tract D and Tract 
E set aside for future residential development. Tract D is bounded by Moonflower Lane on the north, 
Pennycress Drive to the west, Lotus Drive to the south and private property to the east. Just south of Lotus Drive 
begins Tract E, with previously approved Sunfield lots directly to the west, The Little Thompson Ditch to the 
south, and private property to the east.  
 
The Sunfield Filing 3 Preliminary Plat submittal is proposing to subdivide these tracts into 36 residential 
detached lots with an average lots size of 8,224 S.F, encompassing approximately 11.3 acres. These lots will be 
known as Block 1, Lots 11-18; Block 2, Lots 1-9; Block 3, Lots 1-9. At 3.19 dwelling units per acre, this aligns with 
the low-density residential use as called out in the original Centennial Crossing PUD and maintains the density 
between 3-5 dwelling units per acre. As part of filing 3, Tract L and Tract M are being platted to align with the 
originally approved open space calculations with no change to the amount previously considered. As such, Tract 
L and Tract M are both designated for open space and easements. 
 
The original plans and supporting reports prepared for Sunfield Filing 1 included the area now identified as 
Filing 3. As a result, this submittal does not include any substantial changes to the overall design previously 
reviewed and approved by the Town as part of the PUD and original subdivision. This includes, but is not limited 
to, the proposed land use, drainage, traffic, overall roadway network, and open space framework. In 
coordination with Town staff, updated drainage letters, traffic memoranda, and construction plans have been 
included with this submittal to reflect the current filing and support the proposed improvements.  The only 
notable modification incorporated with this submittal was made in coordination with, and at the request of, 
Town staff. East Lotus Drive has been extended to the east and stubbed to accommodate a potential future 
access connection to the adjacent property. A landscape plan has also been provided illustrating the proposed 
landscaping within Tracts L and M. Tract L is proposed to include native seeding and trees to create a natural 
transition to the Little Thompson Ditch to the south. Tract M is proposed to be seeded.  
 
Sunfield Filing 3 completes the final phase of the approved Sunfield community and includes the materials 
necessary to support the proposed development in accordance with the Town of Milliken Municipal Code , 
applicable development standards, and Town adopted plans and policy documents. Our team looks forward to 
working collaboratively with staff throughout the review process.

1.   Willdan Professional Services Agreement 
PURPOSE 
Approval of a Professional Services Agreement with Willdan Financial Services to 
prepare the Town of Milliken Town-Wide Rate and Fee Study 
BACKGROUND 
The Town issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) No. 07-2026 on September 28, 2026, 
seeking a qualified consulting firm to conduct a comprehensive review of the Town's 
existing rates and fees. The purpose of the study is to evaluate whether current fees 
appropriately reflect the costs of providing municipal services, maintaining 
infrastructure, and accommodating future growth. 
 
The study will evaluate several major fee categories, including: 
- Development Impact Fees: Review existing and potential impact fees associated 
with municipal facilities, parks and recreation, transportation, stormwater, and public 
safety. 
- Water and Wastewater Rates: Evaluate utility rates, tap fees, plant investment fees, 
and long-term financial needs associated with operating and maintaining the Town's 
utility systems. 
- User and Development Review Fees: Evaluate the Town's existing fee structures, 
cost recovery practices, and opportunities for adjustments to better reflect the costs 
of providing services. 
 
Page 40 of 138
The Town received two proposals by the October 23, 2026, deadline, from Willdan 
Financial Services and Halos LLC. Following a review of both proposals and contacting 
provided references, staff recommends selecting Willdan Financial Services based on 
its extensive experience conducting similar studies for Colorado municipalities, 
specialized expertise across all major study components, and proposed approach to 
completing the project. Willdan also submitted the lower-cost proposal at $149,375, 
compared to Halos LLC's proposal of $178,500. 
 
Willdan specializes in municipal financial consulting, including utility rate studies, 
development impact fees, and user fee analyses. The firm has completed similar 
projects for Colorado communities, including the Town of Mead, City of Central City, 
City and County of Broomfield, and City of Aurora. 
 
Willdan's proposal outlines a comprehensive approach to evaluating the Town's existing 
fee structures, identifying potential funding gaps, and developing recommendations that 
balance cost recovery, financial sustainability, and community priorities. 
 
The proposed study will include coordination with Town staff, financial modeling, 
stakeholder engagement, and presentations to the Board of Trustees. The study is 
anticipated to take approximately 8–12 months to complete. 
BUDGET IMPLICATIONS 
Willdan Financial Services submitted a proposal totaling $149,375, which includes 
$66,860 for the Development Impact Fee Study, $49,740 for the Utility Rate Study, and 
$32,775 for the User Fee Study. The contract will be established as a not-to-exceed 
amount, with any additional services outside the approved scope requiring separate 
authorization by the Town. 
STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS 
Staff recommends approval of Professional Services Agreement with Willdan Financial 
Services for Preparation of the Town-Wide Rate and Fee Study. 
SUGGESTED MOTION 
I move to approve the Professional Services Agreement with Willdan Financial Services 
for Preparation of the Town-Wide Rate and Fee Study for an amount not to exceed 
$149,375 and direct the mayor to sign such agreement.
