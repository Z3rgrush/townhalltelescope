  a. Milliken Store-N-Go Conditional Use Permit 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director  
Chair Randy Helzer announced the procedures governing the Public Hearing and inquired if any 
members of the Planning Commission had engaged in ex parte communications requiring 
disclosure or if any members needed to recuse themselves from the hearing, along with an 
explanation for doing so. Hearing no response, Chair Helzer formally opened the Public Hearing 
at 6:05 P.M. 
 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director, presented the item, requesting the Planning 
Commission consider the Milliken Store-N-Go Conditional Use Permit (CUP) application and 
forward a recommendation to the Board of Trustees. The applicant proposes a Conditional Use 
Permit to establish a fully enclosed, self-service storage facility within the C-4 Service Business 
Zoning District, pursuant to Section 16-2-170 of the Milliken Land Use Code (LUC). The 
project consists of seven (7) buildings totaling over 52,000 square feet of indoor commercial 
storage space featuring a range of unit sizes, including enclosed units suitable for RV storage. 
Operations will be fully automated with no onsite staff, secure keyless entry systems, automated 
access gates, and 24/7 surveillance with controlled access, and no outdoor storage is permitted. 
Due to the property's location adjacent to Highway 60 and surrounding industrial uses, staff 
recommended no additional conditions be placed on the use. Furthermore, the application was 
reviewed by referral agencies and staff in compliance with Section 16-6-360 of the LUC. 
 
During Commission discussion, Chair Helzer inquired about the historical intent for the property 
and whether the proposed storage facility deviates from its original C-4 platting. Director 
Koelbel reviewed the extensive list of uses permitted by right within the C-4 district—such as 
child care centers, RV/auto repair, truck sales, gas stations, hotels/motels, and lumberyards—as 
well as conditional uses like animal boarding and gun ranges. Discussion followed regarding the 
appropriateness of the location, noting that its industrial context eliminates the need for 
additional conditions. Commissioner Fior then noted the existing pedestrian path on the south 
side of the property and inquired about plans for the north side, whereupon it was noted that any 
improvements would require CDOT approval and that the existing landscape easement would 
Page 5 of 24
experience no impact. 
Hearing no further testimony, Chair Helzer closed the Public Hearing at 6:11 P.M. 
 
Commissioner Kassidee Fior motioned to recommend approval of the Milliken Store-N-Go 
Conditional Use Permit to the Town Board of Trustees.  Commissioner Jimmy Baugh seconded. 
 
Voting Yes: Randy Helzer, Jimmy Baugh, Ron Blackmer, Bernadette Stephenson, Kassidee 
Fior, Mary Schneider 
Voting No: None 
Motion passed 6-0. 
 
 
  b. Milliken Store-N-Go Site Plan 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director  
Chair Randy Helzer announced the procedures governing the Public Hearing and inquired if any 
members of the Planning Commission had engaged in ex parte communications requiring 
disclosure or if any members needed to recuse themselves from the hearing, along with an 
explanation for doing so. Hearing no response, Chair Helzer formally opened the Public Hearing 
at 6:13 P.M. 
 
Kevin Koelbel, Community Development Director, requested that the Planning Commission 
consider the Site Plan application for the Milliken Store-N-Go facility and forward a 
recommendation to the Board of Trustees. The application pertains to the development of a self-
service storage facility located at the south-east corner of Center Drive and WCR 23 ¾, just 
north of Highway 60, on property zoned C-4 Service Business District and designated 
Commercial under the Town’s Comprehensive Plan. The proposal involves constructing seven 
(7) fully enclosed buildings totaling approximately 52,000 square feet of indoor commercial 
storage space with no outdoor storage permitted, offering a variety of unit sizes including 
enclosed RV storage units. Operations will be fully automated with no onsite staff, featuring 
secure keyless entry, automated access gates, 24-hour surveillance, and controlled access with 
lighting designed to avoid negative impacts on neighbors. 
 
The site plan includes approximately 49,000 square feet of landscaped area consisting of rock or 
mulch beds with tree and shrub plantings. Although submitted prior to the formal adoption of 
Ordinance 831, the project includes no turfgrass and incorporates a native seed area along 
Highway 60 and Center Drive with irrigation limited to initial establishment only, maintaining 
compliance with record requirements. To satisfy the Town’s parking standard of one (1) space 
per 5,000 square feet of building area, the site plan provides eleven (11) total parking spaces—
ten (10) standard spaces and one (1) ADA-accessible space—with five of these spaces located 
outside the secured storage area. Pursuant to Section 16-3-235 of the Land Use Code, sidewalks 
are normally required along street frontages, but because Center Drive currently lacks sidewalks 
on adjacent properties, staff agreed to defer construction until full street improvements occur, 
with the timing and installation to be addressed through a Site Development Agreement between 
the Town and the developer. The application was duly sent out for agency referral in a manner 
Page 6 of 24
consistent with Section 16-6-370 of the LUC, with the exception of the deferred sidewalk. 
Abdul Barzak of Ripley Design, representing the applicant as land planner, presented the project 
details and history, noting that an administrative replant (Replat D) was part of the process 
following the original 2000 plat and 2003 replats to clear the path for development. He 
emphasized that the facility serves as a gateway to Milliken, featuring decorative cedar-and-iron 
fencing compliant with Section 16-3-390, a high-quality aesthetic frontage along Highway 60, 
shorter buildings (1 through 5) paired with larger RV storage buildings (6 and 7), and durable 
metal construction utilizing a modern burnt-wood and charcoal color palette. 
During the Commission inquiry, Commissioner Baugh asked whether the units would be 
temperature-controlled, to which Mr. Barzak responded that while they are not heated or air-
conditioned, they fully meet building code and insulation requirements. Chair Helzer inquired 
about the peak roof height, which was clarified as ranging from 8.5 feet for smaller units up to 
20 feet, and asked about potential future disposal stations. Mr. Koelbel and staff noted that while 
gray water stubs could be accommodated in the future through close coordination with property 
owners and Water/Wastewater Compliance to ensure separation, any future modifications 
involving oil and sand separators would require a site plan amendment, though water and sewer 
services are already present. Chair Helzer also questioned utility clearance issues, which staff 
confirmed could be managed without a site plan modification provided no major grading 
modifications are made. Finally, Commissioner Fior inquired about emergency protocols for a 
fully automated facility, and the applicant confirmed that tenant lease agreements, posted 
emergency instructions, and coordination with Front Range Fire Rescue (FRFR) ensure life-
safety compliance, alongside hydrants in the area and posted emergency contact numbers. 
Hearing no further testimony, Chair Helzer closed the Public Hearing at 6:29 P.M. 
 
Commissioner Kassidee Fior motioned to recommend approval of the Milliken Store-N-Go Site 
Plan to the Town Board of Trustees.  Commissioner Bernadette Stephenson seconded. 
 
Voting Yes: Randy Helzer, Jimmy Baugh, Ron Blackmer, Bernadette Stephenson, Kassidee 
Fior, Mary Schneider 
Voting No: None 
Motion passed 6-0.

PURPOSE

Sunfield FIling 3 and make a recommendation to the Milliken Board of Trustees. 
BACKGROUND 
The applicant is requesting approval of a Preliminary Plat to subdivide approximately 
11.3 acres into 36 single-family residential lots. The proposed lots have an average size 
of approximately 8,224 square feet, resulting in a density of 3.19 dwelling units per acre, 
consistent with the low-density residential designation established through the 
Centennial Crossing PUD. The proposal also includes 2 tracts for open space totaling 3 
acres or 26% of the site which meets the required 15% for PUD subdivisions.  
 
The Sunfield Subdivision was originally approved by the Town of Milliken in 2019 as 
part of the Centennial Crossing Planned Unit Development (PUD). The original 
subdivision included Tracts D and E, which were designated for future residential 
development. Sunfield Filing No. 3 represents the final phase of the previously 
approved development. The proposed development is located generally between 
Moonflower Lane to the north and the Little Thompson Ditch to the south. The 
Preliminary Plat maintains the previously approved subdivision layout, with extensions

Project Information 
Sunfield Subdivision was originally platted and approved by the Town of Milliken in 2019, with Tract D and Tract 
E set aside for future residential development. Tract D is bounded by Moonflower Lane on the north, 
Pennycress Drive to the west, Lotus Drive to the south and private property to the east. Just south of Lotus Drive 
begins Tract E, with previously approved Sunfield lots directly to the west, The Little Thompson Ditch to the 
south, and private property to the east.  
 
The Sunfield Filing 3 Preliminary Plat submittal is proposing to subdivide these tracts into 36 residential 
detached lots with an average lots size of 8,224 S.F, encompassing approximately 11.3 acres. These lots will be 
known as Block 1, Lots 11-18; Block 2, Lots 1-9; Block 3, Lots 1-9. At 3.19 dwelling units per acre, this aligns with 
the low-density residential use as called out in the original Centennial Crossing PUD and maintains the density 
between 3-5 dwelling units per acre. As part of filing 3, Tract L and Tract M are being platted to align with the 
originally approved open space calculations with no change to the amount previously considered. As such, Tract 
L and Tract M are both designated for open space and easements. 
 
The original plans and supporting reports prepared for Sunfield Filing 1 included the area now identified as 
Filing 3. As a result, this submittal does not include any substantial changes to the overall design previously 
reviewed and approved by the Town as part of the PUD and original subdivision. This includes, but is not limited 
to, the proposed land use, drainage, traffic, overall roadway network, and open space framework. In 
coordination with Town staff, updated drainage letters, traffic memoranda, and construction plans have been 
included with this submittal to reflect the current filing and support the proposed improvements.  The only 
notable modification incorporated with this submittal was made in coordination with, and at the request of, 
Town staff. East Lotus Drive has been extended to the east and stubbed to accommodate a potential future 
access connection to the adjacent property. A landscape plan has also been provided illustrating the proposed 
landscaping within Tracts L and M. Tract L is proposed to include native seeding and trees to create a natural 
transition to the Little Thompson Ditch to the south. Tract M is proposed to be seeded.  
 
Sunfield Filing 3 completes the final phase of the approved Sunfield community and includes the materials 
necessary to support the proposed development in accordance with the Town of Milliken Municipal Code , 
applicable development standards, and Town adopted plans and policy documents. Our team looks forward to 
working collaboratively with staff throughout the review process.

Housing Needs Assessment (2025) 
The proposed Sunfield Filing 3 Preliminary Plat is consistent with the findings of the Town of Milliken 
Housing Needs Assessment, which identifies a continued need for additional housing as the community 
grows. The assessment concludes that "Milliken faces a growing need for housing, with demand 
projected to rise by 5% to 12% over the next decade" (p. 3). The addition of 36 detached single-family 
homes contributes to meeting this projected demand by expanding the Town's housing inventory 
within an area already planned for residential development. 
 
The assessment also highlights the pace of community growth, noting that "nearly three-quarters of 
residents have moved to town within the past 15 years, indicating strong in-migration and ongoing 
growth" (p. 3). As Milliken continues to attract new residents, additional housing opportunities will be 
needed to accommodate that growth, and Sunfield Filing 3 represents an incremental addition to the 
Town's housing supply. 
 
Further supporting the need for new housing, the report finds that Milliken's housing vacancy rate was 
below the typical healthy vacancy rate, so adding new residential lots can help address this constrained 
housing market and provide additional opportunities for future residents. 
 
The Housing Needs Assessment also emphasizes balancing growth with Milliken's identity, which the 
report describes as a growing community with a small-town residential character. It notes that the 
Town has "steadily evolved into a residential community while maintaining ties to its rural heritage" (p. 
6). Sunfield Filing 3 supports these objectives by providing additional single-family housing 
opportunities and preserving the envisioned community character and open space. 
 
Overall, the preliminary plat submittal supports the Housing Needs Assessment's findings regarding 
continued housing demand and population growth while furthering the Town's goal of 
accommodating growth in a manner consistent with Milliken's established character .
