Program Stats & Overview September & October 2026: 
Adult Services & Programs 
For September, Adult Services featured two programs regarding financial matters, and the 
screening of a film with local interest. The Elevate Your Finances program, presented by 
our partners from the Elevations Credit Union, was attended by 7 engaged community 
members, and Retirement Ready (in partnership with AIS Medicare & More) was also 
attended by 7 folks who brought lots of questions and curiosity. The film screening, Half-
Life of Memory, was shown to our Friday Afternoon Club crowd of 12 audience members. 
Half-Life of Memory was shown here in response to community comments about wanting a 
second showing sometime (the Historical Museum screened the film in 2025). Just like last 
year's presentation, we were able to partner with the filmmaker himself, Jeff Gipe, and 
arrange for him to join us via Zoom for a live, 30-minute Q & A with the audience at the end 
of the showing.  
October was a very busy month in Adult Services, with many exciting offerings and 
engagement opportunities. Our outreach table at the Louisville Senior Wellness and 
Resource Fair on October 2nd was quite popular, and we were able to interact with 147 
community members. We also gave away 21 Fall Gnome Take and Make kits (while 
supplies lasted) at that event. For our Friday Afternoon Club fans, we gave out an 
additional 33 Fall Gnome Take and Make kits, leading up to the Library closure for staff 
training on the typical Friday Afternoon Club meeting date. Our outreach efforts continued 
in October at the Balfour Senior Living facility in Louisville, and at the two locations stops 
we made one new library card and gave assistance to 4 seniors during the visit.  
October also featured three very well-attended programs for adults. The presentation, 
Exploring Bhutan: Land of Gross National Happiness, in partnership with professor of 
psychology emeritus and author Janet Schofield, enjoyed an audience of 27. The Medica re 
Changes program, also in partnership with AIS Medicare & More, had 26 highly engaged 
folks in attendance. Finally, our Ballot Issues event, hosted by the League of Women 
Voters, saw a very full crowd of 36 folks attending. 
Youth & Teen Services & Programs 
In September in October, the Youth Services Department eased back into the school year 
with our reduced programming schedule, offering one Family and Toddler Storytime rather 
than two each week. The extra time allowed staff to prep for bigger events, decorate our 
spaces for the season, and make sure service desks were adequately supported while we 
worked to hire and train new staff.

In Teen, the Tutoring Program opened after Labor Day and is already nearly at capacity. 
Jessica coordinated 21 tutoring sessions in September, and 27 in October. Anime Club 
continues to be a major draw, bringing in 23 teens from Boulder County and beyond these 
past two months. The Teen Volunteer Council also began its 2026/2027 term with 17 active 
members, some new and some returning. They elected officers and committee leadership, 
and each member chose a program-planning committee to join. The group’s first big 
project is planning our annual Stuffie Adoption Fair which will take place in November. One 
of our YS Techs, Melissa, set up multiple passive activities in the Hollow. Twenty-two teens 
completed her Jar Lid Magnet craft in October. 
Children’s Staff hosted over 700 children and caregivers for September Storytimes, and 
500 in October. Our new Youth Services Associate, Rachel, began doing our Superior 
Storytimes in October and hosted 51 people at the Civic Space while the Community 
Center was under repair. Deb ran 7 unique programs for school-aged kids, receiving some 
excellent feedback on her Gravity Lab program in particular. Her monthly Tween Club 
programs have been bigger than ever this school year, drawing 25 kids in September and 
15 in October. The Fall session of Reading Buddies has been very successful with 
Lindsay’s new scheduling format which allows Little Buddies to sign up weekly rather than 
commit to every session. Everyone seems to appreciate the flexibility this format offers. 
Angie was hard at work all month preparing for our annual Halloween Spooktacular which 
brought in 100 kids and caregivers. Many staff members stepped up to help with this event, 
both helping us prep decorations, and handing out candy throughout the morning.

Program Stats & Overview July & August 2026: 
For July and August, Adult Services featured some exciting collaborations with both the 
Louisville Senior Center, and Boulder Open Space. The Louisville Senior Center hosted 
an afternoon of board games with the Friday Afternoon Club crowd in July, and for the 
first time, the Friday Afternoon Club was held in Superior, at the Superior Civic Space, 
where our friends from Boulder Open Space gave a presentation on dragonflies and 
damsels in August.  
 
The collaborations did not end there, however, as Adult Services and Teen Services 
partnered on Scrapbook Journal Take and Makes in July, where 50 kits were picked up 
by our cross-generation community of creatives. Earlier in that month, Adult Services 
celebrated the 4th of July with 50 patriotic windsock Take and Makes (picture attached) 
for our patrons. Adult Services also continued its always-popular Active Minds series in 
August with a United States Semiquincentennial-relevant presentation on Colorado's 
journey to statehood with 20 virtual attendees. Furthermore, August also featured the 
ongoing Library Outreach initiative with the Balfour Senior Living facility, where library 
staff visit two locations on their campus to make library cards, answer questions, and 
set folks up with Overdrive services.

Library Brief: Community Survey Findings
Oct 28, 2026  ·  @Brandi Cummings
For the Library Board of Trustees and the Louisville Public Library Foundation. The Library
is the community's most used and most valued cultural service: 94% rate it excellent or
good, and residents name Library collections among their top investment priorities.
What the community told us
The Library ranks second only to Downtown / Main Street (74%) as a place where
residents feel connected to Louisville.
Where residents see room to grow
Digital access. Library digital services and technology were named in the top five
areas for added investment by 26% of respondents, and 6% of those citing barriers
find eBooks too cumbersome. Open comments ask for more eBooks and audiobooks
and shorter waitlists.
Hours and spaces. 13% of those citing barriers say hours do not fit their schedule.
Comments also ask for longer hours and more meeting rooms.

Teens and families. 32% of households with children want more teen programming,

Superior. Superior respondents rate the Library excellent less often (65%) than all

Investment priorities
Library collections are among the areas least often named as least beneficial (5%,
