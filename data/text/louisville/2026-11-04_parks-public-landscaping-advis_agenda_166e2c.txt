Memorandum 
 
TO: Adam Blackmore, Parks, Recreation and Open Space Director, and the Louisville PROS 
Team 
 
FROM: Parks and Public Landscaping Advisory Board 
 
DATE: November 4, 2026 
 
SUBJECT: Commendation on Achieving National CAPRA Accreditation 
 
On behalf of the Parks and Public Landscaping Advisory Board, we extend our heartfelt 
congratulations to the entire Parks, Recreation, and Open Space (PROS) department on 
earning national accreditation through the National Recreation and Park Association’s 
Commission for Accreditation of Park and Recreation Agencies (CAPRA). 
 
Earning CAPRA accreditation is a remarkable distinction achieved by fewer than 3% of parks 
and recreation agencies nationwide. PROS is one of just twelve agencies in Colorado who 
have met this high standard, which requires an extraordinary amount of diligence and hard 
work. 
 
As an advisory board, we have the privilege of seeing firsthand the dedication and passion 
the PROS team brings to stewardship of Louisville’s 34 parks, nearly 2,000 acres of open 
space, and more than 32 miles of trails. This national honor reaffirms what our community 
already knows: Louisville's parks system operates at the highest levels of quality, efficiency, 
safety, and accountability. 
 
Thank you for your tireless work, professionalism, and unwavering commitment to making 
Louisville’s public spaces world-class. We are proud to partner with such an exceptional team 
and celebrate this well-deserved milestone alongside you. 
 
Sincerely, 
 
Parks and Public Landscaping Advisory Board 
 
City of Louisville, Colorado

Memorandum 
To: Louisville City C ouncil 
From: PPLAB  
Date: November 4, 2026 
PPLAB and the Louisville Bee City USA subcommittee request  that the Louisville 
City Council prioritize the purchase of pesticide-fr ee plant stock for Public 
Landscaping,
 Parks and Open Space requirements.  A recent webinar produced 
by the Xerces Society for Conservation of Invertebrates highlighted the common 
occurrence of
 multiple pesticides and fungicides in commercially available plant 
stock, including plant material available from big box stores, independent, and 
commercial nurseries. 
Many
 of these pesticides are systemic and are present in plant tissues, pollen 
and nectar
 that can result in exposure of people, pets, and wildlife (including 
pollinators
) to potentially harmful substances.  Non-systemic pesticides that are 
present in potting medium or included soils can leach into surrounding soil and 
groundwater, impacting beneficial soil biota and creating a potential 
contamination hazard.  
Pesticide-free (“organic”) plant stock is currently available from a few local 
commercial and privately operated growers on a limited basis.  Currently, the 
availability
 of pesticide-free plant stock from commercial growers is limited due 
to 
a perceived lack of community demand, but the Xerces webinar emphasized 
the potential
 market impact of requests from municipal customers that could 
result
 in better availability and increased variety of both pesticide-free 
ornamental
 and native plant stock.  We understand that there may be a future 
opportunity for a city-operated greenhouse to supply plants for city use. This 
could offer a significant cost savings for pesticide-free, native plant and  
ornamental plant sources. 
We recognize that this request would require some investment of time and 
resources on the part of City Staff. However, the improvements in continued 
healt
h and safety of the community, wildlife habitat, and the local environment 
would be worth the effort.   
Thank you for your c onsideration.  
Respectfully, 
John Webb, Committee Chair, PPLAB
