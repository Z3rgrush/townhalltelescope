Power Ahead Colorado (PACO) 
Power Ahead Colorado (PACO) is a Denver Regional Council of Governments (DRCOG) initiative funded 
through a $200 million federal Climate Pollution Reduction Grant awarded in 2024. The program aims to 
reduce greenhouse gas emissions and improve air quality by helping residents and businesses make 
their homes and buildings healthier, more comfortable, and more energy efficient through 
electrification and energy-efficiency upgrades.

PACO provides free energy advising services to help residents and businesses evaluate projects, identify 
available incentives, and maximize rebate opportunities. The program also connects participants with 
vetted contractors and provides educational resources on electrification technologies and equipment 
maintenance. In addition, PACO is providing a $1,500 rebate for qualifying cold-climate air-source heat 
pumps, which can be combined with available utility and local incentives. Click here to learn more about 
the eligibility requirements for PACO.

Boulder County Heat Pump Group Buy 
Boulder County and local municipalities are partnering with nonprofit Solar United Neighbors to bring 
you Switch Together, a group buying program for residential heat pumps. 
 
By pooling many projects together, Switch Together lowers the cost of equipment and installation by a 
qualified, competitively selected heat pump installer. For even more savings, this opportunity can be 
stacked with heat pump rebates and incentives from EnergySmart, Xcel Energy, Power Ahead Colorado. 
 
• Register: Sign up for Switch Together at no cost. 
• Contractor Selection: Vetted contractors compete in a bidding process. The most competitive 
bids for high-quality installations determine the final group discount. 
• Personal offer: You will receive a personal offer from Switch Together based on your registration 
details. This offer will include your estimated costs, group savings, and information about the 
selected contractor for your neighborhood. 
• You decide: You have until Nov. 18 to decide if you want to accept the offer. There is no 
obligation to accept. To secure your spot, you’ll be asked to place a $150 deposit, which is fully 
refundable if you choose not to proceed. 
• Installation: If you accept, the winning contractor will contact you to schedule an initial 
consultation. If your home is found to be unsuitable, the installation will be canceled, and your 
deposit refunded.

Heat Pumps 101
One system can provide heating and cooling!
• Heat pumps move heat rather than create it. In the winter, they pull heat from outdoor air into the home; 
in the summer, they reverse the process to provide cooling.
Cold-climate Air-Source Heat Pumps (ccASHP)
• Designed to operate efficiently at low outdoor temperatures. Considerations include:
⚬ Proper sizing, over or undersized equipment can increase costs and reduce performance
⚬ Ducted vs. ductless, depending on home’s existing ductwork
• Uses advanced compressor technology to keep extracting heat from outdoor air even in extreme winter 
conditions. 
⚬ Use a refrigerant as fluid to transfer heat between indoor and outdoor spaces

Best Candidates for Electrification
For retrofits, the best candidates for electrification are:
• Buildings replacing AC/furnace systems and equipment that has reached 
end-of-life
• Buildings adding cooling
• Efficiency upgrades paired with weatherization 
• Buildings with inconsistent temperatures/comfort issues
• Buildings interested in improving indoor air quality

Memorandum 
 
To: Louisville City Council Members 
From: Louisville Sustainability Advisory Board 
Date: November 19, 2025 
Re: Louisville Sustainability Advisory Board Legislative Agenda for 2026 
 
Dear Louisville City Council Members, 
 
As the City Council begins to set legislative priorities for 2026, the Louisville Sustainability 
Advisory Board respectfully submits this legislative agenda for your consideration. Our 
recommendations are grounded in the City’s Sustainability Action Plan and aim to support the 
Council adopted Climate Action Goals, reflecting the shared commitment to a sustainable, 
resilient, and equitable future for our community. 
 
We believe that sustainability and resiliency should remain a central lens through which all 
major, long-term City decisions are evaluated. From infrastructure investments to housing, 
energy, and economic development, policies should prioritize outcomes that build resilience, 
reduce emissions, and support community well-being—especially for those most affected by 
climate change. 
 
Louisville has already demonstrated strong leadership in this work through collaboration with 
regional partners such as the Denver Regional Council of Governments (DRCOG), sharing 
knowledge and aligning strategies to advance sustainability goals. The City’s efforts to integrate 
sustainability into both the legislative agenda, Comprehensive Plan, and City Council Work Plan  
further underscore its dedication to environmental stewardship. Building on this momentum, the 
Board urges City Council to continue placing sustainability and resiliency at the center of 
decision-making, ensuring that every action taken today contributes to a thriving and climate-
resilient future. 
 
Vision for 2026 and Beyond 
 
Louisville continues to face the complex challenges of climate change, resource management, 
and equitable growth. To meet these challenges, we urge the City to: 
 
● Continue prioritizing sustainability and resiliency as core criteria in all significant, long-
term policy and financial decisions. 
● Advance the City’s transition to renewable energy and reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) 
emissions through data-driven decision-making that focuses on the highest-impact 
strategies. 
● Support communities disproportionately impacted by climate change through targeted 
adaptation strategies, financial incentives, and inclusive engagement. 
 
Page 20 of 22
These priorities reflect our belief that sustainable practices are not only environmentally 
necessary but also economically and socially beneficial. 
 
Recommended Legislative Tasks for 2026 
 
1. Integrate a Sustainability and Resilience Evaluation Framework into Major City 
Decisions 
Formalize a decision-making framework requiring that all major capital projects, infrastructure 
investments, and policy initiatives include an evaluation of sustainability, resilience, and climate 
equity impacts. Embedding this framework into City processes ensures that sustainability and 
resilience considerations are consistently applied across all departments, fostering 
accountability and long-term planning. 
 
2. Advance Renewable Energy Adoption and Data-Driven Emission Reduction 
Accelerate Louisville’s transition to renewable energy and ensure that greenhouse gas (GHG) 
reduction strategies are guided by accurate, data-driven analysis to achieve the greatest 
possible impact. By grounding decisions in emissions data and cost-benefit evaluation, the City 
can effectively progress toward its goals of reducing emissions by 60% by 2030 and achieving 
carbon neutrality by 2050. This includes continued investment in tools that enable staff to 
efficiently collect, analyze, and interpret emissions data, empowering them to make informed, 
strategic decisions about where to target reductions and how to expand renewable energy 
projects and initiatives most effectively. 
 
3. Strengthen Community Resilience and Climate Equity 
Enhance Louisville’s climate adaptation and disaster recovery planning to better support 
residents and neighborhoods most vulnerable to environmental change. Recognizing that 
climate impacts disproportionately affect low-income households, seniors, renters, and those 
lacking resilient housing or transportation, the City should prioritize equitable access to 
sustainability investments. Key actions include expanding energy-efficiency and home resilience 
programs for income-qualified residents, partnering with community organizations to improve 
heat mitigation, air quality, and emergency preparedness, and pursuing state and federal 
funding for local resilience projects that create green jobs and safeguard public health. 
 
The Louisville Sustainability Advisory Board remains committed to supporting the City Council in 
advancing a resilient, equitable, and climate-conscious future. By continuing to integrate 
sustainability and resilience into every major City decision, we can safeguard our community’s 
long-term well-being, strengthen economic stability, and position Louisville as a regional leader 
in sustainability innovation. 
 
We thank the Council for its continued partnership and look forward to collaborating on the 2026 
legislative agenda to ensure that sustainability remains at the heart of Louisville’s vision for the 
future. 
 
Sincerely,
