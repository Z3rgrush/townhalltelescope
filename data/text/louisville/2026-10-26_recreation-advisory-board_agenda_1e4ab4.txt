Kathy: We are asking the RAB for input on these proposed fees (passes 
and admission but not programming). We have comparisons with other 
local governments. Increased costs are driven in part by chemicals for 
water treatment and fertilizer for the CCGC. 
 
Jesse: from our comparisons, Boulder’s fees are the highest, Louisville is 
second highest, and Lafayette is lowest. Louisville has the second highest 
participation rates and the highest non-resident daily rates. Louisville is 
also second for the most resident pass memberships.  
 
Kathy summarized various pricing options for proposed fee increases. 
Program revenues and attendance has increased from last year. We were 
able to increase membership-based revenue compared to punch card 
revenue from last year as well. 
 
Lisa: do you have a recommendation for which option? 
 
Kathy: yes, a proposed 3% increase in fees overall considering a CPI at 
3.9%. 
 
Jesse summarized how our proposed fee increases compared to other local 
government. Broomfield is the only city proposing increase in fees after 
five years of no increases. He reminded us that senior services are 
subsidized so this cohort would not be impacted by these increases 
including non-residents.  
 
Michele: I can support a 3% increase for residents and a bigger increase for 
non-residents recognizing that many non-residents are subsidized seniors. 
 
Kathy: we are considering only offering New Years Day sales to residents. 
Before we decide, we can look into data to see if the increased demand 
from this popular event is coming from residents vs. non-residents. 
 
Lisa: I think we should consider another 7% increase for non-residents like 
last year. Non-residents include those in Superior which has tried 
unsuccessfully to pass a bond to fund their Community Center expansion. 
 
The RAB moved to approve 3% and 7% increases for residents and non-
residents respectively  
 
Motion: Lisa Norgard 
Second: Michele Van Pelt

B. General Updates — Kathy Martin 
We are reviewing comments on the PROS Long Range Plan. Resulting 
revisions will be given to City Council for approval by the end of October. 
Pickleball Court Update: Approved modifications of final design are 
finished, and we expect construction to start this fall with no need for 
additional funds. 
Bocce Courts at Memory Square: these have been improved for 
tournaments and can be adequately maintained. No new CIP is planned for 
this year.  
Rec Center playground and shade shelter improvements continue. 
Jesse and Kathy and other staff are attending the NRPA conference and 
will see if we continue to maintain our CAPRA re-certification for PROS.  
Jesse summarized data for weekend usage. We have more visits during 
weekdays when we have expanded hours (see links below) vs. weekends. 
We are considering surveying users to inform where we should adjust or 
increase hours. After-hour pool rentals are a very popular event and bring 
in up to $15,000 in revenue on Saturdays. We believe we could absorb 
increased demand if we expand Saturday hours with our existing staff.
