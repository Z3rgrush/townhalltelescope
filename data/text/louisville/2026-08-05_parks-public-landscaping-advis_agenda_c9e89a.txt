VII. Board Updates 
• Bee City – John attend webinar recently. Recommendation of this 
webinar is that the city asks providers for pesticide-free plants. If 
more than one city cooperates, there could be bulk marketing and 
bulk pricing, making it easier for suppliers to provide pesticide-free 
plants. Once pesticides are brought in with acquired plants, 
pesticides can remain in the system. High Plains Environmental 
Center has a large nursery that provides pesticide-free native plants. 
• How does this fit in with how the city is purchasing plants currently? 
Staff replied that this is consistent with current practice, but PPLAB 
can also make a recommendation to the City that Louisville pursue 
pesticide-free plants for landscaping.  
 
 Anticipating Drought Habitat Support 
• Louisville has a Drought Management Plan – The document drafted by 
John and Kristine Peterson of Wild Ones Front Range chapter will be 
useful to review alongside the existing Management Plan.

• PPLAB has interest in updating the flower beds at Community Park, 
and the use of the Arboretum as a demonstration garden of native 
planting opportunities and pollinator plants. 
• Council mentioned interest in beautification and maintaining tree 
cover. 
• Council suggested that PPLAB revisit the budget and make sure the 
PPLAB agrees with current priorities. There is also a suggestion to 
advise on how to use volunteers to assist with maintenance. Should 
Council be considering adding a Volunteer Coordinator position for 
OSAB and PPLAB? 
• Review of the May 18 Finance committee meeting. Parks budget is 
being supplemented by one-time funds.  
• Currently increase in cost of services is outpacing increased revenue. 
Additionally, the recent Open Space Sales Tax vote has reconfigured 
how funds are divided among Parks and Open Space.  
• Is there potential to work with other groups, like cultural groups, to 
add signage or ‘entry features’ to promote Louisville at our parks, etc. 
There are several entrances to the city that do not announce 
‘Louisville’. What could a program look like that uses design 
resources from local Universities or High Schools to aid in design, 
etc.? PPLAB has contributed to the process by recommending areas 
at city entrances where welcoming signage would work.  
• Sundance Film Festival is coming up soon (January) – an art 
competition could be used to get design ideas, but this would have to 
happen relatively quickly (before the school year starts). City 
manager notes this might not be achievable for 2027, and art is not a 
PPLAB issue. 
• Council had questions about Bee City, specifically should this be a 
joint sub-committee of OSAB and PPLAB? Bee City group has 
started to discuss the relationship. 
• PPLAB requested the opportunity to be involved at the 30% Design 
Phase for the playground re-design for the Community Park 
playground – specifically ideas for improving shade structures.  
• Integrated Pest Management is slated for review in 2027; it will come 
to PPLAB for review once it is at a further stage of development. It is 
budgeted for 2027 as a capital-level consultant project.
