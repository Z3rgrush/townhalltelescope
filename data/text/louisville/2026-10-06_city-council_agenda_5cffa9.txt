Prepared By: Melinda Culley, Deputy City Attorney

Summary:  
The Colorado Fair Campaign Practices Act allows City Council to adopt a resolution 
supporting or opposing a ballot question. City Council has indicated it wishes to endorse 
Ballot Question 2B and the support resolution is attached. 
 
The referred measure will appear on the ballot as question 2B as follows: 
 
SHALL CITY OF LOUISVILLE TAXES BE INCREASED $1.5 MILLION IN 2029 AND 
THEN ANNUALLY THEREAFTER BY WHATEVER AMOUNTS ARE GENERATED 
FROM THE CONTINUATION OF THE CITY'S HISTORIC PRESERVATION SALES 
TAX OF 0.125% BEGINNING JANUARY 1, 2029, AND EXPIRING DECEMBER 31, 
2038; WITH THE NET PROCEEDS OF SUCH SALES TAX, ALONG WITH THE CITY'S 
EXISTING 0.125% HISTORIC PRESERVATION USE TAX, TO BE UTILIZED FOR 
HISTORIC PRESERVATION PURPOSES AS ESTABLISHED BY BALLOT ISSUES 
APPROVED BY THE VOTERS IN 2008 AND 2017, AND, COMMENCING ON 
JANUARY 1, 2027, SHALL THE CITY ALSO BE AUTHORIZED TO: (1) EXPEND ANY 
FUNDS FROM SUCH HISTORIC PRESERVATION TAX ON CITY PROGRAMS AND 
CAPITAL PROJECTS THAT PROMOTE AND PRESERVE THE CITY'S HISTORY AND 
CULTURAL HERITAGE, AND (2) INCREASE THE PORTION OF SUCH TAX 
ALLOCATED TO OPERATING AND MAINTAINING THE LOUISVILLE MUSEUM 
CAMPUS FROM 20% TO 30% PER YEAR; AND SHALL THE CITY BE PERMITTED 
TO COLLECT, RETAIN AND EXPEND ALL REVENUES DERIVED FROM SUCH TAX 
AS A VOTER-APPROVED REVENUE CHANGE AND AN EXCEPTION TO LIMITS 
WHICH WOULD OTHERWISE APPLY UNDER ARTICLE X, SECTION 20 OF THE 
COLORADO CONSTITUTION OR ANY OTHER LAW?

A RESOLUTION OF THE LOUISVILLE CITY COUNCIL URGING LOUISVILLE 
COMMUNITY MEMBERS TO VOTE YES ON BALLOT ISSUE 2B AT THE 
NOVEMBER 3, 2026 ELECTION CONCERNING THE CITY’S HISTORIC 
PRESERVATION TAX  
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to voter authorization in 2008, 2009 and 2017, the City 
adopted a temporary 0.125% sales tax and a permanent 0.125% use tax to be used 
exclusively for historic preservation purposes; and  
 
WHEREAS, the temporary 0.125% sales tax will expire on December 31, 2028 
unless the City’s voters approve a continuation of the tax; and  
 
WHEREAS, the historic preservation tax equates to one cent on every $10.00 
spent and generates approximately $900,000 in annual revenue; and   
 
WHEREAS, revenue from the historic preservation tax is the main source of 
revenue for the City’s landmark and historic preservation program, which has designated 
70 properties as landmarks since 2005; awarded $3.8 million in historic preservation 
grants to 40 unique recipients since 2016; and provided over $405,000 in pre-landmarking 
assessments to 73 properties; and  
 
WHEREAS, the historic preservation tax has also provided $1.1 million in funding 
for the Louisville Historical Museum since 2019, including for operation, maintenance and 
exhibits; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Louisville Historical Museum and the historic preservation grant 
programs serve an important public purpose of  preserving and enhancing the unique 
character and charm of Louisville; and  
 
WHEREAS, Old Town Louisville and the Historical Museum attract tourists who 
support local businesses and enhance the City’s economic vitality; and  
 
WHEREAS, if voters choose not to continue the temporary 0.125% sales tax, the 
Louisville Historical Museum will be required to reduce its hours of operation and services 
and the City will have to significantly reduce its historic preservation grant program; and  
 
WHEREAS, the City organized a Historic Preservation Task Force, comprised of 
City residents, business owners and Councilmembers, which recommended the City 
Council refer a ballot issue to voters at the November 3, 2026 election for the purpose of 
extending the historic preservation sales tax, increasing the allocation for the Louisville 
Historical Museum, and allowing the tax to be used for additional purposes; and

WHEREAS, for these reasons, the City Council has referred to the voters Ballot 
Issue 2B, which will continue the temporary 0.125% historic preservation sales tax for an 
additional ten-year period beginning on January 1, 2029; and  
 
WHEREAS, Ballot Issue 2B, if approved, will increase the portion of the tax 
allocated to the Louisville Historical Museum from 20% to 30% per year and will allow 
historic preservation funds to be used for programs and capital projects that promote and 
preserve the City’s history and cultural heritage; and  
 
WHEREAS, City Council finds it is in the best interest of the City and its community 
members that Ballot Issue 2B be approved; and  
 
WHEREAS, state law allows the City Council to adopt this resolution as a 
statement of City Council’s position of support for the adoption of Ballot Issue 2B.  
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY 
OF LOUISVILLE, COLORADO, that the City Council strongly supports the approval of 
Ballot Issue 2B, which will continue the City’s 0.125% historic preservation sales tax, will 
increase the portion of the historic preservation tax allocated to the Louisville Historical 
Museum from 20% to 30% per year, and will allow historic preservation funds to be used 
for programs and capital projects that promote and preserve the City’s history and cultural 
heritage. Therefore, the City Council urges Louisville residents to vote YES on Ballot 
Issue 2B at the November 3, 2026 election.

Subject:  Resolution No. 87, Series 2026 — A Resolution Approving an 
Agreement for the Deputy Municipal Judge Services and 
Establishing Compensation Therefor 
Date: October 6, 2026 
Prepared By: Genny Kline, City Clerk 
Presented By: Genny Kline, City Clerk 
 
Summary:  
Currently, the Louisville Municipal Court does not have a contract for Deputy Judge 
Services. This contract will ensure that Municipal Court services can proceed if the 
Municipal Judge is unavailable.  
 
Additionally, Resolution No. 87 will set the Deputy Judge's hourly compensation rate at 
$150.00 / hour. This is a $10.00 an hour increase from Resolution No. 59, Series 2019, 
which set the compensation at $140.00 / hour. The City only pays the Deputy City 
Judge when filling in for the Municipal Judge. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
Judge Quevli (formerly Moore) has previously served as a Deputy Judge for the 
Louisville Municipal Court. She is well qualified (a former Boulder County Judge) and is 
familiar with Louisville's Municipal Court processes and personnel. 
 
Unfortunately, her contract with the City expired in December 2021 and was not 
renewed. That oversight was recently discovered, and this item works to correct that 
oversight. 
 
Development Proposal: 
N/A 
 
Analysis: 
Contracting with a Deputy Judge on an as-needed basis ensures that Municipal Court 
functions can continue in the absence of the Municipal Judge. This provides certainty 
for defendants and City Court staff. 
 
Council Work Plan: 
This item supports Core Services as it is imperative to have and retain a qualified and 
capable deputy judge to preside over the City's municipal court in the event the 
Municipal Judge is unavailable.

Fiscal Impact: 
Estimated to be less than $2000 / year. 
 
Alternatives: 
Council may choose not to contract with Judge Quevli and instruct staff to follow a 
different direction for Deputy Judge services. 
 
Recommendation: 
Approve Resolution No. 87, Series 2026 and the attached contract for Deputy Judge 
services.

Subject:  Gateway Open Space Property 
Date: October 6, 2026 
Prepared By: Adam Blackmore, Parks, Recreation and Open Space Director 
Presented By: Adam Blackmore, Parks, Recreation and Open Space Director 
Diana Langley, City Manager 
 
Summary:  
In collaboration with partners from Boulder County Open Space & Mountain Parks 
“BCOS-MP”, City/County of Broomfield “Broomfield” and the City of Lafayette 
“Lafayette”; City staff propose a financial contribution towards purchase of 72.2 Acres 
located within unincorporated Boulder County generally at the Southeast corner of 96th 
St. & Dillon Rd., referred to as the proposed Jointly Owned Gateway Open Space. The 
financial contribution of six-hundred seventy-five thousand dollars ($675,000) or 25% of 
the two-million seven-hundred thousand ($2,700,000) purchase price is outlined in the 
attached Intergovernmental Agreement which also includes terms related to land & 
property management, fee & revenue collection, development restrictions, water rights 
& future infrastructure/trail easement collaboration(s). Also included is a separate but 
related Deed of Conservation Easement that the City of Louisville would receive equal 
share for the property with Broomfield & Lafayette in addition to BCOS-MP.  
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
In 1999, the Southeast Boulder County, South 9th Street, Dillon Road, and US 287 Area 
Comprehensive Development Plan Intergovernmental Agreement was executed 
between Louisville, Lafayette, Broomfield, and Boulder County to regulate the use of the 
lands within the Plan Area through joint adoption of a mutually binding and enforceable 
comprehensive development plan.  
 
In 2007, the City executed an amendment to the Southeast Boulder County, South 96th 
Street, Dillon Road and US 287 Area Comprehensive Development Plan 
Intergovermental Agreement with Lafayette, Broomfield, and Boulder County for the 
Hoyle Property, which is located on Dillon Road in the Colorado Technology Center. In 
exchange for the Hoyle Property to change land use designations from Rural 
Preservation to Industrial, the property owners agreed to mitigate the impact of such 
change through the establishment of a funding mechanism to finance the acquisition of 
additional open space, acquired in fee or by perpetual conservation easement, in the 
vicinity of the Plan Area. As the 33.78-acre parcel developed, funds were deposited in 
the amount of $14,000 per acre for each developable acre. In total, the City collected 
$615,368.42 through the development of the Hoyle property.  
Page 18 of 341
 
In 2025, a 72.2-acre property located at the south-east corner of 96th Street and Dillon 
Road with an address of 1716 S 96th Street was listed for sale, along with 69.99 shares 
of the Goodhue Ditch and Reservoir Company for $2,700,000. The property is bounded 
by Louisville to the north, jointly owned open space to the east and west, and a 
conservation easement property to the south. The property has long been regarded as 
a high priority acquisition for open space.  
 
The Boulder County Parks and Open Space Advisory Committee named the acquisition 
the "Gateway Property" and recommended the purchase of the property. On August 26, 
2025, Boulder County purchased the Gateway Property, including the water shares for 
$2,700,000.  
 
Development Proposal: 
N/A 
 
Analysis: 
The proposed Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) provides that Louisville will 
contribute $675,000 and Broomfield will contribute $100,000 toward the purchase of the 
property. Lafayette respectfully declined to contribute to the purchase due to the 
property being located further away from its city boundaries. In addition, the IGA 
outlines that Boulder County will grant to Louisville, Broomfield, and Lafayette an 
undivided interest in a perpetual Conservation Easement. After the execution of the 
Conservation Easement, Boulder County will remain the fee simple owner. Boulder 
County will also maintain the property.  
 
Council should be aware that additional parties (City/County of Broomfield & Lafayette) 
to the agreement(s) are also considering the materials in advance of them being 
presented to the Boulder County Commissioners for final approval.   
 
Council Work Plan: 
While the purchase of the Gateway Property was not specifically identified in Council's 
Work Plan, it has long been a high priority acquisition for the Open Space Advisory 
Board and contributes to the open space buffer around Louisville. 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
The City's contribution toward the purchase is $675,000, of which $615,368.42 has 
been collected through the development of the Hoyle property in the CTC. The 
remaining funds, $59,631.58, will come from the Open Space Acquisition Funds.

This Intergovernmental Agreement Concerning the Purchase of Gateway Open Space Property 
(“Agreement”) is made and entered into on this date __________________, 2026, by and 
between the County of Boulder, a body corporate and politic (“County”); the City of Louisville, a 
Colorado home rule municipal corporation (“Louisville”) and the City and County of Broomﬁeld, 
a Colorado municipal corporation and county (“Broomﬁeld”) (collectively, “Parties”). 
RECITALS 
A. On August 26, 2025, County purchased 72.2 acres of open space known as the 
Gateway property for TWO MILLION SIX HUNDRED THIRTY THOUSAND DOLLARS 
($2,630,000.00), including any seller-owned mineral rights, as legally described on Exhibit A and 
generally depicted on Exhibit B attached hereto (“Land”). On October 3, 2025, County 
purchased 69.99 shares of the Goodhue Ditch and Reservoir Company for SEVENTY THOUSAND 
DOLLARS ($70,000.00) (“Water Rights”). The Land and Water Rights are collectively referred to 
herein as the “Property”. Accordingly, County paid a total of TWO MILLION SEVEN HUNDRED 
THOUSAND DOLLARS ($2,700,000.00) for the purchase of the Property.  
 
B. The Parties and the City of Lafayette (“Lafeyette”)(collectively, “SE County IGA 
Parties”), entered into that certain ‘Intergovernmental Agreement Southeast Boulder County, 
South 96th Street, Dillon Road, and US 287 Area Comprehensive Development Plan’ dated 
February 18, 1999 and recorded in the Boulder County, Clerk & Recorder’s real estate records, 
on March 15, 1999, at reception 1916399 (“SE County IGA”). The SE County IGA provides that 
the Property is located in a rural preservation area, may be acquired either in fee or by 
perpetual conservation easement for open space purposes by any one or more of the SE County 
IGA Parties, and the terms and conditions of purchase will be determined by the SE County IGA 
Parties at the time of such acquisition. 
 
C. The SE County IGA further states that upon acquisition of any parcels in the rural 
preservation area, the acquiring party will grant the other parties to the SE County IGA an 
undivided interest in a perpetual conservation easement over and across the Property. Because 
County has acquired the fee simple interest in the Property, County will now grant Lafayette, 
Louisville and Broomﬁeld, as the other parties to the SE County IGA, each an undivided interest 
in a perpetual conservation easement over and across the Property restricting the property to 
open space uses (“Conservation Easement”).

Broomﬁeld and Lafayette as the other parties to the SE County IGA about contributing to the

contributing SIX HUNDRED SEVENTY-FIVE THOUSAND DOLLARS ($675,000) and Broomﬁeld 
commits to contributing ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND DOLLARS ($100,000) towards the purchase 
price of the Property. Lafayette respectfully declined to contribute to the purchase price of the 
Property due to its location far from its city boundaries.  
 
E. Both Louisville’s and Broomﬁeld’s ﬁnancial contributions, made as a 
reimbursement to County after its acquisition of the fee simple interest in the Property, total

the Property for a 29% partnership contribution.  
 
F. After the execution of the Conservation Easement, the County will remain fee 
simple owner of the Property and the SE County IGA Partners will each own an undivided 
interest in a conservation easement over the Property. Lafayette will still receive an undivided 
interest in the Conservation Easement, as one of the SE County IGA Partners and under the 
terms of the SE County IGA. 
 
G. The Parties desire to set forth their agreement concerning ﬁnancial contributions 
towards the purchase of the Property, management of the Property, and additional terms and 
conditions.

2. Upon execution of this Agreement, Broomﬁeld will contribute ONE HUNDRED

to County.
