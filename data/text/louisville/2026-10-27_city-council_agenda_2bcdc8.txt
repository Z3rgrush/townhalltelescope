Prepared By: Rafael Gutierrez, Chief 
Presented By: Rafael Gutierrez, Chief 
Benjamin Kurtz  
Scott Moore 
 
Summary:  
This presentation will provide Council with updates on staff’s unified response to 
concerns related to electric motorized conveyances within the City of Louisville, and 
also present information related to current trends. The e-device concern on roadways 
has been noted in recent media reports and many jurisdictions across our State are 
taking action, including the City of Louisville. This update will present information about 
what staff has done over the last several years to address community and Council 
concerns related to e-bikes/e-motos and to discuss potential future strategies that may 
be developed to promote continued traffic safety in the community.   
 
This information will be presented by police command staff and the department’s crime 
prevention specialist. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
The city first began to discuss e-bikes in 2018 when deciding whether to allow 
motorized e-bikes on Louisville trails. At the time, it was estimated that only about 5% of 
bicycles used in Louisville were electric. That number has likely increased significantly 
since.  
 
This topic was revisited when Resolution No.22 Series 2023 was approved in April 2023 
to enter into an Intergovernmental Agreement between the City of Louisville and the 
City of Lafayette to promote e-biking as a primary form of transportation within both 
communities.   
 
In 2024, the police department met twice with council to discuss bicycle safety and e-
bike concerns and to develop a strategy to address what was perceived as a growing 
public safety concern. At that time, data showed there were not a significant number of 
reported or investigated traffic conflicts/accidents involving bicyclists, including e-bikes, 
Page 17 of 41
or pedestrians. In reviewing reported accident data, bicycles were involved in 2.7% of 
incidents, pedestrians were involved in 1.7% of accidents, and 0.134% involved a 
fatality. This led to the belief the concerns were a matter of perspective, but the police 
department command staff believed that as the use of e-bikes was anticipated to grow a 
proactive approach to addressing concerns was necessary. As a result of the 2024 
discussions, the police department proactively launched an educational campaign in 
collaboration with the City’s Parks, Recreation, and Open Space department, the 
Boulder Valley School District, Boulder County Sheriff’s Office, District Attorney, local 
bicycle shops, and community groups to educate consumers and address problematic 
behavior. The initial focus was on education rather than an aggressive enforcement 
approach. The educational campaign’s goal was to promote responsible riding and 
ownership of e-bikes.   
 
In August 2025, Council approved Ordinance No. 1899, Series 2025 and amended titles 
4 and 14 of the Louisville Municipal Code to establish a maximum speed limit on bike 
paths and trails within the City. The 15-mph speed limit aligned our community with our 
neighbors, established a uniform speed for riders, and was intended to promote 
responsible riding on trails and paths.

Analysis: 
Staff reviewed data and gathered information on initiatives that have been developed 
between 2024–2026. Data shows, on average, there are approximately 315 reported 
traffic accidents in the City of Louisville per year. The data indicates there has been a 
15% decrease in reported accidents in our city between 2023 and 2025. For this 
presentation, the department specifically looked at accident data from January 2024 
through June 2026 to understand the involvement of bicycles and pedestrians in these 
accidents. Staff also examined the number of injuries or fatalities reported.   
 
The data showed:  
796 total accidents between January 2024 and June 2026:  
• 2.5% or 20 involved a bicycle, with 10 of those having juvenile involvement (ages 
12-17) with 3 riding e-bikes 
• 1.25%  or 10 involved a pedestrian  
Page 18 of 41
• 2.5% or 20 involved a reported injury or complaint of pain  
• 0.125% or 1 involved a fatality  
 
The percentage of accidents involving the above category of traffic unit remained similar 
to what was observed during a presentation made to Council in 2024 when staff looked 
at the accident data for the years 2019 to 2024.  
• 2.7% involving bicycles 
• 1.7% involving pedestrians  
• 0.134% involving a fatality  
  
Beginning in 2024, the police department placed a strong focus on outreach efforts. 
These efforts continue today. During this time, we have had 22 direct engagements with 
the Boulder Valley School District. These engagements include pop-up tent events, 
hosting a public forum, integrating with physical education classes, coordination 
meetings and discussions with school administrators/school security specialists, 
working with student groups, supporting peer contracts, and countless directed patrols 
around schools. The forums are intended to develop positive relationships with 
students, staff, and parents/guardians and to provide education and cautionary advice 
related to the legal use of e-devices throughout the community. The police department 
also collaborated closely with Open Space Rangers and other City departments, and 
staff has participated in more than 50 events and community engagement opportunities 
over the last two years. These include the trail etiquette program, bike to work day, 
community conversations, touch a truck, street faire, parades, and more. 
 
The department’s social media presence has grown significantly over the last few years 
and 76 posts have been created with specific information related to bicycles, e-bikes, 
pedestrian, and vehicle traffic safety. These posts can reach thousands of followers and 
continue to reinforce our safety-focused message. Collaboration continues with local 
vendors and organizations to educate consumers and riders on making appropriate 
choices and ensuring compliance with the law. Representatives from these businesses 
and organizations have attended many of our events and supported our messaging. To 
date, we have established good partnerships with these groups.  
 
In 2026, the police department transitioned to an enforcement-focused approach to e-
bike and e-moto violations. This was noted in various social media posts to inform the 
community about our shift. While we discovered a vast majority of e-bike riders are 
compliant with legal requirements and ride responsibly, we found the message was not 
universally heard or followed. Officers began to notice a concerning trend of e-motos 
and some other e-devices increasing in the community and operating dangerously on 
our roads. This concern became a primary focus for patrol officers. No longer was it the 
traditional e-bike classification concern nor where e-bikes were being ridden; the 
concern has now shifted to the e-motos and the hazardous riding behavior that is being 
observed.   
Page 19 of 41
 
Between April 5, 2026, and September 30, 2026, the police department initiated a 
Summer Traffic Safety Campaign. This was supported with grant funding from the State 
of Colorado and the Department of Transportation. The department received this 
funding for “Click it or Ticket” seatbelt enforcement, Impaired Driving, and High Visibility 
Enforcement initiatives. During the campaign, officers initiated 2,866 traffic contacts and 
issued 1,154 citations. The patrol division placed an emphasis on enforcement of 
bicycles, e-bikes, and e-motos laws. Special operation days were scheduled, and 
bicycle patrol officers worked the trail systems and roadways to support this 
enforcement initiative. Officers contacted and impounded several illegal e-motos and 
issued citations to both riders and parents. These actions have generated greater 
awareness within our community, and we have received feedback that some parents 
are now selling these e-motos/e-devices to avoid problems. The Louisville Police 
Department and the Boulder County Sheriff’s office have engaged in on-going 
collaboration to plan and develop a unified approach to these concerns. The Boulder 
County District Attorney’s office has also been part of our department’s coordination, 
and they fully support charging of riders and parents/guardians when appropriate.   
 
Staff supports transparency in its operations and developed ways to increase 
community awareness. The police department and the city's information technology 
department collaborated to create a public-facing dashboard. This dashboard provides 
mapping and incident information related to police response and activity, including traffic 
enforcement. The dashboard uses call-for-service data to pin the approximate location 
of a variety of police responses, to include where officers are making traffic enforcement 
contacts. The dashboard photo shows enforcement and contacts have occurred 
throughout the city.   
 
As noted, the more significant e-device safety concern has become e-motos. Officers 
have experienced several instances of riders failing to yield or stop for them when trying 
to initiate an enforcement contact. We have also learned some of these individuals will 
post videos of themselves eluding officers in an attempt to gain notoriety. Officers will 
attempt to make the traffic stop but will not initiate a vehicle pursuit because neither the 
type of contact nor the violation we are stopping them for typically meet pursuit policy 
criteria and are extremely dangerous for the rider and others. The riders’ actions 
endanger themselves and others and can be serious enough to result in felony level 
charges depending on the totality of circumstances. Officers will and have used 
available information to follow up and identify some of these individuals successfully.   
 
The current State of Colorado Model Traffic Code is helpful as a universal guide to e-
bike classification and general allowances. Though it provides a framework, it leaves 
some areas of ambiguity. The foundational law governing e-bikes was developed from 
HB17-1151. Enacted in early 2018, many of today’s e-devices are not specifically 
addressed nor clearly defined. This ambiguity can create variations in how the laws are 
interpreted and enforced. Staff created informational flyers to help educate community 
members about classification, type, and where they were allowed, and much of that 
information came from the State code.  
Page 20 of 41
 
On January 1, 2026, as the result of HB25-1197, sellers of electrical assisted bicycles 
must label the e-bike with the highest class or each class in which it is capable of 
operating. There is also a prohibition to falsely label it for sale as an e-bike if its 
capabilities exceed what is defined. This legislation also clarifies that any modification to 
the e-bikes capabilities could change its overall classification. There has also been 
some discussion about the potential for bills to be presented to the legislature in 2027 
after Children's Hospital of Colorado reported an 184% increase in e-bike related 
injuries to children from 2024 to 2025. The potential bills may involve requiring safety 
equipment, such as helmets, and implementing age restrictions for using an e-bike.  
 
To develop a strategy for the City of Louisville, police staff researched what other 
communities are doing to address concerns. Examples of other communities' initiatives 
include: 
• Castle Rock, CO created a voluntary sticker program to assist officers and others 
in identifying the classification of the bicycle being ridden. 
• Douglas County, CO adopted enhanced penalties for off-highway vehicles and 
for parental responsibility violations. Fines can now range between $20-$1,000. 
For comparison, In Louisville, fines for bicycle violations are $75, with nothing 
specific to e-motos or other devices.  
• Broomfield, CO e-motos are specifically banned throughout the city, while off-
highway vehicles and toy vehicles are better defined in their code.  
• Fort Collins, CO has specific prohibitions against riding e-skateboards, 
hoverboards, one-wheels, and e-unicycles.  
• Salt Lake City, UT now have age restrictions in place requiring helmets for 
anyone under the age of 21 riding an e-device. They also have direct parental 
supervision requirements for any riders between the ages of 8 and 14.  
• State of CO HB26-1125 attempted to address parental responsibility was lost.  
 
In August 2025, the City of Louisville Council approved an amendment to portions of its 
municipal code, specifically § 14.12.010(D)(3) and § 4.04.010(D)(3), to add a 15-mph 
speed limit on city paths and trails. This was an effort to promote responsible riding, 
reduce the risk to pedestrians and others on this shared system, and helped align 
speeds with neighboring jurisdictions. To date, no citation has been written for this 
violation.   
 
During prior meetings between the police department and City Council, other potential 
options were discussed to address e-bike concerns. These included developing a City 
registration and licensing process, prohibitions from riding e-bikes on sidewalks, 
additional engineering efforts or projects, and helmet requirements. None of those 
options were explored in much detail due to concerns with certain young rider's safety, 
city staff capacity and management of a program, and the potential associated costs. 
 
Options for Council Consideration 
Page 21 of 41
Looking ahead, staff is seeking direction or guidance on potential next steps. There are 
a few options that have been discussed recently by the police leadership team and may 
include creating or updating the municipal code, pursuing engineering initiatives, or 
continuing with the current education and enforcement efforts.   
 
Updates to Municipal Code: Proposed code updates or new municipal codes would 
require collaboration between the Police Department, City Attorney, City Prosecutor, 
and Municipal Judge to discuss and develop the code updates. Suggested updates 
could focus on enhancing penalties or fines for illegal use of e-devices, expansion of 
dismount zones, including provisions to address parent or guardian responsibility, 
implementing helmet or age requirements, enhancing definitions, and addressing gaps 
within the State Model Traffic Code. This option will likely involve a months-long process 
to evaluate relevant codes and statues and develop the appropriate language and 
penalties.    
 
Engineering Options: Traffic safety is typically addressed through the lens of Education, 
Enforcement, and Engineering. The proposed next step of additional engineering would 
need to originate with City Traffic Engineers to allow these subject-matter experts to 
determine the best options. From the police department's perspective, options might 
include additional signage, protected lanes, and/or roadway or trail modification. This 
would also require collaboration and coordination between multiple City departments. 
This option will likely involve associated costs and an extended timeline to evaluate 
options, conduct studies, and community engagement.  
 
Continue Current Enhanced Efforts: The third option presented involves maintaining 
current efforts related to engagement, enforcement, and education. Police staff has 
developed great relationships with many community stakeholders and organizations 
and believes positive outcomes have been realized because of all the efforts to date. 
Staff recognizes there is no single approach to traffic safety, yet we remain committed 
to this initiative and will continue to prioritize it within our police operations. 
 
Council Work Plan: 
The information presented is related to the Council Work Plan addressing Safety- Traffic 
Safety 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
There will be a fiscal impact depending on Council's direction. Engineering options will 
likely include projects with the highest associated costs. Staff time and administrative 
costs would be associated with all the options presented. The actual Fiscal impact is 
unknown at this time.

Staff believes the enhanced efforts by multiple departments within the City of Louisville 
organization over the past two years have been largely successful, and the police 
department plans to continue most of these efforts. Staff recommends exploring new or 
updated municipal codes that focus on addressing behavior through enhanced 
penalties, parental accountability provisions, and specific codes better defining 
contemporary e-devices and developing regulations specific to those devices.

PUBLIC ADVISORY ON E-BIKES, E-SCOOTERS, LABELING REQUIREMENTS, AND 
PUBLIC SAFETY 
ISSUED OCTOBER 12, 2026 
E-bikes and e-scooters have become increasingly popular as a form of 
sustainable transportation. Along with this rise, however, the Department of Law 
has received an increasing number of inquiries about vehicles that look like e-bikes 
or e-scooters, and are being advertised as e-bikes or e-scooters, but do not qualify as 
e-bikes or e-scooters under Colorado law. As a result, Colorado consumers are 
purchasing e-bikes or e-scooters that are faster and more powerful than the law 
allows. Often, retailers advertise a vehicle as an e-bike, when it really is a motor 
vehicle that is subject to the same laws and regulations as other motor vehicles.  
The proliferation of these vehicles has corresponded with an increase in the 
number of e-bike, e-scooter, and related injuries to Colorado residents, particularly 
children.1 Studies have drawn a link between significant injury and the use of 
vehicles that exceed speeds of 20 miles per hour.2 Many non-compliant electrically 
propelled bicycles that are being sold as “e-bikes” can travel more than 20 miles per 
hour (for class 1 and 2 e-bikes), or more than 28 miles per hour (for class 3 e-bikes).

E-SCOOTER CLASSIFICATION IN COLORADO 
Under Colorado law, an e-scooter is a device that weighs less than 100 
pounds, is powered by an electric motor, has handlebars, and has a “maximum 
speed of twenty miles per hour on a paved level surface.”12 Colorado law generally 
regulates e-scooters in the same manner as class 1 e-bikes, as neither may exceed 
20 mph as the maximum speed.13 Accordingly, local governments may not impose 
greater restrictions on the operation of e-scooters than they impose on class 1 e-
bikes.14  
E-scooters differ from low-power scooters, which are regulated like motor 
vehicles and require at least a license, registration, and insurance to legally use and 
operate.15 
While the 2025 statute that governs e-bikes does not directly address e-
scooters, existing law likewise precludes retailers from misrepresenting the features 
and characteristics of an e-scooter.16 All material information about the e-scooter 
must be disclosed in any online or in-person advertisement about an e-scooter. 
Consumers who encounter retailers engaged in the misleading sale of e-scooters are 
encouraged to file a complaint with the Attorney General at StopFraudColoado.gov.

stops at 28 miles per hour.17 Sellers of e-bikes must tell customers the maximum 
speed of the e-bike. If a seller is advertising an electrically propelled bicycle that can

the rider is pedaling; the motor ceases at 28 mph. Class 3 electric bicycles must

displayed on either the packaging or documentation of an e-bike or directly on the e-

battery safety standards.20 
All e-Bikes must have fully operable pedals— E-bikes without fully operable

Check the Laws in Your Community— State law allows cities and counties to 
create rules for the use of e-bikes and e-scooters on pedestrian sidewalks and off-
street bike paths. These rules can vary based on the class of e-bike you are riding. 
Please check with your city and county to ensure that you are following the rules for 
riding an e-bike or e-scooter on a path. If your city or county requires your vehicle to 
be used only on roads, do so safely and follow all traffic laws.
