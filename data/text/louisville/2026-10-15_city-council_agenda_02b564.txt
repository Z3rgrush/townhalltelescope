Safety. Safety of principal is the foremost objective of the investment program.

requirements that may be reasonably anticipated.

▪ Energy costs linked to the ongoing war with Iran remain the dominant force shaping the U.S. economic outlook.
Gasoline prices reached a recent high in September, while continued attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz
have kept crude-oil markets on edge. Inflation pressures remain elevated amid deteriorating consumer sentiment,
though consumer spending and manufacturing activity have remained resilient.
▪ Second-quarter U.S. GDP growth was revised higher, yet payroll gains have decelerated and prior months were marked
down. The Federal Reserve responded to above-target inflation with a quarter point rate hike, and Treasury yields
moved higher across the curve.
▪ The Federal Reserve’s Federal Open Market Committee raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis
points to 3.75%–4.00% at its September meeting. The unanimous decision marked the Committee’s first rate increase
since 2023. Chair Kevin Warsh said the move would help return inflation to the 2% objective more promptly, while his
post-meeting remarks struck a hawkish tone that may signal additional tightening ahead.
▪ The Chandler team expects the Federal Reserve to focus monetary policy on price stability, with at least one more 25
basis-point rate hike likely before year end.
▪ The U.S. Treasury curve exhibited a butterfly shift in September, as the front end repriced for a higher-for-longer
neutral rate path, longer-dated yields rose on fiscal concerns, global spillovers, and inflation expectations, while the 5-
year sector increased more than the short and long end amid supply pressure and expanding term premiums.
▪ The 2-year rose +55 basis points to 4.89%, the 10-year rose +54 basis points to 5.29%, while the 5-year gained +59
basis points to 5.09%. The spread between the 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields was at +40 basis points at
September month-end. One year ago, the 2s/10s spread stood at +54 basis points.
▪ The 3-month to 10-year spread widened to +117 basis points from +92 in August, as long maturities absorbed the
repricing of inflation risk while bill yields remained anchored near the policy rate.

OBJECTIVES
Investment Objectives
Safety of principal is the foremost objective of the investment program. The investment
portfolio shall remain sufficiently liquid to meet all operating requirements that may be 
reasonably anticipated. The investment portfolio shall be designed with the objective of 
attaining a market rate of return throughout budgetary and economic cycles, taking into 
account the investment risk constraints and liquidity needs.
Chandler Asset Management Performance Objective
The performance objective for the portfolio is to earn a total rate of return through a market
cycle that is equal to or above the return on the benchmark index.
Strategy
In order to achieve its objective, we will invest the City's funds in a well diversified passive
portfolio comprised primarily of U.S. Treasury and Agency securities. All securities will be in 
compliance with the City's Investment Policies and CRS 24-75-601.

Summary:  
Each year, the City Council establishes specific fees, rates, and charges for the 
upcoming year through a resolution. Additionally, Ordinance 1603, Series 2011, 
authorizes the City Manager to set certain fees, rates, and charges not specifically 
determined by the Council. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
Fees established by the City Manager include charges for photocopies, maps, and 
documents, development application reviews, recreation center programs and classes, 
rental rates for various facilities, and fees for cemetery plots and services. The City 
Manager sets these fees and charges to recover costs and offset operational expenses. 
For 2027, certain fees include a 3.9% adjustment, which is based on the regional 
Denver-Aurora- Lakewood - Consumer Price Index (CPI) for the 12-month period 
ending July 2026. Other adjustments to fees include an inflationary increase 
recommended by an Advisory Board, benchmarking fees to other jurisdictions, fee 
eliminations or administrative corrections.  
Of the total ~299 City Manager Fees listed in Attachment 2, 180 fees (61%) are 
proposed to be adjusted by an inflationary factor or CPI, 104 fees (35%) are to remain 
unchanged from 2026, while an alternative methodology adjusted or eliminated 13 fees 
(4%).

CM Fee Adjustment Methodology –
CPI vs Benchmarking & Other
 Of the approximate ~299 City Manager Fees, Most (61%, ~180) are adjusted by 
an inflationary factor. 
 Certain fees are reviewed by Advisory Boards (such as RAB for the Louisville Senior

 Some fees are below a threshold for which annual adjustments make sense; those
