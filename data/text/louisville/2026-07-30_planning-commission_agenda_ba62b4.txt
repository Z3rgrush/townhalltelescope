To the Louisville Planning Commission and City Council: 
On behalf of the Board of the Louisville Downtown Business Association, I am writing to express 
our support for the Delo Boom development at 1390 Cannon Street and 1301 Courtesy Road. 
This project has been working through the city’s entitlement process for about three years, and 
we believe it should be approved as it moves to the PUD review. 
The project proposal includes 135 various residential units along with a small commercial space 
on the 4.39-acre site between Cannon Street and Highway 42. The design is consistent with the 
rezoning approved by City Council in October 2023, and the overall massing, scale, and 
character of the project have remained the same throughout the process. 
The DBA Board believes this to be a good use of the site and it will be of great benefit downtown 
and the surrounding area. Louisville is in need of more accessible and attainable housing, and 
this site is one of the few remaining areas to add more housing within walking distance of 
downtown Louisville. New residents in this location will directly support our local businesses 
and the project’s housing will give employees at Louisville’s restaurants, shops, and offices the 
possibility of actually living in the community where they work — something that is increasingly 
difficult, if not impossible, given housing costs in our area. 
The project also includes sustainability features, improved pedestrian and vehicle connectivity 
through the Caledonia Street extension providing direct access from the property to Highway 
42, and activated commercial space that will complement rather than compete with existing 
businesses. 
The DBA Board emphatically recommends that the Planning Commission and City Council 
approve the PUD for Delo Boom. It will be a welcome, and vital asset to the downtown area, and 
the project has earned the support of neighbors, local businesses, and the broader community 
over the past three years. 
 
Thank you for your time and consideration, 
The Board of the Louisville Downtown Business Association 
Jennifer Hlawatsch, President

This is my feedback as a resident of Louisville and of the Delo neighborhood.
Having reviewed the plans for Delo Boom, much of it tracks well given the position in the

A. Parking
B. Need for / Excess Retail and Commercial space
A. Parking Math is Ignored
Current Planned Spots (Parking Supply) == 161 + 30(?)
The proposal states 191 total, but there are only approximately 161 parking spots in the plans
(There is no number of on-property parking spots in the PUD. Shouldn't that be required in a
final PUD?)
- 31 spots on the north side including 3 ADA
- 42 spots in the north middle lot
- 31 spots in the south middle lot including 2 ADA
- 38 spots in the south including 2 ADA
- 19 spots inside townhomes
The plan talks about "on street parking" - perhaps on Griffith and Cannon streets for the extra
30 spots ... 
- Griffith - 20 cars would park up Griffith St all the way to the train tracks.
- Cannon Street - 20 (new) cars would park Cannon totally up from Griffith to Delo
Apartments. 
Total possible spots (with very tight access on Cannon) : 40?
Current Needed Spots (Parking Demand) = 270 spots
36 townhomes (2/3/4 bedrooms) + 99 apartments (mostly 1 bdr or studio, ~ 15 3BR) 
2 cars per unit + zero spots for retail = 270 cars
This seems realistic given 36 townhomes + 15 3BR appts + multiple adults in 50% of the 1BR
units.
270 spots needed + 0 spots for retail is far greater than the maximum 191 spots. 
1. Where will the extra 79 cars be parked? At the ballfields across HWY 42? In Little Italy? 
2. Given the plans for Light Rail station IN DELO - where will those commuters park? Across

3. Are some number of these spots reserved for retail use? How many? That pushes more
residence cars off the property.
4. Regarding retail: Who can operate a retail / commercial business with difficult or little
parking? Will we get more vacant retail? 
5. Perhaps some of the retail space could be used for parking with a zoning variance?
B. Retail Requirements
I have attended multiple Planning Commision meetings and City Council meetings regarding
the DeloBoom and Coal Creek Village (Hwy 42 and South Boulder road). Many of the plans
contain significant storefront / retail / commercial spaces. 
Demand for Retail?
Is there really demand for these spaces? Now or in the future?  Given Amazon and online
buying trends? Given 13,500 sq ft being added 2 blocks north of Delo Boom?
Currently, in the Delo and downtown Louisville area, there are the following open

1. 2 pad sites in front of Ziggi's Coffee and Tap and Garden - vacant for 6 years.
2. Commercial / office space in Delo development - vacant 2 years (Duda building)
3. Old Empire restaurant, Old Louisville Guitars, Old Waterloo restaurant - empty for multiple

4. New space coming in Coal Creek Village (13,500 sq ft)

residential if it is not used? If we are attempting to lower cost of housing, make the
commercial convertible to housing.

Delo/Boom 1301 Highway 42
 
I am writing regarding the Delo/Boom project located at 1301 Courtesy Road and 1390 Cannon
Street that you are considering for approval on July 30, 2026. As an adjacent landowner, for more
than the last two decades I’ve known it well.  We have accessed our property through the South side
drive apron of this property for more than 50 years.  As a result, we have what is considered a
prescribed easement to this drive way. 
 
When the original Delo Developers approached me nearly 20 years ago, my primary concern was
access via this drive apron.  It is integral to the operation of my business and re-development of my
property.  Justin and Dave from Delo assured me my access would be continued and memorialized. 
This was to be via Caledonia Street or Alley that would run East to West connecting Cannon Street to
Highway 42.  It was written into plans, submitted to planning and presented to city staff.  After
approval, the Delo Developers imploded.  That brought a second developer who was not
accommodating to any of the neighbor’s concerns, thus failed to gain an approval. 
 
I’d like to believe the third time is the charm on this go around.  Live Forward has taken the opposite
approached.  They have reached out to the neighbors with a collaborative, solutions based
approached and done the best to be a good neighbor and accommodate their reasonable needs. 
Developing a property in Louisville comes with a high level of challenges.  Louisville has some of the
most stringent building code in the nation, our cost of water and building fees are extremely high
along with the cost of the land.
 
Live Forward brings a high quality build that not only service a middle housing need, but will be truly
transit oriented development, just 150 yards away from the CoCo Train Stop.  It will also bring to the
neighborhood additional access to and from highway 42 via Caledonia.  This should help to alleviate
some of the bottleneck on the Griffith and Short Street intersections.  
 
Live Forward appears to have gone over and above to check all the boxes and will build a quality
project that will service the needs of our community.  It will complement the adjacent Delo
development that was intended to be completed a decade ago. It will help support our adjacent and
downtown businesses, all are within convenient walking distance.  After over three years in the
planning process, I believe this housing meets Louisville’s sustainability, density and affordability
goals. This is the best we are going to get. 
 
I humbly request that our Planning Commission approve this application without condition and
move forward the process to see this long overdue project to fruition.
