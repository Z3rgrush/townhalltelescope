Agenda Item 3.B. 
 
 
Subject:  Discussion / Direction: Community Engagement 
Discussion / Direction provides Council with the opportunity to learn about an item, ask 
questions, and provide feedback to staff. There will be one round of public comment. 
There will be no final decisions made on this item, and it may return at a later date for 
action by Council.  
Date: November 10, 2026 
Prepared By: Grace Johnson, Communications Manager 
Samma Fox, Deputy City Manager 
Presented By: Grace Johnson, Communications Manager 
 
Summary:  
The City's Communications Manager will provide an overview of the City's current 
approach to community engagement and opportunities to strengthen these efforts. Staff 
will discuss opportunities to establish a more consistent, intentional, and effective 
approach. Near-term improvements will focus on developing shared guidelines, 
decision-making criteria, processes, and resources using existing staff capacity and 
tools. 
 
The presentation will also explore how these foundational improvements can support a 
more comprehensive approach to community engagement over time. Given current 
resource and staffing limitations, the City will need to prioritize sustainable 
improvements while identifying opportunities for future growth as capacity allows. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
Community engagement is taking place across the organization, but practices, tools and 
levels of engagement vary by project. Currently, the City does not have a shared 
approach to guide when community engagement should occur, which methods are most 
appropriate, or how community input should inform decisions. The role of staff and 
Council in community engagement planning is not defined concisely. 
 
City Council included the development of a Community Engagement Framework in their 
2026 City Council Work Plan. The Comprehensive Plan, which was adopted by 
resolution in March 2026, includes the following goal related to community engagement 
in the City Core Services Goals & Strategies section: Goal CS 3. Promote Meaningful 
and Inclusive Community Engagement. Four strategies identified to accomplish this 
goal include: 
• CS 3 .1 . Continue to apply best practices in public engagement, such as the 
International Association of Public Participation (IAP2) principles, for major City 
Page 19 of 40
decisions affecting residents and businesses. 
• CS 3 .2 . Design community engagement opportunities that include and reflect 
the entire community, especially underrepresented groups so that City decision-
making is informed by all voices. 
• CS 3 .3 . Advance the City’s Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion (EDI) Blueprint to 
guide more inclusive communication and engagement strategies. 
• CS 3 .4 . Develop a Citywide Community Engagement Framework to promote 
consistent, transparent, and meaningful engagement on projects that impact 
neighborhoods and/or the broader community. 
 
The proposed path forward will implement many of these strategies, with a phased 
approach for improvements in the future. 
 
Development Proposal: 
N/A 
 
Analysis: 
Community engagement is an invitation to the public to participate in a decision at some 
level. This differs from communications, where the primary purpose is to share 
important and relevant information and updates with the public. Community 
engagement goes beyond sharing information—it is the structured process of involving 
people in discussions, decision-making, and problem-solving. 
 
Good engagement is happening in Louisville. Community engagement tools exist, and 
specialized support is included for long-term planning projects, like the PROST Long-
Range Plan and Development Code Update. The approach for how we engage 
meaningfully, especially for smaller impact projects, is inconsistent across the 
organization. A lack of common criteria to determine when engagement is needed, what 
level of community engagement is appropriate, and how the City will use the input 
creates a challenge for staff, the community, and Council. Symptoms of this challenge 
can include Council engagement requests late into project timelines and misalignment 
of expectations. This can add significant time, effort, and contention to projects, and 
may ultimately erode trust between the community and the City. Staff is proposing 
updates to Louisville's framework using shared language, best practices, and a model 
that is flexible enough to account for the diversity of Louisville activities and 
engagement needs.  
 
The International Association for Public Participation (IAP2) is the leading organization 
in this work, and advocates both nationwide and internationally for best practices in 
public participation. The IAP2 model is a proven framework that outlines public 
participation goals and the "promise to the public" at increasing levels of impact on the 
decision. These categories include inform, consult, involve, collaborate, and empower. 
The key differentiation for each is how much the public is involved in the decision. In 
inform, the first category, the promise is to keep the public informed. Inform is 
considered a baseline for all community engagement work, and is considered standard 
Page 20 of 40
communications. At the other end of the spectrum, "empower," the final decision is 
placed in the community's hands and what they decide will be implemented. There isn't 
as much in this category, but there are opportunities where it makes sense. For 
example. Louisville empowered the public to name snow plows last year. Many of the 
City's community engagement projects will live in between, where the City is either in 
"consult" (receiving feedback and using that to inform decisions), "involve" (working 
directly with the public throughout the process and reflecting that feedback in the 
alternates developed), or "collaborate" (partnering with the public and incorporating their 
recommendations into decisions to the maximum extent possible).  
 
Louisville is fortunate to already have two key staff members trained in IAP2. Staff 
recommends building additional staff expertise in this area in the future and building a 
culture of engagement. For now, staff is proposing a framework that doesn't require 
every member of the organization be an expert, and sets the existing experts up for 
success in supporting the organization. This looks like setting timeline expectations, 
engaging both the subject matter and communication experts early, and starting with a 
list of key questions: 
 
• Is there a decision to be made?  
• Who has the authority to make the decision?  
• How much influence can the community have on the decision at hand?  
• Are there parts of the decision that cannot be influenced?  
• Who is impacted by this decision?  
• How are people/organizations impacted by this decision? (Quality of life, 
accessibility, safety, services, etc.)  
• How, specifically, will the input we receive impact the decision?  
• Is this decision attached to a long-range plan? If yes, was there meaningful 
community engagement during the long-range planning process? If so, when?  
• What are we hearing about this decision from this community already?  
• Can input impact (change) the outcome or improve its fairness?  
• What resources do we have to support community engagement efforts?  
• Is community engagement a requirement? (Regulatory, compliance, grant 
requirements) 
 
In conjunction with this, staff has developed a framework to choose engagement 
techniques and a Louisville-specific toolbox of these techniques. The proposed 
Louisville toolbox aligns with IAP2 categories and makes use of the City's existing tools, 
such as Engage Louisville. The framework includes learning from the public and 
assessing items like stakeholders, perception, and existing efforts related to the project, 
examining resources, and determining the timeline. This, paired with techniques from 
the Louisville toolbox, will then be used to create a community engagement plan. The 
tools are set up to allow the project manager to make and implement a recommendation 
regarding community engagement, with the Communications team acting as a 
consultant, providing guidance, and signing off on the final recommendation. This 
Page 21 of 40
approach empowers the City's project managers, sets standards that create consistency 
throughout the organization, and utilizes the communications team's expertise 
strategically and with capacity in mind.  
 
These foundational improvements will work to create a "community of practice" in the 
City, improving consistency through structure and guidance, which is important in 
maintaining community trust, and relies on a consultative approach with 
communications staff. This approach fits the City's current resource structure. With 
additional resources, this model could be expanded in the future. Training more staff in 
IAP2, dedicating budget to encourage participation (this could look like childcare, 
transportation, etc.), and dedicating additional staff to focus on community engagement 
are all approaches other organizations have taken, and that require additional 
resources. 
 
Council Work Plan: 
2026 Council Work Plan item: "Community Engagement Framework: A framework 
outlining Council expectations for community engagement and providing consistency for 
the community." 
 
Fiscal Impact: 
The current proposal utilizes existing City resources. Expansions of this proposal could 
require additional resources and staff will identify that as this project progresses. 
 
Alternatives: 
Council could direct staff to continue the current approach, change, or modify the 
proposed approach. The current approach, while well intended, is inconsistent and has 
resulted in Council requests for additional engagement, at times extending the timelines 
and cost of projects. Council could request engagement of a third-party in this process, 
which would add unbudgeted expense. Council can also provide direction on a 
spectrum of alternatives within the scope of the current proposed discussion/direction 
and is encouraged to do so to help staff refine the framework before bringing to Council 
for adoption. 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends Council provide direction on the proposed Community Engagment 
Framework approach, including sharing their insight on what the future of community 
engagement should look like in Louisville.
