Subject:  Resolution No. 11, Series 2026 - Recommending Approval of the 
Coal Creek Junction Final Plat to Subdivide 1155 Pine Street 
Date: November 12, 2026 
Prepared By: Kristin Dean AICP Contract Planner 
Rob Zuccaro, Director 
Presented By: Kristin Dean AICP Contract Planner 
Rob Zuccaro, Director 
 
Summary:  
The applicant, the City of Louisville, is requesting approval of a Final Plat to subdivide 
1155 Pine Street into one lot and one tract. Lot 1 will encompass the existing residential 
structure and be retained by the current owner, while Tract A will be purchased by the 
City. The area where Tract A is proposed encompasses two train cars that have been 
re-located by the current property owner. The City has entered into a purchase and sale 
contract with the owner of 1155 Pine Street, which requires that the City initiate the 
subdivision application for the purpose of the City acquiring the train cars and the tract 
where they will be located. 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
The subject property is 10,414.40 square feet (0.24 acres) and located at 1155 Pine St., 
to the northwest of the Pine St. / Hwy 42 intersection. The property is zoned Community 
Commercial (CC), is located within the boundary of the area defined as Downtown 
Louisville, and is subject to the Downtown Design Handbook design standards. The 
property has never been a part of a previous subdivision. 1155 Pine St. is located 
adjacent to the City’s Tomeo Park, which is where the previous rail line was located and 
railcars servicing the local mines would run on this section of tracks. The Louisville 
Sanitation District used the land for a sanitary sewer main. After the district dissolved, 
the City designated this land into a small City park at one of the primary entries into 
Downtown Louisville. 
 
The property at 1155 Pine Street is currently developed with a single-family home and 
includes the placement of a caboose and railcar as accessory to the primary single-
family use. A single-family dwelling is a nonconforming use in the CC zone district. As 
such, the structure may not be structurally altered, extended or expanded per LMC Sec. 
17.36.010 without rezoning the property to the Mixed-Use Zoning District. 
 
Historical records indicate that five of the six existing houses on the north side of Pine 
Page 10 of 104
Street east of the railroad tracks, including 1155 Pine, were likely constructed in the 
mid-1920s, with 1155 Pine estimated to have been constructed circa 1920–1925.  
The subject property is located within the Highway 42 Revitalization Area, established 
through the 2003 Highway 42 Revitalization Area Framework Plan and the Highway 42 
Revitalization Area Comprehensive Plan Amendment. To implement these plans, the 
City established the Mixed-Use Overlay District (Louisville Municipal Code (LMC) 
Chapter 17.14) and adopted the Mixed Use Development Design Standards and 
Guidelines (MUDDSG), which apply to properties within the district. Any future 
development of the property requires rezoning of the property to Mixed Use - 
Residential (MU-R) zone district. 
 
The property is also located within the Highway 42 Urban Renewal Area, governed by 
an adopted Urban Renewal Plan intended to reduce or eliminate blight, stimulate growth 
and reinvestment, and promote appropriate land uses consistent with local objectives 
(Urban Renewal Plan Sec. 1.3). Pursuant to LMC Section 17.14.020, any property 
within the Revitalization Area that undergoes new development or redevelopment must 
be rezoned to a zoning district consistent with the Land Use Plan adopted with the 
Highway 42 Revitalization Area Plan. The Land Use Plan and associated policy 
documents, including the Highway 42 Revitalization Area Plan, were adopted to 
promote coordinated mixed-use redevelopment in support of a future transit rail corridor. 
However, at this time, the property is not proposed for re-development and therefore a 
rezoning is not required, nor are the design standards under the associated policy 
documents applicable. Any future redevelopment of the property will be subject to the 
applicable standards and policies.  
 
The CC zone district has a minimum lot area of 7,000 square feet. Lot 1 will not meet 
this minimum lot size, and thus a subdivision modification to the minimum lot size is 
requested. Adjacent zoning includes Residential Medium Density (R-M) to the north, 
Commercial Community (C-C) to the west and south, with Courtesy Rd. (CO HWY 42) 
to the east.  
Page 11 of 104
 
Figure 1. Zoning map. The subject property is outlined in light blue. The crosshatching 
represents the Downtown Louisville overlay. 
 
Under the CC Zoning District, the front setback is 25-feet. The existing primary structure 
on proposed Lot 1 is located 20’ from the front property line and there is an additional 
5’6” covered porch that further encroaches into the setback. Because these 
improvements existed before the CC zoning district was adopted and applied to the 
property, these improvements are considered to be “legal, non-conforming” structures.  
 
The two antique rail cars were placed on the property in 2023. The train cars and the 
railroad trucks upon which they sit, are located within the front and side setbacks. Thus, 
the train cars and railroad tracks are deemed “existing, non-conforming structures”.  
At this time and under the current subdivision application, the current owner of 1155 
Pine St. has no intentions of building any other improvements on either property. Should 
that change, then any new improvements will be subject to the applicable sections of 
the City’s zoning code and design guidelines.  
The plat is being created in order to create a separate tract where the train cars are 
located. The City is under contract to purchase Tract A once subdivided.  
 
 
Development Proposal: 
The subdivision request is to create one lot and one tract, with Lot 1 being retained by 
the current owner and Tract A being purchased by the City.  
  
Page 12 of 104
The subject property is irregularly shaped and located within the Commercial 
Community (CC) zoning district, which requires a minimum lot area of 7,000 square feet 
and a minimum lot width of 50 feet. The proposed subdivision will create Lot 1, 
containing 5,946.20 square feet with a width of 56.23 feet, and Tract A, containing 
4,468.20 square feet with a width of 130 feet. 
  
The subdivision includes transferring a portion of the Tomeo Park parcel to Lot 1 to 
square off the lot frontage and achieve the proposed lot width fronting Pine Street. 
While Lot 1 meets the minimum lot width requirement, it does not meet the minimum lot 
area requirement; therefore, a subdivision modification is requested. Tract A is not 
subject to a minimum lot area requirement.

 The subdivision would establish new utility easements on Lot 1, including a 10-foot-
wide easement along the northern property line and 5-foot-wide easements along the 
eastern and western property lines.

 Figure 3. Proposed subdivision of property 
  
 Lot 1 is accessed by a driveway connecting directly to Pine St. Tract A has no formal 
vehicular access designated. It is currently only accessible via the pedestrian sidewalk 
located in the Pine St. ROW. Because this will be a city-owned property, additional 
access points are not needed. 
  
 The application also requires approval of the following two subdivision modifications: 
  
1. Section 17.12.040, the CC zone district requires a minimum lot size of 7,000 square 
feet. Lot 1 will not meet this minimum lot size requirement. Lot 1 is proposed at 5,946 sf. 
2. Section 16.16.060.B, for residential subdivisions, requires a minimum of 15 percent of 
the total land area of the tract being subdivided to be dedicated for a park, school, or 
other public purpose, or cash-in-lieu may be accepted at the discretion of the City. It is 
recommended that this not be required via a subdivision modification request. If Council 
does not grant the modification, then the application would need to go to the Open 
Space Advisory Board “OSAB” and Parks and Public Landscaping Advisory Board 
“PPLAB” for review and confirmation of the public dedication requirement.  
 
 
Analysis: 
The subdivision of property in Louisville is regulated by Title 16 of the Louisville Municipal Code 
(LMC). Staff finds the proposed plat satisfies the criteria for a Final Plat procedure found in Sec. 
16.12.075 with the requested waivers. Staff reviewed the plat against the final plat criteria and 
subdivision design standards in LMC Sec. 16.12.090 and LMC Chapter 16.16, and applicable City 
codes and standards for access, drainage and utilities. The City requests the following two 
subdivision modifications:  
Page 14 of 104
1. Minimum Lot Size 
Section 16.16.050.A requires lots to meet applicable zoning requirements. Pursuant to Section 
17.12.040, the Commercial Community (CC) zoning district requires a minimum lot area of 7,000 
square feet. The proposed subdivision will create Lot 1, containing 5,946 square feet, and Tract A, 
containing 4,468 square feet. A subdivision modification is requested to allow Lot 1 to be smaller 
than the minimum required lot area. Tract A is not subject to a minimum lot area requirement. 
Lot 1 will include the minimum lot width of 50 feet and will remain capable of accommodating future 
development, subject to applicable zoning and development standards. If developed in the future, 
Lot 1 would be subject to the Mixed Use (MU) zoning district standards, which do not include 
minimum lot areas. Therefore, the requested modification will not preclude reasonable future 
development of the property. 
2. Public Land Dedication 
LMC Section 16.16.060.B establishes minimum public land dedication requirements of 15 percent 
for residential subdivisions, or a cash-in-lieu contribution may be made. The City requests a 
modification to waive the public land dedication requirement. 
 
Staff supports the modification because the property was developed and the lot boundaries were set 
by deed prior to the City enacting subdivision regulations and public land dedication requirements. 
While the municipal code requires public land dedication for all properties regardless of past 
development status, the City Council may find that there were other satisfactory dedication 
arrangements made at the time of annexation or other previous subdivision of the property.  Staff is 
unaware of any formal public land dedication requirements when the property was annexed into the 
City. However, staff finds that the past development status of the property is an indication that the 
City considered the property to have satisfactory land dedication requirements met or that it was 
acceptable to allow development and annexation into the City without public land dedication 
requirements at the time. To require additional public land dedication following initial development 
and when a subdivision does not create additional development potential may also not be consistent 
with requirements to establish a nexus and proportionality for requiring land dedication. The 
acquisition will also increase Tomeo Park by 4,468 square feet, providing additional land for public 
recreation and community use. This location allows the acquired land to be incorporated into an 
established park, creating a larger, contiguous public space serving the surrounding neighborhood 
and providing flexibility for future park improvements. Given this public benefit and development 
history of the property, staff finds that waiving the dedication requirement is appropriate in the 
circumstances of this subdivision. 
 
Final Plat Criteria: 
Section 16.12.075 of the LMC establishes the following rationale for consideration of final plats by 
Planning Commission and City Council: 
1. Whether the plat conforms to all of the requirements of this title; 
• With the two modifications described above, staff find that the application conforms 
to all other requirements of Title 16. 
2. Whether approval of the plat will be consistent with the city’s comprehensive plan, applicable 
zoning requirements, and other applicable federal, state and city laws; 
• Staff find that the request is consistent with the newly adopted Comprehensive Plan. The 
subject property has a land use designation of “Neighborhood Center”. While re-
development of the property is not proposed under the current subdivision application, 
retaining the residential dwelling while the City’s acquisition of the property where the 
Page 15 of 104
train cars are located creates a public space for social interaction. This site demarcates a 
primary access into downtown Louisville with visual interest reflective of the City’s 
historic rail lines.  
• Staff finds that the application is consistent with all applicable zoning requirements 
except where modifications have been requested as described in this report. 
3. Whether the proposed subdivision will promote the purposes set forth in section 16.04.020 of 
this Code and comply with the standards set forth in chapter 16.16 of this Code and this title.  
• Staff finds that the application will promote the purposes in Section 16.04.020, 
including promoting orderly growth and harmonious development, protecting the 
character of the city, and regulating the land on the basis of the impact thereof on the 
surrounding area. 
• Staff find that the application is consistent with all the standards set forth in Chapter 
16.16 except in the requested modifications that are addressed below in the 
Modification Criteria evaluation section.  
 
Subdivision Modification Criteria: 
Section 16.24.030 of the LMC establishes the following criteria for consideration of modifications to 
final plats; applications must also comply with Section 16.04.020 (Purpose, discussed above) and 
Section 16.24.010 (Modifications).  
1. Section 16.24.010 Modifications: The city council, upon advice of the planning commission, 
may authorize modifications where a subdivider proposes a plat that does not fully comply 
with the regulations in this title or title 17. No modification shall be granted if it would be 
detrimental to the public good or impair the basic intent and purposes of this title or title 17. 
Any modification granted shall be in keeping with the intent of the comprehensive 
development plan of the city.  
• Minimum Lot Size: The Community Commercial (CC) zoning district and Downtown 
Louisville Overlay in Title 17 addresses neighborhood character through standards 
related to lot size, lot width, and building mass, scale, and design. Title 16 (Subdivisions) 
establishes minimum lot widths and depths for subdivisions. This proposal includes a 
modification to minimum lot size. However, this modification has a minimal effect on 
density, and no effect on scale or design. 
• Public Land Dedication: The subdivision modification request to not require the Public 
Land Dedication request is supported because of the past development history of the 
property, considering the subdivision does not result in additional development potential 
for the property, and the City will acquire a 4,468 square foot tract which will expand 
public access to this area of the City where the existing Tomeo Park is located.  
2. Section 16.24.030.A Modification review criteria: 
1. That there are unique physical circumstances or conditions such as irregularity, 
narrowness or shallowness of the property, or exceptional topographical conditions, 
or other physical conditions peculiar to the affected property; 
• Staff find that the application meets this criterion because the existing property 
is uniquely shaped with the future City-owned portion adjacent to abandoned 
railroad ROW. The subdivision does not create any new redevelopment 
opportunities, but facilitates the ability for the City to purchase property where 
the train cars are located. Thus, standard subdivision requirements for access, 
lot size, setbacks, and public land dedication are not applicable to the future 
City-owned property.  
Page 16 of 104
2. That because of such physical circumstances or conditions, the property cannot be 
reasonably developed in conformity with the provisions of title 16 and title 17 
resulting in a hardship; 
• The property is being subdivided to enable the City to purchase a 0.103 ac 
tract where train cars have been relocated. Additional development of either 
property is not proposed with this subdivision plat. Any future development 
or re-development will need to adhere to all applicable regulations in effect 
at the time of application.  
3. That such hardship has not been created by the applicant; 
• The plat is a result of the City’s intention to purchase the land where the train 
cars are located. Due to the unique size of the existing lot, the modifications 
are necessary to create the separate tract for the City’s purchase.  
4. That the modification, if granted, will not alter the essential character of the 
neighborhood or district in which the property is located, nor substantially or 
permanently impair the appropriate use or development of adjacent property; 
• Staff find the modifications will not alter the essential character of the 
neighborhood nor impair the appropriate use or development of adjacent 
property. This area of downtown is characterized by small lots with small 
homes and some commercial businesses on each lot. 
5. That there are no reasonable alternatives that would remove the need for the 
requested modification or would reduce the amount of the modification. 
• Staff find that there are no alternatives to the request that would result in 
creating logical lot boundaries while facilitating the City's acquisition of the rail 
car tract and the public land dedication requirement should be considered in 
context of the timing of past development allowances and annexation of the 
property and that the land dedication is off-set by the City’s acquisition of Tract 
A.  
6. That no additional dwelling units shall result from approval of the modification 
beyond what the underlying zoning would otherwise allow. 
• Staff find that the plat will not result in any additional dwelling units beyond 
what the underlying zoning would otherwise allow.  
 
Staff find that all the modification criteria are met and therefore recommends approval of the 
modification requests for the Minor Subdivision.

Summary:  
The applicant, the City of Louisville, is requesting approval of a Minor Plat to subdivide a 
5-acre parcel into two lots at 101 W. Cherry St. as described in the proposed 
Warembourg Subdivision Replat A subdivision plat. The property is currently developed 
with a single-family home, a barn and several outbuildings. The City exercised a right of 
first refusal to purchase the property on November 3, 2025. Lot 1A (3-acre) will be sold 
to a private entity while the City will retain Lot 1B (2-acre). 
 
Background / Prior Discussions: 
The subject property is located at 101 W. Cherry Street, contains 217,800 square feet 
(approximately 5 acres), and is zoned Agricultural (A). The property was annexed into 
the City in 1983 (Reception No. 567461) and is part of the Warembourg Subdivision, 
recorded on October 27, 1994 (Reception No. 01474015). 
 
On July 21, 1994, the City entered into a Purchase Contract to Buy and Sell Vacant 
Real Estate and Conservation Easement (Reception No. 01451047) with the 
Warembourgs. The agreement provided for the City's purchase of approximately 50 
acres (Parcel 1), acquisition of a small tract known as the "Dutch Creek Parcel" (Parcel 
3), and administration of a conservation easement over approximately 41 acres (Parcel 
2). 
 
Following the Purchase Contract, a Subdivision Agreement was recorded on August 1, 
1994 (Reception No. 01451048), and the Warembourg Subdivision Plat was recorded 
on October 27, 1994. The plat established three single-family residential lots (Lots 1, 2, 
and 3), Outlot A (26.29 acres), and Lot 4 (46.12 acres). Lot 4 comprises approximately 
46 acres of the Warembourg Open Space, while Outlot A remains privately owned and 
is subject to a Deed of Conservation Easement in Gross ("Conservation Easement"), 
recorded October 25, 1994 (Reception No. 01473266), which restricts the property to 
Page 44 of 104
agricultural uses and limited residential development. 
Development Restrictions and Existing Residential Uses 
Both the Conservation Easement and Subdivision Agreement establish restrictions on 
the development and use of the Warembourg Subdivision. 
Section 1 of the Conservation Easement limits the use of the property "solely for 
agricultural uses and for no more than three single family dwellings (the existing 
dwelling plus two additional dwellings)," together with accessory buildings and 
structures customary to those uses. The Conservation Easement further allows 
structures existing at the time of the Purchase Contract to remain, be reconstructed, or 
be replaced, provided that any reconstruction or replacement does not exceed the size 
of the structure as it existed on the date of the Purchase Contract. 
Similarly, Section 5.1 of the Subdivision Agreement limits Lots 1, 2, and 3 to "solely for 
agricultural uses and for no more than three single family dwellings (the existing 
dwelling plus two additional dwellings)." 
At the time of subdivision, the only existing dwelling was located on Lot 1 and was 
constructed in 1965. The dwelling on Lot 2 was subsequently constructed in 1999, 
followed by the dwelling on Lot 3 in 2021. Accordingly, all three dwelling units permitted 
under the Subdivision Agreement have been constructed, exhausting the residential 
development potential established by the agreement. 
A new conservation easement for Lot 1A is being established as part of the purchase 
and sale contract. The new easement will limit the property to agricultural uses and one 
single-family dwelling, with accessory structures customary to those uses. The 
easement allows the existing dwelling to be remodeled, expanded, or replaced, 
provided it does not exceed one story or 3,000 square feet of gross floor area. The 
existing farm utility buildings may also be reconstructed or replaced, provided they do 
not exceed their existing size. 
The easement further restricts access roads to those currently existing, prohibits 
commercial and industrial uses, and prohibits future subdivision without express City 
consent. These restrictions are intended to preserve the property's rural and agricultural 
character while allowing continued use and maintenance of the existing residential and 
agricultural improvements.

retained in ownership by the City.

Analysis: 
The subdivision of property in Louisville is regulated by Title 16 of the Louisville Municipal 
Code (LMC). Staff finds the proposed plat satisfies the criteria for a Final Plat procedure 
found in Sec. 16.12.075 with the requested subdivision modifications. Staff reviewed the 
plat against the final plat criteria and subdivision design standards in LMC Sec. 
16.12.090 and LMC Chapter 16.16, and applicable city codes and standards for access, 
drainage and utilities. The City requests the following  subdivision modifications:  
 
1. Minimum Lot Size 
Section 16.16.050.A requires lots to meet applicable zoning requirements. Pursuant to 
Section 17.12.040, the Agricultural (“A”) zone district requires a minimum lot size of five 
acres. Neither proposed lot will meet this requirement, as Lot 1A will contain approximately 
three acres and Lot 1B will contain approximately two acres. Accordingly, a subdivision 
modification is requested to allow both lots to be smaller than the minimum required lot 
area. 
The proposed reduction in lot area will not prevent Lot 1A from being maintained or 
developed consistent with the Conservation Easement restrictions. The overall intended 
character and density of the subdivision will be maintained, with Lot 1B being used for open 
space or other public purposes. Given the existing development on Lot 1A, the 
Conservation Easement development restrictions, and the intended public use of Lot 1B, 
staff supports the requested modification. 
2. Minimum Lot Width 
Pursuant to Section 17.12.040, the Agricultural (“A”) zone district requires a minimum lot 
width of 165 feet. The proposed subdivision will result in a lot width of 199 feet for Lot 1A 
and 92 feet for Lot 1B. While Lot 1B will not meet the minimum lot width requirement, the lot 
will be retained by the City and will not be developed with a residence. Lot 1B is also 
adjacent to existing City-owned open space and intended to be either incorporated into that 
open space or other complementary public use. Given the intended use of Lot 1B and its 
Page 49 of 104
relationship to the adjacent open space, staff supports the requested modification. 
3. Side Yard Setbacks for Accessory Structures 
Pursuant to Section 17.12.040, the Agricultural (“A”) zone district requires a minimum side-
yard setback of 20 feet for accessory structures.  Lot 1A will have two existing accessory 
structures that are set back 16 feet and 13.5 feet from the newly established side property 
line to the north. This is a result of the negotiated shared property boundary with the 
purchaser and results in the lot configurations desired by the purchaser and the City to 
create a 3-acre residential lot and 2-acre lot to be retained by the City. Because Lot 1B will 
not be developed, the reduced setbacks will not alter the intended character of the A zone 
district.
