Subject: Cooperative Agreement for Enforcement of the Wildfire Resiliency Code 
Attachments: 1.   Cooperative Agreement for Wildfire Resiliency Code Enforcement 
Presented by: Matt LeCerf, Town Manager 
Agenda Item Description: 
Enclosed for your review and consideration is a Cooperative Agreement between the Town of 
Johnstown and the Front Range Fire & Rescue (FRFR) for the oversight and enforcement of the 
Wildfire Resiliency Code. The Wildfire Resiliency Code 2025 Edition was adopted at the end of the 
2025 as part of a major overhaul of the adopted building codes in the Town of Johnstown. This 
agreement would authorize for the enforcement of the fire code elements of the Wildfire Resiliency 
Code by FRFR. Their jurisdiction of enforcement would be within the overlap areas of the service 
territory of the FRFR and within the Town of Johnstown only.  
 
The agreement presented here mimics the agreement approved administratively between 
Johnstown and Loveland Fire & Rescue Authority (LFRA). In the LFRA instance, both the Fire Chief 
and Town Manager signed the agreement.

COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT FOR  
ENFORCEMENT OF THE WILDFIRE RESILIENCY CODE 
 
This COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT FOR ENFORCEMENT OF THE WILDFIRE 
RESILIENCY CODE ("Agreement") is made by and between Front Range Fire Rescue Fire Protection 
District ("District") and the Town of Johnstown ("Town"). The District and Town are referred to 
collectively in this Agreement as the "Parties" or individually as a "Party". 
 
WHEREAS, each Party is a political subdivision of the State of Colorado, authorized to adopt and 
enforce fire codes, building codes, and/or related development codes within its respective jurisdiction, as 
applicable; 
 
WHEREAS, a portion of the District's jurisdiction lies within the Town's jurisdictional 
boundaries, such that the District's and Town's jurisdictions overlap. The area of jurisdictional overlap is 
referred to in this Resolution as the "District/Town Area"; 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-1237, both Parties have adopted the Wildfire 
Resiliency Code, 2025 Edition ("Code") that meets or exceeds the standards of the Model Wildfire 
Resiliency Code adopted by the Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code Board ("Code Board"). The Code 
will become enforceable within each Party's jurisdiction on July 1, 2026; 
 
WHEREAS, C.R.S. § 24-33.5-1237(2)(b) through (d) allow the Parties to enter into cooperative 
agreements with the governing bodies of other jurisdictions in order to enforce the Code; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Parties desire to enter into this Agreement for the purpose of cooperating with 
one another to enforce the Code within the District/Town Area, and to delineate which elements of the 
Code each Party will enforce within the District/Town Area. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual promises and covenants contained herein, 
the Parties hereby agree as follows: 
 
1. District Responsibilities. Within the District/Town Area, the District shall: 
 
A. Enforce the applicable fire code elements of the Code, generally including, but not limited to, those 
elements of the Code found in Chapter 3 (Wildfire Hazard Identification) and Chapter 5 (Site and 
Area Requirements). The District may, at its option, establish a property owner self-compliance 
program for purposes of meeting its obligations pursuant to this Section 1(A). 
 
B. Refer violations of the building code elements of the Code of which the District is actually aware 
to the Town for enforcement. 
 
2. Town Responsibilities. Within the District/Town Area, the Town shall: 
 
A. Enforce the applicable building code elements of the Code, generally including, but not limited to, 
those elements of the Code found in Chapter 4 (Structure Hardening). The Town may, at its option, 
establish a property owner self-compliance program for purpose of meeting its obligations 
pursuant to this Section 2(A). 
 
 
Page 12 of 74
 2 
B. Refer violations of the fire code elements of the Code of which the Town is actually aware to the 
District for enforcement. 
 
3. Mutual Responsibilities. Within the District/Town Area, both Parties shall: 
 
A. Without waiving the legislative discretion of the governing bodies of each Party to determine 
otherwise, adopt amendments to or updated versions of the Code as may be required by applicable 
statute or regulation promulgated by the Code Board from time to time.  
 
B. Advise the other Party at least 60 days in advance of making any petition to the Code Board for a 
modification to the Code. Notwithstanding the terms of Section 6 below, either Party may 
terminate this Agreement within 30 days following the Code Board's approval of any modification 
to the Code petitioned for by the other Party. 
 
C. Utilize the Wildfire Resiliency Code Maps approved by the Code Board, including, but not limited 
to, the Fire Intensity Classification, Fire Intensity Scale, Surface Fuels, and Wildland Urban 
Interface Maps, or such alternate maps as may be developed by the Town and approved by the 
Code Board. The Town shall advise the District at least 60 days in advance of petitioning the Code 
Board for approval of any alternate maps, and provide the District with copies of the alternate 
maps within 5 business days of approval by the Code Board. 
 
D. Reasonably coordinate with each other with respect to properties requiring enforcement of both 
the fire code elements and building code elements of the Code. 
 
E. Upon request of the other Party, reasonably participate in joint inspections of a property, to the 
extent of a Party's technical knowledge, operational capability, and staffing availability.  
 
F. Maintain records of all enforcement actions taken pursuant to this Agreement and in accordance 
with the Code and all other applicable laws, rules, and regulations (collectively, "Applicable 
Law"), and the Party's adopted records retention policies. 
 
4. Compensation and Fees. Neither Party shall owe any compensation to the other Party under this 
Agreement, and each Party shall be solely responsible for all costs of performing its obligations 
hereunder. The Parties shall, at their option, be permitted to charge fees as provided by Applicable 
Law, and shall be solely responsible to enforce, collect, and appropriately deposit all fees charged by 
it. 
 
5. Relationship of the Parties. By entering into this Agreement, the Parties are not creating, and shall not 
be deemed or construed as creating, a joint venture, partnership, authority, or any other type of legal 
relationship, and each Party shall remain a separate and distinct entity for all purposes under this 
Agreement. The Parties understand and agree that all District employees and volunteers, and all Town 
employees and volunteers, shall at all times remain the employees or volunteers of their respective 
Party, and will not be considered an employee or volunteer of the other Party for any reason. Each 
Party is responsible for the compensation, stipends, and/or other employment- or volunteerism-related 
benefits of its employees, and is responsible for, and will pay, all local, State, and Federal taxes that 
are incidental to their employment or volunteerism.

signed by both Parties ("Effective Date"). The term of this Agreement shall commence on the Effective

Agenda Item Description: 
Enclosed for your review and consideration is Resolution 2026-42 approving the Purchase and Sale 
Agreement by and between the Town of Johnstown and Quality Liquid Feeds, Inc. for the purchase 
of three parcels of real property located within the downtown area on Charlotte Street and consisting 
of approximately 1.183 acres altogether. 
 
The property is owned by Quality Liquid Feeds (QLF), which is in the process of relocating its 
business operations out of state. Below is an overview of the three lots: 
 
 
Parcel Number Acreage 
105904301003 0.134 
105904300032 0.250 
105904300033 0.759 
 
Town Staff has been working with QLF for several months to evaluate the feasibility of acquiring the 
property and its potential for future redevelopment consistent with the Town Council adopted 
Downtown Master Plan. 
 
As part of this process, a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) was completed in July. The 
assessment identified recognized environmental conditions and recommended additional 
environmental assessments to determine the extent of any potential contamination and whether 
remediation may be necessary. The property is currently considered a potential brownfield; however, 
a final determination regarding environmental conditions and remediation requirements will require 
additional investigation. Completion of the Phase I ESA prior to acquisition was necessary to 
preserve the Town's eligibility for potential State and Federal brownfield funding for future 
environmental assessments and, if necessary, remediation. 
 
 
Page 30 of 74
Town Staff has also been actively coordinating with the Colorado Department of Public Health and 
Environment (CDPHE) regarding the property and has submitted a request for a Phase II ESA, 
which, if approved, would be funded and completed through CDPHE's brownfield program. Staff is 
also working with Kansas State University Technical Assistance to Brownfields (KSU TAB), which 
works closely with CDPHE and communities throughout the region on brownfield redevelopment. 
KSU TAB will assist the Town in developing a conceptual redevelopment approach for the property 
based on the Downtown Master Plan and its Mixed-Use Downtown (MU-DT) zoning. 
Following completion of the Phase I ESA, the Town also obtained an appraisal in August to establish 
the property's market value. 
Acquisition of the property presents an opportunity to advance the goals of the Downtown Master 
Plan, which calls for the long-term redevelopment of industrial properties within downtown into uses 
that support a vibrant, walkable, and interconnected downtown. Town ownership would allow the 
Town to proactively guide the property's future redevelopment toward compatible mixed-use 
development, improved pedestrian connectivity, and stronger connections to Historic Downtown. 
Ownership would also provide greater flexibility to pursue future redevelopment partnerships and 
funding opportunities, including collaboration with the Johnstown Downtown Development Authority, 
potential TIF revenues, and State and Federal brownfield assistance. 
Town Staff is supportive of acquiring the property as a proactive step toward implementing the 
Downtown Master Plan and positioning the site for future redevelopment into uses compatible with 
MU-DT zoning, while continuing to evaluate and address any environmental conditions through the 
appropriate State and Federal brownfield programs.
