3.   8 Mill Tax Reduction FAQ 
Presented by: Matt LeCerf, Town Manager 
Agenda Item Description: 
Enclosed for your review and consideration is a proposed ordinance requested by Councilmember 
Bashford that would establish a phased reduction in the Town's property tax mill levy. The 
ordinance proposes a reduction of two (2) mills annually over four (4) consecutive calendar/fiscal 
years, resulting in a total reduction of eight (8) mills. The ordinance also includes a provision 
allowing for a one-year pause in the scheduled reductions if the Town's total net assessed valuation 
decreases by more than ten percent (10%) or if a disaster declaration is issued by the Governor or 
the Town pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-704 or C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709 respectively.  If fully 
implemented, the proposed ordinance would reduce the Town's direct mill levy, excluding the library 
mill levy, from 20.647 mills to 12.647 mills. This reduction would be permanent unless a subsequent 
voter-approved increase were authorized. 
 
The proposed reduction would provide ongoing property tax relief to property owners while also 
reducing a recurring revenue source available to support Town services, infrastructure, and 
community investments. As such, consideration of the proposal involves balancing the benefits of 
property tax relief with the Town's ability to meet its existing and anticipated financial obligations. 
 
Role of Tax Resources 
Page 50 of 95
Property tax revenues are an important component of the Town's overall financial structure and 
provide funding for services and investment in the community that benefit residents, businesses, 
and visitors. These revenues support a wide range of municipal services including public safety, 
parks and recreation, senior programming, street maintenance and construction, economic 
development, arts and culture, events, planning and engineering, and general municipal operations. 
Property tax revenues also help fund capital projects, community facilities, park improvements, and 
infrastructure enhancements that support the Town's long-term development and quality of life. 
 
One of the primary benefits of property tax revenue is its relative stability. Unlike sales and use 
taxes, which fluctuate based on consumer activity, development patterns, and broader economic 
conditions, property taxes generally provide a more predictable source of revenue for ongoing 
municipal operations. 
 
An important exception is property tax revenue generated from oil and gas activity, which can 
fluctuate significantly based on production levels, assessed valuations, and market conditions. 
Consistent with the Town's financial management practices, these revenues are directed toward 
capital investments and other one-time expenditures rather than recurring operational costs. A 
reduction in the mill levy would also reduce the property tax revenues generated from oil and gas 
properties, affecting resources available for these investments. 
 
Sales and use taxes are also essential to supporting Town services and capital improvements. 
However, these revenue sources are more sensitive to economic conditions, changes in consumer 
spending, development activity, and the performance of individual commercial areas. Maintaining a 
diversified revenue structure helps the Town manage these fluctuations and reduces its reliance on 
any single source of funding. 
 
Financial Considerations and Long-Term Impacts 
Evaluating the financial implications of the proposed mill levy reduction requires consideration of 
both the initial revenue impacts and the cumulative effects of permanently reducing an existing 
revenue source. 
 
Based on the assumptions presented with the proposed ordinance, the estimated reduction in 
property tax revenues during the first four years is approximately $11.9 million. This estimate 
provides a basis for evaluating the near-term financial impacts; however, actual revenues will vary 
based on several factors, including: 
 
•    Changes in residential and commercial property valuations resulting from periodic 
reassessments. 
•    New construction and development activity. 
•    Changes in oil and gas production, exploration, and assessed valuations. 
•    Property assessment rates established by the Colorado General Assembly. 
•    Broader economic conditions and other factors affecting the Town's assessed valuation. 
 
These variables, none of which are controlled by the Town, make long-term revenue projections 
inherently uncertain. Nevertheless, the permanent nature of the proposed reduction warrants 
consideration of its implications beyond the initial four-year implementation period. 
 
The financial information accompanying the proposal also identifies the potential for a reduction in 
projected reserve balances of approximately 44% over the first four years. This projection illustrates 
the importance of considering how reduced recurring revenues would be addressed through 
expenditure adjustments, changes to capital planning, or other financial management strategies. 
 
Page 51 of 95
Staff would work to mitigate these impacts through responsible budget management and 
expenditure adjustments. However, doing so would require evaluating the timing, scope, and 
prioritization of existing and future financial commitments. 
 
While the Town may be able to accommodate the initial reduction through adjustments to capital 
projects, discretionary expenditures, and new recurring obligations, the cumulative impact of 
reduced revenues could further constrain the financial options available to future Town Councils. 
 
A permanent reduction also limits the Town's ability to respond to changing conditions, including 
infrastructure needs, population growth, service demands, and unforeseen circumstances such as 
natural disasters. These considerations are particularly important when evaluating a revenue source 
that supports both current operations and long-term community investments. 
 
Community Priorities and Financial Planning 
The Town has made a concerted effort to better understand and respond to the priorities of its 
residents through tools such as the Community Survey and the Town's Strategic Plan. Community 
feedback helps inform decisions regarding the allocation of limited resources and provides an 
important foundation for establishing priorities related to infrastructure, public services, community 
amenities, and future investments. 
 
Results from the 2025 Community Survey identified opportunities for improvement in areas 
including mobility, street maintenance, and community engagement.  These findings, along with 
other community input and priorities identified by Town Council, help guide the Town’s strategic and 
financial planning efforts.  Council priorities include exploring opportunities for assisted living and 
affordable housing, expanding staffing for the Senior Center, developing phased improvements for 
Centennial Park, supporting the development of a regional hospital, and improving pedestrian 
connectivity with a sidewalk along Hwy 60. 
 
While the survey does not establish residents' preferences regarding property tax rates, it does 
provide valuable insight into the services, infrastructure, and community improvements that 
residents consider important. 
 
The Town's ability to respond to these identified priorities is an important consideration when 
evaluating a permanent reduction in recurring revenues. Although individual projects and services 
may benefit residents differently, collectively they contribute to the overall functionality, safety, and 
quality of life of the community. 
 
Through long-term financial planning and the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP), the Town works to 
align available resources with these community priorities while balancing immediate needs with 
future obligations. 
 
A permanent reduction in property tax revenues would require these plans to be reevaluated to 
ensure that expenditures and future commitments remain consistent with available financial 
resources. 
 
Potential Impacts on Capital Projects and Community Investments 
The Town's Capital Improvement Plan includes significant investments intended to maintain existing 
infrastructure, accommodate growth, and respond to the evolving needs of the community. 
 
A permanent reduction in recurring revenue would not necessarily result in the cancellation of 
specific projects. However, it could require Town Council to reconsider project timing, scope, 
funding strategies, and overall priorities. 
Page 52 of 95
 
Examples of significant projects and investments that may require reevaluation include: 
 
•    Public Works and Utility Facility: Planned construction of a new facility intended to support the 
operational needs of the Town's expanding infrastructure and service systems. 
•    Long-Term Water Security: Continued efforts to secure water resources for the community, 
including potential participation in the Northern Integrated Supply Project (NISP). 
•    Police Department Facility: A future Police Department facility, currently anticipated in 
approximately eight years, intended to accommodate community growth and operational needs 
while allowing the existing facility to serve as a southside substation. 
•    Centennial Park Development: Future phases of the planned development of Centennial Park, 
beyond the Senior Center facility, including recreational amenities, landscaping, and other 
improvements envisioned for the property. 
 
Additional investments and initiatives that could require reprioritization or adjustments include: 
 
•    Trail development and connectivity. 
•    Transit services and mobility improvements. 
•    Replacement of vehicles, heavy equipment, playgrounds, and other capital assets. 
•    Support for the Johnstown Historical Society. 
•    Downtown Development Authority initiatives. 
•    Affordable housing and assisted living initiatives. 
•    Future staffing additions and other recurring operational commitments. 
 
The extent to which individual projects or initiatives would be affected depends on available funding 
sources, existing commitments, future financial conditions, and Town Council's established 
priorities. 
 
These examples illustrate the types of decisions that may become necessary as the Town 
evaluates how to maintain services, address infrastructure needs, and advance community priorities 
within a reduced revenue structure. 
 
Alternatives to a Permanent Mill Levy Reduction 
Staff recognizes the value of providing property tax relief and supports consideration of approaches 
that allow Town Council to respond to changing financial conditions and community needs. Two 
alternatives to a permanent mill levy reduction are available for Council's consideration: a temporary 
property tax credit and a property tax rebate program. 
 
Temporary Property Tax Credit 
A temporary property tax credit would provide relief through a reduction in the effective mill levy for 
a specified period while maintaining the Town's ability to return to its previously authorized levy in 
subsequent years. 
 
This approach would allow Town Council to periodically evaluate financial conditions, revenue 
projections, infrastructure commitments, and community priorities when determining the appropriate 
level of property tax relief. 
 
A temporary tax credit would provide direct financial relief to property owners while preserving 
flexibility to adjust the amount of relief as circumstances change. This methodology has been 
utilized by Weld County and could be implemented without significant additional administrative 
requirements. 
 
Page 53 of 95
Property Tax Rebate Program 
A property tax rebate program would allow the Town to collect property taxes at the established mill 
levy and subsequently return a portion of those revenues to eligible property owners. Town Council 
could establish the total amount available for rebates and determine an appropriate distribution 
methodology. Options could include a flat-dollar rebate, a proportional rebate based on Town 
property taxes paid, or other eligibility and distribution criteria. The Town has utilized property tax 
rebates in the past, providing an established approach to delivering financial relief to property 
owners. 
 
Both alternatives would allow Council to provide property tax relief while maintaining the ability to 
reevaluate the amount of relief in response to future financial conditions. Unlike a permanent mill 
levy reduction, these approaches would preserve greater flexibility in balancing the benefits of tax 
relief with the Town's long-term financial obligations. 
 
FY 2027 Budget Implications 
If Town Council adopts the proposed ordinance, Staff will begin incorporating the anticipated 
revenue reduction into the proposed FY 2027 budget. The initial impact is estimated at 
approximately $1.2 million, requiring corresponding adjustments to planned expenditures and other 
budget assumptions. Staff will prepare a revised proposed budget for presentation at the first Town 
Council meeting in November, identifying the expenditures and initiatives adjusted or removed to 
accommodate the reduction.  
 
Initial budget adjustments would focus on preserving core municipal services while reevaluating 
discretionary expenditures, planned capital investments, and new recurring financial obligations. 
Although the first-year adjustments may be relatively manageable, each subsequent reduction in 
the mill levy would require further consideration of available resources and the Town's ability to 
sustain existing commitments and address future needs. 
 
Staff Recommendation 
Decisions regarding the Town's property tax mill levy are policy matters within the authority of Town 
Council. Staff's responsibility is to provide information regarding the financial and operational 
implications of the proposed reduction to support an informed policy decision. 
 
Staff recognizes that reducing property taxes would provide ongoing financial relief to property 
owners. At the same time, the proposed permanent reduction would decrease the Town's recurring 
revenue capacity and could limit its ability to address future infrastructure needs, maintain existing 
services, and respond to priorities identified by the community. 
 
Given the permanent nature of the proposed reduction, the uncertainty associated with long-term 
revenue projections, and the Town's anticipated capital and operational needs, Staff does not 
recommend adoption of the proposed permanent mill levy reduction. 
 
Should Town Council wish to provide property tax relief, Staff recommends consideration of a 
temporary property tax credit or property tax rebate program. Either approach would provide a 
mechanism for delivering relief to property owners while preserving greater flexibility to evaluate 
financial conditions, service expectations, and community investment priorities in future years.

(e) Lower Levy Permitted. Each Scheduled Levy is a maximum. The Town Council may, by 
resolution adopted before a Certification, fix a lower levy for that Certification, but no action of 
the Town Council is required for the Scheduled Levy to be certified. A lower levy does not raise 
or lower the Scheduled Levy for any later Certification. In no event shall the Town’s certified 
mill levy for any Certification exceed the lesser of the Scheduled Levy or the Town’s certified 
mill levy for the prior year, except as provided in Section 6. 
(f) Changes Only by Ordinance. A Scheduled Levy may be changed only by an ordinance 
adopted in accordance with Article 4 of the Charter that expressly amends this Section by 
reference to this Ordinance and that takes effect before the Certification to which the change 
would apply. No budget, appropriation, resolution, or motion shall amend, suspend, or waive any 
Scheduled Levy or any other provision of this Ordinance. Each Scheduled Levy not yet certified 
remains in full force until so amended. 
Section 3. Total Permanent Reduction; Certification in Mills. Upon certification of Step 4, 
the Town’s certified mill levy shall be permanently reduced by a total of 8.000 mills from the 
Base Levy (illustratively, from 23.947 mills to 15.947 mills). Each step and the total reduction 
shall be expressed and certified in mills, not as a fixed dollar amount, and the property tax 
revenue affected by the reduction will vary with the Town’s assessed valuation. This Ordinance 
does not establish a property tax credit, rebate, or refund. 
Section 4. Automatic One-Year Pause Only Upon Objective Conditions. 
(a) Conditions. A pause occurs automatically, without action of the Town Council, for a 
Certification at which Step 1, 2, 3, or 4 would otherwise be taken if, and only if, one of the 
following conditions is met: 
(1) Assessed Value Decline. The Town’s total net assessed valuation for that certification 
year, as shown in the most recent certifications of valuation for assessment provided by the Weld 
County and Larimer County Assessors before the Certification, combined, is more than ten 
percent (10%) lower than the Town’s total net assessed valuation as finally certified by those 
Assessors for the prior certification year, combined; or 
(2) Declared Disaster. A disaster emergency declared by the Governor under C.R.S. § 24-
33.5-704, or a local disaster emergency declared under C.R.S. § 24-33.5-709 and continued with 
the consent of the Town Council beyond seven (7) days, in either case for a disaster occurring 
within the Town’s boundaries, was in effect at any time during the twelve (12) months before the 
Certification. A declaration based solely on drought or water-supply conditions does not meet 
this condition. 
(b) Determination. Before each Certification, the Finance Director shall determine whether a 
condition in subsection (a) is met, based solely on the Assessors’ certifications and the 
declarations described in subsection (a), and shall report the determination in writing to the 
Town Council. The determination is ministerial. No motion, resolution, or other action of the 
Town Council is required for a pause to occur, and a pause shall not occur except as provided in

this Section. Nothing in this Section limits the Town Council’s authority under Section 2(e) to 
fix a lower levy. 
(c) One Pause Only. Only one pause shall occur under this Ordinance, and it shall apply to a 
single Certification. After a pause has occurred, no further pause shall occur, even if a condition 
in subsection (a) is met again. No pause shall occur after Step 4 has been certified. 
(d) Effect of a Pause. In a pause year, the step that would otherwise be taken shall not be taken, 
and the Scheduled Levy for that Certification shall be the Scheduled Levy for the prior 
Certification. In no event shall the Town’s certified mill levy for a pause year exceed the Town’s 
certified mill levy for the prior year. A pause delays the next step and each remaining step by one 
Certification; it does not reduce, cancel, or reverse any step. 
(e) Completion. The remaining steps shall resume at the Certification immediately following the 
pause year and shall continue at consecutive annual Certifications. Whether or not a pause 
occurs, all four steps shall be completed no later than the fifth Certification counted from and 
including the First Certification Year. For example, if a pause occurs in the third certification 
year, the levy would be held at 19.947 mills for that year, and Steps 3 and 4 would be taken in 
the fourth and fifth certification years. 
(f) Upon certification of Step 4, the reduction is complete, and the Town’s certified mill levy 
shall not exceed the Base Levy less 8.000 mills (illustratively, 15.947 mills) except as provided 
in Section 6. 
Section 5. Reductions Apply to General Operating Mills; Library and Restricted Mills 
Excluded.  
(a) The reductions under this Ordinance apply to, and shall be taken from, the Town’s 
unrestricted general operating mills. The reductions shall not reduce the mills dedicated to the 
Town’s library, currently 3.3 mills, consisting of 1.8 mills approved by the Town’s voters and 
1.5 mills dedicated by resolution of the Town Council. In each Certification, the library mills 
shall continue to be certified and allocated to the library in the same manner as before this 
Ordinance, and each step shall be taken only from the general operating levy. Illustratively, 
based on a Base Levy of 23.947 mills and assuming no other excluded mills, the Town’s general 
operating mills would be reduced from 20.647 mills to 18.647, 16.647, 14.647, and 12.647 mills 
at Steps 1 through 4, while the library mills remain at 3.3 mills. 
(b) Any debt-service mills and any otherwise restricted, voter-approved, or contractual mills 
identified in writing by the Town Attorney and Finance Director are likewise excluded from the 
reductions under this Ordinance to the extent required by law or existing obligation. The Finance 
Director shall reflect such exclusions in the mill-levy certification materials and any required 
state or county forms. 
Section 6. Restoration Only Upon New Voter Approval; Prior Voter Approvals Not Used.  
(a) It is the intent of the Town Council that this Ordinance permanently reduce the Town’s 
underlying and certified mill levy and that it not constitute a temporary property tax credit or 
temporary mill levy rate reduction under C.R.S. § 39-1-111.5, C.R.S. § 29-1-306, C.R.S. § 29-1-
1702, or any similar provision.
