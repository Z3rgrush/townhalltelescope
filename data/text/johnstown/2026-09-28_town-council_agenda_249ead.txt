SUBJECT: FY 2027 – Proposed Preliminary Budget 
 
 
It is with great pleasure to provide the Mayor, Town Council, and the Community in compliance 
with CRS 29-1-105, with the FY 2027 proposed budget. This document is without question, the 
most important action made annually by the Town Council as a governing body. I am hopeful 
that this important document represents the direction and objectives identified by the Council. As 
part of this presentation, we will move through the budget on a fund level basis, providing an 
overview of the departments’ requests. We welcome questions, comments, requested changes 
and adjustments, and the ensuing dialogue to provide a stronger understanding of the budget and 
the planned outcomes for FY 2027.

Water Revenue Bonds  
In 2024, the Town issued Water Revenue Bonds to fund capital projects related to the water 
treatment plant and water infrastructure. The bonds were issued with a PAR amount of 
$65,025,000 which represents the outstanding principal on the debt schedule. By December 
31, 2026, the outstanding principal balance will be $61,680,000. In 2027, the total payment 
due is $4,684,000, which includes $1,542,000 towards the principal and $3,142,000 in 
interest. A copy of the debt schedule is attached with this memorandum. 
  
Regarding sinking funds of concern, there are funds that have balances that are trending 
lower in FY 2027. These funds include the General Fund, Conservation Trust Fund, Sewer 
Fund, and Drainage Fund.  
 
The General Fund has trended lower in its ending fund balance predominantly due to capital 
projects currently and on the horizon. The general fund will be investing in water through the 
purchase of future cash-in-lieu associated with a pending investment in NISP. This will 
create an asset for the general fund that they can monetize with future cash -in-lieu sales. 
While the fund is trending lower, Staff is believes we  are in a stable position, having a 
positive financial outlook in the General Fund and a healthy fund balance still available to 
cover operational and maintenance costs.  
 
The Conservation Trust Fund experiences notable peaks and valleys of revenue. Funds are 
received from lottery proceeds and uses are statutorily restricted to park maintenance and 
capital projects related items thereto. Reporting requirements are also mandated by the State. 
In an effort to more easily comply with statutory requirements, we opt to accrue funds over 
several years and then complete a capital project at a park with the funding , rather than 
individually tracking granular level maintenance of parks on an annual basis. In FY 2027, we 
are planning for a capital project with the Conservation Trust Funds, hence the reduction in 
fund balances at the end of 2027.  The specific project we are exploring is the installation of 
pickleball courts in the Community. 
 
The Sewer Fund will also be trending downward in 2027. This is due to the fact that a 
number of major capital projects in this fund have been completed. In 2023, this fund 
received a grant from the general fund to support its initiatives and provide rate control on 
the unfunded mandates imposed by CDPHE. With the major projects completed, there was a 
return of funds back to the general fund for general operational purposes and Community 
betterment. 
 
Finally, the Drainage Fund will be trending downward in its ending fund balances. The Town 
recently completed an updated Stormwater Master Plan which was adopted by Council in 
2026. The master plan also called for a substantial increase in monthly rates to stormwater 
management system improvements that could not be appropriated to future development as 
part of their creation of additional impervious surfaces. Rates have not been increased for 
both existing and to be developed property since the inception of the fund. While Town 
Council has yet to make a final decision on the future of both monthly rates to existing 
residential and commercial development and future development impact fees, we are

Introduction 
 
Overall, the budget portions presented are in a stable financial position and balanced. This is due 
to the accountability and expectations created by the Citizens, coupled with the leadership of the 
Town Council. Accordingly, Staff recognizes and understands the importance of ensuring 
financial sustainability for the organization and the Community. The budget is presented on a 
fund level. Within each fund are proposed revenues, expenditures, and ending fund balances. 
The budget presented ensures that services both internal and external are either maintained at 
their current level or improved for a better community experience. Below are brief highlights of 
some of the FY 202 7 projects and outcomes proposed. We intend to walk through the entire 
budget, highlighting other outcomes requested by Council beyond the operational components. 
Feedback from Council on any requested changes they’d like to request and are encouraged prior 
to us beginning the public hearing portions of the FY 202 7 Budget. Two public hearings are 
planned and will be scheduled during each of the November 2026 Regular Council Meetings.

Council Priorities 
 
In June 2026, Council conducted a work session to identify priorities for the FY 202 7 Budget. 
Below is a brief overview of the list of topics visioned by Town Council for consideration in the 
FY 2027 budget . It is also worth noting that some of the projects listed below are from previous 
fiscal years that continue to move forward towards completion : 
 
• Senior & Affordable Housing/Continuum of Care – No funding has been allocated 
specifically for this program. If opportunities do present themselves to the Town, the 
Town Council would have discretion to incentivize a specific housing application and 
project. Staff has attempted to make these projects come to fruition in the past, but the 
financial requests upon the Town were unreasonable. We are working to capture other 
opportunities that may be successful in the Community and meet these specific needs. 
• Senior Center Facility and Staffing – The Town Council is anticipated to award a contract 
for construction of the Senior Center Facility in FY 2026. Funding for this capital 
construction project has been partially budgeted in FY 2026, with the remaining balance 
of the construction project budgeted in FY 2027. The budget allocated for this project 
over both fiscal years is approximately $18MM and includes the facility construction, 
offsite infrastructure needs for the facility, and FFE. Earlier this year, Thompson River 
Parks & Recreation District notified the Town of an intent to terminate the Memorandum 
of Understanding (MOU)  based on construction estimates. Construction of the facility is 
still anticipated in 2027 and will be located at Centennial Park. Finally, included in the 
FY 2027 budget is an additional staff member at the Senior Center.  
• Weld County Road (WCR) 46 – A priority identified by Council has been the WCR 46 
unpaved roadway between Colorado Blvd. (WCR 13) and Parish Avenue (WCR 17). 
This project will be similar to the recently completed roundabout at Roosevelt and 
Colorado Blvd. The partners will include two developers, responsible for Colorado Blvd. 
to Telep Avenue and from WCR 15 ½ to Parish Avenue respectively. This leaves a ½ 
mile section of the roadway from Telep to WCR 15½ incomplete. The Town has been 
working through engineering design of this section with plans to contract with one of the 
two contractors expected to complete this roadway. This will ensure the project is 
completed all at once in an interim configuration. Funding has been allocated in FY 2027 
at $1.5MM for this segment of the project. The Town expects to either receive 
reimbursement for its segment or will mandate the adjacent property owners to complete 
the roadway to its ultimate major arterial design, when the properties are developed. 
• Sandra Drive Drainage Project – Engineering design and easement acquisition continues 
on the Sandra Drive Project. The design is expected to be completed in the next few 
months, pending the acquisition of easement with a subsequent public bid for the project. 
Funding for this project has been allocated in FY 2027 in the general fund in the amount 
of $5.6MM. 
• Centennial Park Phasing and Development – Park development continues to be a priority 
for both the Council and the Community. At Centennial, there are a number of 
environmental clearances that need to be addressed before substantive construction can 
take place on this site beyond the Senior Center. The preliminary analysis of this 
engineering and environmental evaluations will be completed this year. Subsequent to

support these industry types.

and design engineering will be the primary objectives in 2027 for this scope of work.  
• Open Space/Parks Sales Tax & Term Limit Ballot Initiatives– No funding is included in 
the FY 2027 budget for either of these purposes based on recent discussions with Council 
and their desire to not to act on a ballot measure until at least the April 2028 election 
cycle. In April 2026, voters rejected a ballot measure for a 0.5% Open Space, Parks, and 
Trails sales tax by a 52.4% to 47.6% margin.  
• IT Functionality – IT functionality as always continues to be a priority at the Town to 
secure our networks, systems, and infrastructure. To this end and for better coordination 
and overall responsiveness, the Town has hired an in-house IT Manager who will begin 
in 2026 to oversee our systems and coordinate continued 3 rd party IT and software 
contracts. 
• Gradual Decrease of Mill Levy – It is the recommendation of Staff to not reduce the mill 
levy as suggested in 2027. This could have negative impacts on the delivery of services to 
the Community as well as greater impact on future projects being planned in and for the 
Community, especially water asset security. Ultimately, Council will need to vote on the 
mill levy as part of the budget adoption process. It is anticipated that property tax 
revenues will decline in FY 2027 by over $500,000 compared to FY 2026.  
 
General Fund 
 
The General Fund has a healthy reserve balance of approximately $26,541,896 or 1.2 year’s 
operational reserves at the end of FY 202 7. While our current financial policies require 6 
months’ operational reserves, there was a request from Council to consider providing for 1.5 
year’s. It is important to note that operational reserves are different than the total budget. 
Operational reserves are the ending fund balance compared to the annual operational 
expenditures for a specific fund, while the total budget for a fund includes capital outlays. The 
Page 9 of 110
The Community that Cares 
measurement of operational reserves against its expenditures is a more justified measurement 
since the capital items are generally larger expenses which, if necessary, can be paused until 
funding levels are more appropriate for the improvement(s).  
 
As we look out onto the horizon, I do not believe that it is necessary to allocate additional 
funding to projects or programs, but this is completely within the authority and direction of the 
Town Council. I believe Staff has more than enough on their plate currently and adding 
additional responsibilities and accomplishments would be irresponsible, and more than likely not 
achievable unless staffing levels change. If Council would prefer to defer specific projects and 
add others in replacement, this would be a more prudent approach. We have a number of 
upcoming projects in the general fund that will require us to likely draw down on this reserve 
fund balance. These projects that are either budgeted in FY 202 7 or will be in the future include: 
construction of a Senior/Community Center, the desperate need for a new PW/Utility Facility, a 
full commitment to parks and recreation activities that the Community is eager to see developed 
(and Council is committed to), and consideration on the participation in a regional water project 
commonly referred to as the Northern Integrated Supply Project (NISP) . Spending the funds on 
projects that don’t improve services or deliver additional community amenities should be 
carefully considered, in terms of the value add they deliver to our Community.

Water Fund 
 
The Water Fund has several critical projects planned in 2027. The specific projects that will 
assist with water demands on the system (especially during the summer season of high-water 
usage) and improving performance of the treatment system. We have a stable fund balance in the 
water fund and will continue to monitor this balance closely as the number of water projects over

the next decade will be significant to ensure long-term reliability, redundancy, and resiliency for 
our system. Some of the highlights in the water fund include the following items:   
• Payment on the Water Revenue Bonds for FY 2027 totaling $4,684,000.  
• Construction of the new Water Treatment Plant Expansion. This will increase the plant 
capacity from 6.2 MGD to 10.5 MGD with a planned rerating to 12 MGD. The 
construction is scheduled for substantial completion in 2026 and final completion in Q2 
of 2027. The project will create a state of the art treatment facility for the Community; 
replacing a system that had become obsolete and difficult to manage given its estimated 
60 year age. Total construction costs are expected to be approximately $85MM over the 
term of the project. Funding for FY 2027 is $7.5MM. 
• A raw water trunk line is also going to be an essential part to building additional capacity 
in the water system. We are currently near completion of design and easement 
acquisitions now. Earlier this year a Construction Manager at Risk (CMAR) RFP was 
issued and a pending award is expected around the end of the calendar year. Construction 
is scheduled to begin in 2027 and will occur over two fiscal years. The funds budgeted 
for FY 2027 is $25MM.  
• Concurrent with the plant improvements, is the need to replace the Dissolved Air 
Flotation (DAF) skimmers. This takes the solids off the water during the treatment 
process and minimizes turbidity in the system. Completion of the DAF skimmer 
replacement project will occur in 2027.  
• SCADA connectivity is a critical component to ensuring success of our water 
transmission, treatment and distribution system. The Town will be making investments in 
this system in FY 2027 for better operability. 
• Rates increases in the water fund have been substantial and significant over the past three 
(3) years, increasing annually by 30%. These large increases were necessary and 
followed a cost-based rate setting to ensure prices reflect actual expenses rather than 
volatile market demand. Looking forward in 2027, fees are set to increase by 5% for the 
water utility rate charges for monthly service. 
• Town Council earlier this year was introduced to the Northern Integrated Supply Project 
(NISP). Preliminary funding has been allocated in the water fund for participation in the 
Northern Integrated Supply Project. The Northern Integrated Supply Project (NISP) will 
deliver up to 40,000 acre-feet of new, reliable water annually to water providers across 
the Northern Front Range. The project will help secure a reliable water future while 
enhancing recreation, supporting environmental stewardship, improving infrastructure 
safety and reducing water waste. Later in 2026, Council is expected to make a final 
determination of participation in NISP. Assuming participation in this project, Staff has 
allocated funding from the General Fund for water purchase to facilitate payment of this 
project. Support of this project may come through cash in lieu and general fund purchase 
of water from the water fund in the interim given the large financial outlay of this project. 
• The last capital item is the purchase of a new F250 vehicle for the collection and 
distribution division of the utility department. This cost is split between the water and 
sewer funds.

control the various zones of the water distribution system. One is planned at Highway 60
