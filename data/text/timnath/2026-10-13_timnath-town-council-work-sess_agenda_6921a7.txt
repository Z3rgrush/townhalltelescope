Summary
Historically, the Town has prepared five-year financial projections as part of the annual budget process to evaluate 
compliance with reserve policy targets and ensure the budget remains structurally balanced, with recurring revenues 
sufficient to fund recurring expenditures throughout the forecast period. These projections incorporate the priorities 
identified by Council during the August 11, 2026 budget work session, including establishing an open space reserve 
funded through a portion of parks and recreation revenues and land sale proceeds, strategically reducing event 
expenditures while increasing sponsorship support, and continuing economic development initiatives.
The projections also reflect priorities contained within the Strategic Plan and a robust Capital Improvement Plan that 
emphasizes contractual obligations, safety needs, and other critical infrastructure investments while maximizing the 
use of restricted funding sources, including impact fees, Timnath Development Authority revenues, and other 
dedicated funding streams. To address long-term capital needs, the forecast assumes the issuance of Certificates of 
Participation (COPs). Additional appropriations include the implementation of recommendations from the 
compensation study, funding for master planning studies, while balancing staffing needs as the Town grows. Lastly, as 
the Town advances toward construction and operation of the recreation center, several key positions will be added and 
funded through the Community Center Fund.
Strategic Plan Investments
In addition to the capital and personnel requests outlined in detail elsewhere in this report, the proposed 2027 budget 
includes funding for a number of initiatives that advance the Town Council's adopted Strategic Plan. These 
investments support key priorities across Community Character, High-Performing Government, Quality Living, and 
Safe and Healthy Community and are intended to further the community's long-term vision while maintaining fiscal 
responsibility. The following highlights the major initiatives funded within the proposed budget. The priorities align 
with the Town's strategic goals related to community identity, economic vitality, financial sustainability, employee 
retention, open space preservation, and community engagement.   The plan's specific goals and performance 
indicators are detailed here. 
Community Character
To establish and uphold distinctive, high-quality design standards that reinforce Timnath's unique identity, the 
proposed budget includes:
Community Wayfinding Plan: $30,000

To further position Old Town as one of Timnath's civic and social anchors, the proposed budget includes:

To continue fostering connections among residents of all ages and backgrounds, the proposed budget:
Maintains the same number of community events offered in 2026.

approximately $50,000 in savings while maintaining the existing fireworks display.
Funds development of an Open Space Master Plan: $200,000

Long-Range Operating Plan
During the budget process, the Town identifies and forecasts staffing, operating, and capital needs for the budget year and the subsequent four years. 
This long-range operating plan is updated annually as part of the budget process and is summarized by fund below.
Key assumptions reflected in the projections include the expiration of the Timnath Development Authority (TDA) in 2029, construction, financing, and 
operation of the Recreation Center, financing of infrastructure improvements in 2027, and the establishment of an Open Space Acquisition Fund 
beginning in 2027. These projections have been updated to reflect the estimated 2025 year-end fund balances, anticipated 2026 revenues and 
expenditures, and currently known future financial factors. 
While all funds are included for tracking purposes, appropriations for capital expansion fees and capital improvement activities are presented in a 
summarized format. Based on the current projections, the Town is expected to maintain compliance with its reserve policy. The policy allows fund balance 
to fall below the 25% working reserve target during periods of significant capital investment, such as in 2029 and 2030, provided a plan exists to restore 
reserves, which is projected to occur in 2031. 
Residual fund balance in the Capital Improvement Community Center Fund is composed of interest earnings and can be used towards the Recreation 
Center project.
The Community Center forecast operating revenue and expenditures are based on the original pro forma and are highly subjective to change until the 
final design is complete and ongoing operating expenditures are re-evaluated.  The available fund balance will be used to fund an operating and 
replacement reserve and then towards repayment of debt service.

Personnel 
As the Town continues to experience growth and prepares for the future opening of a Recreation Center, additional 
staffing resources are necessary to maintain service levels, support existing and new operations, and meet increasing 
demands across Town departments. Staff have carefully evaluated and prioritized these requests, and in several cases 
have restructured responsibilities or eliminated vacant positions to maximize organizational efficiency. 
For 2027, Department Heads have identified 13 position requests totaling 11.05 FTE's as part of the budget 
development process. These positions are intended to support critical service needs, enhance operational capacity, 
and ensure the organization remains well-positioned to serve a growing community.  
In addition to the personnel changes outlined above, the budget includes the following key assumptions:
2% market adjustment applied to all employee salaries and pay ranges.
3% merit pool allocated to non-step plan employees.
Step plan pay structure was updated from a 6-step, 4% annual increase to a 5-step, 6% annual increase
$132,000 earmarked to address pay compression and equity concerns. Following a recent salary study, more than 
83% of the Town’s non-step employees earn below the midpoint of their salary range. This funding will support 
recruitment and retention efforts.
Fund several promotions that will be evaluated and based on justification and organizational needs.
1.04% increase in retirement contributions as mandated by Colorado PERA, raising the rate from 15.80% to 16.84%.
10% projected increase in medical insurance premiums through its current insurance provider, Cigna. 
The Town proposes authorizing the Police Department to overfill sworn Police Officer positions by up to two 
employees. This flexibility will help maintain service levels during routine vacancies, employee leave, light-duty 
assignments, and training periods that can temporarily reduce available staffing. Consistent staffing is critical to 
meeting the community's public safety needs and maintaining adequate shift coverage. The department 
anticipates this approach will be budget neutral, as vacancies and temporary absences typically occur 
throughout the year. However, if the department remains fully staffed for an extended period, a supplemental 
appropriation may be required to cover the additional personnel costs.
Three internal service positions (IT, Finance and HR) have been added to support the growth of the Recreation 
Center.   These were included in the Recreation Center proforma.   For budgetary purposes, these positions are 
100% appropriated to the Community Center Fund.  However, a proper allocation of all staff hours within these 
departments will occur as we will not have dedicated internal service staff dedicated to the Rec Center, but a 
portion of all staff within these departments will be responsible for increased demands for the Recreation Center.

General Fund
The general fund is a major governmental fund for the town.  The general fund accounts for all revenues and expenditures for the town which are not 
accounted for in other funds.  Revenues are primarily serviced by sales tax, use tax, building permit fees and property taxes.  General fund revenues are 
used to maintain and operate the general government.  The following departments are accounted for in the general fund: Administration, Legal, 
Community Development, Communications, Finance, Human Resources, Information Technology, Municipal Court, Town Clerk, Facilities, Events, Public 
Safety, and Public Works.

Timnath Development Authority 
The Timnath Development Authority (TDA) is an urban renewal authority that finances public improvements within the TDA boundaries through tax 
increment financing (TIF). Primary revenue sources include property tax increment and sales tax increment generated within the district. Sales tax 
revenues in excess of debt service requirements are transferred to the Town's General Fund, while additional transfers support capital projects within the 
TDA area through the Capital Improvement Fund. Debt service payments are made semi-annually on the Authority's outstanding obligations. The TIF 
district is scheduled to expire in 2029, coinciding with the retirement of the Authority's outstanding debt.

Capital Improvement Plan
Capital expenditures for 2027 include facilities, parks, roads, and utility projects that are in various phases of 
completion (planning, design, and construction).  Also included are capital costs for equipment and software and are 
either replacement of existing, enhancements, or new.  Staff projects capital needs for 5 years.  Our 2027–2031 and 
"future years."  Future years are used to capture any known capital needs that exceed the current projection period.  
Capital Budgeting Process
Capital requests are submitted by staff then reviewed and scored by Department Heads.  Once submitted, Finance 
determines available funding streams for capital projects including impact fees, general fund, grants or other 
resources.  If capital projects exceed available resources for any of the five years, Departments are then asked to re-
prioritize or shift timelines of capital projects.  
Basis for Capital Revenue and Expenditures Estimates
The Town does estimate inflation for future years' costs and also estimates any changes to operating costs as a result 
of the capital project and those are then included in the budget and subsequent year projections.  For the purpose of 
reporting, the historical actual amounts for each project include actual expenditures through 2025 and the 2026 
projected expenditure amounts and exclude funding sources. Certain projects are recurring annually.  If they are 
recurring annually, historical amounts are not shown.  
Capital Improvement Project  Prioritization Process
The following considerations are given and projects based on the following: Strategic Goal, Operational Impact, Legal 
Impact, and Safety and Security.  The town will prioritize projects based on this scorecard.  Funding constraints can 
also determine the priority of certain projects if funded by grant or other restricted resources limited by time.  
Definitions and Criteria for Capital Projects
Capital requests are required for improvements, replacements, new purchases of vehicles, buildings and facilities, 
parks, roads and utilities, and stormwater with a useful life of more than one year and a cost of more than $5,000.  All 
new computer software purchases or upgrades, regardless of cost, must be entered as a capital request.  New 
computers or related equipment with a value of $300 or more is also required to be entered as a capital request. 
 Anything else that does not meet this definition will be considered operating.
