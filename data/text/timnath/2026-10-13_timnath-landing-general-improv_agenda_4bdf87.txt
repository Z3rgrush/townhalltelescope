holds responsibility for the preparation of the financial statements, the 
auditors' role is to provide an independent opinion as to whether those 
statements present fairly in all material respects — a standard the 
District met for 2025.

DESCRIPTION 
An Ordinance Authorizing the Issuance and Sale of the District's Limited Tax General 
Obligation Bonds, Series 2026A and Subordinate Limited Tax General Obligation 
Bonds, Series 2026B 
 
SUMMARY 
This ordinance authorizes the issuance and sale of the Timnath Landing General 
Improvement District's Limited Tax General Obligation Bonds (2026A) and Subordinate 
Limited public finance will bonds The Tax Bonds Obligation General (2026B). 
improvements as approved by district electors, reimburse the developer and the Town 
for certain costs, and establish the legal and financial framework for the District's 
obligations. 
 
KEY POINTS 
 The ordinance authorizes two series of bonds (2026A and 2026B) to fund public 
improvements, reimburse the developer, and cover issuance costs.  This 
ordinance was previously approved by the District Board in December 2025. A 
new ordinance is needed to re-authorize given the extended timeframe. 
 District voters previously approved the issuance of debt and imposition of taxes 
for improvements in streets, parks, water, sanitation, safety, operations, and 
Page 1 of 2
Page 4 of 25
refunding (with specified principal amounts). Bonds are sold to accredited 
investors or qualified institutional buyers. 
 Senior and subordinate bonds are secured by pledged revenues and subject to 
specific redemption and maturity parameters. 
 Delegated authority is given to Board members and the Treasurer to finalize 
bond terms within set parameters. 
 The ordinance details the adoption process, including public hearings, Board 
votes, and publication requirements. 
 The developer has paid approximately $6 million to date  
 The originating ordinance of the Timnath Landings General Improvement District, 
and the Timnath Landings Subdivision Improvement Agreement and 
reimbursement agreement between the GID and the Developer are guiding 
documents for allowable costs under the debt issuances. 
 The Finance Committee reviewed the proposed financing terms in 2025. 
 
ADVANTAGES 
 Requires continuing disclosure and public reporting, and procedural safeguards 
for adoption and amendment. 
 Ensures the developer is reimbursed for extraordinary improvement costs, 
supporting ongoing development. 
 Allows the Town to be reimbursed for organizational costs and ongoing 
administrative costs of the District.          
 
DISADVANTAGES 
 Commits the District to long-term debt repayment, which may impact future 
budgets and financial flexibility. 
 The ordinance and related agreements are legally and financially complex, 
requiring careful administration and oversight.        
  
 
RECOMMENDED MOTION 
I move to approve the ordinance Authorizing the Issuance and Sale of the District's 
Limited Tax General Obligation Bonds, Series 2026A and Subordinate Limited Tax 
General Obligation Bonds, Series 2026B.

Pledge of Revenues SECTION 14. .  The creation, perfection, enforcement, and 
priority of the pledge of revenues to secure or pay the Bonds as provided herein shall be 
governed by Section 11-57-208 of the Supplemental Act, the Indentures, and this Ordinance.  
The revenues pledged for the payment of the Bonds, as received by or otherwise credited to the 
District, shall immediately be subject to the lien of such pledge without any physical delivery, 
filing, or further act.  The lien of such pledge on the revenues pledged for payment of the Bonds 
and the obligation to perform the contractual provisions made herein and in the Indentures shall 
have priority over any or all other obligations and liabilities of the District, except for any parity 
obligations.  The lien of such pledge shall be valid, binding, and enforceable as against all 
persons having claims of any kind in tort, contract, or otherwise against the District irrespective 
of whether such persons have notice of such liens.
SECTION 15. No Recourse Against Officers and Agents.  Pursuant to Section 
11-57-209 of the Supplemental Act, if a member of the Board, or any officer or agent of the 
District acts in good faith, no civil recourse shall be available against such member, officer, or 
agent for payment of the principal, interest, or prior redemption premiums on the Bonds.  Such 
recourse shall not be available either directly or indirectly through the Board or the District, or 
otherwise, whether by virtue of any constitution, statute, rule of law, enforcement of penalty, or 
otherwise.  By the acceptance of the Bonds and as a part of the consideration of their sale or 
purchase, any person purchasing or selling such Bond specifically waives any such recourse.
Limitation of Actions SECTION 16. .  Pursuant to Section 11-57-212 of the 
Supplemental Act, no legal or equitable action brought with respect to any legislative acts or 
proceedings in connection with the authorization or issuance of the Bonds shall be commenced 
more than thirty days after the adoption of this Ordinance, which authorizes the Bonds.

SECTION 17. Conclusive Recital. Pursuant to Section 11-57-210 of the 
Supplemental Act, the Bonds shall contain a recital that they are issued pursuant to the 
Supplemental Act.  Such recital shall be conclusive evidence of the validity and the regularity of 
the issuance of the Bonds after their delivery for value.
SECTION 18. Town Ordinance No. 22, Series 2025.  The Board hereby 
acknowledges that the Town Council of the Town has adopted Ordinance No. 22, Series 2025, 
which amends Ordinance No. 9, Series 2019 to allow the District to adjust its maximum 
aggregate mill levy of 15 mills to offset changes caused by SB24-233 and HB24B-1001 and 
future legislative changes impacting the way that property taxes are calculated. The Board 
hereby consents to the amendment made by Ordinance No. 22, Series 2025 to Ordinance No. 9, 
Series 2019 and adopts the changes set forth therein pursuant to this Ordinance. 
Severability SECTION 19. .  If any section, paragraph, clause, or provision of 
this Ordinance shall for any reason be held to be invalid or unenforceable, the invalidity or 
unenforceability of such section, paragraph, clause, or provision shall not affect any of the 
remaining provisions of this Ordinance, the intent being that the same are severable.
Ordinance Irrepealable SECTION 20. .  After any of the Bonds have been 
issued, this Ordinance shall constitute a contract between the District and the Owner or Owners 
of the Bonds, and between the District and the Trustee, and shall be and remain irrepealable until 
the Bonds and the interest thereon, shall have been fully paid, satisfied and discharged, as herein 
and therein provided.
Repealer SECTION 21. .  Ordinance No. GID-1, Series 2025, adopted by the 
Board on December 9, 2025 is hereby repealed. All other acts, ordinances and resolutions or 
parts thereof in conflict with this Ordinance are hereby rescinded, annulled and repealed.  This 
repealer shall not be construed to revive any act, ordinance or resolution, or part thereof, 
heretofore repealed.
Disposition of Ordinance SECTION 22. .  This Ordinance, as adopted by 
the Town Council, acting ex officio as the Board, shall be numbered and recorded by the Town 
Clerk in the official records of the Town.  The adoption and publication shall be authenticated by 
the signatures of the Mayor and the Town Clerk, as Chairperson and Secretary of the District 
respectively, and by the certificate of publication.
