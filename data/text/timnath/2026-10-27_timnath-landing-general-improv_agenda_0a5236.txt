SUMMARY 
This ordinance authorizes the issuance and sale of the Timnath Landing General 
Improvement District's Limited Tax General Obligation Bonds (2026A) and Subordinate 
Limited public finance will bonds The Obligation Bonds General Tax (2026B). 
improvements as approved by district electors, reimburse the developer and the Town 
for certain costs, and establish the legal and financial framework for the District's 
obligations. 
 
KEY POINTS 
 The ordinance authorizes two series of bonds (2026A and 2026B) to fund public 
improvements, reimburse the developer, and cover issuance costs. This 
ordinance was previously approved by the District Board in December 2025. A 
new ordinance is needed to re-authorize given the extended timeframe. 
Page 1 of 2
Page 4 of 46
 District voters previously approved the issuance of debt and imposition of taxes 
for improvements in streets, parks, water, sanitation, safety, operations, and 
refunding (with specified principal amounts). Bonds are sold to accredited 
investors or qualified institutional buyers. 
 Senior and subordinate bonds are secured by pledged revenues and subject to 
specific redemption and maturity parameters. 
 Delegated authority is given to Board members and the Treasurer to finalize 
bond terms within set parameters. 
 The ordinance details the adoption process, including public hearings, Board 
votes, and publication requirements. 
 The developer has paid approximately $6 million to date.  
 The originating ordinance of the Timnath Landings General Improvement District, 
and the Timnath Landings Subdivision Improvement Agreement and 
reimbursement agreement between the GID and the Developer are guiding 
documents for allowable costs under the debt issuances. 
 The Finance Committee reviewed the proposed financing terms in 2025. 
 
ADVANTAGES 
 Requires continuing disclosure and public reporting, and procedural safeguards 
for adoption and amendment. 
 Ensures the developer is reimbursed for extraordinary improvement costs, 
supporting ongoing development. 
 Allows the Town to be reimbursed for organizational costs and ongoing 
administrative costs of the District.          
 
DISADVANTAGES 
 Commits the District to long-term debt repayment, which may impact future 
budgets and financial flexibility. 
 The ordinance and related agreements are legally and financially complex, 
requiring careful administration and oversight.

Requested Action  
As municipal advisor to the Timnath Landing General Improvement District (the “District”), I recommend 
approval of the updated bond authorizing ordinance  on second reading. The ordinance authorizes the 
District’s Series 2026A Senior Bonds and Series 2026B Subordinate Bonds within the limits stipulated. It 
delegates to the Town’s Mayor authority to set final terms at pricing. Proceeds will be used to reimburse 
eligible public infrastructure costs within the District as agreed to by the Town of Timnath, the District, the 
Timnath Lakes Developer (the “Developer”).  
 
Background  
The District was established  in 2019 to reimburse the developer (currently Catellus) for eligible public 
infrastructure serving the Timnath Lakes development, including streets, water, sewer, and other related 
improvements. The District is a separate governmental entity governed by Town Council acting as its Board. 
The proposed bonds would provide funds to reimburse a portion of these infrastructure costs, with 
repayment supported by property tax revenues generated within the District under its authorized 15-mill 
levy.  
 
The Developer and Town have identified an estimated $14.5 million in eligible reimbursable public 
improvement costs . 
 
Timing and Ordinance Update  
The financing brought forward last year was postponed due to developer-related matters that affected the 
transaction schedule. Those maters have been resolved. The ordinance has been updated from the 2025 
version to reflect the revised plan of finance and current market conditions.  
 
The target financing timeline is detailed below:  
 
• October 13: First reading of authorizing ordinance  (complete) 
• October 27: Second reading of authorizing ordinance  
• November 24: Post offering docs / begin marketing of bonds  
• December 10: Target sale date  
• December 23: Target closing date  
 
Updated Plan of Finance  
The District proposes to issue approximately $12.23 million of bonds in 2026, providing roughly $9. 17 
million to reimburse the Timnath Lakes developer for eligible public infrastructure costs. The financing plan 
also illustrates a potential future transaction in 2031, as discussed below.

The 2026 Financing: The 2026 financing is expected to provide approximately $9.17 million for 
infrastructure reimbursement, compared with $6.45 million previously contemplated in 2025. Remaining 
proceeds would fund capitalized interest, a debt service reserve, and pay costs of issuance. The estimated 
aggregate all -in true interest cost is 7.38%.   
 
 
Interest rates reflect this security: a limited levy on a developing tax base with no Town backing. The 
subordinate bonds carry a higher rate because they are paid only after senior debt service is met. Final 
terms will be set in a sale certificate execute d by the Town’s Mayor within the authorizing ordinance’s 
parameters.  
 
The bonds are limited tax obligations of the District, payable from a 15-mill property tax levy, as adjusted 
under the November 2025 amendment. They are not obligations of the Town. No Town revenues or credit 
are pledged, and property outside the District is not subject to the levy. The Board covenants to impose the 
pledged levy each year while the bonds are outstanding.  
 
Most of the District also lies within the Timnath Development Authority (TDA). Under its agreement with the 
TDA, the District receives the increment generated by its levy on these properties as well until the tax 
increment district expires.  
 
After permitted operating expenses, pledged revenues satisfy senior bond requirements first, with 
remaining revenues available for subordinate payments. The plan also includes three years of capitalized 
interest on the senior bonds, with principal repayment increasing over time as the tax base is projected to 
grow. Actual revenues will be dependent on development and assessed values.  
 
The updated Plan of Finance also illustrates a potential refinancing in 2031, when the 2026 bonds are 
expected to become callable. If development as grown the tax base enough, a refinancing could lower 
borrowing costs and fund the remaining eligible costs. Any future issuance would depend on development, 
revenues, and market conditions, and would require separate Board approval.
