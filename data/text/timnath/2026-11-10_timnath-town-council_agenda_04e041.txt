3 PUBLIC COMMENT
Mayor Axmacher opened the meeting for public comment. He explained that items 6.a 
through 6.c were public hearings and that comments on them should be reserved for 
those items. Nine speakers addressed the Council.
• Shayna O'Kelly raised concerns about the proposed Century oil and gas pad 
project near Harmony Road and Weld County Road 15.
• Curt Bear, owner of Local Think Tank, urged better long-term planning for Main 
Street. He also encouraged a "live, work, and play" vision with a stronger 
business climate and support for small businesses.
• Steve Lampo spoke regarding WildWing and the lack of Town communication 
on road construction in front of the community. He questioned the duplication 
of fiber and utility infrastructure and landscaping damage. He additionally 
asked for ADA-accessible trail access and signage in WildWing.
• Dustin Peyser, President and co-founder of the Timnath Chamber of 
Commerce, described the Chamber's work. He reported about 60 members, 30 
events this year, Timnath Connect markets, and the Taste of Timnath passport 
program. He asked that the Chamber be recognized and heard.

• Tom Sweder raised the uneven geographic distribution of developed park 
amenities. He questioned the planned Timnath Ranch park near Community 
Park and asked the Council to consider walking access to developed 
amenities. He also asked for a public timeline and funding strategy for a park 
serving northern Timnath, and for geographic equity to be considered in the 
2027 capital budget. Mr. Sweder additionally emailed his public comment by 
5:00 p.m. on the day prior to the meeting.
• Laura Olive thanked the Town for the Christmas lights and asked that they be 
turned on early in November. She opposed a roundabout on the Parkway,

stated that each speaker receives one opportunity, and she did not speak 
further.

communication affecting Old Town businesses. She said Old Town was not 
mentioned in County Road 5 construction updates, and that promised gateway 
signage had not been installed. She asked if the Christmas lights be turned on 
early and thanked the Town for the new crosswalks.
• Eric Sutherland, a Fort Collins resident, spoke regarding the Timnath 
Elementary and urban renewal property tax diversions.

comment by 5:00 p.m. on the day prior to the meeting with a letter from 55 
residents supporting the development of a Poudre River Natural Park and 
Crossing.
• Danielle and Daren Eccles did not participate in person but did, however, email 
a public comment by 5:00 p.m. on the day prior to the meeting regarding the 
Century oil and gas pad.

a) ORDINANCE NO. 16, SERIES 2026, An Ordinance Amending Municipal Code 
to Add Prohibition on New On-Site Wastewater Treatment Systems and 
Graywater Treatment Works
• Senior Civil Engineer Vincent Gentry presented the ordinance.
Mayor Axmacher opened the hearing for public comments. No public 
comments were made.
• Councilmember Strachota asked about obstacles to allowing graywater, 
given water conservation needs. Community Development Director 
Scott Robinson explained that state law changed from an opt-in to an 
opt-out framework. He said allowing graywater would require amending 
the building code, setting up a monitoring and inspection program, and 
addressing the lack of proven certified technologies. He also cited health 
concerns, water rights limits on reuse of treated water, and possible 
effects on sewer system flows. Mr. Robinson said the building official 
expects the technology to mature. 
• Councilmember Strachota asked how many septic systems exist in 
town, and staff did not have a figure. Staff confirmed that variances for 
substantial hardship could be considered case by case under the Land 
Use Code variance process.
• Councilmember Holt supported setting limits until the technology is 
proven. 
• Councilmember Strachota supported the ordinance and hoped the Town 
would revisit graywater as systems improve.
Councilmember Jeramie Holt made a motion to approve ORDINANCE NO. 
16, SERIES 2026, An Ordinance Amending Municipal Code to Add 
Prohibition on New On-Site Wastewater Treatment Systems and 
Graywater Treatment Works. Mayor Pro Tem Luke Wagner seconded the 
motion. CARRIED unanimously.

SUMMARY 
The appointment of Viraaj Pahwa to the PROST Committee as a youth member who 
will fulfill the term of outgoing member Lauren Henderson. 
 
KEY POINTS 
The PROST Committee consists of 9 members. Members of the PROST committee 
serve 2 year terms. Lauren Henderson resigned her position to attend college leaving a 
vacancy. 
  
Per PROST Bylaws, when a vacancy occurs, a new member should be selected to fulfill 
the existing term.  
  
The vacant position was posted for a period greater than 10 days. We received multiple 
applications. Viraaj Pahwa was interviewed and selected by the interview committee to

Long-Range Operating Plan
During the budget process, the Town identifies and forecasts staffing, operating, and capital needs for the budget year and the subsequent four years. 
This long-range operating plan is updated annually as part of the budget process and is summarized by fund below.
Key assumptions reflected in the projections include the expiration of collection of revenue from the Timnath Development Authority (TDA) in 2029, 
construction, financing, and operation of the Recreation Center, financing of infrastructure improvements in 2027, and the establishment of an Open 
Space Acquisition Fund beginning in 2027. These projections have been updated to reflect the estimated 2026 year-end fund balances, anticipated 2027 
revenues and expenditures, and currently known future financial factors. 
While all funds are included for tracking purposes, appropriations for capital expansion fees and capital improvement activities are presented in a 
summarized format. Based on the current projections, the Town is expected to maintain compliance with its reserve policy. The policy allows fund 
balances to fall below the 25% working reserve target during periods of significant capital investment provided a plan exists to restore reserves.  
The remaining fund balance in the Capital Improvement Community Center Fund consists of interest earnings available for the Recreation Center project. 
 Council recommended allocating 1% of the Recreation Center budget, or $980,000, toward public art. This commitment is expected to be funded from 
interest earnings available to the project.
The Community Center fund operating revenues and expenditures are based on the original pro forma and are subject to change as the final design is 
completed and operating costs are further evaluated.  Available fund balance will first be used to establish operating and replacement reserves, with 
remaining funds available for debt repayment. At Council’s recommendation, excess funds within the Community Center Fund will be transferred to the 
Debt Service Fund which is intended to build a reserve for the early retirement of outstanding debt.

Personnel 
As the Town continues to experience growth and prepares to scale up for the future opening of a Recreation Center, 
additional staffing resources are necessary to maintain service levels, support existing and new operations, and meet 
increasing demands across Town departments. Staff have carefully evaluated and prioritized these requests, and in 
several cases have restructured responsibilities or eliminated future positions to maximize organizational efficiency to 
meet overall budget goals.
For 2027, Department Heads have identified 11 position requests totaling 10.55 FTE's as part of the budget 
development process. These positions are intended to support critical service needs, enhance operational capacity, 
and ensure the organization remains well-positioned to serve a growing community.  
In addition to the personnel changes outlined above, the budget includes the following key assumptions:
2% Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA) applied to all employee salaries and pay ranges.
3% merit pool allocated to non-step plan employees.
$132,000 earmarked for market adjustments for market adjustments to specific jobs that have fallen below the 
market. 
$25,000 to fund several promotions that will be evaluated and based on justification and organizational needs.
1.04% increase in retirement contributions as mandated by Colorado PERA, raising the rate from 15.80% to 16.84%.
10% projected increase in medical insurance premiums through its current insurance provider, Cigna. 
The Town may as available, allow the Police Department to overfill sworn Police Officer positions by up to two 
employees. This flexibility will help maintain service levels during routine vacancies, employee leave, light-duty 
assignments, and training periods that can temporarily reduce available staffing. Consistent staffing is critical to 
meeting the community's public safety needs and maintaining adequate shift coverage. The department 
anticipates this approach will be budget neutral, as vacancies and temporary absences typically occur 
throughout the year. However, if the department remains fully staffed for an extended period, a supplemental 
appropriation may be required to cover the additional personnel costs.
Three internal service positions (IT, Finance and HR) have been added and will be phased over the course of 2027 
to support the growth and development of plans for the upcoming opening of the Recreation Center.   These 
positions were included in the Recreation Center pro forma.  For budgetary purposes, these positions are 100% 
appropriated to the Community Center Fund. However, a proper allocation of all staff hours within these 
departments will occur as we will not have dedicated internal service staff dedicated to the Rec Center, but a 
portion of all staff within these departments will be responsible for increased demands for the Recreation Center.
The following positions will be added in 2027.  
System Adminstrator
Fund: Community Center Fund
Purpose:  Provides senior-level administration of Microsoft 365, cybersecurity, network and server systems, endpoint 
management, CJIS compliance, and technology support across Town operations.
Need: As the Town's technology environment continues to grow in complexity and risk, this position will strengthen 
cybersecurity, improve system reliability and documentation, reduce key-person dependency, and provide dedicated 
support for critical technology systems, including Police Department operations.

Human Resource Manager
Fund: Community Center Fund
Purpose: The HR Manager will provide strategic leadership and oversight for the Town’s human resources function, 
including workforce development, employee engagement, organizational effectiveness, compliance, and talent 
retention. The position will oversee HR operations, supervise the HR Generalist, and serve as a strategic advisor to 
Town leadership.
Need: The Town’s continued growth has increased the need for advanced HR expertise and dedicated leadership. This 
position will provide oversight of complex employee relations, workplace investigations, leave and accommodation 
programs, compensation and classification, policy development, compliance, and organizational development while 
allowing operational and strategic HR responsibilities to be appropriately divided.
Visual Communication Specialist
Fund: General Fund
Purpose: The Visual Communications Specialist will serve as the Town’s primary in-house creative resource, providing 
graphic and publication design, photography, video production, brand stewardship, digital signage, visual asset 
management, and accessible multimedia content for print and digital platforms.
Need: As the Town grows, demand for timely, visual, and accessible communication continues to increase. This 
position will provide dedicated creative expertise, support implementation of the Town’s new brand standards, and 
help maintain a consistent visual identity across departments and public-facing materials.
Accounting Technician
Fund:  Community Center Fund
Purpose: The Accounting Technician position is proposed to provide additional capacity for the Finance Department's 
growing workload in accounts payable, accounts receivable, cash receipts, credit card reconciliation, and general 
accounting support as the Town continues to expand. The position will strengthen day-to-day operations and support 
the increasing transaction volume associated with ongoing growth and future facilities, including the recreation 
center.
Need: The Finance Department's transactional workload continues to increase, with over 3,300 payables processed in 
2025 and 1,900 processed through July 2026. Adding an Accounting Technician will help manage this growth while 
creating a career progression opportunity that allows our more experienced staff to focus on higher-level accounting 
responsibilities.
Police Lieutenant (2 positions)
Fund:  General Fund
Purpose: The Police Lieutenant position is proposed to strengthen the Police Department's leadership structure by 
providing enhanced operational oversight, accountability, and supervision. One Lieutenant will oversee Patrol 
operations, while a second Lieutenant will lead the Professional Standards Division, including Internal Affairs, Training, 
Records & Evidence, and future accreditation efforts.
Need: During the Chief's first year, an assessment of Police Department operations identified a need to accelerate the 
addition of a second Police Lieutenant to strengthen leadership capacity, accountability, and organizational oversight. 
Advancing this position from 2030 to 2027 will improve span of control, provide additional supervisory support, 
enhance mentoring and professional development opportunities, and align the Department's leadership structure 
with best practices commonly found in comparable law enforcement agencies.
Parks and Recreation Program Coordinator
Fund:  Community Center Fund
Purpose: The Recreation Programs Coordinator position is proposed to support the continued growth of recreation 
programming and ensure the department can sustainably meet the needs of the community.

Need: This position was previously planned for 2026, but was moved to 2027.  Recreation programming participation 
has grown significantly, with athletic program participation increasing nearly 300% over the past year. Adding a 
second Recreation Programs Coordinator will provide dedicated staff resources to manage and expand general 
recreation programming while allowing supervisory staff to focus on long-range planning, financial sustainability, 
program evaluation, and operational oversight.
Recreation Center Supervisor
Fund:  Community Center Fund
Purpose:  The Recreation Center Manager position is a critical investment in the successful development and future 
operation of the Town's Recreation Center. Hiring this position prior to construction will provide dedicated leadership 
throughout the planning and construction process, ensuring operational procedures, budgets, policies, staffing plans, 
and service models are developed well in advance of the facility's opening. As construction progresses, the Recreation 
Center Manager will serve as a key project team member, helping guide facility decisions and ensuring the Recreation 
Center is prepared to deliver high-quality programs, services, and experiences from day one.   In general, when the 
Recreation Center opens, this position will plan, manage, administrate, and direct the daily activities for the recreation 
center to ensure maximum use and enjoyment by the community. 
Need: As the Town moves forward with construction of the Recreation Center, dedicated leadership is needed to 
ensure the facility is operationally ready on opening day. Hiring the Recreation Center Manager in advance of the 
facility's completion will provide the capacity to develop budgets, policies, procedures, staffing plans, and operational 
systems while participating in key planning and construction decisions, helping ensure a smooth transition from 
construction to operations.
Parks Seasonal Worker 
Fund:  Parks and Recreation Fund
Purpose:  Add seasonal staffing capacity to support the maintenance and operation of the Town's parks, trails, open 
spaces, and recreational amenities during peak service periods.
Need: Growth in parks and recreation programming has outpaced current staffing.
Code Enforcement Officer (increase in hours for existing position)
Fund:  General Fund
Purpose: Provides consistent code enforcement activities and serves as a support to the building department permit 
area.
Need:   As development activity, population growth, and code compliance responsibilities increase, expanding the 
current Code Enforcement position from 20 to 28 hours per week will provide additional capacity to maintain service 
levels, improve responsiveness, and support a phased transition to a full-time position over the coming years.
Civil Engineer II
Fund: General Fund
Purpose: The Civil Engineer II position is needed to maintain the level of engineering services provided to the Town's 
residents while delivering those services in a more cost-effective and efficient manner. Many of the transportation 
engineering responsibilities currently performed by the Town Engineer or supported through engineering consultants 
can be more effectively managed by dedicated in-house staff.
Need:   This position will assume responsibility for neighborhood traffic-calming projects, pedestrian crossing 
improvements, transportation project management, consultant administration, Traffic Impact Study (TIS) review, 
transportation-related development review, and other transportation engineering functions. It will provide day-to-day 
engineering support that is currently divided between the Town Engineer and outside consultants, creating a more 
consistent and responsive level of service for residents, developers, contractors, and internal departments. These 
responsibilities align with the Civil Engineer II position, which includes transportation engineering, project 
management, consultant administration, development review, and traffic operations.

Purpose:   Maintenance workers will be responsible for landscape maintenance and minor irrigation repairs at town
