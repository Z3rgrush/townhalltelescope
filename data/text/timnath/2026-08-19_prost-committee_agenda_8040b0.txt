5 REPORTS
a) Parks and Rec Staff Reports.
Mrs. Moos provided updates on Recreation programming.
The first session of Youth Sports Camps recently concluded. The second 
session is projected to have even higher attendance than the first and includes 
tennis, soccer, and pickleball.
Adult Pickleball Leagues are underway.
New adult pickleball lessons have been very popular, requiring an additional 
session to accommodate more participants.
A new T-ball program for 5- and 6-year-olds has 24 participants, resulting in 
two teams.
Jerseys are being stocked at 4800 Goodman Street building. Universal jerseys 
will be used across all sports and are included in the registration fee.
Fall soccer and flag football registration begins June 1. A social media and 
marketing push is anticipated.
The first Family Fishing Fun event had 33 registered participants, although not 
everyone attended. There were several lessons learned from the inaugural 
event, and staff are excited to make it an annual event.
Bike to Work Day will be held June 24 from 6:00–8:00 a.m. at Town Center.
A kayak kiosk was installed April 27 and has received great feedback. The self-
service kiosk provides a new amenity appreciated by the community.
The Turf Tank has been useful for field lining, particularly for lacrosse and 
other programs that require more specialized field layouts.
Youth Development programming has seen an increase in registrations, 
including programs such as the Fairy Garden Workshop and End-of-Summer 
Party.
New programming includes Little Explorers and Imaginarium STEAM classes.
Field reservations continue to be in high demand. Resident requests are being 
prioritized while staff continue to accommodate other requests as availability 
allows.
The Fall Activity Guide has been sent to print and is expected to arrive in 
residents' homes after the Fourth of July.
6 BUSINESS
a) Timnath Ranch North Park.
Kurt Friesen, Director of Landscape Architecture at Ayres Associates, provided an 
overview of the master planning process for Peanut Park, also known as Timnath 
Ranch North Park.
During the annual Ice Cream Social, Ayres Associates conducted a community 
poll to gauge resident and attendee interest in potential amenities for the park. 
The 15 options presented were organized into three categories:
1.Nature + Adventure: Natural areas and open space, trails, wetlands, 
challenge play, and learning environments.
2.Recreation + Play: Bike amenities, pump tracks, creek exploration, fitness 
and sport areas, yard games, and casual play.
3.Community Gatherings: Special events and concerts, picnic and seating 
areas, and spaces designed for community connection.
Mr. Friesen also discussed project challenges, including drainage 
considerations and the park's size. He highlighted opportunities presented by
