Small Business Saturday  
WHEREAS, the Town of Wellington celebrates our local small businesses and the contributions they 
make to our local economy and community; and, 
 
WHEREAS, according to the United States Small Business Administration, there are over thirty-six 
million small businesses in the United States; and, 
 
WHEREAS, small businesses employ roughly half of the private-sector workforce in our nation, fostering 
innovation, providing essential goods and services, and enhancing the unique character of our 
neighborhoods; and, 
 
WHEREAS, ninety-six percent of consumers who shop on Small Business Saturday agree that shopping 
at small, independently owned businesses supports their commitment to making a positive impact on their 
community; and  
 
WHEREAS, local independent shops, restaurants, creators, and service providers reinvest a significantly 
higher percentage of their revenue back into the local economy compared to large corporations, directly 
supporting our schools, parks, and public services; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Saturday after Thanksgiving has been designated as Small Business Saturday to encourage 
consumers to support independent businesses that anchor our local communities. 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Rebekka Dailey, Mayor of Town of Wellington, do hereby Proclaim Saturday, 
November 28, 2026 as Small Business Saturday and urge all residents and visitors of our community to 
support small businesses and merchants on this day and throughout the year to help sustain our local 
economy and vibrant community character. 
IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand and caused the seal of the Town of Wellington to 
affixed this 10th day of November, 2026.

BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
The Town is undertaking updates to its Comprehensive Plan in accordance with requirements of SB24-
174.  The senate bill requires municipalities to incorporate a Strategic Growth Element and a Water Supply 
Element into the Comprehensive Plan. The Town has engaged professional services with Logan Simpson 
Design, Inc. to assist with evaluating existing Town efforts and plans, and compiling available information to 
incorporate these two elements into the Wellington Comprehensive Plan 2021.   
 
This presentation is intended to provide an overview of the process, present findings and insights from a review 
of existing conditions, share initial recommendations, and solicit feedback and discussion from the Board of 
Trustees. The findings are informed by work previously completed by the Town in the Wellington 
Comprehensive Plan 2021, the 2025 Water Source Development Plan, the 2025 Housing Needs & Affordability 
Assessment (HNA), recent amendments to the Land Use Code (2025 amendments updating residential lot sizes, 
maximum densities, and other housing related updates), and others.  Findings are also informed by multiple 
work sessions with Town Staff from Planning, Engineering, Finance, Parks & Recreation, Administration, and 
Utilities.  The initial recommendations are based on satisfying guidance for compliance with SB24-174 created 
by the Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) published in July 2026.   
 
Coordination with the Trustees and Planning Commission, review comments from DOLA, and public 
comments will inform the updates that will be incorporated into the Comprehensive Plan for the required 
Elements.  Comprehensive Plan amendments are adopted by the Planning Commission, and ratified by the 
Board of Trustees.  A public hearing to consider adoption of the Comprehensive Plan amendments is 
anticipated to be scheduled for the December 7, 2026 Planning Commission meeting, and a resolution for 
ratification by the Board of Trustees in January 2027.

1983 North Poudre Irrigation Company agreement including the cost increased, including 
significant year-to-year cost volatility, increasingly difficult to support long-term financial planning 
and the need of the Town to be able to store or convey future foreign water supplies. .

payments in the initial four years, which would provide the Town with greater budget predictability.

contentious approval as it’s a modification for existing operating agreements to capture 
requirements.

this was the best negotiated outcome that he could have hoped for.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The Wellington Finance Advisory Board (FAB) was adopted by the Trustees on March 25, 2025 through 
Ordinance No. 03-2025; the ordinance contains additional details about the purpose and the duties of the 
Advisory Board. A summary of the Advisory Board responsibilities include: 
a.    To review the Town's annual draft budget to obtain understanding of the Town's financial position.  
b.    To make recommendations to the Board of Trustees concerning Town finances, annual budget and long-
range financial planning to address the needs of the Town. 
c.    To review Town revenues and actual expenses. 
d.    To review reports generated by outside consultants for matters having a financial impact such as utility rate 
studies, long term financial planning and potential contracts for banking or financial services. 
e.    To review the Town's financial reporting processes and internal controls. 
 
FAB has five vacancies which have been advertised. 
  
BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
Staff opened and advertised all vacancies in Q1 of 2026. Two applications for FAB were received in October of 
2026, and the interview committee of the Town Treasurer and Chair, Nic Redavid, current FAB member, 
Nikiah Nudell and Mayor Pro Tem Brian Mason conducted interviews on October 26, 2026. The panel 
unanimously recommended for approval both applicants.  
 
The vacancies have terms ending 2028 and 2030. The interview panel recommended staggering the 
appointment terms as directed in Ordinance No. 03-2025. Both applicants names were written on paper and the 
2030 term was drawn for doing a lot ballot drawing. Brianna Jones's name was drawn out for the term ending 
2030. Town Clerk Hannah Hill conducted the lot drawing, alongside Town Administrator Patti Garcia. Blake 
Watson is being recommended for a term ending 2028.

2027 Town Administrator’s Budget Message 
 
 
November 10, 2026 
 
Dear Mayor and Board of Trustees,   
 
The development of the Town of Wellington’s 2027 Budget required difficult decisions as 
we navigate ongoing economic uncertainty. With property tax revenues declining and sales 
tax revenue remaining flat, each department was asked to carefully evaluate and make 
significant reductions. These decisions were not easy, but they were necessary to align 
spending with available resources while maintaining essential services and protecting the 
Town’s long-term financial stability. 
 
I want to sincerely thank my Leadership Team for their willingness to make these difficult 
decisions, their thoughtful approach to prioritizing community needs, and their continued 
commitment to responsible financial stewardship. Despite these financial challenges, the 
2027 Budget reflects our shared commitment to making strategic decisions today that 
position Wellington for a strong future. 
 
Economic Factors 
In response to economic uncertainty, limited revenue growth, and slower residential 
development, the Town took a strategic approach to developing the 2027 Budget. 
Departments were directed to evaluate expenditures, identify operational efficiencies, and 
prioritize essential services while minimizing impacts to the community. 
 
Total budgeted revenues decreased from $28.0 million in 2026 to $23.7 million in 2027, a 
reduction of 15.4%. In response, the Town reduced total budgeted expenditures by 13.1%, 
from $36.8 million to $32.0 million. Capital project investments also decreased from $14.5 
million to $10.4 million, reflecting changes in the timing and scope of planned 
infrastructure projects. 
 
Additional expenditure reductions were made across the Town's enterprise and special 
revenue funds. 
 
Key actions taken address financial challenges: 
 Staffing and Compensation: Eliminated funding for two vacant Public Works 
positions and proposed a $1,500 flat base-pay adjustment for employees in place 
of traditional merit and cost-of-living increases for 2027. 
Page 44 of 199
 Administrative and Operational Efficiencies: Reduced professional services, 
consulting contracts, employee training, travel, office supplies, equipment 
purchases, and other discretionary expenditures across multiple departments. 
 Community Programs and Services: Reduced community event expenditures, 
eliminated the Board's discretionary fund, and adjusted recreational programming, 
including discontinuing batting cage operations and transitioning certain programs 
to Town staff. 
 Infrastructure and Utility Operations: Identified operational savings in the Water and 
Sewer Funds, including reduced treatment facility operating costs, professional 
services, equipment, and other expenses, while maintaining essential utility 
services. 
 Parks and Facilities Maintenance: Reduced contractual services, irrigation 
expenses, and selected maintenance expenditures while prioritizing the continued 
upkeep of Town facilities and public spaces. 
 Capital and Technology Priorities: Reevaluated capital expenditures, equipment 
replacements, and technology investments to focus available resources on the 
Town's most immediate operational and infrastructure needs. 
 
While these decisions required difficult trade-offs, they demonstrate the Town's 
commitment to fiscal responsibility and thoughtful resource management. The 2027 
Budget prioritizes maintaining essential services, preserving financial reserves, and 
positioning Wellington to respond to future economic conditions and community needs.

Budget Highlights 
The Town of Wellington's 2027 Budget projects a combined ending fund balance of $24.7 
million, compared to a beginning balance of $33.0 million. This represents a planned 
reduction of approximately $8.3 million, primarily driven by investments in capital 
infrastructure projects. Total budgeted revenues, including transfers and grants, are 
projected at $23.7 million, while total expenditures, including transfers and capital 
projects, are budgeted at $32.0 million. 
 
The 2027 Budget includes $10.4 million in capital investments, partially offset by $1.2 
million in capital grants. These investments span the Street, Water, Sewer, Drainage, 
Parks, and General Funds, supporting the maintenance of existing infrastructure, 
improvements to essential services, and the community's long-term needs. 
 
Despite the planned use of fund balances, all applicable funds are projected to maintain 
reserves above the Town's established minimum requirements. The General Fund and 
special revenue funds are required to maintain reserves equal to 33% of annual operating 
expenditures, while enterprise funds must maintain at least 90 days of operating 
expenditures.  
 
Overall, the 2027 Budget reflects the Town's commitment to responsible financial 
management by balancing necessary infrastructure investments with maintaining 
adequate reserves and protecting long-term financial stability.

revenue is projected at $2.15 million, a 2% decrease from 2026, while sales tax is budgeted 
at $2.89 million, a 4% increase over the 2026 adopted budget. Slower development 
continues to impact revenue collections, with building inspection fees projected to decline 
12% and building materials use tax decreasing 31%. 
 
Other special revenue funds are projected to experience decreased revenues. The Street 
Fund is projected to generate $1.7 million, a 24% decrease from 2026 budget, 
supplemented by $910,980 in capital grants for transportation improvements.  
 
The Town's utility enterprise funds reflect mixed revenue trends. Water Fund revenues are 
projected at $6.1 million, a 12% decrease from 2026 budget, while Sewer Fund revenues 
are expected to increase 3% to $3.8 million. Despite this growth, the Sewer Fund faces an 
operating shortfall of approximately $841,536, highlighting the need for continued financial 
planning to support ongoing operations and debt obligations. The Drainage Fund 
anticipates $830,977 in revenue, primarily from storm drainage utility fees. 
 
The Parks Fund is projected to generate $1.5 million in revenue, remaining relatively flat 
compared to 2026. With a projected ending fund balance of $766,633, only $248,559 
above its required reserve, the Board of Trustees will need to evaluate the fund's long-term 
financial sustainability in 2027, including revenue opportunities, operating costs, and 
future capital needs. 
 
Overall, the 2027 revenue projections emphasize realistic growth assumptions, reduced 
reliance on development-related revenues, and the importance of maintaining financial 
stability while continuing to provide essential Town services. 
 
Expenditure Highlights 
The 2027 Budget reflects a commitment to fiscal responsibility, operational efficiency, and 
maintaining essential services amid ongoing financial challenges. Total budgeted 
expenditures decrease by 13.1%, from $36.8 million in 2026 to $32.0 million in 2027. 
Departments carefully evaluated spending priorities and identified cost-saving measures 
to better align expenditures with available revenues while minimizing impacts to essential 
services. 
 
General Fund operating expenditures are budgeted at $8.3 million, including $2.4 million 
for the Larimer County Sheriff's Office (LCSO). Expenditure reductions include staffing,

professional services, employee development, supplies, and community events. Despite 
these efforts, public safety costs continue to rise, with the LCSO contract increasing 
approximately 4% over 2026. 
 
Capital investments remain a significant component of the 2027 Budget, particularly in 
transportation and utility infrastructure. The Street Fund includes $4.9 million in capital 
projects, primarily supporting the Cleveland Avenue Construction Project and other 
transportation improvements. The Water and Sewer Funds include $3.7 million and 
$306,234 in capital investments, respectively. Combined debt service for the Town's water

The Drainage Fund includes approximately $529,963 in operating expenses and $694,206

Overall, the 2027 Budget prioritizes essential services and critical infrastructure 
investments while reducing discretionary spending, managing ongoing financial 
obligations, and preserving the Town's long-term financial stability.

Budget Philosophy  
 
The Annual Budget Shall be Balanced  
 The 2027 budget presented to the Board of Trustees is balanced by fund. 
Surplus fund and fund balance are considered a financing source. 
 
Link to Long-term Community Plans 
 To ensure that financial decisions reflect the community’s vision, the 2027 
budget was developed in accordance with priorities set forth by the Board of 
Trustees Strategic Plan, Comprehensive Plan, master plans, the needs of the 
community, and state and federal laws. 
 
Alignment with Fund Balance Policy 
 The 2027 budget for enterprise funds ensures compliance with the Town’s Fund 
Balance Policy by maintaining the greater of an unrestricted budgetary fund 
balance of no less than 90 days of regular operating expenditures or a debt 
Page 50 of 199
service coverage ratio of 1.10 as required by State Revolving Fund (SRF) loan 
obligations. 
 Similarly, the 2027 budget for the general fund and special revenue funds 
maintains compliance by requiring an unrestricted budgetary fund balance of no 
less than four months (33%) of regular operating expenditures or 110% of debt 
service, whichever is greater. 
 
Identify Capital Improvement Projects (CIP) 
 To address long-term infrastructure and community needs, the projects 
included in the 2027 CIP budget are consistent with the Town’s strategic goals. 
Projects must cost more than $25,000 and capital assets should have an 
expected life of greater than three (3) years. 
 
Conservatively Estimate Revenue Forecasting 
 To protect the Town from overestimating resources, all revenue projections in 
the 2027 budget were estimated conservatively. Revenue estimates were based 
on trend analysis, economic conditions, and other factors.
