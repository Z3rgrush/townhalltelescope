EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The Town of Wellington draft budget for fiscal year 2027 is presented for review, public, comment, and Board 
direction. 
  
BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
The Town Administrator, per Sec. 4-3-10 of the Municipal Code, shall submit the annual budget no later than 
the first regular meeting in October of each year. The Board of Trustees identified priorities at their June 16, 
2026, Work Session which included economic development, affordable housing and quality of life, fiscal 
sustainability and enhancing municipal services. The draft budget was discussed with the Board at the Budget 
Workday on October 2, 2026, and was available for review by the public at the Budget BOOnanza on October 
6, 2026. 
 
The draft budget for fiscal year 2027 consists of the estimated fund balances, projected revenues, requested 
expenditures/expenses, and estimated capital grants and project costs. The 2027 estimated fund balances were 
calculated using the audited financial statements for fiscal year 2025 and the projected end-of-year revenues 
and expenditures/expenses determined by Staff for fiscal year 2026. 
 
The philosophy for drafting the Town of Wellington budget for fiscal year 2027: balancing the budget by fund, 
including fund balance spending; linking expenditures/expenses to the 2025-2029 Strategic Plan; alignment 
with Board adopted Financial Management Policies effective May 12, 2026; capital improvement projects to 
address long-term infrastructure and community needs; and conservatively estimated revenue forecasting. 
  
CONNECTION WITH ADOPTED MASTER PLANS 
The Town of Wellington draft budget for fiscal year 2027 was developed with direct alignment to the 2025-
2029 Strategic Plan, which reflects the Board of Trustees’ vision, mission, and community priorities. The 
Strategic Plan serves as a guiding framework, ensuring that resources are allocated responsibly and 
intentionally in ways that advance long-term goals while meeting current needs. Each area of the 2026 Budget 
connects to the Board’s four overarching priorities: Grow Responsibly, Cultivate Community Spaces, Foster 
Economic Vibrancy, and Ensure Strong Town Operations

Subject: Ordinance No. 13-2026 - Public Hearing and Consideration of Amendments to the Land Use 
Code, Chapter 15, Article 8 - Floodplain Regulations 
• Presentation:  Nathan Ewert, Deputy Director of Public Works 
     
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The Town must take action to stay in compliance with the requirements of the National Flood Insurance 
Program (NFIP). Staff is proposing changes to Chapter 15, Article 8 of the Municipal Code which would 
ensure continued compliance with the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)'s requirements. 
 
The Planning Commission held a public hearing at it's meeting of September 14, 2026 to consider proposed 
amendments to the Land Use Code.  Following the public hearing, the Planning Commission voted 
unanimously to forward a recommendation of approval to the Board of Trustees.   
 
The Planning Commission's and Town Staff's recommendation is to approve the attached Ordinance No. 13-
2026 effecting the required updates to the floodplain regulations. 
  
BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
Background 
  
On May 27, 2026, the Town received a Letter of Final Determination from FEMA establishing updated flood 
hazard information for Larimer County, including the portion of Boxelder Creek located within Wellington. 
The updated Flood Insurance Study (FIS) and Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs) become effective on 
November 27, 2026. 
 
To maintain compliance with the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), communities must adopt and 
enforce floodplain management regulations that reference the effective FIS and FIRMs in a legally enforceable 
document. The proposed amendments to Chapter 15, Article 8 of the Wellington Municipal Code are necessary 
to satisfy these federal requirements and ensure the Town remains eligible to participate in the NFIP. 
 
Project History 
  
The updated floodplain mapping is the result of a multi-year effort initiated following the significant flooding 
events that occurred throughout northern Colorado in 2013. The project was completed through a partnership 
between local communities, the Colorado Water Conservation Board (CWCB), and FEMA to update floodplain 
mapping throughout Larimer County. 
 
Preliminary mapping affecting Wellington and the Boxelder Creek floodplain was released in 2021. As part of 
that process, CWCB and its consultant hosted public outreach meetings to present the proposed mapping 
updates, explain the NFIP, and provide information regarding flood insurance, appeals, and available resources 
for affected property owners. 
  
Page 58 of 288
 
Following the release of the preliminary maps, FEMA and CWCB worked through comments, appeals, and 
technical revisions before issuing the final determination in 2026. During this period, the Town successfully 
completed a Letter of Map Revision (LOMR) that modified the preliminary mapping. As a result, 52 properties 
were removed from the preliminary Special Flood Hazard Area, and an additional 10 properties and two 
structures were removed from the preliminary floodway. Most of these properties are located within the 
Harvest Village and Boxelder Commons neighborhoods. 
  
The final FIRMs are included in the meeting packet for reference. The mapping updates affect only Boxelder 
Creek and do not include Coal Creek, which was outside the scope of this study.  The FIS is a 6 volume ~600 
page packet of generally technical information.  Hardcopies of both the FIS and FIRMS are retained for public 
viewing at the Municipal Services Building and electronic copies of the document are available from the Town 
Floodplain Manager/Town Engineer's office as well as available for download from the FEMA Map Service 
Center after November 27, 2026. 
  
Proposed Code Changes 
 
The Town of Wellington currently participates in the NFIP. Participation in the program allows property 
owners within the Town to obtain federally backed flood insurance and helps maintain eligibility for certain 
forms of federal disaster assistance and funding. 
  
To remain compliant with NFIP requirements, the Town must update its floodplain regulations before 
November 27, 2026. The primary amendment is the adoption of the updated Flood Insurance Study for Larimer 
County, Colorado and Incorporated Areas, with an effective date of November 27, 2026. Additional 
amendments are required to align the code with current federal and state floodplain management standards. 
  
The proposed changes include: 
  
Sec. 15-8-10. Purpose and Definition section (d) Definitions 
• Remove in its entirety the definition for "Area of Special Flood Hazard" 
• Add the definition for "Base Flood Elevation (BFE) 
• Add the definition for "Special Flood Hazard Area" 
Sec. 15-8-20 General Provisions 
• Update the text to list the new FIRM title "The Flood Insurance Study for Larimer County, Colorado, 
and Incorporated Areas" and effective date of "November 27, 2026" as well as other text as suggested 
by the State of Colorado Model floodplain ordinance. 
• Add Severability Clause as required by Title 44 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 59 and 60, as 
well as the State of Colorado Model floodplain ordinance. 
Sec. 15-8-30 Administration 
• Update section (c)(1)(a.) to match the language as required by the Title 44 Code of Federal Regulations 
(CFR) Part 59 and 60, as well as the State of Colorado Model floodplain ordinance. 
• Update and add section (d)(4) Penalties for non-compliance as required by Title 44 Code of Federal 
Regulations (CFR) Part 59 and 60, as well as the State of Colorado Model floodplain ordinance. 
Add new section for "Certification"
