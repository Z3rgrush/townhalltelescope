CHAPTER 6  
Business Licenses and Regulations 
ARTICLE 2 Business Licenses1 
Sec. 6-2-10. Purpose. 
The purpose of this Article is to require the registration of businesses and vendors with a physical presence in 
the Town; to create opportunity and flexibility for a variety of business and vendor operations; and to enable the 
Town to obtain information regarding such businesses and vendors, including, but not limited to, ownership or 
operator contact information, the nature of the business operation, number of employees and location(s) of 
operation, pursuant to the authority granted to municipalities under C.R.S. 31-15-501, in order to protect the 
public health, safety and welfare of the Town's citizens and residents.  
(Ord. No. 05-2026, § 1, 5-26-26) 
Sec. 6-2-20. Definitions. 
For the purposes of this Article, the following words and phrases shall have the meanings set forth below:  
Business means any commercial activity conducted for gain, profit, or livelihood, whether full-time or part-
time, and whether operated by an individual, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or other legal 
entity.  
Business Location means a fixed physical place where a business is conducted, including but not limited to, 
offices, storefronts, restaurants, or industrial facilities.  
Incidental Physical Presence means limited or temporary presence within the Town that is ancillary to a 
retailer's primary operations, including deliveries by common carrier or infrequent service calls, and that does not 
otherwise constitute Physical Presence under this Article.  
Licensee means any person or entity issued a business license or vendor license under this Article.  
Nonprofit Organization means a corporation or entity recognized as tax-exempt under Section 501(c) of the 
Internal Revenue Code or other applicable federal or state law.  
Person means any individual, firm, partnership, association, corporation, limited liability company, trust, or 
other legal entity.  
Physical Presence means the ongoing or regular occupation, use, or maintenance of a location within the 
Town, including but not limited to an office, storefront, warehouse, jobsite, or the regular presence of employees, 
agents, or vehicles within the Town.

(d) Vendors operating solely as part of an approved special event may satisfy licensing requirements pursuant to 
Section 6-2-50 of this Article. A vendor license may cover multiple approved special events during the 
calendar year, provided the vendor complies with all other applicable requirements of this Article.  
(Ord. No. 05-2026, § 1, 5-26-26) 
Sec. 6-2-50. Licensing for special events. 
(a) Businesses or vendors participating solely in an approved special event shall not be required to obtain an 
individual business license or vendor license provided that all of the following conditions are met:  
(1) The special event has been approved by the Town;  
(2) The event organizer provides the Town with a list of participating vendors upon request; and  
(3) Each vendor participating in a special event shall comply with applicable State, County, or other 
regulatory agency licensing requirements.

Sec. 6-2-110. Renewal of licenses. 
(a) All business licenses and vendor licenses issued under this Article shall be renewed annually.  
(b) Renewal applications shall be submitted to the Town Clerk or designee on a form provided by the Town.  
(c) Renewal applications shall include verification that all information on file remains accurate or identification

(d) The Town Clerk or designee may deny a renewal application for noncompliance with this Code or for

(Ord. No. 05-2026, § 1, 5-26-26) 
Sec. 6-2-120. Revocation. 
(a) A business license or vendor license may be revoked:  
(1) When it appears that the license was obtained by fraud, misrepresentation, or false statements within 
the application;  
(2) When it appears that the activity conducted pursuant to such license is a public nuisance as defined by 
this Code or statute, is a danger to the health or safety of the public, or violates the Town of Wellington 
Municipal Code.  
(b) A business license or vendor license shall be revoked by administrative action of the Town Clerk. Upon 
revocation of a license, the Town Clerk shall notify a licensee of the revocation in writing mailed to the 
mailing address listed on the licensee's business license application. Such notice shall specify the grounds for 
the revocation, the process for an appeal of the revocation, and the process for restoration of the license.  
(c) A revoked business license or vendor license may only be restored upon submission of a new license 
application, payment of the appropriate fee, and confirmation by appropriate Town staff that the condition 
or activity that caused the revocation has been corrected.  
(d) The revocation of any license shall not release or discharge anyone from the payment of taxes, penalty, and 
interest due and owing to the Town.  
(e) For minor infractions, the Town Clerk may provide written notice and allow the licensee a reasonable period 
to correct the violation before pursuing formal revocation. The Town may consider prior compliance history 
and corrective action in determining whether revocation is appropriate.

Sec. 6-2-130. Appeals. 
(a) A licensee may appeal a denial or revocation of a business license or vendor license by submitting a written 
request for a hearing before the Town Clerk within twenty-one (21) days of the denial or revocation. The 
hearing before the Town Clerk shall be administrative in nature and conducted de novo. The Town Clerk, in 
hearing such an appeal, may consider evidence that the denial or revocation was in error at the time the 
decision was made.  
(b) A licensee may further appeal the Town Clerk's decision to the Board of Trustees by submitting a written 
appeal to the Town Administrator within seven (7) days of the hearing and decision of the Town Clerk. Such 
written appeal shall state the reasons for the appeal and may provide any material evidence relating to the 
denial or revocation. The Board of Trustees shall consider the same, together with the report of the Town 
Administrator. The appeal to the Board of Trustees shall be conducted de novo and shall constitute the 
Town's final decision upon the application or revocation.  
(Ord. No. 05-2026, § 1, 5-26-26) 
Sec. 6-2-140. Cease and desist. 
If a business or vendor is operating without a license, the Town Clerk may issue an order to the business to 
cease and desist operations. The licensee shall be provided a reasonable opportunity to obtain the appropriate 
license or correct the violation before further enforcement or revocation actions are taken.  
(Ord. No. 05-2026, § 1, 5-26-26) 
Sec. 6-2-150. Penalty. 
Failure to comply with the terms of this Article shall constitute a noncriminal violation of the Town of 
Wellington Municipal Code enforceable pursuant to Section 1-4-20 of this Code. Each day a violation continues 
shall constitute a separate offense.  
(Ord. No. 05-2026, § 1, 5-26-26)

ARTICLE 4 Mobile Home Parks 
Sec. 6-4-10. Definitions. 
For the purpose of this Article, unless the context requires otherwise, the definitions in Chapter 16 of this 
Code are incorporated herein by this reference as if set out verbatim herein. In addition, the following words are 
defined as set forth herein:  
Applicant means an individual who has applied to the Board of Trustees for a license to operate a mobile 
home park in the Town.  
Licensee means the individual to whom a license to operate a mobile home park has been issued by the 
Board of Trustees.  
Operate means to rent, lease or offer to rent or lease any land located within a mobile home park, or 
otherwise allow any such land to be occupied.  
Operator means any person who operates a mobile home park. (Prior code 6.03.01)  
Sec. 6-4-20. Scope; penalties. 
(a) It is unlawful for any person to operate a mobile home park within the Town except in accordance with this 
Article.  
(b) It is unlawful for any occupant, owner, tenant or lessee of a mobile home located within the Town to violate 
Section 6-4-70 of this Article.  
(c) Before any person shall operate a mobile home park within the Town, such person shall be the recipient of a 
license granted by the Board of Trustees pursuant to the terms of this Article. (Prior code 6.03.02; Ord. 11-
2007 §1)

Any individual may apply to the Board of Trustees for a license pursuant to this Article. Such application shall

merchants who deal in new furniture, dishes or other regular household furnishings and accept such articles for

ARTICLE 7 Trash Collectors 
Sec. 6-7-10. License required. 
No person shall engage in the business of collecting trash or garbage within the Town without first having 
made an application to the Town Administrator/Clerk for a license to do so. Each application shall contain the 
following information:  
(1) Name, address and telephone number of the applicant.  
(2) Name of the business. (Prior code 10.01.04; Ord. 11-2007 §1)  
Sec. 6-7-20. Application fee; duration of license. 
Application for a license shall be made in writing to the Town Administrator/Clerk who, after approval of the 
Board of Trustees, shall issue it upon payment of an annual license fee in an amount established by resolution by 
the Board of Trustees to offset the Town's costs. Such license shall expire on December 31 of each year and shall 
not be apportioned or prorated for the fraction of each year., except in the instance of a new business being 
started after July 1 of any year. In such case, the fee for the remainder of each year shall be five dollars ($5.00). 
(Prior code 10.01.04; Ord. 11-2007 §1)  
Sec. 6-7-30. Definition. 
For the purposes of this Article, the term trash shall be held to include ashes, waste paper and cardboard, 
cans, bottles, glass, sawdust, leaves, grass cuttings, shrubbery and tree trimmings, wood shavings, packing 
materials, furniture, appliances, vehicles or parts thereof and garbage of a household nature, but not to include 
manure or dead animals. This definition shall also include all waste matter attending or resulting from the 
occupancy of a residence, apartment, hotel or other place or dwelling, together with all premises appurtenant 
thereto, and from the operation of any and all businesses. The term shall also include industrial waste or waste 
matter resulting from the construction, demolition or repair of a building or other structure. (Prior code 10.01.04; 
Ord. 11-2007 §1)  
Sec. 6-7-40. Trash hauling trucks. 
Trash hauling trucks operating within the Town shall be limited to tandem-axle, self compacting trash trucks. 
Exceptions include trucks used exclusively for the purpose of collecting recycled material and single-axle trucks 
used in the removal of debris from the construction, demolition or repair of a building or other structure. (Prior 
code 10.01.04; Ord. 25-1998 §1)  
Sec. 6-7-50. Revocation of license. 
The Board of Trustees, after a hearing, shall have the power to revoke at any time the license of the trash 
collector for failure to properly perform the terms and covenants of this Article or for other good cause shown by 
lack of services or operation. (Prior code 10.01.04)

ARTICLE 8 Vendor Licenses  
Sec. 6-8-01 Purpose. 
 
(a) The purpose of this chapter is to regulate temporary and mobile business activities occurring within the Town in 
order to protect the public health, safety, and welfare; provide reasonable oversight of commercial activity; ensure 
compliance with applicable regulations; and provide equitable treatment of businesses operating within the Town. 
(b) The Town Administrator is hereby delegated authority to implement rules and regulations governing 
vendors that are not inconsistent with the provisions of this Article. Such rules and regulations shall be 
on file and available for public examination in the office of the Town Clerk.  
 
Sec. 6-8-02 Definitions. 
 
Event Vendor shall mean vendors operating solely as part of an approved Special Event shall register with the Town 
prior to participation. Registration is valid only for the dates, times, and location of the approved Special Event and 
does not authorize vending outside the event.  
Food Truck shall mean a legal, licensed motorized wheeled vehicle or towed, wheeled trailer designed and 
equipped to prepare and/or serve food from the interior of the truck or trailer; or other mobile unit used for the 
preparation, cooking, storage, service, or sale of food or beverages intended for human consumption. 
Outdoor Vendor shall mean any person, whether as owner, agent, or employee, who sells, attempts to sell, or offers 
to the public, whether for compensation or free of charge, any service, goods, wares, merchandise, food, or 
beverage from an approved outdoor location. Outdoor Vendor includes vendors operating from a temporary stand, 
tent, canopy, cart, trailer (excluding Food Trucks as separately defined), display area, or similar outdoor structure 
used for the purpose of offering goods or services to the public. except that Outdoor Vendor shall not include one 
who:  
(1) Vends directly and exclusively to manufacturers, wholesalers, or retailers for the purpose of resale;  
(2) Vends on behalf of the Town or at an outdoor event sponsored by the Town  
(3) Vends at a garage or yard sale at a residence held outside or within a garage or residential accessory 
building;

Sec. 6-8-04. Application for license. 
(a) The Town Clerk shall create an application noting required elements for the vendor type. The form of

liability, automobile, and workers compensation insurance, in the amount required in the application, shall provide 
certificates of insurance to the Town with the application and, except for workers compensation, name the Town 
as an additionally insured party.  
(d) Each license shall be valid for the then-current calendar year, and assuming compliance with this Article and the 
Vendor Policy, may be renewed beginning of November 1 for the next calendar year.

(f) If the Town Clerk determines that the issuance of a requested vendor license would be consistent with the 
requirements of this Article and with the requirements of the Vendor Policy, with or without additional conditions, 
the Town Clerk shall issue the license, subject to any such conditions. If the Town Clerk determines that the 
issuance of a vendor license would not be consistent with the requirements of this Article or the Vendor Policy, the 
applicant shall be notified in writing with an explanation of the reasons for such denial.

 b. On private property, in a manner that complies with applicable parking regulations set forth in

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
Staff requests approval of two companion agreements between the Town of Wellington and North Poudre 
Irrigation Company (NPIC): the 2027 Superseding Water Supply Agreement and the Storage & Carriage 
Operating (SCO) Agreement. Together, these agreements establish a modernized framework for the Town’s 
long-term municipal water supply, storage, and conveyance operations. The agreements are the result of several 
years of negotiations between the Town and NPIC. The NPIC Board of Directors approved both agreements at 
its September 9, 2026, meeting, contingent upon approval by Northern Water. Staff is requesting Board 
approval through two separate resolutions.  
 
The proposed agreements are intended to work in coordination with one another. The Superseding Water 
Supply Agreement preserves the Town’s perpetual Legacy Water entitlement while replacing the volatile 
pricing structure for additional water supplies with a long-term Contract Water framework. The SCO 
Agreement provides the operational mechanisms necessary for the Town to store, move, account for, and 
manage both Town-owned NPIC share water and future foreign water supplies utilizing NPIC infrastructure, 
including Reservoir No. 3.  
 
Collectively, the agreements advance Board direction established through the Town’s Water Source 
Development Plan and Resolution 48-2025 by increasing budget predictability, preserving operational 
flexibility, and creating a pathway for future water supply diversification.  
  
BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION 
Current Water Sources 
The Town of Wellington currently relies on two sources of raw water supplied through three distinct ownership 
and contractual arrangements: Town-owned municipal groundwater wells, a long standing 1983 Water Supply 
Agreement with North Poudre Irrigation Company (NPIC), and Town-owned NPIC shares.  
Groundwater – Town owned 
The Town owns three municipal groundwater wells, decreed in March 1971 and augmented for use up to 400 
acre-feet per year (AF/year). Approximately 25 AF/year is used for irrigation purposes, with the balance 
available for potable production. Current operational constraints of the nanofiltration membrane treatment unit 
include water quality discharge requirements for Selenium at the Water Reclamation Facility, production 
efficiency limitations of the unit, and distribution system pressure limitations resulting in current annual 
maximum use of the groundwater wells of approximately 260 AF/year for potable water production.  
Surface Water – 1983 Water Supply Agreement 
Page 47 of 358
 
The Town entered into a Water Supply Agreement with NPIC in August 1983, which granted Wellington the 
ability to use a portion of NPIC Reservoir #3 (up to 2,000 AF) for municipal water supply, included provisions 
related to siting the Town’s water treatment facilities, and specified several “layers” of water available to the 
Town: 
• Stock Water/Legacy Water: 275 AF/year based on the Town’s transfer of 53 shares of NPIC stock to 
NPIC. The Town annually pays the assessment on the 53 shares plus an administrative charge.  
• Additional Water: Up to 225 AF/year beyond the Legacy Water, priced using a market-based formula 
tied to annual NPIC share value, current interest rates, and NPIC assessments. 
• Option Water: Up to 1,500 AF/year beyond the Legacy Water and the additional 225 AF, also priced 
using the same market-based formula.  
 
For all water used by the Town beyond the base 275 AF legacy amount, the Town’s cost is driven by the 
market-based formula which increases substantially when interest rates and/or NPIC share values increase. 
The 1983 Water Supply Agreement was in the Town’s best interest at that time. It secured a firm supply of up 
to 2,000 AF at a reasonable price. However, the fixed terms of the agreement are unable to evolve with the 
changing landscape of water supplies in our region. As NPIC share prices have increased and interest rates 
fluctuate, it creates cost volatility that makes responsible financial planning for the Water Utility nearly 
impossible.  
In recent years, the cost per acre-foot for all use exceeding the Legacy Water volume increased from $765/AF 
in 2015 to a high of $3,785/AF in 2023. The increasing costs have placed significant pressure on the Water 
Fund, constraining the growth of reserves and the Town’s ability to pursue alternative water supply options. 
These constraints amplify the need to renegotiate the current Water Supply Agreement.  
Surface Water – Town Owned NPIC Shares 
As part of the arrangement that established the 1983 Water Supply Agreement, the Town transferred the NPIC 
shares it owned at that time to NPIC (53 shares). In the years that followed, the Town rebuilt its NPIC share 
ownership, primarily through developer dedications to satisfy raw water requirements, and to a lesser extent 
through direct purchase. The Town began acquiring shares more intentionally beginning in 2018 and currently 
owns 77 NPIC shares.  
Owning NPIC shares provides the Town with an additional, more direct source of supply within the NPIC 
system. Importantly, this share-based supply reduces the Town’s reliance on annual purchases under the 
“Additional Water” and “Option Water” provisions of the 1983 Agreement, which are subject to the market-
based pricing formula and can fluctuate significantly from year to year. 
Beginning in Water Year 2025, the Town coordinated with NPIC to take delivery of the multiple-use allocation 
of its shares at Reservoir #3. This delivery directly reduces the volume of water needed through the 1983 
Agreement above the 275 AF of Legacy Water, lowering the Town’s annual cost obligation to NPIC and 
reducing exposure to price volatility.  
Proposed New Agreements 
On September 9, 2026, the NPIC Board of Directors approved both the 2027 Superseding Water Supply 
Agreement and the Storage & Carriage Operating Agreement in substantially the form presented for Board

consideration. NPIC’s approval was conditioned upon approval by Northern Water. Under an existing operating 
agreement between NPIC and Northern Water, Northern Water approval is required before NPIC may enter 
into certain agreements involving the delivery of Colorado-Big Thompson Project water for municipal use. 
Because the proposed 2027 Superseding Water Supply Agreement supersedes the Town’s existing 1983 
agreement and may require the use of Colorado-Big Thompson supplies to satisfy certain delivery obligations, 
Northern Water is reviewing a corresponding amendment to its agreement with NPIC. Consideration by the 
Northern Water Board of Directors is currently anticipated on November 12, 2026. The pending Northern 
Water action relates to the NPIC’s obligations under its existing agreements and does not alter the Town’s 
consideration of the proposed agreements.  
The Contract Water framework reflected in the proposed agreement was authorized through a vote of NPIC 
shareholders. Through that process, participating shareholders agreed to make a portion of their future multiple-
use allocation available to support this long-term municipal supply agreement. Contract Water will be supplied 
from NPIC’s multiple-use direct flow allocation associated with existing water rights managed within the NPIC 
system, with annual quantities varying based on hydrologic conditions and shareholder participation levels. The 
resulting shareholder authorization established the initial pool of water available for delivery under the Contract 
Water framework and provided the foundation for negotiating the proposed agreement with the Town. 
 
The proposed 2027 Superseding Water Supply Agreement modernizes the Town’s water supply relationship 
with NPIC while preserving the most important elements of the existing arrangement. Major provisions 
include:  
• Preservation of the Town’s perpetual 275 AF Legacy Water entitlement. 
• Replacement of the existing Additional Water and Option Water provisions with a Contract Water 
framework.  
• A 40-year Contract Water term extending through Water Year 2067.  
• Initial Contract Water pricing of $1,200 per acre-foot adjusted annually by the Consumer Price Index 
for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U). 
• Step-down minimum annual payment commitments of $1.2 million in 2027, $1.0 million in 2028, 
$800,000 in 2029, and $600,000 in 2030.  
• Land lease provisions removed based on the Town’s land purchase from NPIC in 2023. 
• Improved operational scheduling and coordination requirements.  
• Accommodation of Town-owned NPIC share water and leased NPIC water supplies within Reservoir 
No. 3.  
 
The 2027 Superseding Water Supply Agreement provides substantially greater cost predictability than the 
existing market-based pricing structure and supports more effective long-range utility planning.  
The proposed Storage & Carriage Operations (SCO) Agreement is a companion agreement to the Water 
Supply Agreement and establishes the operational framework necessary to store, move, and manage water 
supplies through NPIC facilities. The agreement is intended to function alongside the Water Supply Agreement 
and is a critical component of the Town’s long-term water supply strategy. Major provisions include:  
• Authorization for storage and management of Town-owned NPIC share water in Reservoir No. 3. 
• Authorization for storage and management of leased NPIC water supplies.  
• Creation of an operational pathway for delivery and storage of future foreign water supplies.  
Page 49 of 358
 
• Establishment of accounting, storage, carriage, and administration procedures. 
• Definition of operational responsibilities for both parties.  
 
The SCO is key for diversifying the Town’s water supply portfolio as some supply alternatives identified 
through the Water Source Development plan will require an infrastructure pathway to storage and treatment. 
Summary of Key Differences: 1983 Water Supply Agreement vs. 2027 Water Supply Agreement + SCO 
Agreement 
Term/Duration 
• 1983: Overall framework is effectively perpetual, including legacy supply and additional market-based 
formula-priced supplies. 
• 2027: Legacy Water remains perpetual; Contract Water is term-limited to 40 years (through Water Year 
2067). 
 
Pricing and Cost Predictability 
• 1983: Additional/Option Water priced using a market-based formula driven by NPIC share values and 
interest rates, creating volatility and significant escalation in recent years. The cost of the 1983 
Agreement has become financially unsustainable for the Water Utility.  
• 2027: Contract Water starts at $1,200/AF in 2026 and adjusts annually by CPI-U, which is substantially 
lower than recent market-based formula pricing. Includes minimum annual payment commitments in 
early years as part of the negotiated structure ($1.2M in 2026 down to $600K by 2029). This pricing 
structure creates predictability for financial planning.  
 
Operational Clarity and Flexibility 
• 1983: Less detailed operational structure; focuses on the “layers” of water, storage, and prior land lease 
provisions. 
• 2027: Establishes operational expectations such as an Annual Water Schedule, improves clarity for 
Town-owned share water use, and includes periodic coordination requirements. 
 
Storage & Carriage for Diversification 
• 1983: Does not provide for delivery or storage of foreign water in NPIC facilities.  
• 2027 + SCO Agreement: Creates a pathway for the Town to use NPIC infrastructure to bring foreign 
water to Reservoir #3. This is a key building block for future water supply diversification.

FISCAL IMPLICATIONS 
The proposed agreements establish both one-time and ongoing financial obligations for the Water Utility 
Enterprise Fund. The SCO Agreement includes a one-time Initial Storage and Carriage Usage Fee of $230,000, 
payable in two installments of $115,000 upon execution in 2026 and $115,000 in 2027 with the annual invoice. 
Sufficient appropriated funding is available within the Water Utility Operations budget to accommodate the 
expenditure for 2026. For accounting and budget tracking purposes, the expense will be charged to the existing 
NPIC Lease Agreement line item. The total one-time obligation of $230,000 is within the Town 
Administrator’s signing authority under the Town’s Financial Policy.  
 
In addition, the agreements include recurring annual payments for water supply and operations, including 
annual minimum Contract Water purchase commitments through 2030, annual operations fees, applicable 
Northern Water use charges, and variable storage and carriage charges based on actual utilization. These costs 
will be budgeted annually through the Water Utility Enterprise Fund and incorporated into rate and financial 
planning processes.  
 
While the agreements establish new long-term financial commitments, they will replace the Town’s existing 
water supply agreement. The agreements provide greater cost certainty and budget predictability by 
transitioning the Town from an increasingly costly arrangement to one that better supports the Town’s long-
term water supply and financial planning objectives. A cost comparison summary illustrating the estimated 
five-year costs under the current and proposed agreement structure is included in the packet materials for 
reference.
